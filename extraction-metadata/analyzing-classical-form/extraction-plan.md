# Extraction Plan: Analyzing Classical Form (Caplin)

## Source

- **Title:** Analyzing Classical Form: An Approach for the Classroom
- **Author:** William E. Caplin
- **Scope:** Classical form analysis (Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven) using a function-based theory of formal organization

---

## Domain Taxonomy

### 1. Formal Functions (Core Theoretical Framework)

#### 1.1 Intrinsic Formal Functions
Functions that arise from the internal thematic/harmonic content of a passage.

- **Presentation function** -- statement of a basic idea and its repetition; tonic prolongation
- **Continuation function** -- fragmentation, acceleration of harmonic rhythm, sequential activity
- **Cadential function** -- conventional harmonic progression that closes a formal unit (I6-ii6-V-I or variants)
- **Initiating function** -- opening gesture that establishes tonality and motivic material
- **Medial function** -- transitional or connective activity between formal regions
- **Concluding function** -- post-cadential material that confirms closure

#### 1.2 Extrinsic Formal Functions
Functions defined by a passage's role within a larger form, independent of its internal content.

- **Main-theme function** -- opens a sonata exposition; typically tight-knit
- **Transition function** -- destabilizes home key and prepares subordinate key
- **Subordinate-theme function** -- establishes and confirms the subordinate key; typically loose
- **Development function** -- destabilizes tonality through sequential and modulatory activity
- **Recapitulatory function** -- restates exposition material resolving to the home key
- **Coda function** -- post-structural-close rhetorical confirmation
- **Introduction function** -- pre-structural material (slow introduction)

### 2. Theme-Types

#### 2.1 Simple Theme-Types
- **Sentence** -- presentation (basic idea + repetition) + continuation (+ cadential)
- **Period** -- antecedent (basic idea + contrasting idea -> HC) + consequent (basic idea + contrasting idea -> PAC)

#### 2.2 Hybrid Theme-Types
- **Hybrid 1** -- antecedent + continuation
- **Hybrid 2** -- antecedent + cadential
- **Hybrid 3** -- compound basic idea + continuation
- **Hybrid 4** -- compound basic idea + consequent

#### 2.3 Compound Theme-Types
- **Compound sentence** -- sentence whose presentation uses periods or sentences as components
- **Compound period** -- period whose antecedent and consequent are themselves sentences, periods, or hybrids
- **Compound hybrid** -- compound-level combinations

### 3. Cadential Types and Deviations

#### 3.1 Cadence Types
- **Perfect authentic cadence (PAC)** -- root-position V to root-position I, soprano on scale-degree 1
- **Imperfect authentic cadence (IAC)** -- V-I with soprano not on scale-degree 1, or inverted bass
- **Half cadence (HC)** -- phrase ending on V
- **Deceptive cadence (DC)** -- V resolving to vi or other non-tonic harmony

#### 3.2 Cadential Deviations
- **Evaded cadence** -- expected PAC undermined at point of resolution; new formal unit begins
- **Abandoned cadence** -- cadential progression breaks off before reaching V
- **Deceptive cadence (as deviation)** -- V resolves deceptively, requiring continuation

### 4. Formal Organization Parameters

#### 4.1 Tight-Knit Organization
Characteristics associated with main-theme and stable formal regions:
- Conventional theme-type (sentence or period)
- Harmonic stability and tonic prolongation
- Symmetrical phrase grouping
- Strong cadential closure (PAC in home key)

#### 4.2 Loose Organization
Characteristics associated with subordinate-theme, transition, and developmental regions:
- Non-conventional or hybrid theme-types
- Harmonic instability, modulation, sequence
- Asymmetrical or expanded phrase grouping
- Cadential deviation, delayed closure

### 5. Framing Functions
- **Before-the-beginning** -- introduction, "standing on the dominant"
- **After-the-end** -- codetta, coda, postcadential extensions

### 6. Large-Scale Forms

#### 6.1 Binary and Ternary
- **Small binary** -- two-part form without recapitulation of opening
- **Small ternary (rounded binary)** -- A-B-A' with contrasting middle and recapitulatory return

#### 6.2 Sonata Form
- **Exposition** -- main theme, transition, subordinate theme, (closing section)
- **Development** -- pre-core, core (model-sequence-fragmentation-cadence), retransition
- **Recapitulation** -- adjusted restatement resolving subordinate material to tonic
- **Coda** -- optional post-structural section

#### 6.3 Rondo Forms
- **Five-part rondo** -- A-B-A-C-A
- **Seven-part rondo** -- A-B-A-C-A-B-A
- **Sonata-rondo** -- rondo with sonata-form tonal procedures

#### 6.4 Other Movement Forms
- **Slow-movement forms** -- sonata without development, ABA, theme and variations
- **Minuet/Trio form** -- compound ternary (Minuet-Trio-Minuet da capo)
- **Concerto form** -- double-exposition sonata with orchestral ritornello and solo cadenza

### 7. Harmonic Concepts (as Prerequisites)

- Cadential progressions and bass patterns
- Tonicization and modulation
- Sequential harmony (model-sequence technique)
- Dominant prolongation ("standing on the dominant")
- Pedal point

## Notation Conventions

### Roman Numeral Analysis
- Upper-case Roman numerals for major triads: **I, IV, V**
- Lower-case for minor triads: **ii, vi**
- Superscript or inline figures for inversions: **I6, V6/5, V4/3, V4/2**
- Diminished: **vii-dim-6** (viio6)
- Applied (secondary) dominants: **V/V, V7/IV, viio7/V**

### Cadence Labels
- **PAC** -- perfect authentic cadence
- **IAC** -- imperfect authentic cadence
- **HC** -- half cadence
- **DC** -- deceptive cadence
- **EC** -- evaded cadence

### Key and Tonal References
- Home key indicated by upper-case letter for major, lower-case for minor: **C, c**
- Subordinate key: typically **V** (in major) or **III** (in minor)
- Keys indicated as Roman numerals relative to home key when discussing formal regions

### Formal Diagrams
- Section labels in vertical alignment showing: **formal function | harmony | key | measure numbers**
- Braces or brackets grouping theme-type components
- Abbreviations: **b.i.** (basic idea), **c.i.** (contrasting idea), **pres.** (presentation), **cont.** (continuation), **cad.** (cadential)
- Theme-type labels: **sent.** (sentence), **per.** (period)

### Structural Closure Labels
- **EEC** -- essential expositional closure (first satisfactory PAC in subordinate key within exposition)
- **ESC** -- essential structural closure (corresponding PAC in recapitulation, in home key)

### Measure References
- Measure numbers in parentheses: **(mm. 1-8)**
- Beat references where needed: **m. 4, beat 3**

### Tight-Knit vs. Loose Parameters
When characterizing formal regions, the following parameters are assessed:
- Harmonic stability vs. instability
- Phrase-structural symmetry vs. asymmetry
- Cadential strength vs. weakness/deviation
- Motivic unity vs. diversity
- Conventional vs. non-conventional theme-type
