# Quick Definition

The principles and mechanics of combining motive-forms into coherent phrases, governed by three factors contributing to logic: common content, rhythmic similarities, and coherent harmony.

# Core Definition

"The connexion of motive-forms depends on factors which can only be discussed at a later stage. However, the mechanics of combination can be described and demonstrated" (Ch. 4, p. 11). Three principal factors contribute to logic: "Common content is provided by using motive-forms derived from the same basic motive. Rhythmic similarities act as unifying elements. Coherent harmony reinforces relationship" (Ch. 4, p. 11).

# Prerequisites

- **motive** -- Must understand the motive and its features
- **variation** -- Must understand how variation produces motive-forms
- **variation-methods** -- Must know the specific techniques for producing motive-forms

# Key Properties

1. Three connecting principles: common content, rhythmic similarity, coherent harmony
2. Results must have the character of true phrases -- complete musical units
3. Not all combinations are suitable for every structural function
4. Some may begin a theme; others continue it; others provide contrast
5. Remotely related motive-forms may endanger comprehensibility
6. The practice of sketching combinations should never be abandoned

# Construction / Recognition

1. Derive all motive-forms from the same basic motive (common content)
2. Maintain rhythmic similarities as unifying elements
3. Support connections with coherent harmonic progressions
4. Ensure that results function as complete musical units (true phrases)
5. Distinguish phrases suitable for theme openings vs. continuations vs. contrasts
6. Accept that some results may be "stiff and overcrowded" -- practice improves fluency

# Context & Application

This chapter bridges the theoretical discussion of individual motives (Ch. 3) and the practical construction of themes (Ch. 5). It provides the intermediate step of learning to build phrases from motive-forms. Schoenberg acknowledges that the artistic aspects of connection "can only be discussed at a later stage," but the mechanics of combination are learnable through systematic practice.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Producing combinations that lack the character of complete phrases
  **Correction**: "It is important that the results have the character of true phrases -- of complete musical units" (Ch. 4, p. 13)

- **Error**: Assuming all phrase constructions are suitable for theme openings
  **Correction**: "Some of them might be used to begin a theme, others to continue it; and some...as material to meet other structural requirements"

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Expecting all motive-form connections to sound polished immediately
  **Clarification**: Schoenberg acknowledges that some results will be "stiff and overcrowded" -- the purpose is practice

- **Confusion**: Treating remotely related motive-forms as always acceptable
  **Clarification**: "The use of such remotely related motive-forms may endanger comprehensibility" (Ch. 4, p. 13)
