# Quick Definition
The principles and mechanics of combining motive-forms into coherent phrases, governed by common content, rhythmic similarities, and coherent harmony.

# Formal Definition
The connexion of motive-forms depends on three principal factors that contribute to logic: common content (provided by using motive-forms derived from the same basic motive), rhythmic similarities (acting as unifying elements), and coherent harmony (reinforcing relationship). While the artistic aspects of connection can only be discussed at a more advanced stage, the mechanics of combination can be described and demonstrated.

# Construction/Recognition
When connecting motive-forms into phrases:
- Derive all forms from the same basic motive to ensure common content
- Maintain rhythmic similarities as unifying elements
- Support connections with coherent harmonic progressions
- Ensure that results have the character of true phrases -- complete musical units
- Some combinations may be stiff at first; practice improves fluency

# Musical Context
This chapter bridges the gap between the theoretical discussion of individual motives (Chapter 3) and the practical construction of themes (Chapter 5). It provides the intermediate step of learning to build phrases from motive-forms before tackling complete thematic structures.

# Common Confusions
- The "stiffness" of some resulting phrases is acknowledged; the pedagogical point is to practice the mechanics of connection
- Not all motive-form combinations are suitable for theme openings; some are better suited for continuations, contrasts, or subordinate ideas
- Remotely related motive-forms may endanger comprehensibility even though they are technically derived from the same basic motive
