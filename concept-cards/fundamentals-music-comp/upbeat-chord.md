# Quick Definition
The harmony at the end of the B-section that leads to the recapitulation, called an "upbeat chord" because it functions like a rhythmic upbeat to the accentuated downbeat of the returning tonic -- regardless of its actual rhythmic placement.

# Formal Definition
The B-section ends on a harmony which leads to the recapitulation. In classical music this harmony is the dominant, because it reintroduces the tonic in its tonality-defining sense. Its effect in such cases is comparable to that of an upbeat to the subsequently accentuated downbeat. Because of this function, such chords will be called "upbeat chords," regardless of their rhythmic placement. The upbeat chord is sometimes postponed to the last eighth or sixteenth of the B-section.

# Construction/Recognition
The upbeat chord is identified by: (1) its position at the end of the B-section, (2) its function of leading to and preparing the recapitulation, (3) in classical music, it is typically V, (4) a small connective figure often serves as a bridge between the upbeat chord and the recapitulation. Other harmonies (ii, iii, and their derivatives) can also function as upbeat harmonies to I. If the recapitulation begins on a degree other than I, the upbeat chord must be chosen accordingly.

# Musical Context
The upbeat chord is a critical structural juncture in the small ternary form. It is the moment where the B-section's harmonic exploration resolves into the preparation for return. The term "upbeat chord" is Schoenberg's specific usage, defined by harmonic function rather than rhythmic position.

# Examples
- In Beethoven Op. 2/1-II, Op. 2/2-IV, Op. 2/2-II, and many others: the B-section ends on V as upbeat chord, with a small connective figure leading to the recapitulation
- Ex. 105: the upbeat chord at the end of all ten B-sections is V, sometimes postponed to the last eighth or sixteenth
- Ex. 106: ii and iii (and their derivatives) used as upbeat harmonies to I
- Ex. 107b: the upbeat harmony is bIII, introducing a recapitulation that begins on V
- Ex. 107a: the recapitulation begins on II, introduced by bVI

# Common Confusions
- "Upbeat chord" does not refer to rhythmic placement; it is defined by harmonic function (leading to the recapitulation) regardless of where it falls in the measure
- The upbeat chord is not always V; other harmonies can serve this function, especially when the recapitulation begins on a degree other than I
- The connective figure between the upbeat chord and the recapitulation is a separate structural element, not part of the upbeat chord itself
