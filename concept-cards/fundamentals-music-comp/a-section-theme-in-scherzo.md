# Quick Definition

The opening thematic section of the scherzo, which in principle does not differ from previously discussed A-sections, but is often double the usual number of measures on account of the rapid tempo, with relatively stable harmony and structural simplicity.

# Core Definition

The A-section of the scherzo does not differ in principle from A-sections discussed in the context of smaller forms. The theme may be constructed as a period or a sentence. Often the theme is double the usual number of measures on account of the rapid tempo -- what would be eight measures in moderate tempo becomes sixteen measures. The A-section exposes its motive-forms in various aspects over relatively stable harmony. The rapid tempo prevents frequent change of harmonies and remote variation of motive-forms.

# Prerequisites

- **Scherzo form**: Understanding the ternary structure
- **Period structure**: One option for A-section theme construction

# Key Properties

- May be a period or a sentence, often doubled in length due to rapid tempo
- Harmony is relatively stable compared to the B-section
- Motive-forms are exposed in various aspects
- Remote variation of motive-forms is constrained by rapid tempo
- The theme structure may reveal connections to other movements (monothematicism)
- The skeleton may consist of circumscriptions of ascending tonic and descending dominant

# Construction / Recognition

1. Identify the theme type (period or sentence)
2. Account for measure-count doubling due to rapid tempo
3. Verify relatively stable harmony
4. Note the motive-forms and their variations within the A-section
5. Check for monothematic connections to other movements
6. Analyze the structural skeleton (circumscription of tonic/dominant arpeggios)

# Context & Application

The A-section establishes the thematic material that will be elaborated in the contrasting middle section. Its relatively stable harmony contrasts with the modulatory instability of the B-section. The Eroica Symphony scherzo theme is paradigmatic: structurally a circumscription of the ascending tonic and descending dominant, with strong connections to the first and fourth movement themes -- supporting the concept of monothematicism in cyclic works.

# Examples

- Beethoven, Op. 2/2, Op. 2/3, Op. 28, Symphony No. 2 (Ex. 120c): Begin with periods
- Beethoven, Violin Sonata Op. 30/2 (Ex. 120a), String Quartet Op. 18/1 (Ex. 120b): Begin with sentences
- Beethoven, Eroica Symphony (Ex. 121a): Sentence; circumscription of ascending tonic and descending dominant (Exs. 121b, c); related to first and fourth movement themes (Exs. 121d, e) -- evidence of monothematicism

# Common Errors

- Making the A-section theme too complex for the rapid tempo (remote motive-forms will be incomprehensible)
- Changing harmony too frequently for the tempo (the scherzo requires slower harmonic rhythm than the minuet)
- Ignoring the measure-count doubling principle

# Common Confusions

- **Doubling of measures**: The doubling is a consequence of rapid tempo, not a formal requirement. An eight-measure scherzo theme functions like a four-measure theme at a slower tempo.
- **Monothematicism**: The connection between scherzo and other movement themes is not always present, but when it occurs, it strengthens the cyclic unity of the work.
