# Quick Definition
The opening thematic section of the scherzo, which in principle does not differ from previously discussed A-sections, but is often double the usual number of measures on account of the rapid tempo.

# Formal Definition
The A-section of the scherzo does not differ in principle from A-sections discussed in the context of smaller forms. The theme may be constructed as a period or a sentence. Often the theme is double the usual number of measures on account of the rapid tempo -- what would be eight measures in a moderate tempo becomes sixteen measures in a scherzo. The A-section exposes its motive-forms in various aspects over relatively stable harmony.

# Construction/Recognition
- May be a period or a sentence, doubled in length due to rapid tempo
- Harmony is relatively stable compared to the B-section
- Motive-forms are exposed in various aspects
- The theme structure may reveal connections to other movements (monothematicism)
- The skeleton may consist of circumscriptions of ascending tonic and descending dominant arpeggios, clothed with auxiliaries and passing notes

# Musical Context
The A-section establishes the thematic material that will be elaborated in the contrasting middle section. Its relatively stable harmony contrasts with the modulatory instability of the B-section. The structural simplicity of the A-section theme is important because the rapid tempo prevents complex motive variation and frequent harmonic change.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/2, Op. 2/3, Op. 28, Symphony No. 2 (Ex. 120c): Begin with periods
- Beethoven, Violin Sonata Op. 30/2 (Ex. 120a), String Quartet Op. 18/1 (Ex. 120b), Eroica Symphony (Ex. 121a): Begin with sentences
- Eroica Symphony Scherzo: Structurally a circumscription of the ascending tonic and descending dominant; related to the themes of the first and fourth movements, supporting the concept of monothematicism in a cyclic work

# Common Confusions
- The doubling of measure count due to rapid tempo does not change the underlying structural principles
- A-section themes in scherzos should be relatively simple due to the constraints of rapid tempo
- The relationship between scherzo themes and themes of other movements is an important aspect of cyclic form
