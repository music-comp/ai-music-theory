# Quick Definition
The most elaborate rondo type (aba-CC'-aba), containing both a trio (C) and a Durchfuhrung (C') in its central section, combining the maximum structural resources of both rondo and sonata forms at the apex of the rondo hierarchy.

# Core Definition
The great sonata-rondo has the pattern aba-CC'-aba, containing both trio and Durchfuhrung. It represents the apex of the rondo form hierarchy, combining the repetitive thematic character of the rondo with both the contrasting middle section of the ternary form (trio) and the modulatory elaboration of the sonata-allegro (Durchfuhrung). The C and C' sections may interpenetrate or blend characteristics, and the ordering may vary.

# Prerequisites
- Sonata-rondo form
- Trio in rondo
- Elaboration (Durchfuhrung) technique

# Key Properties
- Pattern: aba-CC'-aba
- Contains both trio (C) and Durchfuhrung (C')
- Apex of the rondo form hierarchy
- The ordering of C and C' may vary
- Both sections may interpenetrate or blend characteristics
- Exposition and recapitulation follow standard rondo procedure
- Provides maximum structural weight for a concluding movement

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the exposition (aba): principal theme, transition, subordinate group, return of A
2. Note the presence of both trio and Durchfuhrung in the central section
3. Determine whether the sections are clearly separated or blended
4. The trio provides contrasting key and character
5. The Durchfuhrung provides modulatory elaboration
6. Identify the recapitulation (aba) with material in tonic region

# Context & Application
This form represents the most complex end of the rondo spectrum, typically found in finales of major works. It provides the maximum structural weight for a concluding movement, combining the thematic returns and cheerful character of the rondo with the developmental depth of the sonata-allegro. The blending of trio and Durchfuhrung characteristics is a deliberate feature.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 22-IV: the C-section begins like an etude-like trio in f (minor v) but gives way (m. 81) to a modulatory-contrapuntal group derived from transitional material; the incipient Durchfuhrung is followed by a return of the etude-like segment in bb (tonic minor), continuing the modulatory process instead of returning to the first key as normal ternary procedure would demand -- thus combining trio character with Durchfuhrung procedures

# Common Errors
- Failing to integrate the trio and Durchfuhrung sections when they interpenetrate
- Making both sections too similar in character, reducing the contrast
- Treating the form as merely additive (trio + Durchfuhrung) rather than as an organic synthesis

# Common Confusions
- The great sonata-rondo is distinguished from the sonata-rondo by the presence of both a trio and a Durchfuhrung, not just one or the other
- The C and C' sections may blend their characteristics, making it difficult to draw sharp boundaries
- The form is classified by its structural principles, not by its length
