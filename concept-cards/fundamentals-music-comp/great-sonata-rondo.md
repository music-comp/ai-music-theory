# Quick Definition
The most elaborate rondo type (aba-CC'-aba), containing both a trio (C) and a Durchfuhrung (C') in its central section, combining the maximum structural resources of both rondo and sonata forms.

# Formal Definition
The great sonata-rondo has the pattern aba-CC'-aba, containing both trio and Durchfuhrung. It represents the apex of the rondo form hierarchy, combining the repetitive thematic character of the rondo with both the contrasting middle section of the ternary form (trio) and the modulatory elaboration of the sonata-allegro (Durchfuhrung).

# Construction/Recognition
- Pattern: aba-CC'-aba
- Exposition (aba): principal theme, transition, subordinate group in contrasting key, return of principal theme
- C: Trio section providing contrasting key and character
- C': Durchfuhrung section providing modulatory elaboration of previously exposed material
- Recapitulation (aba): return of all principal material in the tonic region
- The ordering of C and C' may vary
- Both trio and elaboration sections may interpenetrate or blend characteristics

# Musical Context
This form represents the most complex end of the rondo spectrum, typically found in finales of major works. It provides the maximum structural weight for a concluding movement, combining the thematic returns and cheerful character of the rondo with the developmental depth of the sonata-allegro.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 22-IV illustrates the blending of trio and Durchfuhrung characteristics: the C-section begins like an etude-like trio in f (minor v) but gives way to a modulatory-contrapuntal group derived from transitional material; the incipient Durchfuhrung is followed by a return of the etude-like segment, but in bb (tonic minor), continuing the modulatory process instead of returning to the first key as normal ternary procedure would demand

# Common Confusions
- The great sonata-rondo is distinguished from the sonata-rondo by the presence of both a trio and a Durchfuhrung, not just one or the other
- The C and C' sections may blend their characteristics, making it difficult to draw sharp boundaries between trio and elaboration
- The form is classified by its structural principles, not by its length; a great sonata-rondo need not be the longest rondo type
