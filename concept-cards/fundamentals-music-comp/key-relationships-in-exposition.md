# Quick Definition
The standard tonal relationships between principal and subordinate themes in the sonata-allegro exposition: dominant (V) for major-key movements, relative major (III) or minor dominant (v) for minor-key movements.

# Formal Definition
Key relationships are substantially the same as in contrasting middle sections for the simpler forms. When the principal theme is major, the subordinate group nearly always lies in the key of V. Sometimes the interchangeability of parallel major and minor permits the appearance of part or all of the subordinate group in the key of v (minor), with the exception of codettas. In minor, the preferred keys are III (relative major) and v (minor). Since a theme which first appears in a major key does not always work satisfactorily in minor, and since the minor-major contrast between principal and subordinate themes may be essential, considerable modification may be required.

# Construction/Recognition
- **Major key movements**: subordinate group in V (dominant major); occasionally v (dominant minor) for part of the group
- **Minor key movements**: subordinate group in III (relative major) or v (minor dominant)
- Departures from these standard relationships exist, particularly in composers after Beethoven
- The interchangeability of parallel major and minor permits some flexibility
- Codettas typically remain in the major mode even when the subordinate group touches on the minor dominant

# Musical Context
The key relationship between principal and subordinate themes creates the fundamental tonal tension of the exposition that the recapitulation will resolve. The standard relationships evolved through Classical practice and remained normative through the Romantic period, though departures became more common.

# Examples
- Standard major key: subordinate group in V (most Beethoven major-key first movements)
- Beethoven, Op. 2/2-I and Op. 2/3-I: part of the subordinate group in v (minor dominant), with codettas in major
- Beethoven, Op. 13-I (c minor): subordinate themes in Eb (III, relative major)
- Brahms, Piano Quintet Op. 34 in f: subordinate theme in c#, recapitulated in f# -- a striking departure requiring "masterly adaptations of the form and the material"

# Common Confusions
- The "standard" key relationships are norms, not rules; departures are possible but always require careful formal adaptations
- The interchangeability of parallel major and minor means that v (minor dominant) can substitute for V in parts of the subordinate group, but this is not a modulation to a different key area
- In minor keys, the choice between III and v as the subordinate key has significant consequences for the recapitulation
