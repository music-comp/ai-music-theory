# Quick Definition
The pattern for a sequence is usually a transformation or condensation of preceding motive-forms, constructed so that it introduces the degree on which the sequence begins and provides smooth melodic connection.

# Formal Definition
The pattern for sequential treatment is usually a transformation or condensation of preceding motive-forms. It must be so built, harmonically and melodically, that it introduces the degree on which the sequence is to begin and provides a smooth melodic connection. Assuming correct harmonic connection, the pattern may begin on any degree. The patterns reveal various ways of combining and transforming preceding motive-forms: the order may be the same as in the first phrase, reversed, or selective (using only some of the original motive-forms).

# Construction/Recognition
- The pattern transforms or condenses preceding motive-forms
- Must introduce the starting degree of the sequence smoothly
- Must provide melodic connection between pattern and sequence
- May begin on any degree if harmonic connection is correct
- Order of motive-forms may be preserved, reversed, or selective
- The motive of the accompaniment may shift from weak to strong beats (or vice versa) across the pattern boundary

# Musical Context
Pattern formation is the crucial step between the established thematic material and its sequential continuation. The pattern acts as a bridge, transforming familiar material into a form suitable for transposition while maintaining motivic connection. The quality of the pattern determines whether the sequence will sound organic or mechanical.

# Examples
- Exs. 54d, 55b: Pattern preserves the same order as the first phrase
- Exs. 54e, f: Pattern reverses the order ('b'-'a')
- Ex. 56: Pattern uses only 'b' and 'c' from the original motive-forms
- Ex. 55a: Pattern begins with a transposition of the feature that ended the preceding phrase ('c1', m. 8), associating it with 'b' in the form used in m. 3 and 7
- Exs. 54-56: The accompaniment motive 'e' shifts from weak to strong measure in m. 9-10 (analyzed in 6/4 metre)

# Common Confusions
- The pattern is not simply the original motive placed on a new degree; it is a transformation or condensation that may reorder or select from the original material.
- A good pattern must work both harmonically and melodically -- either alone is insufficient.
- The pattern must introduce its own starting degree, creating a smooth harmonic link; abrupt harmonic shifts undermine the sequential logic.
