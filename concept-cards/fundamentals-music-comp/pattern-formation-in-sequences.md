# Quick Definition

The pattern for a sequence is usually a transformation or condensation of preceding motive-forms, constructed so that it introduces the degree on which the sequence begins and provides smooth melodic connection.

# Core Definition

The pattern for sequential treatment is usually a transformation or condensation of preceding motive-forms. It must be so built, harmonically and melodically, that it introduces the degree on which the sequence is to begin and provides a smooth melodic connection. Assuming correct harmonic connection, the pattern may begin on any degree (Schoenberg, p. 40).

The patterns reveal various ways of combining and transforming preceding motive-forms: the order may be the same as in the first phrase, reversed, or selective (using only some of the original motive-forms). The motive of the accompaniment may also shift from weak to strong beats across the pattern boundary (p. 41).

# Key Properties

1. Pattern transforms or condenses preceding motive-forms
2. Must introduce the starting degree of the sequence smoothly
3. Must provide melodic connection between pattern and sequence
4. May begin on any degree if harmonic connection is correct
5. Order of motive-forms may be preserved, reversed, or selective
6. The accompaniment motive may shift metric position across the pattern boundary

# Construction / Recognition

## To Construct a Pattern

1. Select motive-forms from the preceding material
2. Transform or condense them into a suitable pattern
3. Choose the order: same as original, reversed, or selective
4. Ensure the pattern introduces the starting degree of the sequence
5. Provide smooth melodic connection
6. Verify harmonic connection at the junction

## To Recognize a Pattern

1. Identify the repeated unit in the sequential passage
2. Trace its derivation from preceding motive-forms
3. Note the order of motive-forms compared to the original
4. Check the harmonic and melodic connection at boundaries

# Context & Application

Pattern formation is the crucial step between established thematic material and sequential continuation. The pattern acts as a bridge, transforming familiar material into a form suitable for transposition while maintaining motivic connection. The quality of the pattern determines whether the sequence sounds organic or mechanical.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Using the original motive unchanged as the pattern
  **Correction**: The pattern should be a transformation or condensation, not a literal copy

- **Error**: Creating a pattern that does not smoothly introduce the starting degree
  **Correction**: Both harmonic and melodic connection must be smooth at the junction

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the pattern must preserve the original order of motive-forms
  **Clarification**: The order may be preserved, reversed, or selective

- **Confusion**: Assuming the pattern must work only harmonically or only melodically
  **Clarification**: It must work both harmonically and melodically
