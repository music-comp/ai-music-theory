# Quick Definition
A three-part form (A-B-A1) in which the third part is a repetition or modified repetition of the first, and the second part provides contrast -- the most prevalent structural pattern in music.

# Core Definition
An overwhelming proportion of musical forms is structurally composed of three parts. The third part is sometimes a true repetition (recapitulation) of the first, but more often it is a more or less modified repetition. The second part is organized as a contrast. This form may derive from the early "Rondeau," in which interludes were inserted between repetitions of a refrain. Repetition satisfies the desire to hear again what was pleasing at first hearing and simultaneously aids comprehension. Contrast is useful to avoid the possibility of monotony.

# Key Properties
- Three-part structure: A-B-A1
- A-section: sentence or period, ending on I, V, or III (iii) in major; i, III, V, or v in minor
- A-section beginning must clearly express tonality (because of the contrast to follow)
- B-section: organized as a contrast, typically built from two-measure units
- A1-section: recapitulation, usually modified, ending on tonic if completing an independent piece
- Practice form: 8+4+4 measures (A=8, B=4, A1=4)
- Derived possibly from the early Rondeau (interludes between refrain repetitions)
- Formerly called "Three-Part Song Form" but not limited to songs

# Construction / Recognition
The A-section establishes tonality clearly. The B-section contraposes a contrasting region while maintaining motivic coherence. The A1-section returns modified A-material and cadences on the tonic. The practice form (8+4+4) is a pedagogical abstraction; actual proportions vary considerably.

# Context & Application
Sections producing contrasts of various types and degrees appear in many forms: small ternary form, larger ternary forms such as Minuet or Scherzo, and Sonata or Symphony. The small ternary form is foundational to understanding all larger formal structures. It provides the essential practice ground for handling contrast and coherence.

# Examples
- Beethoven Op. 2/1-II, m. 1-16: model practice form; A-section is 8-measure period with caesura on V at m. 4; B-section is 4 measures; A1 condensed to 4 measures
- Beethoven Op. 2/2-IV, m. 1-16: another practice form illustration with B-section on a pedal
- Beethoven Op. 2/2-II, m. 1-19: differs from practice form mainly in recapitulation length
- Beethoven Op. 7-II: longer B-section (6 measures) and extended recapitulation (10 measures)
- Beethoven Op. 26-I: each section double the usual length, with extended middle section

# Common Errors
- Making the A-section's tonal beginning unclear, weakening the subsequent contrast
- Using an unchanged recapitulation when modification would avoid monotony
- Creating a B-section that lacks motivic connection to the A-section

# Common Confusions
- "Small ternary form" was formerly called "Three-Part Song Form," but it is not limited to songs
- The recapitulation is seldom a literal repetition; some degree of modification is typical
- The practice form (8+4+4) is a pedagogical abstraction; actual examples vary considerably in proportions
