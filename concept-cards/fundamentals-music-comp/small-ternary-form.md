# Quick Definition
A three-part form (A-B-A1) in which the third part is a repetition or modified repetition of the first, and the second part provides contrast -- the most prevalent structural pattern in music.

# Formal Definition
An overwhelming proportion of musical forms is structurally composed of three parts. The third part is sometimes a true repetition (recapitulation) of the first, but more often it is a more or less modified repetition. The second part is organized as a contrast. This form may derive from the early "Rondeau," in which interludes were inserted between repetitions of a refrain. Repetition satisfies the desire to hear again what was pleasing at first hearing and simultaneously aids comprehension. Contrast is useful to avoid the possibility of monotony.

# Construction/Recognition
The A-section may be a sentence or period, ending on I, V, or III (iii) in major; on i, III, V, or v in minor. The beginning should clearly express the tonality because of the contrast to follow. The B-section provides harmonic and motivic contrast. The A1-section (recapitulation) ends on the tonic if it completes an independent piece, and is seldom an unchanged repetition.

# Musical Context
Sections producing contrasts of various types and degrees appear in many forms: small ternary form (formerly called "Three-Part Song Form"), larger ternary forms such as Minuet or Scherzo, and Sonata or Symphony. The small ternary form is foundational to understanding larger formal structures.

# Examples
- Beethoven Op. 2/1-II, m. 1-16: a model from which the practice form can be abstracted; A-section is an 8-measure period, B-section is 4 measures, A1-section condensed to 4 measures
- Beethoven Op. 2/2-IV, m. 1-16: another illustration of the practice form
- Beethoven Op. 2/2-II, m. 1-19: differs from practice form mainly in length of recapitulation

# Common Confusions
- "Small ternary form" was formerly called "Three-Part Song Form," but it is not limited to songs
- The recapitulation is seldom a literal repetition; some degree of modification is typical
- The practice form (8+4+4 measures) is a pedagogical abstraction; actual examples vary considerably in proportions
