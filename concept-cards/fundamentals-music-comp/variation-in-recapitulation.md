# Quick Definition
The principles of variation applied to the recapitulation, including ornamental variation, harmonic insertions and substitutions, accompaniment changes, contrapuntal additions, and changed cadences -- all used with moderation to maintain recognition.

# Formal Definition
The principles of variation can be applied to all the elements of the A-section, but with moderation, so as not to conceal the presence of a repetition. The contour of the melody should not be entirely changed, except at points where essential structural changes occur. Ornamental variation (breaking down longer notes through passing notes, circumscribing notes, tone repetitions) need not change the contour essentially. Excessive rhythmic changes or shifts of accent are likely to prevent recognition.

# Construction/Recognition
Types of variation in recapitulation: (1) ornamental variation of the melody, (2) harmonic insertions and substitutions, (3) changes in accompaniment figuration (less far-reaching than melodic changes), (4) contrapuntal or semi-contrapuntal additions (imitation, counter-melody, melodic elaboration of subordinate voices), (5) changed cadence requiring reformulation or reconstruction. In a small form, alterations should maintain balance with preceding sections.

# Musical Context
The tension between recognition and variation is the central artistic challenge of the recapitulation. Too little variation produces monotony; too much prevents the listener from recognizing the return of the A-section material. The composer must calibrate variation to the scale of the form -- a small form demands restraint.

# Examples
- Beethoven Op. 27/1, Adagio: recapitulation repeats the entire A-section with minor elaborations of piano style and a changed cadence
- Haydn Ex. 102d: only the embellishments are varied in m. 13-17; the cadence (m. 18-20) requires more change, replacing half-cadence to V with full cadence to I
- Beethoven Op. 31/1-III: the main melody is transferred to the tenor voice, with a quasi-melodically elaborated accompaniment added in the right hand

# Common Confusions
- Variation in recapitulation is not free variation; it must preserve enough of the original to be recognized as a repetition
- Contrapuntal additions (counter-melodies, imitations) are often semi-contrapuntal rather than true counterpoint based on invertible combinations
- Accompaniment variation should be less extensive than melodic variation
