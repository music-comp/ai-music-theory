# Quick Definition

A ternary (A-B-A') dance form in 3/4 metre, typically of moderate tempo, serving as an independent piece or middle movement in cyclic forms such as the suite, symphony, or sonata.

# Core Definition

The minuet is an A-B-A' form quite similar to the small ternary form. A practice form derived from it need not diverge from this A-B-A' structure. However, the customary repetition marks (placing the B-section after the A-section and then the A'-section) must be taken into consideration. The only specific rhythmic feature is the metre, 3/4 (or rarely 3/8). Many minuets in the literature differ from the practice form through structural deviations in all three parts: unequal length of phrases, sequential or other internal repetitions, extensions, codettas, episodes, and even co-ordinate ideas.

# Prerequisites

- **Small ternary form**: The minuet is essentially an expanded application of the small ternary form
- **Period structure**: A-sections are typically built as periods
- **Sentence structure**: Alternative A-section construction

# Key Properties

- Three-part structure: A-section, contrasting middle section (B), recapitulation (A')
- Metre: 3/4 (rarely 3/8)
- Tempo: moderate, typically dotted half-note = 60-70 (Mozart, Haydn); Beethoven's extremes reach dotted half-note = 108
- More small note values (eighths and sixteenths) than the scherzo due to slower tempo
- Harmony changes more frequently than in the scherzo -- seldom lasts longer than one or two measures, often two or more harmonies within a single measure
- Character: ranges from unpretentiously singable to stubbornly insistent, but generally moderate
- Restrained rhythmic accentuation compared to popular dances (courtly origin)
- Usually followed by a trio, after which the minuet is repeated (da capo)

# Construction / Recognition

1. Identify the A-B-A' ternary structure
2. Verify 3/4 metre and moderate tempo
3. Check harmonic rhythm (frequent changes typical of minuet vs. slower changes in scherzo)
4. Look for structural deviations: unequal phrases, extensions, codettas, episodes
5. Note the customary repetition scheme: ||: A :||: B A' :||
6. Identify the trio that typically follows

# Context & Application

The minuet was the favoured dance of eighteenth-century courts. As a courtly dance, it did not call for strong rhythmic accentuation -- conventional accompaniment patterns were probably never much used; vestiges that appear are generally stylized. The minuet appears as an independent piece or as a middle movement in cyclic forms (suite, symphony, sonata). It was largely supplanted by the scherzo from Beethoven onwards, though Beethoven's Symphony No. 1 Minuet (dotted half-note = 108) is already scherzo-like in tempo.

# Examples

- Bach, French Suite No. 5, Menuetto (Ex. 113): Simple minuet corresponding closely to the practice form
- Haydn, String Quartet Op. 54/1, Menuetto (Ex. 114): A-section period of 5+5 measures (uneven but symmetrical)
- Beethoven, Op. 31/3-III: Unpretentiously singable character
- Mozart, Symphony No. 40 in g minor: Stubbornly insistent character
- Beethoven, First and Eighth Symphonies: Extreme tempo of dotted half-note = 108

- Contrasting middle section in minuet (the B-section)
- Recapitulation in minuet (the A'-section)
- Codetta in dance forms (closing material)

## Contrasts With
- Scherzo form: faster tempo, more rhythmic accentuation, less frequent harmonic change, more modulatory middle section

# Common Errors

- Composing a minuet with scherzo-like rhythmic drive and infrequent harmonic change
- Failing to account for the customary repetition scheme (||: A :||: B A' :||)
- Writing overly simple B-sections -- many minuets have genuinely modulatory middle sections

# Common Confusions

- **Minuet vs. scherzo**: The distinction is not always clear-cut; Beethoven sometimes called scherzo-like movements "Minuet" and vice versa. The key differences are tempo (moderate vs. fast), harmonic rhythm (frequent vs. slow change), and rhythmic accentuation (restrained vs. prominent).
- **Minuet vs. any 3/4 piece**: Not every piece in 3/4 is a minuet; the minuet has a specific ternary form and moderate character.
