# Quick Definition
A ternary (A-B-A') dance form in 3/4 metre, typically of moderate tempo, serving as an independent piece or middle movement in cyclic forms such as the suite, symphony, or sonata.

# Formal Definition
The minuet is an A-B-A' form quite similar to the small ternary form. A practice form derived from it need not diverge from this A-B-A' structure. However, the customary repetition marks (placing the B-section after the A-section and then the A'-section) must be taken into consideration. The only specific rhythmic feature is the metre, 3/4 (or rarely 3/8). Many minuets in the literature differ from the practice form through structural deviations in all three parts.

# Construction/Recognition
- Three-part structure: A-section, contrasting middle section (B), recapitulation (A')
- Metre: 3/4 (rarely 3/8)
- Tempo: moderate, typically dotted half-note = 60-70 (Mozart, Haydn), though Beethoven's extremes reach dotted half-note = 108
- More small note values (eighths and sixteenths) than the scherzo due to slower tempo
- Harmony changes more frequently than in the scherzo -- seldom lasts longer than one or two measures
- Character ranges from unpretentiously singable to stubbornly insistent

# Musical Context
As the favoured dance of eighteenth-century courts, the minuet did not call for strong rhythmic accentuation. Conventional accompaniment patterns were probably never much used; vestiges that appear are generally stylized. The minuet appears as an independent piece or as a middle movement in cyclic forms (suite, symphony, sonata). It was largely supplanted by the scherzo from Beethoven onwards.

# Examples
- Bach, French Suite No. 5, Menuetto (Ex. 113): Simple minuet corresponding closely to the practice form
- Beethoven, Op. 31/3-III: Unpretentiously singable character
- Mozart, Symphony No. 40 in g minor: Stubbornly insistent character
- Beethoven, First Symphony: Dotted half-note = 108 (extreme tempo)
- Beethoven, Eighth Symphony: Dotted half-note = 108 (extreme tempo)

# Common Confusions
- The minuet is not simply any piece in 3/4 -- it has a specific ternary form and moderate character
- Despite the name "dance," the courtly minuet was more restrained in rhythmic accentuation than popular dances
- The difference between minuet and scherzo is not always clear-cut; Beethoven sometimes called scherzo-like movements "Minuet" and vice versa
