# Quick Definition
The first main division of the sonata-allegro, presenting the principal and subordinate themes in contrasting but related tonalities, connected by a transition and concluded by a closing section -- essentially stable in harmony despite internal modulation.

# Formal Definition
The exposition resembles the first division of the larger rondos in several respects. The statements of the principal and subordinate themes are in contrasting but related tonalities. A transition connects them in all but the simplest cases. A distinct contrast of character helps to distinguish the various themes, though analysis will demonstrate their inter-connexion through common use of basic motive-features. It differs from the rondos in that the principal theme is not repeated prior to the elaboration, and there is no return to the principal key. Instead, a closing section, still in a contrasting region, completes the exposition.

# Construction/Recognition
The exposition typically contains:
1. **Principal theme or group**: in the tonic region
2. **Transition**: modulatory passage (independent or derived from main theme)
3. **Subordinate group**: in a contrasting but related key (V in major; III or v in minor)
4. **Closing section/codettas**: completing the exposition in the contrasting region

The harmony is essentially stable: apart from transitions, everything stands solidly within the region of a definite tonality. This stability requires a different kind of contrast in the elaboration.

# Musical Context
The exposition "exposes" the wealth of thematic material that will be worked out in the elaboration. The stability of its harmonic organization provides the necessary foundation for the modulatory contrast of the middle section.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1-I: simple sentence theme (8 measures), short transition, subordinate theme in m. 21
- Beethoven, Op. 7-I: extensive subordinate group with six distinct sections
- Beethoven, Op. 28-I: 39-measure principal theme above a tonic pedal point, subordinate group of 124 measures

# Common Confusions
- The exposition differs from the rondo exposition in two key ways: no return of the principal theme, and no return to the principal key before the elaboration
- "Essentially stable" does not mean no modulation occurs; transitions modulate, and the subordinate group is in a different key; stability refers to the fact that themes are firmly established within definite tonal regions
- The closing section may use a formulation derived from the principal theme (as in Op. 18/1-I, m. 72) or introduce a special closing theme
