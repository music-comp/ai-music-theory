# Quick Definition
Rhythmic features serve as principal means of distinguishing specific musical characters, both between genres (e.g., dance forms) and within individual pieces (providing internal contrasts).

# Core Definition
Rhythmic characteristics are the principal means of distinguishing one type of music from another. Old dance forms were characterized by certain rhythms in the accompaniment, which were also reflected in the melody. More generally, rhythmic features help to establish the mood and special character of an individual piece, as well as to provide the structurally necessary internal contrasts.

# Prerequisites
- character-and-mood: Rhythmic characteristics are one component of the larger concept of character

# Key Properties
- Principal means of distinguishing between dance forms (mazurka, gavotte, polka)
- Rhythmic patterns appear in both accompaniment and melody
- Rhythmic features establish mood and special character of individual pieces
- Rhythmic contrast provides structurally necessary internal variety
- Operates at multiple levels: beat pattern, phrase rhythm, larger structural rhythm

# Construction / Recognition
Identify rhythmic characteristics by examining: (1) the metric and rhythmic patterns in the accompaniment, (2) the rhythmic profile of the melody, (3) how rhythmic patterns differ between sections to create contrast, and (4) what rhythmic gestures are associated with specific genres or moods.

# Context & Application
Dance forms historically relied on specific rhythmic patterns to define their identity. Beyond genre identification, rhythmic features are essential for creating the internal contrasts needed within any movement or piece. Students should practice composing with widely different rhythms and metres to develop their capacity for contrast.

# Examples
- Old dance forms (mazurka, gavotte, polka) are distinguished principally by their rhythmic patterns in the accompaniment
- In Beethoven Op. 57-I, rhythmic changes contribute to the many contrasts within the movement
- The difference between the character of various movements within a Beethoven sonata is partly defined by rhythmic characteristics

# Common Errors
- Confusing rhythm with tempo; a piece can maintain the same tempo while shifting character through rhythmic change
- Using the same rhythmic pattern throughout without variation
- Neglecting the rhythmic dimension when focusing on harmony and melody

# Common Confusions
- Rhythm is not the same as tempo; rhythmic characteristics operate at multiple levels simultaneously
- Rhythmic features in the accompaniment are as important as those in the melody
