# Quick Definition
The principle that the elaboration section uses previously exposed thematic material rather than introducing new ideas, deriving maximum variety from the wealth of motives already presented in the exposition -- with formerly inconspicuous features often dominating the entire division.

# Core Definition
Economy dictates the use of previously "exposed" thematic material (a wealth of differing ideas is presented in the first division). The elaboration normally makes use of variants of previously exposed themes, seldom evolving new musical ideas. Often a small number of features, formerly inconspicuous, dominate the entire division. The thematic material may be drawn from the themes of the exposition in any order. Occasionally an idea appears which, though deriving from the basic material, never appeared before as formulated -- demonstrating "masterly imagination and intuition."

# Prerequisites
- Elaboration (Durchfuhrung) technique
- Exposition structure
- Motive and motive-form manipulation

# Key Properties
- Uses previously exposed thematic material, not new ideas
- Material may be drawn from exposition themes in any order
- Formerly inconspicuous features may become dominant elements
- Some elaborations are essentially monothematic
- Others draw on multiple themes but transform them
- The rare appearance of genuinely new formulations is an exception demonstrating mastery
- Economy ensures the exposition's motivic investment yields maximum return

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify which exposition themes appear in the elaboration
2. Note whether formerly inconspicuous features have become dominant
3. Determine whether the elaboration is monothematic or polythematic
4. Look for transformation and recontextualization of existing material
5. Identify any genuinely new formulations and trace their derivation from the basic motive

# Context & Application
The principle of economy is both aesthetic and practical: the exposition presents such a wealth of thematic material that introducing entirely new ideas in the elaboration would be wasteful and would undermine the motivic unity of the movement. The elaboration achieves variety not through new invention but through transformation, modulation, and recontextualization.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1-I: principal theme appears only in introductory measures and retransition; the main body uses subordinate theme and its residues
- Beethoven, Op. 10/1-I: built upon a theme not found in the exposition in that form, though related to transition and subordinate themes -- "a masterly imagination and intuition are at work here"
- Beethoven, Op. 10/2-I: chief material derives from the incidental octaves at the end of the exposition (m. 65-66) and ornamental triplet sixteenths -- formerly inconspicuous features
- Mozart, K.V. 465-I: built entirely from the initial motive-form (m. 23-24), demonstrating the fullest exploitation of a single idea through motival transformation
- Beethoven, Op. 28-I: essentially monothematic, based on the principle of the sequence plus progressive reduction

# Common Errors
- Introducing entirely new themes in the elaboration when existing material has not been exhausted
- Ignoring inconspicuous features from the exposition that could serve as elaboration material
- Using exposition material without transformation, producing mere repetition rather than elaboration

# Common Confusions
- Economy does not mean restriction; the elaboration achieves great variety through transformation, recontextualization, and modulation of existing material
- "Formerly inconspicuous" features becoming dominant is not a violation of economy; it reveals hidden potential in the exposition's material
- The rare appearance of genuinely new formulations (Op. 10/1-I) demonstrates that economy is a guiding principle, not an absolute rule
