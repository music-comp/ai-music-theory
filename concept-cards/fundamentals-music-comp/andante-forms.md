# Quick Definition
The simplest rondo-like forms (aba and abab), typically used for slow or moderate-tempo movements in which more complex structures would become excessively long, with the A-section often itself a ternary form.

# Core Definition
The andante forms (aba and abab) are formal schemes found throughout the literature in all kinds of cyclic works: sonatas, trios, quartets, and even symphonies. They are confined to movements of slow or moderate tempo, in which more complex structures would become excessively long. The A-section is frequently a ternary form in itself. In the abab pattern, the B-section recurs transposed to the tonic region.

# Key Properties
- **aba**: Three-part form; A-section often itself ternary, followed by contrasting B-section and return of A
- **abab**: Four-part form; B-section recurs transposed to the tonic region
- Confined to slow or moderate tempo movements
- A-section typically includes a small ternary structure
- B-section begins in a contrasting region (flat submediant, dominant, etc.)
- Written-out repetitions often include pianistic elaboration
- Codas and codetta-like measures may close the movement

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the A-section (often itself a small ternary form)
2. Note the contrasting B-section in a different key region
3. Determine whether the form is aba (no B return) or abab (B transposed to tonic)
4. In abab, the returning B-section is transposed to the tonic, often with modifications
5. Look for codas, which may quote leading motives from both a and b

# Context & Application
These forms serve as slow movements in sonatas, quartets, and symphonies. The slow tempo means that even a simple formal structure produces a movement of substantial length. Calling these forms "rondos" is perhaps an exaggeration; they are better understood as the simplest end of the rondo spectrum.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1-II (abab): A-section is ternary (m. 1-16); transition to subordinate theme in dominant; B transposed to tonic in recapitulation
- Beethoven, Op. 7-II (abab): dramatic episode intervenes before recapitulation with quotation in remote Bb (m. 42); B-section recapitulated with reduced length
- Beethoven, Op. 28-II (aba): both a and b are ternary forms; short coda quotes motives of both
- Beethoven, Op. 31/1-II (aba): 119 measures long despite only three sections
- Beethoven, Seventh Symphony-II (abab): extremely long due to many repetitions; fugato elaboration (m. 183) replaces expected repetitions
- Haydn, String Quartet Op. 76/2-II (aba): B-section is merely a modulatory contrast without subordinate theme

# Common Errors
- Making the A-section too simple when it needs to sustain interest across multiple returns
- Failing to transpose the B-section to tonic in the abab recapitulation
- Creating excessive length by using a complex formal structure at a slow tempo

# Common Confusions
- Calling these "rondos" is acknowledged as perhaps an exaggeration; the rondo label is tentative for the simplest aba forms
- The length of an andante form movement can be considerable despite simple formal structure, due to slow tempo and internal elaboration
- The abab form is not the same as a binary form; the second b is a transposed recapitulation, not a mere repeat
