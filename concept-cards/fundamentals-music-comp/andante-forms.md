# Quick Definition
The simplest rondo-like forms (aba and abab), typically used for slow or moderate-tempo movements, in which the A-section is often itself a ternary form and the B-section provides a contrasting key and character.

# Formal Definition
The andante forms (aba and abab) are formal schemes found throughout the literature in all kinds of cyclic works: sonatas, trios, quartets, and even symphonies. They are confined to movements of slow or moderate tempo, in which more complex structures would become excessively long. The A-section is frequently a ternary form in itself. In the abab pattern, the B-section recurs transposed to the tonic region.

# Construction/Recognition
- **aba**: Three-part form; the A-section (often itself ternary) is followed by a contrasting B-section and a return of A, possibly varied
- **abab**: Four-part form; after the aba pattern, the B-section recurs transposed to the tonic region, often with modifications
- The A-section typically includes a small ternary structure with internal contrast
- The B-section begins in a contrasting region (flat submediant, dominant, etc.)
- Codas and codetta-like measures may close the movement
- Written-out repetitions often include typically pianistic elaboration

# Musical Context
These forms serve as slow movements in sonatas, quartets, and symphonies. The slow tempo means that even a simple formal structure produces a movement of substantial length.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1-II (abab): A-section is a ternary form (m. 1-16); transition leads to subordinate theme in dominant; B-section transposed to tonic in recapitulation
- Beethoven, Op. 7-II (abab): dramatic episode intervenes before recapitulation, with quotation of main theme in remote tonality of Bb (m. 42)
- Beethoven, Op. 28-II (aba): both a and b are ternary forms; short coda quotes leading motives of both
- Beethoven, Op. 31/1-II (aba): 119 measures long despite only three sections
- Beethoven, Seventh Symphony-II (abab): extremely long due to many repetitions of the main theme; fugato elaboration (m. 183) replaces expected repetitions
- Haydn, String Quartet Op. 76/2-II (aba): B-section is merely a modulatory contrast without a subordinate theme

# Common Confusions
- Calling these "rondos" is acknowledged as perhaps an exaggeration; the rondo label is tentative for the simplest aba forms
- The length of an andante form movement can be considerable despite the simple formal structure, due to the slow tempo and internal elaboration
- The abab form is not the same as a binary form; the second b is a transposed recapitulation, not a mere repeat
