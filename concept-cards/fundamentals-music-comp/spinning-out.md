# Quick Definition
The technique of extending musical material by deriving succeeding motive-forms from preceding ones in a chain-like fashion, leading to sequences, condensation, and liquidation.

# Formal Definition
Spinning out (Fortspinnung) is the derivation of succeeding motive-forms from preceding ones, leading to sequences, condensation, and liquidation. It produces chain-like interconnexion within sections, particularly within the subordinate group of the sonata-allegro exposition. Each new fragment grows from the end of the previous one, creating continuous musical momentum.

# Construction/Recognition
- Succeeding motive-forms are derived from preceding ones (chain-like linkage)
- Sequential treatment of derived fragments
- Progressive condensation as fragments become shorter
- Eventual liquidation as distinctive features are progressively eliminated
- Contributes to the evasion of definite cadences, maintaining harmonic momentum
- Incidental modulation may derive from this technique without disturbing essential stability

# Musical Context
Spinning out is a primary technique within the subordinate group of sonata-allegro forms, where it contributes to the loose construction that distinguishes this section from the more tightly organized principal theme. It also appears in transitions and in the elaboration section.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/3-IV: a figure from the end of the main theme supplies all material for the transition through chain-like linkage, reduced to its upbeat (m. 36)
- Beethoven, Op. 7-I: from m. 82 to the end of the exposition, each cadence overlaps the beginning of the ensuing section, maintaining momentum through chain-like interconnexion
- Beethoven, Op. 10/1-I: the subordinate group derives successive ideas from preceding ones, with a series of approaches to the cadence from m. 72 to m. 94

# Common Confusions
- Spinning out is not the same as sequence; sequence is one technique used within the broader process of spinning out
- Spinning out maintains momentum and avoids cadential closure, unlike developmental variation which typically works with more clearly articulated phrases
- The chain-like quality means each new idea grows from the previous one, rather than being juxtaposed independently
