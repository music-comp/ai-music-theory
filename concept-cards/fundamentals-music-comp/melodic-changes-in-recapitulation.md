# Quick Definition
Modifications to the melody in the recapitulation, ranging from ornamental elaboration to complete reconstruction, necessitated by structural changes such as altered cadences, register shifts, or the need to avoid monotony.

# Formal Definition
The contour of the melody should not be entirely changed, except at points where essential structural changes occur. Ornamental variation -- breaking down longer notes through the use of passing notes, circumscribing notes, tone repetitions -- need not change the contour essentially. Excessive rhythmic changes or shifts of accent are likely to prevent recognition. A changed cadence may require considerable reformulation, or even complete reconstruction.

# Construction/Recognition
Melodic changes in recapitulation include: (1) ornamental elaboration that preserves contour, (2) register transfer (moving melody an octave higher for climactic effect), (3) ascending reformulation of previously descending passages, (4) free reconstruction to accommodate a different cadential goal, (5) reassignment of the melody to a different voice. Changes should preserve enough of the original contour to be recognizable.

# Musical Context
Melodic changes serve both structural and expressive purposes. Register changes can create climactic emphasis. Free reconstruction allows the cadence to redirect to the tonic. Voice transfer creates textural variety. All changes must balance the need for recognition against the need for freshness.

# Examples
- Beethoven Op. 2/1-II: only m. 13 preserved from the original, transposed an octave higher; m. 15 is a figuration of a descending contour resembling m. 3 and 7
- Beethoven Op. 2/2-II: m. 17 is an ascending reformulation of m. 6, causing the repetition in m. 18-19 to occur climactically an octave higher
- Beethoven Op. 31/1-III: main melody transferred to the tenor voice, with new quasi-melodic accompaniment in the right hand
- Haydn Ex. 102c: recapitulation avoids monotony by reorganization and by omitting the motive-form "a," using chiefly motive-form "b" instead

# Common Confusions
- Contour preservation is the primary goal; ornamental changes that maintain contour are safe, while rhythmic changes that alter the perceived pattern are risky
- Complete reconstruction is acceptable at cadential points where the harmonic destination has changed
- Transferring the melody to another voice is a form of variation, not a departure from the recapitulation
