# Quick Definition

The bass should be treated like a secondary melody, remaining within a single register and possessing melodic continuity, since the ear is trained to pay close attention to the bass voice.

# Core Definition

Except in the case of a pedal point, the bass should participate in every change of the harmony. The bass should be treated like a secondary melody, which means that it should remain (except for special purposes) within a single register and possess a certain degree of continuity. The ear is trained to pay much attention to the bass. Even a short note is understood as a continuing bass until another bass note can be heard as a melodic continuation. For the sake of fluency, a bass which is not countermelodic should make free use of inversions, even where they are not harmonically necessary (Schoenberg, p. 55).

# Key Properties

1. Bass participates in every harmonic change (except over pedal point)
2. Should remain within a single register (except for deliberate special effects)
3. Should possess melodic continuity -- smooth connections preferred
4. Even short bass notes are perceived as continuing until replaced
5. Free use of inversions for fluency, even when root position suffices
6. A moving, melodic bass line is always a greater merit than a static one

# Construction / Recognition

1. Ensure the bass participates in every harmonic change
2. Maintain register consistency (single register unless deliberate contrast)
3. Prefer stepwise motion and smooth connections
4. Use inversions freely for bass fluency, even when not harmonically necessary
5. Remember that even a short bass note is perceived as the continuing bass until a new one sounds

# Context & Application

The bass voice occupies a privileged position in the listener's perception. Schoenberg treats it not as a mere harmonic foundation but as a voice with its own melodic requirements. This principle connects to the broader understanding of voice leading as a melodic activity. The bass's melodic quality distinguishes sophisticated writing from primitive harmonization.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating the bass as a static harmonic foundation
  **Correction**: The bass should be treated as a secondary melody with register continuity and smooth connections

- **Error**: Using only root position when inversions would improve bass fluency
  **Correction**: Use inversions freely for melodic bass movement, even when not harmonically required

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking "secondary melody" means the bass should compete with the main melody
  **Clarification**: It means the bass should have its own melodic logic and continuity, not that it should be equally prominent

- **Confusion**: Equating inversions for bass fluency with inversions for harmonic effect
  **Clarification**: Here the purpose is melodic smoothness, not harmonic reinterpretation

- **Confusion**: Thinking a short bass note is insignificant
  **Clarification**: Even a brief bass note is perceived as continuing until another bass note replaces it
