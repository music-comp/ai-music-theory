# Quick Definition
The bass should be treated like a secondary melody, remaining within a single register and possessing a certain degree of continuity, since the ear is trained to pay close attention to the bass voice.

# Formal Definition
Except in the case of a pedal point, the bass should participate in every change of the harmony. Schoenberg insists that the bass should be treated like a secondary melody, meaning it should remain (except for special purposes) within a single register and possess a certain degree of continuity. The ear is trained to pay much attention to the bass; even a short note is understood as a continuing bass until another bass note can be heard as a melodic continuation. For fluency, a non-countermelodic bass should make free use of inversions, even where they are not harmonically necessary.

# Construction/Recognition
- Bass participates in every harmonic change (except over pedal point)
- Maintains a single register (except for deliberate special effects)
- Possesses melodic continuity -- stepwise motion and smooth connections preferred
- Free use of inversions for fluency, even when root position is harmonically adequate
- Even short bass notes are perceived as continuing until a new bass note supersedes
- A melodic, moving bass line is always a greater merit than a static one

# Musical Context
The bass voice occupies a privileged position in the listener's perception. Schoenberg treats it not as a mere harmonic foundation but as a voice with its own melodic requirements. This principle connects to the broader understanding of voice leading as a melodic activity and explains why good bass lines contribute so significantly to the quality of a composition. The bass's melodic quality distinguishes sophisticated writing from primitive harmonization.

# Common Confusions
- "Secondary melody" does not mean the bass should compete with the primary melody; it means the bass should have its own melodic logic and continuity.
- Using inversions for bass fluency is not the same as using inversions for harmonic effect; the purpose here is melodic smoothness.
- A bass note heard briefly is still perceived as "the bass" until replaced; this perceptual fact has compositional consequences.
