# Quick Definition
The minimum required change in the sonata-allegro recapitulation: transposing the subordinate group to the tonic region -- with varying degrees of additional modification from simple transposition with minor changes to extensive reconstruction, particularly in minor-key movements.

# Core Definition
As in the larger rondos, the minimum change in the recapitulation is the transposition of the subordinate group to the tonic region. In simpler cases, the material is merely transposed with slight changes and adaptations of register. But a composer's artistry usually demands more, and the subordinate group may undergo omissions, additions, extensions, harmonic changes, register shifts, contrapuntal treatment, or reconstruction. The repetition must be recognizable as such, especially where themes enter.

# Key Properties
- Minimum change: transposition to the tonic region
- Simple cases: mere transposition with minor register adaptations
- Complex cases: omissions, additions, extensions, harmonic changes, reconstruction
- Recognizability must be preserved, especially at theme entries
- Minor key creates special complications (see minor-key-recapitulation-complications)
- The "adventures" of the elaboration and functional changes of form context require modification
- Codettas may be enriched and extended to introduce a coda

# Construction / Recognition
1. Verify the subordinate group appears in the tonic region
2. Compare with the exposition to identify changes beyond transposition
3. Note omissions, additions, or reconstruction
4. Check that theme entries are recognizable despite variation
5. Note the handling of codettas and closing material

# Context & Application
The transposition of the subordinate group to the tonic is the fundamental formal obligation of the recapitulation. Beyond this minimum, the degree of modification depends on the composer's artistic judgment and the demands of the individual work. Minor-key movements present special challenges because a major-mode subordinate theme may not work well in tonic minor.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/2-I: remainder of recapitulation is mere transposition to tonic key with slight register adaptations; no coda
- Beethoven, Op. 7-I: subordinate group recurs in principal key (m. 221) with only superficial changes; closing segment interrupted (m. 313) for coda
- Beethoven, Op. 2/3-I: subordinate group repeated without significant change up to m. 218; sudden shift through deceptive progression to Ab inaugurates coda; closing segment postponed to end of coda
- Beethoven, Op. 10/2-I: ambiguous section now transposed to F like any subordinate theme; ten-measure modulatory segment inserted (m. 153-62)

# Common Errors
- Failing to transpose the subordinate group to the tonic region
- Transposing too literally without artistic adaptation
- Varying the beginning of the subordinate theme too much, preventing recognition

# Common Confusions
- "Transposition to the tonic" does not necessarily mean literal transposition of every note; adjustment of register, harmonic detail, and structure is normal
- In some cases, ambiguous passages from the exposition are clarified by their recapitulation treatment (Op. 10/2-I)
- The closing segment or coda material may be displaced, not eliminated
