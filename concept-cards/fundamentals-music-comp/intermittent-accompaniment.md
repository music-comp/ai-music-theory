# Quick Definition

An accompaniment type where the harmony appears only once per measure or once per several measures, either sustained or as a short chord, providing maximum transparency.

# Core Definition

The harmony may appear only once in a measure, or once in several measures. It may be sustained, or a short chord may be stated at the beginning of a measure, or a short chord may occur during a rest or a sustained note of the melody (Schoenberg, p. 53). Intermittent accompaniment provides the sparsest harmonic support, maximizing transparency while still clarifying the tonal context.

# Prerequisites

- **Accompaniment function** -- Intermittent accompaniment serves the accompaniment function in its most economical form

# Key Properties

1. Harmony appears only once per measure or less frequently
2. Three subtypes: sustained, short chord at measure beginning, short chord during melody rest
3. Provides maximum textural transparency
4. Effective even with minimal harmonic support
5. Related to the principle that omission of accompaniment can serve contrast and character

# Construction / Recognition

1. Place harmonic support at only one point per measure (or less frequently)
2. Choose between sustained harmony, short chord at the downbeat, or short chord during a melody rest
3. Ensure the melody is harmonically self-sufficient enough for sparse accompaniment
4. Use transparency as an expressive and structural device

# Context & Application

Intermittent accompaniment occupies the middle ground between full accompaniment and complete omission. It provides just enough harmonic clarification while preserving maximum transparency. The technique is particularly effective in passages where the melody should be heard with minimal textural support.

# Examples

- **Sustained** (p. 53): Beethoven, Op. 2/3-I, m. 97-108; Ex. 58h

- **Short chord at beginning of measure** (p. 53): Op. 10/1-I, m. 1-3; Op. 10/2-I, m. 1-4; Exs. 58c, f

- **Short chord during rest or sustained note** (p. 53): Op. 2/2-III, m. 1-2, 5-6; Ex. 46d

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Using intermittent accompaniment for harmonically ambiguous passages
  **Correction**: Sparse accompaniment works only when the melody is harmonically self-sufficient

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking intermittent means weak or ineffective
  **Clarification**: Even sparse harmonization can be highly effective; transparency is a merit in itself
