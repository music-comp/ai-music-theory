# Quick Definition
The three-dimensional nature of themes, incorporating interval, harmony, and rhythmic development, with structural features such as juxtaposition of remotely coherent segments, unstable harmony, and direct leaps to remote motivic developments.

# Formal Definition
The importance of rhythmic development makes the problem of the theme three-dimensional, in contrast to the two-dimensional melody (comprising chiefly interval and latent harmony). In themes, remotely coherent segments are often juxtaposed in a co-ordinate meaning, without connectives. Seldom is a theme extended by spinning a continuation for mere formal balance; rather, it leaps directly to remote developments of the basic motive. The harmony of a theme is often active, "roving," unstable, though even complex themes still move around a tonic or a definite contrasting region.

# Construction/Recognition
Identify thematic complexity by: (1) presence of rhythmic development alongside intervallic and harmonic development, (2) juxtaposition of segments without smooth connectives, (3) remote motivic developments rather than simple continuations, (4) roving or unstable harmony, (5) separation of segments that offers openings for bridges or continuations, (6) the sense that development must follow.

# Musical Context
Thematic complexity is essential in forms requiring development, such as sonata form. The theme's complexity generates the material and the necessity for elaboration in subsequent sections. Despite complexity, the organization cannot be so loose that one feels a lack of structure.

# Examples
- Beethoven Op. 10/1-I: the theme leaps directly to remote developments of the basic motive at m. 9 and m. 17 (Ex. 95c)
- Beethoven Op. 90-I, Op. 14/2-I: remotely coherent segments juxtaposed without connectives
- Beethoven Op. 7-I, Op. 31/2-I: themes that leap to remote motivic developments
- Brahms First Symphony-I, Piano Quartet Op. 60-I: complex themes with roving harmony that still move around a tonic

# Common Confusions
- Thematic complexity is not the same as incoherence; even the most complex themes maintain organizational structure
- Complexity does not mean length; a brief passage can be thematically complex if it incorporates all three dimensions densely
