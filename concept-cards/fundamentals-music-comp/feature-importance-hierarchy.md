# Quick Definition
The evaluative ordering of musical features (repetitions, climax placement, melodic compass, phrase direction) that guides the composer in avoiding monotony while maintaining coherent development.

# Core Definition
Too many repetitions of tones or melodic figures are annoying, if they do not exploit the advantage of a repetition -- emphasis. Watch especially the highest tone of the melody. The climax normally appears toward the end, and can be repeated or exceeded only with caution. Beware of too restricted a compass, and avoid moving too long in one direction. Evaluate sensitively the endings of the phrases. These guidelines establish a hierarchy of concerns for self-critical evaluation.

# Prerequisites
- self-criticism: Feature hierarchy is part of the self-criticism framework
- analysis-of-significant-features: Knowing which features matter before prioritizing them

# Key Properties
- Repetitions should serve emphasis; excessive repetitions are annoying
- The climax normally appears toward the end
- Repeating or exceeding the climax requires caution
- Too restricted a compass risks monotony
- Moving too long in one direction should be avoided
- Phrase endings require sensitive evaluation
- These are practical checks, not abstract theory

# Construction / Recognition
Evaluate a composition by checking: (1) whether repetitions serve emphasis or merely fill space, (2) whether the climax is appropriately placed (normally toward the end), (3) whether the melodic compass is too restricted, (4) whether the melody moves too long in one direction, (5) whether phrase endings are satisfying.

# Context & Application
This advice is intensely practical. It gives the student concrete criteria for self-evaluation. The hierarchy reflects Schoenberg's view that monotony is a greater threat to the beginner than complexity. These checks should be applied systematically to every composition exercise.

# Examples
- A melody with too many repeated notes on the same pitch creates monotony rather than emphasis
- A climax that occurs too early leaves the remainder of the melody anticlimactic
- A melody confined to a narrow compass for too long becomes tiresome

# Common Errors
- Repeating the climax point when the passage lacks the sophistication to support it
- Moving in one direction without compensating motion
- Using too restricted a compass throughout an entire section

# Common Confusions
- The climax "normally" appearing toward the end does not mean it must always be at the end; it means the student should not attempt to repeat or exceed it without caution
- Many good melodies do repeat their climax point, but the student had better not attempt it yet
