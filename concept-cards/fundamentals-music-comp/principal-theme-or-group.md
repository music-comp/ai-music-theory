# Quick Definition
The opening thematic section of the sonata-allegro exposition, ranging from a simple period or sentence to a complex group of themes, characterized by thematic (rather than melodic) ideas and flexible, irregular internal organization.

# Formal Definition
The structure may vary widely -- from a straightforward period or sentence, through something resembling a ternary form, to a group of distinct themes joined in the most subtle fashion. The ideas of the principal group are nearly always more thematic than melodic, and the internal organization tends to be flexible and irregular, forecasting the metamorphoses to which the ideas will later be subjected. If the structure is too symmetrical, subsequent freedom of articulation may be impeded.

# Construction/Recognition
Key characteristics:
- **More thematic than melodic**: the ideas are designed for development rather than lyric expression
- **Flexible and irregular internal organization**: forecasting later metamorphoses
- A **striking, motto-like beginning** is a useful feature
- Great variety in length: from 8 measures (Op. 2/1-I) to 39 measures (Op. 28-I)
- Free articulation of phrases of varying lengths
- Irregularity in the length of segments and number of distinct motive formulations
- Various connective possibilities with what follows: from definite isolation by strong cadences to complete merger without any break

# Musical Context
The principal theme establishes the tonic key and presents the basic motivic material from which much of the movement will be derived. Its thematic rather than melodic character ensures that the material is suitable for the developmental procedures of the elaboration.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1-I: simple eight-measure sentence
- Beethoven, Op. 2/2-I: two distinct ideas (motto-like broken chords and lyrical continuation, m. 9); both repeated in shortened form; free articulation places chief cadence in m. 20
- Beethoven, Op. 7-I: motto-like beginning (m. 1-4), series of two-measure phrases extending to m. 13, overlapping varied repetition, further extension through passage work
- Beethoven, Op. 14/1-I: though short (12 measures), a prototype of the "group" -- four distinct motive formulations in succession
- Beethoven, Op. 28-I: 39-measure theme above a tonic pedal point; ten-measure theme immediately repeated an octave higher with subsequent period-like continuation

# Common Confusions
- "Group" means the principal section may contain multiple distinct thematic ideas, not just a single theme
- The principal group should not be too symmetrical; excessive regularity impedes the freedom needed for later development
- The connective to the transition may be seamless (merger) or clearly articulated (strong cadence followed by codettas); neither approach is inherently preferable
