# Quick Definition

The continuation of a sentence employs two complementary techniques: development (growth, augmentation, extension, expansion) and liquidation (reduction, condensation, intensification leading to closure), which together shape the second half of the sentence.

# Core Definition

The technique applied in the continuation of a sentence is a kind of development, comparable in some respects to the condensing technique of liquidation. Development implies not only growth, augmentation, extension, and expansion, but also reduction, condensation, and intensification. The purpose of liquidation is to counteract the tendency toward unlimited extension (Schoenberg, p. 39).

The end of a sentence calls for the same treatment as the consequent of a period. A sentence may close on I, V, or III, with a suitable cadence: full, half, Phrygian, plagal; perfect or imperfect; according to its function (p. 39).

# Prerequisites

- **Beginning the sentence** -- The sentence's beginning (tonic form/dominant form) establishes the material that development and liquidation transform
- **Motive** -- Development and liquidation operate on motive-forms
- **Connecting motive-forms** -- Understanding motivic variation is essential for development

# Key Properties

1. Development: growth, augmentation, extension, expansion of motival material
2. Liquidation: reduction, condensation, intensification toward closure
3. The two processes are complementary, not opposing
4. Liquidation counteracts the tendency toward unlimited extension
5. Sentence continuation is generally supported by phrase shortening
6. The end requires cadential treatment similar to the consequent of a period
7. Reduction: omitting part of the model; Condensation: compressing content, possibly reordering features

# Construction / Recognition

## To Apply Development and Liquidation

1. After the twofold statement (m. 1-4), begin development through more remote motive-form variation
2. Use sequence-like procedures to extend and transform preceding material
3. Progressively shorten phrase units (two-measure to one-measure to half-measure)
4. Eliminate characteristic features, leaving only uncharacteristic residues
5. Support the process with a cadence or half cadence
6. Choose appropriate cadential goal (I, V, or III) and cadence type

## To Recognize Development and Liquidation

1. Identify the developmental passage after the twofold statement
2. Note progressive shortening of phrase units
3. Identify the point where characteristic features begin to disappear
4. Confirm cadential arrival with appropriate harmonic closure

# Context & Application

This dual process is the driving force of the sentence's continuation and distinguishes it from the consequent of a period. The sentence's beginning already includes repetition (tonic form/dominant form), so the continuation must go further -- into genuine development -- before finding closure through liquidation. In masterpieces, this has given rise to great structural variety. Homophonic music can be called the style of "developing variation," and the liquidation process brings developing variation to its purposeful conclusion.

# Examples

- **Exs. 52a, b** (p. 39): Two-measure phrases reduced to one measure in m. 5-6; Ex. 52b further reduces to half-measure units

- **Exs. 54, 55, 56** (p. 40): Based on broken-chord forms, demonstrating how progressive variation reaches motive-forms suitable for sentences of sharply contrasted character

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating development and liquidation as separate, sequential phases
  **Correction**: They are complementary aspects of the same continuation process; they may overlap

- **Error**: Continuing development without liquidation, producing an endlessly extending sentence
  **Correction**: Liquidation counteracts the tendency toward unlimited extension

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Equating "development" here with the "development section" of sonata form
  **Clarification**: Development in this context refers to the technique within a sentence continuation, though the principles are related

- **Confusion**: Thinking liquidation is the absence of development
  **Clarification**: Liquidation is a specific technique that brings development to a purposeful conclusion, not merely the end of developmental activity

- **Confusion**: Confusing reduction and condensation
  **Clarification**: Reduction merely omits part of the model; condensation compresses content, potentially reordering features
