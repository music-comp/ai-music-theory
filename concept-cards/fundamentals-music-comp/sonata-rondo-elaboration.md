# Quick Definition
The modulatory middle section in a sonata-rondo, which elaborates previously exposed thematic elements through sequential treatment, key changes, and contrapuntal combinations, functioning like the Durchfuhrung of a sonata-allegro but typically more compact and sometimes retaining trio-like characteristics.

# Core Definition
The sonata-rondo's c-section elaborates previous thematic elements in a modulatory manner resembling the middle section of the sonata-allegro form. This section resembles the modulatory middle section of the scherzo but is usually more elaborately organized. It calls for treatment like the Durchfuhrung, using motive-forms from the main theme or other exposed material in a modulatory process through a planned scheme of contrasting keys. The combination of trio and Durchfuhrung characteristics may create deliberate formal ambiguity.

# Prerequisites
- Sonata-rondo form
- Elaboration (Durchfuhrung) technique
- Modulatory contrast in elaboration

# Key Properties
- Typically begins with material from the principal theme, often in tonic minor
- Proceeds through a planned modulatory scheme
- Uses motive-forms from previously exposed themes
- May include contrapuntal combinations of existing material
- Typically closes with a pedal point on V preparing the recapitulation
- May combine characteristics of both trio and Durchfuhrung
- Incipient Durchfuhrung may emerge from within what begins as a trio-like section
- More compact than the typical sonata-allegro elaboration

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the beginning (often main theme in tonic minor)
2. Map the modulatory scheme (e.g., tonic minor, subdominant minor, flat submediant)
3. Note whether the section begins trio-like and transforms into developmental treatment
4. Look for motive-forms from previously exposed themes
5. Identify contrapuntal combinations
6. Find the pedal point on V preparing the recapitulation

# Context & Application
This section represents the developmental core of the sonata-rondo, providing the modulatory contrast that balances the stable tonic regions of the exposition and recapitulation. It is more elaborate than the modulatory contrast in a simple scherzo but typically more compact than a full sonata-allegro Durchfuhrung.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 31/1-III: C-section begins (m. 82) with main theme in left hand in tonic minor; modulatory scheme: g (m. 83), c (m. 91), Eb (m. 98), c (m. 106), unstable (m. 114), g (m. 121); closes with pedal point on V (m. 129)
- Beethoven, Op. 22-IV: C-section (m. 72-111) begins like an etude-like trio in f (minor v); gives way (m. 81) to a modulatory-contrapuntal group derived from transitional material; incipient Durchfuhrung followed by return of etude-like segment in bb (tonic minor), continuing the modulatory process instead of returning to the original key

# Common Errors
- Making the elaboration too long relative to the rest of the rondo movement
- Failing to plan a systematic modulatory scheme
- Forcing a clear division between trio and Durchfuhrung when ambiguity is the intended quality

# Common Confusions
- The sonata-rondo elaboration is not identical to a full sonata-allegro Durchfuhrung; it is usually more compact and may retain trio-like characteristics
- The combination of trio and elaboration characteristics is a feature, not a defect; Schoenberg notes that "ambiguity is sometimes a quality to be recognized, not necessarily to be explained away"
- The modulatory scheme is planned and systematic, not random; it typically follows recognizable key relationships
