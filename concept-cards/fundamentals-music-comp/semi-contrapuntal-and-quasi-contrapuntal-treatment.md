# Quick Definition

Semi-counterpoint involves free melodic movement of accompanying voices with motival and thematic implications, while quasi-counterpoint merely embellishes, melodizes, and vitalizes otherwise unimportant harmonic voices without genuine thematic significance.

# Core Definition

Semi-counterpoint has motival and even thematic implications but is not based on combinations such as multiple counterpoint, canonic imitations, etc. -- only on a free melodic movement of one or more voices. Quasi-counterpoint is often little more than a way of embellishing, melodizing, and vitalizing otherwise unimportant voices in the harmony (Schoenberg, p. 54).

In real homophonic music there is always one main voice; the addition of imitations, canonic or free, is principally a method of accompanying this main voice. The addition of a countermelody is a semi-contrapuntal device. Countermelodic phrases or fragments are often used to embellish a repetition or as bridge-like fillers between phrases of the principal voice. Ostinatos may be understood similarly, as adding voices to a "cantus firmus" (p. 55).

# Key Properties

1. Semi-counterpoint: free melodic movement with motival/thematic implications
2. Quasi-counterpoint: embellishing, melodizing unimportant voices (no thematic significance)
3. Neither uses formal contrapuntal devices (multiple counterpoint, canon, etc.)
4. In homophonic music, there is always one main voice
5. Countermelodies are semi-contrapuntal devices
6. Countermelodic phrases may embellish repetitions or serve as bridge-like fillers
7. Ostinatos may be understood as semi-contrapuntal (adding voices to a cantus firmus)

# Construction / Recognition

## Semi-Contrapuntal

1. Create free melodic movement in accompanying voices with motivic connections
2. Add countermelodies for repetitions or as bridge-like fillers
3. Use rhythmic figures that may derive from free imitation
4. Maintain the principal voice's primacy

## Quasi-Contrapuntal

1. Embellish sustained tones in the harmony with melodic movement
2. May imitate phrasing (not thematic content) of the melody
3. Add movement where harmony alone would be static
4. Keep embellishment subordinate to the main voice

## Distinguishing the Two

- If the accompanying movement has motivic weight: semi-contrapuntal
- If the accompanying movement is purely ornamental: quasi-contrapuntal

# Context & Application

Both techniques occupy the middle ground between pure homophony and real counterpoint. They vitalize the texture without requiring the formal rigor of fugal or canonic writing. Beethoven, Op. 10/1-I provides a clear illustration: in m. 48-55, both upper voices are so well organized that it is difficult to determine which is main voice and which is countermelody (semi-contrapuntal); in m. 56-85, the bass moves more melodically than harmony requires without becoming a true countermelody (quasi-contrapuntal).

# Examples

- **Semi-contrapuntal** (p. 54): Beethoven, Op. 2/2-I, m. 11-16 and imitations in m. 32 ff.; Op. 2/3-IV, m. 57 ff.

- **Quasi-contrapuntal** (p. 55): Beethoven, Op. 14/2-I, m. 49 ff. (lowest voice imitates only the phrasing); Op. 31/1-II, m. 16 ff. (auxiliaries disguised as imitations); Op. 28-IV, m. 28-31, 33-35 (definitely not contrapuntal since they present only one harmony)

- **Semi-contrapuntal countermelody** (p. 55): Op. 26-II, m. 45 ff.; Op. 28-I, m. 183 ff.; Op. 31/1-III, m. 17 ff.

- **Bridge-like fillers** (p. 55): Op. 13-II, m. 37 ff.

- **Embellishing repetition** (p. 55): Op. 10/3-II, compare m. 17-19 with 21-23

- **Beethoven, Op. 10/1-I** (pp. 54-55): M. 48-55 semi-contrapuntal; m. 56-85 quasi-contrapuntal

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Confusing semi-contrapuntal writing with real counterpoint
  **Correction**: Semi-counterpoint lacks formal contrapuntal devices (multiple counterpoint, canon, etc.)

- **Error**: Making quasi-contrapuntal embellishments too prominent
  **Correction**: Quasi-contrapuntal movement should serve the harmony, not compete with the melody

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Equating semi-counterpoint and quasi-counterpoint
  **Clarification**: Semi has motivic/thematic implications; quasi is purely ornamental

- **Confusion**: Thinking all melodic bass movement is contrapuntal
  **Clarification**: It may be quasi-contrapuntal (purely embellishing) without genuine thematic independence

- **Confusion**: Believing imitations in quasi-counterpoint are genuine contrapuntal devices
  **Clarification**: They may look like counterpoint but serve an entirely different (decorative) purpose
