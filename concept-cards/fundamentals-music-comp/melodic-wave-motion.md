# Quick Definition
Melodies characteristically proceed in waves -- moving upward, then downward, seldom continuing long in one direction -- with varying amplitudes, as demonstrated by tonal contour graphs of melodies by Bach, Haydn, Mozart, and Beethoven.

# Core Definition
Melodies proceed in waves, a fact readily observed in tonal contour analysis. The amplitude of these waves varies. A melody seldom moves long in one direction. Though the student was advised to avoid repeating the culmination point (the climax), contour graphs show that many good melodies do repeat it. However, Schoenberg advises the student not to attempt this.

# Key Properties
- Melodies proceed in waves (observable in tonal contour graphs)
- Amplitude of waves varies
- A melody seldom moves long in one direction
- The climax normally appears toward the end
- Many good melodies repeat the climax, but the student should not attempt it
- Wave-like motion connects to the broader ideal of melodic balance and self-containment

# Construction / Recognition
Examine wave motion by: (1) graphing the tonal contour, (2) checking for alternation of ascending and descending motion, (3) identifying intermediate high points before the climax, (4) assessing whether the melody moves too long in one direction, (5) locating the climax placement.

# Context & Application
Contour analysis of melodies across styles and periods consistently reveals wave-like motion. This principle connects to the broader ideal of balance in melody: a line that rises must eventually fall, creating the self-regulating quality that makes melody self-contained.

- Contours from Mozart compositions (Ex. 99): String Quartet, Symphony, Opera, Piano Sonata

# Common Errors
- Moving too long in one direction without compensating motion
- Placing the climax too early, leaving the remainder anticlimactic
- Repeating the climax without sufficient craft to support it

# Common Confusions
- Wave motion does not mean mechanical alternation; the waves are irregular and varied in amplitude
- Repeating the climax point is observed in good melodies but remains risky for students
