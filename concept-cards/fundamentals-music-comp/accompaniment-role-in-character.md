# Quick Definition
The accompaniment plays an essential role in establishing character; no performer can express a given character if the accompaniment contradicts it.

# Formal Definition
The type of accompaniment plays an important role in the establishment of character. The relationship between melody and accompaniment must be congruent with the intended character. A mismatch between the two renders the intended character unplayable regardless of the performer's skill or intention.

# Construction/Recognition
When composing, ensure the accompaniment pattern, rhythm, texture, and style support the intended character of the melody. When analyzing, examine how the accompaniment reinforces or creates the expressive quality of a passage. Old dance forms were characterized by specific rhythmic patterns in the accompaniment, which were reflected in the melody.

# Musical Context
The accompaniment is a primary vehicle for character, not merely a harmonic support. Dance forms historically depended on accompaniment rhythms to define their character -- the mazurka, gavotte, and polka are distinguished principally through their accompaniment patterns.

# Examples
- No player could express the character of a march if the accompaniment were that of a chorale
- No one could play a restful adagio melody if the accompaniment were like a torrent
- A mazurka is distinguished from a gavotte or a polka principally by its rhythmic accompaniment characteristics

# Common Confusions
- The accompaniment is not merely a harmonic backdrop; it is an active determinant of character
- A melody extracted from its accompaniment may suggest a different character than the complete texture
