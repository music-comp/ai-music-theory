# Quick Definition
The accompaniment plays an essential role in establishing character; no performer can express a given character if the accompaniment contradicts it.

# Core Definition
The type of accompaniment plays an important role in the establishment of character. The relationship between melody and accompaniment must be congruent with the intended character. A mismatch between the two renders the intended character unplayable regardless of the performer's skill or intention. Old dance forms were characterized by specific rhythmic patterns in the accompaniment, which were also reflected in the melody.

# Prerequisites
- character-and-mood: Understanding the composite nature of character before considering the accompaniment's specific role within it

# Key Properties
- The accompaniment is an active determinant of character, not merely harmonic support
- Melody-accompaniment congruence is essential for character expression
- A mismatch between melody and accompaniment makes the intended character impossible to convey
- Dance forms historically depended on accompaniment rhythms to define their character

# Construction / Recognition
When composing, ensure the accompaniment pattern, rhythm, texture, and style support the intended character of the melody. When analyzing, examine how the accompaniment reinforces or creates the expressive quality of a passage.

# Context & Application
The accompaniment is a primary vehicle for character. Dance forms historically depended on accompaniment rhythms to define their character -- the mazurka, gavotte, and polka are distinguished principally through their accompaniment patterns.

# Examples
- No player could express the character of a march if the accompaniment were that of a chorale
- No one could play a restful adagio melody if the accompaniment were like a torrent
- A mazurka is distinguished from a gavotte or a polka principally by its rhythmic accompaniment characteristics

# Common Errors
- Treating the accompaniment as merely a harmonic backdrop
- Choosing an accompaniment pattern without considering its character implications
- Extracting a melody from its accompaniment and assuming it retains the same character

# Common Confusions
- The accompaniment is not merely a harmonic backdrop; it is an active determinant of character
- A melody extracted from its accompaniment may suggest a different character than the complete texture
