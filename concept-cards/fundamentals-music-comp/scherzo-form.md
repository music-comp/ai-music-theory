# Quick Definition
A ternary instrumental form characterized by rapid tempo and rhythmic accentuation, typically in 3/4 metre, that replaced the minuet in sonatas and symphonies from Beethoven onwards, with a more modulatory and thematic middle section than the minuet.

# Formal Definition
The scherzo is distinctly an instrumental piece, characterized by rhythmical accentuations and rapid tempo. The tempo prevents frequent change of harmonies and remote variation of motive-forms. With regard to structure, scherzos of the masters have only one thing in common: they are ternary forms. They differ from smaller ternary forms and the minuet in that the middle section is more modulatory and more thematic. In some cases, there is a special type of modulatory contrasting middle section which approaches the elaboration (Durchfuhrung) of the sonata allegro.

# Construction/Recognition
- Ternary form: A-section, modulatory contrasting middle section (B), recapitulation (A')
- Rapid tempo: average around dotted quarter-note = 100 for Beethoven
- Metre: often 3/4, but also 3/8, 6/8, 6/4, 2/2, 4/4, 2/4, etc.
- Rhythmic accentuation more prominent than in the minuet
- Harmony changes less frequently than in the minuet due to faster tempo
- Middle section is more modulatory and thematic than the minuet's B-section
- Often followed by a trio, after which the scherzo is repeated

# Musical Context
The term "scherzo" literally means "joke" or "play," and Webster defines it as a "playful, humorous movement." However, this definition is only partly correct. The range of character is far broader, encompassing vivacious, sparkling, brilliant, witty, enthusiastic, ecstatic, ardent, fiery, energetic, vehement, impassioned, dramatic, tragic, heroic, gigantesque, diabolical, and grotesque qualities.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/2, Op. 2/3, Op. 28, Symphony No. 2: Begin with periods
- Beethoven, Violin Sonata Op. 30/2, String Quartet Op. 18/1, Eroica Symphony: Begin with sentences
- Beethoven, Septet Op. 20: Contains both a Minuet and a Scherzo in the same work
- Beethoven's symphonies, sonatas, and string quartets labelled Allegro, Vivace, Presto: Scherzo-like movements without the title

# Common Confusions
- The scherzo is not necessarily playful or humorous -- it encompasses a wide range of characters
- Not all rapid middle movements are titled "Scherzo" -- Beethoven often used Allegro, Vivace, or Presto
- The scherzo is not restricted to 3/4 metre or to major mode
- The difference between scherzo and minuet is not always clear-cut
