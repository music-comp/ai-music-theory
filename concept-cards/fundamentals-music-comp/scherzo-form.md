# Quick Definition

A ternary instrumental form characterized by rapid tempo and rhythmic accentuation, that replaced the minuet in sonatas and symphonies from Beethoven onwards, with a more modulatory and thematic middle section than the minuet.

# Core Definition

The scherzo is distinctly an instrumental piece, characterized by rhythmical accentuations and rapid tempo. The tempo prevents frequent change of harmonies and remote variation of motive-forms. With regard to structure, scherzos of the masters have only one thing in common: they are ternary forms. They differ from smaller ternary forms and the minuet in that the middle section is more modulatory and more thematic. In some cases, there is a special type of modulatory contrasting middle section which approaches the elaboration (Durchfuhrung) of the sonata allegro. Schoenberg preferred the term "elaboration" to "development" because the material is adapted, varied, expanded, condensed, and recombined -- but seldom grows or evolves into a "more mature or advanced state."

# Prerequisites

- **Minuet form**: The simpler ternary dance form from which the scherzo evolved
- **Small ternary form**: The underlying formal model

# Key Properties

- Ternary form: A-section, modulatory contrasting middle section (B), recapitulation (A')
- Rapid tempo: average around dotted quarter-note = 100 for Beethoven
- Metre: often 3/4, but also 3/8, 6/8, 6/4, 2/2, 4/4, 2/4, etc.
- Rhythmic accentuation more prominent than in the minuet
- Harmony changes less frequently than in the minuet due to faster tempo
- Middle section is more modulatory and thematic than the minuet's B-section
- Often followed by a trio, after which the scherzo is repeated
- May be enlarged to rondo-like dimensions (two trio insertions)

# Construction / Recognition

1. Identify the ternary A-B-A' structure
2. Verify rapid tempo and prominent rhythmic accentuation
3. Check that the B-section is modulatory (not merely a simple contrast)
4. Note that harmony changes less frequently than in a minuet (consequence of faster tempo)
5. Look for the trio that typically follows
6. Check for rondo-like expansion (Scherzo-Trio-Scherzo-Trio-Scherzo)

# Context & Application

The term "scherzo" means "joke" or "play," but this etymological meaning is misleading. Beethoven seldom uses the title "Scherzo" for movements in minor. Among Brahms's eight chamber music scherzos, five are in minor. The character ranges far beyond playful: vivacious, sparkling, brilliant, witty, enthusiastic, ecstatic, ardent, fiery, energetic, vehement, impassioned, dramatic, tragic, heroic, gigantesque, diabolical, grotesque.

# Examples

- Beethoven, Op. 2/2, Op. 2/3, Op. 28, Symphony No. 2: A-sections begin with periods
- Beethoven, Violin Sonata Op. 30/2, String Quartet Op. 18/1, Eroica Symphony: A-sections begin with sentences
- Beethoven, Septet Op. 20: Contains both a Minuet and a Scherzo in the same work
- Beethoven, Op. 74, Op. 95, Symphonies 4, 7: Trio interpolated twice (rondo-like structure)

## Related
- Modulatory contrasting middle section in scherzo (the distinguishing feature)
- A-section theme in scherzo (thematic construction under tempo constraints)
- Trio in scherzo (the contrasting middle section at movement level)

## Contrasts With
- Minuet form: slower tempo, more frequent harmonic change, less modulatory middle section, more restrained rhythmic accentuation

# Common Errors

- Composing a scherzo with minuet-like frequent harmonic changes (tempo prevents this)
- Using remotely varied motive-forms (tempo prevents comprehension of remote variation)
- Assuming the scherzo must be playful or in major mode

# Common Confusions

- **"Scherzo" = playful/humorous**: Factually incorrect; the range includes tragic, heroic, and diabolical.
- **Scherzo must be in 3/4**: No; Beethoven and others use 3/8, 6/8, 6/4, 2/2, 4/4, 2/4.
- **Scherzo vs. minuet**: The distinction is often blurred; Beethoven's Symphony No. 1 "Minuet" is scherzo-like in tempo and elaboration.
