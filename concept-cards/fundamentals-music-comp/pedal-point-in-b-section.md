# Quick Definition
A sustained bass note (usually the dominant) held throughout the B-section, providing contrast through harmonic stasis against the moving bass of the A-section, while suggesting the contraposition of the dominant region.

# Formal Definition
The pedal point on V, stationary in contrast to the moving bass of the A-section, suggests the contraposition of the dominant region to the tonic region. The pedal point provides both contrast (through its static quality) and coherence (through its harmonic connection to the tonic). In some cases, the mind retains the pedal point even when the bass is temporarily omitted, provided the upper voices express the harmony definitively.

# Construction/Recognition
A pedal point in the B-section is recognized by: (1) a sustained or reiterated bass note, usually the dominant, (2) harmonic activity above the pedal that creates surface variety, (3) contrast with the active bass line of the A-section, (4) eventual resolution or connection to the upbeat chord leading to the recapitulation.

# Musical Context
The pedal point is one of the most common devices for organizing the B-section of a small ternary form. It provides a stable harmonic foundation that allows motivic development above it while maintaining a clear sense of harmonic direction. The dominant pedal naturally prepares the return of the tonic in the recapitulation.

# Examples
- Beethoven Op. 2/1-II: pedal point on V in the B-section, stationary in contrast to the moving bass of the A-section
- Beethoven Op. 2/2-IV: contrasting middle section again on a pedal
- Beethoven Op. 28-II: pedal point on V, with each reappearance of V introduced by II, progressively condensed
- Beethoven Op. 31/1-III: unusually, a tonic pedal in the B-section (because the A-section uses a dominant pedal)
- Haydn Ex. 102e: B-section dwells on the dominant of the tonic minor, partly as pedal point; bass omitted in m. 13-17 on the supposition that the mind retains the pedal

# Common Confusions
- The pedal point is not always literally sustained; it may be reiterated or even temporarily absent while retained in the listener's memory
- A tonic pedal in the B-section (as in Op. 31/1-III) is unusual but effective when the surrounding sections use a dominant pedal
