# Quick Definition

Piano writing requires keeping the accompaniment within reach of the fingers, sometimes necessitating cooperation of both hands or shifts between hands, while ensemble writing demands more independent part elaboration balanced against the danger of textural overcrowding.

# Core Definition

In piano writing, the necessity of keeping the accompaniment within reach of the fingers sometimes requires cooperation of both hands or a shift from hand to hand, with or without a change of register. Changes of register also occur for reasons of expressiveness or sonority. In writing for instruments other than keyboard, the individual parts should be elaborated more independently to sustain the interest of the players. But there is danger of imbalance produced by too rich an accompaniment. Economy and transparency are indispensable. The variety of chamber-music combinations precludes detailed discussion, but the principles remain the same as for keyboard instruments (Schoenberg, pp. 55-56).

# Key Properties

1. Piano: keep everything within easy reach of fingers
2. Piano: minimize pedal dependence
3. Piano: both hands may cooperate for accompaniment
4. Piano: shift from hand to hand (with or without register change)
5. Register changes serve expressiveness or sonority, not just convenience
6. Ensemble: elaborate individual parts more independently
7. Ensemble: maintain economy and transparency to avoid imbalance
8. Same principles apply to all instrumental combinations

# Construction / Recognition

1. For piano: test that everything is physically playable without pedal
2. Consider hand cooperation or hand-to-hand shifts
3. For ensemble: give each part enough interest to sustain players' engagement
4. Balance part independence against the risk of overcrowding
5. Prioritize economy and transparency in all contexts

# Context & Application

Practical instrumental considerations are integral to good writing, not secondary to compositional ones. The accompaniment must be both musically effective and physically playable. Schoenberg's emphasis on keeping piano writing independent of the pedal reflects a philosophy of compositional self-sufficiency.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Writing accompaniment that requires heavy pedal use
  **Correction**: Good piano writing should work without the pedal as much as possible

- **Error**: Writing ensemble parts that are too rich or complex
  **Correction**: Economy and transparency take priority; overcrowding creates imbalance

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking register changes are only practical adjustments
  **Clarification**: Register changes for convenience and for expression are both legitimate, but the composer should be conscious of which is operative

- **Confusion**: Equating part independence with part complexity
  **Clarification**: Parts can be interesting and independent without being complex; economy and transparency serve player engagement too
