# Quick Definition
Piano writing requires keeping the accompaniment within reach of the fingers, sometimes necessitating cooperation of both hands or shifts between hands, while ensemble writing demands more independent part elaboration balanced against the danger of textural overcrowding.

# Formal Definition
In piano writing, the necessity of keeping the accompaniment within reach of the fingers sometimes requires cooperation of both hands or a shift from hand to hand, with or without a change of register. Changes of register also occur for reasons of expressiveness or sonority. In writing for instruments other than keyboard, the individual parts should be elaborated more independently to sustain the interest of the players. But there is danger of imbalance produced by too rich an accompaniment. Economy and transparency are indispensable. The variety of chamber-music combinations precludes detailed discussion, but the principles remain the same as for keyboard instruments.

# Construction/Recognition
- Piano: keep everything within easy reach of fingers; minimize pedal dependence
- Piano: both hands may cooperate for accompaniment (Op. 2/3-I, m. 141 ff.; Op. 13-II, m. 41-42 ff.)
- Piano: shift from hand to hand, with or without register change (Op. 27/2-I, m. 15-16, 23-24; Op. 31/2-I, m. 28 ff.)
- Ensemble: elaborate individual parts more independently to sustain player interest
- Ensemble: maintain economy and transparency to avoid imbalance
- Register changes serve expressiveness or sonority, not just convenience

# Musical Context
Practical instrumental considerations are not secondary to compositional ones; Schoenberg treats them as integral to good writing. The accompaniment must not only be musically effective but physically playable. For keyboard instruments, this means respecting the reach of the hands and the limitations of the pedal. For ensemble instruments, it means balancing the need for interesting individual parts against the risk of overcrowding the texture.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/3-I, m. 141 ff.; Op. 13-II, m. 41-42 ff.: Both hands cooperating for accompaniment
- Beethoven, Op. 27/2-I, m. 15-16, 23-24; Op. 31/2-I, m. 28 ff.: Accompaniment shifting from hand to hand

# Common Confusions
- Good piano writing should work without the pedal as much as possible; the pedal should enhance, not rescue, poor voice leading.
- Making parts interesting for ensemble players must not compromise the overall texture; economy and transparency take priority.
- Register changes for convenience and register changes for expression are both legitimate, but the composer should be conscious of which is operative.
