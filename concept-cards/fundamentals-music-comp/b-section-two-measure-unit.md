# Quick Definition
The fundamental building block of the contrasting middle section: a two-measure phrase followed by a more or less varied repetition, possessing an obvious natural logic that can be extended through additional repetitions, sequences, or variations.

# Formal Definition
The basic construction of the contrasting middle section consists of two units, the second a more or less varied repetition of the first. This construction possesses an obvious natural logic. The B-section of the practice form comprises four measures built from this two-measure phrase and its varied repetition. Extensions arise through: double repetition of the opening phrase, sequential treatment, imitative treatment, chain-like (overlapping) construction, or deceptive progressions.

# Construction/Recognition
Identify the two-measure unit by: (1) locating the initial phrase of the B-section, (2) observing its repetition (exact, varied, sequential, or imitative), (3) noting how the unit is extended or multiplied. Extension methods include: double or triple repetition, sequential progression (often along a circle of fourths), progressive condensation, and overlapping chain-like construction.

# Musical Context
This building block is the workhorse of B-section construction across the classical repertoire. Its simplicity provides a stable framework within which harmonic and motivic contrast can operate. The varied repetition ensures both coherence (through the repetition) and interest (through the variation).

# Examples
- Beethoven Op. 2/1-II: two-measure phrase (m. 9-10) and varied repetition (m. 11-12), the simplest model
- Beethoven Op. 7-II: m. 9-10 doubled with triple repetition, any one of three units could be omitted (m. 9-14)
- Beethoven Op. 2/2-IV (second ternary): B-section consists of imitations of a two-measure unit progressing along a circle of fourths
- Haydn Ex. 102a: opening two-measure phrase (m. 9-10) repeated, sequenced, varied, and concluded in overlapping chain-like construction (2+4+2+1)

# Common Confusions
- The two-measure unit is a building block, not a rigid rule; B-sections can use longer or shorter initial phrases
- "Varied repetition" encompasses a wide range from slight modification to sequential transposition to imitative treatment
- Chain-like (overlapping) construction blurs the boundaries between units, creating longer continuous phrases from the basic two-measure material
