# Quick Definition
The fundamental building block of the contrasting middle section: a two-measure phrase followed by a more or less varied repetition, possessing an obvious natural logic that can be extended through additional repetitions, sequences, or variations.

# Core Definition
The basic construction of the contrasting middle section consists of two units, the second a more or less varied repetition of the first. This construction possesses an obvious natural logic. The B-section of the practice form comprises four measures built from this two-measure phrase and its varied repetition. Extensions arise through: double repetition of the opening phrase, sequential treatment, imitative treatment, chain-like (overlapping) construction, or deceptive progressions.

# Key Properties
- Basic unit: a two-measure phrase
- Second unit: a more or less varied repetition of the first
- This pattern has "obvious natural logic"
- Practice form B-section = 4 measures (2+2)
- Extension methods: double/triple repetition, sequential progression (often circle of fourths), progressive condensation, overlapping chain-like construction, deceptive progressions
- The varied repetition ensures both coherence (repetition) and interest (variation)

# Construction / Recognition
Identify the two-measure unit by: (1) locating the initial phrase of the B-section, (2) observing its repetition (exact, varied, sequential, or imitative), (3) noting how the unit is extended or multiplied. Various extension methods produce B-sections from 4 to 8+ measures.

# Context & Application
This building block is the standard construction method for B-sections across the classical repertoire. Its simplicity provides a stable framework within which harmonic and motivic contrast can operate.

# Examples
- Beethoven Op. 2/1-II: two-measure phrase (m. 9-10) and varied repetition (m. 11-12), the simplest model
- Beethoven Op. 7-II: m. 9-10 with triple repetition, any one of three units could be omitted (m. 9-14)
- Beethoven Op. 2/2-IV (second ternary): B-section consists of imitations of a two-measure unit along a circle of fourths
- Haydn Ex. 102a: 2+4+2+1 construction from overlapping chain-like treatment

# Common Errors
- Writing B-sections without a clear two-measure unit as foundation
- Using only exact repetition rather than varied repetition
- Extending the unit without maintaining motivic coherence

# Common Confusions
- The two-measure unit is a building block, not a rigid rule; B-sections can use longer or shorter initial phrases
- "Varied repetition" encompasses a wide range from slight modification to sequential transposition to imitative treatment
- Chain-like (overlapping) construction blurs unit boundaries, creating longer phrases from basic material
