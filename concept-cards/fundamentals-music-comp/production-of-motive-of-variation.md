# Quick Definition

The process of deriving suitable motives for variation by first reducing the theme to its essential skeleton (principal tones and harmonies), then constructing figures that circumscribe or elaborate those principal tones.

# Core Definition

To arrive at a suitable motive of variation, it is necessary to recognize the essentials of the theme. Simplification by omission of everything subordinate (embellishments, grace notes, passing notes, suspensions, appoggiaturas, trills, runs, etc.) unveils the basic construction. In making this reduction, the relation to the harmony must be taken into consideration. The simultaneous rhythmic simplification sometimes requires regularization, where some features must be shifted to other beats. Since the viewpoint determining what features are essential is not necessarily uniform for all variations, there may be more than one suitable "skeleton."

# Prerequisites

- **Motive of variation**: Understanding what the process produces
- **Theme structural constitution**: Understanding the theme's features that determine the skeleton

# Key Properties

- Step 1: Reduce the theme to its essentials (skeleton) by removing subordinate elements
- Step 2: Consider the harmonic framework in the reduction
- Step 3: Regularize the rhythm if necessary (shifting features to other beats)
- Step 4: Construct figures that circumscribe the principal tones of the skeleton
- Step 5: Ensure the motive fits the harmony and contains all principal tones
- Multiple valid skeletons are possible (different viewpoints on essential features)
- The motive's length is limited by the distribution of principal tones and harmonies

# Construction / Recognition

1. Identify and remove subordinate elements (embellishments, grace notes, passing notes, etc.)
2. Retain the principal tones and their harmonic context
3. Regularize the rhythm if features need to be shifted to other beats
4. Construct a motive that circumscribes these principal tones
5. Verify the motive fits the expressed harmony
6. Consider alternative skeletons (different views on what is essential)

# Context & Application

The production of the motive of variation is a systematic, analytical process that reveals variation is not arbitrary decoration but disciplined derivation. Composers begin by thoroughly exploring the theme and making many sketches. Even unsuccessful attempts help acquire intimate acquaintance with the theme's possibilities and limitations. The process connects directly to the sketching of variations.

# Common Errors

- Reducing only the melody without considering the harmonic framework
- Assuming there is only one correct skeleton
- Neglecting rhythmic regularization when necessary

# Common Confusions

- **Skeleton = melody alone**: The skeleton includes the harmonic framework, not just the melody.
- **Single correct skeleton**: There is no single correct skeleton; the viewpoint determining essential features may differ between variations.
- **Rhythmic regularization**: The skeleton's rhythm may not match the theme's surface rhythm; features sometimes need to be shifted to other beats.
