# Quick Definition
A closing section in larger scherzo forms, consisting of multiple codettas or codetta-like segments that counterbalance the preceding harmonic movement, with later segments progressively shortened in a liquidating manner.

# Formal Definition
In larger forms, even a considerable number of codettas may not be sufficient to counterbalance all the preceding harmonic movement. Though this is seldom the case in the scherzo, short coda sections are often found in classical scherzos. They consist of a number of codettas or codetta-like segments, occasionally modulatory, but always returning to the tonic. Generally, the later segments are progressively shortened in a liquidating manner, even to the smallest residues.

# Construction/Recognition
- Comprises multiple codettas or codetta-like segments
- May be occasionally modulatory but always returns to the tonic
- Later segments are progressively shortened (liquidating manner)
- Functions to counterbalance the harmonic movement of the entire form
- Distinguished from individual codettas by its scope and independence
- More detailed treatment is reserved for Chapter XVIII (subsidiary formulations)

# Musical Context
The coda provides the final structural closure of the scherzo, ensuring that the tonic is definitively established after all the modulatory activity of the B-section. It is more substantial than individual codettas and functions at the level of the entire form rather than a single section. While not always present in scherzos, it becomes more important as the scope and complexity of the form increase.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 3, Scherzo: Very long coda section beginning at m. 115, comprising fifty-two measures, which elaborates and liquidates two codetta-like segments
- Beethoven, Symphonies Nos. 1, 2, 4: Exhibit codettas in the recapitulation that approach coda function
- Brahms, Sextet Op. 36, Scherzo: Three groups of codettas conclude the real recapitulation

# Common Confusions
- A coda is larger and more structurally independent than a codetta
- The coda is not merely a string of codettas -- it functions as a counterbalance to the entire form
- Modulatory passages within the coda always return to the tonic
