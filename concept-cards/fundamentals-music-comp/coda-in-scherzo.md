# Quick Definition

A closing section in larger scherzo forms, consisting of multiple codettas or codetta-like segments that counterbalance all the preceding harmonic movement, with later segments progressively shortened in a liquidating manner.

# Core Definition

In larger forms, even a considerable number of codettas may not be sufficient to counterbalance all the preceding harmonic movement. Though this is seldom the case in the scherzo, short coda sections are often found in classical scherzos. They consist of a number of codettas or codetta-like segments, occasionally modulatory, but always returning to the tonic. Generally, the later segments are progressively shortened in a liquidating manner, even to the smallest residues. A more detailed discussion is reserved for Chapter XVIII.

- **Scherzo form**: Understanding the formal context

# Key Properties

- Comprises multiple codettas or codetta-like segments
- May be occasionally modulatory but always returns to the tonic
- Later segments are progressively shortened (liquidating manner)
- Functions to counterbalance the harmonic movement of the entire form
- Distinguished from individual codettas by its scope and independence
- More substantial than individual codettas; functions at the level of the entire form

# Construction / Recognition

1. Identify a section following the recapitulation that exceeds normal codetta scope
2. Check for multiple codetta-like segments
3. Note progressive shortening of later segments
4. Verify that modulatory passages always return to the tonic
5. Assess whether the coda counterbalances the entire form's harmonic movement

# Context & Application

The coda provides the final structural closure of the scherzo, ensuring definitive establishment of the tonic after all modulatory activity. While not always present, it becomes more important as the scope and complexity of the form increase. The Eroica Symphony scherzo's fifty-two-measure coda (beginning at m. 115) represents an extreme case.

# Examples

- Beethoven, Symphony No. 3, Scherzo: Very long coda beginning at m. 115, comprising fifty-two measures, elaborating and liquidating two codetta-like segments
- Beethoven, Symphonies Nos. 1, 2, 4: Exhibit codettas that approach coda function
- Brahms, Sextet Op. 36, Scherzo: Three groups of codettas conclude the recapitulation

# Common Errors

- Confusing a coda with a string of codettas (the coda has broader structural function)
- Including modulatory passages that fail to return to the tonic

# Common Confusions

- **Coda vs. codettas**: A coda is larger and structurally independent, counterbalancing the entire form. Codettas are shorter, reaffirming a single section's cadence.
- **Coda rarity in scherzos**: Schoenberg notes that this is "seldom the case" in the scherzo -- codas are more characteristic of larger forms.
