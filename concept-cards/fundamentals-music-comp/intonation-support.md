# Quick Definition
The harmonic support that assists a singer in finding correct pitch, which is almost indispensable at the beginning of a vocal melody, especially since singers lack an absolute pitch reference.

# Core Definition
Support of intonation through harmony is almost indispensable, especially at the beginning. Unless a singer possesses a memory for absolute pitch, the singer has no yardstick whatever for intonation. Chromaticism, augmented intervals (or successions of them), and tones or tone successions which cannot be related to the harmony -- especially if outside the tonality -- offer difficulties.

- vocal-melody: Intonation support is a concern specific to vocal melody writing

# Key Properties
- Harmonic support is almost indispensable at the beginning of a vocal melody
- Singers generally lack an absolute pitch reference
- Chromaticism, augmented intervals, and tones outside the tonality present intonation difficulties
- Successive leaps spanning a seventh or ninth should be avoided
- Intonation difficulty depends on harmonic context, not interval size alone
- Trained singers should be able to sing tempered scale intervals

# Construction / Recognition
When writing vocal melody: (1) provide clear harmonic support at the beginning, (2) ensure melodic intervals can be related to the underlying harmony, (3) avoid chromatic passages, augmented intervals, and tones outside the tonality unless the harmony makes them comprehensible, (4) note that successive leaps spanning a seventh or ninth should be avoided.

# Context & Application
The trained singer should be able to sing the intervals of the tempered scale, which deviates from natural acoustical intervals. Chromaticism is difficult partly because natural semitones differ in size from tempered ones, a fact which causes choirs to "get off pitch." Sparingly used, chromaticism need not be avoided entirely.

# Common Errors
- Beginning a vocal melody without clear harmonic support
- Writing intervals that cannot be related to the underlying harmony
- Placing chromatic passages where the harmony does not clarify them

# Common Confusions
- Intonation difficulty is not the same as interval difficulty; a large interval may be easy to intonate if the harmony supports it, while a small chromatic interval may be very difficult without harmonic support
- The problem is not chromaticism per se but chromaticism that cannot be related to the underlying harmony
