# Quick Definition
The B-section of a small ternary form, organized as a contrast to the A-section, typically built from a two-measure phrase and its varied repetition, providing both harmonic and motivic contrast while maintaining coherence.

# Formal Definition
The most effective factor in a contrasting section is the harmony. The A-section establishes a tonic; the B-section contraposes another region (one of the more closely related). This provides both contrast and coherence. Further coherence is furnished by the metre and by using forms of the basic motive which are not too remote from those of the A-section. Further contrast can be achieved through the use of new variations of the basic motive or change in the order of previous motive-forms.

# Construction/Recognition
The basic construction of the B-section consists of: (1) a two-measure phrase, (2) followed by a more or less varied repetition of it. This construction possesses an obvious natural logic. The section typically ends on V as an "upbeat chord" leading to the recapitulation. Extensions occur through double repetitions, sequential treatment, deceptive progressions, or condensed repetitions.

# Musical Context
The practice form has a four-measure B-section, but actual compositions vary considerably. The B-section may dwell on a pedal point (often on V), use imitation, employ sequential repetition, or create contrast through texture and style changes (e.g., legato vs. staccato).

# Examples
- Beethoven Op. 2/1-II: simplest possible model; four measures built from a two-measure phrase and varied repetition; mere interchange of V and I contrasts with the opening I-V interchange
- Beethoven Op. 7-II: six measures (m. 9-14) from triple repetition of opening phrase; any one of the three units could be omitted
- Beethoven Op. 26-I: sequentially repeated two-measure unit followed by a cadence, extended by a deceptive progression (m. 24)
- Beethoven Op. 28-II: eight measures on dominant pedal point, with progressively condensed repetition

# Common Confusions
- The B-section is not arbitrary contrast; it must maintain motivic and harmonic connection to the A-section
- The practice form's four-measure B-section is a pedagogical model; actual B-sections range from four to eight or more measures
- The two-measure unit plus varied repetition is the basic building block, but this can be extended, doubled, or condensed
