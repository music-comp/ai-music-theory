# Quick Definition
The B-section of a small ternary form, organized as a contrast to the A-section, typically built from a two-measure phrase and its varied repetition, providing both harmonic and motivic contrast while maintaining coherence.

# Core Definition
The most effective factor in a contrasting section is the harmony. The A-section establishes a tonic; the B-section contraposes another region (one of the more closely related). This provides both contrast and coherence. Further coherence is furnished by the metre and by using forms of the basic motive which are not too remote from those of the A-section. Further contrast can be achieved through the use of new variations of the basic motive or change in the order of previous motive-forms.

# Key Properties
- Harmony is the most effective factor in contrast
- B-section contraposes a closely related region to the A-section's tonic
- Basic construction: two-measure phrase + varied repetition (= 4 measures in practice form)
- Extensions through: double repetition, sequential treatment, imitation, chain-like construction, deceptive progressions
- B-section typically ends on V as "upbeat chord" leading to recapitulation
- A small connective figure often bridges to the recapitulation
- Pedal point (usually on V) is a common organizing device
- B-sections can begin on various degrees: V, i, v, ii, iii, II, IV, iv, vi

# Construction / Recognition
The basic construction consists of: (1) a two-measure phrase, (2) a more or less varied repetition. The section typically ends on V as an "upbeat chord." Extensions occur through additional repetitions, sequential treatment, deceptive progressions, or condensed repetitions. In all cases, motivic connection to the A-section must be maintained.

# Context & Application
The practice form has a four-measure B-section, but actual compositions vary considerably. The B-section may dwell on a pedal point, use imitation, employ sequential repetition, or create contrast through texture and style changes. The student should try B-sections beginning on all degrees to expand harmonic knowledge.

# Examples
- Beethoven Op. 2/1-II: simplest possible model; mere interchange of V and I contrasts with opening I-V; pedal point on V; dotted rhythm and suspensions as connecting elements
- Beethoven Op. 7-II: 6 measures (m. 9-14), triple repetition of opening phrase; any one of three units could be omitted
- Beethoven Op. 26-I: sequentially repeated two-measure unit, extended by deceptive progression (m. 24)
- Beethoven Op. 28-II: 8 measures on dominant pedal, with progressively condensed repetition
- Beethoven Op. 14/2-II: two-measure unit + varied repetition; contrast through legato style, coherence through phrasing
- Ex. 105: ten different B-sections from a single A-section, beginning on various degrees

# Common Errors
- Creating a B-section without motivic connection to the A-section
- Using too remote a harmonic region, breaking coherence
- Failing to end on an appropriate upbeat chord
- Making the B-section the same length as the practice form when the material warrants extension

# Common Confusions
- The B-section is not arbitrary contrast; it must maintain motivic and harmonic connection to the A-section
- The practice form's four-measure B-section is a pedagogical model; actual B-sections range widely in length
- The two-measure unit plus varied repetition is the basic building block, but can be extended, doubled, or condensed
