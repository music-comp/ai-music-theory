# Quick Definition

The return of the A-section material in the scherzo, which may be changed, modified, varied, or reconstructed, and which often differs from the original A-section by including extensions, episodes, and added codettas.

# Core Definition

Disregarding literal repetition, the recapitulation may be changed, modified, varied, or reconstructed. The A'-section of many scherzos differs from the A-section in including extensions of the recapitulation, episodes, and added codettas. The recapitulation is typically introduced after the liquidation process has reached the upbeat harmony. Modifications may include enrichment through countermelodies, interchange of voices (as in double counterpoint), and structural adjustments to ensure both antecedent and consequent end on the tonic.

# Prerequisites

- **Scherzo form**: Understanding the ternary structure
- **Liquidation in scherzo**: Understanding how the recapitulation is prepared

# Key Properties

- May range from nearly literal repetition to substantial reconstruction
- Often includes extensions, episodes, and codettas not present in the A-section
- May feature enrichment through countermelodies or voice interchange (double counterpoint)
- Antecedent and/or consequent may be modified to end on the tonic
- The "wrong key" recapitulation occasionally appears (Beethoven's later works)
- A simple codetta may provide the full cadence missing in the recapitulation proper

# Construction / Recognition

1. Compare to the original A-section for modifications
2. Look for extensions, episodes, and added codettas
3. Check for countermelodies and voice interchange
4. Note whether both antecedent and consequent end on the tonic
5. Check for "wrong key" recapitulation
6. Identify concluding codettas that provide the full cadence

# Context & Application

The recapitulation balances the modulatory instability of the B-section by re-establishing the tonic and the main thematic material. The degree of modification reflects how much the B-section has destabilized expectations. More extensive B-sections often call for more extensive recapitulations with additional codettas and extensions.

# Examples

- Beethoven, Op. 2/3, Scherzo: Modified so both antecedent and consequent end on the tonic
- Beethoven, Op. 26, Scherzo: Enriched by countermelody in right hand; melody lowered an octave to left hand; written-out repetition varied by voice interchange (double counterpoint, m. 53-60)
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 7-III: "Recapitulation in the wrong key" in the subdominant
- Beethoven, Op. 74 (Harp Quartet), Presto: Quotes only three measures of the beginning, continues with liquidating forms and episodes on pedal points

# Common Errors

- Using literal repetition when modification would better balance the B-section's instability
- Omitting the full cadence that the recapitulation proper may lack

# Common Confusions

- **Literal repetition is an option**: Not all recapitulations are modified; Schoenberg says "disregarding literal repetition" to focus on the more interesting cases.
- **"Wrong key" recapitulation**: This is intentional, not an error; it represents Beethoven's most innovative formal procedure.
