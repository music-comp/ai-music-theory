# Quick Definition
The return of the A-section material in the scherzo, which may be changed, modified, varied, or reconstructed, and which often differs from the original A-section by including extensions, episodes, and added codettas.

# Formal Definition
Disregarding literal repetition, the recapitulation may be changed, modified, varied, or reconstructed. The A'-section of many scherzos differs from the A-section in including extensions of the recapitulation, episodes, and added codettas. The recapitulation is typically introduced after the liquidation process has reached the upbeat harmony, creating the conditions for the theme's re-entrance. Modifications may include enrichment through countermelodies, interchange of voices (as in double counterpoint), and structural adjustments to ensure both antecedent and consequent end on the tonic.

# Construction/Recognition
- May range from nearly literal repetition to substantial reconstruction
- Often includes extensions, episodes, and codettas not present in the A-section
- May feature enrichment through countermelodies or voice interchange
- Antecedent and/or consequent may be modified to end on the tonic
- The "wrong key" recapitulation occasionally appears in Beethoven's later works
- A simple codetta may provide the full cadence missing in the recapitulation proper

# Musical Context
The recapitulation balances the modulatory instability of the B-section by re-establishing the tonic and the main thematic material. The degree of modification reflects the composer's assessment of how much the B-section has destabilized expectations. More extensive B-sections often call for more extensive recapitulations with additional codettas and extensions.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/3, Scherzo: Modified so both antecedent and consequent end on the tonic
- Beethoven, Op. 26, Scherzo: Enriched by a countermelody in the right hand; principal melody lowered an octave to the left hand; written-out repetition varied by interchange of voices (double counterpoint)
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 7-III: "Recapitulation in the wrong key" -- occurring in the subdominant instead of the tonic
- Beethoven, Op. 74 (Harp Quartet), Presto: Recapitulation quotes only three measures of the beginning, continues with liquidating forms and episodes

# Common Confusions
- Literal repetition is only one option for the recapitulation -- modification is the norm
- The recapitulation in the "wrong key" is intentional, not an error
- Extensions within the recapitulation are part of the A'-section, not separate sections
