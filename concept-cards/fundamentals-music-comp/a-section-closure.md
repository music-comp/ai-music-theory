# Quick Definition
The A-section of a small ternary form may end on various degrees (I, V, or III/iii in major; i, III, V, or v in minor), but its beginning must clearly establish the tonality to prepare for the contrast to follow.

# Core Definition
The A-section of the A-B-A1 form may be a sentence or a period, ending on I, V, or III (iii) in major; on i, III, V, or v in minor. The beginning, at least, should clearly express the tonality, because of the contrast to follow. The choice of closing degree affects the harmonic relationship with the B-section and influences what kinds of contrast are possible.

# Prerequisites
- small-ternary-form: The A-section is the first part of the A-B-A1 form

# Key Properties
- A-section may be structured as either sentence or period
- Closing degrees in major: I, V, or III (iii)
- Closing degrees in minor: i, III, V, or v
- The beginning must clearly express tonality (to prepare for contrast)
- The clarity of tonal establishment is directly proportional to contrast effectiveness
- The closing degree affects what harmonic contrast is possible in the B-section
- A deceptive cadence may require an added cadence, extending the section
- The A-section beginning on the dominant (as in Op. 7-IV) allows the B-section to begin on the tonic

# Construction / Recognition
When constructing the A-section: (1) begin with clear tonal establishment, (2) choose the closing degree based on what harmonic contrast will follow, (3) structure as either a sentence or period, (4) ensure the ending provides a clear point of departure for the B-section. When analyzing, identify the structural type and closing degree.

# Context & Application
The A-section establishes the tonic, which the B-section will contrapose. The clarity of tonal establishment is directly proportional to the effectiveness of the subsequent contrast. A deceptive cadence in the A-section may require an added cadence, extending the section beyond the practice form.

# Examples
- Beethoven Op. 2/1-II: A-section is a period with caesura on V at m. 4, comprising 8 measures
- Beethoven Op. 7-IV: begins with dominant harmony, allowing the B-section to begin with tonic -- reversing the usual relationship
- Haydn Ex. 102e: deceptive cadence in m. 8 requires added cadence to dominant, extending A-section to 10 measures
- Haydn Ex. 102b: A-section extended by addition of m. 8-9

# Common Errors
- Beginning the A-section ambiguously, weakening the subsequent contrast
- Ignoring the implications of the closing degree for the B-section
- Not extending when a deceptive cadence requires an additional cadence

# Common Confusions
- The A-section need not end on the tonic; ending on V or III is common and creates different possibilities for the B-section
- Clear tonal establishment at the beginning is more important than the specific closing degree
