# Quick Definition
The A-section of a small ternary form may end on various degrees (I, V, or III/iii in major; i, III, V, or v in minor), but its beginning must clearly establish the tonality to prepare for the contrast to follow.

# Formal Definition
The A-section of the A-B-A1 form may be a sentence or a period, ending on I, V, or III (iii) in major; on i, III, V, or v in minor. The beginning, at least, should clearly express the tonality, because of the contrast to follow. The choice of closing degree affects the harmonic relationship with the B-section and influences what kinds of contrast are possible.

# Construction/Recognition
When constructing the A-section: (1) begin with clear tonal establishment, (2) choose the closing degree based on what harmonic contrast will follow, (3) structure as either a sentence or period, (4) ensure the ending provides a clear point of departure for the contrasting section. When analyzing, identify the structural type (sentence/period) and the degree on which the section closes.

# Musical Context
The A-section establishes the tonic, which the B-section will contrapose with another region. The clarity of the A-section's tonal establishment is directly proportional to the effectiveness of the subsequent contrast. A deceptive cadence in the A-section may require an added cadence, extending the section beyond the practice form.

# Examples
- Beethoven Op. 2/1-II: A-section is a period with a caesura on V in m. 4, comprising eight measures
- Beethoven Op. 7-IV: begins with the dominant harmony, allowing the B-section to begin with the tonic -- reversing the usual relationship
- Haydn Ex. 102e: deceptive cadence in m. 8 requires an added cadence to the dominant, extending the A-section to ten measures

# Common Confusions
- The A-section need not end on the tonic; ending on V or III is common and creates different possibilities for the B-section
- Clear tonal establishment at the beginning is more important than the specific closing degree
