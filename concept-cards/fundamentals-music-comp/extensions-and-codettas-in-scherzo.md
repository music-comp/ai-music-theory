# Quick Definition
Extensions prolong the recapitulation through repetition (often sequential) of elements, while codettas reaffirm the cadence; both typically appear in the A'-section and may feature progressive shortening through liquidation.

# Formal Definition
Extension is usually produced by repetition (often sequential) of an element. In simple cases it is associated with a turn toward the subdominant; in others a real modulation occurs. Motivally, if it is not the simple repetition of a segment, it usually consists of a developing elaboration of preceding motive-forms. Codettas are primarily cadences serving as reaffirmations of the ending of a section. If more than one codetta appears, the later ones are typically shortened in a liquidating manner, even to the smallest residues. Codettas may range from rudimentary V-I cadences to highly complex progressions, and from simple repetitions to independent formulations.

# Construction/Recognition
- Extensions: repetition (often sequential) of an element within the recapitulation; may involve a turn toward the subdominant or actual modulation
- Codettas: cadential passages following the main body; progressively shortened
- Multiple codettas typically decrease in length (liquidating manner)
- Codettas may feature interchange of voice positions (multiple counterpoint)
- A codetta may provide the full cadence missing from the recapitulation proper
- Written-out ritardando (augmentation) may serve as a final codetta

# Musical Context
Extensions and codettas are standard features of the scherzo's A'-section, providing additional structural weight to balance the modulatory B-section. The progressive shortening of codettas mirrors the liquidation process in miniature, bringing the section to a decisive close. In some cases, codettas are augmented (written-out ritardando) to make the ending of the piece distinct.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 1-III: Extension associated with a turn toward the subdominant
- Beethoven, Septet Op. 20: Simple repetition of a segment as extension
- Beethoven, Op. 2/2, Scherzo: Simple codetta provides the full cadence missing in the recapitulation proper
- Beethoven, Op. 18/2, Scherzo: Codetta of four measures, motivally remote, repeated with parts interchanged (multiple counterpoint); smaller codetta repeated and liquidated
- Beethoven, Op. 59/2, Allegretto: Last codetta (m. 48-49) is an augmentation of m. 47, functioning as a written-out ritardando
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 2, Scherzo: Last codetta augmented like a ritardando

# Common Confusions
- Extensions occur within the recapitulation (before the cadence); codettas occur after the cadence
- Codettas are not codas -- codettas are short cadential reaffirmations, while codas are larger closing sections
- The progressive shortening of codettas is a form of liquidation, not mere abbreviation
