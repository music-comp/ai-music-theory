# Quick Definition

Extensions prolong the recapitulation through repetition (often sequential) of elements, while codettas reaffirm the cadence; both typically appear in the A'-section and may feature progressive shortening through liquidation.

# Core Definition

Extension is usually produced by repetition (often sequential) of an element. In simple cases it is associated with a turn toward the subdominant; in others a real modulation occurs. Motivally, if it is not the simple repetition of a segment, it usually consists of a developing elaboration of preceding motive-forms. Codettas are primarily cadences serving as reaffirmations of the ending of a section. If more than one codetta appears, the later ones are typically shortened in a liquidating manner, even to the smallest residues. Codettas may range from rudimentary V-I to highly complex, and from simple repetitions to independent formulations.

# Prerequisites

- **Recapitulation in scherzo**: The structural context in which extensions and codettas appear

# Key Properties

- Extensions: repetition (often sequential) within the recapitulation; may involve subdominant turn or real modulation
- Codettas: cadential passages after the main body; progressively shortened
- Multiple codettas decrease in length (liquidating manner)
- Codettas may feature interchange of voice positions (multiple counterpoint)
- A codetta may provide the full cadence missing from the recapitulation proper
- Written-out ritardando (augmentation) may serve as a final codetta

# Construction / Recognition

1. Identify extensions within the recapitulation (sequential repetitions, subdominant turns)
2. Identify codettas after the main cadence
3. Check for progressive shortening of multiple codettas
4. Note voice interchange in codetta repetitions
5. Check for augmentation (written-out ritardando) in the final codetta

# Context & Application

Extensions and codettas are standard features of the scherzo's A'-section, providing additional structural weight to balance the modulatory B-section. The progressive shortening of codettas mirrors the liquidation process in miniature, bringing the section to a decisive close.

# Examples

- Beethoven, Symphony No. 1-III: Extension with subdominant turn
- Beethoven, Septet Op. 20: Simple repetition of a segment as extension
- Beethoven, Op. 2/2, Scherzo: Simple codetta provides the full cadence missing in the recapitulation proper
- Beethoven, Op. 18/2, Scherzo: Four-measure codetta, motivally remote, repeated with parts interchanged (multiple counterpoint); smaller codetta repeated and liquidated
- Beethoven, Op. 18/6, Scherzo: Closely related motive-forms in codetta; rhythm condensed to equal eighth-notes (m. 39), liquidated into scale lines and broken chords
- Beethoven, Op. 59/2, Allegretto: Last codetta (m. 48-49) is augmentation of m. 47, functioning as written-out ritardando
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 2, Scherzo: Last codetta augmented like a ritardando

# Common Errors

- Confusing extensions with codettas (extensions before the cadence; codettas after)
- Failing to progressively shorten multiple codettas
- Confusing codettas with codas (different scope)

# Common Confusions

- **Extension vs. codetta**: Extensions occur within the recapitulation (before the cadence); codettas occur after the cadence.
- **Written-out ritardando**: The augmentation of a final codetta functions as a written-out ritardando, making the ending distinct without requiring the performer to slow down arbitrarily.
