# Quick Definition

The concluding portion of a phrase, marked by a combination of rhythmic, melodic, and intervallic features that differentiate it from the body of the phrase and provide structural punctuation analogous to a comma.

# Core Definition

"Phrase endings may be marked by a combination of distinguishing features, such as rhythmic reduction, melodic relaxation through a drop in pitch, the use of smaller intervals and fewer notes; or by any other suitable differentiation" (Ch. 2, p. 3). The ending "suggests a form of punctuation such as a comma," providing the listener with a sense of the phrase as a complete structural unit.

# Prerequisites

- **phrase** -- Must understand the phrase as a structural unit before discussing how it ends

# Key Properties

1. Endings are marked by differentiation from the body of the phrase
2. Rhythmic reduction (longer note values, fewer notes) is a key marker
3. Melodic relaxation through a drop in pitch signals closure
4. Use of smaller intervals contributes to the sense of settling
5. Fewer notes create rhythmic space at the boundary
6. Multiple features may combine for stronger punctuation
7. A single distinguishing feature may be sufficient
8. The ending suggests punctuation like a comma, not necessarily a full stop

# Construction / Recognition

## To Create a Phrase Ending

1. Reduce rhythmic activity relative to the body of the phrase
2. Allow the melody to descend or settle
3. Use smaller intervals than those in the body of the phrase
4. Reduce the number of notes or voices
5. Combine multiple features for stronger punctuation
6. Ensure the ending differentiates itself from what precedes it

## To Recognize a Phrase Ending

1. Look for a reduction in rhythmic activity
2. Note any melodic descent or relaxation
3. Identify smaller intervals compared to the phrase body
4. The sense of "breathing space" indicates a boundary

# Context & Application

Phrase endings are critical for articulating form at the smallest structural level. They create the "breathing points" that make music comprehensible through subdivision. The strength of a phrase ending affects whether the listener perceives continuation or closure. Rhythm is "particularly important in moulding the phrase" and "is often the determining factor in establishing the unity of the phrase" (Ch. 2, p. 3).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Using the same ending technique for every phrase
  **Correction**: Vary the combination of features to create different degrees of punctuation

- **Error**: Creating endings that are too abrupt relative to the phrase body
  **Correction**: The ending should differentiate itself from the body while maintaining continuity

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Equating phrase endings with cadences
  **Clarification**: A phrase ending is a local punctuation; a cadence has specific harmonic implications and is a more formal type of closure

- **Confusion**: Assuming all the listed features must be present in every ending
  **Clarification**: "Any other suitable differentiation" can serve; the listed features are common but not exhaustive
