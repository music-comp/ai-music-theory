# Quick Definition
The concluding portion of a phrase, marked by a combination of rhythmic, melodic, and intervallic features that differentiate it from the body of the phrase and provide structural punctuation.

# Formal Definition
Phrase endings are marked by a combination of distinguishing features such as rhythmic reduction, melodic relaxation through a drop in pitch, the use of smaller intervals and fewer notes, or by any other suitable differentiation. The ending suggests a form of punctuation such as a comma, providing the listener with a sense of the phrase as a complete structural unit.

# Construction/Recognition
To create an effective phrase ending, employ one or more of the following:
- **Rhythmic reduction**: use longer note values or fewer notes
- **Melodic relaxation**: allow the melody to descend or settle
- **Intervallic reduction**: use smaller intervals than those in the body of the phrase
- **Textural thinning**: reduce the number of notes or voices

The more of these features combined, the stronger the sense of punctuation.

# Musical Context
Phrase endings are critical for articulating form at the smallest structural level. They create the "breathing points" that make music comprehensible through subdivision. The strength of a phrase ending affects whether the listener perceives continuation or closure.

# Common Confusions
- A phrase ending is not the same as a cadence; a phrase ending is a local punctuation, while a cadence has specific harmonic implications
- The features that mark a phrase ending are relative to the body of the phrase -- what counts as "reduction" depends on what came before
- Not every phrase ending involves all the listed features; sometimes a single distinguishing feature is sufficient
