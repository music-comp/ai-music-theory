# Quick Definition
The modulatory middle section of the sonata-allegro, consisting of segments passing through contrasting key regions while working out previously exposed thematic material -- technically resembling the scherzo's Durchfuhrung but more elaborately organized, with the relative length varying widely from approximately half to equal the exposition.

# Core Definition
The elaboration is essentially a contrasting middle section. Because the exposition is basically stable, the elaboration tends to be modulatory. Because the exposition uses closely related keys, the elaboration usually includes more remote regions. Because the exposition "develops" a wealth of differing themes from a basic motive, the elaboration normally makes use of variants of previously exposed themes, seldom evolving new musical ideas. Technically, the elaboration resembles the Durchfuhrung of the scherzo. It consists of a number of segments, passing systematically through a number of contrasting keys or regions. It ends with the establishment of an appropriate upbeat chord or retransition.

# Key Properties
- Essentially modulatory (contrasts with exposition's stability)
- Uses variants of previously exposed themes
- Consists of numbered segments passing through contrasting keys
- Earlier segments tend to be longer and more stable
- Approaching the retransition: shorter segments, more rapid key changes, climactic condensation and partial liquidation
- Occasional "roving" passages pertaining to no definite region
- Segments overlap freely
- Dominants emphasized more than tonics for momentum
- Relative length: approximately half to equal the exposition
- The beginning may relate to the end of the exposition in various ways

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the beginning (may continue exposition's key, juxtapose new key, or use introductory segment)
2. Map the sequence of key regions traversed
3. Note the thematic material used (which exposition themes, in what order)
4. Track segment lengths (typically decreasing toward the retransition)
5. Identify the retransition preparation (pedal point on V or codettas approaching upbeat chord)
6. Note overlapping between segments

# Context & Application
The importance and significance of this division was much overrated during the late nineteenth century. While the Durchfuhrung is usually considered the most characteristic feature, in many important works it is short and sketch-like. While one expects development of the most important themes, sometimes the elaboration deals with themes that were unimportant or subordinate. The formal purpose is to provide a related contrast through modulatory treatment of previously exposed material.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 13-I: monothematic; four-measure quotation from introduction, six-measure segment sequenced; reduced versions appear three times; extended dominant pedal (m. 167-87)
- Beethoven, Op. 28-I: monothematic; sequence plus progressive reduction; principal theme in subdominant, then minor; reduction from eight to four to two to one measure
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1-I: principal theme in introductory measures and retransition only; main body uses subordinate theme
- Beethoven, Op. 10/1-I: built on a theme not found in the exposition, though related to transition and subordinate themes
- Mozart, K.V. 465-I: built entirely from initial motive-form; gradual expansion of compass, upbeat becomes broken chord, motive shortened to single measure
- Mozart, K.V. 464-I: gradual reduction and liquidation beginning m. 123; retransition overlaps recapitulation beginning

# Common Errors
- Making the elaboration too long relative to the exposition
- Failing to systematically traverse contrasting key regions
- Introducing entirely new themes when existing material has not been exhausted
- Neglecting the progressive shortening of segments toward the retransition

# Common Confusions
- "Development" is a misnomer; Schoenberg prefers "elaboration" (Durchfuhrung) because themes do not truly "develop" in the sense of growth
- The elaboration's importance was overrated in the late 19th century; in many works it is short and sketch-like
- The elaboration may deal with formerly inconspicuous themes rather than the most prominent ones
