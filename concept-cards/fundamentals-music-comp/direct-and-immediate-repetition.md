# Quick Definition
The technique of repeating a short musical segment immediately after its first statement, often with slight variation, as a primary means of establishing thematic material in subordinate theme groups.

# Formal Definition
In subordinate themes, mere repetition and juxtaposition often replace development and elaboration. The direct and immediate repetition of relatively short segments -- typically two to four measures -- is a defining characteristic of the loose construction that distinguishes subordinate from principal themes. Such repetition establishes each formulation before it is abandoned to make way for the next.

# Construction/Recognition
- A short segment (often two to four measures) is stated and immediately repeated
- The repetition may be exact, sequential (transposed), or slightly varied
- After establishment through repetition, the idea typically gives way to another distinct formulation
- Multiple such repetition-and-abandonment units are strung together to form the subordinate group
- Distinguishable from the developmental repetitions in principal themes, which involve progressive variation

# Musical Context
Direct and immediate repetition is fundamental to the construction of subordinate theme groups in sonata-allegro and rondo forms. It provides a simpler, more accessible type of thematic presentation that contrasts with the developmental procedures of principal themes.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1-I: a phrase appears three times in m. 21-25
- Beethoven, Op. 2/2-I: a small segment (m. 59-62) is twice sequentially repeated; a two-measure segment (m. 70) is also sequenced twice
- Beethoven, Op. 2/2-IV: a two-measure phrase (m. 27-28) is repeated and then liquidated through sequential repetitions
- Beethoven, Op. 14/1-III: the entire subordinate theme comprises eight measures (m. 22-29), a four-measure segment and its repetition
- Beethoven's Fifth Symphony-I (m. 63-93): continuous quarter-note movement; the first four-measure phrase is repeated twice without structural change

# Common Confusions
- Direct repetition in subordinate themes differs from repetition in principal themes: in principal themes, repetition serves development and variation; in subordinate themes, it serves establishment before the material is abandoned
- "Immediate" repetition means the segment is repeated right away, not after intervening material
