# Quick Definition
The three ranges of the voice -- highest, middle, and lowest -- each with distinct capabilities, colors, and structural implications for melody writing.

# Formal Definition
The highest register is "vulnerable"; its use is always a strain on the singer, and remaining too long in it tires the voice. Used carefully, it produces a true climax, for which it should be reserved (a structural consideration). The lowest register is stronger than the middle register but should not be overburdened with dramatic expression. The middle register is not capable of extreme expression and offers no great dynamic compass, but within these limits it is the most convenient register. The registers differ not only in volume but also in color (timbre).

# Construction/Recognition
When writing vocal melody: (1) reserve the highest register for climactic moments, (2) do not linger too long in the highest register, (3) use the middle register as the primary working range, (4) avoid overburdening the lowest register with excessive drama, (5) be cautious with sudden register changes as they endanger unity of tone quality.

# Musical Context
Understanding register is essential for both vocal and instrumental melody writing. The voice needs minimal time to develop a full sound; accordingly, rapid notes (especially staccatos) and notes requiring strong accentuation (sforzati) are difficult, though not beyond a virtuoso's ability. These principles apply by analogy to instrumental writing as well.

# Examples
- Wagner (Ex. 78a): uses the highest register frequently in an explosive outburst of emotion, jumps suddenly to the lowest register -- demonstrating dramatic effect but extreme vocal difficulty
- Mahler (Ex. 77): not only difficult intervals and rapid tempo, but especially the passionate expression, makes the impression of an instrumental rather than vocal melody

# Common Confusions
- The highest register is not simply "louder" -- it differs in timbre and expressive quality, not just volume
- Sudden register changes are not forbidden but require awareness of the unity of tone quality they may endanger
