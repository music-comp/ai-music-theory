# Quick Definition
The contrasting middle section at the movement level that follows the scherzo, functioning identically to the trio in the minuet but with additional techniques for connecting the trio to the scherzo's repetition, including transitional liquidation and mediating passages.

# Formal Definition
The relation between scherzo and trio is the same as that between minuet and trio. In many cases, the repetition of the scherzo follows a complete ending of the trio. In other cases, the recapitulation of the trio is liquidated into a transition, introducing an upbeat chord for the scherzo's repetition. Often a little segment may be inserted between trio and scherzo. Scherzo movements are sometimes enlarged to the dimensions of a rondo by interpolating the trio twice, alternating with three appearances of the scherzo.

# Construction/Recognition
- Provides contrast to the scherzo in tonality, character, or both
- May end completely before the scherzo returns, or may transition into the scherzo
- Transitional techniques: liquidation of trio's recapitulation into upbeat chord; inserted connecting segments; reintroduction of scherzo motive on modulatory harmony
- May be interpolated twice for rondo-like structure (Scherzo-Trio-Scherzo-Trio-Scherzo)
- Some composers introduce two different trios (Schumann)

# Musical Context
The trio provides large-scale contrast at the movement level, and the techniques used to connect it back to the scherzo reveal important principles of mediation between contrasting materials. The handling of the transition from trio to scherzo is a compositional challenge, especially when the two are thematically heterogeneous. The best examples show careful derivation and mediation between the two themes.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/3 and String Quartet Op. 18/1: Trio's recapitulation liquidated into a transition introducing the upbeat chord
- Beethoven, Fifth Symphony, m. 224-35: Similar transitional liquidation
- Beethoven, Op. 26 and String Quartet Op. 18/2: Scherzo motive reintroduced on modulatory harmony between trio and scherzo
- Beethoven, Seventh Symphony, m. 221-234: A reminiscence of the trio, with transposition of the motive reintroducing F in the last two measures
- Beethoven, String Quartet Op. 18/6, m. 65-68: Tonic minor segment inserted to produce contrast between trio and scherzo, both in B-flat major
- Brahms, Sextet Op. 36: Extraordinary mediation between heterogeneous themes; eight-measure segment (m. 227) whose first half prepares the scherzo, second half derives from the trio
- Beethoven, Op. 74, Op. 95, Symphonies 4, 7: Trio interpolated twice (rondo-like structure)
- Schumann, Piano Quintet Op. 44: Two different trios

# Common Confusions
- The trio in a scherzo follows the same principles as the trio in a minuet -- it is not a different form
- The transition from trio to scherzo is a distinct compositional challenge, not a mere da capo
- A rondo-like scherzo (with two trio insertions) is an expansion of the basic form, not a different form
