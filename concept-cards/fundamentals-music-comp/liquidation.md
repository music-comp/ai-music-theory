# Quick Definition
The process of gradually eliminating characteristic features of a motive until only uncharacteristic residues remain, which no longer demand continuation, thereby enabling structural closure in sentences and other thematic forms.

# Formal Definition
Liquidation consists in gradually eliminating characteristic features, until only uncharacteristic ones remain, which no longer demand a continuation. Often only residues remain, which have little in common with the basic motive. In conjunction with a cadence or half cadence, this process provides adequate delimitation for a sentence. The liquidation is generally supported by a shortening of the phrase -- two-measure phrases reduced to one measure, four-measure phrases condensed to two, and sometimes to still smaller units (half-measures). The technique is introduced conceptually in Chapter 3 through reduction and omission as variation methods, and receives its full treatment in Chapter 8 as the primary mechanism for concluding sentence structures.

# Construction/Recognition
Liquidation is recognized by the progressive simplification of motivic material:
- Characteristic intervals are reduced, abandoned, or combined in a different order
- Distinctive rhythmic features are simplified or cease entirely
- Phrase units progressively shorten (2-measure to 1-measure to half-measure)
- The motive loses its individual identity, leaving only generic, uncharacteristic residues
- The process works in conjunction with cadential harmony and cadence contour
- Reduction: merely omitting part of the model
- Condensation: compressing the content, whereby even the order of features may change

The technique appears in the variation methods catalog as "reduction through omission or condensation" (Ex. 21) and "omission of features" (Ex. 34), and is fully developed in Chapter 8's sentence continuation examples.

# Musical Context
Liquidation serves a crucial formal function as the primary mechanism for ending a sentence, counteracting the tendency toward unlimited extension that development naturally creates. Development implies growth, augmentation, extension, and expansion, but also reduction, condensation, and intensification -- and liquidation is the complement that brings development to a purposeful conclusion. Nearly all sentence continuations in the literature employ residues of the basic motive in their closing measures. The process also appears in cadential passages of consequents, where the melody reduces characteristic features to uncharacteristic ones. Combined with harmonic concentration and cadence contour, liquidation provides effective structural delimitation.

# Common Confusions
- Liquidation is not destruction; it is a controlled, purposeful process of simplification that serves structural delimitation
- Reduction and condensation are different: reduction omits part of the model, while condensation compresses content and may reorder features
- Liquidation is distinct from mere simplification; it specifically involves the progressive removal of characteristic (identifying) features until only uncharacteristic (generic) ones remain
- The term is Schoenberg's own and carries specific technical meaning beyond its everyday usage
- Liquidation does not mean the music becomes uninteresting; the residues serve a structural function, and the process itself can be compositionally compelling
