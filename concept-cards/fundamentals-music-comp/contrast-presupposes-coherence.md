# Quick Definition
A fundamental principle: contrasting sections must maintain connection to the whole through shared motivic and structural processes; incoherent contrast, though tolerated in descriptive music, is intolerable in well-organized form.

# Formal Definition
Contrast presupposes coherence. Incoherent contrast, though tolerated in "descriptive" music, is intolerable in a well-organized form. Contrasting sections, therefore, must utilize the same processes by which motive-forms are connected in simpler formulations. The contrast must arise from within the material itself, not from arbitrary juxtaposition of unrelated ideas.

# Construction/Recognition
When creating contrasts: (1) use forms of the basic motive that are not too remote from those of the A-section, (2) maintain coherence through shared metre, (3) derive new variations from the basic motive rather than introducing entirely new material, (4) ensure harmonic contrast is within closely related regions. The contrast should be felt as a different perspective on the same material, not as a foreign intrusion.

# Musical Context
This is one of Schoenberg's most fundamental principles, applicable at every level of musical form from the small ternary to the symphony. It reflects his view that musical coherence is the foundation upon which all other structural qualities depend. Without coherence, contrast becomes mere juxtaposition; with coherence, contrast becomes dialectical development.

# Examples
- Beethoven Op. 2/1-II: the B-section uses the dotted rhythm of the upbeat, frequent suspensions, and increasing sixteenth notes as connecting elements to the A-section
- All contrasting middle sections in the Beethoven sonata examples maintain motivic connection to their A-sections
- In Op. 27/1-III: the syncopations of m. 13-16 are connected to those in m. 6-7 and m. 9, maintaining coherence despite seeming contrast

# Common Confusions
- Contrast does not mean disconnection; the strongest contrasts are those that maintain deep structural connection
- "Incoherent contrast" is tolerated in descriptive music but not in abstract forms -- this is not an absolute prohibition but a contextual guideline
- Coherence in contrast is achieved through motivic processes, not through surface similarity
