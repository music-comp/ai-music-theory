# Quick Definition

The accompaniment contributes importantly to the character of the antecedent and should be incorporated from the very beginning of composition, using features such as detached/legato differentiation, rests, unharmonized upbeats, semi-contrapuntal middle voices, afterbeat harmony, and special rhythmic figures.

# Core Definition

Schoenberg insists that accompaniment features be employed from the very beginning of theme construction, not added as an afterthought. Envisioning a definite character helps stimulate inventiveness. The accompaniment makes important contributions to the expression of character (p. 23). Features include: differentiation between detached and legato notes; rests where harmonization is not required; unharmonized upbeats; semi-contrapuntal treatment of middle voices; afterbeat harmony; and special rhythmic figures. A complex accompaniment must be reconciled with good piano style, keeping everything within easy reach of the fingers and minimizing pedal dependence.

The composer must beware of (1) imbalance through overcrowding, (2) destruction of the character, and (3) obscuration of the harmonic progression. Fluent part-writing in the accompaniment does not endanger the clarity of the harmony, but control of the root progressions is essential (p. 23).

# Key Properties

1. Accompaniment should be conceived together with the theme, not as an afterthought
2. Character-defining features include detached/legato, rests, unharmonized upbeats, afterbeat harmony
3. Semi-contrapuntal middle voices add interest without full contrapuntal complexity
4. Must be reconciled with good piano style (within reach of fingers)
5. Must not overcrowd, destroy character, or obscure harmonic progression
6. Fluent part-writing does not endanger harmonic clarity
7. Control of root progressions is essential

# Construction / Recognition

1. Envision the character of the piece before beginning
2. Choose appropriate accompaniment features that express that character
3. Incorporate accompaniment from the first measure, not after the melody is complete
4. Use detached/legato differentiation to define character
5. Leave upbeats unharmonized when harmonic meaning is clear
6. Insert rests where harmonization is not required
7. Add semi-contrapuntal movement in middle voices for interest
8. Use afterbeat harmony or special rhythmic figures for rhythmic vitality
9. Ensure everything stays within easy reach of the fingers
10. Check that root progressions remain clear despite surface activity

# Context & Application

Schoenberg emphasizes that the accompaniment is not a separate problem to be solved after composing the melody. The character and mood of the piece emerge from the interaction of melody and accompaniment together. This principle applies from the earliest stages of composition, including practice forms.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Composing the melody first and adding accompaniment later
  **Correction**: Conceive accompaniment together with the theme from the start

- **Error**: Overcrowding the texture with complex accompaniment
  **Correction**: Maintain balance; too rich an accompaniment destroys character and obscures harmony

- **Error**: Writing accompaniment that requires heavy pedal use
  **Correction**: Good piano style should ignore the pedal as much as possible; keep everything within finger reach

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking accompaniment is secondary to melody
  **Clarification**: The accompaniment is an integral part of theme construction that contributes essentially to character and mood

- **Confusion**: Believing fluent part-writing risks harmonic confusion
  **Clarification**: Fluent part-writing does not endanger harmonic clarity; only poor root-progression control does
