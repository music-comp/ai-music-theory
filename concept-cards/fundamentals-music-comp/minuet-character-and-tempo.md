# Quick Definition
The minuet is characterised by moderate tempo, frequent harmonic change, restrained rhythmic accentuation, and a character ranging from singable to insistent.

# Formal Definition
The character and tempo of the minuet are generally moderate. Most minuets by Mozart and Haydn average dotted half-note = 60-70, though some are as slow as dotted half-note = 40-50. Beethoven's extreme markings of dotted half-note = 108 (First and Eighth Symphonies) are exceptional. Because of the moderate tempo, more small note values (eighths and sixteenths) appear in the minuet, and the harmony changes more frequently than in the scherzo. In the minuet, harmony seldom lasts longer than one or two measures, and often there are two or more harmonies within a single measure.

# Construction/Recognition
- Tempo: typically dotted half-note = 60-70 (moderate)
- Harmonic rhythm: changes every one to two measures, often multiple harmonies per measure
- Note values: frequent use of eighths and sixteenths due to slower tempo
- Rhythmic accentuation: restrained compared to scherzo and popular dances
- Accompaniment patterns: stylized rather than conventional dance patterns

# Musical Context
As a courtly dance of the eighteenth century, the minuet did not require the rhythmic emphasis of more popular dances. The conventional accompaniment patterns (such as those shown in Ex. 65) were probably never much used in composed minuets. The moderate character and tempo distinguish the minuet from the faster, more rhythmically driven scherzo.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 31/3-III: Unpretentiously singable character
- Mozart, Symphony No. 40 in g minor: Stubbornly insistent character
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 1, Minuet: Dotted half-note = 108 (extreme for a minuet)
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 8, Minuet: Dotted half-note = 108 (extreme for a minuet)

# Common Confusions
- Beethoven's fast metronome markings for some minuets are exceptional, not representative of the norm
- The frequent harmonic change in minuets distinguishes them from scherzos, where harmony often remains unchanged for several measures
- "Moderate" character encompasses a wide range, from gentle to forceful
