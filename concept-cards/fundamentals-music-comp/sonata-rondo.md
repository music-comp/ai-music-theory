# Quick Definition
A rondo form (aba-c'-aba) in which the central C-section functions as a modulatory elaboration (Durchfuhrung) rather than a trio, combining rondo's thematic return pattern with sonata-allegro developmental procedures.

# Core Definition
The sonata-rondo, with a modulatory c-section elaborating previous thematic elements, and the great sonata-rondo, which combines trio structure with the Durchfuhrung, call for treatment like the middle section of the sonata-allegro form. This section resembles the modulatory middle section of the scherzo but is usually more elaborately organized. The exposition and recapitulation need not differ from those already discussed, though greater complexity and more far-reaching modifications may arise.

# Prerequisites
- Rondo form characterization
- Large rondo form
- Elaboration (Durchfuhrung) technique

# Key Properties
- Pattern: aba-c'-aba (c' indicates elaboration/Durchfuhrung)
- C-section elaborates previously exposed thematic material in a modulatory manner
- C-section resembles the scherzo's modulatory middle section but more elaborate
- Exposition and recapitulation follow standard rondo procedure
- The C-section typically begins with material from the main theme, often in tonic minor
- Proceeds through a planned modulatory scheme
- Typically closes with a pedal point on V preparing the recapitulation
- Greater complexity and more far-reaching modifications possible than in simple rondos

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the exposition (aba): A in tonic, transition, B in contrasting key, return of A
2. Note the C-section using elaboration/Durchfuhrung technique rather than trio structure
3. Map the modulatory scheme of the C-section
4. Look for the pedal point on V preparing the recapitulation
5. Check the recapitulation for variations, lengthened transitions, and modified retransitions

# Context & Application
The sonata-rondo represents a synthesis of rondo and sonata principles. It is frequently used as a finale in sonatas and chamber works, providing the developmental depth of the sonata-allegro within the cheerful, return-based structure of the rondo. The ambiguity between trio and Durchfuhrung characteristics is sometimes a deliberate quality.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 31/1-III: C-section begins (m. 82) with main theme in left hand in tonic minor; modulatory scheme: g (m. 83), c (m. 91), Eb (m. 98), c (m. 106), unstable (m. 114), g (m. 121); pedal point on V (m. 129); recapitulation presents slight variations; final a recurs in m. 224, much expanded by rests and tempo changes; "ambiguity is sometimes a quality to be recognized, not necessarily to be explained away"
- Beethoven, Op. 22-IV: C-section (m. 72-111) begins like an etude-like trio in f (minor v); gives way (m. 81) to a modulatory-contrapuntal group derived from transitional material; combines trio character with Durchfuhrung procedures

# Common Errors
- Making the C-section too trio-like, undermining the developmental character
- Failing to plan a systematic modulatory scheme for the C-section
- Neglecting the pedal point or retransition preparing the recapitulation

# Common Confusions
- The sonata-rondo is distinguished from the large rondo by the developmental character of its C-section, not merely by its length
- The great sonata-rondo (aba-CC'-aba) combines both trio and Durchfuhrung, while the sonata-rondo proper has only the Durchfuhrung
- The C-section of Op. 22-IV illustrates the ambiguity between trio and Durchfuhrung characteristics
- "Ambiguity is sometimes a quality to be recognized, not necessarily to be explained away" (Schoenberg on Op. 31/1-III)
