# Quick Definition
A rondo form (aba-c'-aba) in which the central C-section functions as a modulatory elaboration (Durchfuhrung) rather than a trio, combining rondo principles with sonata-allegro developmental procedures.

# Formal Definition
The sonata-rondo, with a modulatory c-section elaborating previous thematic elements, and the great sonata-rondo, which combines trio structure with the Durchfuhrung, call for treatment like the middle section of the sonata-allegro form. This section resembles the modulatory middle section of the scherzo but is usually more elaborately organized. The exposition and recapitulation need not differ from those already discussed, though greater complexity and more far-reaching modifications may arise.

# Construction/Recognition
- Pattern: aba-c'-aba (where c' indicates elaboration/Durchfuhrung)
- The exposition (aba) follows standard rondo procedure: A in tonic, transition, B in contrasting key, return of A
- The C-section begins with material from the main theme (often in tonic minor) and proceeds as a modulatory elaboration
- The elaboration passes through a scheme of contrasting keys, often including tonic minor, subdominant minor, flat submediant, and unstable regions
- Closes with a pedal point on V preparing the recapitulation
- The recapitulation (aba) presents variations of setting, with transitions and retransitions somewhat lengthened

# Musical Context
The sonata-rondo represents a synthesis of rondo and sonata principles, inheriting the thematic return pattern of the rondo and the developmental procedures of the sonata-allegro. It is frequently used as a finale in sonatas and chamber works.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 31/1-III: C-section begins (m. 82) with main theme in left hand in tonic minor; modulatory scheme passes through g minor, c minor, Eb, c minor, unstable harmony, and back to g minor; pedal point on V (m. 129) prepares recapitulation; the final a recurs in m. 224, much expanded by rests and tempo changes
- Beethoven, Op. 22-IV: C-section (m. 72-111) begins like an etude-like trio in the unusual region of f (minor v); gives way (m. 81) to a modulatory-contrapuntal group; combines trio character with Durchfuhrung procedures

# Common Confusions
- The sonata-rondo is distinguished from the large rondo by the developmental character of its C-section, not merely by its length
- The great sonata-rondo (aba-CC'-aba) combines both trio and Durchfuhrung, while the sonata-rondo proper has only the Durchfuhrung
- The C-section of Op. 22-IV illustrates the ambiguity between trio and Durchfuhrung: it combines etude-like trio character with modulatory procedures, making classification difficult
- "Ambiguity is sometimes a quality to be recognized, not necessarily to be explained away" (Schoenberg on Op. 31/1-III)
