# Quick Definition
The necessary counterbalance to logic and coherence in musical form: parts of a piece differ in content, character, mood, tonality, size, and structure, presenting an idea from various viewpoints.

# Formal Definition
The parts of a piece differ more or less in content, character and mood, in tonality, size and structure. These differences permit presentation of an idea from various viewpoints, producing those contrasts on which variety is based. However, variety must never endanger comprehensibility or logic -- comprehensibility requires limitation of variety, especially when notes, harmonies, motive-forms, or contrasts follow each other in rapid succession.

# Construction/Recognition
Contrast is achieved through changes in any musical parameter: rhythm, melody, harmony, register, dynamics, texture. Variety is the cumulative effect of contrasts distributed through a piece. The composer must balance the need for variety against the need for coherence -- too much variety produces incoherence, too little produces monotony.

# Musical Context
The demands of logic, coherence, and comprehensibility must be fulfilled in relation to the need for contrast, variety, and fluency of presentation. Pieces in rapid tempo exhibit a lesser degree of variety because rapidity obstructs one's grasp of an idea. Delimitation, subdivision, and simple repetition are means to control the tendency toward too rapid development caused by disproportionate variety.

# Examples
- Dance forms achieve contrast through changes of metre, tempo, and rhythmic characteristics
- Sonata form achieves contrast through the complexity of interrelationships between parts
- In rapid tempos, variety is limited to maintain comprehensibility

# Common Confusions
- Contrast is not the opposite of coherence; both are necessary and must be balanced
- Variety does not mean changing everything -- changing every feature of a motive produces "something foreign, incoherent, illogical"
- More variety is not always better; rapid succession of contrasts can destroy comprehensibility
