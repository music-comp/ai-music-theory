# Quick Definition
The duration of a phrase, which varies within wide limits depending on metre and tempo, with two measures being average in compound metres and four measures normal in simple metres.

# Formal Definition
The length of a phrase may vary within wide limits. Metre and tempo have a great deal to do with phrase length. In compound metres a length of two measures may be considered the average; in simple metres a length of four measures is normal. In very slow tempos the phrase may be reduced to half a measure; in very rapid tempos eight measures or more may constitute a single phrase. The phrase is seldom an exact multiple of the measure length -- it usually varies by a beat or more.

# Construction/Recognition
Phrase length is determined by the interaction of metre, tempo, and musical content. The phrase nearly always crosses the metrical subdivisions rather than filling measures completely. There is no intrinsic reason for a phrase to be restricted to an even number of measures, but the consequences of irregularity are far-reaching and are treated separately in Chapter XIV.

# Musical Context
Understanding phrase length is essential for constructing balanced themes. In sentence and period structures, two-measure and four-measure phrases are the norm, but the composer must remain sensitive to how tempo affects the perceived length of a phrase. What constitutes "one phrase's worth" of musical content changes dramatically between adagio and presto.

# Examples
- Compound metres: two-measure phrases typical
- Simple metres: four-measure phrases typical
- Very slow tempos: phrases may be as short as half a measure
- Very rapid tempos: eight or more measures may form a single phrase

# Common Confusions
- Phrase length is not fixed at four measures; this is only the norm for simple metres at moderate tempos
- The phrase rarely aligns exactly with barlines -- it typically crosses metrical boundaries
- Even-numbered phrase lengths are conventional, not mandatory; irregular phrases are addressed in Chapter XIV
