# Quick Definition

The duration of a phrase, which varies within wide limits depending on metre and tempo: two measures average in compound metres, four measures normal in simple metres, with extremes from half a measure to eight or more.

# Core Definition

"The length of a phrase may vary within wide limits. Metre and tempo have a great deal to do with phrase-length. In compound metres a length of two measures may be considered the average; in simple metres a length of four measures is normal. But in very slow tempos the phrase may be reduced to half a measure; and in very rapid tempos eight measures or more may constitute a single phrase" (Ch. 2, pp. 3-4).

# Key Properties

1. Phrase length varies within wide limits
2. Metre and tempo are the primary determinants
3. Compound metres: two-measure phrases typical
4. Simple metres: four-measure phrases normal
5. Very slow tempos: phrases may be as short as half a measure
6. Very rapid tempos: eight or more measures may form a single phrase
7. Phrases are seldom exact multiples of the measure length
8. Phrases usually vary by a beat or more from measure boundaries
9. Phrases nearly always cross metrical subdivisions
10. Even-numbered phrase lengths are conventional, not mandatory

# Construction / Recognition

1. Consider the metre: compound metres favor shorter phrase-lengths; simple metres favor longer ones
2. Consider the tempo: slower tempos compress phrase-length; faster tempos expand it
3. Recognize that the phrase boundary is marked by punctuation (rhythmic reduction, melodic relaxation), not by barlines
4. Note that irregularity in phrase length has far-reaching consequences (treated in Chapter XIV)

# Context & Application

Understanding phrase length is essential for constructing balanced themes in sentence and period structures. The composer must remain sensitive to how tempo affects perceived phrase length: what constitutes "one phrase's worth" of musical content changes dramatically between adagio and presto. In sentence and period structures, two-measure and four-measure phrases are the norm, but these are guidelines, not rules.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming all phrases are exactly four measures long
  **Correction**: Four measures is normal only in simple metres at moderate tempos; phrase length varies with metre and tempo

- **Error**: Aligning phrase boundaries with barlines
  **Correction**: Phrases typically cross metrical boundaries; they "usually vary by a beat or more"

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Believing irregular phrase lengths are errors
  **Clarification**: "There is no intrinsic reason for a phrase to be restricted to an even number," though irregularity has consequences discussed in Chapter XIV

- **Confusion**: Confusing the number of measures with the amount of musical content
  **Clarification**: In very rapid tempos, eight measures may contain the same amount of content as two measures at a slow tempo
