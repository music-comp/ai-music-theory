# Quick Definition

The accompaniment should not be a mere addition but a functional complement to the theme, revealing its inherent harmony, establishing a unifying motus, and serving the tonality, rhythm, phrasing, contour, character, and mood.

# Core Definition

The accompaniment should not be a mere addition. It should be as functional as possible, and at best should act as a complement to the essentials of its subject: the tonality, rhythm, phrasing, contour, character, and mood. It should reveal the inherent harmony of the theme and establish a unifying motus (the regular subdivision of the measure). It should satisfy the necessities and exploit the resources of the instrument or group of instruments (Schoenberg, p. 51).

Accompaniment becomes imperative if the harmony or rhythm is complicated. In descriptive music, the accompaniment contributes much to the expressive sonority.

# Prerequisites

- **Motive** -- The accompaniment relates to the theme's motivic content

# Key Properties

1. Should complement the theme's tonality, rhythm, phrasing, contour, character, and mood
2. Should reveal the harmony inherent in the theme
3. Should establish and maintain motus (regular rhythmic subdivision)
4. Should satisfy instrumental necessities and exploit instrumental resources
5. Becomes mandatory for harmonically or rhythmically complicated passages
6. Must avoid: imbalance through overcrowding, destruction of character, obscuration of harmonic progression
7. Organized through its own motive (the motive of the accompaniment)

# Construction / Recognition

1. Determine what the theme needs harmonically, rhythmically, and expressively
2. Choose an accompaniment type suited to the character and instrument
3. Establish a motive of the accompaniment for textural unity
4. Reveal the harmony inherent in the theme
5. Maintain motus (regular beat subdivision) through the accompaniment's activity
6. Avoid overcrowding, character destruction, and harmonic obscuration
7. Keep everything within instrumental capability

# Context & Application

Schoenberg treats the accompaniment as an integral component of composition, not a secondary concern. The accompaniment's organization parallels that of a theme: it requires its own motive and must be adapted to changing needs. The quality of the accompaniment significantly affects the character, mood, and intelligibility of the composition. Piano writing should ignore the pedal as much as possible, keeping everything within easy reach of the fingers.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating the accompaniment as mere harmonic filler
  **Correction**: It must be functionally organized with its own motivic identity

- **Error**: Adding the accompaniment as an afterthought
  **Correction**: The accompaniment should be conceived together with the theme

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking "functional" means "complex"
  **Clarification**: Sometimes the most functional accompaniment is simple and transparent

- **Confusion**: Equating the absence of accompaniment with a compositional deficiency
  **Clarification**: Strategic omission of accompaniment is itself a compositional technique
