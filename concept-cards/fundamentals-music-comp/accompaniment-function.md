# Quick Definition
The accompaniment should not be a mere addition but a functional complement to the theme, revealing its inherent harmony, establishing a unifying motus, and serving the tonality, rhythm, phrasing, contour, character, and mood of the subject.

# Formal Definition
The accompaniment should be as functional as possible, acting as a complement to the essentials of its subject: the tonality, rhythm, phrasing, contour, character, and mood. It should reveal the inherent harmony of the theme and establish a unifying motus (the regular subdivision of the measure). It should satisfy the necessities and exploit the resources of the instrument or group of instruments. Accompaniment becomes imperative when the harmony or rhythm is complicated. In descriptive music, the accompaniment contributes much to the expressive sonority.

# Construction/Recognition
- Must complement, not merely decorate, the principal voice
- Reveals the harmony inherent in the theme
- Establishes and maintains motus (regular rhythmic subdivision)
- Should satisfy instrumental necessities and exploit instrumental resources
- Becomes mandatory for harmonically or rhythmically complicated passages
- Must avoid: imbalance through overcrowding, destruction of character, obscuration of harmonic progression

# Musical Context
Schoenberg treats the accompaniment not as a secondary concern but as an integral component of thematic composition. The accompaniment's organization parallels that of a theme: it requires its own motive (the motive of the accompaniment) and must be adapted to the changing needs of the music. The quality of the accompaniment significantly affects the character, mood, and intelligibility of the composition.

# Examples
- Beethoven piano sonatas provide the primary repertoire for accompaniment study
- The accompaniment makes important contributions to character expression through features like detached/legato differentiation, rests, and special rhythmic figures

# Common Confusions
- The accompaniment is not a mere harmonic filler; it must be functionally organized with its own motivic identity.
- "Functional" does not mean complex; sometimes the most functional accompaniment is simple and transparent.
- The accompaniment should be conceived together with the theme, not added as an afterthought.
