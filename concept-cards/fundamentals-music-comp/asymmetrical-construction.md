# Quick Definition

A period or sentence divided into segments of unequal length (e.g., 4+6 or 5+7), producing a deliberate structural imbalance between its parts, distinct from merely uneven measure counts.

# Core Definition

An asymmetrical period is one divided into segments of unequal length. This is distinct from symmetrical periods whose segments, though possibly of odd length, are equal to each other. Asymmetrical construction may arise from the length of basic units (motives or phrases), the number of such units, or the combination of units of differing lengths. Schoenberg emphasizes that asymmetry refers to the relationship between parts, not to the total measure count -- a 5+7 period is asymmetrical despite having an even total of twelve measures.

# Prerequisites

- **Period structure**: Understanding of antecedent-consequent construction is required to recognize how segments relate
- **Sentence structure**: Sentences are more frequently composed of elements of differing lengths than periods

# Key Properties

- Refers specifically to unequal division between parts (e.g., 4+6), not to odd totals
- A 5+5 period is symmetrical despite odd segment length; a 4+6 period is asymmetrical despite even total
- Sentences more frequently exhibit asymmetry than periods
- Master composers introduce asymmetry deliberately, contributing fluency and spontaneity
- Never arbitrary or casual -- requires high skill and sensitivity to achieve balance and proportion

# Construction / Recognition

1. Identify whether the period divides into equal or unequal halves
2. A 4+6 division is asymmetrical (unequal halves)
3. A 5+5 division is symmetrical despite odd segment length
4. A 5+7 period is asymmetrical with uneven segments yet has an even total (12 measures)
5. In sentences, check whether elements have differing lengths (e.g., 4+5, 2+2+5)

# Context & Application

Asymmetrical construction is one of three related but distinct concepts Schoenberg discusses in Chapter 14. While uneven phrase lengths concern the absolute length of segments (odd vs. even), and irregular construction concerns internal complications (stretching, additions), asymmetry specifically addresses the proportional relationship between the halves of a period or sentence. It becomes more common in later nineteenth-century music.

# Common Errors

- Confusing asymmetry with odd total measure count (a 5+5 period is symmetrical)
- Treating asymmetrical construction as a defect rather than a deliberate compositional choice
- Failing to distinguish asymmetry (proportional imbalance) from irregularity (internal complications)

# Common Confusions

- **Asymmetrical vs. uneven**: Uneven refers to odd-numbered segments; asymmetrical refers to unequal halves. A 3+3 period is uneven but symmetrical.
- **Asymmetrical vs. irregular**: Irregular refers to internal complications (stretching, additions, evasions); asymmetrical refers to the proportional relationship between halves.
