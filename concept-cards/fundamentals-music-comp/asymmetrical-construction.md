# Quick Definition
Asymmetrical construction occurs when a period or sentence is divided into unequal parts, such as a 4+6 or 5+7 measure grouping, producing a deliberate imbalance in phrase structure.

# Formal Definition
An asymmetrical period is one divided into segments of unequal length. This contrasts with symmetrical periods whose segments, though possibly uneven in total measure count, are divisible into equal halves. Asymmetrical construction may arise from the length of basic units (motives or phrases), the number of such units, or the combination of units of differing lengths.

# Construction/Recognition
- Identify whether the period divides into equal or unequal halves
- A 4+6 division is asymmetrical (unequal halves)
- A 5+5 division is symmetrical despite the odd number of measures per segment
- A 5+7 period is asymmetrical with uneven segments, yet comprises an even total (12 measures)
- Sentences are more frequently composed of elements of differing lengths than periods

# Musical Context
Asymmetrical construction is distinct from merely uneven measure counts. A period of 10 measures (5+5) is symmetrical, while one of 10 measures (4+6) is asymmetrical. Master composers freely introduce asymmetrical procedures as demanded by the musical idea or structure, contributing fluency and spontaneity -- though such procedures are never arbitrary.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Exs. 45a, 47a, 59b, c, f: Asymmetrical periods divided 4+6
- Ex. 46c: Asymmetrical period of 5+7, with uneven segments but even total measure count
- Ex. 57: Sentence constructed as 4+5
- Ex. 61c: Sentence of 4(2+2)+5(1+1+1+2)

# Common Confusions
- Asymmetrical does not simply mean "odd number of measures" -- a 5+5 period is symmetrical despite having an odd segment length
- Asymmetry refers specifically to the relationship between parts, not to the total measure count
- Asymmetrical construction is deliberate and controlled, not arbitrary or casual
