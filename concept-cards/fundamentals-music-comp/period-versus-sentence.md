# Quick Definition
The two fundamental structures for articulating a complete musical idea or theme, similar in that both center around a tonic and have definite endings, but distinguished by their treatment of the second phrase and the continuation after it.

# Formal Definition
A complete musical idea or theme is customarily articulated as a period or a sentence. These structures usually appear in classical music as parts of larger forms (e.g., as the "a" in aba form), but occasionally are independent (e.g., in strophic songs). They are similar in two respects: they centre around a tonic, and they have a definite ending. In the simplest cases, these structures consist of an even number of measures, usually eight or a multiple of eight.

# Construction/Recognition
The distinction between sentence and period lies in:
- **The treatment of the second phrase**: in the sentence, the second phrase is an immediate repetition (exact or varied) of the first; in the period, the second phrase responds to the first with contrasting material
- **The continuation after the second phrase**: sentence and period follow different patterns of continuation and cadential closure

Both structures typically span 8 measures (or multiples: 16, or in very rapid tempos, 32 where two or four measures equal the content of one).

# Musical Context
Period and sentence are the two basic templates for theme construction in classical music. Understanding their distinction is essential for analyzing and composing themes. The sentence is characterized by immediate repetition of its opening material, while the period presents a more contrasting relationship between its two halves.

# Common Confusions
- Period and sentence are not interchangeable terms; they describe fundamentally different structural approaches
- Both can be eight measures long; the distinction is not about length but about internal organization
- "Period" in Schoenberg's usage has a specific technical meaning distinct from its use in everyday language
- Very rapid tempos can make 16 or 32 measures function as the equivalent of 8
