# Quick Definition

Sentences from masterworks often deviate from the eight-measure practice form, with irregularities caused by remote motive-forms requiring extra repetitions, connection of units of unequal size, insertions, or interpolations.

# Core Definition

As a practice form is only an abstraction from art forms, sentences from masterworks often differ considerably from the eight-measure scheme. The most obvious deviation lies in the number of measures -- there may be either more or less than eight. Extensions may result from: (1) remote motive-forms whose establishment demands more than a single repetition; (2) connection of units of unequal size (e.g., one measure plus two measures); (3) insertions or interpolations (Schoenberg, pp. 41-43).

The best method to discover the cause of an extension is to test which measures could be omitted to restore the basic form. The omittable measures reveal the cause and nature of the extension. It is characteristic of Mozart's Rococo technique to produce irregularity within the phrasing through interpolation of incidental repetitions of small segments, residues of a preceding phrase (p. 42).

All more complicated structures can be related to the practice form by generalizing the concept: the tonic- and dominant-forms (or any comparable twofold statement) establish the idea and tonality; after such an adequate establishment, remotely varied motive-forms may appear at once without endangering comprehensibility. The appropriate treatment of remote forms through varied or sequential repetitions justifies irregularity in length and unusual harmonization (p. 43).

# Key Properties

1. Practice form is an abstraction; real music regularly deviates from it
2. Extensions caused by remote motive-forms needing extra repetitions
3. Extensions caused by connection of unequal units (e.g., 1+2 measures)
4. Extensions caused by insertions or interpolations
5. Omission test: determine which measures could be omitted to restore eight measures
6. Overlapping: end of one sentence may coincide with beginning of the next
7. After adequate twofold statement, remote forms may appear freely
8. Sentences may have fewer than eight measures (Mozart Figaro: seven measures)

# Construction / Recognition

## To Analyze Sentence Irregularities

1. Identify the basic twofold statement (tonic form/dominant form or equivalent)
2. Locate the continuation/development section
3. Test which measures could be omitted to restore the eight-measure practice form
4. The omittable measures reveal the cause of the extension
5. Check for overlapping (one section's end coinciding with the next's beginning)
6. Consider Mozart's technique of interpolating incidental repetitions

## Generalized Sentence Concept

After an adequate establishment (twofold statement, even if incomplete), remotely varied motive-forms may appear at once without endangering comprehensibility. The appropriate treatment through varied or sequential repetitions then justifies irregularity in length and unusual harmonization.

# Context & Application

Understanding irregularities is essential for analyzing real music. The generalized sentence concept allows for great flexibility: once the idea and tonality are established, the composer is free to pursue remote developments. This principle is further developed in Chapter 14 on uneven, irregular, and asymmetrical construction.

# Examples

- **Mozart, Overture to The Marriage of Figaro** (Ex. 59f, p. 42): Only seven measures

- **Ex. 53b** (p. 41): Twelve measures, caused by a remote variation in m. 5 whose repetitions extend the sentence

- **Mozart, Ex. 59a** (p. 42): After two one-measure phrases in m. 5-6, a three-measure segment with varied repetition; omitting m. 10-12 reduces to ten; omitting m. 7-11 restores eight measures (revealing an insertion)

- **Ex. 59d** (p. 42): Nine measures minimum; extension from sequence in m. 6-7; end overlaps with beginning of repetition

- **Schubert, Ex. 60b** (p. 43): Repetition (m. 9-16) ends on subdominant instead of expected tonic; recapitulation starts on subdominant but ends on tonic, creating scheme I-IV : IV-I

- **Ex. 60f** (p. 43): Omitting m. 7-11 and m. 13 would reduce to eight-measure practice form

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming all sentences must be exactly eight measures
  **Correction**: Eight measures is a practice form; real music regularly deviates

- **Error**: Failing to test which measures could be omitted to find the cause of extension
  **Correction**: The omission test is the best method for understanding irregularities

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking irregular length is a compositional defect
  **Clarification**: Irregularity results from legitimate compositional needs and is a feature of masterworks

- **Confusion**: Believing overlapping is an analytical error
  **Clarification**: Overlapping (one section's end coinciding with the next's beginning) is a genuine structural phenomenon

- **Confusion**: Extension vs. irregularity
  **Clarification**: Extension adds material through repetition, stretching, or addition; irregularity is the broader category that includes extensions plus other departures from the norm
