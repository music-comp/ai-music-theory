# Quick Definition
Sentences from masterworks often deviate from the eight-measure practice form, with irregularities caused by remote motive-forms requiring extra repetitions, connection of units of unequal size, insertions, or interpolations.

# Formal Definition
As a practice form is only an abstraction from art forms, sentences from masterworks often differ considerably from the eight-measure scheme. Extensions may result from: (1) remote motive-forms whose establishment demands more than a single repetition; (2) connection of units of unequal size (e.g., one measure plus two measures); (3) insertions or interpolations of incidental segments. The best method to discover the cause of an extension is to test which measures could be omitted to restore the basic form. Mozart's Rococo technique frequently produces irregularity through interpolation of incidental repetitions of small segments or residues.

# Construction/Recognition
- Test which measures could be omitted to reduce the example to the eight-measure practice form
- The omittable measures reveal the cause and nature of the extension
- Remote motive-forms may require extra repetitions for comprehensibility
- Overlapping: the end of one sentence may coincide with the beginning of a repetition
- After adequate establishment (twofold statement), remotely varied motive-forms may appear freely
- Sequential treatment within the continuation may produce extension (extra measures from the sequence)

# Musical Context
Understanding irregularities is essential for analyzing real music as opposed to practice forms. The generalized concept of the sentence allows for flexibility: after an adequate establishment of the idea and tonality through a twofold statement, remotely varied motive-forms may appear without endangering comprehensibility. The appropriate treatment of these remote forms through varied or sequential repetitions justifies irregularity in length and unusual harmonization.

# Examples
- Mozart, Overture to The Marriage of Figaro (Ex. 59f): Only seven measures
- Ex. 53b: Twelve measures, caused by a remote variation in m. 5 whose repetitions extend the sentence
- Ex. 59a (Mozart): After two one-measure phrases in m. 5-6, a three-measure segment appears with varied repetition; omitting m. 10-12 would reduce to ten measures; omitting m. 7-11 would restore eight measures
- Ex. 59b: M. 5-6 could be omitted
- Ex. 59c: Omitting m. 7-8 reduces ten measures to eight
- Ex. 59d: Nine measures minimum, with extension produced by sequence in m. 6-7
- Ex. 60f: Omitting m. 7-11 and m. 13 would reduce to eight-measure practice form

# Common Confusions
- Irregular length is not a defect; it results from legitimate compositional needs (establishment of remote material, sequential extension, etc.).
- The practice form is a pedagogical abstraction, not a prescription; real music rarely conforms exactly to the model.
- Overlapping (where one sentence's end coincides with the next section's beginning) is a genuine structural phenomenon, not a mistake in analysis.
