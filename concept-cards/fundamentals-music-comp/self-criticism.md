# Quick Definition
The disciplined practice of evaluating one's own compositions for errors and weaknesses, essential for all composers regardless of talent, using the ear as the primary tool.

# Core Definition
The sense of melody enables one who has it to do the right thing at once without the intervention of self-criticism, but even a master may stray onto the wrong track. When such a deviation occurs, one has to discover where and why one erred, and which is the right track. Self-criticism is therefore necessary to a composer, gifted or not. The best tool of a musician is his ear. Technical advice can be formulated more easily in the negative than in the positive.

# Prerequisites
None. Self-criticism is a foundational practice applicable at all levels.

# Key Properties
- Necessary for all composers, gifted or not
- Even masters may stray onto the wrong track
- The ear is the musician's best tool
- Technical advice is more easily formulated negatively (what to avoid)
- Self-criticism involves seven systematic steps: listen, analyse, eliminate non-essentials, avoid monotony, watch the bass line, make many sketches, watch the harmony
- Every good musician must possess the "inner ear" (auditory imagery)
- A beginner should consider everything written as tentative

# Construction / Recognition
Self-criticism involves systematic evaluation through: (1) listening -- playing harmony and melody separately, (2) analysing -- identifying significant features and whether they are developed, (3) eliminating non-essentials, (4) avoiding monotony, (5) watching the bass line, (6) making many sketches, and (7) watching harmony, root progressions, and bass line.

# Context & Application
Self-criticism is the practical complement to aesthetic and technical knowledge. A beginner who is not too self-assured and who knows technical maturity has not yet been reached will consider everything written as tentative. Later, the composer will be able to base composing exclusively on the sense of form. The student should frequently review methods of motive variation as a "warm-up."

# Common Errors
- Excessive self-assurance that prevents critical evaluation
- Relying solely on inspiration without systematic checking
- Applying self-criticism destructively rather than constructively

# Common Confusions
- Self-criticism is not self-doubt; it is a disciplined analytical practice that even masters require
- Technical advice is more easily formulated negatively (what to avoid) than positively; this is a feature of compositional pedagogy, not a limitation
