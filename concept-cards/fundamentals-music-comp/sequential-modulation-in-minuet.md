# Quick Definition
The technique of moving through keys by transposing a motivic pattern to successive degrees, used as the chief modulatory device in the contrasting middle section of the minuet.

# Formal Definition
Sequential modulation is the process of transposing a clearly delimited motivic segment (pattern) through a series of harmonic degrees, thereby effecting a modulation. In the minuet's contrasting middle section, this is the primary means of harmonic movement. The pattern is typically a phrase of two to four measures, derived from A-section material but modified for modulatory purposes. The sequence follows a logical harmonic plan, often progressing through the circle of fifths.

# Construction/Recognition
- A clearly delimited pattern is established (usually 2-4 measures)
- The pattern is transposed to successive degrees (e.g., iii-vi-II-V)
- Motive-forms from the A-section are adapted to the modulatory procedure
- The order of motive-forms may be changed, and some may be repeated within the segment
- The end of the sequential process requires neutralization through liquidation before the recapitulation

# Musical Context
Sequential modulation is the most characteristic feature of the minuet's B-section, shared with many scherzos and more developed forms. The technique provides both structural clarity (through the repetition inherent in the sequence) and harmonic movement (through the transposition). After the sequential modulation, the harmony typically settles on the dominant via a pedal point to prepare for the recapitulation.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 10/3-III: Two-measure phrase appears four times, moving through the circle of fifths (iii-vi-II-V)
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1-III: Sequential modulation leads to the subdominant region
- Mozart, String Quartet in A, K.V. 464: Sequence of motive b accompanied by the interval of a fourth derived by reduction from both a and b

# Common Confusions
- Sequential modulation is not mere transposition -- it involves genuine movement through keys, not just pitch displacement
- The sequence need not be exact; variation of the sequence is common and desirable
- Remotely varied motive-forms are dangerous in sequential passages because the modulation already introduces instability
