# Quick Definition

The progressive shortening of phrase units in the continuation of a sentence, from two-measure phrases to one-measure units to half-measure residues, supporting the process of liquidation and driving toward cadential closure.

# Core Definition

The liquidation is generally supported by a shortening of the phrase. Two-measure phrases are reduced or condensed to one measure; four-measure phrases may condense to two measures. This procedure sometimes results in still smaller units: half-measures or even shorter fragments (Schoenberg, p. 39). Schoenberg distinguishes between reduction (merely omitting part of the model) and condensation (compressing the content of the model, whereby even the order of features may be somewhat changed) (p. 39, footnote 1).

# Prerequisites

- **Beginning the sentence** -- Phrase condensation occurs in the sentence continuation following the opening
- **Liquidation** -- Phrase condensation supports and accompanies the liquidation process

# Key Properties

1. Progressive shortening: two-measure to one-measure to half-measure units
2. Reduction: straightforward omission of material from the model
3. Condensation: compression that may reorder surviving features
4. Creates a sense of acceleration even at constant tempo
5. Accompaniment phrasing often confirms the condensation
6. Works alongside motivic liquidation and harmonic concentration

# Construction / Recognition

## To Apply Phrase Condensation

1. Begin with the established phrase length (typically two or four measures)
2. In the next statement, reduce or condense to half the length
3. Continue shortening to one measure or half-measure units
4. Coordinate with liquidation of characteristic features
5. Support with cadential harmony

## To Recognize Phrase Condensation

1. Track phrase lengths through the sentence continuation
2. Identify progressive shortening patterns (4->2, 2->1, 1->1/2)
3. Confirm with accompaniment phrasing if available
4. Note whether reduction (omission) or condensation (compression/reordering) is used

# Context & Application

Phrase condensation creates a sense of acceleration and intensification even when the tempo remains constant. By progressively shortening the units of thematic discourse, the composer creates momentum toward the cadence. This process is one of the most reliable indicators of an approaching cadence in sentence-form themes.

# Examples

- **Exs. 52a, b** (p. 39): Two-measure phrases reduced to one measure in m. 5-6; Ex. 52b continues to half-measure units

- **Ex. 52c** (p. 39): Four measures condensed to two (m. 9-10 and 11-12), then to one measure

- **Ex. 61b** (Brahms, p. 43): One-measure phrases of m. 3-4 reduced to half-measure residues in m. 7-8, as confirmed by accompaniment phrasing

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Shortening phrases abruptly without progressive stages
  **Correction**: The shortening should be gradual (4->2->1->1/2), not sudden

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Equating condensation with reduction
  **Clarification**: Condensation compresses content and may reorder features; reduction simply omits material

- **Confusion**: Thinking phrase condensation is just writing shorter phrases
  **Clarification**: It involves progressive shortening from an established longer unit, creating the impression of acceleration
