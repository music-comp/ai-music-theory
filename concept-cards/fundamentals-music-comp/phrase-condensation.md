# Quick Definition
The progressive shortening of phrase units in the continuation of a sentence, from two-measure phrases to one-measure units to half-measure residues, which supports the process of liquidation and drives toward cadential closure.

# Formal Definition
Phrase condensation is the progressive shortening of structural units that generally supports liquidation in the sentence continuation. Two-measure phrases are reduced or condensed to one measure; four-measure phrases may condense to two measures. This procedure sometimes results in still smaller units: half-measures or even shorter fragments. Schoenberg distinguishes between reduction (merely omitting part of the model) and condensation (compressing the content of the model, whereby even the order of features may be somewhat changed).

# Construction/Recognition
- Two-measure phrases reduced to one measure (Exs. 52a, b, m. 5-6)
- Four-measure phrases condensed to two measures (Ex. 52c, m. 9-10, 11-12)
- Further condensation to half-measure units (Ex. 52b) or one-measure units (Ex. 52c)
- Reduction: straightforward omission of material from the model
- Condensation: compression that may reorder the surviving features
- Accompaniment phrasing often confirms the condensation (Ex. 61b)

# Musical Context
Phrase condensation creates a sense of acceleration and intensification even when the tempo remains constant. By progressively shortening the units of thematic discourse, the composer creates momentum toward the cadence. This process works alongside motivic liquidation (elimination of characteristic features) and harmonic concentration to produce effective structural closure. The shortening of phrase units is one of the most reliable indicators of an approaching cadence in sentence-form themes.

# Examples
- Exs. 52a, b: Two-measure phrases reduced to one measure in m. 5-6; Ex. 52b continues to half-measure units
- Ex. 52c: Four measures condensed to two (m. 9-10 and 11-12), then to one measure
- Ex. 61b: One-measure phrases of m. 3-4 reduced to half-measure residues in m. 7-8, as confirmed by accompaniment phrasing

# Common Confusions
- Condensation is not the same as reduction: condensation compresses content and may reorder features, while reduction simply omits material.
- Phrase condensation is not the same as simply writing shorter phrases; it involves the progressive shortening from an established longer unit.
- The condensation process creates the impression of acceleration without actual tempo change.
