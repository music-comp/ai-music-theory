# Quick Definition
The expressive range of the scherzo extends far beyond the "playful and humorous" of its etymological meaning, encompassing characters from sparkling and witty to tragic, heroic, and diabolical.

# Formal Definition
The restriction of the scherzo to the playful and humorous is not factual. Even the tempo is not decisive -- Beethoven's own metronome indications for rapid middle movements range around dotted quarter-note = 100, which is his average scherzo tempo. However, these middle movements, with only the rapid tempo in common, differ very widely in expressive qualities. A survey of scherzos by Beethoven, Schubert, Mendelssohn, Schumann, Chopin, Brahms, Tchaikovsky, Berlioz, Bruckner, Mahler, Reger, Debussy, and Ravel reveals an enormous range of character.

# Construction/Recognition
- Identify the character through tempo markings, dynamics, articulation, and texture
- Rapid tempo is the common element, but expressive quality varies enormously
- Beethoven seldom uses the title "Scherzo" if the movement is in minor
- Among Brahms's eight scherzos from chamber music, five are in minor
- Mode, metre, and tempo alone do not determine whether a movement is a scherzo

# Musical Context
The character of the scherzo evolved significantly from its origins. While early scherzos tended toward lighter moods, later composers (especially Beethoven in his later works, and Brahms, Bruckner, and Mahler) expanded the expressive range dramatically. The scherzo became a vehicle for some of the most intense and complex expression in the symphonic repertoire.

# Examples
- Character types found in scherzos: vivacious, sparkling, brilliant, witty, enthusiastic, ecstatic, ardent, fiery, energetic, vehement, impassioned, dramatic, tragic, heroic, gigantesque, diabolical, grotesque
- Beethoven, String Trio Op. 9/3: Scherzo in minor (Beethoven rarely titles minor movements "Scherzo")
- Brahms: Five of eight chamber music scherzos are in minor

# Common Confusions
- "Scherzo" does not mean the movement must be playful or humorous
- The mode (major/minor) does not define whether a movement is a scherzo
- Tempo alone is insufficient to distinguish a scherzo from other rapid movements
