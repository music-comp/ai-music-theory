# Quick Definition
A family of forms characterized by the repetition of one or more themes separated by intervening contrasts, ranging from simple andante forms (aba) to the great sonata-rondo (aba-CC'-aba), with classification based on the number and position of parts rather than actual length.

# Core Definition
The rondo forms are characterized by the repetition of one or more themes, separated by intervening contrasts. The ternary form (minuet-trio-minuet, scherzo-trio-scherzo) is the prototype of this kind of organization. The structural elements of these forms can be simple and short, or compound and long. There are transitions, codettas, episodes, etc., as described in the preceding chapter. Each of the larger parts may consist of several segments. The classification of the form is based on the number and position of the parts, not on the actual length of the piece.

# Key Properties
- Repetition of one or more themes separated by contrasts
- Classification based on number and position of parts, not length
- A-section repetitions almost exclusively in the tonic region
- B-section transposed to tonic region if it recurs in the recapitulation
- Structural elements can be simple/short or compound/long
- The hierarchy: andante forms, small rondos, large rondo, sonata-rondo, great sonata-rondo
- Large rondo forms generally express dance-song character: moderate or rapid tempo, cheerful/playful/brilliant mood

# Construction / Recognition
The hierarchy of rondo forms:
1. **Andante forms**: aba and abab (slow/moderate tempo)
2. **Small rondo forms**: ababa and abaca (relatively infrequent as independent forms)
3. **Large rondo form**: aba-c-aba (with trio C)
4. **Sonata-rondo**: aba-c'-aba (with Durchfuhrung C')
5. **Great sonata-rondo**: aba-CC'-aba (with both trio and Durchfuhrung)

# Context & Application
The classical composers frequently used rondo forms as final movements in cyclic works (sonatas, string quartets, symphonies). Andante forms serve slow movements. The increasing complexity of the hierarchy provides forms suitable for movements of varying weight and character.

# Examples
- Beethoven's Fourth and Seventh Symphonies: double repetition of the scherzo produces ababa form
- Schumann: adding a second trio (First and Second Symphonies, String Quartet in a, Piano Quintet) produces abaca form
- The ternary form minuet-trio-minuet and scherzo-trio-scherzo are prototypes of rondo organization

# Common Errors
- Classifying rondo type by length rather than by number and position of parts
- Failing to maintain the tonic region for A-section returns
- Neglecting variation of the returning A-section, producing monotonous repetition

# Common Confusions
- The classification is based on the number and position of parts, not the actual length; a very short piece may have the same formal structure as a very long one
- The ternary form (aba) is the prototype of rondo organization, but calling simple aba forms "rondos" is acknowledged as perhaps an exaggeration
- The hierarchy of rondo types represents increasing structural complexity, not merely increasing length
