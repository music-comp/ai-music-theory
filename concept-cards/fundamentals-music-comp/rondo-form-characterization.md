# Quick Definition
A family of forms characterized by the repetition of one or more themes separated by intervening contrasts, ranging from simple andante forms (aba) to the great sonata-rondo (aba-CC'-aba).

# Formal Definition
The rondo forms are characterized by the repetition of one or more themes, separated by intervening contrasts. The ternary form (minuet-trio-minuet, scherzo-trio-scherzo) is the prototype of this kind of organization. The structural elements of these forms can be simple and short, or compound and long. There are transitions, codettas, episodes, etc. The classification of the form is based on the number and position of the parts, not on the actual length of the piece.

# Construction/Recognition
The hierarchy of rondo forms:
1. **Andante forms**: aba and abab
2. **Smaller rondo forms**: ababa and abaca
3. **Large rondo form**: aba-c-aba (with trio)
4. **Sonata-rondo**: aba-c'-aba (with Durchfuhrung)
5. **Great sonata-rondo**: aba-CC'-aba (with both trio and Durchfuhrung)

Key structural principles:
- Repetitions of the A-section are almost exclusively in the tonic region
- The B-section, which initially appears in a contrasting region, is transposed to the tonic region if it recurs in the recapitulation
- Each of the larger parts may consist of several segments

# Musical Context
The classical composers frequently used rondo forms as final movements in cyclic works (sonatas, string quartets, symphonies). The large rondo forms generally express the character of the dance-song, with moderate or rapid rate of movement and cheerful, playful, or brilliant mood.

# Examples
- Beethoven's Fourth and Seventh Symphonies: double repetition of the scherzo produces ababa form
- Schumann: adding a second trio (First and Second Symphonies, String Quartet in a, Piano Quintet) produces abaca form

# Common Confusions
- The classification is based on the number and position of parts, not the actual length; a very short piece may have the same formal structure as a very long one
- The ternary form (aba) is the prototype of rondo organization, but calling simple aba forms "rondos" is acknowledged as perhaps an exaggeration
- The hierarchy of rondo types represents increasing structural complexity, not merely increasing length
