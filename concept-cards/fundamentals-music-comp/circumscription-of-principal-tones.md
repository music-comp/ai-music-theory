# Quick Definition
The technique of embellishing the principal (essential) tones of a theme with neighbouring tones, scale lines, or broken chords, creating the melodic content of a variation while preserving the theme's underlying structure.

# Formal Definition
In the motives for at least the first few variations, it is common practice to circumscribe the principal tones with neighbouring tones. Often tones of the theme are included in parts of scale lines or broken chords. Such elements must fit the expressed harmony. The principal tones may appear at the same point in the figure (more primitive treatment) or be distributed more freely within the half-measure or measure (more fluent and varied treatment). The rhythmic displacement of principal tones relative to their position in the theme is a significant feature of this technique.

# Construction/Recognition
- Principal tones of the theme are embellished with upper and lower neighbours
- Scale lines and broken chords may connect or surround principal tones
- All circumscribing elements must fit the expressed harmony
- Primitive treatment: principal tones always appear at the same point in the figure
- More sophisticated treatment: principal tones are distributed freely, even rhythmically displaced
- The technique preserves the theme's tonal outline while adding melodic interest

# Musical Context
Circumscription is the most fundamental technique of melodic variation. It operates at the intersection of the theme's skeleton and the motive of variation, generating the surface melody of each variation. The technique appears not only in variation sets but also in thematic construction generally -- the Eroica Scherzo theme, for example, is described as a circumscription of the ascending tonic and descending dominant.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Diabelli Variations: A considerable number of variations (2, 9, 11, 12, 14, 18, 28) derive the motive of variation from circumscription of the principal tones; rhythmic displacement of principal tones is notable
- Ex. 126b: Melody notes circumscribed with appoggiaturas and intervening sixteenth-notes providing continuous movement; main notes always at the same point (primitive treatment)
- Ex. 126c: More fluency and variety by avoiding mechanical reiteration; main notes freely distributed within the half-measure
- Ex. 126d: Even greater variety using notes of various lengths
- Beethoven, Eroica Scherzo (Ex. 121): Theme is a circumscription of ascending tonic and descending dominant

# Common Confusions
- Circumscription is not arbitrary ornamentation -- it is disciplined elaboration of specific principal tones
- The principal tones need not appear on the same beats as in the theme; rhythmic displacement is common
- Circumscription must always fit the expressed harmony, not merely the melodic outline
