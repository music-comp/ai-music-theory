# Quick Definition
A transition is a connecting passage between principal and subordinate themes that serves both to introduce a contrast and to be itself a contrast.

# Formal Definition
The purpose of a transition is not only to introduce a contrast; it is, itself, a contrast. It may begin after the end of the main theme with new thematic formulations, or the end of the main theme may be modified thematically and harmonically into a connecting segment. Transitions appear at various places in larger forms: between principal and subordinate themes (modulating to a different region), as retransitions (returning to the tonic), or in the recapitulation (reformulated as a roundabout way from tonic to tonic).

# Construction/Recognition
A transition is recognized by its modulatory character, its position between stable thematic sections, and its use of liquidation and motival reduction. It belongs to the group of subsidiary ideas and may be an independent section or may grow out of the preceding theme.

# Musical Context
Transitions are essential in larger forms where a modulatory passage is organized into an independent section connecting the main theme with another stable contrasting idea (the subordinate theme). In simpler forms, the chief contrast is furnished by harmony alone, but larger forms require a dedicated transitional passage.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/2-I: transition starts at m. 32 with an independent theme
- Beethoven, Op. 13-III: transition (m. 18-21) consists of a modulatory progression
- Beethoven, Op. 10/3-IV: a very short rondo contains all normal constituent parts in miniature; the transition (m. 9), despite internal repetition, consists of only seven measures

# Common Confusions
- A transition is not merely a bridge or a passage of connective filler; it is itself a source of contrast and often contains its own thematic material
- Transitions in the recapitulation serve a different harmonic function (tonic to tonic) than in the exposition (tonic to contrasting region), yet they remain valuable for the contrast they provide
