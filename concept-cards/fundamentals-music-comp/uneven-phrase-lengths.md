# Quick Definition

Phrases built from an odd number of measures or from segments not divisible by four (e.g., 3+3, 5+5, 6+6), departing from the conventional four-measure norm while often remaining internally balanced.

# Core Definition

Uneven phrase lengths arise when basic units (motives or phrases) are not of the standard even-measure length. The uneven number of measures may be caused by the length of basic units, the number of such units, or the combination of units of differing lengths. Symmetrical periods may have segments of odd length (e.g., 3+3 or 5+5) that are not divisible by four yet remain internally balanced. Forms built exclusively from phrases of uneven length are more common than one might expect, and their naturalness is often demonstrated when the phrase is reconceived at a different metrical level.

# Prerequisites

- **Period structure**: Understanding antecedent-consequent construction to see how uneven segments relate
- **Sentence structure**: Sentences are frequently composed of elements of differing lengths

# Key Properties

- Symmetrical periods may have segments of odd length: 3+3 (Ex. 51c), 5+5 (Ex. 44a), 6+6 (Ex. 102c)
- A phrase of six quarter-notes can be understood as resembling one measure of 3/2, demonstrating naturalness
- Entire forms may be built exclusively from uneven-length phrases
- Uneven length does not imply irregularity or asymmetry
- The bar lines may not accurately reflect the true phrase structure

# Construction / Recognition

1. Look for phrases of 3, 5, 6, or other non-standard lengths
2. Check whether the segments are equal (symmetrical) or unequal (asymmetrical)
3. Consider whether the phrase can be reconceived in a different metre (e.g., 3 measures of 3/4 = 1 measure of 3/2)
4. Examine whether the uneven length arises from motive length, motive count, or combination of differing units
5. Note that rapid tempo often makes individual measures feel like beats within a larger unit

# Context & Application

Uneven phrase lengths are distinct from both asymmetrical construction (which concerns unequal halves) and irregular construction (which concerns internal complications). Schoenberg shows that what appears "uneven" at the notated bar-line level often has a natural explanation at a higher metrical level. This insight connects directly to the concept of ritmo di tre battute.

# Examples

- Brahms, Piano Quartet in e minor, last movement: Opening theme is an a-b-a form of ten three-measure phrases
- Beethoven, Harp Quartet, Op. 74: "si ha s'imaginar la battuta di 6/8" -- imagining the measure as 6/8
- Beethoven, Ninth Symphony, Scherzo: "ritmo di tre battute" -- reduces the four-measure unit to three
- Ex. 51c (Brahms): Period of six measures, 3+3, combining one 3/4 and two 2/4 measures per segment -- symmetrical and regular despite uneven segments

# Common Errors

- Assuming uneven phrase length indicates structural deficiency
- Confusing uneven with asymmetrical (a 3+3 period is uneven but symmetrical)
- Failing to reconceive the phrase at the appropriate metrical level

# Common Confusions

- **Uneven vs. asymmetrical**: Uneven refers to absolute segment length (odd-numbered); asymmetrical refers to unequal halves. A 3+3 period is uneven but symmetrical.
- **Uneven vs. irregular**: Irregular refers to internal complications (stretching, evasions); uneven simply means the segments are not divisible by four.
