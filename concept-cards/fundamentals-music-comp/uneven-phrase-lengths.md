# Quick Definition
Phrases built from an odd number of measures or from segments not divisible by four, producing constructions that depart from the conventional four-measure norm.

# Formal Definition
Uneven phrase lengths arise when basic units (motives or phrases) are not of the standard even-measure length. Symmetrical periods may have segments of odd length (e.g., 3+3 or 5+5) that are not divisible by four, yet remain internally balanced. The uneven number of measures may be caused by the length of the basic units, the number of such units, or the combination of units of differing lengths.

# Construction/Recognition
- Look for phrases of 3, 5, 6, or other non-standard lengths
- A 3+3 period contains six-quarter-note phrases resembling one measure of 3/2
- A 5+5 period doubles the odd-length unit symmetrically
- A 6+6 period uses three-measure sub-segments
- Uneven phrase lengths may be natural when understood as larger metrical units

# Musical Context
Forms built exclusively from phrases of uneven length are more common than one might expect. A phrase of six quarter-notes may be understood as resembling one measure of 3/2, demonstrating its naturalness. Beethoven used the concept of "ritmo di tre battute" (rhythm of three measures) to organize groups of three measures as single metrical units, reducing the four-measure norm to three.

# Examples
- Brahms, Piano Quartet in e minor, last movement: Opening theme is an a-b-a form consisting of ten three-measure phrases
- Beethoven, Harp Quartet, Op. 74: "si ha s'imaginar la battuta di 6/8" -- imagining the measure as 6/8
- Beethoven, Ninth Symphony, Scherzo: "ritmo di tre battute" -- reduces the four-measure unit to three
- Ex. 51c (Brahms): Period of six measures, 3+3, combining one 3/4 and two 2/4 measures per segment

# Common Confusions
- Uneven phrase length does not necessarily mean irregular or asymmetrical -- a 3+3 period is both uneven and symmetrical
- Phrases of unusual length often have a natural explanation when reconceived in a different metre
- The bar lines may not accurately reflect the true phrase structure
