# Quick Definition

The clear statement of the basic motive in the opening segment of a theme, establishing tonality, tempo, metre, and the motivic material that will govern the entire piece.

# Core Definition

"In its opening segment a theme must clearly present (in addition to tonality, tempo and metre) its basic motive. The continuation must meet the requirements of comprehensibility" (Ch. 5, p. 21). The motive "generally appears in a characteristic and impressive manner at the beginning of a piece" (Ch. 3, p. 8). The presentation is the foundation on which the entire theme is built, because "the construction of the beginning determines the construction of the continuation" (Ch. 5, p. 21).

# Prerequisites

- **motive** -- Must have a basic motive to present
- **phrase** -- The presentation takes the form of a phrase

# Key Properties

1. The opening must clearly present the basic motive
2. Tonality must be established unambiguously
3. Tempo and metre must be immediately evident
4. The motive should appear in a "characteristic and impressive manner"
5. The presentation is typically a two-measure phrase in standard sentences
6. I and V are especially useful for opening harmonies (they express the key most clearly)
7. The presentation functions as a self-contained unit despite its brevity
8. The choices made in the presentation constrain everything that follows

# Construction / Recognition

## To Create an Effective Presentation

1. Choose a clear, memorable motive (not too many different features)
2. Establish the key using I and V (the most key-defining harmonies)
3. Set the tempo and rhythmic character immediately
4. Make the motive "characteristic and impressive" -- memorable, not merely loud
5. Keep the presentation concise (typically two measures)
6. Ensure it functions as a complete phrase (structural unit)

## To Recognize a Presentation

1. Identify the opening motive and its features (rhythm, intervals, contour)
2. Note the harmonic framework (typically I and V)
3. Observe how the motive establishes the character of the piece
4. Look for the immediate repetition that follows (characteristic of the sentence)

# Context & Application

The presentation is the foundation of the entire theme and, by extension, the composition. Schoenberg places the presentation at the beginning of his treatment of theme construction (Ch. 5) because it establishes the material from which everything else develops. The quality of the presentation -- its clarity, memorability, and harmonic definiteness -- determines the possibilities for continuation.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Making the opening motive too complex or too feature-rich
  **Correction**: "There should not be too many different features" (Ch. 3, p. 8) -- simplicity allows for development

- **Error**: Failing to establish tonality clearly in the opening
  **Correction**: "I and V are especially useful, since they express the key most clearly" (Ch. 2, p. 3)

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Equating "impressive" with loud or dramatic
  **Clarification**: "Characteristic and impressive" means memorable and distinctive, not necessarily dynamic

- **Confusion**: Thinking the presentation must be self-sufficient as a composition
  **Clarification**: It must be self-contained as a phrase but is designed to generate continuation through repetition

- **Confusion**: Believing the presentation only applies to sentence structure
  **Clarification**: While discussed in the context of sentences, the principle of clear motive presentation applies to all theme types
