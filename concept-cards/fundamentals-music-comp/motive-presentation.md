# Quick Definition
The clear statement of the basic motive in the opening segment of a theme, establishing tonality, tempo, metre, and the motivic material that will govern the entire piece.

# Formal Definition
In its opening segment, a theme must clearly present (in addition to tonality, tempo, and metre) its basic motive. The construction of the beginning determines the construction of the continuation. The continuation must meet the requirements of comprehensibility, and an immediate repetition is the simplest solution, characteristic of the sentence structure.

# Construction/Recognition
Effective motive presentation requires:
- **Clear tonality**: the opening should establish the key unambiguously
- **Clear tempo and metre**: rhythmic character should be immediately evident
- **Memorable motive**: the basic motive should appear in a "characteristic and impressive manner"
- **Brevity**: typically occupying two measures in a standard sentence
- **Completeness**: the opening phrase should function as a self-contained unit despite its brevity

The presentation typically uses I and V to express the key clearly, establishing the harmonic framework before more complex progressions appear.

# Musical Context
The opening presentation is the foundation on which the entire theme is built. Because "the construction of the beginning determines the construction of the continuation," the choices made in the presentation -- the specific motive, its rhythm, its intervals, its harmonic implications -- constrain and guide everything that follows.

# Common Confusions
- Motive presentation is not merely stating the motive; it involves establishing the entire musical context (key, tempo, metre)
- The "impressive manner" of the opening does not mean loud or dramatic; it means memorable and characteristic
- The presentation constrains the continuation; a complex opening requires a different kind of continuation than a simple one
- Presentation in the sentence is followed by immediate repetition, not by contrasting material (which would characterize a period)
