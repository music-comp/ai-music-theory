# Quick Definition
The fundamental balance between introducing variety through contrast and maintaining connection through coherence in the small ternary form, where harmony provides the most effective contrast while metre and motivic connection furnish coherence.

# Core Definition
The most effective factor in a contrasting section is the harmony. The A-section establishes a tonic; the B-section contraposes another region (one of the more closely related). This provides both contrast and coherence simultaneously. Further coherence is furnished by the metre, and by using forms of the basic motive which are not too remote from those of the A-section. Further contrast can be achieved through the use of new variations of the basic motive, or change in the order of previous motive-forms.

# Key Properties
- Harmony is the most effective contrast factor
- The B-section contraposes a closely related region to the tonic
- This harmonic shift provides both contrast AND coherence simultaneously
- Metre furnishes additional coherence
- Motive-forms not too remote from the A-section maintain connection
- New variations and reordering of motive-forms create further contrast
- Balance between contrast and coherence determines the success of the form

# Construction / Recognition
To create effective contrast-with-coherence: (1) shift to a closely related harmonic region, (2) maintain the metre, (3) use motive-forms related to but varied from the A-section, (4) consider reordering previous motive-forms, (5) ensure the contrast is felt as a different perspective on the same material, not as a foreign intrusion.

# Context & Application
This principle extends beyond the small ternary form to all levels of musical structure. The balance between contrast and coherence is the fundamental compositional challenge. Schoenberg's treatment here provides the practical foundation for handling this balance in larger forms (minuet, scherzo, sonata).

# Examples
- Beethoven Op. 2/1-II: the B-section's interchange of V and I coherently contrasts with the A-section's interchange of I and V
- Beethoven Op. 14/2-II: contrast through legato style (vs. A-section), coherence through phrasing
- Haydn Ex. 102b: B-section begins on bIII, reinterpreted through multiple meaning -- demonstrating both remote contrast and tonal coherence

# Common Errors
- Creating contrast that is too remote, destroying coherence
- Maintaining coherence so strongly that no real contrast is felt
- Relying on surface contrast (dynamics, texture) without harmonic contrast

# Common Confusions
- Contrast and coherence are not opposites to choose between; they must coexist simultaneously
- The most effective contrast comes from harmony, not from texture or dynamics alone
- A closely related harmonic region provides more effective contrast than a distant one, because it maintains coherence
