# Quick Definition
The rhythmic differentiation at the end of a phrase that marks its boundary, functioning like a comma or other punctuation mark in language.

# Formal Definition
Rhythm is particularly important in moulding the phrase. It contributes to interest and variety, establishes character, and is often the determining factor in establishing the unity of the phrase. The end of the phrase is usually differentiated rhythmically to provide punctuation. The phrase ending suggests a form of punctuation such as a comma.

# Construction/Recognition
Phrase endings may be marked by a combination of distinguishing features:
- Rhythmic reduction (longer note values, fewer notes)
- Melodic relaxation through a drop in pitch
- The use of smaller intervals
- Fewer notes
- Any other suitable differentiation from the body of the phrase

# Musical Context
Rhythmic punctuation is essential for making the structural boundaries of a phrase perceptible to the listener. Without clear punctuation, phrases run together and the subdivision that makes music comprehensible is lost. The type and strength of punctuation affects the sense of continuation or closure.

# Examples
- Phrase endings marked by longer note values after a passage of shorter ones
- Melodic descent at phrase boundaries creating a sense of relaxation
- Reduction in rhythmic activity signaling the end of a structural unit

# Common Confusions
- Rhythmic punctuation does not always mean a full stop; it can be as light as a comma, allowing the music to continue
- Punctuation is not solely rhythmic -- it involves the coordination of rhythm, melody, and sometimes harmony
- The phrase boundary is not necessarily at a barline; phrases typically cross metrical boundaries
