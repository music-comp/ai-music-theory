# Quick Definition

In cadences, the melody commonly reduces characteristic features (which demand continuation) to uncharacteristic ones, abandoning distinctive intervals and rhythmic figures to signal structural closure.

# Core Definition

The melody in the cadence commonly reduces characteristic features to uncharacteristic ones. Characteristic features are those distinctive intervallic, rhythmic, or contour elements that create ongoing momentum and demand continuation. Uncharacteristic features are simpler, less distinctive elements that no longer demand continuation. This melodic reduction is a component of the broader liquidation process that provides delimitation of the structure (Schoenberg, p. 25).

# Prerequisites

- **Motive** -- Melodic simplification operates on the characteristic features of motives
- **Cadence contour** -- Melodic simplification is one aspect of the cadence contour

# Key Properties

1. Characteristic intervals are abandoned or combined in a different order
2. Distinctive rhythmic patterns may be simplified or cease entirely
3. The result is uncharacteristic material that no longer demands continuation
4. Works alongside harmonic concentration and liquidation of motival obligations
5. Combined with register decline if a climax precedes the cadence

# Construction / Recognition

1. Compare the cadential measures with the opening measures
2. Identify which characteristic intervals have been abandoned or reordered
3. Note where rhythmic activity has been simplified or ceased
4. Confirm that the remaining material is "uncharacteristic" (generic, non-distinctive)

# Context & Application

Melodic simplification in the cadence is not a loss but a structural necessity. The reduction of distinctive features signals that the phrase is concluding, working in concert with harmonic closure. This principle connects to the larger concept of liquidation (Chapter 8), where characteristic features are progressively eliminated until only uncharacteristic residues remain.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Maintaining distinctive melodic features into the cadence
  **Correction**: Distinctive features demand continuation; the cadence requires uncharacteristic material

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking melodic simplification makes the cadence "boring"
  **Clarification**: It is a structural technique that signals closure; the simplification itself can be compositionally compelling

- **Confusion**: Equating simplification with mere repetition of simple figures
  **Clarification**: It specifically involves transforming characteristic features into uncharacteristic ones, not merely writing simple music
