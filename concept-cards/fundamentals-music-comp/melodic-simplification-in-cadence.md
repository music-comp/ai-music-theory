# Quick Definition
In cadences, the melody commonly reduces characteristic features (which demand continuation) to uncharacteristic ones, abandoning distinctive intervals and rhythmic figures to signal structural closure.

# Formal Definition
The melody in the cadence commonly reduces characteristic features to uncharacteristic ones. Characteristic features are those distinctive intervallic, rhythmic, or contour elements that create a sense of ongoing momentum and demand continuation. Uncharacteristic features are simpler, less distinctive elements -- conjunct motion, repeated notes, or common figures -- that no longer demand continuation. This reduction process is a melodic component of the broader liquidation process that provides delimitation of the structure.

# Construction/Recognition
- Characteristic intervals are abandoned or combined in a different order
- Distinctive rhythmic patterns may be simplified or cease entirely
- Stepwise motion often replaces leaps
- The melody recedes from climactic points toward the middle register
- This process works alongside harmonic concentration and liquidation of motival obligations
- Combined, these produce effective delimitation of the structure

# Musical Context
Melodic simplification in the cadence is not a loss but a structural necessity. The reduction of distinctive features signals to the listener that the phrase is concluding, working in concert with harmonic closure. This principle connects to the larger concept of liquidation (discussed fully in Chapter 8), where characteristic features are progressively eliminated until only uncharacteristic residues remain.

# Common Confusions
- Melodic simplification is not the same as making the melody "boring" -- it is a deliberate structural technique that signals closure.
- This process is distinct from mere repetition of simple figures; it involves the specific transformation of characteristic elements into uncharacteristic ones.
- Melodic simplification works together with harmonic and rhythmic processes; it is not the sole agent of cadential closure.
