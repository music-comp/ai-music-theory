# Quick Definition
The characteristic melodic shape assumed by the melody as it approaches and executes a cadence, typically contrasting with what precedes it through changes in rhythmic activity, intervallic character, and register.

# Formal Definition
The cadence contour is the special melodic profile the melody must assume in order to exercise the function of a cadence. It usually contrasts with what precedes it and parallels the changes in harmony. The melody may obey the tendency of the smallest notes (producing an accelerando effect through increasing small note values) or contradict it (producing a ritardando effect through longer notes). The cadence contour commonly reduces characteristic features (which demand continuation) to uncharacteristic ones, and if there is a climax, the melody recedes from it toward the middle register.

# Construction/Recognition
- Contrasts with preceding material in rhythm, contour, or both
- Increase of smaller note values (accelerando): more frequent in cadences
- Decrease of smaller note values (ritardando): less common but effective
- Reduction of characteristic intervals to uncharacteristic ones
- Decline from a climactic point toward the middle register
- Combined with harmonic concentration and liquidation of motival obligations

# Musical Context
The cadence contour works together with harmonic concentration and liquidation of motival obligations to provide effective delimitation of the structure. The decline in the cadence contour, combined with these other processes, creates the sense of closure necessary at the end of the consequent. The cadence contour is not merely decorative but functionally essential to period construction.

# Examples
- Rhythmic increase (accelerando): Beethoven, Op. 2/1, Adagio, m. 6-7; Op. 2/2-IV, m. 7; Op. 22, Menuetto, m. 7; Ex. 46g, m. 7-8; Exs. 44b, m. 7; 44f, m. 7; 47c, m. 6-7; 49a, m. 7
- Rhythmic decrease (ritardando): Ex. 46h, m. 7; Exs. 44j and 46f (very conspicuous)
- "Written-out ritardando" as extension: Exs. 44f and 47a
- Reduction of characteristic features: Compare m. 7 of Exs. 45c and f with their respective m. 1-2 -- characteristic intervals are abandoned or reordered; in Ex. 45c, eighth-note movement stops entirely
- Decline from climax: Exs. 45f, g

# Common Confusions
- The cadence contour is not simply "the ending melody"; it is a functionally specific melodic profile that serves the structural purpose of delimitation.
- Rhythmic increase (accelerando) is more common than decrease (ritardando) in cadences, but both are legitimate approaches.
- A "written-out ritardando" is not a performance indication but a compositional device that extends the phrase through progressively longer note values.
