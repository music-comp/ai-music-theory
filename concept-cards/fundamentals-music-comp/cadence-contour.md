# Quick Definition

The characteristic melodic shape assumed by the melody as it approaches and executes a cadence, typically contrasting with what precedes it through reduction of characteristic features, rhythmic change, and return to middle register.

# Core Definition

In order to exercise the function of a cadence, the melody must assume certain characteristics, producing a special cadence contour that usually contrasts with what precedes it. The melody parallels the changes in the harmony, obeying the tendency of the smallest notes (like an accelerando) or, on the contrary, contradicting the tendency by employing longer notes (like a ritardando). The melody in the cadence commonly reduces characteristic features (which demand continuation) to uncharacteristic ones. If there is a climax, the melody is likely to recede from it, balancing the compass by returning to the middle register. This decline in the cadence contour, combined with concentration of the harmony and the liquidation of motival obligations, can be depended upon to provide effective delimitation of the structure (Schoenberg, pp. 24-25).

# Key Properties

1. Contrasts with preceding melodic material
2. Parallels harmonic changes in the cadence
3. May follow the tendency of the smallest notes (rhythmic increase/accelerando)
4. May contradict the tendency (rhythmic decrease/ritardando)
5. Reduces characteristic features to uncharacteristic ones
6. Recedes from climactic points toward the middle register
7. Works with harmonic concentration and liquidation to delimit structure

# Construction / Recognition

## To Construct a Cadence Contour

1. Contrast the cadential melody with the preceding material
2. Choose rhythmic increase (more common) or decrease
3. Reduce characteristic intervals -- abandon distinctive patterns or reorder them
4. If a climax preceded the cadence, recede toward the middle register
5. Coordinate with harmonic concentration (more harmonies per measure)
6. Allow the liquidation of motival obligations to simplify the material

## To Recognize a Cadence Contour

1. Identify the melodic shape change at or near the cadence
2. Compare rhythmic activity with preceding measures
3. Check whether characteristic intervals have been abandoned or simplified
4. Note any decline from a climactic point

# Context & Application

The cadence contour is not merely decorative but functionally essential to period and sentence construction. It is the melodic component of the structural delimitation process, working in concert with harmonic concentration and liquidation. Variety needs no justification -- it is a merit in itself -- but some variations in the melody are involuntary results of the changed harmonic construction, particularly of the cadence.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Writing a cadence melody that maintains the same characteristic features as the preceding material
  **Correction**: The cadence contour requires contrast -- reduce characteristic features to uncharacteristic ones

- **Error**: Ignoring the coordination between melodic cadence contour and harmonic concentration
  **Correction**: The cadence contour works together with harmonic processes; both must be aligned

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the cadence contour is simply "the ending melody"
  **Clarification**: It is a functionally specific melodic profile that serves structural delimitation through characteristic-to-uncharacteristic reduction

- **Confusion**: Assuming rhythmic increase (accelerando) is always the correct approach
  **Clarification**: Rhythmic decrease (ritardando) is less common but also legitimate; a "written-out ritardando" can create phrase extensions
