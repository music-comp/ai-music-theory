# Quick Definition
The principal large form of instrumental music, essentially a ternary structure of exposition, elaboration (Durchfuhrung), and recapitulation, distinguished by extraordinary flexibility in accommodating musical ideas of any character, length, or number -- with the contrasting middle section devoted to the modulatory working-out of previously exposed thematic material.

# Core Definition
This form, like the earlier ones, is essentially a ternary structure. Its main divisions are the exposition, elaboration, and recapitulation. It differs from other complex ternary forms in that the contrasting middle section (elaboration) is devoted almost exclusively to the working out of the rich variety of thematic material "exposed" in the first division. Its greatest merit, which enabled it to hold a commanding position over a period of 150 years, is its extraordinary flexibility in accommodating the widest variety of musical ideas, long or short, many or few, active or passive, in almost any combination. The internal details may be subjected to almost any mutation without disturbing the aesthetic validity of the structure as a whole.

# Key Properties
- Ternary structure: exposition (A), elaboration (B), recapitulation (A')
- Elaboration devoted to working-out of exposed thematic material
- Extraordinary flexibility -- accommodates ideas of any character, length, or number
- Length may vary between 100 measures and 300 or more
- The sonatina does not differ essentially from the form's minimum realization
- Complete diversity in relative lengths of elements is possible
- The customary term "development" is a misnomer; Schoenberg prefers "elaboration" (Durchfuhrung)
- The Durchfuhrung's importance was much overrated in the late 19th century

# Construction / Recognition
Basic structural diagram:
- **A (Exposition)**: Tonic region -- Principal theme/group, Transition, Subordinate group in related region, Closing/Codettas
- **B (Elaboration)**: Modulatory -- Working out of exposed thematic material through various keys
- **A' (Recapitulation)**: Tonic region -- Principal theme, Transition (modified), Subordinate group transposed to tonic, Coda (optional)

# Context & Application
This form is used for first movements and often last movements of sonatas, quartets, symphonies, and concertos. It represents the most developed and flexible of all instrumental forms. "The form of a piece is what it is" -- Schoenberg emphasizes that the sensitive formal feeling of the artist determines the evolution of a motive into the fully elaborated masterpiece.

# Examples
- Small-scale examples: Beethoven, Op. 14/1, Op. 49/1, Op. 78, Op. 79
- The Eroica Symphony first movement represents one of the most extended applications
- The form accommodates both the simplest sonatinas and the most elaborate symphonic movements

# Common Errors
- Assuming the form requires enormous length and complexity
- Treating the elaboration as an opportunity for new thematic invention rather than working out existing material
- Neglecting the flexibility principle -- trying to force material into a rigid formal mold

# Common Confusions
- The assumption that sonata-allegro is necessarily very extended and complex is not true; the sonatina is essentially the same form with smaller parts
- The customary term "development" is a misnomer; it suggests germination and growth which rarely occur; "elaboration" (Durchfuhrung) better describes the thematic working out
- The Durchfuhrung's importance was much overrated during the latter half of the nineteenth century
- The form cannot be constructed by "laying musical bricks together, or pouring musical concrete into preconceived moulds"
