# Quick Definition
The principal large form of instrumental music, essentially a ternary structure consisting of exposition, elaboration (Durchfuhrung), and recapitulation, distinguished by its extraordinary flexibility in accommodating musical ideas of any character, length, or number.

# Formal Definition
This form, like the earlier ones, is essentially a ternary structure. Its main divisions are the exposition, elaboration, and recapitulation. It differs from other complex ternary forms in that the contrasting middle section (elaboration) is devoted almost exclusively to the working out of the rich variety of thematic material "exposed" in the first division. Its greatest merit, which enabled it to hold a commanding position over a period of 150 years, is its extraordinary flexibility in accommodating the widest variety of musical ideas, long or short, many or few, active or passive, in almost any combination.

# Construction/Recognition
The basic structural diagram:
- **A (Exposition)**: Tonic region -- Principal theme, Transition, Subordinate group in related region, Closing/Codettas
- **B (Elaboration)**: Modulatory -- Working out of exposed thematic material
- **A' (Recapitulation)**: Tonic region -- Principal theme, Transition (modified), Subordinate group transposed to tonic, Coda (optional)

Key characteristics:
- The internal details may be subjected to almost any mutation without disturbing the aesthetic validity
- Length may vary between 100 measures and 300 or more
- The so-called sonatina does not differ essentially from sonata-allegros with minimum parts
- Complete diversity in relative lengths of elements is possible

# Musical Context
This form is used for first movements and often last movements of sonatas, quartets, symphonies, and concertos. It represents the most developed and flexible of all instrumental forms.

# Examples
- Small-scale examples: Beethoven, Op. 14/1, Op. 49/1, Op. 78, Op. 79
- The Eroica Symphony first movement represents one of the most extended applications
- The form accommodates both the simplest sonatinas and the most elaborate symphonic movements

# Common Confusions
- The assumption that the sonata-allegro is necessarily a very extended and complex form is not true; the sonatina is essentially the same form with smaller parts
- The customary term "development" for the middle section is a misnomer; it suggests germination and growth which rarely occur; "elaboration" (Durchfuhrung) better describes the thematic working out and modulatory process
- The Durchfuhrung's importance was much overrated during the latter half of the nineteenth century
