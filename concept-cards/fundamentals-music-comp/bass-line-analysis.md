# Quick Definition
The bass line is a "second melody" subject to many of the same requirements as the principal melody: rhythmic balance, variety of contour, avoidance of monotonous repetitions, and effective use of inversions and chromatic progression.

# Core Definition
The bass was previously described as a "second melody." This means that it is subject to somewhat the same requirements as the principal melody. It should be rhythmically balanced, should avoid the monotony of unnecessary repetitions, should have some variety of contour, and should make full use of inversions (especially of seventh chords). Chromatic progression can be as melodically advantageous in the bass as in the principal melody. Semi- and quasi-counterpoint help to make the bass interesting.

# Prerequisites
- self-criticism: Bass line analysis is the fifth step in the self-criticism framework

# Key Properties
- The bass is a "second melody" with its own requirements
- Should be rhythmically balanced
- Should avoid monotony of unnecessary repetitions
- Should have variety of contour
- Should make full use of inversions, especially of seventh chords
- Chromatic progression can be melodically advantageous
- Semi- and quasi-counterpoint help make the bass interesting

# Construction / Recognition
Evaluate the bass line by: (1) playing it separately, (2) checking for rhythmic balance, (3) identifying unnecessary repetitions, (4) evaluating contour variety, (5) checking whether inversions (especially seventh chord inversions) are used effectively, (6) looking for chromatic movement opportunities, (7) assessing whether semi-contrapuntal techniques are employed.

# Context & Application
The bass line is often neglected by beginners who focus exclusively on melody and harmonic progression. Treating the bass as a "second melody" elevates its importance and leads to richer, more satisfying textures. The final admonition of Chapter 12 combines all related concerns: "Watch the harmony; watch the root progressions; watch the bass line."

# Common Errors
- Treating the bass as merely the lowest note of each chord
- Neglecting rhythmic balance in the bass line
- Avoiding inversions, especially of seventh chords
- Failing to consider chromatic melodic possibilities in the bass

# Common Confusions
- The bass is not merely the lowest note of each chord; it is a melodic line in its own right
- "Second melody" does not mean the bass should always be tuneful; it means it should be crafted with the same care as the principal melody
