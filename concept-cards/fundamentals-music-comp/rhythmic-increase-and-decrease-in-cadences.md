# Quick Definition
In cadential passages, the melody may employ increasingly smaller note values (rhythmic increase, producing an accelerando effect) or increasingly larger note values (rhythmic decrease, producing a ritardando effect), with increase being more common.

# Formal Definition
Rhythmic increase refers to the progressive introduction of smaller note values in the cadence, obeying the tendency of the smallest notes and creating an accelerando-like effect. Rhythmic decrease is the contrary motion toward longer note values, contradicting the tendency and producing a ritardando-like effect. In some remarkable cases, instead of a simple rhythmic decrease, a "written-out ritardando" appears as an extension of the phrase, effectively lengthening the period beyond its expected boundaries.

# Construction/Recognition
- Rhythmic increase: Look for progressively faster note values approaching the cadence
- Rhythmic decrease: Look for progressively slower note values
- Written-out ritardando: The slowing creates an actual phrase extension rather than a mere slowing within the existing phrase length
- Increase is more frequent than decrease in cadences
- Both techniques parallel or counteract the harmonic changes in the cadence

# Musical Context
The choice between rhythmic increase and decrease affects the character of the cadence and, by extension, the character of the entire period. Increase creates urgency and drive toward the cadential goal; decrease creates a sense of settling and relaxation. The written-out ritardando is a particularly expressive device that blurs the boundary between rhythmic technique and formal extension.

# Examples
- Rhythmic increase: Beethoven, Op. 2/1, Adagio, m. 6-7; Op. 2/2-IV, m. 7; Op. 22, Menuetto, m. 7; Exs. 44b, m. 7; 44f, m. 7; 47c, m. 6-7; 49a, m. 7
- Rhythmic decrease: Ex. 46h, m. 7 (unusual); Exs. 44j and 46f (very conspicuous)
- Written-out ritardando as extension: Exs. 44f and 47a -- remarkable because instead of simple rhythmic decrease, the slowing creates an actual extension of the phrase

# Common Confusions
- Rhythmic increase and decrease are compositional techniques built into the notation, not performance indications like accelerando or ritardando markings.
- A written-out ritardando is distinct from a simple decrease: it actually extends the phrase length, creating a formal irregularity.
- Rhythmic decrease is less common in cadences and thus can be a more striking or unexpected effect when used.
