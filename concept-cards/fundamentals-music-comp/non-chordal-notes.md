# Quick Definition
Notes that do not belong to the prevailing harmony but contribute fluency and interest to a phrase through conventional formulas that ensure harmonic clarity via resolution.

# Formal Definition
The addition of non-chordal notes contributes to the fluency and interest of the phrase, provided they do not obscure or contradict the harmony. The various conventional formulas for using non-chordal notes -- passing notes, auxiliary notes, changing notes, suspensions, appoggiaturas, etc. -- provide for harmonic clarity through the resolution of non-chordal into chordal notes. Schoenberg prefers the term "ancillary notes" to the traditional "embellishing" or "ornamental" notes, to avoid aesthetically misleading connotations.

# Construction/Recognition
Non-chordal notes are identified by their relationship to the prevailing harmony: they are notes that fall outside the current chord. They are recognized by their resolution -- they move to (resolve into) chord tones according to established conventions. Types include:
- **Passing notes**: move stepwise between two chord tones
- **Auxiliary notes**: step away from and return to a chord tone
- **Changing notes**: combine auxiliary motion in two directions
- **Suspensions**: hold a chord tone from the previous harmony, then resolve downward
- **Appoggiaturas**: approach a chord tone by leap, then resolve by step

# Musical Context
Non-chordal notes are the primary means of adding melodic interest to a phrase built on a harmonic skeleton. Schoenberg shows progressive levels of embellishment: Exs. 8-9 add simple passing notes to basic chord-tone phrases; Exs. 10-11 add more elaborate embellishments but risk overburdening the melody and obscuring the harmony.

# Examples
- Ex. 8: simplest melodic additions (passing notes) contributing fluency to chord-outline phrases
- Ex. 9: note repetitions and passing notes adding vitality
- Exs. 10-11: elaborate embellishments using appoggiaturas and changing notes
- Beethoven Violin Concerto: passing notes embellishing a chord-based melody

# Common Confusions
- Non-chordal notes must resolve to chord tones; unresolved non-chordal notes obscure the harmony
- Schoenberg prefers "ancillary" over "embellishing" or "ornamental" because the latter terms imply the notes are merely decorative, when in fact they are structurally important
- More elaborate embellishment is not always better -- it can overburden the melody with small notes
