# Quick Definition

Notes that do not belong to the prevailing harmony but contribute fluency and interest to a phrase through conventional formulas that ensure harmonic clarity via resolution into chordal notes.

# Core Definition

"The addition of non-chordal notes contributes to the fluency and interest of the phrase, provided they do not obscure or contradict the harmony. The various 'conventional formulas' for using non-chordal notes (passing notes, auxiliary notes, changing notes, suspensions, appoggiaturas, etc.) provide for harmonic clarity through the resolution of non-chordal into chordal notes" (Ch. 2, p. 3). Schoenberg prefers the term "ancillary" to the traditional "embellishing" or "ornamental" to avoid "aesthetically misleading and corrupted terms" (Ch. 3, footnote, p. 9).

# Prerequisites

- **phrase** -- Non-chordal notes function within the context of a phrase and its implied harmony

# Key Properties

1. Non-chordal notes fall outside the prevailing harmony
2. They contribute fluency and interest to the melodic line
3. They must not obscure or contradict the harmony
4. They resolve into chordal notes according to conventional formulas
5. Types include passing notes, auxiliary notes, changing notes, suspensions, and appoggiaturas
6. Schoenberg's preferred term is "ancillary notes"

# Construction / Recognition

## Types of Non-Chordal Notes

1. **Passing notes**: move stepwise between two chord tones
2. **Auxiliary notes**: step away from and return to a chord tone
3. **Changing notes**: combine auxiliary motion in two directions
4. **Suspensions**: hold a chord tone from the previous harmony, then resolve downward
5. **Appoggiaturas**: approach a chord tone by leap, then resolve by step

## Guidelines for Use

1. Ensure each non-chordal note resolves to a chord tone
2. Begin with simple additions (passing notes) before attempting elaborate embellishments
3. Avoid overburdening the melody with too many small notes
4. Maintain harmonic clarity even with extensive embellishment

# Context & Application

Non-chordal notes are the primary means of adding melodic interest to a phrase built on a harmonic skeleton. Schoenberg demonstrates progressive levels of embellishment: Exs. 8-9 add simple passing notes to basic chord-tone phrases; Exs. 10-11 add more elaborate embellishments but risk "overburdening the melody with small notes and obscuring the harmony" (Ch. 2, p. 4). The concept of non-chordal notes also connects to variation methods (Ch. 3), where "filling up intervals with ancillary notes" is one of the basic interval-variation techniques.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Adding so many non-chordal notes that the harmony becomes unclear
  **Correction**: Non-chordal notes must resolve to chord tones and must not obscure the harmonic framework

- **Error**: Using non-chordal notes that contradict the prevailing harmony
  **Correction**: Ensure resolution follows conventional formulas; the result should clarify, not confuse, the harmony

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Treating non-chordal notes as mere decoration
  **Clarification**: Schoenberg rejects "embellishing" and "ornamental" as misleading terms; non-chordal (ancillary) notes are structurally important contributors to fluency and interest

- **Confusion**: Believing more embellishment is always better
  **Clarification**: Elaborate embellishments "tend to overburden the melody with small notes and obscure the harmony" (Ch. 2, p. 4)
