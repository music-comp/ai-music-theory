# Quick Definition
Construction complicated by internal repetitions (sequential or varied), stretching of cadences, additions after cadences, or evasion of expected cadence points, producing phrases that depart from regular patterns.

# Formal Definition
Irregular construction arises when the internal organization of a sentence or period is complicated by procedures that expand, compress, or otherwise alter the expected regularity. These include: sequential or varied internal repetitions; stretching (where a cadence that normally would occupy two measures is extended to three); additions after the cadence; and the ingenious evasion of cadential opportunities to extend a phrase well beyond its expected length.

# Construction/Recognition
- Internal repetitions: Elements repeated sequentially or with variation within a phrase
- Stretching: Cadences expanded beyond their normal duration (e.g., two measures stretched to three)
- Post-cadential additions: Extra material appended after the cadence
- Cadence evasion: Expected cadence points are bypassed, extending the phrase
- Combination of elements of differing lengths creates complex internal structures (e.g., 3+3+4+2+2)

# Musical Context
Irregular construction becomes more frequent in the second half of the nineteenth century. Brahms and Mahler, under the influence of folk music, developed a feeling for free rhythmic organization that does not correspond to bar lines. In extreme cases, frequent changes of metre bring about correspondence between phrase structure and bar lines. Such procedures contribute fluency and spontaneity but require a high degree of skill and sensitivity.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Ex. 59a: Possible cadences at m. 8, 10, or 11 are ingeniously evaded, extending the sentence to thirteen measures
- Ex. 60f: Constructed by internal repetitions of differing lengths: 3(2+1)+3(2+1)+4(2+2)+2+2
- Exs. 44f and 47a: Cadences normally occupying two measures are stretched to three
- Exs. 59c, 60n: Additions appended after the cadence
- Ex. 112: Frequent changes of metre used to align phrase structure with bar lines

# Common Confusions
- Irregular does not mean arbitrary -- master composers introduce irregularity deliberately, with high skill and sensitivity
- Irregular construction is not a defect; it often contributes fluency and spontaneity
- The distinction between stretching (extending a cadence) and addition (appending material after a cadence) is important
