# Quick Definition

Construction complicated by internal repetitions (sequential or varied), stretching of cadences, additions after cadences, or evasion of expected cadence points, producing phrases that depart from regular patterns through internal complications rather than proportional imbalance.

# Core Definition

Irregular construction arises when the internal organization of a sentence or period is complicated by procedures that expand, compress, or otherwise alter the expected regularity. These include: sequential or varied internal repetitions; stretching (where a cadence that normally would occupy two measures is extended to three); additions after the cadence; and the ingenious evasion of cadential opportunities to extend a phrase well beyond its expected length. This is distinct from asymmetrical construction, which concerns the proportional relationship between halves.

# Prerequisites

- **Period structure**: The regular period is the norm from which irregularity departs
- **Sentence structure**: Understanding the expected phrase structure reveals where irregularity occurs
- **Cadence contour**: Recognizing cadential patterns is essential to identifying stretching, evasion, and additions

# Key Properties

- Internal repetitions: Elements repeated sequentially or with variation within a phrase
- Stretching: Cadences expanded beyond their normal duration (e.g., two measures stretched to three)
- Post-cadential additions: Extra material appended after the cadence
- Cadence evasion: Expected cadence points bypassed, extending the phrase
- Combination of elements of differing lengths creates complex internal structures (e.g., 3+3+4+2+2)
- Becomes more frequent in the second half of the nineteenth century
- In extreme cases, frequent changes of metre bring correspondence between phrase structure and bar lines

# Construction / Recognition

1. Identify expected cadence points and check whether they are reached, evaded, or stretched
2. Look for sequential or varied internal repetitions that expand the phrase
3. Check for post-cadential additions (extra material after the expected close)
4. Test which measures could be omitted to restore the regular form
5. Note combination of elements of differing lengths (e.g., 3(2+1)+3(2+1)+4(2+2)+2+2)
6. In later Romantic music, check for metre changes that accommodate irregular phrase structure

# Context & Application

Irregular construction is never arbitrary. Schoenberg insists that "a high degree of skill and sensitivity are necessary to achieve the necessary balance and proportion." Brahms and Mahler, under the influence of folk music, developed free rhythmic organization that does not correspond to bar lines. This represents a natural evolution from strict period/sentence practice forms.

# Examples

- Beethoven, Ex. 59a: Possible cadences at m. 8, 10, or 11 are ingeniously evaded, extending the sentence to thirteen measures
- Ex. 60f: Constructed by internal repetitions of differing lengths: 3(2+1)+3(2+1)+4(2+2)+2+2
- Exs. 44f and 47a: Cadences normally occupying two measures are stretched to three
- Exs. 59c, 60n: Additions appended after the cadence
- Ex. 112: Frequent changes of metre used to align phrase structure with bar lines (Brahms/Mahler influence)

# Common Errors

- Treating irregular construction as a compositional defect rather than a deliberate technique
- Confusing irregular with asymmetrical (they address different aspects of phrase structure)
- Failing to test which measures could be omitted to reveal the underlying regular structure

# Common Confusions

- **Irregular vs. asymmetrical**: Irregular refers to internal complications (stretching, additions, evasions); asymmetrical refers to unequal division between halves. Both may coexist.
- **Stretching vs. addition**: Stretching expands material within the phrase (a cadence stretched from 2 to 3 measures); addition appends material after the cadence.
