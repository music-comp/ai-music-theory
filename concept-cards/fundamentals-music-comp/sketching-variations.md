# Quick Definition

The preparatory process for composing variations, involving thorough exploration of the theme, reduction to its essentials, and creation of many partial sketches exploring different motives of variation before selecting and completing the most promising ones.

# Core Definition

In preparation for the composition of a set of variations, the theme should be thoroughly explored and the best openings for variations determined. After the theme has been reduced to its essentials, a large number of sketches which explore a variety of motives of variation should be made. Even if many prove stiff and awkward, or if an otherwise promising motive turns out to be unworkable, the composer acquires an intimate acquaintance with the theme, its possibilities, and the limitations it imposes. Often elements of different sketches may be combined to provide a striking and effective motive of variation. From the many partial sketches, the more promising may then be selected for completion and polishing.

# Prerequisites

- **Production of the motive of variation**: Understanding how to derive motives from the theme's skeleton
- **Theme structural constitution**: Understanding the theme's features and limitations

# Key Properties

- Step 1: Thoroughly explore the theme
- Step 2: Reduce the theme to its essentials (skeleton)
- Step 3: Create many partial sketches exploring different motives of variation
- Step 4: Accept that many sketches will be unsuccessful -- this is part of the process
- Step 5: Look for elements from different sketches that might be combined
- Step 6: Select the most promising sketches for completion
- Step 7: Polish and refine the selected variations
- Unsuccessful sketches build understanding of the theme's limitations

# Construction / Recognition

1. Begin by reducing the theme to its skeleton
2. Generate as many different motive sketches as possible
3. Do not discard failed attempts -- they build familiarity
4. Look for combinable elements across sketches
5. Select the most promising for completion
6. Polish and refine through iteration

# Context & Application

Sketching is a practical compositional method, not merely an academic exercise. The emphasis on producing many sketches reflects the reality that variation composition is exploratory: the best motives are discovered through trial and error, not predetermined by theory. The process also reveals the theme's limitations, which are as important to understand as its possibilities.

# Common Errors

- Discarding failed sketches instead of learning from them
- Generating too few sketches before selecting
- Trying to compose finished variations without the sketching phase

# Common Confusions

- **Sketching vs. composing**: Sketching is deliberately exploratory; many sketches will be discarded. This is not wasted effort but essential preparation.
- **Combining sketches**: The final variation may combine elements from multiple different sketches; each sketch need not be viable as a complete variation.
