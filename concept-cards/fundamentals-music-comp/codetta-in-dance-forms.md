# Quick Definition

Short cadential passages added after the main body of a section to reaffirm the ending, ranging from rudimentary V-I cadences to quite independent formulations, commonly found at the end of A-sections and recapitulations in minuets and scherzos.

# Core Definition

Codettas are primarily cadences that serve as reaffirmations of the ending of a section. Harmonically, they may consist of the most rudimentary cadence (V-I) or may be highly complex. Motivally, they may range from simple repetitions of small elements to rather independent formulations. If more than one codetta appears, the later ones are usually shortened, often in the manner of a liquidation. Codettas are added to A-sections, recapitulations, and occasionally to B-sections.

# Prerequisites

- **Minuet form**: Understanding the structural context in which codettas function

# Key Properties

- Appear after the main cadence of a section
- Harmonically: range from simple V-I to complex progressions
- Motivally: range from repetitions of small elements to independent formulations
- Multiple codettas are typically progressively shortened (liquidating manner)
- May feature subdominant or plagal character in their final stages
- Distinguished from extensions by their primarily cadential function
- Distinguished from codas by their shorter scope

# Construction / Recognition

1. Identify the main cadence of the section
2. Check for additional cadential material following the main cadence
3. If multiple codettas, verify progressive shortening
4. Assess the motivic content (simple repetition vs. independent formulation)
5. Note subdominant or plagal elements in later codettas
6. Distinguish from extensions (which occur before the cadence) and codas (which are larger)

# Context & Application

Codettas provide structural closure and are a standard feature of dance forms. In minuets and scherzos, they often appear at the end of the recapitulation to provide the full cadence that may have been missing in the recapitulation proper. They serve as a counterbalance to the preceding harmonic movement of the contrasting middle section.

# Examples

- Haydn, String Quartet, Op. 54/1, Menuetto (Ex. 114): Ten-measure added section (codetta) built from a one-measure rest and nine measures of content
- Beethoven, Op. 10/3-III: Several codettas conclude the Minuet
- Beethoven, Op. 22-III: Recapitulation concluded by several codettas
- Beethoven, Op. 2/3, Scherzo: Codettas (m. 56-64) hint at subdominant minor, becoming plagal in liquidated form at m. 61
- Mozart, String Quartet in A, K.V. 464: Codetta in m. 25-28 alongside contrapuntal display

# Common Errors

- Confusing codettas with codas (codettas are smaller)
- Confusing codettas with extensions (extensions occur before the cadence; codettas after)
- Failing to progressively shorten multiple codettas

# Common Confusions

- **Codetta vs. coda**: Codettas are short cadential reaffirmations; codas are larger closing sections that counterbalance the entire form's harmonic movement.
- **Codetta vs. extension**: Extensions expand the phrase before the cadence; codettas add material after it.
- **Progressive shortening**: Multiple codettas should get progressively shorter, not maintain the same length -- this mirrors the liquidation process.
