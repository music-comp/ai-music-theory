# Quick Definition
The most effective factor in creating contrast in the B-section is harmony: the B-section contraposes a closely related region to the tonic established by the A-section, providing both contrast and coherence simultaneously.

# Formal Definition
The A-section establishes a tonic; the B-section contraposes another region (one of the more closely related). This provides both contrast and coherence. The B-section may begin on various degrees (V, i, v, ii, iii, II, IV, iv, vi), and all but one can be joined back to an A-section ending on I. The use of minor tonic and minor subdominant effectively contrasts with the return of major in the recapitulation.

# Construction/Recognition
Harmonic contrast in the B-section is achieved through: (1) establishing a different harmonic region from the A-section's tonic, (2) using pedal points (especially on V) to suggest a contrasting region while remaining connected, (3) the mere interchange of I and V in the B-section contrasting with the A-section's interchange of the same, (4) using minor tonic or subdominant for modal contrast. The B-section typically ends on V to prepare the return of the tonic in the recapitulation.

# Musical Context
The student should exploit all degrees in B-section beginnings to expand harmonic knowledge, even at the risk of overburdening so small a form. This develops harmonic resources and skill for larger and more complex forms. The principle of "multiple meaning" allows chords to be reinterpreted between regions.

# Examples
- Beethoven Op. 2/1-II: the mere interchange of V and I in the B-section is a coherent contrast to I-V at the beginning
- Beethoven Op. 31/1-III: unusually, the B-section starts with a tonic pedal and ends on the tonic, because the A-section and recapitulation stand upon dominant pedals; the usual I-V relation is reversed
- Haydn Ex. 102b: B-section begins on the flat mediant (bIII), reinterpreted as III of the tonic minor via multiple meaning
- Ex. 105: ten different B-sections beginning on V, i, v, ii, iii, II, IV, iv, vi, demonstrating the range of harmonic possibilities

# Common Confusions
- Harmonic contrast need not mean remote modulation; even a simple I-V interchange can provide effective contrast
- The B-section should use closely related regions; excessively remote harmonies may fail to balance the surrounding sections
- The reversal of the usual I-V relationship (as in Op. 31/1-III) is rare but demonstrates that the principle is flexible
