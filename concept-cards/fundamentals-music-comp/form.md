# Quick Definition
The organization of a musical piece into functionally related parts, analogous to a living organism, making the music intelligible rather than an amorphous mass.

# Formal Definition
Form, used in the aesthetic sense, means that a piece is organized -- that it consists of elements functioning like those of a living organism. The term is used in several senses: in connection with binary, ternary, or rondo form it refers chiefly to the number of parts; "sonata form" suggests the size of parts and the complexity of their interrelationships; dance forms (minuet, scherzo) refer to metre, tempo, and rhythmic characteristics.

# Construction/Recognition
Form is achieved through the interplay of logic, coherence, and comprehensibility. The presentation, development, and interconnection of ideas must be based on relationship. Ideas must be differentiated according to their importance and function. Appropriate subdivision facilitates understanding and determines the form.

# Musical Context
Without organization, music would be "as unintelligible as an essay without punctuation, or as disconnected as a conversation which leaps purposelessly from one subject to another." A composer conceives an entire composition as a spontaneous vision, then proceeds to form the material directly -- like Michelangelo chiselling his Moses out of marble without sketches.

# Examples
- Binary, ternary, and rondo forms illustrate form as number of parts
- Sonata form illustrates form as complexity of interrelationships
- Minuet and scherzo illustrate form as rhythmic and metric character

# Common Confusions
- Form is not merely the number of sections in a piece; it encompasses organization at every level from the smallest phrase to the entire composition
- The size and number of parts does not always depend on the size of the piece -- "a midget has the same number of limbs, the same form, as a giant"
- Simplified practice forms used by students do not always correspond to art forms
