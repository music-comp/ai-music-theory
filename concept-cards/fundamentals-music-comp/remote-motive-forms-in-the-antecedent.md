# Quick Definition

M. 3-4 of the antecedent introduce more remote, contrasting motive-forms derived from but significantly different from the basic motive, providing the contrast that justifies the consequent's repetition.

# Core Definition

Since the consequent is a kind of repetition, the antecedent should be completed with more remote motive-forms in m. 3-4 (Schoenberg, p. 23). The contrast can be achieved through increase of smaller notes (following the "tendency of the smallest notes"), through decrease (appearing as a reduction), or through changes in register, direction, or contour. In some cases, coherence is more evident than the contrast, which consists merely of a change of register, direction, or contour. When coherence between basic motive-forms and remote derivatives is not obvious, a connective may bridge the gap (Exs. 44l, 45c, d, g, 46c, 47d). However, abrupt juxtaposition of contrasting forms need not necessarily produce imbalance (Ex. 45a).

# Prerequisites

- **Motive** -- Remote motive-forms are derivations of the basic motive
- **Connecting motive-forms** -- Understanding variation techniques is essential for deriving remote forms
- **Antecedent of the period** -- The remote forms serve the antecedent's structural role

# Key Properties

1. Appear in m. 3-4 of the antecedent (contrasting with m. 1-2)
2. Derived from the basic motive through progressive variation
3. Must maintain coherence despite being "remote" from the original
4. Can be achieved through increase or decrease of smaller note values
5. Changes of register, direction, or contour can provide sufficient contrast
6. A connective may bridge the gap when coherence is not obvious
7. Abrupt juxtaposition can work without creating imbalance
8. Building motive-forms from broken chords assures sound melody-harmony relationship

# Construction / Recognition

1. Start from the basic motive and derive more distant variations
2. Use one or more variation techniques: rhythmic increase/decrease, register change, direction change, contour change
3. If the connection is not obvious, insert a connective (often derived from the upbeat or a preceding motive-form)
4. Coordinate remote motive-forms with the harmonic progression
5. Build motive-forms from broken chords to assure a sound relationship between melody and harmony

# Context & Application

The remote motive-forms in m. 3-4 serve a double function: they provide the contrast that makes the antecedent distinct from a sentence opening (which would simply repeat), and they create the need for the consequent's repetition (since after contrast, repetition can no longer be postponed without endangering comprehensibility). Continuous and thorough study of examples from musical literature is essential for mastering this technique.

# Examples

- **Coherence more evident than contrast** (p. 23): Exs. 44f, h, i, j, l, 45f, 46a, f, and 47b, c, d, e -- contrast consists merely of a change of register, direction, or contour

- **Increase of small notes** (p. 23): Exs. 44a, b, c, d, g, k, 45a, b, c, d, and 46e show the tendency of the smallest notes driving the contrast

- **Decrease/reduction** (p. 23): Exs. 43a, 45i, 46g -- a decrease of smaller notes appears as a reduction

- **Connective bridging** (p. 23): Exs. 44l, 45c, d, g, 46c, 47d use an upbeat or preceding motive-form to bridge the gap

- **Haydn, Ex. 44f** (p. 22): Even with continuous sixteenth-notes, figuration and harmony of m. 3-4 are clearly differentiated

- **Mozart, Exs. 45b, g** (p. 23): Coordination of small notes with harmony as ancillary notes achieves exceptional perfection

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Making m. 3-4 too distant from the basic motive, losing coherence
  **Correction**: Even remote motive-forms must derive from and relate to the basic motive

- **Error**: Making m. 3-4 too similar to m. 1-2
  **Correction**: If m. 3-4 merely repeat, the result is a sentence opening, not an antecedent

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Believing "remote" means unrelated to the basic motive
  **Clarification**: Remote motive-forms are derived from the basic motive through progressive variation; they are distant but connected

- **Confusion**: Thinking a connective is always necessary
  **Clarification**: Abrupt juxtaposition of contrasting forms does not necessarily produce imbalance (Ex. 45a)

- **Confusion**: Expecting Mozart-level perfection in coordinating small notes with harmony
  **Clarification**: This is a skill that no beginner dare hope for; it develops through continuous study
