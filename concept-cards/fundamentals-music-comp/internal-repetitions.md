# Quick Definition
Sequential or varied repetitions of motivic elements within a phrase that complicate the construction and often produce irregular or uneven phrase lengths.

# Formal Definition
Internal repetitions are repetitions of motivic units that occur within a single phrase or sentence, as opposed to the large-scale repetition of entire sections. They may be sequential (transposed to different pitch levels) or varied (modified in contour, rhythm, or other features). When combined with units of differing lengths, they produce complex internal structures that depart from regular four-measure groupings.

# Construction/Recognition
- Identify recurring motivic patterns within a single phrase or sentence
- Distinguish between sequential repetitions (transposed) and varied repetitions (modified)
- Note whether the repeated elements are of equal or differing lengths
- Map the internal structure using measure counts (e.g., 3+3+4+2+2)
- Internal repetitions often produce sentences with uneven total measure counts

# Musical Context
Internal repetitions are a primary source of irregular phrase construction. They allow composers to develop motivic material within a single phrase while creating unexpected proportions. The technique is especially common in sentences, which are more frequently composed of elements of differing lengths than periods. The combination of internal repetitions with stretching and cadence evasion produces the most complex irregular structures.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Exs. 57, 59d, 60ff, 61c: Construction complicated by sequential or varied internal repetitions
- Ex. 60f: Internal repetitions of elements of differing lengths: 3(2+1)+3(2+1)+4(2+2)+2+2
- Ex. 59f: Five units totalling seven measures (1+2+1+1+2)

# Common Confusions
- Internal repetitions differ from the large-scale repetition of entire sections (recapitulation)
- Sequential repetition (transposition) should be distinguished from varied repetition (modification)
- The presence of internal repetitions does not always produce irregular construction -- they may be balanced to produce regular phrases
