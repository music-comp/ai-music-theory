# Quick Definition
The principle that melody implies an inherent harmony and that the coordination of melody with its harmonic underpinning is essential to coherence; melody and harmony are not independent dimensions but mutually defining.

# Core Definition
A melody could be called two-dimensional, comprising chiefly interval and latent harmony. Every melody implies a harmonic framework. Inadequate integration with the harmony or accompaniment is a criterion of the unmelodious. Imbalance, incoherence, inadequate integration with the harmony, and incongruity of phrasing and rhythm are the criteria by which the unmelodious is judged. A theme's harmony is often active, "roving," and unstable, yet even complex themes still move around a tonic or definite contrasting region.

# Key Properties
- Melody is two-dimensional: interval and latent harmony
- Every melody implies a harmonic framework
- Inadequate integration with harmony is a criterion of the unmelodious
- A theme's harmony may be roving and unstable but maintains a tonal center
- Melody and harmony are mutually defining, not independent dimensions
- Imbalance, incoherence, and incongruity of phrasing are markers of failed coordination

# Construction / Recognition
Evaluate melody-harmony coordination by: (1) checking whether the melody's intervals can be understood against its implied harmony, (2) ensuring that harmonic changes align with the phrasing, (3) verifying that the melody and its accompaniment support the same character, (4) checking for passages where the melody seems unrelated to any comprehensible harmony.

# Context & Application
The coordination of melody and harmony is fundamental to Schoenberg's approach. In homophonic-harmonic music, the essential content is concentrated in one voice, which implies an inherent harmony. Even instrumental melodies should have clear harmonic implications. The concept applies equally to vocal and instrumental writing.

# Examples
- Strauss, Salome (Ex. 80b): the multitude of incoherent rhythmic features produces imbalance, making phrasing incomprehensible; it would be difficult to imagine what harmony could integrate the melody
- Melodies proceed in waves, and their harmonic dimension supports this wave-like quality
- Melodies remain within closer harmonic relationships; themes may use roving harmony

# Common Errors
- Writing melody without consciousness of its implied harmony
- Creating melody-harmony mismatches that produce incoherence
- Allowing too remote harmonies to disconnect the melody from comprehensibility

# Common Confusions
- Melody does not exist independently of harmony in homophonic music; every melody implies a harmonic framework
- Being "conscious of harmony" does not require simple harmony; it means understanding the harmonic implications of the melodic line
