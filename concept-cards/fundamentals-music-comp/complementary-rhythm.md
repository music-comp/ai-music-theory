# Quick Definition
The rhythmic relationship between voices in which one voice fills out the gaps in another's movement, thereby maintaining the motus -- the regular subdivision of the measure.

# Formal Definition
Complementary rhythm is that relation between voices or groups of voices in which one voice fills out the gaps in the movement of the others, thus maintaining the motus, i.e., the regular subdivision of the measure. The accompaniment is often added as a complement to the basic rhythm of the principal part. Some cases derive from semi-contrapuntal devices, where the melodic independence of the filling voice gives the texture a contrapuntal character even though the primary purpose is rhythmic complementation.

# Construction/Recognition
- The accompaniment enters where the melody rests or sustains
- Together, melody and accompaniment produce continuous rhythmic activity
- The motus (regular beat subdivision) is maintained even when individual voices have gaps
- May involve simple harmonic filling or more melodically independent voice movement
- Hybrid cases exist where complementary rhythm combines with other accompaniment types

# Musical Context
Complementary rhythm is one of the most fundamental accompaniment techniques, ensuring that the sense of ongoing pulse is never lost even when the principal voice pauses. It creates textural richness and forward motion while maintaining the structural clarity of the individual voices. The technique bridges simple homophonic accompaniment and semi-contrapuntal writing.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 14/2-I, m. 1-4, 26-27: Accompaniment complements melody rhythm
- Beethoven, Op. 109-I, m. 1-8: Clear complementary rhythm
- Exs. 67a, b: Direct illustrations
- Beethoven, Op. 2/2, Scherzo: Accompaniment as complement to basic rhythm
- Exs. 45d, 66a, 67c: Cases deriving from semi-contrapuntal devices
- Hybrid cases: Op. 10/3-IV, m. 17-22; Op. 31/2-III, m. 17-21; Op. 54-I, m. 1-2-5-6

# Common Confusions
- Complementary rhythm is not counterpoint; its primary purpose is rhythmic filling, not melodic independence (though the two may overlap).
- The motus is not the same as the meter; it is the regular subdivision within the meter that provides continuous rhythmic pulse.
- Complementary rhythm does not require the accompaniment to be silent when the melody is active; it requires filling the gaps.
