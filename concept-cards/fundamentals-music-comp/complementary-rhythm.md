# Quick Definition

The rhythmic relationship between voices in which one voice fills out the gaps in another's movement, maintaining the motus -- the regular subdivision of the measure.

# Core Definition

Complementary rhythm is that relation between voices or groups of voices in which one voice fills out the gaps in the movement of the others, thus maintaining the motus, i.e., the regular subdivision of the measure. The accompaniment is often added as a complement to the basic rhythm of the principal part (Schoenberg, p. 53).

Some cases derive from semi-contrapuntal devices, where the melodic independence of the filling voice gives the texture a contrapuntal character even though the primary purpose is rhythmic complementation. There exist many hybrid cases.

# Prerequisites

- **Accompaniment function** -- Complementary rhythm is one way the accompaniment functions
- **Motive of the accompaniment** -- The complementary figure often constitutes the accompaniment motive

# Key Properties

1. One voice fills gaps in another's movement
2. Together, all voices maintain the motus (regular beat subdivision)
3. The motus is not the same as the meter; it is the regular subdivision within the meter
4. May involve simple harmonic filling or more melodically independent voice movement
5. Some cases derive from semi-contrapuntal devices
6. Many hybrid cases combining complementary rhythm with other types

# Construction / Recognition

1. Identify where the melody rests or sustains
2. Have the accompaniment enter during those gaps
3. Together, melody and accompaniment should produce continuous rhythmic activity
4. The motus should be maintained even when individual voices have gaps
5. Balance between harmonic filling and melodic independence

# Context & Application

Complementary rhythm is one of the most fundamental accompaniment techniques, ensuring that the sense of ongoing pulse is never lost. It creates textural richness and forward motion while maintaining structural clarity. The technique bridges simple homophonic accompaniment and semi-contrapuntal writing.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Writing complementary rhythm that competes with the melody
  **Correction**: The primary purpose is rhythmic filling, not melodic independence

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Equating complementary rhythm with counterpoint
  **Clarification**: Its primary purpose is rhythmic filling, not melodic independence (though the two may overlap)

- **Confusion**: Confusing motus with meter
  **Clarification**: Motus is the regular subdivision within the meter that provides continuous rhythmic pulse
