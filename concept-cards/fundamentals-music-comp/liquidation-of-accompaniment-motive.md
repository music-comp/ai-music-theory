# Quick Definition
Near cadences or half cadences, harmonic requirements often force the modification, liquidation, or abandonment of the accompaniment motive, paralleling the liquidation process in the thematic voice.

# Formal Definition
The motive of the accompaniment, as a unifying device, should be maintained for at least several measures or phrases. Even if varied, it should not disappear completely during the body of a theme. However, harmonic requirements in the vicinity of cadences or half cadences often force modification or liquidation of the motive of accompaniment. Changes of character or construction, or increase in the number of harmonies, may justify or even require modification of the accompaniment. This process takes a variety of forms, from gradual simplification to outright abandonment and replacement.

# Construction/Recognition
- Modification near cadences due to increased harmonic activity
- Gradual simplification of the accompaniment figure
- Reduction of the figure to its essential rhythmic or harmonic component
- Abandonment and replacement when character or construction changes significantly
- The motive should not disappear completely during non-cadential passages
- More than one figure may appear, especially in Mozart's Rococo style

# Musical Context
The liquidation of the accompaniment motive parallels the thematic liquidation that occurs in the melody. Both processes work together toward structural closure. The accompaniment motive's simplification or disappearance near cadences is a natural result of the increased harmonic complexity that cadences require -- the motive may no longer accommodate the rapid harmonic changes. This parallel process reinforces the cadential effect across all musical dimensions simultaneously.

# Examples
- Exs. 45f, 46f, 48c, 51a, 55a, b, 56b, 58c, 59g, h, 61d: Various forms of accompaniment motive modification or liquidation near cadences
- Op. 14/2-I: Clear change in accompaniment at m. 5 ff. compared with m. 1-4
- Op. 31/1-II: Accompaniment motive modified in m. 5 where climactic melodic ascension requires richer harmony
- Op. 31/3-III: Special broken-chord figure reduced in m. 3 ff. to what is necessary to maintain motus

# Common Confusions
- Liquidation of the accompaniment motive is not a failure to maintain consistency; it is a structural necessity near cadences.
- The accompaniment motive should be maintained through the body of the theme; only near cadences does modification or liquidation become appropriate.
- Replacement by a different figure (as in Mozart's Rococo style) is distinct from liquidation; both are legitimate but serve different purposes.
