# Quick Definition
Augmented and diminished intervals, successive large leaps, and chromatic passages that present intonation difficulties for singers and should be used with caution in vocal melody.

# Core Definition
Augmented and diminished intervals present varying degrees of difficulty in vocal music. Not all such intervals, when accompanied by comprehensible harmony, are excessively difficult, but many are not easy. Successive leaps having a compass of a seventh or ninth should be avoided. These intervals challenge the singer's intonation and may produce an impression that is more instrumental than vocal.

# Key Properties
- Augmented and diminished intervals are difficult but not always impossible
- Harmonic context determines the actual difficulty level
- Successive leaps spanning a seventh or ninth should be avoided
- Complex intervals may produce an instrumental rather than vocal impression
- The boundaries of singability have expanded historically, especially since Wagner
- Hanslick quoted Wagner's voice writing to ridicule its "melodiousness"

# Construction / Recognition
Identify non-singable intervals by evaluating: (1) augmented or diminished intervals, especially in succession, (2) successive leaps spanning a seventh or ninth, (3) chromatic passages without clear harmonic support, (4) intervals that cannot be related to the underlying harmony, especially when outside the tonality.

# Context & Application
The boundaries of what is "singable" have expanded historically. Wagner, for dramatic purposes, often went beyond the limits of the voice. Debussy and Strauss took advantage of this progress in voice writing -- progress that "did not turn out to enhance singability." The Italian tradition also embraced more challenging intervals under Wagner's influence.

# Common Errors
- Avoiding all augmented and diminished intervals categorically, when some are manageable with harmonic support
- Writing successive large leaps without compensation
- Ignoring the instrumental impression that non-singable intervals create

# Common Confusions
- "Non-singable" does not mean absolutely impossible; trained virtuosi can perform these intervals
- The difficulty depends heavily on harmonic context: an augmented interval with clear harmonic support is far easier than one without
