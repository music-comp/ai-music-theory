# Quick Definition
Augmented and diminished intervals, successive large leaps, and chromatic passages that present intonation difficulties for singers and should be used with caution in vocal melody.

# Formal Definition
Augmented and diminished intervals present varying degrees of difficulty in vocal music. Not all such intervals, when accompanied by comprehensible harmony, are excessively difficult, but many are not easy. Successive leaps having a compass of a seventh or ninth should be avoided. These intervals challenge the singer's intonation and may produce an impression that is more instrumental than vocal.

# Construction/Recognition
Identify non-singable intervals by evaluating: (1) augmented or diminished intervals, especially in succession, (2) successive leaps spanning a seventh or ninth, (3) chromatic passages without clear harmonic support, (4) intervals or progressions that cannot be related to the underlying harmony, especially when outside the tonality.

# Musical Context
The boundaries of what is "singable" have expanded historically. Wagner, for dramatic purposes, often went beyond the limits of the voice, using difficult and dissonant intervals within short phrases. Debussy and Strauss, as followers of Wagner, took advantage of this progress in voice writing -- progress that did not enhance singability. The Italian tradition, with its respect for the voice, also eventually embraced more challenging intervals under Wagner's influence.

# Common Confusions
- "Non-singable" does not mean absolutely impossible; trained virtuosi can perform these intervals, but they remain difficult and potentially problematic for general vocal writing
- The difficulty depends heavily on harmonic context: an augmented interval with clear harmonic support is far easier than one without
