# Quick Definition
True contrapuntal style appears in otherwise homophonic music when fugues or fugatos are incorporated, either as movements beginning with fugato, as episodes within elaboration sections, or as concluding fugues in variation sets.

# Formal Definition
A real contrapuntal style appears occasionally when fugues or fugatos are incorporated in otherwise homophonic music. Sometimes a movement begins with a fugato, though such sections are often repeated in a more developed form while the remainder should generally be classified as homophonic. In the elaboration (development) sections of larger forms, there are frequently fugato episodes using imitations or invertible counterpoint. Some composers conclude sets of variations with real fugues, and some sonata finales are themselves fugues.

# Construction/Recognition
- Fugato openings in otherwise homophonic movements (Op. 10/2-III; Mozart, String Quartet in G, Finale; Beethoven, Op. 59/3, Finale)
- Fugato episodes in elaboration sections (Beethoven, Op. 59/1-I; Eroica Symphony, first and fourth movements; Brahms, Piano Quintet, third movement)
- Concluding fugues in variation sets (Beethoven, Op. 120, Op. 35; Brahms, Op. 24)
- Sonata finales as fugues (Beethoven, Op. 106, Op. 110)
- Contrapuntal episodes using imitations or invertible counterpoint in elaboration sections (Op. 2/3-I, m. 115 ff.; Op. 22, Rondo, m. 81 ff.; Op. 81a-I, Coda)
- Unusually long canonic passages (Op. 106-I, E-flat section)

# Musical Context
Contrapuntal treatment within homophonic music represents a textural enrichment and intensification. It typically appears at structurally significant moments: beginnings of movements (establishing seriousness), elaboration sections (heightening developmental tension), and conclusions (providing culmination). The contrapuntal texture provides maximum independence of voices and creates the most complex form of "accompaniment," where accompaniment becomes genuinely co-equal voicing.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 10/2-III: Movement begins with fugato
- Beethoven, Op. 59/3, Finale: Fugato opening
- Beethoven, Op. 59/1-I: Fugato episode in elaboration
- Beethoven, Eroica Symphony (first and fourth movements): Contrapuntal episodes in elaboration
- Beethoven, Op. 106 and Op. 110: Sonata finales as fugues
- Beethoven, Op. 120; Brahms, Op. 24: Variation sets concluding with fugues

# Common Confusions
- Contrapuntal passages within homophonic works do not make those works "contrapuntal" overall; the classification remains homophonic with contrapuntal episodes.
- A fugato is not a complete fugue; it uses fugal exposition technique without necessarily following through with full fugal development.
- Contrapuntal treatment in elaboration sections serves the development process, not merely textural variety.
