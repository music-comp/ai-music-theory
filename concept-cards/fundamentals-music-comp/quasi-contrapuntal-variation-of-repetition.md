# Quick Definition
A technique for varying a repeated section by adding quasi-contrapuntal treatment to the accompanying lower voices while leaving the melody and harmony unchanged, providing textural enrichment without altering the thematic substance.

# Formal Definition
Quasi-contrapuntal variation of a repetition involves keeping the melody and harmony of the original statement intact while varying the texture through added melodic activity in the accompanying voices. This is not true counterpoint (which involves independent thematic voices) but rather embellishment and melodization of otherwise unimportant harmonic voices. It provides a means of creating varied repetition at a larger scale -- entire sections can be repeated with new textural interest.

# Construction/Recognition
- Melody and harmony remain unchanged from the original statement
- Lower or inner voices are given more melodic independence
- The added voice movement embellishes and vitalizes the harmonic voices
- Does not involve genuine contrapuntal techniques (invertible counterpoint, canonic imitation)
- Provides textural contrast without structural alteration

# Musical Context
This technique is particularly useful for the Romantic style, where large-scale repetition is common but literal repetition risks monotony. By varying only the accompanimental texture, the composer maintains the recognizability essential for comprehensibility while adding freshness and interest. The technique bridges the gap between simple homophonic accompaniment and genuine contrapuntal writing.

# Examples
- Brahms, Ex. 51c: A repetition (m. 17-32), unchanged in melody and harmony, is varied by supplying quasi-contrapuntal treatment of the accompanying lower voices

# Common Confusions
- Quasi-contrapuntal variation is not true counterpoint; it lacks independent thematic voices and contrapuntal devices like invertible counterpoint or imitation.
- The term "quasi-contrapuntal" specifically means the voice movement embellishes rather than carrying independent thematic significance.
- This technique applies to larger-scale repetition (whole sections), not typically to the measure-by-measure variation within a consequent.
