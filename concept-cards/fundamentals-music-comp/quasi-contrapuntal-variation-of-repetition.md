# Quick Definition

A technique for varying a repeated section by adding quasi-contrapuntal treatment to the accompanying voices while leaving the melody and harmony essentially unchanged, providing textural enrichment without altering the thematic substance.

# Core Definition

Quasi-contrapuntal variation involves keeping the melody and harmony of the original statement essentially intact while varying the texture through added melodic activity in the accompanying voices. This is not true counterpoint (which involves independent thematic voices with strict contrapuntal procedures) but rather embellishment and melodization of otherwise unimportant harmonic voices. It provides a means of creating varied repetition at a larger scale -- entire sections can be repeated with new textural interest. Quasi-contrapuntal pianistic imitations contribute to coherence within variations.

# Key Properties

- Melody and harmony remain essentially unchanged from the original statement
- Lower or inner voices are given more melodic independence
- Does not involve genuine contrapuntal procedures (invertible counterpoint, strict canon)
- Provides textural contrast without structural alteration
- Contributes to coherence through imitative figures
- Found in Brahms's Handel Variations (Vars. 16, 18, 23, 24)

# Construction / Recognition

1. Compare the passage with the original statement -- melody and harmony should be essentially unchanged
2. Look for increased melodic activity in accompanying voices
3. Check for imitative figures that create a contrapuntal impression
4. Verify that the treatment does not involve genuine strict counterpoint
5. Distinguish from true contrapuntal variation (canons, fugues, invertible counterpoint)

# Context & Application

This technique bridges the gap between simple homophonic accompaniment and genuine contrapuntal writing. It is particularly useful where large-scale repetition is needed but literal repetition would risk monotony. By varying only the accompanimental texture, the composer maintains recognizability while adding freshness.

# Examples

- Brahms, Handel Variations, Vars. 16, 18, 23, 24: Quasi-contrapuntal pianistic imitations contribute to coherence
- Brahms, Ex. 51c: Repetition (m. 17-32) unchanged in melody and harmony, varied by quasi-contrapuntal treatment of accompanying lower voices

# Common Errors

- Treating quasi-contrapuntal imitations as strict counterpoint in analysis
- Neglecting to maintain the melody and harmony unchanged (which distinguishes this from genuine contrapuntal variation)

# Common Confusions

- **Quasi-contrapuntal vs. true counterpoint**: Quasi-contrapuntal treatment embellishes and melodizes accompanying voices without the rigor of strict contrapuntal procedures. True counterpoint involves independent thematic voices with procedures like inversion, canon, or fugue.
