# Quick Definition

A simplified, standardized version of the scherzo's modulatory middle section, designed as a pedagogical tool that replaces the freedom of artistic composition with guiding restrictions, featuring a pattern followed by a sequence, liquidation, and pedal point.

# Core Definition

The practice form is an abstraction that differs from reality by replacing freedom with guiding restrictions. It is designed to acquaint students with the modulatory contrasting middle section through controlled exercises. In the practice form, a pattern is followed by a sequence, beginning on a degree related to the ending of the A-section. The modulation must be organized to promote intelligibility, proceeding gradually according to a plan that provides for returning to the tonic. Clearly delimited segments of reasonable length should be provided, with repetitions built as patterns for sequences.

# Key Properties

- Pattern: a clearly delimited thematic segment built as a model for transposition
- Sequence: the transposition of the pattern to another degree
- The pattern begins on a degree related to the A-section's ending (V, v, III, i, iii, bIII, ii, iv, VI, etc.)
- The end of the last sequence must offer an opening for return to the upbeat harmony
- Motive-forms from the A-section are modified for the modulatory procedure
- After the sequence: reduced pattern, liquidation, pedal point, recapitulation
- If the sequence ends too remotely, additional reduced sequences are needed

# Construction / Recognition

1. Establish a pattern derived from A-section motive-forms (typically 2-4 measures)
2. Transpose to create a sequence on a logically related degree
3. Reduce the pattern (typically to half its length) for further sequences
4. Liquidate by dissolving characteristic features into scales, broken chords, etc.
5. Arrive at the upbeat harmony (usually dominant)
6. Use a pedal point for repose and suspense before the recapitulation

# Context & Application

Schoenberg emphasizes that the practice form is explicitly pedagogical and must be supplemented with analysis of master works. The diversity of construction in actual compositions far exceeds what any practice form can represent. However, the practice form teaches essential principles: sequential modulation, liquidation, structural control, and the balance between emphasis (sequence) and neutralization (liquidation).

# Examples

# Common Errors

- Following the practice form rigidly in actual composition (it is a teaching tool, not a recipe)
- Failing to plan the sequence ending to connect with the upbeat harmony
- Neglecting liquidation after the sequential process

# Common Confusions

- **Practice form vs. reality**: The practice form is an abstraction; actual scherzos show "inexhaustible diversity of construction."
- **Limitation vs. variety**: Even within the practice form, Ex. 122 shows twenty-five different possibilities from a single A-section, demonstrating that the restrictions still allow enormous variety.
