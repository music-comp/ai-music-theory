# Quick Definition
A simplified, standardized version of the scherzo's modulatory middle section, designed as a pedagogical tool that replaces the freedom of artistic composition with guiding restrictions for students learning the technique.

# Formal Definition
The practice form is an abstraction that differs from reality by replacing freedom with guiding restrictions. It is designed to acquaint students with the modulatory contrasting middle section through controlled exercises. In the practice form, a pattern is followed by a sequence, beginning on a degree related to the ending of the A-section. The modulation must be organized to promote intelligibility, proceeding gradually according to a plan that provides for returning to the tonic. Clearly delimited segments of reasonable length should be provided, with repetitions built as patterns for sequences.

# Construction/Recognition
- Pattern: a clearly delimited thematic segment built as a model for transposition
- Sequence: the transposition of the pattern to another degree
- The pattern should begin on a degree related to the A-section's ending (V, v, III, i, iii, bIII, ii, iv, VI, etc.)
- The end of the last sequence must offer an opening for return to the upbeat harmony
- Motive-forms from the A-section are modified for the modulatory procedure
- The combination of motive-forms may resemble the antecedent of a period or middle section of a ternary form
- After the sequence: reduced pattern, liquidation, pedal point, recapitulation

# Musical Context
Schoenberg emphasizes that composition in the practice form must be supplemented with analysis of master works, because the diversity of construction in actual compositions far exceeds what any practice form can represent. The practice form is explicitly a pedagogical tool -- a stepping stone toward the freedom exercised by master composers. It teaches the principles of sequential modulation, liquidation, and structural control.

# Common Confusions
- The practice form is not how actual scherzos are composed -- it is a teaching abstraction
- The practice form should not be followed rigidly in actual composition; it is a starting point
- The twenty-five examples in Ex. 122 show that even within the practice form, enormous variety is possible
