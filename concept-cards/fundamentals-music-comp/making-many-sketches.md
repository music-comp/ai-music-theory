# Quick Definition
The humble practice of producing multiple tentative versions of a passage, changing methods of variation frequently, combining the best results, and improving until satisfied -- treating all early work as provisional.

# Formal Definition
To make sketches is a humble and unpretentious approach toward perfection. Change the method of variation frequently. Try each method several times. Join the best sketches to produce others and improve them until the result is satisfactory. A beginner who is not too self-assured and who knows that technical maturity has not yet been reached will consider everything written as tentative. Later the composer will be able to base composing exclusively on the sense of form.

# Construction/Recognition
The practice of sketching involves: (1) producing multiple versions of a passage using different variation methods, (2) evaluating each version critically, (3) combining the best elements from different versions, (4) iterating until the result is satisfactory, (5) frequently reviewing methods of motive variation as a "warm-up." Methodical variation is no substitute for invention, but it may be stimulating.

# Musical Context
This advice reflects Schoenberg's pedagogical emphasis on craft alongside inspiration. The student should frequently review the methods of motive variation. Methodical variation is like an athlete's warm-up -- not the performance itself, but essential preparation for it.

# Common Confusions
- Sketching is not a sign of inability; it is the working method of serious composers
- Methodical variation (the warm-up) is not the same as genuine invention, but it develops the technical facility that serves invention
