# Quick Definition

The use of shared or related rhythmic patterns as a unifying element when connecting motive-forms, one of the most effective means of producing coherence.

# Core Definition

"Rhythmic similarities act as unifying elements" in the connection of motive-forms (Ch. 4, p. 11). As established in Chapter 3, "preservation of rhythmic features effectively produces coherence" (Ch. 3, p. 8). Rhythmic similarity is the second of three principal factors (alongside common content and coherent harmony) that contribute to logic in the connection of motive-forms.

# Prerequisites

- **motive** -- Must understand the motive's rhythmic features
- **variation** -- Must understand how variation changes some features while preserving others

# Key Properties

1. Rhythmic similarity is one of three fundamental connecting principles
2. Rhythm is the most cohesion-producing feature of a motive
3. Preserving rhythm while changing other features produces closely related motive-forms
4. Rhythmic identity can maintain coherence even through substantial intervallic change
5. Slight rhythmic changes are still needed to avoid monotony
6. The technique of preserving rhythm with transposition is standard in traditional music

# Construction / Recognition

1. Preserve the rhythmic profile of the basic motive while changing other features
2. Use related rhythmic patterns that share key characteristics
3. Maintain consistent rhythmic patterns even when intervals and contour change
4. When combining motive-forms, prioritize rhythmic similarity for coherence
5. Allow slight rhythmic variation to prevent monotony

# Context & Application

Rhythm is the most reliable connecting device because it is the most immediately perceivable feature. When rhythm is preserved, even substantial changes in intervals and direction produce recognizable relationships. This is demonstrated most clearly in Ex. 32, where rhythm is strictly preserved while intervals and direction change, a procedure "often used in traditional music to produce entire themes" (Ch. 4, p. 12).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Preserving rhythm without any variation, producing monotony
  **Correction**: "Monotony cannot be avoided without slight changes" even to rhythmic features

- **Error**: Neglecting rhythm when varying a motive, losing coherence
  **Correction**: Rhythm is the most cohesion-producing feature; preserve it unless there is a specific compositional reason not to

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Equating rhythmic similarity with identical rhythm
  **Clarification**: Related patterns also unify; strict identity is not always required

- **Confusion**: Assuming rhythm alone guarantees coherence
  **Clarification**: Rhythmic similarity is the most reliable device but works best in conjunction with common content and coherent harmony
