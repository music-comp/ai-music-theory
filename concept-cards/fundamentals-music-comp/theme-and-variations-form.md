# Quick Definition
A structural form consisting of a theme followed by several variations upon it, where variation serves as the structural principle for the entire piece, representing an approach to the logic of larger compositions.

# Formal Definition
As the name indicates, the piece consists of a theme and several variations upon it. The term "variation" has multiple meanings in music: it creates motive-forms for constructing themes, produces contrast in middle sections, and provides variety in repetitions. But in "Theme and Variations" it is the structural principle for a whole piece. Production of an entire piece merely through the application of variation is an approach to the logic of larger compositions. The number of variations depends on whether it is a movement in a cyclic work (fewer variations) or an independent piece (more variations).

# Construction/Recognition
- A theme is presented, followed by a series of variations
- Each variation preserves the main structural features of the theme
- The course of events should not be changed, even if the character is changed
- The number and order of segments remains the same
- Proportions and structural relations are preserved
- Metre and tempo may be changed; measure count may be systematically multiplied by two or three
- Every variation should possess the same quality of formal self-sufficiency and coherence as the theme
- Often concluded by a coda, finale, or fugue

# Musical Context
The form originated perhaps in the custom of repeating a pleasant theme several times, avoiding decline of interest by introducing embellishments and additions. The classic masters made it a point that the theme should be recognizable in the variation. The form appears both as an independent piece and as a movement within cyclic works (sonatas, symphonies).

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 26-I, Op. 14/2-II, Op. 111-II: Movements in cyclic works (fewer variations)
- Beethoven, 32 Variations in c minor: Independent piece with many variations
- Beethoven, 33 Diabelli Variations, Op. 120: Independent piece with extensive variation set
- Bach, Goldberg Variations: Independent piece based on the composer's own theme
- Brahms, String Quartet Op. 67-IV: Variation movement based on the composer's own theme

# Common Confusions
- "Variation" as a compositional technique (used throughout all music) is different from "Theme and Variations" as a formal structure
- The viewpoint as to which features are "main" is subject to change between variations
- Not all variations preserve every feature of the theme -- the question is which features are considered essential
