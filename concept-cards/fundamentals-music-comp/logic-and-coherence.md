# Quick Definition
The chief requirements for creating comprehensible musical form: that ideas are presented, developed, and interconnected based on relationship, and differentiated according to importance and function.

# Formal Definition
Logic and coherence are the chief requirements for the creation of a comprehensible form. The presentation, development, and interconnection of ideas must be based on relationship. Ideas must be differentiated according to their importance and function. These principles ensure that music communicates intelligibly rather than appearing as disconnected or amorphous.

# Construction/Recognition
Logic is achieved when musical ideas relate to one another through shared motivic, rhythmic, or harmonic content. Coherence arises when these relationships are perceivable by the listener -- when the connections between ideas create a continuous, comprehensible flow. Together they prevent music from becoming "as unintelligible as an essay without punctuation."

# Musical Context
Logic and coherence operate at every structural level, from the connection between adjacent motive-forms within a phrase to the relationship between large sections of a complete work. They are the foundational principles underlying all of Schoenberg's compositional pedagogy.

# Examples
- An essay without punctuation (Schoenberg's analogy for music without logic)
- A conversation that leaps purposelessly from subject to subject (analogy for incoherent music)

# Common Confusions
- Logic does not mean rigidity; it means that relationships between ideas are perceivable
- Coherence is not the same as sameness -- variety and contrast are also necessary, but must be governed by logical relationships
- Logic operates through musical relationships (motivic, rhythmic, harmonic), not through extra-musical narrative
