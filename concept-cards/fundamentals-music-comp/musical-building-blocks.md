# Quick Definition

The fundamental structural units of music -- phrases, motives, and other small elements -- that provide material for building larger units of various kinds according to the requirements of the structure.

# Core Definition

"These musical blocks (phrases, motives, etc.) will provide the material for building larger units of various kinds, according to the requirements of the structure. Thus the demands of logic, coherence and comprehensibility can be fulfilled, in relation to the need for contrast, variety and fluency of presentation" (Ch. 1, p. 2). Schoenberg uses "part" in the most general sense to indicate undifferentiated elements, sections, or subdivisions of a piece, with more specific terms introduced later to distinguish parts of various sizes and functions.

# Key Properties

1. The smallest blocks are phrases and motives
2. Blocks combine into larger units according to structural requirements
3. Combination must fulfill logic, coherence, and comprehensibility
4. Combination must also address contrast, variety, and fluency of presentation
5. The structural hierarchy proceeds from motive to phrase to theme to section to movement

# Construction / Recognition

1. Begin with the smallest musical units: motives and phrases
2. Connect them intelligently according to structural requirements
3. Build progressively larger units from smaller ones
4. Ensure each level satisfies both comprehensibility and variety
5. Proceed gradually from simpler to more complex structures

# Context & Application

Schoenberg explicitly warns against a mechanical understanding of building blocks: "A composer does not, of course, add bit by bit, as a child does in building with wooden blocks. He conceives an entire composition as a spontaneous vision" (Ch. 1, p. 1). The building-block metaphor is pedagogical -- the beginner must learn construction from the ground up because he "is not capable of envisaging a composition in its entirety" (Ch. 1, p. 2). This bottom-up approach is the organizing principle of the entire book.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating composition as a mechanical assembly of blocks
  **Correction**: Schoenberg explicitly warns against this; composition is organic, not mechanical

- **Error**: Starting with large-scale form before mastering small units
  **Correction**: The pedagogical approach requires mastering blocks before attempting larger structures

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Believing the "building blocks" metaphor describes how composers actually work
  **Clarification**: Mature composers conceive compositions as spontaneous visions; the block-building approach is a pedagogical method for beginners

- **Confusion**: Treating "part" as a specific structural term
  **Clarification**: Schoenberg uses "part" in the most general sense; specific terms (phrase, motive, segment, section) are introduced later for precise structural levels
