# Quick Definition
The fundamental structural units of music -- phrases, motives, and other small elements -- that provide material for building larger units according to structural requirements.

# Formal Definition
Musical blocks (phrases, motives, etc.) provide the material for building larger units of various kinds, according to the requirements of the structure. Through their intelligent connection, the demands of logic, coherence, and comprehensibility can be fulfilled, in relation to the need for contrast, variety, and fluency of presentation. Schoenberg uses the term "part" in the most general sense to indicate undifferentiated elements, sections, or subdivisions of a piece.

# Construction/Recognition
The beginner proceeds from simpler to more complex structures, starting by building small musical blocks and connecting them intelligently. Simplified practice forms help students acquire the sense of form and knowledge of the essentials of construction. Schoenberg's structural hierarchy from smallest to largest: motive, phrase, sentence/period, theme, section, movement.

# Musical Context
A composer does not "add bit by bit, as a child does in building with wooden blocks" -- a mature composer conceives an entire composition as a spontaneous vision. But for pedagogical purposes, Schoenberg teaches construction from the ground up, beginning with the smallest units. This bottom-up approach is the organizing principle of the entire book.

# Examples
- Phrases and motives as the smallest building blocks
- These blocks combine into themes (sentences and periods)
- Themes combine into larger formal sections

# Common Confusions
- The metaphor of "building blocks" should not suggest that composition is a mechanical assembly process; Schoenberg explicitly warns against this misconception
- "Part" is used in the most general sense and does not imply a specific structural level -- more specific terms (phrase, motive, segment, section) are introduced later
- Practice forms are pedagogical tools, not descriptions of how composers actually work
