# Quick Definition
The progressive shortening of a motivic pattern to its smallest identifiable fragments (residues), often treated in an ostinato-like manner over a dominant pedal point, as the final stage of liquidation before the recapitulation.

# Formal Definition
Reduction to residues is the process whereby the motivic pattern used in the sequential elaboration is progressively shortened to its smallest recognizable elements. These residues -- typically fragments of one or two notes -- may be dwelt upon in an ostinato-like treatment over the dominant pedal. The technique represents the final stage of liquidation, where the characteristic features of the pattern have been almost entirely dissolved, leaving only the barest motivic remnants to sustain the musical texture before the recapitulation.

# Construction/Recognition
- The pattern is reduced in stages: full pattern to half, to quarter, to individual notes
- Residues are treated in an ostinato-like manner (repeated insistently)
- Usually occurs over a dominant pedal point
- Scale lines, broken chords, and other "uncharacteristic" forms may accompany the residues
- Marks the transition from the modulatory instability of the B-section to the stability of the recapitulation
- The repose of the pedal and the suspense of the diminished pattern create the conditions for the theme's re-entrance

# Musical Context
Reduction to residues is the culmination of the liquidation process. After the sequential modulation has created emphasis and instability, the progressive reduction dissolves this emphasis, preparing the listener for the return of the theme. The technique creates a characteristic texture: minimal motivic content over static harmony, generating anticipation.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 26, Scherzo: Pattern reduced from four measures to two, then to five notes, four notes, two notes, and finally one note (m. 41 ff.)
- Ex. 122a, m. 25-29: Residues dwelt upon over the dominant in ostinato-like treatment
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 4-III: Reduction from four measures to two, to one, to two-thirds of a measure
- Beethoven, Op. 18/6, Scherzo: Rhythm condensed to equal eighth-notes, finally liquidated into scale lines and broken chords

# Common Confusions
- Residues are not random fragments -- they are the smallest still-recognizable elements of the original pattern
- The ostinato-like treatment of residues is not a new thematic idea; it is the final stage of dissolution
- Reduction to residues is a motivic process that typically coincides with harmonic stasis (pedal point), but the two are separate techniques working together
