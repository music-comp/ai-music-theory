# Quick Definition
The shortening or lengthening of the recapitulation relative to the A-section, achieved through elimination, reduction, condensation, insertion, interpolated repetitions, extension, or addition.

# Formal Definition
Shortening (by elimination, reduction, or condensation) may help to avoid monotony. Lengthening (by insertion, interpolated repetitions, extension, or addition) may create emphasis. Reduction and extension may occur simultaneously within a single recapitulation, with some measures omitted and others added through sequential or other repetitive treatment. The resulting proportions may differ considerably from both the A-section and the practice form.

# Construction/Recognition
Shortening techniques: (1) elimination -- omitting entire measures or phrases, (2) reduction -- compressing material into fewer measures, (3) condensation -- combining elements from separate measures. Lengthening techniques: (1) insertion -- adding new connecting material, (2) interpolated repetitions -- repeating phrases not previously repeated, (3) extension -- stretching cadential passages, (4) addition -- appending codetta-like material. Both may be combined to maintain overall balance.

# Musical Context
The flexibility of recapitulation length is a key feature of the small ternary form in practice, as opposed to the fixed proportions of the practice form. The choice between shortening and lengthening depends on what has preceded: if the B-section was extensive, a shorter recapitulation may provide balance, while a brief B-section may call for an extended recapitulation.

# Examples
- Beethoven Op. 2/1-II: reduced to four measures from an eight-measure A-section
- Beethoven Op. 2/2-II: combines reduction (omitting m. 3-5) and extension (adding m. 15-16 sequentially) for seven measures total
- Beethoven Op. 7-II: extended to ten measures through sequences, repetitions, condensations, and a deceptive cadence requiring a second cadence
- Beethoven Op. 14/2-II: reduced to four measures, followed by a codetta-like addition
- Beethoven Op. 26-I: repeats only the second half of the sixteen-measure period (m. 27-34)
- Haydn Ex. 102b: omits first four measures of A-section but extends the second half to ten measures

# Common Confusions
- Shortening is not simply cutting; it involves restructuring to maintain musical logic despite omitted material
- A recapitulation can be both shorter and longer than the A-section simultaneously -- shorter in some passages, longer in others
- The practice form proportion (A=8, B=4, A1=4) is a pedagogical model, not a prescription
