# Quick Definition
A melody can be compared to an apercu or aphorism -- a concentrated insight that advances rapidly from problem to solution, achieving completeness through condensation rather than elaboration.

# Formal Definition
A melody can be compared to an apercu, an aphorism, in its rapid advance from problem to solution. The melody tends to achieve balance in the most direct way. It avoids intensifying the unrest; it supports comprehensibility by limitation, and facilitates lucidity through subdivision; it extends itself rather by continuation than by elaboration or development. Condensation does not admit a too-detailed elaboration; concentration of the content in a single line excludes presentation of remote consequences.

# Construction/Recognition
A melody achieves its apercu-like quality through: (1) directness -- moving from problem to solution without digressions, (2) limitation -- not exploring every implication, (3) subdivision -- breaking into lucid, graspable units, (4) continuation rather than development, (5) slightly varied motive-forms rather than radical transformations, (6) remaining within closer harmonic relationships.

# Musical Context
This formulation permits much more than the expression of popular ideas and commonplace feelings. Aesthetically, and for reasons of economy, "there is no nobler contrast than to speak lightly of a great grief." A melody is a simpler formulation than a theme, but simplicity here means concentration, not superficiality.

# Examples
- Heine's verse from Buch der Lieder: "Aus meinen grossen Schmerzen / Mach ich die kleinen Lieder" ("Out of my great sorrows / I make the little songs") -- cited by Schoenberg as an analogy
- "So heroes belittle their wounds and their achievements -- this is the modesty of great men"
- Beethoven String Quartet Op. 132-III (Ex. 96c): has only one symptom of melody -- singability -- yet makes the impression of a melody

# Common Confusions
- "Apercu" does not mean "trivial" or "shallow"; it means a concentrated insight that captures something essential in brief form
- The simplicity of melody is a simplicity of formulation, not of content; profound ideas can be expressed in melodic form
