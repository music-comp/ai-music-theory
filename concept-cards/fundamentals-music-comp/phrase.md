# Quick Definition
The smallest structural unit of music -- a kind of musical molecule consisting of integrated musical events, possessing a certain completeness, and well adapted to combination with other similar units.

# Formal Definition
The phrase is the smallest structural unit, a kind of musical molecule consisting of a number of integrated musical events, possessing a certain completeness, and well adapted to combination with other similar units. Structurally, a phrase approximates what one could sing in a single breath. Its ending suggests a form of punctuation such as a comma.

# Construction/Recognition
A phrase is recognized by its sense of completeness as a unit and by the punctuation at its ending. Often some features appear more than once within the phrase -- these "motivic" characteristics provide internal coherence. The phrase is seldom an exact multiple of the measure length; it usually varies by a beat or more and nearly always crosses metrical subdivisions rather than filling measures completely.

# Musical Context
The phrase is the fundamental unit from which all larger structures are built. In the early stages of composition study, a composer's invention seldom flows freely. Schoenberg recommends making many sketches of phrases based on predetermined harmony to develop technical facility and stimulate the inventive faculties.

# Common Confusions
- The phrase is not defined by a fixed number of measures; its length varies with metre and tempo
- A phrase is not the same as a measure or a motive -- it is a complete structural unit that may contain multiple motives
- There is no intrinsic reason for a phrase to be restricted to an even number of measures, though irregularity has far-reaching consequences
