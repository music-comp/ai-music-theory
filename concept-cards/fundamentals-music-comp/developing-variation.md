# Quick Definition

The characteristic technique of homophonic music in which successive motive-forms produced through variation exhibit something comparable to development or growth, with each new form building cumulatively upon previous ones.

# Core Definition

"Homophonic music can be called the style of 'developing variation'. This means that in the succession of motive-forms produced through variation of the basic motive, there is something which can be compared to development, to growth" (Ch. 3, p. 8). "In the course of a piece, a motive-form may be developed further through subsequent variation" (Ch. 3, p. 9). This is explicitly distinguished from local embellishments: "Changes of subordinate meaning, which have no special consequences, have only the local effect of an embellishment. Such changes are better termed variants" (Ch. 3, p. 8).

# Key Properties

1. Characteristic technique of homophonic music
2. Successive motive-forms exhibit growth comparable to biological development
3. Each variation may serve as the basis for further variation (cumulative process)
4. Changes have structural consequences, influencing what follows
5. Distinguished from mere "variants" (local embellishments without consequences)
6. The process is directional: later forms may be quite distant from the original
7. Connection to the original is maintained through chains of intermediate steps

# Construction / Recognition

## To Apply Developing Variation

1. State the basic motive clearly in the opening
2. Vary the motive to produce a second form, preserving important features
3. Use the second form as the basis for a third form, varying further
4. Continue the chain, allowing cumulative distance from the original
5. Ensure each step is perceivable as growing from the previous one
6. Distinguish purposeful development from mere local embellishment

## To Recognize Developing Variation

1. Trace the chain of motive-forms from beginning to end
2. Identify how each form grows from the preceding one
3. Note the directional quality -- does the music develop or merely repeat?
4. Check whether changes have structural consequences (true variation) or are merely decorative (variants)

# Context & Application

Developing variation is perhaps the most important single concept in Schoenberg's compositional theory. It describes how the great classical composers -- particularly Beethoven and Brahms -- generated entire movements from a basic motive. The concept distinguishes purposeful compositional development from mere repetition with embellishment. It is the mechanism through which the motive-as-germ grows into a complete musical organism. The technique is not limited to development sections in sonata form; it operates throughout a composition at every structural level.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating developing variation as identical to theme-and-variations form
  **Correction**: Developing variation is a pervasive compositional technique, not a formal structure

- **Error**: Making each variation step too distant from the previous one
  **Correction**: Each step should be perceivable as growing from the previous form; maintain intermediate connections

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Believing developing variation operates only in development sections
  **Clarification**: It operates throughout a composition at every structural level

- **Confusion**: Equating "variants" with developing variation
  **Clarification**: "Variants" are local embellishments without structural consequences; developing variation involves changes that drive further development

- **Confusion**: Thinking developing variation means constant change
  **Clarification**: It means changes are cumulative and directional, not that every moment introduces new material
