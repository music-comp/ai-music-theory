# Quick Definition
The characteristic technique of homophonic music in which successive motive-forms produced through variation exhibit something comparable to development or growth, with each new form building upon previous ones.

# Formal Definition
Homophonic music can be called the style of "developing variation." This means that in the succession of motive-forms produced through variation of the basic motive, there is something which can be compared to development, to growth. In the course of a piece, a motive-form may be developed further through subsequent variation -- each variation potentially serving as the basis for further variation, creating a chain of organic development.

# Construction/Recognition
Developing variation is distinguished from mere local embellishment ("variants") by its cumulative, directional quality. Key characteristics:
- Each motive-form grows from previous forms, not just from the original motive
- The succession of forms has a developmental trajectory, like biological growth
- Changes have structural consequences, influencing what follows
- The process is progressive: later forms may be quite distant from the original, but connected through a chain of intermediate steps

# Musical Context
Developing variation is perhaps the most important single concept in Schoenberg's compositional theory. It describes how the great classical composers (particularly Beethoven and Brahms) generated entire movements from a basic motive. The concept distinguishes purposeful compositional development from mere repetition with embellishment. It is the mechanism through which the motive-as-germ grows into a complete musical organism.

# Common Confusions
- Developing variation is not the same as theme and variations form; it is a pervasive compositional technique, not a formal structure
- "Variants" (local embellishments without structural consequences) are explicitly distinguished from developing variation
- The "development" in developing variation is not limited to the development section of sonata form; it operates throughout a composition
- Developing variation does not mean constant change; it means that changes are cumulative and directional
