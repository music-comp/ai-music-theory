# Quick Definition
The principle that music must be divided into manageable parts because human mental limitations prevent grasping anything too extended; appropriate subdivision facilitates understanding and determines form.

# Formal Definition
Man's mental limitations prevent him from grasping anything which is too extended. One can comprehend only what one can keep in mind. Thus appropriate subdivision facilitates understanding and determines the form. The size and number of parts does not always depend on the size of the piece -- generally, the larger the piece, the greater the number of parts, but sometimes a short piece may have the same number of parts as a longer one.

# Construction/Recognition
Subdivision is applied hierarchically: a composition divides into sections, sections into segments, segments into phrases, phrases into motive-forms. Each level of subdivision makes the music graspable at that scale. The beginner, unable to envisage a composition in its entirety, must proceed gradually from simpler to more complex structures.

# Musical Context
This principle underlies Schoenberg's entire pedagogical approach. Students begin by building small musical blocks (phrases, motives) and then learn to connect them intelligently into larger units. The demands of logic, coherence, and comprehensibility are fulfilled in relation to the need for contrast, variety, and fluency of presentation.

# Examples
- Schoenberg compares a short piece having the same number of parts as a longer one to "a midget [having] the same number of limbs, the same form, as a giant"
- Simplified practice forms help students acquire the sense of form before tackling art forms

# Common Confusions
- Subdivision is not merely mechanical division into equal parts; it is functional, with parts differentiated by importance and role
- More subdivision does not always mean more comprehensibility -- the subdivisions themselves must be organized logically
- Practice forms designed for learning do not always correspond to the forms found in actual compositions
