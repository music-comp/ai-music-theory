# Quick Definition
Rondo forms with the patterns ababa or abaca, featuring three appearances of the principal theme separated by contrasting sections, used relatively infrequently as independent forms.

# Formal Definition
The patterns ababa and abaca are rather infrequently used as independent forms, though they appear by analogy in the minuet with conventional repetitions (||:a:||:ba:||, which is in fact a-a-b-a-b-a) and in scherzos with more than one trio. In ababa, the same contrasting material returns twice; in abaca, two different contrasting sections alternate with the principal theme.

# Construction/Recognition
- **ababa**: The principal theme appears three times, each separated by the same contrasting B-section; the B-section recurs transposed to the tonic in its final appearance
- **abaca**: The principal theme appears three times with two different contrasting sections (b and c); this provides greater variety than ababa
- The leading theme may be relatively short due to its multiple appearances
- The B-section may be surprisingly long with "loose formulation"
- Recapitulation of b is typically transposed to the tonic and may be reduced in length

# Musical Context
These forms serve as slow movements or finales. The ababa pattern achieves its effect through the rhythmic alternation of familiarity and contrast, while abaca provides more variety through different contrasting episodes.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/3-II (ababa): the leading theme consists of only ten measures due to numerous appearances of its basic motive; the B-section is surprisingly long at 32 measures, a striking instance of loose formulation; the syncopated figure (m. 19) functions as embellishment of harmony -- "the harmony is the melody"
- Beethoven, Op. 13-II (abaca): leading theme of eight measures repeated an octave higher; subordinate theme (m. 17) is rudimentary; second subordinate theme resembles the first in character but is more elaborate, reaching the flat submediant region by enharmonic change
- Ravel, Pavane pour une Infante Defunte (abaca): main structure is self-evident; irregular dimensions (b is 6.5 measures, c is 9.5) enhance interest of the simple construction

# Common Confusions
- The ababa pattern is formally distinct from the conventional minuet-with-repeats, even though the latter produces the same sequence of sections by analogy
- In ababa, the recapitulated b-section is typically shortened and transposed to the tonic, not merely repeated verbatim
- The looseness of the B-section's formulation is a deliberate compositional strategy appropriate to its subordinate function
