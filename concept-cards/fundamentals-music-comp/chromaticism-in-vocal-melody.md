# Quick Definition
Chromatic movement in vocal lines presents intonation challenges because natural semitones differ in size from tempered ones, but can be used sparingly when the harmony supports comprehension.

# Core Definition
Chromaticism is difficult in vocal melody partly because the natural semitones differ in size from the tempered ones -- a fact which causes choirs to "get off pitch." Sparingly used, chromaticism need not be avoided. Chromatic notes functioning as circumscribing or embellishing notes can easily be understood. More complex chromatic passages require highly developed technical skill, and some chromatic usages are difficult to justify musically.

# Key Properties
- Natural semitones differ in size from tempered semitones
- This difference causes choirs to drift in pitch
- Chromatic passing and embellishing notes are manageable when harmonically supported
- Extended chromatic passages require advanced vocal skill
- The development of harmony from the Wagnerian epoch expanded chromatic usage in melody
- Modern musicians can comprehend remote harmonies as coherent tonal elements

# Construction / Recognition
When using chromaticism in vocal melody: (1) ensure chromatic tones function as passing, neighboring, or embellishing notes comprehensible in context, (2) avoid extended or harmonically unsupported chromatic passages, (3) be aware that choirs are particularly susceptible to pitch drift, (4) reserve complex chromaticism for highly skilled singers.

# Context & Application
The ear of the modern musician gradually acquired the capacity to comprehend the most remote harmonies as coherent elements of a tonality. To composers of the Wagnerian and post-Wagnerian epoch, the use of chromatic intervals and progressions in melodies became as natural as scalewise movement was to their predecessors.

# Common Errors
- Using extended chromatic passages without harmonic support
- Ignoring the practical difference between tempered and natural semitones in choral writing
- Assuming chromatic movement is inherently expressive without ensuring it is comprehensible

# Common Confusions
- The problem with chromaticism in vocal melody is not aesthetic but practical: the voice lacks fixed pitch references and must rely on harmonic context
- Tempered and natural semitones differ in size, making even simple chromatic passages an intonation challenge
