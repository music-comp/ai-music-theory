# Quick Definition
"Rhythm of three measures" -- a metrical technique in which three written measures function as a single larger rhythmic unit, reducing the standard four-measure grouping to three.

# Formal Definition
Ritmo di tre battute is a technique, notably used by Beethoven, in which three notated measures are heard and organized as a single metrical unit. Instead of the standard four-measure grouping (ritmo di quattro battute), the hypermetre operates in groups of three. This connects two sub-units (2+1 or 1+2) and reduces the four-measure pattern to three. The naturalness of such groupings is demonstrated by the fact that a phrase of six quarter-notes can be understood as resembling one measure of 3/2 time.

# Construction/Recognition
- Three consecutive written measures function as one rhythmic unit
- Reduces the standard four-measure hypermetre to three-measure hypermetre
- Sub-units connect as 2+1 or 1+2 within the three-measure group
- May be indicated by the composer (as Beethoven does in his Ninth Symphony)
- A related concept: "si ha s'imaginar la battuta di 6/8" -- imagining a different bar length

# Musical Context
This technique creates large-scale rhythmic irregularity while maintaining internal regularity within each three-measure group. It is especially associated with scherzo movements, where the rapid tempo makes individual measures feel like beats within a larger metrical unit. The technique demonstrates that irregular phrase lengths often have a natural explanation when reconceived at a different metrical level.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Ninth Symphony, Scherzo: Marked "ritmo di tre battute," organizing the music in three-measure groups
- Beethoven, Harp Quartet, Op. 74: Marked "si ha s'imaginar la battuta di 6/8"
- Brahms, Piano Quartet in e minor, last movement: Ten three-measure phrases, each resembling one measure of 3/2

# Common Confusions
- Ritmo di tre battute does not mean 3/4 time -- it means three measures are grouped as one hypermeasure
- The written bar lines remain unchanged; it is the perceived grouping that shifts
- This is a hypermetrical concept, operating above the level of individual measures
