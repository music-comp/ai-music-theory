# Quick Definition

The three categories of motive repetition -- exact, modified, and developed -- which range from strict preservation of all features to progressive transformation through variation.

# Core Definition

"A motive is used by repetition. The repetition may be exact, modified or developed" (Ch. 3, p. 9):

- **Exact repetitions** "preserve all features and relationships. Transpositions to a different degree, inversions, retrogrades, diminutions and augmentations are exact repetitions if they preserve strictly the features and note relations" (Ch. 3, p. 9).
- **Modified repetitions** "are created through variation. They provide variety and produce new material (motive-forms) for subsequent use" (Ch. 3, p. 9).
- **Developed repetitions** occur when "a motive-form may be developed further through subsequent variation" (Ch. 3, p. 9), creating cumulative, progressive transformation.

# Prerequisites

- **motive** -- Must understand the motive and its features
- **variation** -- Modified and developed repetition depend on the concept of variation

# Key Properties

1. Exact repetition preserves all features and relationships, even through transformation (transposition, inversion, retrograde, diminution, augmentation)
2. Modified repetition changes some features while preserving others, producing new motive-forms
3. Developed repetition builds cumulatively, with each variation serving as the basis for further variation
4. Some modifications are merely local "variants" with little or no influence on continuation
5. Multiple variation methods may be applied simultaneously

# Construction / Recognition

## Exact Repetition

1. All intervallic and rhythmic relationships preserved
2. Includes transposition, inversion, retrograde, diminution, and augmentation
3. The internal structure of the motive remains intact

## Modified Repetition

1. Some features changed, others preserved
2. Produces new motive-forms for subsequent use
3. Distinguish true variations (structural consequences) from variants (local embellishments)

## Developed Repetition

1. Variation builds upon previous variations, not just the original
2. Creates a chain of progressive transformation
3. Characteristic of developing variation technique

# Context & Application

The choice among types of repetition depends on the structural function of the passage. Opening presentations often use exact or near-exact repetition for clarity. Continuations and developments use modified and developed repetition to generate forward motion. The distinction between "variants" (local modifications without consequences) and true variations (modifications that drive further development) is critical for effective composition.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Classifying transposition as modified rather than exact repetition
  **Correction**: "Transpositions to a different degree, inversions, retrogrades, diminutions and augmentations are exact repetitions if they preserve strictly the features and note relations"

- **Error**: Using only exact repetition, producing monotony
  **Correction**: Variation through modified and developed repetition is necessary to maintain interest

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Believing "exact" means only literal note-for-note restatement
  **Clarification**: Transposition, inversion, retrograde, etc. are exact repetitions because they preserve internal relationships

- **Confusion**: Equating "modified" with "developed" repetition
  **Clarification**: Development implies cumulative, progressive transformation; modification may be a single, isolated change

- **Confusion**: Treating all local modifications as structurally significant
  **Clarification**: "Variants" -- local embellishments with no special consequences -- are distinguished from true variations
