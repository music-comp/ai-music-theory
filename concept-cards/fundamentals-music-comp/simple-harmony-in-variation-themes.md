# Quick Definition
Variation themes should employ simple, regular harmonic progressions -- often featuring mere interchange of I and V -- to provide a stable foundation that permits extensive harmonic variation in subsequent variations.

# Formal Definition
The harmony of a variation theme should be simple and should not change too frequently or too irregularly. The use of mere interchange of I and V contributes to the simplicity of many themes. In the best themes, there may be only one or two harmonies per two measures. Even the Diabelli Waltz, whose first eight measures change harmony only once, proved suitable for thirty-three variations. Simple but characteristic harmony (such as a sequential middle section) contributes to the memorability of the theme.

# Construction/Recognition
- Harmonic changes should be infrequent and regular
- Interchange of I and V is a common harmonic foundation
- One or two harmonies per two measures is typical
- Characteristic harmonic features (e.g., sequences) may appear but should remain simple
- The harmony provides the skeletal framework that variations can elaborate or alter
- Simple harmony allows greater scope for harmonic variation in later variations

# Musical Context
The harmonic simplicity of the theme serves a dual purpose: it makes the theme immediately comprehensible and memorable, and it provides a stable foundation against which harmonic variations can register as meaningful departures. The most sophisticated variations (such as Beethoven's Diabelli Variations and Brahms's Handel Variations) demonstrate how far harmonic variation can depart from a simple harmonic foundation while remaining recognizably connected to it.

# Examples
- Beethoven, 15 Variations in E-flat: Two harmonies to two measures in the theme
- Beethoven, Diabelli Variations Op. 120: Harmony changes only once in the first eight measures of the theme
- Beethoven, 6 Variations in G: Simple but characteristic harmony, especially the sequence in the middle section
- Beethoven, Op. 26-I: Sequences contribute to memorability
- Brahms, Handel Variations: Simple I-based progression with mediant relationship (B-flat to D) providing the basis for extensive harmonic variation

# Common Confusions
- "Simple" does not mean "uninteresting" -- characteristic harmonic features are desirable as long as they remain regular
- The simplicity of the theme's harmony is precisely what enables the sophistication of the variations' harmony
- Harmonic variation in later variations may depart very far from the original, but the underlying progression remains the reference point
