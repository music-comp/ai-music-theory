# Quick Definition
The principle that the elaboration section provides harmonic contrast to the exposition's stability through roving, modulating harmony that passes through remote key regions, with a tendency toward flat-side keys to compensate for the exposition's sharp-side subordinate group.

# Core Definition
The harmony of the exposition is essentially stable: apart from transitions, everything stands solidly within the region of a definite tonality. This requires a different kind of contrast in the elaboration. Economy dictates the use of previously "exposed" thematic material. Variety requires roving, modulating harmony as a contrast to the generally stable nature of the exposition. Movement from region to region is usually effected through the closer key relationships, though the most remote regions may be reached in the process. There is a tendency to favour regions employing more flats or fewer sharps than the principal key, perhaps because the subordinate group almost always lies on the dominant side (more sharps or fewer flats).

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of elaboration (Durchfuhrung) as a technique
- Exposition structure and key relationships

# Key Properties
- Essentially modulatory character contrasts with the essentially stable exposition
- More remote regions are used than in the exposition
- Movement between regions typically follows closer key relationships step by step
- Tendency toward flat-side keys (compensating for the sharp-side subordinate group)
- This flat-side tendency is particularly evident approaching the retransition
- Occasional "roving" passages pertain to no definite region
- Approach chords (especially dominants) emphasized more than tonics
- Deceptive progressions useful for maintaining modulatory momentum

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the stable vs. modulatory character of exposition vs. elaboration
2. Map the key regions traversed in the elaboration
3. Note the tendency toward flat-side keys
4. Look for "roving" passages that pertain to no definite region
5. Note emphasis of dominants over tonics as a momentum device
6. Identify deceptive progressions maintaining modulatory flow

# Context & Application
The modulatory character of the elaboration is not arbitrary but functionally complementary to the stability of the exposition. The two sections balance each other: one provides harmonic stability, the other harmonic variety. This complementary relationship is the formal raison d'etre of the elaboration. The flat-side tendency provides a further level of complementarity with the sharp-side subordinate group.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/2-I: reaches the "surprisingly remote region of Ab" from E major; includes Eb (bIII) region
- Beethoven, Op. 2/3-I: follows the circle of fifths through sequential treatment
- Beethoven, Op. 28-I: passes through G (IV), g, regions a fourth lower, d, a; residues dwell on III as dominant of relative minor
- Mozart, K.V. 464-I: harmonic subtlety fully evident throughout the elaboration

# Common Errors
- Random key changes without logical harmonic paths between regions
- Remaining too long in a single region, undermining the modulatory purpose
- Failing to compensate for the exposition's sharp-side subordinate group with flat-side regions

# Common Confusions
- The modulatory character does not mean random key changes; movement between regions typically follows logical harmonic paths
- The flat-side tendency is a general observation, not an absolute rule
- "Roving" passages (pertaining to no definite region) are interspersed among more stable passages; the entire elaboration is not uniformly unstable
