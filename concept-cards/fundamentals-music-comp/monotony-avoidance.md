# Quick Definition
The practice of identifying and remedying excessive repetition of tones, figures, or restricted compass that produces annoying monotony rather than purposeful emphasis.

# Core Definition
Too many repetitions of tones or melodic figures are annoying if they do not exploit the advantage of a repetition -- emphasis. The highest tone of the melody (the climax) normally appears toward the end and can be repeated or exceeded only with caution. One should beware of too restricted a compass and avoid moving too long in one direction. The endings of phrases should be evaluated sensitively.

# Prerequisites
- self-criticism: Monotony avoidance is the fourth step in the self-criticism framework

# Key Properties
- Repetitions should serve emphasis; purposeless repetitions are annoying
- The climax normally appears toward the end
- The climax should be repeated or exceeded only with caution
- Beware of too restricted a compass
- Avoid moving too long in one direction
- Evaluate phrase endings sensitively
- Monotony is the opposite extreme from excessive variation
- Both monotony and excessive variation produce imbalance

# Construction / Recognition
Check for monotony by evaluating: (1) whether repetitions serve emphasis or merely fill space, (2) whether the climax appears toward the end, (3) whether the climax is repeated or exceeded only with justification, (4) whether the compass is sufficiently varied, (5) whether the melody moves too long in one direction, (6) whether phrase endings are varied and sensitive.

# Context & Application
Monotony is the opposite extreme from excessive variation. The composer must find the balance between too much repetition (monotony) and too much variation (incoherence). Repetition is valuable only when it serves emphasis; otherwise, it becomes irritating.

# Common Errors
- Using repetition without exploiting its advantage (emphasis)
- Placing the climax too early, leaving the rest anticlimactic
- Confining the melody to too narrow a compass
- Moving too long in one direction

# Common Confusions
- Monotony is not just literal note repetition; it can arise from harmonic stasis, restricted compass, or persistent movement in one direction
- The problem with repetition is not repetition itself but purposeless repetition; repetition for emphasis is desirable
