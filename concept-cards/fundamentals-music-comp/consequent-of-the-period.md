# Quick Definition
The consequent is the second half of a period, functioning as a modified repetition of the antecedent, made necessary by the remote motive-forms introduced in the antecedent's continuation (m. 3-4).

# Formal Definition
The consequent (Schoenberg's "after-sentence") is a modified repetition of the antecedent. An unchanged and complete repetition is very rare; even in the simplest cases, at least the last measure is modified. The introduction of a full cadence in the consequent usually requires earlier changes. If the period is a complete piece, the consequent must end (usually with a perfect authentic cadence) on I. If it is part of a larger form, it may end on I, V, or III (major or minor).

# Construction/Recognition
- Begins as a repetition of the antecedent, often retaining the first one or two measures
- Harmonic deviation may begin as early as the first measure or as late as the seventh
- The variation may consist of inversions of the same harmonies (Ex. 45f)
- The consequent may begin on a different degree: on II as a sequence of the antecedent (Beethoven, Op. 10/3, Menuetto, m. 9), or on V (Exs. 44b, 48d)
- The full cadence requirement necessitates changes in the approach, often through enriched cadence
- Must end with appropriate cadence according to function within larger form

# Musical Context
The consequent serves a double function: providing the satisfying repetition that comprehensibility demands, while simultaneously introducing sufficient variation to maintain interest. The balance between preservation and variation is the central challenge. The consequent resolves the harmonic openness created by the antecedent's typical ending on V.

# Examples
- Exs. 50b, c, e: Even in very simple cases, at least the last measure is modified
- Exs. 45f, k, 48a, g, 50a: Harmonic deviation occurs in the seventh measure
- Exs. 44f and 47c, e: Deviation occurs in the sixth measure
- Ex. 45f: Variation consists in use of inversions of the same harmonies
- Beethoven, Op. 10/3, Menuetto: Consequent starts on II, as a sequence of the antecedent

# Common Confusions
- The consequent is not a literal repetition of the antecedent; even the simplest cases require modification for cadential purposes.
- Starting the consequent on a different degree (II, V) does not violate the principle of modified repetition; it is a form of harmonic variation.
- The consequent's cadence must differ from the antecedent's cadence, even when arriving on the same scale degree.
