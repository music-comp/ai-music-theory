# Quick Definition
The use of transposition (sequential repetition) as a method for the second phrase of a sentence, where the opening material is restated at a different pitch level, often to III (the relative major in minor keys) or to V.

# Formal Definition
If the beginning is a two-measure phrase, the continuation (mm. 3-4) may be either an unvaried or a transposed repetition. Slight changes in the melody or harmony may be made without obscuring the repetition. Transposition to a different degree, when it preserves strictly the features and note relations, constitutes an exact repetition (a sequence). This is one of the simplest methods of constructing the second phrase of a sentence.

# Construction/Recognition
Transposition in sentence construction is identified by:
- The second phrase restating the first at a different pitch level
- Preservation of rhythmic profile and intervallic relationships
- The melody conforming to the harmony of the new degree
- Common transposition targets: V (dominant), III (mediant/relative major)

Types of transposition observed in examples:
- Simple unvaried repetition (Exs. 58d, 59f, 60c)
- Repetition at the lower octave (Ex. 58f)
- Sequential transposition to III (Ex. 53a: "an otherwise unvaried transposition to III, i.e. a sequence")
- Partial sequence in melody and accompaniment (Ex. 57a)

# Musical Context
Transposition is the simplest form of repetition in sentence construction and one of the most common in classical music. It provides just enough contrast (through the change of pitch level and harmonic area) to avoid monotony while maintaining maximum recognizability of the basic motive.

# Examples
- Exs. 58d, 59f, 60c: simple repetition with little or no change
- Exs. 58c, g: harmony unchanged, accompaniment slightly varied, melody transposed
- Ex. 58f: repetition at the lower octave
- Ex. 53a: unvaried transposition to III (relative major), i.e., a sequence
- Ex. 57a: sequential in melody and partially sequential in accompaniment
- Ex. 61c: second measure ascends conforming with harmonic progress toward III

# Common Confusions
- Transposition in this context is not modulation; it is a local restatement at a different pitch level within the same key
- A sequence (transposition preserving all intervals) is classified as "exact repetition" even though the pitch level changes
- The amount of melodic adaptation needed depends on the harmonic context of the new degree
- Transposition and complementary repetition (tonic/dominant form) are different techniques, though both produce the second phrase of a sentence
