# Quick Definition

The use of transposition (sequential repetition) as a method for constructing the second phrase of a sentence, where the opening material is restated at a different pitch level -- often to III (the relative major) or to V -- while preserving rhythm and intervallic relationships.

# Core Definition

"If the beginning is a two-measure phrase, the continuation (mm. 3-4) may be either an unvaried or a transposed repetition. Slight changes in the melody or harmony may be made without obscuring the repetition" (Ch. 5, p. 21). Transposition to a different degree, "when it preserves strictly the features and note relations," constitutes an exact repetition -- a sequence (Ch. 3, p. 9). This is one of the simplest methods of constructing the second phrase of a sentence, alongside complementary repetition (dominant form).

# Key Properties

1. The second phrase restates the first at a different pitch level
2. Rhythmic profile and intervallic relationships are preserved
3. The melody conforms to the harmony of the new degree
4. Common transposition targets: V (dominant), III (mediant / relative major)
5. A sequence (strict transposition) is classified as "exact repetition"
6. This is one of the simplest methods for sentence construction
7. May involve partial sequencing (melody sequential, accompaniment partially so)

# Construction / Recognition

## Types of Transposition Observed

1. **Simple unvaried repetition** (Exs. 58d, 59f, 60c) -- no transposition, just exact restatement
2. **Repetition at the lower octave** (Ex. 58f)
3. **Sequential transposition to III** (Ex. 53a) -- "an otherwise unvaried transposition to III (the relative major), i.e. a sequence"
4. **Partial sequence** (Ex. 57a) -- "sequential in the melody and partially sequential in the accompaniment"
5. **Conforming to harmonic progress** (Ex. 61c) -- "the second measure ascends in conformity with the progress of the harmony towards III"
6. **Slight melodic variation with harmonic preservation** (Exs. 58c, g) -- "harmony remains the same, the accompaniment is slightly varied and the melody transposed"

# Context & Application

Transposition is the simplest form of repetition in sentence construction because it provides contrast (through pitch-level change and harmonic area shift) with maximum recognizability of the basic motive. It is an alternative to complementary repetition (tonic/dominant form), and the two techniques represent different approaches to the same structural problem: how to repeat mm. 1-2 in mm. 3-4.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Transposing without adapting the melody to the new harmonic context
  **Correction**: The melody must conform to the harmony of the new degree

- **Error**: Treating transposition as modulation
  **Correction**: Transposition in sentence construction is a local restatement at a different pitch level within the same key, not a modulation

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Classifying a strict sequence as "modified repetition"
  **Clarification**: A sequence is "exact repetition" because it "preserves strictly the features and note relations" even though the pitch level changes

- **Confusion**: Equating transposition with complementary repetition
  **Clarification**: They are different techniques -- transposition changes pitch level; complementary repetition changes harmonic function (tonic to dominant area)
