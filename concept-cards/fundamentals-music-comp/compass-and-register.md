# Quick Definition

The principle that a good melody generally remains within a reasonable compass (range), not straying too far from a central range, ensuring balance, proportion, and graspability.

# Core Definition

"A good melody generally remains within a reasonable compass, not straying too far from a central range" (Ch. 4, p. 12). This principle operates in conjunction with wave-like melodic motion and the compensation of large intervals: "upward movements are balanced by downward movements; large intervals are compensated for by conjunct movement in the opposite direction" (Ch. 4, p. 12).

# Prerequisites

- **phrase** -- Compass and register are properties of phrases and larger melodic units
- **motive** -- The motive's intervallic content affects the compass

# Key Properties

1. A good melody stays within a reasonable compass
2. The melody should not stray too far from a central range
3. Extreme registers should be used purposefully (e.g., for climax)
4. Excursions to extremes should be followed by return to the central range
5. Compass relates to singability and natural phrasing
6. The principle applies when connecting motive-forms into phrases

# Construction / Recognition

1. Establish a central range early in the melody
2. Limit excursions to extreme high and low registers
3. Use excursions to extremes purposefully (climax, dramatic effect)
4. Return to the central range after excursions
5. Consider the overall range in relation to tempo and character
6. When connecting motive-forms, ensure successive transformations do not push the melody into extreme registers

# Context & Application

Compass and register are practical considerations when building phrases from motive-forms. Successive variations of a motive can inadvertently push the melody higher and higher (or lower), creating an unbalanced phrase. The composer must monitor the overall range and ensure that the melody remains within a graspable compass. This principle works together with wave-like motion to produce balanced, well-proportioned phrases.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Allowing successive variations to push the melody into extreme registers
  **Correction**: Monitor the overall range and return to the central range after excursions

- **Error**: Restricting compass so severely that the melody becomes static
  **Correction**: "Reasonable compass" allows for purposeful excursions, especially for climactic moments

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Equating "reasonable compass" with a fixed range (e.g., always one octave)
  **Clarification**: Reasonable compass is relative to the instrument, character, and tempo of the piece

- **Confusion**: Confusing compass with tessitura
  **Clarification**: Compass is the total range; tessitura is the prevalent or most-used register

- **Confusion**: Believing limited compass means avoiding large intervals
  **Clarification**: Large intervals are acceptable if compensated by conjunct contrary motion; overall compass is about the total span, not individual intervals
