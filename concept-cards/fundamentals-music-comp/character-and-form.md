# Quick Definition
While character may influence the choice of form, no particular character demands a particular form; however, profound ideas generally require more complex forms, while lighter moods suit simpler ones.

# Core Definition
Differences of character may have an influence on the structure, but no particular character can be said to demand a particular form. Light, graceful, simple moods will not require complicated forms nor adventurous elaboration. On the other hand, profound ideas, deeply moving emotions, and heroic attitudes require the bold contrasts and thorough elaboration of more complex forms. The relationship between character and form is one of tendency, not absolute prescription.

# Key Properties
- No fixed rule maps characters to forms
- Light moods tend toward simpler forms
- Profound ideas tend toward complex forms with bold contrasts and thorough elaboration
- The relationship is flexible, guided by musical judgment
- Descriptive music develops its forms in harmony with the emotions it illustrates

# Construction / Recognition
When choosing a form, consider the depth and complexity of the musical idea. Match the elaborateness of the form to the weight of the content. Recognize that the relationship is one of tendency, not absolute prescription.

# Context & Application
This principle guides the composer's judgment about how to develop material. One would scarcely write a waltz in the form of a symphony, yet there are surprising exceptions that demonstrate the flexibility of the principle.

# Examples
- Beethoven Op. 54-I: a sonata movement written "In tempo d'un Menuetto," showing that form and character do not have a rigid one-to-one mapping
- Beethoven's Seventh Symphony: called the "Dance Symphony" due to its character, despite being cast in symphonic form
- One would scarcely write a waltz in the form of a symphony, illustrating the practical limits of the principle

# Common Errors
- Rigidly matching characters to forms without considering musical context
- Using an overly complex form for simple material
- Using an overly simple form for profound ideas

# Common Confusions
- There is no fixed rule mapping characters to forms; the relationship is flexible and guided by musical judgment
- A simple character does not necessarily require a trivially simple form, nor does a complex character always require the most elaborate form
