# Quick Definition
A passage that returns from a contrasting section (subordinate theme or elaboration) back to the principal theme or tonic region, typically consisting of liquidation over a dominant pedal point, with the actual remodulation usually occurring within the preceding modulatory section.

# Core Definition
In rondos, the return from the subordinate theme to the principal theme, and in sonatas the return to the beginning after the exposition, are generally so simple that no special segment is necessary. Nevertheless, there are occasionally small connecting segments which act as both "bridges" and "spacers." Functionally and psychologically more important are the retransitions after modulatory contrasts and Durchfuhrungen. Since the actual remodulation usually takes place as a part of the preceding modulatory section, the retransition proper starts on the upbeat harmony, and generally consists of little more than liquidation of the motival residues. The motive material often derives from subsidiary themes; sometimes references to features of the principal theme forecast its return.

# Key Properties
- Begins on the upbeat harmony (usually the dominant) after remodulation has already occurred
- Consists primarily of liquidation of motival residues
- May use material from subsidiary themes or anticipatory references to the principal theme
- Often built over a dominant pedal point
- Dynamic and rhythmic changes frequently contribute to dramatic effect
- Simple retransitions in rondos are mere bridges and spacers
- More dramatic retransitions after Durchfuhrungen are functionally and psychologically important

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the point where the dominant (or upbeat chord) is reached after remodulation
2. The retransition proper begins at this point
3. Look for liquidation of remaining motival residues
4. Listen for anticipatory references to the principal theme
5. Note dominant pedal points, dynamic changes, and rhythmic contrasts that prepare the recapitulation

# Context & Application
Retransitions are most significant after the elaboration (Durchfuhrung) in sonata-allegro forms, where they prepare the psychological return to the recapitulation. In rondos, simpler retransitions serve as bridges between sections. The retransition is distinct from the remodulation itself -- the harmonic return to the dominant usually happens within the preceding modulatory section.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/2-I (m. 210-24) and Op. 10/1-I (m. 158-67): little more than liquidating devices around the dominant harmony
- Beethoven, Op. 7-I: extraordinarily, remodulation plus retransition occurs in only two measures (m. 187-8), after dwelling in the remote regions of a minor and d minor
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1-I (m. 93): motive material clearly anticipates the second measure of the principal theme
- Beethoven, Op. 90-I: a sixteenth-note figure (m. 130) undergoes rhythmic metamorphoses, preparing the first three notes of the theme (m. 144)
- Eroica Symphony-I (m. 366): the famous horn entrance presents the tonic form of the main theme over sustained dominant harmony
- Beethoven, Op. 13-I: dominant pedal (m. 167-187) with frequent dynamic changes and sforzandi makes this the dramatic climax of the movement
- Beethoven, Op. 10/3-IV (m. 46): entire retransition derives from the principal theme, beginning with a quotation that could be mistaken for the recapitulation were it not in the "wrong key"

# Common Errors
- Making the retransition too long, dissipating the momentum toward the recapitulation
- Omitting anticipatory references to the principal theme, making the return feel arbitrary
- Confusing the remodulation (harmonic process) with the retransition (formal section)

# Common Confusions
- The retransition is not the same as the remodulation; the actual harmonic return to the dominant usually happens within the preceding modulatory section, and the retransition proper begins on that dominant
- Simple retransitions in rondos (small bridging segments) should not be confused with the more dramatic and psychologically important retransitions after Durchfuhrungen
- The retransition starting on the upbeat harmony does not mean it starts on a weak beat; "upbeat harmony" refers to the chord that prepares the tonic return
