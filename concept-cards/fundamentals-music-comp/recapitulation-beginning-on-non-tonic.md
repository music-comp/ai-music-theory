# Quick Definition

The comparatively rare practice of beginning the recapitulation on a degree other than the tonic (e.g., II, V, or I6/4), requiring the upbeat chord to be chosen accordingly to prepare the unexpected starting harmony.

# Core Definition

The possibility that the recapitulation might begin on a different degree than the beginning is illustrated in cases where the recapitulation begins on II, V, or I6/4 (the tonic in second inversion). Such cases are comparatively rare in the small ternary form. When the recapitulation begins on a non-tonic degree, the upbeat chord must be chosen to lead logically to that degree rather than to I. This technique adds harmonic interest to the moment of return, creating a subtle surprise at the point where the listener expects the most familiar harmony.

# Prerequisites

- **Recapitulation in scherzo**: Understanding the standard recapitulation procedure

# Key Properties

- The recapitulation begins on a degree other than I (e.g., II, V, I6/4)
- The upbeat chord must be adjusted to prepare the unexpected starting harmony
- Comparatively rare in small ternary forms; becomes more common in larger forms
- The non-tonic beginning must eventually reach the tonic for the final cadence
- The upbeat chord's identity depends on what follows it, not on a fixed rule

# Construction / Recognition

1. Compare the first harmony of the recapitulation with the first harmony of the A-section
2. Examine the upbeat chord and its relationship to the recapitulation's opening harmony
3. Trace how the non-tonic beginning eventually reaches the tonic for the final cadence
4. Note that the augmented 6/5 chord and diminished seventh chord are among the harmonies used for unusual openings

# Context & Application

This technique demonstrates the flexibility of formal conventions beyond the practice model. It is distinct from the "recapitulation in the wrong key" (where the entire theme returns in a non-tonic key), being instead a local harmonic recoloring of the opening that resolves within the recapitulation itself.

# Examples

- Ex. 107a: Recapitulation begins on II, introduced by bVI
- Ex. 107b: Upbeat harmony is bIII, introducing a recapitulation beginning on V
- Ex. 107c: Recapitulation begins on I6/4
- Schubert, Ex. 104: Recapitulation (m. 13) uses augmented 6/5 chord on #II of the submediant region

# Common Errors

- Forgetting to adjust the upbeat chord for the non-tonic opening
- Failing to reach the tonic for the final cadence

# Common Confusions

- **Non-tonic beginning vs. wrong-key recapitulation**: A non-tonic beginning is a local harmonic event (the first chord or two); a wrong-key recapitulation begins the entire theme in a different key.
