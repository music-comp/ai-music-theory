# Quick Definition
The comparatively rare practice of beginning the recapitulation on a degree other than the tonic, requiring the upbeat chord to be chosen accordingly to prepare the unexpected starting harmony.

# Formal Definition
The possibility that the recapitulation might begin on a different degree than the beginning is illustrated in cases where the recapitulation begins on II, V, or I6/4 (the tonic in second inversion). Such cases are comparatively rare in the small ternary form. When the recapitulation begins on a non-tonic degree, the upbeat chord must be chosen to lead logically to that degree rather than to I.

# Construction/Recognition
Identify non-tonic recapitulation beginnings by: (1) comparing the first harmony of the recapitulation with the first harmony of the A-section, (2) examining the upbeat chord and its relationship to the opening harmony of the recapitulation, (3) tracing how the non-tonic beginning eventually reaches the tonic for the final cadence.

# Musical Context
This technique adds harmonic interest to the moment of return, creating a subtle surprise at the point where the listener expects the most familiar harmony. It demonstrates the flexibility of the small ternary form beyond the practice model. The augmented 6/5 chord and diminished seventh chord are among the harmonies used to create these unusual openings.

# Examples
- Ex. 107a: recapitulation begins on II, introduced by bVI
- Ex. 107b: upbeat harmony is bIII, introducing a recapitulation that begins on V
- Ex. 107c: recapitulation begins on I6/4; if the first two harmonies were reversed, bVI could logically introduce I
- Schubert Ex. 104: recapitulation (m. 13) uses the augmented 6/5 chord on #II of the submediant region, treated as if it were a dominant seventh chord on IV

# Common Confusions
- A non-tonic beginning does not mean the recapitulation avoids the tonic altogether; it must still reach the tonic for the final cadence
- The upbeat chord's identity depends on what follows it, not on a fixed rule that it must be V
- These cases are rare in the small ternary form; they become more common in larger forms
