# Quick Definition
A construction technique where the end of each motive-form is simultaneously the beginning of the next, with successive forms overlapping like the links of a chain.

# Formal Definition
Chain-like construction is a device for creating continuity and contrast in m. 3-4 of the antecedent (or elsewhere). In this technique, motive-forms are connected by overlap: the concluding element of one motive-form serves as the opening element of the following one. This produces seamless forward motion and creates a sense of inevitability in the thematic flow, as each ending generates its own continuation.

# Construction/Recognition
- Identify where the final note(s) or figure of one motive-form serve double duty as the beginning of the next
- The overlapping creates a sense of continuous forward motion
- Each "link" is both an ending and a beginning
- Useful for creating a sense of acceleration or intensification in contrasting passages

# Musical Context
Chain-like construction provides an especially effective way of building coherent contrast in the antecedent's continuation. By linking motive-forms through overlap rather than simple juxtaposition, the composer maintains continuity even while introducing new material. This technique can also appear in consequents and in sentence continuations.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 10/3, Rondo (Ex. 42): M. 3-4 demonstrate chain-like construction where the end of each motive-form is the beginning of the next, overlapping like links of a chain

# Common Confusions
- Chain-like construction is not mere sequential repetition; each link involves genuine overlap, not just adjacency.
- The technique differs from a sequence, which transposes a complete pattern; here, the patterns share material at their boundaries.
