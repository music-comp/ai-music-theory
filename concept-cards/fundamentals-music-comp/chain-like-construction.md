# Quick Definition

A construction technique where the end of each motive-form is simultaneously the beginning of the next, with successive forms overlapping like the links of a chain.

# Core Definition

Chain-like construction is a device for creating continuity and contrast, particularly in m. 3-4 of the antecedent. In this technique, motive-forms overlap: the end of each motive-form is the beginning of the next; they overlap like the links of a chain (Schoenberg, p. 22, discussing Beethoven Op. 10/3, Rondo, Ex. 42).

# Prerequisites

- **Motive** -- The technique operates on motive-forms, which must be understood first
- **Connecting motive-forms** -- Chain-like construction is a specific method of connecting motive-forms through overlap

# Key Properties

1. The concluding element of one motive-form serves as the opening element of the next
2. Produces seamless forward motion through overlap
3. Each "link" functions simultaneously as ending and beginning
4. Creates continuity even while introducing contrasting material
5. Distinct from sequential repetition, which transposes complete patterns without overlap

# Construction / Recognition

## To Construct Chain-Like Passages

1. Compose a motive-form whose ending can serve double duty as the start of the next form
2. Continue by deriving the next motive-form from this shared element
3. Maintain the overlap consistently to create the "chain" effect

## To Recognize Chain-Like Construction

1. Identify where the final note(s) or figure of one motive-form serve as the beginning of the next
2. Confirm genuine overlap (shared material), not merely adjacency

# Context & Application

Chain-like construction is an especially effective way of building coherent contrast in the antecedent's continuation (m. 3-4). By linking motive-forms through overlap rather than simple juxtaposition, the composer maintains continuity even while introducing new material. This technique creates a sense of inevitability in the thematic flow, as each ending generates its own continuation.

# Examples

- **Beethoven, Op. 10/3, Rondo** (Ex. 42, p. 22): M. 3-4 demonstrate chain-like construction. Ex. 42 shows that the end of each motive-form is the beginning of the next. The consequent then introduces two sequences of the motive (m. 5-6) in place of the single repetition in m. 1-2.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Creating adjacency without genuine overlap
  **Correction**: The technique requires that the same material function as both ending and beginning, not merely that two motive-forms be placed next to each other

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Equating chain-like construction with sequential repetition
  **Clarification**: Sequences transpose a complete pattern to a new degree; chain-like construction overlaps boundaries so endings and beginnings share material
