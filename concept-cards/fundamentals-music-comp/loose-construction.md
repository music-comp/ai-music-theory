# Quick Definition
A compositional approach characteristic of subordinate themes and contrasting sections, relying on immediate repetition of short segments, juxtaposition of distinct ideas, and minimal internal development.

# Formal Definition
Loose construction depends on immediate repetition of relatively short segments, joined to others by mere juxtaposition, with a lesser degree of internal development. It contrasts with the tighter organization of principal themes, where repetitions enhance memorability and, through variation, prepare for development and elaboration. In subordinate themes, mere repetition and juxtaposition often replace the more rigorous developmental procedures.

# Construction/Recognition
Characteristics of loose construction include:
- Direct and immediate repetition of short segments (often two to four measures)
- Juxtaposition of contrasting segments without connective development
- Phrases that differ in length and whose motive-forms change freely in interval and direction
- Parts that start like sequences but are not carried through consistently
- Harmony serving as the effective melody, with individual melodic lines functioning more as embellishment of harmonic tones
- An etude-like quality where the procedure resembles the figuration of a study piece

# Musical Context
Loose construction is characteristic of subordinate theme groups in sonata-allegro and rondo forms. It provides evidence of subordination and serves as the aesthetically most important type of contrast with the principal theme.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/3-II: the B-section (32 measures) is a striking instance of loose formulation; three distinct motive-forms are repeated irregularly, phrases differ in length, and the syncopated figure (m. 19) is essentially embellishment of harmonic tones -- "the harmony is the melody"
- The lyric theme tradition from Schubert onward represents looseness through "disregarding almost all features except the rhythmic ones, thus neglecting the profounder implications, and providing richness of content through the multiplication of themes"

# Common Confusions
- Loose construction is not careless or weak writing; it is a deliberate structural strategy that provides essential contrast to the tighter organization of principal themes
- The "looseness" refers specifically to the type of thematic organization (repetition and juxtaposition vs. development), not to the quality of the composition
- Loose construction in subordinate themes is functionally different from the motivic development expected in principal themes and elaboration sections
