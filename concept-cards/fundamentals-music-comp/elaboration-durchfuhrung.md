# Quick Definition
The technique of adapting, varying, expanding, condensing, recombining, and carrying motive-forms through various keys or regions -- Schoenberg's preferred term for the process commonly called "development," which he rejected because the material does not truly develop in the sense of growth or evolution.

# Formal Definition
Schoenberg uses the terms "elaboration" and "Durchfuhrung" interchangeably to refer to the technique in which motive-forms are adapted, varied, expanded, condensed, recombined, and carried through various keys or regions. He rejected the more usual term "development" on the grounds that little development (in the sense of growth, maturation, evolution) takes place in the material used. Motive-forms seldom grow or evolve into a "more mature or advanced state." The technique appears in the B-sections of scherzos and minuets, and more fully in the development section of the sonata allegro.

# Construction/Recognition
- Motive-forms from the exposition/A-section are modified for modulatory purposes
- Material is carried through fluctuating, unstable harmonic situations
- Techniques include: adaptation, variation, expansion, condensation, recombination
- Sequential treatment is the most common organizational device
- The process typically concludes with liquidation and a pedal point
- Freedom of structure does not imply absence of logic or balance

# Musical Context
The concept of elaboration (Durchfuhrung) applies across multiple formal levels -- from the contrasting middle section of a minuet or scherzo to the development section of a sonata. The key insight is that the material is not "developed" toward a goal but rather "elaborated" -- displayed in various aspects and combinations. This terminological distinction reflects a fundamental difference in how Schoenberg understood compositional process.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 3, Scherzo: First ten measures of the Durchfuhrung utilize motive-forms of m. 1-4 in chromatic ascension
- Beethoven, various scherzos and minuets: Contrasting middle sections that resemble the Durchfuhrung of the sonata allegro
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 1-III, Menuetto: Although called a minuet, the contrasting middle section uses scherzo-like elaboration technique

# Common Confusions
- "Elaboration" and "development" are not synonymous in Schoenberg's usage -- elaboration is his preferred term
- The material does not "develop" in the sense of growing more mature; it is adapted and recombined
- Elaboration technique appears in scherzos and minuets as well as in sonata forms
