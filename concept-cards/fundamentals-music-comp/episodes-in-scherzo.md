# Quick Definition

Passages that interrupt the normal flow of a section by dwelling upon non-modulatory, non-cadential progressions, often settling in a harmonically remote region and introducing small phrases strangely foreign to the previously used motive-forms.

# Core Definition

Episodes interrupt the normal flow of a section. They dwell upon such progressions as neither modulate nor produce a cadence. They often settle down in a more or less remote contrasting region, especially if within a modulatory section. They often introduce small phrases, strangely foreign to the previously used motive-forms. In some cases, the modulation of the B-section is interrupted by an episode which settles down at a harmonically remote point, after which a remodulation follows, using residues or other derivatives. The remoteness of the region often makes a relatively long and rapid remodulation necessary.

# Prerequisites

- **Scherzo form**: Understanding the structural framework within which episodes occur
- **Recapitulation in scherzo**: Episodes often appear in or affect the A'-section

# Key Properties

- Interrupts the sequential or cadential flow of the section
- Harmony neither modulates nor cadences -- it "settles down"
- Often in a remote harmonic region
- May introduce motivally foreign phrases
- Followed by a remodulation to return to the structural plan
- May appear within the B-section (interrupting modulation) or within the A'-section
- Distinguished from extensions by their digressive, non-progressive character

# Construction / Recognition

1. Look for passages where the sequential or cadential flow is interrupted
2. Check if the harmony settles into a region without modulating or cadencing
3. Note any motivally foreign phrases
4. Identify the remodulation that follows the episode
5. Distinguish from extensions (which prolong the flow rather than interrupting it)

# Context & Application

Episodes provide moments of contrast and harmonic color within the larger formal framework. They function as structural surprises, departing from the expected progression. A passage may have the independence of an episode but function as something else (e.g., a remodulation), demonstrating the flexibility of formal categories.

# Examples

- Beethoven, Op. 2/2, Scherzo, m. 19 ff.: Episode both motivally and harmonically remote; connected to basic material merely by its upbeat (related to tone repetitions of m. 3); remoteness requires long, rapid remodulation
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 2: Modulation interrupted by episode settling at a harmonically remote point
- Beethoven, Op. 74 (Harp Quartet), Presto, m. 17: Long episode in the Neapolitan region; partial repetition remodulates
- Beethoven, Op. 59/2, Allegretto, m. 21-28: Possesses the independence of an episode but functions as a remodulation (Neapolitan triad, followed by V and I)

# Common Errors

- Treating episodes as mere digressions without structural purpose
- Failing to provide adequate remodulation after a remote episode
- Confusing episodes with extensions

# Common Confusions

- **Episode vs. extension**: Episodes interrupt the flow and settle in a remote region; extensions prolong the flow through sequential repetition.
- **Formal ambiguity**: A passage may have the independence of an episode but function as a remodulation (Op. 59/2 example).
