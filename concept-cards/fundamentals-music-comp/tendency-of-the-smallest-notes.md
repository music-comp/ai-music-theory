# Quick Definition

The principle that the smallest note values in any segment of a piece exert a momentum-like influence on the continuation, causing an increasing number of small notes to appear progressively, much like the acceleration of a falling body.

# Core Definition

The smallest notes in any segment of a piece, even in a motive or motive-form, have an influence on the continuation comparable to the momentum of acceleration in a falling body: the longer the movement lasts, the faster it becomes. Thus, if in the beginning only one sixteenth-note is used, very soon an increasing number will appear, growing often into whole passages of sixteenths. To restrict this tendency of the smallest notes requires specified care (Schoenberg, p. 23, footnote 3).

# Prerequisites

- **Motive** -- The tendency originates from the rhythmic content of motives and motive-forms
- **Phrase** -- The tendency affects how phrases develop and continue across structural units

# Key Properties

1. Smallest note values exert momentum on the continuation
2. The effect is cumulative: the longer the movement lasts, the more small notes appear
3. Analogous to the acceleration of a falling body
4. Restricting the tendency requires deliberate compositional care
5. Can be observed across m. 3-4 of antecedents in numerous examples
6. Can be deliberately contradicted (decrease instead of increase) for structural purposes such as marking the caesura

# Construction / Recognition

## To Apply the Tendency

1. Introduce small note values in the opening motive
2. Allow an increase of these small notes in the continuation (m. 3-4)
3. Use the increasing activity to create coherent contrast in the antecedent

## To Restrict the Tendency

1. Employ a decrease of smaller notes to mark structural boundaries (e.g., the caesura)
2. Use reduction as a contrasting motive-form
3. Coordinate small notes with the harmony as ancillary notes, as in Mozart's technique

# Context & Application

The tendency of the smallest notes is one of the primary forces driving the continuation of thematic material in m. 3-4 of the antecedent. However, it is only one way of constructing a contrasting yet coherent continuation. Coherence can also be produced through decrease of smaller notes, in which case the motive-form appears as a reduction. In the cadence contour, contradicting the tendency (using longer notes, like a ritardando) is a valid structural device.

Schoenberg notes that in masterpieces (particularly Mozart), the coordination of small notes with the harmony as ancillary notes reaches a perfection "which no beginner dare hope for" (p. 23). The tendency also operates at all structural levels, not just within antecedents.

# Examples

- **Increase of small notes in antecedents** (p. 23): Observable in m. 3 (and sometimes m. 4) of Exs. 44a, b, c, d, g, k, 45a, b, c, d, and 46e

- **Decrease/reduction as contrast** (p. 23): Exs. 43a, 45i, 46g show a decrease of smaller notes as a contrasting motive-form

- **Marking the caesura through decrease** (p. 23): Exs. 44a, d, g, h, i, 45b, 46e, f, g and 47a, d use a decrease of smaller notes to mark the caesura melodically

- **Brahms, Ex. 51f** (p. 26): The tendency accounts for a variant in m. 5 where rhythmic figure 'a' shifts from the second beat to the first, producing an almost continuous flow of eighth-notes

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Allowing unrestricted accumulation of small notes, producing a crowded texture
  **Correction**: To restrict the tendency requires deliberate care; too many small notes produce a crowded effect

- **Error**: Always following the tendency without considering structural context
  **Correction**: Decrease of smaller notes is also a valid technique, especially at the caesura

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the tendency is an absolute rule that must always be followed
  **Clarification**: It is a natural tendency that can be either followed or deliberately contradicted for structural purposes

- **Confusion**: Equating the tendency with mere rhythmic acceleration
  **Clarification**: The tendency is a compositional force requiring management, not simply a tempo change; it is about note values in the notation, not performance tempo

- **Confusion**: Believing the tendency applies only to antecedents
  **Clarification**: The tendency applies to all segments of a piece, even within a single motive or motive-form
