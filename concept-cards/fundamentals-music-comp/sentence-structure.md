# Quick Definition

A theme structure in which the opening phrase is immediately repeated (exact, transposed, or in dominant form), with the continuation employing development and liquidation toward a cadence -- distinguished from the period by this immediate repetition.

# Core Definition

The sentence is a theme structure characterized by immediate repetition of the opening phrase. "The construction of the beginning determines the construction of the continuation. In its opening segment a theme must clearly present (in addition to tonality, tempo and metre) its basic motive. The continuation must meet the requirements of comprehensibility. An immediate repetition is the simplest solution, and is characteristic of the sentence structure" (Ch. 5, p. 21). The practice form consists of eight measures: mm. 1-2 present the basic motive (tonic form), mm. 3-4 provide immediate repetition (dominant form or variant), and mm. 5-8 employ development and liquidation toward a cadence (treated in Ch. 8).

# Key Properties

1. The opening phrase is immediately repeated (characteristic feature)
2. The beginning determines the construction of the continuation
3. The opening must clearly present tonality, tempo, metre, and basic motive
4. Immediate repetition may be exact, transposed, or in dominant form
5. Slight changes in melody or harmony are permitted without obscuring the repetition
6. The practice form spans eight measures (2+2+4)
7. Much used in leading themes of sonatas and symphonies
8. Applicable to smaller forms as well

# Construction / Recognition

## Practice Form (8 measures)

1. **Mm. 1-2** (Tonic form): Present the basic motive clearly, establishing tonality
2. **Mm. 3-4** (Repetition): Immediate repetition -- exact, transposed, or dominant form
3. **Mm. 5-8** (Continuation): Development and liquidation toward cadence (Ch. 8)

## Types of Repetition in mm. 3-4

- Unvaried repetition (Exs. 58d, 59f, 60c)
- Transposed repetition / sequence (Ex. 53a)
- Complementary repetition with dominant form (Exs. 35, 52b, c)
- Slightly varied repetition (Exs. 59d, g)
- Repetition at the lower octave (Ex. 58f)

# Context & Application

The sentence is "a higher form of construction than the period" because it already includes repetition within the first four measures, requiring the continuation to employ "more remotely varied motive-forms" (Ch. 8, p. 39). The sentence "is much used in leading themes of sonatas and symphonies" (Ch. 8, p. 39). Chapter 5 treats only the opening four measures; the completion of the sentence (mm. 5-8) is covered in Chapter 8.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Confusing the sentence with the period
  **Correction**: The sentence uses immediate repetition in mm. 3-4; the period postpones repetition to the consequent

- **Error**: Treating the eight-measure practice form as an invariable rule
  **Correction**: "Masterwork sentences regularly deviate in length" (Ch. 8) through extensions, insertions, and irregular unit sizes

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking "sentence" means any sequence of phrases
  **Clarification**: In Schoenberg's usage, "sentence" is a specific theme structure with immediate repetition followed by development

- **Confusion**: Believing "higher form" means "better" than the period
  **Clarification**: "Higher form" means more complex in developmental demands, not qualitatively superior
