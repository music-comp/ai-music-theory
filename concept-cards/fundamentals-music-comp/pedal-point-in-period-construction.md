# Quick Definition
A sustained bass note over which the harmony changes; in period construction, the pedal point serves the constructive purpose of retarding harmonic movement, balancing remote motival variation, or preparing reintroduction of the tonic.

# Formal Definition
Though the pedal point is often used in masterpieces for expressive or pictorial purposes, Schoenberg insists its real meaning should be constructive. The pedal point holds back the forward progress of the harmony, creating retardation. Its primary constructive uses include: (1) at the end of a transition or elaboration, emphasizing the close of a modulation and preparing for the reintroduction of the tonic; and (2) balancing remote motival variation -- a method paralleled by balancing centrifugal harmony with simpler motival variation. If no such constructive purpose is involved, a pedal point should be avoided. A moving, melodic bass line is always a greater merit.

# Construction/Recognition
- Sustained bass note (typically tonic or dominant) while upper voices move through changing harmonies
- Most constructive at the end of transitions or elaborations
- Used to prepare reintroduction of the tonic after modulation
- Can balance remote motival variation by stabilizing the harmonic foundation
- Should have a clear structural purpose; avoid purely decorative use

# Musical Context
In period construction, pedal points appear at structurally significant moments. Brahms uses them at both beginning and end of a period. The pedal point's retardation effect is analogous to the principle of compensating complexity: when motival variation becomes remote, harmonic stability (via pedal) counterbalances the tension.

# Common Confusions
- A pedal point is not merely a decorative effect; Schoenberg insists it should serve a constructive purpose.
- A moving, melodic bass line is preferable when no structural need for a pedal point exists.
- The pedal point creates the effect of retardation, not stasis; the upper voices continue to move harmonically above it.
