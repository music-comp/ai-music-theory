# Quick Definition
The technique of establishing a thematic segment (pattern) and then transposing it to other harmonic degrees (sequence) as the principal means of carrying out modulation in the contrasting middle section.

# Formal Definition
Harmonically, the segments of the contrasting middle section should be built as patterns for sequences. Thematically, they should possess a certain degree of independence; the combination of motive-forms may resemble that in the antecedent of a period or the middle section of a ternary form. Motive-forms from the A-section must be modified or adapted to the modulatory procedure. The order may be changed, and some forms may be repeated within the segment (e.g., abac, abcc, ahbc, aaab, etc.). The pattern must be built so that the end of the last sequence offers an opening for the return to an appropriate upbeat harmony.

# Construction/Recognition
- Pattern: a clearly delimited segment, typically 2-4 measures, combining A-section motive-forms
- Sequence: transposition of the pattern to a new harmonic degree
- The pattern may begin on V, v, III, i, iii, bIII, ii, iv, VI, or other degrees relative to the A-section's ending
- Variation of the sequence is common and desirable (not strict transposition)
- The principal voice may shift to a different voice between pattern and sequence (double counterpoint)
- If the end of the sequence is too harmonically remote, further modulation through additional (reduced) sequences is needed

# Musical Context
The emphasis contributed by a sequence (as a form of repetition) creates obligations that must be neutralized through liquidation before the recapitulation can be introduced. Beethoven expressed the goal as making the recapitulation arrive in a manner "at once surprising and expected." The sequential process is the most common single feature shared across classical scherzos and scherzo-like movements.

# Examples
- Ex. 122: Twenty-five illustrations of patterns with sequences derived from one A-section
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 4-III: Many sequences of a four-measure pattern
- Beethoven, String Quartet Op. 18/1, Scherzo: Three-measure pattern in flat mediant sequenced in tonic minor; two-measure pattern followed by three sequences leading to the dominant
- Brahms, Sextet Op. 36, Scherzo: Elaboration organized in imitative sequences as dialogue between upper and lower voices

# Common Confusions
- A sequence need not be a strict transposition -- variation of the sequence is common
- The pattern should be clearly delimited but need not be a literal quote of A-section material
- The end of the sequential process is critical: it must connect to the upbeat harmony for the recapitulation
