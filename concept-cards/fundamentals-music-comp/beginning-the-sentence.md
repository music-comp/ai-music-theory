# Quick Definition

The opening segment of a sentence structure, consisting of a two-measure phrase presenting the basic motive (tonic form) followed by its immediate repetition (mm. 3-4), which may be unvaried, transposed, or in dominant form, and which determines the construction of the entire continuation.

# Core Definition

"The construction of the beginning determines the construction of the continuation. In its opening segment a theme must clearly present (in addition to tonality, tempo and metre) its basic motive. The continuation must meet the requirements of comprehensibility. An immediate repetition is the simplest solution, and is characteristic of the sentence structure" (Ch. 5, p. 21). "If the beginning is a two-measure phrase, the continuation (m. 3 and 4) may be either an unvaried or a transposed repetition. Slight changes in the melody or harmony may be made without obscuring the repetition" (Ch. 5, p. 21).

# Key Properties

1. The opening phrase (mm. 1-2) presents the basic motive
2. The second phrase (mm. 3-4) provides immediate repetition
3. The beginning determines the construction of the continuation
4. Tonality, tempo, and metre must be established clearly
5. The repetition may be unvaried, transposed, or in dominant form
6. Slight changes are permitted without obscuring the repetition
7. The choice of repetition type shapes the required continuation

# Construction / Recognition

## Step-by-Step Construction

1. Compose a two-measure phrase presenting the basic motive clearly (tonic form)
2. Establish tonality (I and V are especially useful for opening phrases)
3. Choose a repetition type for mm. 3-4:
   - **Unvaried repetition**: exact restatement (Exs. 58d, 59f, 60c)
   - **Transposition**: restatement at a different pitch level (Ex. 53a to III)
   - **Dominant form**: harmonic shift preserving rhythm and contour (Exs. 35, 52b, c)
   - **Slight variation**: small melodic or harmonic changes (Exs. 59d, g)
4. Ensure the repetition is recognizable despite any changes
5. Use a consistent accompaniment pattern as a unifying factor

# Context & Application

The sentence beginning is treated separately from the continuation because the principles governing each are distinct. The beginning establishes the motivic material and harmonic framework; the continuation (mm. 5-8, treated in Ch. 8) develops and liquidates this material toward a cadence. Schoenberg emphasizes that "even in these short passages, a definite and regular accompaniment is employed to animate the harmony and to express a specific character. Consistency in the application of accompanimental characteristics is a powerful unifying factor" (Ch. 5, p. 22).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Making the repetition in mm. 3-4 too different from mm. 1-2
  **Correction**: "Slight changes in the melody or harmony may be made without obscuring the repetition" -- the repetition must remain recognizable

- **Error**: Neglecting to establish tonality in the opening
  **Correction**: The opening must clearly present "tonality, tempo and metre" in addition to the basic motive

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking "immediate repetition" means exact, note-for-note restatement
  **Clarification**: Transposition, dominant form, and slight variation all qualify as "immediate repetition"

- **Confusion**: Assuming the beginning rigidly prescribes the continuation
  **Clarification**: The beginning "determines" the continuation -- it constrains and guides but does not dictate every detail

- **Confusion**: Treating the sentence beginning as the entire sentence
  **Clarification**: The beginning is only mm. 1-4; the continuation (mm. 5-8) is treated separately in Ch. 8
