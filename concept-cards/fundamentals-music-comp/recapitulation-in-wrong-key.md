# Quick Definition
A technique found occasionally in Beethoven's later works where the recapitulation begins in a key other than the tonic, creating a structural surprise that is subsequently resolved.

# Formal Definition
"Recapitulation in the wrong key" is a technique that appears occasionally in Beethoven's later works, where the return of the main theme occurs in an unexpected key rather than the tonic. This creates a deliberate structural deception: the listener recognizes the thematic material but is surprised by the harmonic context. The "wrong" key is eventually corrected as the recapitulation continues, restoring the tonic.

# Construction/Recognition
- The main theme returns recognizably but in an unexpected key
- The "wrong" key typically has a logical relationship to the preceding modulation
- The tonic is eventually restored during the course of the recapitulation
- Creates a combination of recognition (thematic) and surprise (harmonic)
- Usually occurs when the modulatory process of the B-section has arrived at a key that relates logically to the theme but is not the tonic

# Musical Context
This technique represents one of Beethoven's most innovative formal procedures. By beginning the recapitulation in the "wrong" key, he achieves a more seamless transition from the modulatory B-section while maintaining the surprise of the theme's return. The technique became influential on later composers and is a key example of how formal conventions can be productively subverted.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 7-III: The modulation follows the circle of fifths from mediant major to subdominant, at which point one of those "recapitulations in the wrong key" occurs

# Common Confusions
- The "wrong key" is intentional, not an error -- it is a deliberate structural device
- The term refers specifically to the recapitulation beginning in a non-tonic key, not to brief tonicizations within the recapitulation
- This is distinct from a "false recapitulation," which is a premature, deceptive return in the wrong key within the development section
