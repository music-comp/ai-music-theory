# Quick Definition
The B-section of the minuet, which provides contrast through sequential modulation, remote motive derivation, and modulatory movement, often resembling the elaboration (Durchfuhrung) technique found in scherzos and sonata forms.

# Formal Definition
The contrasting middle section of the minuet serves as the B-section of the ternary form. Its chief characteristic is sequential modulation -- moving through related keys by transposing a pattern to successive pitch levels. The end of the section is often marked by dwelling on the dominant over a pedal point. This pedal point is a retarding device, employed where the harmony must be prevented from developing too rapidly or too distantly. Motive-forms used in the B-section are typically remote derivations from the A-section material.

# Construction/Recognition
- Built from motive-forms remotely derived from the A-section
- Sequential modulation is the chief characteristic
- The section often ends by dwelling on the dominant over a pedal point
- The pedal point retards harmonic movement: usually the sustained note is in the bass, with upper voices carrying a progression from V to V
- May include episodes and co-ordinate ideas (sometimes rudimentary, sometimes independent)
- Modulation often follows the circle of fifths

# Musical Context
The contrasting middle sections of many minuets resemble the elaboration (Durchfuhrung) discussed under the scherzo. Sequential modulation allows the motive material to pass through multiple keys while maintaining connection to the A-section. The technique of dwelling on the dominant via pedal point creates the necessary repose and suspense before the recapitulation.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1-III: Sequential modulation leads to the subdominant; motive reduced (liquidated) to three notes in m. 23-24, then reassembled in eighth-note chains moving to the dominant
- Beethoven, Op. 10/3-III: Two-measure phrase makes four appearances moving in a circle of fifths (iii-vi-II-V)
- Beethoven, Op. 22-III: Trill-like segment derived from the A-section's left-hand eighth-notes under influence of sixteenth-notes
- Beethoven, Op. 7-III and Op. 27/2-II: Movements not specifically called Minuets that feature the same sequential modulation technique

# Common Confusions
- The B-section of a minuet is not always a simple contrasting theme -- it often involves genuine modulatory elaboration
- The distinction between a minuet's B-section and a scherzo's elaboration is often blurred in practice
- Pedal point at the end of the section is retarding, not cadential -- it prepares for the return, not the close
