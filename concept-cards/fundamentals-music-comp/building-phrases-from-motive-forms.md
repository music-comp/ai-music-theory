# Quick Definition
The practical process of combining derived motive-forms into complete phrases, ensuring that the results function as true musical units rather than mere mechanical assemblages.

# Formal Definition
In working out derivatives of a motive, it is important that the results have the character of true phrases -- of complete musical units. Various methods of producing a large number of different phrases out of one basic motive are demonstrated. Some phrases might be used to begin a theme, others to continue it; and some, especially those which do not begin with I, serve as material for contrasts, subordinate ideas, and the like.

# Construction/Recognition
Methods for building phrases from motive-forms include:
- **Adding ancillary notes** while retaining all notes of the basic motive (Ex. 31)
- **Preserving rhythm** while changing intervals and direction, combined with transposition to other degrees (Ex. 32) -- "often used in traditional music to produce entire themes"
- **Combining rhythmic changes** with ancillary notes and interval/direction changes (Ex. 33)
- **Rhythmic shifting and rearrangement** of features for far-reaching variations (Ex. 34) -- produces material for continuations and contrasts

Each note of the resulting melody should be either a harmony note or a non-chordal note corresponding to established conventional formulas.

# Musical Context
This is the practical application chapter where theoretical principles become compositional practice. Schoenberg emphasizes that even results that are "stiff and overcrowded" have pedagogical value: "the practice of making such sketches, which attempt various methods of variation, should never be abandoned." The distinction between phrases suitable for beginnings versus continuations versus contrasts is crucial for theme construction.

# Common Confusions
- Not every phrase built from motive-forms is suitable for every structural function; beginning phrases differ from continuation phrases
- Phrases that do not begin on I may be useful for contrasts or subordinate ideas, not just for openings
- "Far-reaching variations" (Ex. 34) produce useful material but "may endanger comprehensibility"
- The goal is complete musical units, not merely connected motive-forms
