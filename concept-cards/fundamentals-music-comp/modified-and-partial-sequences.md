# Quick Definition

Modified sequences transpose only the melody or only the harmony (with the other element freely varied), while partial sequences transpose some features but vary others; both represent departures from strict sequential treatment toward freer compositional practice.

# Core Definition

Sequential treatment is not always complete. It may be applied to either melody or harmony alone, with the remaining element more or less freely varied. Such cases may be called modified or partial sequences. In other cases, the general character of a transposed repetition may be present, though without literal transposition of any element. These instances, and others involving variation of some features, may be described as "sequentially controlled" (Schoenberg, p. 41).

# Key Properties

1. Modified sequence: one element (melody or harmony) transposed, the other freely varied
2. Partial sequence: some features transposed, others varied
3. Sequentially controlled: general character of transposition without literal transposition of any element
4. The freer the sequence, the more it resembles developing variation
5. Most sequences in the literature are modified, partial, or sequentially controlled rather than strict

# Construction / Recognition

1. Identify the sequential model (pattern)
2. Determine which elements are transposed (melody, harmony, both, or neither)
3. Classify as modified (one element), partial (some features), or sequentially controlled (impression only)
4. Note how freely varied elements maintain coherence despite not being transposed

# Context & Application

In actual practice, strict sequences are the exception rather than the rule. Most sequential passages in the literature employ modified, partial, or sequentially controlled treatments. This reflects the tension between structural clarity and the need for harmonic and melodic flexibility. The student should understand strict sequences as a foundation but recognize that masterworks typically employ freer treatments.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Insisting on strict transposition when freer treatment would serve the music better
  **Correction**: Most masterwork sequences are modified or partial; strict sequences are the exception

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking modified or partial sequences are failures to achieve strict sequence
  **Clarification**: They are compositional choices offering greater flexibility

- **Confusion**: Difficulty distinguishing very free sequences from ordinary developing variation
  **Clarification**: The distinction can be a matter of interpretation; both are legitimate analyses
