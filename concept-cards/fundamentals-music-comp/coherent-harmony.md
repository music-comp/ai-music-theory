# Quick Definition
The use of logical, well-organized harmonic progressions to reinforce relationships between connected motive-forms, with every piece of music resembling a cadence in a general way.

# Formal Definition
Coherent harmony reinforces relationship and is one of three principal factors contributing to logic in connecting motive-forms. In a general way, every piece of music resembles a cadence, of which each phrase will be a more or less elaborate part. In simple cases, a mere interchange of I-V-I, if not contradicted by controversial harmonies, can express a tonality. As used in traditional music, such an interchange is generally concluded with a more elaborate cadence. The concept of coherent harmony used here is deduced from the practice of the period from Bach to Wagner.

# Construction/Recognition
Coherent harmony is characterized by:
- Logical harmonic progressions that support the tonal center
- Regular harmonic rhythm (harmony generally moves more slowly than melody)
- Avoidance of "controversial harmonies" that would contradict the established tonality
- Cadential organization -- the overall harmonic motion resembles an elaborated cadence
- Consistency of harmonic rhythm and progression patterns

# Musical Context
Ordinarily the harmony moves more slowly than the melody -- a number of melodic notes usually refers to a single harmony. The contrary occasionally occurs when harmony moves quasi-contrapuntally against a melody in long notes. The accompanying harmony should reveal a certain regularity, which through motive-like repetitions contributes to unity and comprehensibility.

# Common Confusions
- "Coherent" does not mean "simple"; it means logically organized according to tonal principles
- The cadential analogy does not mean every phrase must end with a formal cadence; it means the overall harmonic direction is cadential
- Harmonic coherence is deduced from practice (Bach to Wagner), not from abstract rules
- Schoenberg refers readers to his other theoretical works for detailed treatment of root progressions
