# Quick Definition
The principle that a well-balanced melody progresses in waves, with each elevation countered by a depression, approaching a climax through intermediate high points interrupted by recessions.

# Formal Definition
A well-balanced melody progresses in waves: each elevation is countered by a depression. It approaches a high point or climax through a series of intermediate lesser high points, interrupted by recessions. Upward movements are balanced by downward movements; large intervals are compensated for by conjunct movement in the opposite direction.

# Construction/Recognition
The wave-like quality of a well-balanced melody involves:
- **Compensating motion**: upward movements followed by downward movements and vice versa
- **Intervallic balance**: large intervals compensated by stepwise (conjunct) movement in the opposite direction
- **Graduated climax**: the high point is approached through a series of progressively higher intermediate peaks
- **Recessions**: the ascent to the climax is interrupted by temporary descents, creating a wavelike profile

# Musical Context
This principle governs the melodic dimension of motive connection. When connecting motive-forms into phrases and themes, the resulting melody should exhibit this wave-like balance rather than moving relentlessly in one direction. The principle applies both within individual phrases and across larger melodic spans.

# Examples
- A melody that leaps up a sixth and then descends stepwise demonstrates intervallic compensation
- A theme that rises to progressively higher points with intervening descents demonstrates graduated climax
- The wave-like principle can be observed in virtually all well-constructed classical melodies

# Common Confusions
- "Wave-like" does not mean mechanical alternation of up and down; the waves are organic and varied
- The climax is not necessarily the highest note; it is the point of greatest intensity
- Conjunct compensation for large intervals is a tendency, not an absolute rule
- This principle describes well-balanced melody specifically; some effective melodies deliberately violate it for expressive purposes
