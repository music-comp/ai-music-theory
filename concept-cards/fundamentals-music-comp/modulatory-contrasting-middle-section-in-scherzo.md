# Quick Definition
The B-section of the scherzo, which provides contrast through modulatory elaboration of A-section material, approaching the Durchfuhrung (elaboration/development) technique of the sonata allegro, with motive-forms passing through fluctuating, unstable harmonic situations.

# Formal Definition
The main function of the B-section is to provide contrast. It is generally held that the B-section of a scherzo should be an elaboration (Durchfuhrung), but in fact it often resembles the B-section of the minuet, while many minuets possess a modulatory contrast. In the modulatory middle section, changes of shape and even of constitution occur as A-section material passes through fluctuating, unstable harmonic situations. This freedom of structure and motive treatment does not imply that regularity, logic, and balance can be ignored.

# Construction/Recognition
- Motive-forms from the A-section are modified and adapted for modulatory purposes
- The modulation is carried out at least partly by sequential treatment
- Harmony is fluctuating and unstable, contrasting with the A-section's stability
- The order of motive-forms may be changed; some may be repeated within segments
- Remotely varied motive-forms are dangerous due to harmonic instability
- The section concludes with liquidation and a pedal point preparing the recapitulation
- Modulation should proceed gradually, not in one great leap

# Musical Context
The modulatory contrasting middle section represents the scherzo's most distinctive structural feature, differentiating it from the minuet. Schoenberg preferred the term "elaboration" (Durchfuhrung) to "development" because the material is adapted, varied, expanded, condensed, recombined, and carried through various keys -- but it seldom grows or evolves into a "more mature or advanced state." The technique is the same as that found in sonata-allegro development sections, scaled to the proportions of the scherzo.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 26, Scherzo: After the sequence, the four-measure pattern is progressively reduced through liquidation
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 3, Scherzo: Chromatic ascension to the supertonic using m. 1-4 motive-forms; second theme element quoted and sequenced
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 4-III: Modulation by many sequences of a four-measure pattern, liquidated by reduction to two measures, one measure, and two-thirds of a measure
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 7-III: Modulation follows the circle of fifths from mediant major to subdominant

# Common Confusions
- The B-section of a scherzo is not always a full-scale Durchfuhrung -- it sometimes resembles a simpler minuet B-section
- "Elaboration" is Schoenberg's preferred term; he rejected "development" because the material does not truly develop in the sense of growth or evolution
- Freedom of structure does not mean absence of logic -- regularity and balance must still be maintained
