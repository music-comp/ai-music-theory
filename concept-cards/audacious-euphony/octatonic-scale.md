# Quick Definition
The octatonic scale is an eight-note scale formed by alternating half-steps and whole-steps, with three distinct transpositions in the chromatic universe, serving as the macroharmonic background for octatonic regions bridging Boretz regions.

# Formal Definition
The **octatonic scale** (also called the diminished scale) is an eight-note collection formed by alternating half-steps and whole-steps. It is:
- Symmetric under transposition by minor third (T3)
- Contains four interlocking diminished seventh chord subsets
- Has exactly three distinct forms in chromatic space

The scale provides the pitch content for octatonic regions and octatonic transformations in the Tristan-genus voice-leading system.

# Construction/Recognition
Scale structure:
- Interval pattern: H-W-H-W-H-W-H-W (or W-H-W-H-W-H-W-H)
- Pitch classes: 8 of 12 total
- Symmetry: T3, T6, T9 equivalence

Three octatonic collections:
- Oct 0,1: {C, Db, Eb, E, F#, G, A, Bb}
- Oct 1,2: {Db, D, E, F, G, Ab, Bb, B}
- Oct 2,3: {D, Eb, F, Gb, Ab, A, B, C}

Relationship to Boretz regions:
- Each octatonic collection spans two adjacent Boretz regions
- The collection contains all tones of an octatonic pole pair
- Minor seventh and French sixth chords also inhabit octatonic collections

# Musical Context
In Cohn's tetrachordal system, octatonic scales:
- Define the pitch content of octatonic bridging regions
- Provide macroharmony for transformations between Boretz regions
- Contain both members of an octatonic pole pair
- Relate to octatonic regions as hexatonic scales relate to hexatonic regions

The scale appears prominently in:
- Late Romantic chromatic practice
- Russian nationalist composers (Rimsky-Korsakov)
- Early twentieth-century music (Stravinsky, Bartok, Messiaen)

# Examples
**Tristan Prelude, chord pairs**: Certain consecutive Tristan-genus chords share an octatonic collection, enabling parsimonious connection between Boretz regions.

**Octatonic pole pairs**: The combined pitch content of any dominant seventh and its octatonic pole half-diminished seventh exhausts one octatonic scale.

# Common Confusions
- "Octatonic" refers to the 8-note scale, not to Boretz regions (which also have 8 chords, coincidentally)
- Three octatonic scales exist (not four like Weitzmann regions)
- The scale is symmetric under minor third transposition, not major third
- Octatonic scale content defines octatonic regions, not Boretz regions
