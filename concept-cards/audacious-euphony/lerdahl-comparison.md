# Quick Definition
Lerdahl's (2001) analysis of *Dichterliebe* through Weber's graph of regions reveals coherent scalar downshift but fractures after song 12; Cohn's voice-leading zone analysis reveals continuous triadic upshifting throughout, demonstrating that the two frameworks track different but compatible dimensions.

# Core Definition
"Fred Lerdahl (2001, 138) plots the key sequence of Robert Schumann's cycle of sixteen songs, *Dichterliebe*, as a coherent path through Weber's graph of regions" (p. 148). Key signatures of songs 1-8 decrement monotonically. "Beginning with the reversal from three flats to two at song 12, the pattern in key-signature space is fractured" (p. 149). Lerdahl notes final songs substitute parallel or minor-third related keys. Cohn's complementary analysis: "The continuous downshifting in scalar space through the first eleven songs of *Dichterliebe* is realized in triadic space as a phased upshifting that continues straight through to the end of the cycle" (p. 149).

# Key Properties
1. Scalar downshift (Weber/Lerdahl) and triadic upshift (Cohn) are independent dimensions
2. What fractures in scalar space continues coherently in triadic space
3. The two frameworks are complementary, not competing

# Construction / Recognition
Compare:
- Key-signature space: decrementing sharps, then fracture
- Voice-leading zone space: continuous upshifting through two rotations

# Context & Application
The comparison demonstrates the explanatory power of voice-leading zones for passages where scalar/regional analysis loses coherence.

# Examples
- Songs 1-11: monotonic scalar downshift = coherent in Lerdahl; continuous triadic upshift = coherent in Cohn
- Songs 12-16: fractured in Lerdahl; continues coherently in Cohn
