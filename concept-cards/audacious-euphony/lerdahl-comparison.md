# Quick Definition
Fred Lerdahl's analysis of *Dichterliebe*'s key sequence through Weber's graph of regions provides a comparison point for Cohn's voice-leading zone analysis, revealing that what appears as coherent scalar downshift in Lerdahl's framework is realized as phased upshifting in triadic voice-leading space.

# Formal Definition
Lerdahl (2001, 138) plots *Dichterliebe*'s key sequence as a coherent path through Weber's graph of regions, observing that key signatures of songs 1-8 decrement from three to zero sharps. Beginning with the reversal at song 12, the key-signature pattern fractures. Lerdahl notes that the final songs fulfill the projected path by substituting parallel or minor-third related keys, which are privileged in Weber's space. Cohn's complementary analysis reveals that the continuous downshifting in scalar space is realized in triadic space as phased upshifting, providing a different but compatible perspective.

# Construction/Recognition
Two analytical frameworks compared:
- **Lerdahl/Weber**: Tracks key signatures and their positions in Weber's graph; notes monotonic decrement then fracture
- **Cohn/Voice-leading zones**: Tracks boundary harmonies and their voice-leading zone positions; reveals continuous upshifting across two complete rotations

The two frameworks are not competing but complementary: scalar downshift and triadic upshift describe different aspects of the same tonal trajectory.

# Musical Context
The comparison illustrates how different analytical frameworks illuminate different aspects of tonal structure. Weber's graph and Lerdahl's extension privilege diatonic relationships, while voice-leading zones privilege chromatic voice-leading proximity.

# Examples
- *Dichterliebe* songs 1-8: Key signatures decrement (3 sharps to 0) = scalar downshift; voice-leading zones upshift
- Songs 12-16: Key-signature pattern fractures in Lerdahl's framework but voice-leading zone upshift continues coherently
- Schumann's Op. 24 *Liederkreis*: Parallel upshifting rotation in nine songs

# Common Confusions
- Scalar downshift and triadic upshift are not contradictory; they track different musical dimensions
- Lerdahl's observation about substituting parallel and minor-third keys is consistent with voice-leading zone equivalence
- The "fracture" in key-signature space at song 12 does not indicate incoherence; it indicates the limits of that analytical framework
