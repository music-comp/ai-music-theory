# Quick Definition
The relation between two consonant triads that share a common third (e.g., C major and c# minor), or the transformation that produces one from the other, moving root and fifth by semitone in parallel motion.

# Core Definition
S is defined in the Glossary as "the relation between two consonant triads that share a common third, for example, C major and c# minor; or the transformation that produces one of these triads from the other" (Glossary, p. 229). S is an involution, involves 2 semitones of voice-leading work (root and fifth both move by semitone in the same direction), and preserves the third as common tone. The algebraic identity LP = S shows how slide relations arise from hexatonic cycles. The name derives from both Karg-Elert's *Terzgleich* (same-third) and Lewin's "Slide."

# Key Properties
1. S is an involution: S(S(X)) = X
2. S preserves the third as common tone
3. Root and fifth move by semitone in parallel motion
4. 2 semitones of voice-leading work
5. LP = S (compound of L and P equals S)
6. S preserves Weitzmann region membership
7. S-related triads share no diatonic scale membership

# Construction / Recognition
- S(C major) = c# minor: (C, E, G) -> (C#, E, G#)
- S(c# minor) = C major: (C#, E, G#) -> (C, E, G)
- S(F major) = f# minor; S(f# minor) = F major

# Context & Application
S produces the most distant-sounding of the common-tone relationships, connecting triads whose roots are a semitone apart. On the Tonnetz, S appears as diagonal motion across the augmented-triad axis. S is characteristic of Romantic chromaticism and film music.

# Examples
- Schubert's characteristic key relationships
- Chopin Nocturne Op. 9 No. 1: gb minor to F major (S with Bbb/A enharmony)
- Film music staple (Lehman 2010)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing S with chromatic semitonal motion
  **Correction**: S changes mode while moving root by semitone; pure chromatic motion does not change mode

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: "Slide" refers to portamento
  **Clarification**: It refers to the visual motion on the Tonnetz, where the perfect-fifth dyad "slides" by semitone
