# Quick Definition
Three compositional scripts for navigating the triadic universe: neighborhoods and pitch retention loops (visiting adjacent zones while retaining focal tones), departure-return (moving away from and back to a referential point through complementary voice-leading directions), and continuous upshift (sustained unidirectional voice leading through multiple zones).

# Formal Definition
The three scripts organize the analytical work of Chapter 6:

1. **Neighborhoods and Pitch Retention Loops**: Compositions establish neighborhoods around a central pitch class and enact cyclical tours of the six triads sharing that tone, using **P**, **R**, and **L** transformations. The analytical focus is on localized harmonic motion with pitch-class continuity.

2. **Departure -> Return**: Compositions execute a classical path-reversal trope using voice-leading zones to define route, destination, and reversal. Variations include palindromic voice leading and departure-overshoot-return. The script can be fulfilled by any progression whose zones trace a symmetric path.

3. **Continuous Upshift**: Compositions sustain a unidirectional voice-leading trajectory across an extended span, whether as a slow unfolding of structural keys (Schumann song cycles) or an energized developmental core (Beethoven, Dvorak).

# Construction/Recognition
- **Neighborhood script**: Look for a central pitch class retained across multiple triads; check for complete or partial tours of the six triads in a neighborhood
- **Departure-return script**: Look for a zone trajectory that descends then ascends (or vice versa), possibly with substitutions or overshoot
- **Continuous upshift script**: Look for sustained directional voice leading across multiple zones, possibly spanning an entire movement or cycle

# Musical Context
These scripts provide alternatives to classical tonal schemes for organizing large-scale chromatic passages. They do not claim exclusivity; a single composition may employ multiple scripts or combine them.

# Examples
- **Neighborhood**: Schubert "Der Doppelganger" (F# neighborhood), Brahms Symphony No. 2 retransition (D and A neighborhoods)
- **Departure-return**: Schubert "Auf dem Flusse," Schubert Bb Sonata, Liszt Kyrie, Schubert "Liedesend'"
- **Continuous upshift**: Schumann *Dichterliebe*, Beethoven "Tempest" and "Waldstein" developments, Dvorak "New World" development

# Common Confusions
- The three scripts are not mutually exclusive; a composition may combine elements of multiple scripts
- The scripts describe voice-leading trajectories, not thematic or formal processes
- Repetition plays a less systematic role in Chapter 6 than in Chapter 5's sequential models; here, flexibility is the norm
