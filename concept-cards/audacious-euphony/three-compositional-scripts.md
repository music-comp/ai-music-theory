# Quick Definition
The three scripts organizing chapter 6: (1) neighborhoods and pitch retention loops (visiting adjacent zones while retaining focal tones), (2) departure-return (moving away and back via complementary voice-leading directions), and (3) continuous upshift (sustained unidirectional voice leading).

# Core Definition
The three scripts organize analytical work in chapter 6. "Some compositions establish neighborhoods and enact pitch retention loops. They visit a small set of adjacent voice-leading zones, and create continuity through retention of one or more focal tones. . . . Other compositions execute a classical departure -> return trope, using novel resources to define a route, a destination, and the nature of reversal. . . . The final script, continuous upshift, is also a variation on a classical trope" (p. 129).

# Prerequisites
- **Voice-leading zones**: The metric all three scripts traverse
- **Compositional script**: The general concept of a navigational strategy

# Key Properties
1. Scripts differ from ch5 sequences: repetition is less systematic; flexibility is the norm
2. Scripts are not mutually exclusive; compositions may combine them
3. Each script applies at different time scales and in different genres
4. Scripts describe voice-leading trajectories, not thematic/formal processes

# Construction / Recognition
- **Neighborhood**: Look for retained pitch class; adjacent zones; P/R/L tours
- **Departure-return**: Look for symmetric zone trajectory (descent then ascent)
- **Continuous upshift**: Look for sustained directional motion across multiple zones

# Context & Application
The three scripts demonstrate that "pan-triadic voice-leading models can make contributions to interpretation" (p. 129) across diverse genres: Lieder, opera, sonata, symphony, and song cycle.

# Examples
- **Neighborhood**: Schubert "Der Doppelganger," Verdi "Ah si, ben mio," Brahms Symphony No. 2 retransition
- **Departure-return**: Schubert "Auf dem Flusse," Schubert Bb Sonata, Liszt Kyrie, Schubert "Liedesend'"
- **Continuous upshift**: Schumann *Dichterliebe*, Beethoven "Tempest"/"Waldstein," Dvorak "New World"

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Each composition uses exactly one script
  **Correction**: Scripts can combine; the Dvorak development features both upshift and neighborhood elements

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The three scripts are exhaustive
  **Clarification**: Cohn makes "no claim to completeness" (p. 129)
