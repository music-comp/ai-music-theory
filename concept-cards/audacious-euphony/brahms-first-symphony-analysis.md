# Quick Definition
Cohn's analysis of the recapitulation opening in Brahms's First Symphony finale (mm. 186-221) demonstrating how thematic statements erect diatonic walls on hypermetric downbeats while liquidations dissolve into L/R and hexatonic chains.

# Formal Definition
The passage presents the principal theme in C major (mm. 186-200), diverted into a chromatic episode at m. 204, and presented again in C major counterstatement at m. 220. Both episode phrases begin with partial thematic statements (Eb major at m. 204, B major at m. 212) followed by four-measure liquidations in horns and pizzicato strings. Two Tonnetz analyses are provided: Figure 8.13(a) tracks the surface harmony crawling "fairly low to the ground"; Figure 8.13(b) proposes a middleground hearing oriented to C major throughout, organizing harmonies about an L/P (hexatonic) chain.

# Construction/Recognition
Surface analysis (Figure 8.13a):
- C major (mm. 186-200) inflected by two chromatic tones
- Flatward inflections coalesce around Eb major at m. 204
- R/P chain on southeast diagonal to eb minor (m. 207)
- Horn fifths project Bb major as eb minor's nebenverwandt
- Db at mid-measure moves to bb minor (southward)
- L/R chain westward to Cb major on hypermetric downbeat

Middleground hearing (Figure 8.13b):
- Hexatonic (L/P) chain at middleground: C major - Eb major - B major - C major
- Eb and B are salient stations by virtue of thematic statements
- eb minor (m. 207) arises through Gb-for-G substitution
- Prolongation through pitch retention loop about Bb
- Bb discharges as leading tone to Cb-qua-B major
- Similar pattern: B minor through substitution, retention loop around F#, overshoot to C major

# Musical Context
From C major standpoint, the entire chromatic episode fulfills dominant function, with the "entire alley acquiring the function of its most diatonic member." By extension, pitch retention loops visit double-dominant region. But functional reduction is not mandatory; voice-leading zones provide alternative:
- C major to eb minor: downshift
- Hexatonic alley: balanced about fulcrum
- Retention loops: temporarily lower voice-leading zone
- Final overshoot (G to C): initial downshift reversed

Brahms invokes different resources than Wagner to halt drift: no supplementary dissonances, no reversals, no syntonic images. "His walls are erected instead through the sounding of a diatonic and triadic theme on a hypermetric downbeat" - Harrison's (1994) "rhetorical" rather than "structural" means.

# Common Confusions
- The two analyses (surface vs. middleground) are not competing but complementary
- Hypermetric position is crucial to diatonic encapsulation in Brahms
- The hexatonic reading does not deny functional hearing; it provides parallel interpretation
- The liquidations are not functionally random; they trace systematic voice-leading paths
