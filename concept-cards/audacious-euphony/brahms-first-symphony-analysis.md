# Quick Definition
Cohn's analysis of the recapitulation opening in Brahms's First Symphony finale (mm. 186-221), showing how thematic statements erect diatonic walls on hypermetric downbeats while liquidations dissolve into L/R and hexatonic chains.

# Core Definition
The passage presents the principal theme in C major (mm. 186-200), diverted into a chromatic episode (m. 204), and re-presented in C major at m. 220. Both episode phrases begin with partial thematic statements (Eb major at m. 204, B major at m. 212) followed by four-measure liquidations. Two complementary analyses: Figure 8.13(a) tracks surface harmony; Figure 8.13(b) proposes a middleground hearing oriented to C major via an L/P (hexatonic) chain (pp. 209-211).

# Prerequisites
- **Convertible Tonnetz**: The model being applied
- **Diatonic encapsulation**: What thematic statements create
- **Hexatonic cycle**: The middleground organizing principle
- **Pitch retention loop**: Prolongs eb and b minor within the episode

# Key Properties
1. Two complementary analyses (surface vs. middleground) are not competing but complementary
2. Thematic statements provide rhetorical (not structural) means of encapsulation
3. Hexatonic chain at middleground: C-Eb-B-C
4. Pitch retention loops about Bb and F-sharp visit the double-dominant region

# Construction / Recognition
**Surface analysis** (Figure 8.13a): Flatward inflections coalesce around Eb major (m. 204), R/P chain to eb minor (m. 207), horn fifths to Bb/bb minor, L/R chain to Cb major on hypermetric downbeat. B major liquidation parallels exactly with one extra measure allowing overshoot to C major.

**Middleground hearing** (Figure 8.13b): L/P chain organizing Eb major and B major as salient hexatonic stations; eb minor arises through Gb-for-G substitution, prolonged through retention loop about Bb; Bb discharges as leading tone to B major.

**Contrast with Wagner**: "Where Wagner methodically stakes his tent using structural means, Brahms simply plants his feet using what Harrison (1994, 81) refers to as rhetorical ones" (p. 211). No supplementary dissonances, no reversals, no syntonic images; walls erected through "the sounding of a diatonic and triadic theme on a hypermetric downbeat" (p. 211).

# Context & Application
From the C major standpoint, the entire chromatic episode fulfills dominant function, but voice-leading zones provide an alternative: C to eb minor is downshift, hexatonic alley balances along the fulcrum, retention loops temporarily lower the zone, final G-to-C overshoot reverses the initial downshift (p. 211).
