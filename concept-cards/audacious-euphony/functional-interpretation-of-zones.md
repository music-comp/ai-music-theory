# Quick Definition
Voice-leading zones may optionally be interpreted in terms of Riemannian harmonic functions (tonic, dominant, subdominant), where downshifting correlates with dominant motion and upshifting with subdominant, but this functional scaffolding can be shed if it proves uncomfortable or Procrustean.

# Formal Definition
The potential affiliation of downshifting and upshifting with dominant and subdominant function, respectively, creates a bridge between voice-leading zone analysis and Riemannian functional harmony. Zones can be assigned functional values relative to a tonic zone: adjacent zones in the downshift direction may be heard as dominant, those in the upshift direction as subdominant. However, this hypothesis (advanced in Cohn 1999) immediately attracts counterexamples and is critiqued in Chapter 8. The zones provide a more theoretically neutral way of documenting trajectories without requiring functional commitment.

# Construction/Recognition
If tonic is at zone X:
- Zones X-1, X-2 (downshift direction): potentially dominant
- Zones X+1, X+2 (upshift direction): potentially subdominant
- The hypothesis that chords sharing an augmented triad always share harmonic function is compared to Lendvai's axis tonality theory, which makes an analogous claim about diminished seventh chords

# Musical Context
Functional interpretation of zones is most comfortable in passages with clear tonal orientation (like the Schubert Bb Sonata) and least comfortable in passages without diatonic grounding (like Liszt's Kyrie or Schubert's "Liedesend'"). The zones supervene upon functional hearings, suggesting but not mandating them.

# Examples
- Schubert, "Auf dem Flusse": Zone 10 as tonic, zones 7-8 as dominant, zone 5 as double-dominant; but g minor (m. 50) can be heard as either tonic representative or relative of dominant
- Schubert, Bb major Sonata: Zones 5 (tonic), 2 (dominant), 4 (intermediate), 1 (double-dominant), 7 (subdominant overshoot)
- Liszt, Kyrie: Functional ascriptions sit uncomfortably; zones carry structural claims alone

# Common Confusions
- Functional interpretation of zones is optional, not mandatory
- The upshift/downshift duality is immune to the metaphysical objections that plague historical harmonic dualism
- The hypothesis that entire zones share function is "Procrustean" and attracts counterexamples
- Zones provide structural claims independent of whether functional labels are applied
