# Quick Definition
Voice-leading zones may optionally be interpreted in terms of Riemannian functions (downshifting ~ dominant, upshifting ~ subdominant), but this functional scaffolding "can be shed" if uncomfortable, leaving zones to carry structural claims without functional commitment.

# Core Definition
"The potential affiliation of downshifting and upshifting, respectively, with dominant and subdominant" (p. 141) creates a bridge to functional hearing. Zones adjacent in the downshift direction may be heard as dominant; upshift direction as subdominant. But this hypothesis "immediately attracts counterexamples" (p. 141) and is "as Procrustean as Erno Lendvai's theory of axis tonality" (p. 141). "One of the benefits of voice-leading zones is that they supervene upon such functional hearings, suggesting but not mandating them" (p. 141).

# Prerequisites
- **Voice-leading zones**: The system being functionally interpreted
- **Departure-return script**: The framework revealing functional potential

# Key Properties
1. Downshifting correlates with dominant/authentic motion
2. Upshifting correlates with subdominant/plagal motion
3. The correlation is suggestive, not mandatory
4. The hypothesis that entire zones share function is Procrustean
5. Zones provide structural claims independent of functional labels
6. The dualism is immune to metaphysical objections facing historical harmonic dualism

# Construction / Recognition
If tonic at zone X:
- Zones X-1, X-2 (downshift): potentially dominant
- Zones X+1, X+2 (upshift): potentially subdominant
But: counterexamples arise readily (e.g., g minor at m. 50 of "Auf dem Flusse")

# Context & Application
The concept is most applicable when functional hearing feels natural (Schubert Bb Sonata) and least comfortable in non-functional contexts (Liszt Kyrie). The key insight is that structural coherence through zones does not require functional commitment.

# Examples
- **"Auf dem Flusse"**: Zone 10 as tonic, zones 7-8 as dominant, zone 5 as double-dominant; but counterexamples arise (p. 141)
- **Schubert Bb Sonata**: Zones 5 (T), 2 (D), 4 (intermediate), 1 (DD), 7 (S overshoot)
- **Liszt Kyrie**: Functional ascriptions sit uncomfortably; zones carry weight alone

# Common Confusions
