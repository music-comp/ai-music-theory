# Quick Definition
A chromatic sequence transposing by semitone, most commonly generated by N/L alternation (ascending form more frequent), with S/P chains executing complete Cube Dance circumnavigations.

# Core Definition
Semitone (T1/T11) sequences result from three H/W combinations: N/L (most common, ascending version more frequent), S/P (executes complete Cube Dance tour), and H/R (rare). The ascending N/L progression appears frequently; the descending version "was used by Rameau in one of the most famously expressive passages in the history of pre-Classical opera" (Rehding 2005, cited p. 113). Both directions were catalogued by Langle (1797) among his *tours de l'harmonie*.

# Key Properties
1. N/L chain: ascending form more common historically; descending used by Rameau
2. S/P chain: connects PL-related triads through complete Cube Dance circumnavigation
3. H/R: rare, involving maverick H
4. All three voices move in parallel motion for T1 (unlike T3 and T5 where motion is mixed)

# Construction / Recognition
N/L ascending: C major --(N)--> f minor --(L)--> Ab major --(N)--> db minor --(L)--> E major ...

S/P descending (Liszt *Il Penseroso*): Gb major --(S)--> f# minor --(P)--> F# major --(S)--> f minor --(P)--> F major ...
- "Connects Gb major to D major through a series of semitonal descents, first lowering the third of each major triad and then lowering the root and fifth of each minor triad" (p. 113)
- Executes a complete Cube Dance tour, "elliptically connecting two PL-related harmonies that stand directly adjacent in the same hexatonic and Weitzmann region" (p. 113)

# Context & Application
The N/L chain is the most common semitone sequence and frequently provides the basis for semitonally ascending passages in Romantic music. The S/P chain is rarer but analytically striking because it traverses the entire Cube Dance while connecting adjacent hexatonic/Weitzmann neighbors by the longest possible route.

# Examples
- **Rameau**: Descending N/L chain in expressive pre-Classical opera (Rehding 2005)
- **Langle 1797**: Both N/L directions catalogued as *tours de l'harmonie*
- **Liszt "Wilde Jagd"**: Both N/L directions represented (Ahn 2003)
- **Liszt *Il Penseroso*, mm. 17-20** (figure 5.14): S/P chain from Gb major to D major
- **Brahms *Lelio Fantasy***: Ascending N/L chain, mm. 379-439 (figure 5.21)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming ascending is the only N/L direction
  **Correction**: Both directions are well documented, with the descending form having earlier historical precedent (Rameau)

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: S/P chains are merely theoretical
  **Clarification**: They appear in Liszt's *Il Penseroso* and Prokofiev's *War and Peace* (Gollin 2000)
