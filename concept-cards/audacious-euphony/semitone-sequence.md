# Quick Definition
A chromatic sequence transposing by semitone, most commonly generated by N/L alternation, involving all three voices in directed parallel motion.

# Formal Definition
Semitone (T1/T11) sequences result from three H/W combinations:
- N/L (most common): Ascending version more frequent
- S/P: Generates complete Cube Dance tour
- H/R: Involves maverick H (rare)

The N/L chain was modeled by Rameau in famously expressive operatic passages and catalogued by Langle (1797) in both directions. S/P chains connect PL-related triads through complete Cube Dance circumnavigation.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
N/L ascending example:
C major --(N)--> f minor --(L)--> Ab major --(N)--> db minor --(L)--> E major ...

N/L descending example:
(Used by Rameau in expressive passages)

S/P example (Liszt Il Penseroso):
Gb major --(S)--> f# minor --(P)--> F# major --(S)--> f minor --(P)--> F major ...
Executes complete Cube Dance tour, connecting PL-related Gb major and D major elliptically.

# Musical Context/Application
The ascending N/L progression appears frequently, including in extended passages. The descending version was used by Rameau for expressive effect. S/P chains are rarer but execute striking complete tours of Cube Dance, as in Liszt's Il Penseroso where Gb major reaches D major through eight intermediate triads.

# Examples
- Rameau expressive passages: Descending N/L chain
- Langle 1797: Both directions catalogued as tours de l'harmonie
- Liszt "Wilde Jagd": Both N/L directions represented
- Liszt Il Penseroso, mm. 17-20: S/P chain (Figure 5.14)

- Ascending N/L is more common than descending
- S/P chains are rare but powerful (complete Cube Dance tour)
- H/R chains remain undiscovered in the repertoire
