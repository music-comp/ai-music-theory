# Quick Definition
Table 7.1 systematically aligns the analogous terms from the triadic (n=3) and tetrachordal (n=4) domains, providing a comprehensive map of correspondences that guides the development of the Tristan-genus system.

# Core Definition
Cohn develops the system of nearly even tetrachords "by analogy with the trichordal case, up to the limits of that analogy's productive power" (p. 168). Table 7.1 "aligns analogous terms from the two domains" and "can serve as a guide to this development, as it unfolds in the pages ahead. Because the analogy relies on generalization, the exposition is more technical and abstract than most other parts of this book" (p. 168). The table contains eleven rows of correspondences, from perfectly even chords through transformations to voice-leading maps.

# Key Properties
The eleven-row correspondence (Table 7.1, p. 169):
1. **Perfectly even chord**: Augmented triad / Diminished seventh
2. **Downward displacement**: Major triad / Dominant seventh
3. **Upward displacement**: Minor triad / Half-diminished seventh
4. **Union of both species**: Consonant triads / Tristan genus
5. **Regional cluster (around one perfectly even chord)**: Weitzmann region / Boretz region
6. **Geometric representation of region**: Weitzmann water bug / Boretz spider
7. **Bridges between adjacent regions**: Hexatonic region / Octatonic region
8. **Voice-leading zones**: {1,2,4,5,7,8,10,11} / {1,3,5,7,9,11}
9. **Intra-regional transformations**: R, N, S / R*, S3(2), S3(4), S6
10. **Inter-regional transformations**: L, P (+ H maverick) / S2, S4, S5 (+ octatonic pole maverick)
11. **Voice-leading map**: Cube Dance, Tonnetz / 4-Cube Trio, 3-D Tonnetz (Gollin 1998)

# Construction / Recognition
The analogy is structured through generalization:
- Replace "3" (number of chord tones) with "4"
- Replace "augmented triad" (perfectly even trichord) with "diminished seventh" (perfectly even tetrachord)
- Replace "major third" (generating interval for n=3) with "minor third" (generating interval for n=4)
- Track each concept through the replacement

# Context & Application
The analogy provides the conceptual scaffolding for the entire Tristan-genus theory. Understanding one domain deeply enables rapid understanding of the other. The critical difference: for n=3, bridging transformations involve n-2 = 1 moving voice (minimal work); for n=4, they involve n-2 = 2 moving voices. This makes the triadic case uniquely efficient at the bridges.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming the analogy is perfect in every respect
  **Correction**: The analogy has limits -- notably, the bridging efficiency differs (n-2 moving voices), and the triadic and tetrachordal zone systems are "false friends" that do not communicate

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the two systems are interconnected
  **Clarification**: They are structurally parallel but independent; triadic zone 3 and tetrachordal zone 3 have no harmonic relationship
