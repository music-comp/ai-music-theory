# Quick Definition
Sequences that transpose by an odd number of semitones, generated by alternating one H-group transformation with one W-group transformation, moving parsimoniously between adjacent hexatonic and Weitzmann regions.

# Formal Definition
Chromatic sequences transpose by odd semitonal values (T1, T3, T5, T7, T9, T11) and involve three units of voice-leading work per transposition. They are generated by alternating one transformation from the H-group (L, P, H) with one from the W-group (R, N, S). Since there are three transformations in each group, there are 3 x 3 = 9 possible combinations generating chromatic sequences.

# Construction/Recognition
Combination table (Table 5.1):
| H\W | R | N | S |
|-----|-----|-----|-----|
| L | T5 | T1 | T3 |
| P | T3 | T5 | T1 |
| H | T1 | T3 | T5 |

Classical combinations (darker shaded): L/R, L/N, P/R, P/N
Later combinations (gray shaded): L/S, H/N, H/R
Rare/undiscovered: P/S, H/S

Three semitones of voice-leading work can be conceived as combining two parsimonious transformations (one H, one W).

# Musical Context/Application
The 18th century's earliest chromatic sequences use classically normative combinations (L/R, L/N, P/R, P/N). Schubert and Liszt expand the repertoire to include maverick combinations involving S or H. The sequences enable circumnavigation of the triadic universe while maintaining parsimonious voice leading.

# Examples
- T3 sequences: P/R (Zauberharfe), N/H (Schubert Drei Klavierstucke), S/L (Liszt Lelio Fantasy)
- T5 sequences: L/R (Brahms Symphony No. 2), N/P (Bach g minor Fantasy)
- T1 sequences: N/L (many examples), S/P (Liszt Il Penseroso)

# Common Confusions
- Even transpositions (T2, T4, T6) stay within regions; odd transpositions move between them
- Three voice-leading units = two parsimonious steps combined
- Mode of initial triad affects direction (up or down) of transposition
