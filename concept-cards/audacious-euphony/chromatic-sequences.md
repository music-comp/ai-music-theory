# Quick Definition
Sequences that transpose by an odd number of semitones, generated by alternating one H-group transformation with one W-group transformation, each involving three units of voice-leading work and moving parsimoniously between adjacent hexatonic and Weitzmann regions.

# Core Definition
In chapters 2 and 4, sequences transposing by major thirds (even values) remain internal to a hexatonic or Weitzmann region. "We are now in a position to study those sequences that move parsimoniously between adjacent regions . . . The transpositional values that generate those sequences represent an odd number of semitones" (p. 107). Transposition by semitone (T1, T11) engages all three voices in parallel motion; the remaining transpositions (T3, T5, T7, T9) feature two voices in similar motion. All odd-value transpositions involve three units of semitonal work. Since three units of work implicitly combine two parsimonious transformations, an odd-valued transpositional sequence is generated by alternating one transformation from the H-group (L, P, H) with one from the W-group (R, N, S), yielding 3 x 3 = 9 combinations.

# Prerequisites
- **H-group transformations**: L, P, H -- preserve hexatonic region, shift Weitzmann region
- **W-group transformations**: R, N, S -- preserve Weitzmann region, shift hexatonic region
- **Hexatonic-Weitzmann interaction**: Why alternation is required for cross-regional motion

# Key Properties
1. Nine possible H/W combinations generate three transpositional intervals (T1, T3, T5)
2. Four classically normative combinations (L/R, L/N, P/R, P/N) use only standard transformations
3. Three later combinations (S/L, N/H, H/R) involve one maverick transformation (in Schubert, Liszt)
4. Two remaining combinations (P/S, H/S) have no documented examples
5. The transposition direction (up or down) depends on the order of operations and mode of initial triad

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the transpositional interval of the sequence
2. If odd (1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11): the sequence combines one H-group and one W-group transformation
3. Consult the combination table (Table 5.1) to identify which pair generates this interval
4. Three voice-leading units per step = two parsimonious transformations combined

# Context & Application
Chromatic sequences are the primary topic of chapter 5 before the introduction of voice-leading zones. They demonstrate how the unified model enables systematic analysis of passages crossing regional boundaries, which are the norm rather than the exception in nineteenth-century practice.

# Examples

- **T5 (perfect fifth)**: L/R (most common), N/P, H/S (undocumented)
- **T1 (semitone)**: N/L (most common), S/P, H/R

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming three voice-leading units means a minor third interval
  **Correction**: Three units refers to total semitonal displacement across all three voices, not a single-voice interval

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Even-transposition sequences also cross regions
  **Clarification**: Even transpositions (T2, T4, T6) preserve hexatonic or Weitzmann region; only odd transpositions move between adjacent regions
- **Confusion**: The nine combinations are equally common historically
  **Clarification**: Four normative combinations appear earliest (18th century); three maverick ones emerge with Schubert and Liszt; two remain undocumented
