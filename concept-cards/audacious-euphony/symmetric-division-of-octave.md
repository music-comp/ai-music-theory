# Quick Definition
Dividing the twelve semitones into equal parts, producing perfectly even collections like the augmented triad (3 parts), diminished seventh (4 parts), whole-tone scale (6 parts), or tritone (2 parts).

# Core Definition
When the chromatic octave (12 semitones) is divided into n equal parts, where n is a divisor of 12, the result is a symmetric collection that is invariant under transposition by 12/n semitones. These collections have special voice-leading properties: because they lack intervallic variety, they cannot voice-lead to other instances of the same collection type via single semitonal displacement. They serve as the "axes" around which nearly even chords (like consonant triads) rotate. "The reason that the major third has this special status is that it divides the octave into as many equal parts as the triad has notes" (p. 52).

# Prerequisites
- Basic chromatic interval theory

# Key Properties
1. Invariant under transposition by their generating interval
2. Cannot voice-lead smoothly to another instance of the same type
3. Potentially ambiguous in root or tonal orientation
4. Fewer distinct collections exist than for asymmetric types (12/n transpositions)
5. Serve as structural axes for nearly even chords
6. Each division generates a unique set of voice-leading properties

# Context & Application
Symmetric divisions play a central role in the book's theoretical framework. The augmented triad's symmetric division generates hexatonic systems (Chapter 2); the diminished seventh's symmetry underlies octatonic collections and Weitzmann regions (Chapter 4). "Via single semitonal displacement, each major triad communicates with two minor triads, and each minor triad with two major triads, precisely because major and minor triads are nearly even" (p. 52). The consonant triad's special voice-leading properties arise from its status as a minimal perturbation of the perfectly even augmented triad.

# Examples
- **Augmented triad**: {C, E, G#} divides the octave into three major thirds; 4 distinct transpositions
- **Diminished seventh**: {C, Eb, F#, A} divides into four minor thirds; 3 distinct transpositions
- **Whole-tone scale**: {C, D, E, F#, G#, A#} divides into six whole steps; 2 distinct transpositions
- **The major third's special status**: "If we are concerned with projecting neutrality about enharmonic choices, we might say that the roots form a symmetric division of the octave into three equal parts" (p. 37)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: A symmetric collection can voice-lead smoothly to another of its type
  **Correction**: Symmetric collections cannot connect to other instances of the same type via single semitone displacement

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: "Symmetric" means inversionally symmetric
  **Clarification**: Here it means transpositionally symmetric (invariant under transposition by the generating interval), though inversional symmetry often coincides

- **Confusion**: The number of transpositions equals 12
  **Clarification**: The number of distinct collections = 12 / number of notes
