# Quick Definition
A specific transformational substitution where N replaces R within an L/R chain, creating a "file transfer" that expedites arrival at a tonal goal by jumping the chain forward by eight stations.

# Formal Definition
N-for-R substitution occurs when a chromatic L/R chain replaces an R transformation with N. Since both R and N belong to the W-group, the substitution maintains the overall voice-leading trajectory while changing which specific triad follows. The substitution shifts the progression to a different "file" (parallel L/R chains related by T4), reducing the distance to the target triad.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Effect on L/R chain:
- Standard L/R: major -> submediant minor -> mediant major -> ...
- With N-for-R: major -> subdominant minor -> (continues L/R from new position)

Chain reduction:
- Full L/R circuit: 24 triads (before returning to origin modally)
- Each N-for-R substitution: saves 8 stations (one complete T4 transposition)
- Two substitutions: saves 16 stations

Tonnetz representation:
- 45° fault line shows orthogonal cut against major-third regions
- Jog transfers between parallel alleys

# Musical Context/Application
N-for-R substitution is the most common transformational substitution in 19th-century chromatic music. It enables composers to reach remote tonal goals while maintaining parsimonious voice leading and avoiding the mechanical quality of extended strict sequences.

# Examples
- Brahms German Requiem, mm. 261-71: bb minor to Bb major via one N-for-R substitution (9 moves instead of 17)
- Chopin Ballade Op. 23: Eb major to A major to Eb major via two N-for-R substitutions
- Bruckner Symphony No. 3 development: N-for-R plus PL creates whole-step modulation
- Liszt Lelio Fantasy, mm. 379-439: N/L chain with R-for-N at end (reverse pattern)

# Common Confusions
- N-for-R and R-for-N are distinct (opposite effects on trajectory)
- The substitution changes which triad comes next but maintains overall direction
- Multiple substitutions can be combined for larger shortcuts
