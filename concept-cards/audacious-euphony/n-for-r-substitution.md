# Quick Definition
A specific transformational substitution where N replaces R within an L/R chain, creating a "file transfer" on the Tonnetz that expedites arrival at a tonal goal by advancing the chain forward by eight stations.

# Core Definition
N-for-R substitution occurs within a chromatic L/R chain when N (nebenverwandt) replaces R (relative). Since both belong to the W-group, the substitution maintains the overall voice-leading trajectory while shifting to a different "file" (parallel L/R chains related by T4). Each substitution advances the chain by eight stations, reducing a full 24-triad circuit by one-third. On the Tonnetz, this appears as a 45-degree "fault line" that transfers between parallel alleys, "upshifting across all four major third alleys and restoring the original" (p. 116).

# Prerequisites
- **Transformational substitution**: The general principle of same-group substitution
- **Perfect-fifth sequence**: The L/R chain being modified

# Key Properties
1. N replaces R (both W-group); trajectory preserved
2. Each substitution saves 8 stations in the L/R chain
3. On the Tonnetz: 45-degree jog transfers between parallel alleys
4. The substitution can continue, reverse, or temporarily retreat the trajectory
5. When interpolated between two W-group operations (rather than between H-group operations), N temporarily reverses direction

# Construction / Recognition
Standard L/R: major -> submediant minor -> mediant major -> ...
With N-for-R: major -> subdominant minor -> (continues L/R from new position)

Chain reduction:
- Full L/R circuit: 24 triads
- One N-for-R: saves 8 stations (one T4 transposition)
- Two N-for-R: saves 16 stations

# Context & Application
N-for-R is the most commonly documented transformational substitution in nineteenth-century chromatic music. It enables composers to reach remote tonal goals while maintaining parsimonious voice leading and avoiding the mechanical quality of extended strict sequences.

# Examples
- **Brahms *Deutsches Requiem*, mm. 261-71** (figure 5.15): bb minor to Bb major, one N-for-R saves 8 stations (9 moves instead of 17)
- **Chopin Ballade Op. 23** (figure 5.17): Two N-for-R substitutions, each at tritone distance, saving 16 stations total; connects Eb major -> A major -> Eb major
- **Bruckner Symphony No. 3 development** (figure 5.18): Two transpositionally related phrases connect f minor to a minor. Each phrase begins with slowly evolving soundsheet arpeggios upshifting across four L/R chain stations, then interpolates a PL transformation (major tonic to German sixth, lingering in a Weitzmann region, e.g. Gb major to D major to g minor), and ends with an N substitution carrying a major dominant to a minor tonic. The PL interpolation produces whole-step transposition between phrases (rather than tritone as in Chopin Ballade), and the combined phrases traverse all four major-third alleys right to left on the Tonnetz
- **Chopin Fantasy Op. 49** (figure 5.19-5.20): N interpolated between two W-group operations (R/N chain segment), creating temporary retreat rather than continuation; the "sharp angle of the jog" represents retreat before further advance
- **Liszt *Lelio Fantasy*, mm. 379-439** (figure 5.21-5.22): R-for-N substitution within N/L chain (reverse pattern); temporary downshift to C major before cyclic closure at Db major

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming N-for-R always continues the trajectory
  **Correction**: When interpolated between W-group operations (rather than H-group), it temporarily reverses direction (as in Chopin Fantasy)

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: N-for-R and R-for-N produce the same effect
  **Clarification**: They are distinct substitutions with opposite effects on trajectory direction
