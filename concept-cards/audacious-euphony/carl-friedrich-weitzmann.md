# Quick Definition
German music theorist (1808-1880), student of Moritz Hauptmann, who first described the six-triad groups now called Weitzmann regions in his 1853 monograph *Der ubermassige Dreiklang*.

# Core Definition
Carl Friedrich Weitzmann published *Der ubermassige Dreiklang* (The Augmented Triad) in 1853, describing the systematic relationship between augmented triads and consonant triads reachable by single-semitone displacement. Weitzmann "conceived of nebenverwandt and relative as bilateral relations between keys" (p. 80), not as triadic transformations. The transformational interpretation was "not egregiously anachronistic: Oettingen 1866 interpreted the nebenverwandt in explicitly transformational terms" (p. 80). Weitzmann did not explicitly close the N/R chain into a cycle, "since his interest is in the contents of a region rather than its internal structure" (p. 80).

# Key Properties
1. Student of Moritz Hauptmann
2. Published *Der ubermassige Dreiklang* in 1853
3. Identified four augmented triads as organizing structures for all 24 consonant triads
4. Described nebenverwandt and relative as key relations (not transformations)
5. Created Figure 3.9/5.1 showing systematic organization
6. Did not close the N/R chain into a cycle
7. Hauptmann defined nebenverwandt as "inversion about the tone that participates in both the major third and the perfect fifth" (p. 80)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Attributing transformational thinking to Weitzmann
  **Correction**: He described key relations, not triadic transformations; Oettingen (1866) first interpreted his work transformationally

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Weitzmann described the N/R cycle
  **Clarification**: He described a chain, not a cycle; Cohn closes it
