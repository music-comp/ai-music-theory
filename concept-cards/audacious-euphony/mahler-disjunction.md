# Quick Definition
Mahler's aspiration for a D major chord that "had to sound as though it had fallen from heaven" in his First Symphony illustrates the Romantic aesthetic of privileging disjunction, which the voice-leading zone model can systematically address by identifying zone-diametric relationships.

# Formal Definition
Mahler's letter about the C major finale of his First Symphony describes seeking a D major arrival that sounds otherworldly. The transition from C major to D major represents maximum voice-leading distance: the two triads are zone-diametric (zones 11 and 5, respectively, a distance of 6 -- the maximum possible). This inverts the usual assumption that conjunct voice leading is privileged, demonstrating that Romantic composers also valued the "remote, unattainable, and inexplicable." The voice-leading zone model provides a metric for such disjunction, eliminating the need for "vain groping."

# Construction/Recognition
To find maximally disjunct triads from a given triad:
1. Calculate the triad's voice-leading zone
2. Add 6 (mod 12) to find the diametric zone
3. Select triads from that zone
4. The zone-diametric interval is either T2 (whole step) or T6 (tritone)

For C major (zone 11): zone 11 + 6 = zone 5, containing Bb major, D major, and f# minor.

# Musical Context
The passage exemplifies a broader nineteenth-century aesthetic: the deliberate contravention of smooth voice leading as a compositional resource. The voice-leading zone model, though designed for conjunction, provides the means to model disjunction as well.

# Examples
- Mahler, Symphony No. 1, finale: C major to D major (zone 11 to zone 5, maximum distance)
- "No need to vainly grope" -- the zone model systematizes what Mahler achieved intuitively

# Common Confusions
- Maximum disjunction is not arbitrary; it is a precise metric distance in voice-leading space
- Zone-diametric relations involve T2 or T6 transposition, both producing maximum distance
- The model privileges conjunction but provides disjunction as a "residual benefit"
