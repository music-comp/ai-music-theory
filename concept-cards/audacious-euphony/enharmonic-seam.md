# Quick Definition
The boundary between diatonic spellings where enharmonic equivalence must be invoked, distinguishing passages that "tease" the seam (four-chord complex) from those that "plunge through" it (five- or six-chord N/R chain).

# Core Definition
The enharmonic seam appears at the S-related exterior triads of a double-agent complex. "The Bb/Cb enharmony is essential: no renotation will bring them into orthographic conformance without sacrificing locally consonant and diatonic connections, each of which is fully salient to perception" (p. 87). The four-chord complex "may merely tease the enharmonic seam," while the six-chord N/R chain "necessarily plunges through it" (p. 97). This distinction maps "an incremental historical process that moves from the circumscribed realm of classically determinate diatonic tonality into the symmetric chromatic universe of twelve equally tempered tones" (p. 97).

# Prerequisites
- **Double-agent complex**: The seam appears at the S connection of the exterior triads
- **S transformation**: The transformation that crosses the seam

# Key Properties
1. The seam is notational, not acoustic (the pitches are the same in equal temperament)
2. S-related triads share no diatonic scale, necessitating enharmonic respelling
3. The four-chord complex can avoid the seam; the complete N/R cycle cannot
4. "Teasing" vs. "plunging" describes compositional treatment, not absolute categories
5. The seam location depends on which triads frame the double-agent complex

# Construction / Recognition
In a Bb minor context: Gb major contains Bbb (minor third); F major contains A (major third). These are enharmonically equivalent but spell differently. Direct Gb -> F motion crosses the seam via S transformation.

# Context & Application
The expansion from four-chord complexes (which can tease the seam) to five- and six-chord chains (which must plunge through it) traces the historical development from Classical diatonic tonality to Romantic chromatic practice.

# Examples
**Chopin, Mazurka Op. 6 No. 1** (pp. 87-88): Teases the seam, "preventing the latter from coming into direct contact with its enharmonic equivalent."
**Chopin, Nocturne Op. 9 No. 1** (pp. 87-88): Plunges through with "Bbb to A in same voice."
**Historical trajectory** (p. 97): "From the four-chord double-agent complex, which might merely tease the enharmonic seam, to the six-chord N/R chain, which necessarily plunges through it."

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating the seam as an acoustic phenomenon
  **Correction**: The seam is notational; the pitches are acoustically identical in equal temperament

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: "Teasing" and "plunging" are fixed properties of passages
  **Clarification**: They describe compositional treatment, with a spectrum of possibilities between them
