# Quick Definition
Analysis of Liszt's Grande fantaisie symphonique (Lelio Fantasy, 1834) showing N/L chain with R-for-N substitution that achieves cyclic closure on Cube Dance while enabling tonic return.

# Formal Definition
Measures 379-429 feature an oboe melody transposed successively upward by semitone. The underlying harmonies execute an N/L chain segment from Db major to E major. At the expected N transformation from E major to a minor, the chain substitutes R, going to C major instead. From C major, the N/L chain resumes and arrives not at the expected F major but at tonic Db major, demonstrating cyclic closure.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
N/L chain with R-for-N substitution:
Db major --(N/L)--> ... --(N/L)--> E major --(R, not N)--> C major --(N/L)--> ... --(N/L)--> Db major

The R-for-N substitution:
- Expected: E major --(N)--> a minor --(L)--> F major ...
- Actual: E major --(R)--> c# minor (respelled c minor context?) -> C major
- Creates temporary retreat, colonizing Weitzmann region

Cube Dance representation (Figure 5.23):
- Shows consistent clockwise/upshifting trajectory
- Temporary downshift to C major visible
- Cyclic closure at return of Db major explicit

Tonnetz representation (Figure 5.22):
- Shows sequential nature of upshift progression
- Jog at substitution point
- Cyclic closure disguised (requires recognizing enharmonic equivalence)

# Musical Context/Application
This analysis demonstrates Cube Dance's superiority for showing cyclic closure and voice-leading trajectory, while Tonnetz better reveals the sequential nature of triadic choices. The comparison illustrates when each representational tool is preferable.
