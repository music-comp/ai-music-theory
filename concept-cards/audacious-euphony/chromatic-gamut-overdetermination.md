# Quick Definition
The twelve-element equal-tempered chromatic gamut is doubly determined: arising through Pythagorean fifth-generation (acoustic closure at 12 elements) but proving abundantly divisible by 2, 3, 4, and 6, enabling the perfectly even chords essential to Neo-Riemannian theory.

# Formal Definition
The chromatic collection has twelve elements through "an arithmetic fortuity: it is when m = 12 that 3^m suitably approximates 2^n for some value of n." Projecting along the chain of just fifths, the twelfth generation achieves pseudo-closure (the Pythagorean comma), halting the proliferation of tones. Once temperament distributes the twelve tones equally, another property emerges as stylistically determinant: "twelve is an abundant number, divisible by 2, 3, 4, and 6, that is, by every nonunit interval besides its just-fifth generator." This divisibility enables whole-tone scales, augmented triads, and diminished seventh chords - the perfectly even structures that anchor hexatonic and Weitzmann regions.

# Construction/Recognition
Acoustic origin (Pythagorean closure):
- 3^12 = 531,441
- 2^19 = 524,288
- Ratio: approximately 1.0136 (Pythagorean comma)
- Accepting this as pseudo-unison yields 12-tone closure

Structural property (abundant divisibility):
- 12 / 2 = 6 (whole-tone scale, tritone)
- 12 / 3 = 4 (augmented triad)
- 12 / 4 = 3 (diminished seventh chord)
- 12 / 6 = 2 (tritone)

Counterfactual: "Had the eleventh or thirteenth powers of 3 been suitable approximations of some power of 2, we would have inherited a chromatic system incapable of equal division: no whole-tone scales, no augmented triads, no diminished seventh chords."

Without perfectly even divisions:
- No hexatonic systems (no augmented triads to perturb)
- No Weitzmann regions (same reason)
- No Boretz regions (no diminished seventh chords)
- All nearly even chords would form single exhaustive cycles

# Musical Context/Application
The chromatic gamut's overdetermination is the second level enabling 19th-century chromatic syntax:

Level 1: Triads are preadapted as voice-leading optimizers by near-evenness
Level 2: The 12-gamut is preadapted for multiple even divisions enabling the structures triads perturb

"The projection of just fifths 'knew nothing' of the abundant divisibility of the number 12." Yet this divisibility proves essential for the structures (hexatonic cycles, Weitzmann regions, Boretz regions) that organize chromatic triadic space.

# Examples
- Augmented triad (4+4+4 semitones): Divides octave in 3
- Diminished seventh chord (3+3+3+3 semitones): Divides octave in 4
- Whole-tone scale (2+2+2+2+2+2 semitones): Divides octave in 6
- Tritone (6+6 semitones): Divides octave in 2
- The four hexatonic regions: Each contains one augmented triad
- The four Weitzmann regions: Same structure
- The three Boretz regions: Each contains one diminished seventh chord

# Common Confusions
- The 12-gamut's divisibility was not the reason for its adoption; it was discovered afterward
- Temperament is necessary to realize the divisibility; untempered 12-tone scales lack it
- The double determination is genuinely independent; acoustic closure and abundant divisibility are separate properties
- "Abundant" is a technical number-theoretic term (12 is an abundant number because 1+2+3+4+6 > 12)
