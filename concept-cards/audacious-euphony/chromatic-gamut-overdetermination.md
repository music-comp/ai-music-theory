# Quick Definition
The twelve-element equal-tempered chromatic gamut is doubly determined: arising through Pythagorean fifth-generation (acoustic closure at 12 elements) but proving abundantly divisible by 2, 3, 4, and 6, enabling the perfectly even chords essential to neo-Riemannian theory.

# Core Definition
The chromatic collection has twelve elements "through an arithmetic fortuity: it is when m = 12 that 3^m suitably approximates 2^n for some value of n" (p. 231). Once temperament distributes the twelve tones equally, another property emerges: "twelve is an abundant number, divisible by 2, 3, 4, and 6, that is, by every nonunit interval besides its just-fifth generator" (p. 232). This divisibility enables whole-tone scales, augmented triads, and diminished seventh chords -- the perfectly even structures that anchor hexatonic and Weitzmann regions.

# Prerequisites
- **Overdetermination**: The general concept applied to the gamut
- **Perfectly even chord**: The structures enabled by the gamut's divisibility

# Key Properties
1. Pythagorean closure: 3^12 = 531,441 approximates 2^19 = 524,288 (ratio ~1.0136)
2. Abundant divisibility: 12 / 2, 3, 4, 6 = whole-tone, augmented triad, diminished 7th, tritone
3. "The projection of just fifths 'knew nothing' of the abundant divisibility of the number 12" (p. 232)
4. Had closure occurred at 11 or 13, no equal divisions would exist
5. Without perfectly even chords, no hexatonic, Weitzmann, or Boretz regions

# Construction / Recognition
**Counterfactual** (p. 232): "Had the eleventh or thirteenth powers of 3 been suitable approximations of some power of 2, we would have inherited a chromatic system incapable of equal division: no whole-tone scales, no augmented triads, no diminished seventh chords."

Without divisibility (p. 233): "the consonant triads would behave like 7-in-12 diatonic collections in chromatic space, or 3-in-7 diatonic triads in diatonic-scalar space. Without perfectly even trichords to minimally perturb, the consonant triads would group neither into hexatonic systems nor Weitzmann regions."

# Context & Application
The gamut's overdetermination is the second essential level: "the preadaptation of the fifth-generated series of pitch classes to the multiple even divisions of chromatic space is just as essential" as the triad's overdetermination (p. 232). Together they generate "much of the structural intricacy and affective multiplicity of much of the music at the heart of the repertory of concert halls and operatic stages" (p. 233).

# Examples
- 12 / 3 = 4: Augmented triad (divides octave in 3)
- 12 / 4 = 3: Diminished seventh chord (divides octave in 4)
- 12 / 6 = 2: Tritone (divides octave in 6)
- 12 / 2 = 6: Whole-tone scale (divides octave in 2)
- 12 is an abundant number: 1+2+3+4+6 = 16 > 12

# Common Errors
- **Error**: The 12-gamut's divisibility was the reason for its adoption
  **Correction**: It arose through Pythagorean closure; divisibility was discovered afterward

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Temperament creates the divisibility
  **Clarification**: Temperament realizes it; the mathematical fact about 12 is independent of temperament
