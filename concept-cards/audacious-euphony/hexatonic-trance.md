# Quick Definition
The hexatonic trance is an analytical concept describing the hypnotic, suspended quality of passages that cycle through triads within a single hexatonic system, lacking the cadential resources of classical tonality and creating tonal multistability.

# Formal Definition
When a musical passage confines itself to the six triads of a single hexatonic system, it enters a state of "hexatonic trance" characterized by: (1) absence of fifth-related roots, (2) absence of dissonance requiring resolution, (3) absence of diatonic coordination, and (4) equal potential for any constituent triad to serve as tonic. The progression is "neutral with respect to its potential tonics" and achieves stability only through external rhetorical means (first, last, loudest, longest) or by recruitment of external syntactic resources.

# Construction/Recognition
Structural features producing the trance state:
- 3:2 phasing (triple periodicity within voices, duple periodicity between them)
- Hocket canon structure (voices enter canonically at two-beat intervals)
- Balanced voice leading (alternating up and down, no net directional motion)
- Center of balance remains roughly constant despite local fluctuations
- Circumnavigation produces "neither upward nor downward motion through the pitch spectrum"

Comparison to African musical traditions: "Hexatonic cycles embed a hocket canon that projects a 3:2 phasing" similar to patterns associated with spirit possession and trance (p. 44).

# Musical Context
The hexatonic trance helps explain the "semantic charge" of chromatic progressions by major third and their association with "altered and unstable mental states." The effect is not purely conventional but arises from the paradoxical nature of the progressions themselves: simultaneous contrary motion that confounds the consonance/dissonance binary.

# Examples
From the text (p. 40-44):
- Wagner's Tarnhelm progression: depicting invisibility through tonal instability
- Rimsky-Korsakov's Antar: "reclusive Antar adrift on the sands of the Sahara desert"
- Wagner's Parsifal (Klingsor): "another recluse in the Arabian wasteland"
- Schubert's "Nacht und Traume": "nocturnal fixation on evanescent dreams"

Richard Taruskin's observation: chromatic progressions by major third have a "link to altered and uncanny states" (p. 40-41).

Semiotic associations: supernatural phenomena, altered states of consciousness, dreams, invisibility, liminality between life and death.

# Common Confusions
- The "trance" is an analytical metaphor, not a claim about actual psychological states in listeners
- Hexatonic trance does not require literal cycling; even partial traversal of a hexatonic system can produce the effect
- The trance state can be broken by introducing diatonic elements or cadential gestures
- Do not confuse with general "hypnotic" effects in music; hexatonic trance specifically requires confinement to a single hexatonic system
