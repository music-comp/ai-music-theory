# Quick Definition
Cohn's analysis of Chopin's f minor Mazurka, Op. 68 no. 4, demonstrates transformation-class substitutions converting T5 seventh-chord sequences to T11 sequences while maintaining consistent downshifting trajectory, with zone 1 serving as a structural articulating point.

# Core Definition
The **Chopin Mazurka analysis** examines mm. 33-40 of Op. 68 no. 4, showing how "class substitutions can introduce variety while still maintaining an overall voice-leading trajectory" (p. 177). Tymoczko (2011b, 284-86) shows the initial phrases execute a T11 chain of dominant seventh chords, and conjectures the Mazurka is "a virtual rewriting" of the e minor Prelude. In mm. 33-37, a T5 chain alternates S2 and S3(4). "Leading into m. 38, S4 substitutes for S2, so that Aø7 sounds in place of projected Eø7. This substitution triggers a conversion of the T5 sequence to one generated by T11" (p. 177). The substitution does not affect the downshifting trajectory.

# Key Properties
1. T5 to T11 conversion through S4-for-S2 substitution at m. 38
2. Voice-leading trajectory continues downshifting uniformly despite substitution
3. Zone 1 as recurring structural marker: G7 (m. 33), Bb7 (m. 36), Db7 (m. 39)
4. Interpolated diminished seventh chords (mm. 37-39) engage even zones
5. The Mazurka text is "notoriously unstable" (Kallberg 1985) (p. 178 n. 16)

# Construction / Recognition
Transformation patterns (Figure 7.18):
- Mm. 33-37: T5 chain (S2 + S3(4))
- M. 38: S4 substitutes for S2 (Aø7 replaces projected Eø7)
- Mm. 38-39: T11 chain (S2 + S3(2)) until Db7 (German sixth in f minor) triggers reprise
- Diminished sevenths interpolated on beat 2 of mm. 37-39

Zone 1 articulations:
- G7 (m. 33): double dominant, initiates sequence
- Bb7 (m. 36, beat 3): prompts rhythmic acceleration
- Db7 (m. 39, beat 3): breaks T11 sequence, triggers reprise
- E7 (m. 5 of reprise): triggers dream fantasy in A major (hexatonic pole of f minor)

# Context & Application
The analysis demonstrates how transformation-class substitutions work in tetrachordal space, paralleling the triadic substitutions of Chapter 5. Different transformation combinations produce the same transpositional value, and substitutions maintain voice-leading direction while changing the specific operations.

# Examples
- **Mm. 33-40**: T5 chain converts to T11 via substitution; Aø7 at m. 38 replaces projected Eø7 (p. 177)
- **Interpolated diminished sevenths (mm. 37-39)**: Bridge gaps in voice-leading descent, engaging even zones (p. 179)
- **Zone 1 returns**: Each appearance of zone 1 (G7, Bb7, Db7) marks a structural articulation (pp. 178-179)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking the substitution changes the voice-leading direction
  **Correction**: The substitution changes the transformation pair but the downshifting trajectory continues uniformly

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming the "rewriting" relationship is biographical
  **Clarification**: Tymoczko's conjecture is analytical -- both pieces realize similar abstract scripts -- not a claim about Chopin's compositional process
