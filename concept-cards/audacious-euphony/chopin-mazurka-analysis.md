# Quick Definition
Cohn's analysis of Chopin's f minor Mazurka, Op. 68 no. 4, demonstrates transformation-class substitutions in Tristan-genus progressions, showing how T5 sequences convert to T11 sequences while maintaining consistent downshifting trajectory.

# Formal Definition
The **Chopin Mazurka analysis** examines how transformation-class substitutions operate in seventh-chord progressions, specifically showing:
1. T5 chains of dominant seventh chords (as in the e minor Prelude)
2. Conversion to T11 chains through class substitution
3. Consistent voice-leading trajectory despite substitutional variety
4. Zone 1 as a structural articulating point throughout the piece

Tymoczko (2011b) suggests the Mazurka is "a virtual rewriting" of the e minor Prelude, both exploring systematic seventh-chord descent.

# Construction/Recognition
Transformation patterns (mm. 33-40):
- Mm. 33-37: T5 chain of dominant seventh chords
- Local alternation: S2 and S3(4)
- At m. 38: S4 substitutes for S2
- This substitution converts T5 sequence to T11 sequence
- Downshift trajectory continues unaffected

Zone 1 structuring:
- G7 at m. 33 (double dominant)
- Bb7 at m. 36, beat 3 (prompts rhythmic acceleration)
- Db7 at m. 39, beat 3 (breaks sequence, triggers reprise)
- E7 at m. 5 of reprise (triggers dream fantasy in A major)

# Musical Context/Application
The analysis demonstrates:
- How transformation-class substitutions work in tetrachordal space
- The parallel between Mazurka Op. 68/4 and Prelude Op. 28/4
- Zone 1 as a recurring structural marker
- The flexibility of Tristan-genus sequences

The Mazurka shows how:
- Different transformation combinations produce same transpositional result
- Substitutions maintain voice-leading direction
- Seventh-chord progressions can be systematically varied

# Examples
**Mm. 33-40**: T5 dominant seventh chain (G7 → C7 → F7 → Bb7 → Eb7) converts to T11 chain through S4-for-S2 substitution. The Aø7 at m. 38 replaces projected Eø7, triggering the conversion.

**Interpolated diminished sevenths (mm. 37-39)**: Diminished seventh chords appear on beat 2, bridging gaps in the cumulative voice-leading descent and engaging even-numbered voice-leading zones.

**Zone 1 returns**: Each return to zone 1 (G7, Bb7, Db7) marks a structural articulation in the passage.

# Common Confusions
- The substitution changes the transformation, not the voice-leading trajectory
- T5 and T11 are different transpositional values, but both produce downshifting
- The Mazurka text is unstable (Kallberg 1985); analysis must be tempered
- The "rewriting" relationship with the Prelude is analytical, not biographical
