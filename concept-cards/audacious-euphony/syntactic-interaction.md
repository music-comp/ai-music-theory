# Quick Definition
The intertwining of diatonic and chromatic syntaxes in 19th-century music, where both systems of organizing triadic relations operate simultaneously without collapsing into each other.

# Formal Definition
Syntactic interaction describes how the two syntaxes governing consonant triads - classical diatonic tonality (based on acoustic consonance and root relations) and pan-triadic chromaticism (based on voice-leading parsimony and near-evenness) - interpenetrate in compositional practice. As Steven Rings emphasized, a model of syntactic interaction "must do more than dump transformational labels into the same cage with Roman numerals or Schenkerian representations; it needs to study how they get along within those confines" (2007, 34). Without such modeling, "the theoretical divide is thus reified in analytical practice, resulting in a curiously bifocal view of chromatic harmony, one in which the triad seems not so much 'overdetermined' as dichotomous."

# Construction/Recognition
The challenge is to model intertwining without collapsing:
- Classical tonality: Triads organized by acoustic consonance, root-indexing, T-S-D-T functions, Rings arrows (semantic forces of gravity, attraction, leading)
- Pan-triadic chromaticism: Triads organized by voice-leading work, L/P/R transformations, hexatonic and Weitzmann regions

Key mechanisms of interaction:
1. Local diatonic encapsulation within global chromatic journeys
2. Overdetermined triads functioning in both systems simultaneously
3. Cadential processes that erect/dissolve diatonic walls
4. Rings arrows that can be activated or deactivated depending on context

# Musical Context
Ernst Kurth's observation serves as epigraph: "Everywhere, Romanticism exploits the ability to hear one and the same phenomenon in two and more ways; it is fond of this coexistence and its indefiniteness."

Syntactic interaction is necessary because:
- The two syntaxes are deeply intertwined in most 19th-century compositions
- Chromatic progressions are often locally tonicized even while participating in voice-leading chains
- Sequential passages temporarily suspend diatonic orientation while using diatonic materials
- Composers cultivated ambiguity between diatonic and chromatic hearings

# Examples
- Schubert Bb Sonata first movement: Cohn (1999) modeled hexatonic cycles positioned as cross-sections of a cylinder, with fifth relations connecting horizontally
- Schubert Eb Impromptu D. 899 no. 2: Rings (2007) positioned triads at intersection of "vertical tonal-functional" and "horizontal hexatonic" axes
- Liszt Consolation no. 3: Each hexatonic station is locally tonicized, functioning simultaneously as voice-leading object in Db hexatonic space and acoustic object in local diatonic spaces
- Wagner Parsifal Faith Proclamation: Plagal drift through L/R chain halted by diatonic encapsulation forces

# Common Confusions
- Syntactic interaction is not about choosing between diatonic and chromatic analyses; it is about understanding how both operate simultaneously
- The model does not privilege one syntax over another; both are equally real aspects of the music
- Interaction does not mean the syntaxes blend into a single hybrid; they remain distinct systems that can be separately traced even when co-present
