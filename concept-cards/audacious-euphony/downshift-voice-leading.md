# Quick Definition
Voice leading in which all moving voices proceed downward under idealized voice leading, associated with transformations like Parallel (P) from major to minor.

# Formal Definition
Downshift voice leading occurs when, under idealized voice leading, all voices that move between two chords proceed in the descending direction. Stationary voices (common tones) are permissible, but no voice moves upward. This contrasts with upshift voice leading (all movement upward) and balanced voice leading (some voices up, some down). The direction of voice motion connects to Harrison's (1994) discussion of harmonic dualism.

# Construction/Recognition
For a connection from triad X to triad Y:
- Sum of directed voice-leading intervals <= 0
- Each moving voice moves by a negative (descending) interval
- Common tones contribute 0 to the sum

Examples:
- C major to c minor (P): E descends to Eb, C and G stationary = downshift
- E major to e minor (P): G# descends to G, E and B stationary = downshift

Downshift voice leading characterizes:
- P transformations from major to minor triads
- Certain sequential patterns with consistent descending motion

# Musical Context
The distinction between upshift and downshift:
- Relates to Riemann's harmonic dualism (major vs. minor as "up" vs. "down")
- Affects perceived directionality of progressions
- Can characterize entire passages or sequences
- Provides refinement beyond simple voice-leading distance

Cohn argues that dualism is "a particular instance of a more fundamental phenomenon, the relationship between upward and downward melodic motion."

# Examples
- C major to c minor: downshift (E to Eb)
- The P transformation from any major triad to its parallel minor is always downshift
- Sequences with consistent descending chromatic lines exhibit downshift character

# Common Confusions
- Downshift refers to pitch direction, not registral direction (under idealized voice leading)
- A downshift progression can be realized with ascending registers in actual music
- Downshift is not necessarily "darker" or more "minor" - it describes voice-leading direction
- The concept applies to the connection between chords, not to chords in isolation
