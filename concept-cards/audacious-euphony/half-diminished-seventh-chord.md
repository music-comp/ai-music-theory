# Quick Definition
In Cohn's framework, the half-diminished seventh chord is a nearly even tetrachord formed by displacing one tone of a diminished seventh chord upward by semitone, making it a member of the Tristan genus alongside the dominant seventh.

# Formal Definition
The **half-diminished seventh chord** (ø7) is one of two species of nearly even tetrachords in the Tristan genus. It is formed by:
1. Taking a fully diminished seventh chord (perfectly even tetrachord)
2. Displacing one of its four tones upward by semitone

There are twelve half-diminished seventh chords in the chromatic universe, each built on a different root. Each is a semitonal displacement of one of three diminished seventh chords.

Standard structure: minor triad with minor seventh above root, or equivalently, diminished triad with major seventh above root (e.g., B-D-F-A)

Alternative analysis (dualist): minor triad with under-seventh (D-F-A with B below)

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Interval structure:
- Standard view: [3, 3, 4, 2] (m3 + m3 + M3 + M2)
- Cohn's view: diminished seventh with one tone raised

Boretz region membership:
- Each diminished seventh generates four ø7 chords (one per displaced tone)
- Example: B-D-F-G# generates Bø7, Dø7, Fø7, G#ø7 (by raising B→C, D→D#, etc.)

Voice-leading properties:
- 2 units of work to any chord in same Boretz region
- Contrary semitonal motion to same-species chords (other ø7s in region)
- Mixed motion to opposite-species chords (V7s in region)

# Musical Context/Application
Half-diminished seventh chords in Cohn's analysis:
- May be heard as minor triads with under-sevenths (reduction strategy)
- Function as voice-leading entities in Tristan-genus progressions
- Form hexatonic pole relationships when their embedded minor triads are considered
- Occupy odd-numbered voice-leading zones (complementary to diminished seventh)

The chord's analysis depends on context:
- Tonal context: pre-dominant function, leading to dominant
- Tristan-genus context: nearly even tetrachord in voice-leading network
- Dualist context: minor triad with supplementary bass dissonance

# Examples
**Tristan Prelude, chord 1**: The opening F-A-B-D# (enharmonically spelled) is a ø7 chord that "spoils" the B-D-F-G# diminished seventh by raising D to D#.

**Tristan Prelude, chord 5**: The Aø7 chord continues the pattern, with C as the "spoiler" displacing the B-D-F-G# core.

**Wagner's late music**: ø7 chords frequently treated as interchangeable with minor triads, supporting the under-seventh/reduction analysis.

**Brunnhilde's Immolation**: ø7 chords alternate with V7 chords within the E-G-A#-C# Boretz region, creating balanced voice-leading oscillation.

# Common Confusions
- The ø7 can be analyzed as either a seventh chord (standard) or a minor triad with added bass (dualist)
- "Half-diminished" refers to interval structure (diminished fifth, minor seventh); in Cohn's view, it's "half-displaced" from diminished seventh
- The chord is inversionally related to V7, not derived from it
- Context determines whether functional or voice-leading analysis is appropriate
