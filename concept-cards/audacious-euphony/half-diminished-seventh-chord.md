# Quick Definition
In Cohn's framework, the half-diminished seventh chord is a nearly even tetrachord formed by displacing one tone of a diminished seventh chord upward by semitone, making it a member of the Tristan genus alongside the dominant seventh.

# Core Definition
The **half-diminished seventh chord** (ø7) is one of two species of nearly even tetrachords in the Tristan genus. It is formed by taking a fully diminished seventh chord and displacing one of its four tones upward by semitone. Twelve half-diminished seventh chords exist in the chromatic universe. The chord admits dual analysis: standard theory treats it as a diminished triad with minor seventh above root (e.g., B-D-F-A); dualist theory treats it as a minor triad (D-F-A) with under-seventh (B) generated downward. Under reduction, it uniquely embeds a minor triad, and Wagner "often treats minor triads and their ø7 supersets as interchangeable in his late music" (p. 162).

# Prerequisites
- **Diminished seventh chord**: The perfectly even core from which ø7 is derived by upward displacement
- **Nearly even tetrachord**: ø7 is the upward-displacement species of nearly even tetrachord

# Key Properties
1. Upward displacement from diminished seventh (one tone raised by semitone)
2. Each diminished seventh generates four ø7 chords
3. Two voice-leading units to any chord in the same Boretz region
4. Inversionally related to V7 (same set class [0258])
5. Occupies odd-numbered voice-leading zones (1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11)
6. Under reduction strategy, uniquely embeds a minor triad (under-seventh analysis)

# Construction / Recognition
- From diminished seventh B-D-F-G#: raise B to C = Cø7, raise D to D# = D#ø7, raise F to F# = F#ø7, raise G# to A = Aø7
- Interval structure: [3, 3, 4, 2] (m3 + m3 + M3 + M2)
- Boretz region: determined by which diminished seventh it displaces

Context-dependent analysis:
- **Tonal context**: Pre-dominant function (iiø7)
- **Tristan-genus context**: Nearly even tetrachord in voice-leading network
- **Dualist context**: Minor triad with supplementary bass dissonance

# Context & Application
The half-diminished seventh chord's dual identity makes it central to Cohn's argument. Its status as both a functional harmony (pre-dominant, or Tristan chord) and a voice-leading entity (nearly even tetrachord in a Boretz region) exemplifies the "convertibility" between tonal and chromatic syntax developed in Chapter 8.

# Examples
- **Tristan Prelude, chord 1**: F-A-B-D# (enharmonically a ø7) "spoils" B-D-F-G# diminished seventh by raising D to D# (p. 168)
- **Tristan Prelude, chord 5**: Aø7 with C as the spoiler displacing B (p. 169)
- **Wagner's late music**: ø7 chords frequently treated as interchangeable with minor triads (pp. 161-162)
- **Parsifal, Herzeleide motive**: c# minor with under-seventh (ø7) creates hexatonic pole embedded in dissonance (p. 163)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming the "root" of a ø7 must be the lowest third-stack tone
  **Correction**: Under the reduction strategy, the unique embedded minor triad determines the functional core, which may differ from the third-stack root

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking "half-diminished" means halfway between diminished triad and diminished seventh
  **Clarification**: In Cohn's framework, the "half" could be understood as "half-displaced" from the perfectly even diminished seventh
