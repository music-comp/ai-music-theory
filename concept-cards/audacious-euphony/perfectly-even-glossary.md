# Quick Definition
A collection of tones that divides the octave into equal parts, such as the augmented triad (dividing into three equal parts) or the diminished seventh chord (dividing into four equal parts).

# Formal Definition
Perfectly even is defined in the Glossary as "a collection of tones that divides the octave into equal parts." In twelve-tone equal temperament, perfectly even collections are possible only for cardinalities that divide 12 evenly: 2 (tritone), 3 (augmented triad), 4 (diminished seventh chord), and 6 (whole-tone scale). Perfectly even chords serve as axes or centers for the regions that organize nearly even chords: augmented triads center hexatonic and Weitzmann regions; diminished seventh chords center octatonic and Boretz regions.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Perfectly even chords in 12-tone space:
- Cardinality 2: Tritone (6+6 semitones) - 6 instances
- Cardinality 3: Augmented triad (4+4+4 semitones) - 4 instances
- Cardinality 4: Diminished seventh chord (3+3+3+3 semitones) - 3 instances
- Cardinality 6: Whole-tone scale (2+2+2+2+2+2 semitones) - 2 instances

Mathematical property: Perfectly even n-chords exist only when n divides 12

Role in Neo-Riemannian theory:
- Perfectly even chords are transpositionally symmetric
- They serve as "switching stations" between transformational regions
- Nearly even chords are defined as minimal perturbations of perfectly even chords

# Musical Context/Application
Perfectly even chords are structurally central but traditionally considered unstable:
- Augmented triads require resolution in tonal contexts
- Diminished seventh chords are "omnitonic" (no clear root)
- In pan-triadic syntax, they serve as hubs connecting regions

The Cube Dance graph places augmented triads at the center of hexatonic/Weitzmann organization. The 4-Cube Trio places diminished seventh chords at the center of octatonic/Boretz organization.

# Examples
- Augmented triads: {C, E, G#}, {Db, F, A}, {D, F#, A#}, {Eb, G, B}
- Diminished seventh chords: {C, Eb, Gb, Bbb}, {Db, E, G, Bb}, {D, F, Ab, B}
- Whole-tone scales: {C, D, E, F#, G#, A#}, {Db, Eb, F, G, A, B}
- Tritones: {C, F#}, {Db, G}, {D, Ab}, {Eb, A}, {E, Bb}, {F, B}

# Common Confusions
- "Perfectly even" is a structural description, not a quality judgment
- Perfectly even chords are maximally symmetric, which makes them harmonically ambiguous
- Not all symmetric chords are perfectly even; near-evenness admits slight asymmetry
- The number of perfectly even chord types depends on the chromatic cardinality (12 in standard temperament)
