# Quick Definition
A collection of tones distributed as evenly as possible within the octave without being perfectly even - the defining property of consonant triads (major and minor) and Tristan-genus chords.

# Formal Definition
A nearly even collection distributes its pitch classes as evenly as possible around the chromatic circle without achieving perfect evenness. For three-note chords in 12-tone equal temperament, perfectly even would be 4+4+4 semitones (augmented triad); nearly even is 3+4+5 or 4+3+5 semitones (consonant triads). The concept, developed in work by Clough, Douthett, and others, identifies a structural property that distinguishes consonant triads from other trichords and underlies their unique voice-leading properties.

# Construction/Recognition
For a chord of n notes in a chromatic universe of c pitch classes:
- Perfectly even: all adjacent intervals equal c/n
- Nearly even: intervals are as close to c/n as possible given that c/n is not an integer

For triads (n=3) in chromatic space (c=12):
- Perfectly even: 12/3 = 4, so intervals of 4+4+4 (augmented triad)
- Nearly even: intervals of 3+4+5 or 4+3+5 (consonant triads)

For seventh chords (n=4):
- Perfectly even: 12/4 = 3, so intervals of 3+3+3+3 (diminished seventh)
- Nearly even: intervals close to 3+3+3+3 (Tristan-genus: dominant and half-diminished sevenths)

# Musical Context/Application
Near-evenness explains:
- Why consonant triads are a single semitone from augmented triads
- Why Tristan-genus chords are a single semitone from diminished sevenths
- The voice-leading proximity between nearly even and perfectly even chords
- The central role of augmented triads and diminished sevenths in chromatic voice-leading networks

This property constitutes the "second nature" of consonant triads, independent of their acoustic consonance.

# Examples
- C major {C, E, G}: intervals 4+3+5 = nearly even
- c minor {C, Eb, G}: intervals 3+4+5 = nearly even
- C augmented {C, E, G#}: intervals 4+4+4 = perfectly even
- C dominant 7 {C, E, G, Bb}: nearly even four-note chord
- C diminished 7 {C, Eb, Gb, A}: perfectly even four-note chord

# Common Confusions
- Nearly even is a geometric property, not an acoustic one
- The term applies to pitch-class distribution, not to interval content in the traditional sense
- Near-evenness is what makes consonant triads special for voice leading, independent of their consonance
- Different nearly even chords can have different interval content while sharing the near-evenness property
