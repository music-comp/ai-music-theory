# Quick Definition
Analysis of C.P.E. Bach's f minor Piano Sonata (1763) showing the earliest expansion of the i-III-V# complex by adding a fourth triad (Fb/E major) via the Slide relation, in a passage that perplexed the contemporary critic Forkel.

# Core Definition
In the development of C.P.E. Bach's f minor Sonata (H. 173, Wq. 57/6), "Ab major (III) is connected to C major (V#) through an anomalous Fb major, the tonic's Slide relation" (p. 86). The contemporaneous critic Forkel "found no beauty in this passage" but "defended it on the grounds that it was written by a great composer and ipso facto must contain something of virtue" (Kramer 2008, 11-12; p. 86). "Once enharmonic distinctions are neutralized in chromatic space, the progression is understood in terms of two parallel PL motions that substitute for the single LP of figure 4.8" (p. 86). This 1763 passage is cited as the chronological starting point of the book's survey, with Strauss's "Fruhling" (1949) at the other end.

# Prerequisites
- **i-III-V# complex**: The three-triad core being expanded
- **S transformation**: The Fb/E major represents the tonic's S relation

# Key Properties
1. Expands the i-III-V# complex by adding a fourth triad (Fb/E major)
2. Fb major is "diatonically indeterminate" -- understood as Fb major only in retrospect, E major only in prospect
3. Two parallel PL motions substitute for a single LP
4. Forkel's perplexity demonstrates the passage's novelty in 1763
5. Represents one of the earliest documented S-related juxtapositions

# Context & Application
This passage anchors the "near end" of the long 19th century that Cohn surveys. It shows how the expansion from three-triad to four-triad complexes began in late 18th-century practice, presaging the double-agent complex that Chopin, Wagner, and Liszt would fully exploit.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Dismissing the Fb major as an error or anomaly
  **Correction**: It represents a deliberate compositional choice expanding the i-III-V# complex, as Forkel intuited

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The passage uses the S transformation consciously
  **Clarification**: C.P.E. Bach did not have the transformational concept; the analysis is retrospective
