# Quick Definition
Twelve equivalence classes of trichords that share a center of balance, allowing voice-leading distance to be computed as subtraction modulo 12, with zone labels corresponding to pitch-class sums.

# Formal Definition
Voice-leading zones are equivalence classes of trichords with the capacity to share a center of balance. Consonant triads share a zone if they are transpositionally related by major third (T4). The twenty-four consonant triads partition into eight zones; the four augmented triads occupy the remaining four zones, for twelve zones total. On Cube Dance, zone-equivalent triads occupy the same radius.

# Construction/Recognition
Zone structure:
- 8 zones for consonant triads (3 triads per zone, T4-related)
- 4 zones for augmented triads (1 triad per zone)
- 12 zones total, labeled 0-11 (like a clock face)

Zone calculation:
- Zone number = sum of pitch classes, modulo 12
- Example: d minor {2, 5, 9} -> 2+5+9 = 16 = 4 (mod 12) -> Zone 4
- Example: G major {7, 11, 2} -> 7+11+2 = 20 = 8 (mod 12) -> Zone 8

Voice-leading distance:
- Distance = |zone1 - zone2| (mod 12)
- d minor (zone 4) to G major (zone 8): |8-4| = 4 units

# Musical Context/Application
Zone equivalence underlies transformational substitution: T4-related triads can substitute for each other in fulfilling voice-leading trajectories without changing the overall motion. The zone system enables tracking voice-leading paths at an abstract level, independent of specific triadic choices.

# Common Confusions
- Zone number is pitch-class sum, not root pitch class
- The hexatonic pole is the only exception to distance = zone difference rule (due to contrary motion)
- Augmented triads occupy zones 0, 3, 6, 9 (multiples of 3)
