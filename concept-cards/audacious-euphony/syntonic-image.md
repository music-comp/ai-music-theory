# Quick Definition
Two tones that differ by a syntonic comma in just intonation, occupying the remote corners of a diatonic parallelogram on the Tonnetz, whose identification as a single pitch class creates the first stage of systemic closure in diatonic space.

# Formal Definition
A syntonic image is the relation between two tones separated by a syntonic comma (81:80, slightly larger than one-fifth of an equally tempered semitone). One tone is typically two 3:2 perfect fifths (minus an octave) above the tonic (3/2 x 3/2 x 1/2 = 9/8), while the other is a 4:3 perfect fourth minus a 6:5 minor third above the tonic (4/3 / 6/5 = 10/9). On the diatonic Tonnetz parallelogram, syntonic images occupy the remote corners. Accepting their identity creates the first stage of systemic closure, allowing the diatonic collection to form a closed figure rather than an infinitely extending lattice.

# Construction/Recognition
- Syntonic comma: 81:80 ratio (approximately 21.5 cents)
- Example in C major: The D derived from two ascending fifths (C-G-D) versus the D derived as the Pythagorean ditonic comma adjustment
- Location: Remote corners of the diatonic parallelogram
- Effect of identification: Creates bounded diatonic space; enables T-S-D-T closure
- On the Mobius strip: The syntonic vault is negotiated around the backside of the strip

When the acoustic identity of the two tones at syntonic-image positions is recognized, "it is a small step to honor their unification in conception, leading to a more conjunct relation between subdominant and dominant."

# Musical Context
Syntonic images play structural roles in:
- Defining the boundaries of diatonic regions on the Tonnetz
- Establishing the relationship between subdominant and dominant (sharing D in C major)
- Creating the potential for Mobius strip topology when the pseudo-fifth (B-F) is also accepted
- Marking modulation: Each shift of the diatonic capsule creates a new pair of syntonic images

Riemann imagined the subdominant as a "stretched bow which slings the arrow beyond the mark" - the slung arrow may be interpreted as the vault across the syntonic comma, executed in the imagination rather than in acoustic signal.

# Examples
- In C major: The D between F major and G major (subdominant and dominant) represents syntonic images at the parallelogram's corners
- In Liszt Consolation no. 3: The initial tonicization of Db major has syntonic images at Eb; modulation to f minor shifts them to Bb
- Each modulation by fifth creates new syntonic images: moving from C major toward G major shifts the images rightward on the Tonnetz

# Common Confusions
- Syntonic images are not equal temperament; they represent a conceptual identification of tones that differ in just intonation
- The identification is cognitive/theoretical, not necessarily acoustic; it could apply even in just intonation contexts through performer adjustment
- Syntonic images are not the same as enharmonic equivalents (Db/C#); they are diatonic, not chromatic, identifications
- Each diatonic region has its own pair of syntonic images; they shift with modulation
