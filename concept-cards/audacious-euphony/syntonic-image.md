# Quick Definition
Two tones separated by a syntonic comma (81:80) in just intonation, occupying the remote corners of a diatonic parallelogram on the Tonnetz, whose identification as a single pitch class creates the first stage of systemic closure in diatonic space.

# Core Definition
A syntonic image is the relation between two tones separated by a syntonic comma in just intonation. One tone is typically two 3:2 perfect fifths (minus an octave) above the tonic (9/8), and the other is a 4:3 perfect fourth minus a 6:5 minor third above the tonic (10/9). The difference is 9/8 / 10/9 = 81/80, "slightly larger than one-fifth of an equally tempered semitone" (Glossary, p. 229). Cohn introduces the term for "the relation between the circled tones occupying [the parallelogram's] remote corners" (p. 194). Accepting their identity "creates a first stage of systemic closure, completing the portrait of the diatonic collection as a parallelogram" (p. 194).

# Prerequisites
- **Diatonic Tonnetz**: Must understand the parallelogram within which syntonic images arise
- **Consonant triad**: The building blocks that generate the parallelogram through fifth transposition

# Key Properties
1. The syntonic comma is approximately 21.5 cents (81:80 ratio)
2. Syntonic images occupy remote corners of the diatonic parallelogram
3. Their identification is the first stage of closure (before the pseudo-fifth)
4. Each diatonic region has its own pair of syntonic images
5. Images shift with modulation: each horizontal shift creates a new pair

# Construction / Recognition
In C major: The D derived from two ascending fifths (C-G-D = 9/8) differs from the D derived via F-A-D (10/9) by a syntonic comma. These tones occupy the remote corners of the diatonic parallelogram.

Riemann imagined the subdominant as a "stretched bow which slings the arrow beyond the mark" (Riemann 1893, qtd. in Rings 2007, 50); the slung arrow represents the vault across the syntonic comma, "executed not in the acoustic signal but rather in the imagination" since Riemann took equal temperament as an empirical given (p. 195).

Once the acoustic identity of the two tones is accepted, their "unification in conception" leads to a "more conjunct relation between subdominant and dominant" (p. 195).

# Context & Application
Syntonic images play critical structural roles:
- Defining boundaries of diatonic regions on the Tonnetz
- Establishing the relationship between subdominant and dominant
- Marking modulation: each shift of the diatonic capsule creates new syntonic images
- Enabling the Mobius strip topology when combined with pseudo-fifth acceptance

In analysis, syntonic images appear as forces that halt chromatic drift and establish diatonic walls (e.g., in the Parsifal Faith Proclamation, where eb minor is "flanked by its syntonic images," p. 210).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating syntonic images as equal-temperament phenomena
  **Correction**: They represent a conceptual identification of tones that differ in just intonation

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Syntonic images are the same as enharmonic equivalents (Db/C#)
  **Clarification**: Syntonic images are diatonic identifications (two versions of the same letter name); enharmonic equivalents are chromatic identifications (different letter names)
