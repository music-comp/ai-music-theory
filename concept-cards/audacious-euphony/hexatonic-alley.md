# Quick Definition
A bounded path through pan-triadic space connecting triads in a hexatonic cycle via L and P transformations, functioning as a corridor through which voice-leading efficient motion occurs while diatonic syntax is temporarily suspended.

# Formal Definition
The hexatonic alley is the L/P chain connecting the six triads of a hexatonic region. On the convertible Tonnetz, it represents a bounded corridor for chromatic motion, distinct from the unbounded L/R chain that extends horizontally. Within the hexatonic alley, voice-leading work is minimal (single semitonal displacement between adjacent triads), and the balanced voice leading temporarily suspends the directional upshift or downshift that characterizes motion toward or away from tonic. The alley provides a controlled path through chromatic space that can intersect with diatonic encapsulations at any of its stations.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Structure:
- Six triads connected by alternating L and P transformations
- Path: C - c - Ab - ab - E - e - C (example)
- Voice-leading work: 1 semitone between adjacent triads
- Balanced voice leading: Neither net upshift nor downshift across the hexatonic pole

Relation to convertible Tonnetz:
- The alley "sprouts" from a diatonic station
- Triads bob as "sealed capsules" on the pan-triadic sea
- Each station can be locally tonicized, creating temporary diatonic encapsulation
- The alley can be traversed partially or completely

# Musical Context/Application
In the Liszt Consolation analysis (Figure 8.9):
- The hexatonic alley sprouts from f minor in the second sentence
- Progressive ascent: f minor - F major - a minor - A major
- Each station is locally tonicized while participating in voice-leading journey
- The wave of voice-leading efficiency "crests at the hexatonic alley"

In the Schubert C major Symphony analysis (Figure 9.2):
- The hexatonic alley at the interior (mm. 123-40) represents "transient emergence into the hexatonic light"
- Voice-leading perspective inverts monotonal perspective: "What we initially conceived as tunneling into diatonic/tonal darkness becomes... a transient emergence into the hexatonic light"

# Examples
- Liszt Consolation no. 3 (mm. 23-40): Hexatonic ascent through f minor - F major - a minor - A major
- Schubert C major Symphony Scherzo (mm. 117-140): Central hexatonic passage where voice-leading efficiency governs
- Brahms First Symphony finale (mm. 204-220): Eb major - B major as hexatonic stations with liquidations filling the alleys
- Wagner Parsifal: Hexatonic passages within larger plagal drift

# Common Confusions
- The hexatonic alley is not a permanent feature; it "sprouts" and retracts depending on musical context
- Motion through the alley can be partial; not all six stations need be visited
- Local tonicizations do not contradict chromatic voice-leading; both operate simultaneously
- The alley is bounded (6 triads) unlike the L/R chain (24 triads)
