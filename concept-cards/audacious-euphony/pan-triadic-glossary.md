# Quick Definition
Any composition, or segment thereof, that consists exclusively or predominantly of major or minor triads without determining a tonal center.

# Formal Definition
Pan-triadic is defined in the Glossary as "any composition, or segment thereof, that consists exclusively or predominately of major or minor triads without determining a tonal center." The term captures the phenomenon at the heart of Audacious Euphony: chromatic progressions using familiar consonant triads that resist functional-tonal analysis. Pan-triadic syntax operates alongside (and sometimes instead of) diatonic syntax, organizing triads by voice-leading parsimony rather than root relationships to a tonic.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Characteristics of pan-triadic passages:
- Harmonic vocabulary: Major and minor triads (24 total)
- Organizational principle: Voice-leading efficiency, not tonal function
- Tonal center: Absent, weakly present, or multiply determined
- Progression logic: Single semitonal displacement, common-tone retention

Pan-triadic vs. diatonic syntax:
- Diatonic: Triads oriented toward tonic through T-S-D-T relations
- Pan-triadic: Triads related through voice-leading parsimony and transformational chains
- The double syntax hypothesis: Both can coexist in the same composition

# Musical Context/Application
Pan-triadic describes the repertoire and analytical approach central to Audacious Euphony:
- 19th-century chromatic practice (Schubert, Liszt, Wagner, Brahms)
- Passages that "cruise from triad to triad through pan-triadic space"
- Sequences and chains organized by transformational logic
- Music that sounds "tonal" (uses triads) without being "tonally functional"

The term avoids the negative connotations of "atonal" while capturing the suspension of tonic orientation.

# Examples
- Schubert C major Symphony Scherzo mm. 117-140: Hexatonic passage without clear tonal center
- Wagner Parsifal Faith Proclamation: Plagal drift through L/R chain
- Liszt Consolation no. 3 second sentence: Hexatonic journey through locally tonicized stations
- Any passage traversing hexatonic cycles, Weitzmann regions, or pitch retention loops

# Common Confusions
- Pan-triadic is not atonal; it uses consonant triads throughout
- Pan-triadic passages may include local tonicizations without establishing global tonic
- The term describes syntax, not vocabulary; the same triads appear in diatonic contexts
- Pan-triadic and diatonic are not mutually exclusive; double syntax allows both
