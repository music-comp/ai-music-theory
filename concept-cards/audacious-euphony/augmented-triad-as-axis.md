# Quick Definition
In pan-triadic harmony, augmented triads function as invisible central axes around which consonant triads spin, generating smooth voice-leading progressions by virtue of their status as perfectly even trisections of the octave.

# Formal Definition
Cohn's central theoretical claim is that "the capacity for minimal voice leading between chords of a single type is a special property of consonant triads, resulting from their status as minimal perturbations of perfectly even augmented triads" (p. 36). Each hexatonic system has an augmented triad at its center, and all six consonant triads in that system are single-semitone displacements of that augmented triad. The augmented triad thus "generates" the hexatonic system.

# Construction/Recognition
Geometric representation (Figures 2.15-2.17):
- A circle with vertices of an equilateral triangle represents the augmented triad
- Each vertex is flanked by two positions (one semitone up, one down) representing consonant triads
- Major triads result from downward displacement; minor from upward
- The transformation between consonant triads can be modeled as reflection about an axis that holds two vertices invariant

The two augmented triads bounding a Tonnetz strip (p. 51-52):
"When triadic progressions are pursuing the logic of smooth voice leading rather than that of acoustic consonance, augmented triads play a central role in their syntax, even when occluded from the music's surface and hence not directly accessible to perception."

# Musical Context/Application
The augmented triad's role is analogous to that of the harmonic series in diatonic syntax: "the concealed noumenon that gives rise to the revealed phenomenon" (p. 56). Even when no augmented triad appears in a passage, the smooth voice leading is governed by the geometry centered on the augmented triad.

# Examples
From the text (p. 51-52):
- Tonnetz strips show augmented triads at their external boundaries
- "In none of the passages does an augmented triad appear as a surface harmony"
- Yet the augmented triad structures the voice-leading possibilities

The text questions traditional analysis: "Th e notion that augmented triads are 'prolonged' is particularly problematic in the current context, since it suggests that the smooth voice leading of these passages is a by-product. If we consider semitonal voice leading as primary, then such a conception places the tail where the head should be" (p. 52).

# Common Confusions
- The augmented triad need not sound as a surface harmony to play its structuring role
- "Axis" is metaphorical - the augmented triad provides the geometric center, not necessarily a musical axis (like a tonal axis)
- There are only FOUR distinct augmented triads (one per hexatonic system), not twelve
- The augmented triad is not being "prolonged" in the Schenkerian sense; it is the structural generator of the system
