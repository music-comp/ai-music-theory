# Quick Definition
In pan-triadic harmony, augmented triads function as invisible central axes around which consonant triads spin, generating smooth voice-leading progressions by virtue of their status as perfectly even trisections of the octave, even when absent from the musical surface.

# Core Definition
"When triadic progressions are pursuing the logic of smooth voice leading rather than that of acoustic consonance, augmented triads play a central role in their syntax, even when occluded from the music's surface and hence not directly accessible to perception. By virtue of their status as *perfectly even* trisections of the octave, augmented triads are the invisible axes about which pan-triadic progressions spin. Consonant triads acquire their distinctive voice-leading features in chromatic space by virtue of their status as *minimal perturbations* of the perfectly even augmented triads" (Cohn, p. 52). Cohn argues that augmented triads "*generate* pan-triadic syntax by way of nearly even trichords, just as many theorists have found value in the claim that the harmonic series generates diatonic syntax by way of consonant triads" (p. 55).

# Prerequisites
- **near-evenness** — Consonant triads are nearly even perturbations of augmented triads
- **perfectly-even-chord** — The augmented triad is the perfectly even trichord

# Key Properties
1. Only 4 distinct augmented triads exist (one per hexatonic system)
2. Each generates 6 consonant triads (3 major + 3 minor) by semitonal displacement
3. Need not appear on the musical surface to structure the voice-leading space
4. Both augmented triads bounding a Tonnetz strip play equal structural roles
5. The role is analogous to that of the harmonic series in diatonic syntax: "the concealed noumenon that gives rise to the revealed phenomenon" (p. 55)

# Construction / Recognition
From Figure 2.15 (p. 53):
1. Take augmented triad C-E-G#
2. Each vertex can be displaced up or down by semitone
3. Downward displacement: C-E-G (C major), C-Eb-G# (c minor respelled), etc.
4. Upward displacement: C#-E-G# (c# minor), C-E-A (A minor), etc.
5. Result: 6 consonant triads per augmented triad, forming one hexatonic system

# Context & Application
Cohn explicitly rejects the notion that augmented triads are "prolonged" in the Schenkerian sense: "The notion that augmented triads are 'prolonged' is particularly problematic in the current context, since it suggests that the smooth voice leading of these passages is a by-product. If we consider semitonal voice leading as primary, then such a conception places the tail where the head should be" (p. 52).

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 51-52): In the hexatonic passages analyzed (Mozart, Haydn, Beethoven, Schubert), "In none of the passages does an augmented triad appear as a surface harmony." Yet the augmented triads bound the Tonnetz strips and structure the voice-leading possibilities.

**Example 2** (p. 52): "Even if we are comfortable with assigning fundamental status to the augmented triad at the left boundary of each strip, what is the role of its partner at its right boundary?" — Both play equal generative roles.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Looking for augmented triads on the musical surface to confirm their structural role
  **Correction**: Their role is generative, not presentational; they structure the space even when absent

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the augmented triad is "prolonged" in the Schenkerian sense
  **Clarification**: Cohn explicitly rejects this; the augmented triad is the structural generator, not a prolonged surface harmony
