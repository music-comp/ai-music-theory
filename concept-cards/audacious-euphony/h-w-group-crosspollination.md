# Quick Definition
The interaction ("crosspollination") of H-group transformations (which preserve hexatonic region) with W-group transformations (which preserve Weitzmann region) generates all odd-transposition chromatic sequences, with the historically earliest sequences arising from the four normative combinations.

# Formal Definition
H-group (hexatonic-group) transformations (**L**, **P**, **H**) preserve hexatonic region but shift Weitzmann region. W-group (Weitzmann-group) transformations (**R**, **N**, **S**) preserve Weitzmann region and shift hexatonic region. An odd-valued transpositional sequence is generated by alternating two transformations, one from each group. Two transformations from each group are classically normative: **L** and **P** from the H-group, **R** and **N** from the W-group. The 2 x 2 = 4 normative crosspollinations produce the earliest chromatic sequences (eighteenth century). The remaining five combinations involve the maverick **S** or **H** and appear in later music.

# Construction/Recognition
Normative combinations (earliest):
- **L**/**R**: T5 (perfect fifth)
- **L**/**N**: T1 (semitone)
- **P**/**R**: T3 (minor third)
- **P**/**N**: T5 (perfect fifth)

Later combinations involving mavericks:
- **S**/**L**: T3 (Liszt, Schubert)
- **N**/**H**: T3 (Schubert)
- **H**/**R**: T1 (less documented)
- **S**/**P**: T1 (Liszt *Il Penseroso*)
- **H**/**S**: T5 (undocumented)

# Musical Context
The distinction between normative and maverick combinations correlates with historical chronology: the four normative crosspollinations dominate eighteenth-century practice, while maverick combinations emerge in Schubert and Liszt. The analogy of the hexatonic and Weitzmann regions as "right and left leg hopping in place" captures how their individual closure gives way to "perambulation" when combined.

# Examples
- Eighteenth-century hexachord fantasias: **L**/**R** chains (T5 upshift)
- Schubert *Die Zauberharfe*: **P**/**R** chain (T3)
- Schubert *Drei Klavierstucke*: **N**/**H** chain (T3)
- Liszt *Lelio Fantasy*: **S**/**L** chain (T3) and **N**/**L** chain (T1)

# Common Confusions
- "Crosspollination" means combining one H-group with one W-group transformation, not mixing within a single group
- Combining two H-group transformations preserves hexatonic region (no interregional motion)
- Combining two W-group transformations preserves Weitzmann region (no interregional motion)
- Only the H/W cross-group combination produces interregional chromatic sequences
