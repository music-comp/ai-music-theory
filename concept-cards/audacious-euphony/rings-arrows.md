# Quick Definition
Directional arrows in Steven Rings's model representing the syntactic flow and semantic charge of tonal harmony - the gravity, magnetism, and attraction associated with dominant-to-tonic and subdominant-to-tonic motion in classical tonality.

# Formal Definition
Rings arrows, adapted from David Lewin's DOM and SUBD transformations, accompany the vertical (tonal-functional) axis of Rings's 2007 model. DOM (equivalent to T5) indicates the source chord "becomes the dominant of" the target; SUBD (T7) indicates "becomes the subdominant of." The arrows are single-headed because these transformations work in a single direction - they are not their own inverses. Rings emphasizes that these transformations capture not only syntactic flow but also the "semantic charge" of classical tonality, roughly standing for "summoning, leading, gravity, magnetism, attraction, desire."

# Construction/Recognition
- DOM: T5 transposition (root motion down by fifth or up by fourth)
- SUBD: T7 = T-5 transposition (root motion up by fifth)
- Single-headed arrows: Not involutions like L, P, R
- Semantic content: Captures tonal gravitation, leading-tone discharge, cadential forces
- Default activation: Present in diatonic encapsulation; deactivated in sequential or chromatic contexts

Rings writes that his dimensions "mark out a unified space in which we can map progressions that exploit both the triad's tonal-gravitational properties and its triadic-transformational potential, without privileging one at the expense of the other" (2007, 52).

# Musical Context/Application
Rings arrows activate when:
- Music operates within an encapsulated diatonic space
- Cadential progressions confirm tonal centers
- Dominant and subdominant functions are semantically charged

Rings arrows deactivate when:
- Sequential motion neutralizes appellative powers (as Fetis observed)
- L/R chains traverse chromatic space without cadential confirmation
- Voice-leading parsimony governs rather than root-functional logic

The convertible Tonnetz shows arrows retracting "into the body of the moving vehicle" during chromatic sequences and re-emerging when approaching diatonic stations.

# Examples
- Liszt Consolation no. 3, basic idea (mm. 3-6): T-S-D-T paradigm with Rings arrows indicating syntactic flow
- The subdominant-to-dominant motion involves an "overshooting arrow" that vaults across the syntonic comma on the planar Tonnetz
- On the Mobius strip interpretation, this motion continues uniformly around the backside
- During hexatonic journeys (mm. 23-40), arrows temporarily suspend as voice-leading parsimony governs
- Cadential confirmation at each hexatonic station temporarily reactivates arrows within local diatonic capsules

# Common Confusions
- Rings arrows are not inherent properties of fifth-related motion; they are contingent on diatonic encapsulation and can be deactivated
- The arrows represent semantic content (gravitation, desire), not merely syntactic position
- According to Lewin's definition, G major is not the dominant of C minor (different mode), even though musicians commonly speak that way
- The single-headed nature distinguishes these from involutory Neo-Riemannian transformations
