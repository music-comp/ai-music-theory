# Quick Definition
A perfectly even four-note chord dividing the octave into four equal minor thirds, serving as the structural center of Boretz regions in the tetrachordal voice-leading system, analogous to augmented triads in Weitzmann regions.

# Core Definition
The **diminished seventh chord** is a four-note chord consisting of stacked minor thirds, dividing the twelve-semitone octave into four equal parts. Only three distinct diminished seventh chords exist: {C, Eb, F#, A}, {C#, E, G, Bb}, {D, F, Ab, B}. In Cohn's framework, Boretz "bestows primacy on the diminished seventh by virtue of its status as an equal division of the octave" (p. 168). Each diminished seventh serves as the core of a Boretz region, generating eight Tristan-genus chords through single semitonal displacement. The diminished seventh's role for tetrachords is exactly analogous to the augmented triad's role for triads: it is the perfectly even chord whose perturbations create the nearly even chords that populate the voice-leading system.

# Prerequisites
- **Perfectly even chord**: The diminished seventh is the specific perfectly even chord for cardinality 4

# Key Properties
1. Only three distinct diminished seventh chords exist (due to transpositional symmetry at T3, T6, T9)
2. Each generates a Boretz region of eight nearly even tetrachords (4 V7 + 4 ø7)
3. The diminished seventh may not appear in the music yet still structure the region through "virtual presence"
4. In *Tristan*, "the virtual presence of the diminished seventh chord is corroborated by Wagner's initial sketch of the Prelude" (p. 170 n. 11)
5. Occupies even-numbered voice-leading zones (at 2:00, 6:00, and 10:00 on 4-Cube Trio)

# Construction / Recognition
- Pitch-class set: [0, 3, 6, 9] or transpositions
- Interval content: 3+3+3+3 semitones
- Three distinct forms: T0 = {C,Eb,F#,A}, T1 = {C#,E,G,Bb}, T2 = {D,F,Ab,B}
- Each is transpositionally equivalent to itself at T3, T6, T9

Relation to Tristan-genus chords:
- Each Tristan-genus chord is a single semitonal displacement of exactly one diminished seventh
- Downward displacement yields V7; upward yields ø7

# Context & Application
In the Tristan-genus system, diminished seventh chords function as structural pivots even when absent from the musical surface. They partition the twenty-four Tristan-genus chords into three Boretz regions (Table 7.2). When diminished seventh chords do appear in passages otherwise dominated by Tristan-genus chords, they engage the even-numbered voice-leading zones (as in Chopin's Mazurka Op. 68 no. 4, mm. 37-39).

# Examples
- **Table 7.2**: The three Boretz regions organized by their central diminished seventh chords (p. 170)
- **Tristan Prelude**: Chords 1-5 are displacements of B-D-F-G# (region III); this chord never appears on the surface but structures the passage (p. 167)
- **Chopin Mazurka Op. 68 no. 4**: Interpolated diminished seventh chords at mm. 37-39 bridge gaps in the cumulative voice-leading descent (p. 179)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating the diminished seventh as a functional dominant in Tristan-genus analysis
  **Correction**: In this framework, diminished sevenths are structural centers of voice-leading regions, not functional harmonies

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking there are twelve diminished seventh chords
  **Clarification**: Due to their perfect symmetry, only three distinct diminished seventh chords exist; enharmonic spellings do not create new ones
