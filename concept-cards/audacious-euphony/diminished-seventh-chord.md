# Quick Definition
A perfectly even four-note chord that divides the octave into four equal parts (minor thirds), serving as a structural center in Boretz regions analogous to augmented triads in Weitzmann regions.

# Formal Definition
A diminished seventh chord is a four-note chord consisting of stacked minor thirds, dividing the 12-semitone octave into four equal parts of 3 semitones each. Because 12 divides evenly by 4, there are only 3 distinct diminished seventh chords (under enharmonic equivalence): {C, Eb, F#, A}, {Db, E, G, Bb}, {D, F, Ab, B}. Like augmented triads among three-note chords, diminished seventh chords are "perfectly even" four-note chords that serve as structural centers for voice-leading networks.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Structure: 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 semitones (four minor thirds)
- Pitch-class set: [0, 3, 6, 9] or transpositions
- Only 3 distinct forms exist (T0, T1, T2)
- Each is transpositionally equivalent to itself at T3, T6, and T9

Relation to Tristan-genus chords:
- Each Tristan-genus chord (half-diminished or dominant seventh) is a single semitonal displacement from exactly one diminished seventh
- Each diminished seventh generates a Boretz region of 8 Tristan-genus chords
- The diminished seventh plays the same structural role for seventh chords that augmented triads play for triads

# Musical Context/Application
Diminished seventh chords:
- Function as structural pivots in seventh-chord voice-leading space
- Enable analysis of chromatic seventh-chord passages
- Connect to the Boretz region framework
- Appear in the 4-Cube Trio graph

In pan-triadic theory, diminished sevenths are the perfectly even "centers" around which nearly even seventh chords orbit, paralleling the role of augmented triads for consonant triads.

# Examples
- Each diminished seventh chord is surrounded by 8 Tristan-genus chords (4 half-diminished, 4 dominant)
- The 3 diminished seventh chords partition the Tristan genus into 3 Boretz regions
- Chromatic sequences moving through seventh chords can be analyzed via diminished seventh pivots

# Common Confusions
- There are only 3 distinct diminished seventh chords (not 12) due to their symmetry
- In pan-triadic theory, diminished sevenths are structural centers, not functional dominants
- The parallel with augmented triads is exact: both are perfectly even chords generating regions of nearly even chords
- Enharmonic spellings do not create additional diminished sevenths
