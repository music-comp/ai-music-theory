# Quick Definition
Analysis of Chopin's Nocturne Op. 9 No. 1 in bb minor (1830), mm. 1-8, showing a double-agent complex that "plunges through" the enharmonic seam with direct Bbb-to-A transformation in the same voice.

# Core Definition
The opening phrase of the Nocturne presents the canonical double-agent complex: bb minor (tonic) -> Db major (relative, m. 5) -> gb minor (minor subdominant of Db, three times) -> F major (dominant, via S transformation). The S transformation "transforms Bbb3 directly into A3 in the same voice; the two notated pitches are metrically accented and analogously positioned in the arpeggiated figuration" (pp. 87-88). "What is entered as the third of a minor triad, pressing downward by semitone, exits as the third of a major triad, pressing upward by semitone" (p. 88). This contrasts with the contemporaneous Mazurka Op. 6 No. 1, which "teases" the seam by substituting F# for F to avoid direct enharmonic contact.

# Prerequisites
- **Double-agent complex**: The four-triad structure present in the passage
- **Double-agent pitch**: Bbb/A is the double agent
- **S transformation**: gb minor to F major is an S transformation
- **Enharmonic seam**: The passage "plunges through" rather than "teasing" it

# Key Properties
1. Canonical double-agent ordering: i -> III -> iv of III -> V (via S)
2. Direct S transformation: gb minor -> F major
3. Double agent: Bbb (third of gb minor, pressing down) becomes A (third of F major, pressing up)
4. The two pitches are "metrically accented and analogously positioned in the arpeggiated figuration"
5. Cohn: "I find that I need to sing the pitch continuously in order to convince myself that the two pitches are identified; and even then, it feels like a trick" (p. 89)
6. Contrasts with Mazurka Op. 6 No. 1, which avoids the direct enharmonic contact

# Construction / Recognition
Progression: bb minor -> Db major (R) -> gb minor -> [gb minor repeated] -> F major (S)
- The R-related interior pair: bb minor and Db major
- The N-related connections: F major (N of bb minor); gb minor (N of Db major, respelled as f# minor)
- The S connection: gb minor to F major crosses the seam

# Context & Application
This passage is the canonical example of the double-agent complex "plunging through" the enharmonic seam, and is referenced throughout Chapter 4 as a model for understanding the structure.
