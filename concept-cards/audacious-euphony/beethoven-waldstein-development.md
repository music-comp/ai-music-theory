# Quick Definition
The development of Beethoven's "Waldstein" Sonata (Op. 53) executes a broader upshift arc than the "Tempest," beginning with an F major theme that descends to D7, then accelerating through a T5 chain before stalling in a Weitzmann region via an **N**/**R** chain to reach the retransitional dominant.

# Formal Definition
The development proceeds through several phases with varying upshift speeds:
1. Principal theme in F major (m. 90) begins a downshift culminating at D7 (dominant of g minor)
2. A T5 chain from m. 96 at four-measure pace
3. Eightfold acceleration at m. 104, prolonging bb minor and Gb major
4. Brief downshift
5. Continuation of the sequence at four-measure pace
6. Beginning at m. 120, the upshift stalls in a Weitzmann region, connecting Bb major to b minor through an **N**/**R** chain
7. Retransitional dominant reached at m. 130

# Musical Context
The "Waldstein" development demonstrates a broader, more gradually paced upshift compared to the "Tempest"'s compressed orbit. The varying pace of upshift (four-measure, then accelerated, then stalled) creates a dramatic arc within the development.

# Common Confusions
- The development is not a single continuous upshift; it has phases of varying speed and direction
- The **N**/**R** chain represents a "stall" within a Weitzmann region, not a continuation of the upshift
- The varying pace (four-measure, eightfold acceleration, stall) is a key compositional feature
