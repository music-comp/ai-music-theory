# Quick Definition
The "Waldstein" Sonata (Op. 53) development executes an upshift across a broader span than the "Tempest," with varying pacing: a T5 chain at four-measure pace, eightfold acceleration, and a Weitzmann-region stall via N/R chain before reaching the retransitional dominant.

# Core Definition
The development (figure 6.16b) proceeds in phases: F major theme (m. 90) begins a downshift to D7 (dominant of g minor). A T5 chain from m. 96 at four-measure pace accelerates eightfold at m. 104, prolonging bb minor and Gb major. After a brief downshift, the sequence continues at four-measure pace. "Beginning at m. 120, the upshift stalls in a Weitzmann region, connecting Bb major -> b minor through an N/R chain and reaching the retransitional dominant at m. 130" (p. 153). The Tonnetz interpretation is based in part on Lubin 1974.

# Key Properties
1. Multi-phase development with varying pacing
2. T5 chain (L/R) dominates the central upshift
3. Eightfold acceleration at m. 104
4. N/R chain stall in Weitzmann region (mm. 120-130)
5. Broader span and more gradual pacing than "Tempest"

# Construction / Recognition
Figure 6.16(b): F major -> downshift to D7 -> T5 chain (4-bar pace) -> acceleration (m. 104) -> brief downshift -> resumption -> N/R stall (m. 120) -> retransitional dominant (m. 130)

# Context & Application
Paired with the "Tempest" analysis, this demonstrates that "extended upshifts are ingredients of the adrenal concoction with which Beethoven so often injected his developments" (p. 153).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: The development is a single continuous upshift
  **Correction**: It has phases of varying speed and includes a Weitzmann-region stall

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The N/R chain continues the upshift
  **Clarification**: It represents a stall within a single Weitzmann region, not upshift continuation
