# Quick Definition
A three-triad core progression (minor tonic, relative major, major dominant) that represents the Classical syntactic origins of Weitzmann regions.

# Formal Definition
The i-III-V# complex consists of minor tonic, its relative major (III), and its major dominant (V#). In transformational terms, N is executed by combining R with LP: the relative major divides the motion from minor tonic to major dominant. This three-chord complex forms the kernel from which the four-chord double-agent complex and eventually the six-chord Weitzmann region expand.

# Construction/Recognition
Example in c minor:
- c minor (i) -> Eb major (III) -> G major (V#)
- Transformational path: R from c minor to Eb major, LP from Eb major to G major
- Combined: N = R + LP (or the order can be exchanged)

The three chords form an unordered space by the late 18th century, as C.P.E. Bach noted that direct motion from C major to E major is possible for "anyone who knows that E is the dominant of a [minor] and that a minor is very closely related to C major."

# Musical Context/Application
This complex appears frequently in Classical practice, often at the middleground level (structural pillars) rather than as direct successions. The relative major divides the motion from minor tonic to major dominant in typical cases. Alternatively, the order can be exchanged: minor tonic to major dominant via N, then disjunction to relative major at faster tempo.

# Common Confusions
- The complex is "unordered" meaning the three triads can appear in various sequences
- This is not a complete Weitzmann region, just its Classical syntactic core
- The V# notation indicates major dominant (raised third) in minor key context
