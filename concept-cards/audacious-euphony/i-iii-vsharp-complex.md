# Quick Definition
A three-triad core progression (minor tonic, relative major, major dominant) representing the Classical syntactic origins of Weitzmann regions, expressible as N = R + LP.

# Core Definition
"The most typical case [involves] relative major divid[ing] the motion from minor tonic to major dominant" (p. 85). In transformational terms, "N is executed by combining R with LP" (p. 85). The three chords form "an unordered space during the later part of the eighteenth century," as C.P.E. Bach asserted that direct motion from C major to E major was possible for "anyone who knows that E is the dominant of a and that a minor is very closely related to C major" (Kramer 1985, 552; p. 86). This three-chord complex forms the kernel from which the double-agent complex and eventually the complete Weitzmann region expand.

# Prerequisites
- **Weitzmann region**: The complex is a three-triad subset of the six-triad region
- **N and R transformations**: The complex combines these transformations

# Key Properties
1. Three triads: minor tonic (i), relative major (III), major dominant (V#)
2. Transformational decomposition: N = R + LP
3. The three chords form an unordered space (any sequence is possible)
4. The complex is a contiguous segment of a Weitzmann region
5. Classical origin: middleground progressions, not necessarily direct successions

# Construction / Recognition
Example in c minor: c minor (i) -> Eb major (III) -> G major (V#)
- c minor to Eb major: R transformation
- Eb major to G major: LP transformation
- c minor to G major directly: N transformation

# Context & Application
The i-III-V# complex provides the historical foundation for understanding how Weitzmann regions emerged from Classical practice. It expands to the four-triad double-agent complex and eventually to the complete six-triad N/R cycle.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating the complex as requiring a specific ordering
  **Correction**: "The three chords form an unordered space" -- any sequence is possible

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: V# means an augmented triad
  **Clarification**: V# indicates the major dominant (raised third) in a minor key context, not an augmented triad
