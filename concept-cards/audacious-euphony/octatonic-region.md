# Quick Definition
An octatonic region is a bridging structure between adjacent Boretz regions, containing eight Tristan-genus chords (four half-diminished and four dominant sevenths) that share tones from an octatonic scale, analogous to hexatonic regions bridging Weitzmann regions.

# Formal Definition
**Octatonic regions** bridge adjacent Boretz regions in the same way that hexatonic regions bridge adjacent Weitzmann regions. An octatonic region combines:
- The four half-diminished seventh chords of one Boretz region (upshifters)
- The four same-rooted dominant seventh chords from the neighboring Boretz region (downshifters)

The eight chords draw their tones exclusively from one of the three octatonic scales.

# Construction/Recognition
Structure:
- 4 half-diminished chords from Boretz region N (e.g., Bø7, Dø7, Fø7, G#ø7)
- 4 dominant seventh chords from Boretz region N+1 with same roots (B7, D7, F7, G#7)
- All 8 chords draw from same octatonic collection

Relationship to octatonic scale:
- Octatonic scale = 8 notes alternating half-steps and whole-steps
- Three distinct octatonic collections in chromatic space
- Each octatonic region corresponds to one octatonic collection

Voice-leading within octatonic region:
- Transformations involve 2 voices moving by semitone (n-2 where n=4)
- Less parsimonious than hexatonic bridges (which involve only 1 moving voice)

# Musical Context/Application
Octatonic regions enable:
- Movement between Boretz regions (modulation in tetrachordal space)
- Directed voice-leading trajectories (upshift or downshift)
- Circumnavigation of 4-Cube Trio
- Analysis of progressions bridging diminished seventh collections

The regions appear at the 12:00, 4:00, and 8:00 positions on 4-Cube Trio, where minor seventh chords and French sixth chords also serve as octatonic bridges.

# Examples
**Tristan Prelude, chords 5-6**: The progression from Aø7 (chord 5) to C#7 (chord 6) bridges two Boretz regions via an octatonic transformation. This marks the first "interregional modulation" in Boretz's analysis.

**Brunnhilde's Immolation**: The progression Eb7 → C#ø7 → C#7 crosses from one Boretz region into another via the octatonic bridge, sustaining the double-semitone voice leading.

# Common Confusions
- Octatonic regions bridge Boretz regions; they don't replace them
- The "octatonic" refers to scale content, not the number of chords (which is eight, coincidentally)
- Octatonic transformations are less parsimonious than hexatonic ones (2 moving voices vs. 1)
- Three octatonic regions exist (corresponding to three octatonic scales)
