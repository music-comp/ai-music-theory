# Quick Definition
In the Tristan-genus system, the central diminished seventh chord of a Boretz region may exert structural influence without ever sounding on the musical surface -- a "virtual presence" analogous to the virtual augmented triads in the triadic system.

# Core Definition
The central diminished seventh chord of a Boretz region may never appear as a surface sonority while still governing the voice-leading behavior of the surrounding Tristan-genus chords. The eight members of a region "form a tight voice-leading system even when, as in the Tristan Prelude, the diminished seventh chord is absent from the surface" (p. 170). This virtual presence parallels the triadic case where augmented triads may structure passages without sounding. "In Tristan, the virtual presence of the diminished seventh chord is corroborated by Wagner's initial sketch of the Prelude" (Bailey 1985, 131), and "diminished seventh chords often replace Tristan-genus chords in contexts where the latter normally appear, as in m. 68 of the Prelude" (Mitchell 1967, 190-91) (p. 170 n. 11).

# Prerequisites
- **Boretz region**: The structure asserting virtual influence
- **Diminished seventh chord**: The chord asserting virtual presence
- **Spoiler tone**: The displacement concept that makes virtual presence analytically detectable

# Key Properties
1. The diminished seventh need not sound for the Boretz region to be operative
2. Three tones of each Tristan-genus chord belong to the virtual diminished seventh
3. Spoiler tracking reveals the virtual chord's organizing influence
4. Historical corroboration: Wagner's sketch shows the diminished seventh explicitly where the final version uses Tristan-genus displacements
5. Parallels the virtual augmented triads of the triadic system

# Construction / Recognition
To identify a virtual diminished seventh:
1. List the Tristan-genus chords in a passage
2. For each, identify the three pitches belonging to a diminished seventh
3. If all chords share the same majority diminished seventh, that chord has virtual presence
4. When the majority shifts, interregional modulation has occurred

# Context & Application
Virtual presence explains how passages of Tristan-genus chords maintain coherence without sounding the perfectly even chord at their center. The concept justifies treating the Boretz region as a structural unit even when its defining feature is absent from the audible surface.

# Examples
- **Tristan Prelude chords 1-5**: All displace B-D-F-G# diminished seventh, which never sounds (pp. 167-170)
- **Wagner's sketch**: The initial sketch shows the diminished seventh explicitly, confirming its structural role (p. 170 n. 11)
- **M. 68 of Tristan Prelude**: A diminished seventh replaces a Tristan-genus chord, briefly surfacing the normally virtual harmony (p. 170 n. 11)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Requiring the diminished seventh to sound for the Boretz region to be valid
  **Correction**: The region is defined by voice-leading relationships among its members, not by the presence of its center

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking virtual presence is a speculative claim
  **Clarification**: It is supported by voice-leading analysis, composer sketches, and occasional surface appearances of the virtual chord
