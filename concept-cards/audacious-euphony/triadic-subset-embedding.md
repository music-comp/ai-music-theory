# Quick Definition
Triadic subset embedding is the analytical technique of identifying consonant triads uniquely contained within dissonant harmonies, thereby revealing voice-leading relationships (such as hexatonic poles) that are concealed beneath the dissonant surface.

# Core Definition
**Triadic subset embedding** extends the reduction strategy to track triadic voice-leading relationships through dissonant surfaces. "Admitting that consonant triads may be embedded into dissonances enables us to see that Wagner uses hexatonic poles in *Parsifal* not only to deform the Communion and the Grail but also to depict the other two components of this schematic knot, the blood and the agony" (p. 164). A V7 chord uniquely embeds a major triad (over-seventh analysis); a ø7 chord uniquely embeds a minor triad (under-seventh analysis). When successive embedded triads form specific transformational relationships, those relationships are analytically present even though the surface harmonies are dissonant.

# Prerequisites
- **Reduction strategy**: The foundational approach identifying triadic subsets
- **Hexatonic pole**: The specific relationship most often revealed through embedding

# Key Properties
1. Only chords with a unique consonant triadic subset are amenable (V7, ø7)
2. The embedded triads may form any triadic relationship (hexatonic pole, L, P, R, etc.)
3. The characteristic voice-leading of the triadic relationship is preserved in the dissonant context
4. Stacked-third analysis may grant or deny the relationship depending on whether it changes the root progression

# Construction / Recognition
1. For each dissonant chord in a passage, apply reduction to find the unique consonant triad
2. Track the progression of embedded triads
3. Check whether successive triads form recognizable transformational relationships
4. Verify that the characteristic voice-leading motion is present (e.g., contrary semitonal motion for hexatonic poles)

# Context & Application
The technique is most productive in Wagner's late music, where dissonant formations (V7 and ø7 chords) often conceal hexatonic pole relationships. It broadens motivic networks by revealing connections that surface-level analysis would miss, enriching both musical and dramatic interpretation.

# Examples
- **Parsifal, Amfortasklage (Figure 7.4)**: Dominant G# major with over-seventh paired with Herzeleide motive's c# minor with under-seventh -- the embedded triads form a hexatonic pole (p. 163)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Applying embedding to chords without unique consonant triadic subsets
  **Correction**: Diminished seventh chords (no consonant subset) and major/minor sevenths (multiple subsets) cannot be reduced unambiguously

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking embedded triadic relationships are merely theoretical
  **Clarification**: Cohn demonstrates that the characteristic voice-leading (e.g., contrary semitonal motion) is physically present in the voice parts, not just abstractly implied
