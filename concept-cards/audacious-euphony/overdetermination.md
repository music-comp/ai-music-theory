# Quick Definition
The condition where consonant triads satisfy the requirements of multiple independent syntactic systems simultaneously, optimizing both acoustic consonance (for diatonic syntax) and voice-leading parsimony via near evenness (for pan-triadic syntax).

# Core Definition
"The general phenomenon described here turns up frequently in the natural and social worlds, where it is referred to as *overdetermination*, *robustness*, or *Babylonianism*. These terms characterize 'the use of multiple means of determination to "triangulate" on the existence and character of a common phenomenon, object, or result' (Wimsatt 1981, 125)" (Cohn, p. 60). The consonant triad is overdetermined because it uniquely optimizes both acoustic consonance and voice-leading parsimony. Tymoczko's Deist parable illustrates: whether you ask for the most consonant chords or the most voice-leading-efficient, "he would hand you *the very same chords*, no matter which choice you made" (p. 60). The concept extends to three levels: the triad, the diatonic scale, and the 12-tone chromatic gamut. "The richness of nineteenth-century chromatic syntax results from overdetermination and preadaptation at two distinct levels of structure. One might say, with only a slight abuse of language, that its double determination is itself doubly determined" (Ch. 9, p. 232).

# Prerequisites
- **near-evenness** — One of the two independent determinations (voice-leading parsimony)

# Key Properties
1. Two independent properties (consonance and near evenness) converge in the same objects
2. Each property independently motivates selecting triads as primary harmonic structures
3. The coincidence is mathematical, not designed or intended
4. Overdetermination is not redundancy; the two determinations are independent
5. Human physiology provides accessible examples: "Mouths are for eating, talking, and breathing" (p. 60)
6. Operates at three levels: triad (acoustic/near-even), diatonic scale (generative/modulatory), chromatic gamut (Pythagorean/divisible)
7. Without overdetermination, the transition from classical to romantic syntax would require new harmonic objects, not just new organizing principles

# Construction / Recognition
Three levels of overdetermination (Ch. 2 introduces the first; Ch. 9 develops all three):
1. **Triad**: Acoustic consonance AND near-evenness (voice-leading parsimony)
2. **Diatonic scale**: Fifth-generation (acoustic) AND unique multiplicity enabling modulatory variety (Ch. 9)
3. **Chromatic gamut**: Pythagorean closure at 12 AND abundant divisibility by 2, 3, 4, 6 (Ch. 9)

Without the gamut's divisibility: no augmented triads, no diminished sevenths, no hexatonic/Weitzmann regions. "All nearly even chords of a given cardinality would form a single exhaustive cycle" (Ch. 9, p. 233).

# Context & Application
Overdetermination explains why the same triads can serve in both syntaxes (shared lexicon), why transitions between syntaxes can be smooth (homophonous diamorphs), and how composers discovered chromatic syntax through diatonic practice. "How, then, does triadic music execute the transformation between the multiple potentials of its constituent objects?" — a question deferred to Chapters 8-9 (p. 60).

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 59-60): Tymoczko's Deist parable: God handing out the same suitcase regardless of the criterion specified.

**Example 2** (p. 60): "Human physiology presents many easily accessible examples of overdetermination. Mouths are for eating, talking, and breathing."

**Example 3** (Ch. 9, p. 233): Counterfactual: Had just fifths closed at a prime number, "the chromatic system would lack perfectly even divisions."

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking overdetermination is redundancy
  **Correction**: The two determinations are independent; each would suffice alone to motivate triadic syntax

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming the coincidence of properties is designed or necessary
  **Clarification**: It is a mathematical fact about integers and ratios, not a design feature
- **Confusion**: Thinking overdetermination resolves ambiguity
  **Clarification**: It creates productive dual functionality
