# Quick Definition
In Daniel Harrison's theory of harmonic function, the "agents" are the thirds of triads, which carry the most intense melodic energy: major thirds strive upward with dominant energy, while minor thirds press downward with subdominant "attitude."

# Formal Definition
Harrison (1994) proposes that the melodic energy invested in thirds bestows *agency* upon their constituent harmonies. Triadic major thirds strive upward, bearing dominant energy, while triadic minor thirds press downward with subdominant attitude. These appellative tendencies are intensified when the thirds are chromatic inflections that displace their diatonic equivalents. In the *nebenverwandt* relation between C major and f minor, the agents are the upward-pressing E (borrowed from f minor's parallel major) and the downward-pressing Ab (borrowed from C major's parallel minor).

# Construction/Recognition
To identify agents in a triadic context:
- The **major third** of any triad is the dominant agent, pressing upward by semitone
- The **minor third** (from the root) of any minor triad is the subdominant agent, pressing downward
- In a *nebenverwandt* pair, the two agents point in opposite directions, creating reciprocal tension
- In a double-agent complex, a single pitch redirects its agency through enharmonic respelling

# Musical Context
The concept of agency becomes particularly important in chromatic contexts where the same pitch can function as both an upward-pressing dominant agent and a downward-pressing subdominant agent, depending on enharmonic spelling. This redirection of an energized tone through enharmonic transformation is what Cohn calls the "double agent" -- central to the analysis of Weitzmann regions.

# Examples
- **Nebenverwandt pair C major / f minor**: E is the upward-pressing agent (dominant energy toward F), Ab is the downward-pressing agent (subdominant energy toward G)
- **Chopin Nocturne Op. 9 No. 1**: Bbb (downward agent of gb minor) converts instantly into A (upward agent of F major) -- "I find that I need to sing the pitch continuously in order to convince myself that the two pitches are identified"
- **Harrison's broader formulation**: The conception originates in dualist thinking but was adopted by non-dualist theorists including Louis and Thuille (1913), Kurth (1923), and Lorenz (1933)

# Common Confusions
- "Agent" refers specifically to the third of a triad, not to any active tone
- Agency is directional: major thirds press up, minor thirds press down
- The concept originates in dualist theory but is applicable outside that framework
- Agency is intensified by chromatic inflection but exists even in diatonic contexts
