# Quick Definition
Extensions of the diatonic Tonnetz along the fifth axis, allowing modulation to closely related keys through incremental shifts of the diatonic parallelogram and enabling recursive migrations through the L/R chain.

# Core Definition
Horizontal extensions arise when the chain of perfect fifths extends outside the diatonic parallelogram, a process historically begun in Renaissance modality and compounded over centuries (Lowinsky 1946, 1989). Under classical tonality, limited mobility embeds the diatonic capsule into a larger "modulatory" shell. "Sharps to the right and flats to the left temporarily displace their diatonic letter-name equivalents, shifting the parallelogram along a horizontal track. With each shift comes a new pair of syntonic images, a new pair of potential tonics, and a reversal of Rings arrows and the syntactic and semantic forces they represent" (p. 197).

# Prerequisites
- **Diatonic Tonnetz**: The encapsulated space that gets shifted
- **Diatonic encapsulation**: Must understand what activates within the shifted capsule
- **L/R chain**: The track along which recursive migrations occur

# Key Properties
1. Close modulation: shift limited to single degree on either side of tonic
2. Each shift creates new syntonic images and reverses Rings arrows
3. Recursive application precipitates more ambitious excursions
4. The L/R chain extends to 24 stations before replication
5. Three modes of chain navigation: locking, sliding, slippery slope

# Construction / Recognition
**Three modes of L/R chain navigation** (pp. 199-200):
1. **Locking migrations**: Each station cadentially confirmed (classical development sections)
2. **Sliding surveillance**: Window "slows to inspect each station but alights at none" (Bach d minor Toccata BWV 913)
3. **Slippery slope**: Triads fly by; "after several hypothesized tonics fail to materialize, we give ourselves over to the momentum of the journey" (Beethoven Ninth Symphony Scherzo mm. 159-71)

As the passage progresses: "Exiting the initial diatonic space, the parallelogram slides incrementally... Flying past multiple diatonic spaces, its diagonal edges ease open, and the horizontal track converts to an alley of indefinite extension. Rings arrows retract into the body of the moving vehicle" (p. 200).

# Context & Application
Horizontal extensions reveal three layers of overdetermination in L/R chain motion: acoustic properties of triads (Brower's recursive fifth generation), maximization of scalar common tones (Babbitt's observation), and common-tone relations among chain triads (parsimonious voice leading). "Ranging along such a chain, we can forget about diatonic constituency and acoustic privilege and simply enjoy the smooth ride provided by parsimonious voice leading" (p. 200).

# Examples
- **Liszt Consolation no. 3, first sentence**: Tonicization of f minor shifts syntonic images from Eb to Bb (Figure 8.7c, p. 198)
- **Bach d minor Toccata** (BWV 913): After establishing g minor, moves leftward through six flats, reverses rightward; no station cadentially confirmed (p. 199)
- **Beethoven Ninth Symphony Scherzo** (mm. 159-71): L/R chain as slippery slope (p. 199)

# Common Confusions

  **Clarification**: They trace historical expansion from Renaissance through Classical era modulation practice
