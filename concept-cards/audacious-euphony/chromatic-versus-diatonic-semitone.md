# Quick Definition
The chromatic semitone connects tones of the same letter name (E-Eb, produced by P), while the diatonic semitone connects tones of different letter names (C-B, produced by L); the distinction corresponds to different geometric directions on the Tonnetz.

# Core Definition
In hexatonic voice leading, the two types of semitonal motion correlate with different transformations. Table 2.1 (Cohn, p. 48) summarizes: P (Parallel) produces a chromatic semitone with 0-degree planar angle on the Tonnetz; L (Leittonwechsel) produces a diatonic semitone with 120-degree planar angle. Figure 2.10 shows "downward arrows, indicating chromatic-semitone descents, alternate with diagonal ones, indicating diatonic-semitone ascents" (p. 47). The distinction connects to diatonic hearing defaults: "the classically conditioned ear interprets ... a single semitone ... as a change of diatonic degree, rather than as a chromatic inflection of an invariant degree" (p. 41).

# Key Properties
1. Chromatic semitone: same letter name (E-Eb), produced by P, 0 degrees on Tonnetz
2. Diatonic semitone: different letter names (C-B), produced by L, 120 degrees on Tonnetz
3. Both represent 1 unit of voice-leading work
4. In equal temperament, both sound identical; the distinction is notational/conceptual
5. The distinction matters for enharmonic spelling and diatonic interpretation

# Context & Application
The distinction affects how the "classically conditioned ear" interprets hexatonic progressions. The default principle that a semitone represents a change of diatonic degree (diatonic semitone default) produces the consonance/dissonance ambiguity central to the uncanny semiotics of hexatonic progressions (pp. 41-42).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating chromatic and diatonic semitones as different amounts of voice-leading work
  **Correction**: Both represent exactly 1 unit of work
