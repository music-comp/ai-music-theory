# Quick Definition
The chromatic semitone connects tones of the same letter name (E-Eb), while the diatonic semitone connects tones of different letter names (C-B); this distinction corresponds to the P and L transformations respectively on the Tonnetz.

# Formal Definition
In hexatonic voice leading, the two types of semitonal motion correlate with different transformations: the P transformation produces a chromatic semitone (preserving letter name, e.g., E to Eb), while the L transformation produces a diatonic semitone (changing letter name, e.g., C to B). On the Tonnetz, these appear as different planar angles: P moves vertically (0 degrees from horizontal), while L moves diagonally (120 degrees).

# Construction/Recognition
Table 2.1 (p. 48) summarizes:

| Name | Symbol | Root motion | Common dyad | Semitonal species | Planar angle on Tonnetz |
|------|--------|-------------|-------------|-------------------|------------------------|
| Parallel | P | No change | Perfect fifth | Chromatic | 0 degrees |
| Leittonwechsel | L | Major third | Minor third | Diatonic | 120 degrees |

Examples:
- P: C major to c minor - E moves to Eb (chromatic semitone, same letter)
- L: C major to e minor - C moves to B (diatonic semitone, different letter)

The distinction affects enharmonic spelling and relates to classical diatonic expectations about interval relationships.

# Musical Context
The distinction between chromatic and diatonic semitones connects to deeper questions about diatonic interpretation. By default, "the classically conditioned ear interprets a two-semitone interval as a major second rather than a diminished third" (Agmon 1986). Similarly, "a single semitone [is heard] as a change of diatonic degree, rather than as a chromatic inflection of an invariant degree" (p. 41).

# Common Confusions
- In equal temperament, chromatic and diatonic semitones sound identical; the distinction is notational/conceptual
- The terminology "chromatic"/"diatonic" refers to the semitone's relationship to letter names, not to scale membership
- Both types of semitone represent one unit of voice-leading work
- The distinction matters for understanding enharmonic seams and spelling choices in chromatic music
