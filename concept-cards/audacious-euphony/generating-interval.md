# Quick Definition
An interval that forms a chord or scale by recursive (repeated) application, such as the whole tone generating the whole-tone scale or the perfect fifth generating the diatonic scale.

# Formal Definition
A generating interval is an interval that, when applied recursively, produces a particular chord or scale. Starting from any pitch class and repeatedly adding the generating interval produces the complete collection. For example, the whole tone (2 semitones) generates the whole-tone scale: C + 2 = D + 2 = E + 2 = F# + 2 = G# + 2 = A# + 2 = C. Similarly, the perfect fifth (7 semitones) generates the diatonic scale (and, extended further, the chromatic scale).

# Construction/Recognition
For an interval i (measured in semitones) to generate a scale of n distinct pitch classes:
- The interval must divide 12 (the chromatic octave) into n equal or near-equal parts
- Mathematically: gcd(i, 12) determines the cycle length

Examples:
- Whole tone (i=2): generates 6-note whole-tone scale (12/gcd(2,12) = 12/2 = 6)
- Minor third (i=3): generates 4-note diminished seventh (12/3 = 4)
- Major third (i=4): generates 3-note augmented triad (12/4 = 3)
- Perfect fifth (i=7): generates all 12 pitch classes, but the first 7 form the diatonic scale
- Tritone (i=6): generates 2-note tritone (12/6 = 2)

# Musical Context/Application
The concept of generating intervals:
- Explains the internal structure of scales and chords
- Connects to theories of scale formation (Carey-Clampitt, Clough-Douthett)
- Illuminates why certain collections have special properties
- Relates to the "nearly even" and "perfectly even" distinction

For pan-triadic theory, the generating interval concept helps explain:
- Why augmented triads (generated by major thirds) divide the octave perfectly
- Why diminished seventh chords (generated by minor thirds) are perfectly even
- How consonant triads relate to these perfectly even structures

# Examples
- Whole-tone scale: generated by whole tone (interval 2)
- Diatonic scale: generated by perfect fifth (interval 7)
- Augmented triad: generated by major third (interval 4)
- Diminished seventh: generated by minor third (interval 3)
- Chromatic scale: generated by semitone (interval 1)

# Common Confusions
- Not every interval generates a musically useful scale
- The diatonic scale is generated by fifths but is typically presented in stepwise order
- Generating interval is about recursive construction, not about the intervals within the resulting collection
- The number of iterations needed depends on the relationship between the interval and 12
