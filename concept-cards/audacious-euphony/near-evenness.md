# Quick Definition
The property of chords that are minimal perturbations of perfectly even divisions of the octave, enabling smooth voice-leading connections between chords of the same type; consonant triads are nearly even because they are single-semitone displacements of augmented triads.

# Core Definition
"The crucial property that consonant triads bear is one that Dmitri Tymoczko has named *near evenness*" (Cohn, p. 52). "Major triads are nearly even because they can be formed from an augmented triad by a single semitonal displacement downward; minor triads, conversely, are formed by a semitonal displacement upward" (p. 52). "A consonant triad is thus like a wheel that is dented just enough to affect a wobble but not so much that it is knocked off its rotation. It is this property that makes possible the minimal voice leading that hexatonic progressions feature" (p. 52).

# Prerequisites
- **perfectly-even-chord** — Near evenness is defined relative to perfect evenness
- **consonant-triad** — Consonant triads are the paradigmatic nearly even chords

# Key Properties
1. A nearly even chord can be formed by displacing one tone of a perfectly even chord by one semitone
2. Major triads: augmented triad with one tone displaced downward
3. Minor triads: augmented triad with one tone displaced upward
4. The direction of displacement (up vs. down) determines major vs. minor
5. Near evenness is independent of acoustic consonance, though the two properties happen to coincide
6. The property generalizes: dominant/half-diminished sevenths are nearly even tetrachords, diatonic collections are nearly even heptachords

# Construction / Recognition
Figure 2.15 (p. 53): A circle with vertices of an equilateral triangle (augmented triad). Each vertex is flanked by two positions (semitone up, semitone down):
1. Start with augmented triad C-E-G#
2. Displace G# down to G -> C major (nearly even, major)
3. Displace G# up to A -> A minor respelled (nearly even, minor)
4. Each vertex generates two consonant triads (one major, one minor)
5. Result: 6 consonant triads clustered around each augmented triad

Figure 2.16 (p. 53): Transformations modeled as reflections about axes that hold two vertices invariant, with the displacement of the third vertex proportional to its distance from the axis.

# Context & Application
Near evenness explains WHY consonant triads voice-lead smoothly: "Via single semitonal displacement, each major triad communicates with two minor triads, and each minor triad with two major triads, precisely because major and minor triads are nearly even" (p. 55). Cohn argues that "the role of near evenness, with respect to the participation of triads in hexatonic progressions, is thus analogous to that of consonance, with respect to their participation in diatonic ones" (p. 55).

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 52): Figure 2.15(b) shows C major generated from C-E-G# augmented by displacing G# down to G.

**Example 2** (p. 55): Other nearly even structures in music: "Dominant and half-diminished seventh chords, as minimal displacements of perfectly symmetric fully diminished seventh chords, voice lead to each other more smoothly than any other tetrachord type." Also: mystic chords (from whole-tone collections), diatonic collections (from chromatic even divisions), diatonic triads in seven-tone space.

**Example 3** (p. 54): Contrasting case: Figure 2.17(b) shows an [015]-type trichord (relatively uneven), where reflection about an axis "slings F a tritone away, to B" — demonstrating that uneven chords have large displacements under reflection.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Equating near evenness with consonance
  **Correction**: They are independent properties that happen to coincide for triads; not all nearly even chords are consonant (e.g., dominant seventh chords contain tritones)

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking near evenness is about external relations between chords
  **Clarification**: Near evenness is about internal structure (how evenly a chord divides the octave); voice-leading efficiency is the external consequence
- **Confusion**: Assuming the direction of displacement is arbitrary
  **Clarification**: Downward displacement produces major triads; upward produces minor — this is the melodic dualism underlying the system
