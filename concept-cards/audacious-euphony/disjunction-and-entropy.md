# Quick Definition
The capacity of the pan-triadic model to represent not only conjunct voice leading but also its opposite (disjunction, maximized at zone-diametric distance 6) and maximum variety along the conjunct/disjunct spectrum (entropy, achieved when triadic quartets form all-interval tetrachords).

# Core Definition
The model premised on conjunct voice leading also enables modeling disjunction as a "residual benefit" (p. 126). Maximal disjunction places triads at zone-diametric positions (6 zones apart, via T2 or T6). Maximum entropy distributes triadic pairs evenly across all interval classes. This occurs when voice-leading zones form an all-interval tetrachord ([0146] or [0137]), both of which are octatonic subsets. Since the eight consonant-triad zones have octatonic structure, maximally entropic triadic quartets exist.

# Prerequisites
- **Voice-leading zones**: The metric space enabling distance measurement
- **Zone-diametric relations**: The maximum distance of 6 zones

# Key Properties
1. Disjunction privileges the contravention of conjunct privilege (Romantic inversion of hierarchies)
2. Maximal disjunction: zone-diametric triads (distance 6), via T2 or T6
3. Aggregate completion: zone-diametric pairs combined with hexatonic poles produce four triads using all twelve tones
4. Maximum entropy: voice-leading zones forming all-interval tetrachord [0146] or [0137]
5. The homology between zones and pitch classes enables application of atonal pitch-class theory to triadic contexts

# Construction / Recognition
**Maximal disjunction with lexical diversity:**
1. Take two zone-diametric triads (same mode)
2. Combine with their hexatonic poles
3. Result: four triads with no pitch-class overlap (aggregate completion)

**Maximum entropy:**
1. Take two zone-diametric triads (one mode)
2. Combine with two triads of opposite mode related by odd transposition
3. Check if zones form [0146] or [0137]

# Context & Application
This section connects pan-triadic analysis to Romantic aesthetics of the remote and unattainable, and to poststructural approaches to analysis. It also bridges to atonal pitch-class theory (all-interval tetrachords), showing that "atonal pitch-class theory has the capacity to stimulate, undergird, or nuance" attributions of disjunction (p. 131).

# Examples
- **Mahler Symphony No. 1 finale** (p. 127): C major to D major -- "as though it had fallen from heaven, as though it had come from another world"
- **Bach Prelude** (Dahlhaus 1967b, figure 5.25a): Four-chord disjunction complex rotating through zones 1, 8, 7, 2; became a trope recurring in Mozart, Mendelssohn, Chopin, Schumann, Wagner, Debussy, Elgar, Distler
- **Wagner *Parsifal*** (figure 5.25b): H-related pair transposed by tritone
- **Beethoven "Tempest" Sonata finale, mm. 8-12** (figure 5.26a): Zones {7, 8, 2, 4} = [0146]
- **Wagner *Gotterdammerung* opening** (figure 5.26b): Zones {7, 8, 1, 5} = [0137]

# Common Errors
- **Error**: The model only handles conjunct voice leading
  **Correction**: Disjunction is a "residual benefit" of the same model that privileges conjunction

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Entropy means randomness
  **Clarification**: Maximum entropy maximizes variety of interval classes, not randomness
- **Confusion**: Disjunction and entropy are the same thing
  **Clarification**: Disjunction maximizes distance; entropy maximizes variety of distances
