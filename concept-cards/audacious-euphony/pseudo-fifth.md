# Quick Definition
The B-F tritone treated as if it were a perfect fifth, whose acceptance as the second stage of closure in the diatonic Tonnetz transforms the chain of fifths into a circuit and creates the Mobius strip topology.

# Core Definition
The second stage of diatonic Tonnetz closure involves "accepting the pseudo-fifth BF as if it were perfect, creating a direct connection between its components" (p. 195). Unlike the syntonic comma (first stage), "no amount of tempering will cure [the pseudo-fifth]; one is asked to simply accept it axiomatically" (p. 197). The result is that the coiling line of fifths becomes a circuit. The new B-F edge locally closes a triangle representing the diminished BDF triad and "globally closes the chain of triangles into a loop, representing the seven diatonic triads arranged into a continuous series of diatonic mediants" (Agmon 1995, cited p. 195).

# Prerequisites
- **Diatonic Tonnetz**: The structure within which the pseudo-fifth operates
- **Syntonic image**: The first closure that precedes pseudo-fifth acceptance

# Key Properties
1. The B-F tritone is approximately 590 cents (vs. 702 for a perfect fifth)
2. No temperament can reconcile this difference (unlike the syntonic comma)
3. Must be accepted axiomatically
4. Acceptance creates the Mobius strip topology through half-twist
5. Fetis recognized the pseudo-fifth's special nature: its "appellative powers are neutralized by the symmetry of movement and succession" (Fetis 2008 [1844], 30)

# Construction / Recognition
The closing process: since the coiling process positioned B two ranks above F on the FDB minor-third line, bringing them into direct contact requires crooking the line at its center, fixing the DF edge, and folding the BD edge so that B comes alongside F. This half-twist transforms the parallelogram into a Mobius strip.

# Context & Application
Renaissance musicians often opted to extend the chain of perfect fifths beyond the parallelogram rather than accept the pseudo-fifth, leading to the historical expansion of the diatonic Tonnetz along the fifth axis (p. 197). The pseudo-fifth's properties become crucial in sequential passages, where its neutralized appellative powers allow the chain of triads to flow without functional gravity.

# Examples
- **B-F in C major**: The diminished triad BDF forms when the pseudo-fifth is accepted (p. 195)
- **Fetis on sequences**: The pseudo-fifth's appellative neutralization enables uniform sequential motion (p. 196)
- **Figure 8.5** (p. 194): The T-S-D-T progression requires traversing the pseudo-fifth

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Equal temperament solves the pseudo-fifth problem
  **Correction**: No temperament can make a tritone into a perfect fifth; it must be accepted axiomatically

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The pseudo-fifth is the same issue as the syntonic comma
  **Clarification**: The syntonic comma can be absorbed by temperament; the pseudo-fifth requires axiomatic acceptance
