# Quick Definition
Analysis of a passage from Faure's Requiem (1877), Introit/Kyrie, mm. 50-61, demonstrating the augmented triad FAC# as a "switching station" connecting consonant triads (F major, f# minor, bb minor) that lack unified diatonic explanation.

# Core Definition
The passage is "animated by the tonic's relationship to an F major triad that consistently functions as dominant" (p. 73). The FAC# augmented triad appears at m. 60 before the cadence and three times in the interior as the second component of two-chord units: after F major (m. 52), f# minor (m. 54), and bb minor (m. 56). Analyzing "parallel passages in parallel ways" presents a choice: either the consonant triads are structural (producing an incoherent diatonic progression) or the augmented triad is structural. "The second option understands this bouquet of harmonies in terms of the augmented triad to which each one leads. This alternative places FAC# at the conceptual center of the passage, assigning it the role of a switching station through which the various consonant triads are threaded" (p. 74).

# Prerequisites
- **Augmented triad as switching station**: The analysis illustrates this concept
- **Consonance/dissonance reciprocity**: The passage demonstrates how a dissonant center can organize consonant harmonies

# Key Properties
1. d minor tonic, with F major functioning as dominant throughout
2. FAC# augmented triad appears four times (once cadential, three interior)
3. Interior appearances follow consonant triads: F major, f# minor, bb minor
4. Two-bar groupings confirmed by parallel melodic features
5. Diatonic analysis fails: "F major is embellished by f# minor and bb minor before resolving as dominant of d minor" is incoherent
6. The augmented triad analysis succeeds: all consonant triads are single-semitone displacements of FAC#

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the recurring augmented triad (FAC#) in the passage
2. Note that each consonant triad leads to this augmented triad
3. Verify that no single diatonic framework explains F major, f# minor, and bb minor together
4. Recognize that all three are single-semitone displacements of FAC#

# Context & Application
The Faure analysis is the clearest demonstration of the switching-station concept. It shows how the augmented triad acts as "chronological mediator" -- actually sounded between the consonant triads it connects -- while also serving as structural center.

# Examples
**The failed diatonic analysis** (p. 74): "The first of these options does not present a very coherent species of diatonic tonality: F major is embellished by f# minor and bb minor before resolving as dominant of d minor. bb minor is easily reconciled as the minor subdominant of F major. What remains intractable is the f# minor triad."

**The successful augmented-triad analysis** (p. 74): "There is no consistent diatonic explanation that accounts for the simultaneous presence of this particular group of triads in a single phrase. What draws them together is their shared status as single semitonal displacements of FAC#."

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Forcing a diatonic analysis of the f# minor triad
  **Correction**: "Perhaps it functions as iii of a D major that otherwise has no presence in the passage... This feels a little desperate" (p. 74)

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The augmented triad needs to sound simultaneously to function as center
  **Clarification**: Here it does sound, but the concept works even when it does not (as in *Antar*)
