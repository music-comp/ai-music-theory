# Quick Definition
Tonnetz analyses can function as interpretive "performances" upon the data of a composition, with different readings of the same passage constituting different acts of analytical performance, analogous to musical performances of a score.

# Formal Definition
Two different Tonnetz analyses of the same passage (such as the two components of figure 6.20 for Dvorak's "New World" development) constitute two "performances" upon a single pattern of data points. Both are "note accurate" but shape the data in different ways, drawing attention to different articulation points. The question of whether these analytical acts relate directly to musical performance, or are merely metaphoric and suggestive, is left open for performers to determine.

# Construction/Recognition
Different analytical "performances" may:
- Emphasize different grouping structures
- Draw breath at different articulation points
- Highlight different transformational chains (e.g., **N**/**L** chain vs. retention loops)
- Overlay different interpretive schemas on the same data

# Musical Context
This concept positions pan-triadic analysis not as a fixed procedure producing unique results but as a representational "instrument" with interpretive leeway. The analyst, like a performer, makes choices about emphasis, phrasing, and trajectory.

# Examples
- Dvorak "New World" Symphony development, figure 6.20: (a) emphasizes the **N**/**L** chain and overall upshift trajectory; (b) emphasizes retention loops around E and A, highlighting the slower pitch-class turnover in the first two paragraphs vs. accelerated upshifting in the final two
- Both readings are "note accurate" but reveal different structural features

# Common Confusions
- Different analytical "performances" are not competing; they illuminate complementary aspects of the same passage
- The metaphor of performance emphasizes the analyst's active role in shaping interpretation
- Whether analytical choices translate directly to performance decisions is an open question
