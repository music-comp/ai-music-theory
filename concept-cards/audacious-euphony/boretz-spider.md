# Quick Definition
The Boretz spider is a graph representing voice-leading relationships within a single Boretz region, analogous to the Weitzmann water bug for triads, showing how eight Tristan-genus chords connect through two-unit voice-leading motions.

# Formal Definition
The **Boretz spider** is a geometric representation of voice leading within a Boretz region. It visualizes:
1. Eight nodes: four dominant seventh chords and four half-diminished seventh chords
2. Edges connecting chords related by two units of voice leading
3. A central position (often virtual) for the generating diminished seventh chord

The spider is analogous to the Weitzmann water bug, which represents the six triads of a Weitzmann region.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Spider structure:
- 8 peripheral nodes (4 V7, 4 half-diminished)
- Central node (diminished seventh, may be virtual)
- Edges represent 2 voice-leading units
- All peripheral nodes equidistant from center (1 semitone = 1 edge)

Voice-leading types shown:
- **Same-species edges**: contrary semitonal motion (two voices move, two hold)
- **Opposite-species edges**: either two parallel semitones or one whole step

Transformations labeled on edges:
- **S3(2)**: stationary interval class 3, moving interval class 2
- **S3(4)**: stationary interval class 3, moving interval class 4
- **S6**: stationary tritone, moving perfect fifth
- **R***: one voice moves by whole step

# Musical Context/Application
The Boretz spider enables:
- Visualizing motion within a single Boretz region
- Understanding balanced (oscillating) vs. directed voice leading
- Tracking "spoiler" tones that displace the diminished seventh core
- Analyzing passages like the Tristan Prelude opening

Back-and-forth motion across the spider:
- Toggles upshift with downshift
- Balances between adjacent voice-leading zones
- Maintains connection to central diminished seventh

# Examples
**Tristan Prelude opening (chords 1-5)**: Motion across the Boretz spider centered on B-D-F-G#. The first five chords alternately upshift and downshift, moving back and forth across the spider, balancing between voice-leading zones 3 and 1.

**Brunnhilde's Immolation**: Similar spider-traversal pattern within the E-G-A#-C# Boretz region.

**Figure 7.10 in the text**: Models voice leading within the third Boretz region (D-F-Ab-B), showing the eight Tristan-genus chords and their connections.

# Common Confusions
- The spider has 8 nodes (not 6 like the water bug) because tetrachords have more displacement possibilities
- The central diminished seventh may not appear in the music; it exerts "virtual" influence
- Spider motion is balanced; circumnavigation requires moving between spiders via octatonic bridges
- The spider is a subgraph of 4-Cube Trio
