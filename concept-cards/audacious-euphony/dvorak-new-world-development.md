# Quick Definition
The development of Dvorak's "New World" Symphony executes a relentless continuous upshift across ninety-six measures, tracing two complete rotations of voice-leading space through free thematic circulation rather than sequential repetition, with an accelerating **N**/**L** chain dominating the third paragraph.

# Formal Definition
The development section (mm. 177-273) of the first movement of Dvorak's E minor Symphony (1895) is organized in clear four-bar hypermeter with four paragraphs defined by hortatory thematic entries. The first two paragraphs feature the pastoral third theme; the final two use the principal theme (related by contour inversion, linked by Hungarian choriambus rhythm). The series of initial stations (E major -> F major -> eb minor -> f# minor) becomes coherent when interpreted as voice-leading zone representatives, revealing prevailing upshift winds.

# Construction/Recognition
Table 6.2 and figure 6.19 segment the development:
- **Paragraph 1** (m. 189): E major (zone 11), then c# minor (zone 1), to F major (zone 2); traces an E retention loop
- **Paragraph 2** (m. 209): F major continues, proceeds to f# minor (zone 4); A retention loop bridges paragraphs 1-2
- **Paragraph 3** (m. 233): Extended **N**/**L** chain: eb minor -> e minor -> f minor -> f# minor, each minor tonic ascending by semitone every eight measures, with dominant arrivals at four-bar medians
- **Paragraph 4** (m. 257): Reprises **N**/**L** chain with additions; f# minor fulfills both slow (8-bar) and fast (1-bar) trajectories simultaneously

# Musical Context
The development is the longest continuous segment analyzed in the book, constituting a "fittingly weighty close to the six-chapter arc" of pan-triadic syntax. The movement avoids the dominant throughout; B is never tonicized. Off-tonic roles are filled by four upper mediant triads presented in upshifting order.

# Common Confusions
- The upshift is continuous but not sequential; thematic materials circulate freely without strict repetition
- Two interpretations of figure 6.20 are both "note accurate" but shape the data differently
- The movement's avoidance of the dominant is a key structural feature, not an oversight
- The fourth paragraph's f# minor simultaneously fulfills two periodicities (8-bar and 1-bar)
