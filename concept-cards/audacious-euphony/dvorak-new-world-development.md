# Quick Definition
The ninety-six-measure development of Dvorak's "New World" Symphony (mm. 177-273) executes relentless continuous upshift through two complete rotations of voice-leading space via free thematic circulation, with an accelerating N/L chain dominating the third paragraph.

# Core Definition
The development is "the longest continuous segment of music analyzed in this book" (p. 153), constituting "a fittingly weighty close to the six-chapter arc that constitutes the core presentation of pan-triadic syntax." Organized in four-bar hypermeter with four paragraphs defined by thematic entries (pastoral third theme and principal theme, linked by Hungarian choriambus rhythm and related by contour inversion), the passage traces two complete voice-leading rotations from G major to e minor without sequential repetition.

# Prerequisites
- **Continuous upshift**: The compositional script
- **Voice-leading zones**: The tracking metric
- **Semitone sequence**: The N/L chain dominating paragraph 3

# Key Properties
1. Ninety-six measures, clear four-bar hypermeter
2. Four paragraphs: two with pastoral third theme, two with principal theme
3. Third paragraph: extended N/L chain (eb minor at m. 233 -> e minor at m. 241 -> f minor at m. 249 -> f# minor at m. 257) semitonally ascending every eight measures; the N/L chain is the most common T1 sequence type and appears as a southwest slide on the Tonnetz
4. At m. 257, f# minor fulfills both slow (8-bar) and fast (1-bar) trajectories simultaneously
5. The movement avoids the dominant throughout; B is never tonicized

# Context & Application
The two components of figure 6.20 constitute "two 'performances' upon a single pattern of black dots" (p. 155). Both are "note accurate" but shape data differently, illustrating "the interpretive leeway available to the pan-triadic analyst" (p. 155). The relationship to musical performance is suggestive: "only performers can determine" the extent of the connection (p. 155).

# Examples
- **Paragraph 3** (m. 233): N/L chain dominates; dominant sevenths at four-bar medians prepare each ascending minor tonic
- **M. 253**: Dvorak synopsizes twenty measures in four bars by planing minor triads with under-sevenths (one exception: m. 253 dominant arrives two bars early, enabling acceleration); at m. 255 the harmonic motion compresses to plane eb minor, e minor, f minor triads rapidly
- **Second theme keys**: Climb voice-leading space 7 -> 8 -> 10 -> 11 as part of P/S chain; recapitulation continues to Ab major (hexatonic pole of tonic)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: The upshift requires sequential repetition
  **Correction**: Thematic materials circulate freely; upshift is maintained without strict repetition

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The two Tonnetz interpretations are contradictory
  **Clarification**: They are complementary "performances" of the same data, emphasizing different analytical features
