# Quick Definition
A maximally entropic ensemble of four triads whose constituent pairs are distributed as evenly as possible among the six interval classes, achieved when the voice-leading zones of the four triads form an all-interval tetrachord.

# Core Definition
Maximum entropy in a triadic quartet requires that the six pairwise distances between the four triads be distributed as evenly as possible across the six possible interval classes. This is achieved when the voice-leading zones of the four triads correspond to one of the two all-interval tetrachords: prime form [0146] or [0137]. Both are octatonic subsets. Following Robert Morris (1990), inspired by Elliott Carter's compositional practice, any nonintersecting union of a tritone with a minor third creates an all-interval tetrachord.

# Prerequisites
- **Voice-leading Zone**: The classification system for triadic position in voice-leading space

# Key Properties
1. Six pairwise distances distributed as evenly as possible
2. Zone set corresponds to [0146] or [0137] (the two all-interval tetrachords)
3. Both all-interval tetrachords are octatonic subsets
4. Possible because consonant-triad voice-leading zones have octatonic structure
5. Consists of two zone-diametric triads of one mode + two of opposite mode
6. Bridges atonal pitch-class theory and pan-triadic voice-leading analysis

# Construction / Recognition
A maximally entropic quartet consists of:
- Two zone-diametric (T2- or T6-related) triads of one mode
- Two triads of opposite mode related by an odd transposition (three zones apart)
- The resulting zone set corresponds to [0146] or [0137]

# Context & Application
The concept demonstrates that analytical tools from atonal pitch-class theory (Carter, Morris) can illuminate 19th-century chromatic practice. The octatonic structure of consonant-triad voice-leading zones makes these constructions possible.

# Examples
- **Beethoven, "Tempest" Sonata finale, mm. 8-12**: iv, bII, V6, i progression in d minor; zones {g=7, Eb=8, A=2, d=4} = [0146] (figure 5.26a)
- **Wagner, Gotterdammerung opening**: eb minor alternating with Cb major, db minor, D major; zones {eb=7, Cb=8, db=1, D=5} = [0137] (figure 5.26b)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Maximum entropy means randomness
  **Correction**: It is the most even distribution of intervals, requiring careful selection

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The two all-interval tetrachord types are equivalent
  **Clarification**: [0146] and [0137] are distinct set classes, both exhausting the possibilities for maximum entropy

- **Confusion**: This concept applies only to abstract theory
  **Clarification**: Beethoven and Wagner exemplify it in practice
