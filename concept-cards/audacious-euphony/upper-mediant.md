# Quick Definition
Upper mediant triads are those whose roots lie a third above the tonic, used as off-tonic harmonic goals in place of the traditional dominant, particularly prominent in nineteenth-century music.

# Formal Definition
**Upper mediant** (Kopp's term, 2002) refers to triads whose roots lie a major or minor third above the tonic. In chromatic music, upper mediants often appropriate the structural role traditionally assigned to the dominant, serving as:
- Off-tonic harmonic goals in expositions
- Development section platforms
- Alternatives to dominant prolongation

The four upper mediant triads (for a C tonic) are:
- E major (chromatic mediant)
- E minor (diatonic mediant)
- Eb major (chromatic mediant)
- Eb minor (mixture mediant)

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Upper mediants relate to tonic through:
- Root motion by third (major or minor)
- Voice-leading connections (often involving common tones)
- Hexatonic or chromatic mediant relationships

In voice-leading terms:
- Upper mediants often represent different voice-leading zones than dominant
- Sequential presentation of upper mediants creates upshifting patterns
- Upper mediants may be presented in upshifting order through a movement

# Musical Context/Application
Upper mediants serve structural functions including:
- Replacing dominant as secondary key area
- Providing colorful tonal contrast
- Creating large-scale upshifting trajectories
- Avoiding conventional dominant emphasis

The use of upper mediants reflects nineteenth-century trends toward:
- Weakened dominant function
- Third-related key schemes
- Chromatic voice-leading logic over root-based function

# Examples
**Dvorak's "New World" Symphony, first movement**: The movement avoids dominant B throughout; B is never tonicized as major or minor. The off-tonic role is appropriated by four upper mediant triads presented in upshifting order:
- Second theme: g minor (m. 91), then G major (m. 137) closes exposition
- Recapitulation: transposed up by chromatic semitone
- Ab major (proxy for G#): hexatonic pole of tonic, closes recapitulation

The progression through upper mediants (zones 7 → 8 → 10 → 11) forms part of a P/S chain.

# Common Confusions
- Upper mediant is not the same as relative major/minor (which is lower mediant for minor keys)
- Upper mediants can be either major or minor quality
- "Upper" refers to root position, not register
- Upper mediant usage does not necessarily negate dominant function; both may operate in the same piece
