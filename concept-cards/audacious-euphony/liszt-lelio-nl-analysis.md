# Quick Definition
Liszt's *Grande fantaisie symphonique* ("Lelio Fantasy," 1834) contains an **N**/**L** chain with an **R**-for-**L** substitution that temporarily retreats before achieving cyclic closure, demonstrating that H/W substitutions are not limited to **L**/**R** chains.

# Formal Definition
Measures 379-429 feature an oboe melody transposed upward by semitone, harmonized by an **N**/**L** chain transposing from Db major to E major. At E major's *nebenverwandt* a minor, **R** substitutes for the projected **L**, taking the progression to C major rather than F major. This amounts to a temporary retreat, briefly colonizing a Weitzmann region. From C major, the **N**/**L** chain resumes, arriving not at the projected F major but at tonic Db major, achieving cyclic closure.

# Construction/Recognition
- The **N**/**L** chain generates T1 (semitone) upshift
- The **R**-for-**L** substitution (W-class for H-class) creates a temporary directional reversal
- Cyclic closure is achieved: Db major returns as tonic
- Cube Dance (figure 5.23) shows the consistent clockwise trajectory, the temporary downshift to C major, and the cyclic return
- The Tonnetz (figure 5.22) shows the sequential nature but conceals cyclic closure (Bering Strait distortion)

# Musical Context
This passage demonstrates that transformational substitutions apply to any chain type, not only **L**/**R** chains. It also provides the key example for comparing Tonnetz and Cube Dance as representational modes: Cube Dance shows cyclic closure, while the Tonnetz shows sequential patterning.

# Common Confusions
- The substitution here is **R**-for-**L** (W for H class), not the more common **N**-for-**R** (W for W within same class)
- The Cube Dance representation is superior for this passage because cyclic closure is the key structural feature
- The Tonnetz shows the sequential character but disguises the return to tonic
