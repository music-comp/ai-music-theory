# Quick Definition
A chromatic sequence transposing by perfect fourth or fifth, most commonly generated by L/R alternation, traversing all 24 triads before closing.

# Formal Definition
Perfect-fifth (T5/T7) sequences result from three H/W combinations:
- L/R (most common): Circle-of-fifths motion through triads
- N/P: Authentic and plagal directional options
- H/S: Involves both maverick transformations (rare)

The L/R chain is too long to circumnavigate in a single gesture (24 stations), but extended passages traverse significant portions. The sequence was recognized as a modulation map by Werckmeister (1698) and as harmonic progression by Vogler (1778) and Langle (1797).

# Construction/Recognition
L/R example (upshifting):
f# minor --(L)--> A major --(R)--> f# minor ... wait, that closes too soon.

Actually:
f# minor --(L)--> A major --(R)--> a minor --(L)--> C major --(R)--> c minor ...
(This traverses the entire 24-triad universe)

N/P authentic direction:
Major triads preceded by major dominants, whose agents discharge upward

N/P plagal direction:
Major tonics preceded by minor subdominants, whose agents discharge downward

# Musical Context
The L/R chain became central to Moritz Hauptmann's tonal space conception (1888 [1853]). Beethoven's Ninth Symphony Scherzo nearly circumnavigates the entire cycle (19 triads from C major to A major). Brahms's D major Symphony first movement uses the L/R chain in its subordinate theme liquidation.

# Examples
- Beethoven Ninth Symphony Scherzo: 19-triad L/R chain (Web score 5.9)
- Brahms Symphony No. 2, 1st mvt., mm. 102-18: L/R chain from f# minor to d minor
- Bach g minor Organ Fantasy: N/P authentic chain
- Schubert d minor String Quartet: N/P plagal chain

# Common Confusions
- The full L/R cycle is 24 triads, not 12 (unlike the pitch-class circle of fifths)
- N/P produces T5 with different functional character than L/R
- Harrison's dominant/subdominant dualism maps onto N/P directional options
