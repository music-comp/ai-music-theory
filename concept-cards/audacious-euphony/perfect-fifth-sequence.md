# Quick Definition
A chromatic sequence transposing by perfect fourth or fifth, most commonly generated by L/R alternation, traversing all twenty-four triads before closing, with a rich history from Werckmeister (1698) through Hauptmann (1853).

# Core Definition
Perfect-fifth (T5/T7) sequences result from three H/W combinations: L/R (most common), N/P (authentic and plagal directional options), and H/S (rare, both maverick). The L/R chain traverses all twenty-four triads before returning to its starting triad. It was recognized as a modulation map by Werckmeister (1698), as harmonic progression by Vogler (1778) and Langle (1797), and became central to Hauptmann's tonal space conception (1888 [1853]). Carl Stein recommended students learn the entire cycle, noting its "double union of intervals -- two of them always remaining undisturbed" (1888, p. 38, cited p. 111).

# Key Properties
1. L/R chain: 24 triads before closing (too long for single-gesture circumnavigation)
2. N/P chain: Authentic direction = major dominants preceding minor tonics (agents discharge upward); plagal direction = minor subdominants preceding major tonics (agents discharge downward)
3. N/P dualism maps onto Harrison's dominant/subdominant, major/minor, plagal/authentic, upward/downward leading-tone energy dualisms
4. H/S involves both maverick transformations (rare/undocumented)

# Construction / Recognition
L/R chain: f# minor --(L)--> A major --(R)--> a minor --(L)--> C major --(R)--> c minor ... (traverses all 24 triads)

N/P authentic direction: E major --(N)--> a minor --(P)--> A major --(N)--> d minor --(P)--> D major ...
N/P plagal direction: (reversal of the above)

# Context & Application
The L/R chain is one of the most historically significant chromatic sequences, appearing from Werckmeister's circle diagram through Beethoven's Ninth Symphony Scherzo (19 triads) to Brahms's Second Symphony. The N/P variant connects to Harrison's functional dualism theory.

# Examples
- **Beethoven Ninth Symphony Scherzo**: Nearly circumnavigates the entire L/R cycle (19 triads from C major to A major, completing more than two Cube Dance tours; p. 111)
- **Brahms Symphony No. 2, 1st mvt., mm. 102-18** (figures 5.9-5.11): L/R chain from f# minor liquidating the subordinate theme; traverses nine triads (f# -> A -> a -> C -> c -> Eb -> eb -> Gb -> gb -> d), providing closure by returning to the original Cube Dance radius at 4:00 (figure 5.11). On the Tonnetz (figure 5.10, with web animation), the passage shows leftward drift along the horizontal axis. The sequence exits the f# minor diatonic system at G-natural, with subsequent C-natural and F-natural creating chromatic alterations. Cohn describes "free fall in bass" and "free rise in upper voices" as the voice-leading sensation
- **N/P examples** (figure 5.12): Harrison (1994, pp. 33-34) points to Bach g minor Organ Fantasy (authentic) and Schubert d minor String Quartet (plagal)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing the L/R triadic chain with the pitch-class circle of fifths
  **Correction**: The L/R chain traverses 24 triads (not 12), alternating major and minor

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: N/P and L/R produce the same result
  **Clarification**: Both produce T5 transposition but with different functional characters; N/P carries authentic/plagal directional meaning that L/R does not
