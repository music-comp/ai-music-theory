# Quick Definition
An **N**/**P** chain is a chromatic sequence generated by alternating Nebenverwandt and Parallel transformations, producing transposition by perfect fifth (T5 or T7), with each pair of progressions realizing authentic or plagal harmonic motion.

# Formal Definition
The **N**/**P** chain combines a W-group transformation (**N**) with an H-group transformation (**P**), generating a perfect-fifth (T5) chromatic sequence. Both transformations are classically normative. Daniel Harrison (1994) identifies this pair as typifying a set of dualisms: major/minor, plagal/authentic, dominant/subdominant, and upward/downward leading-tone energy. In the authentic direction, minor tonics are preceded by their major dominants whose agents discharge upward; in the plagal direction, major tonics are preceded by their minor subdominants whose agents discharge downward.

# Construction/Recognition
Alternate **N** and **P** transformations:
- Authentic direction (a): major dominant → minor tonic → major dominant → minor tonic...
- Plagal direction (b): minor subdominant → major tonic → minor subdominant → major tonic...
- Each pair produces T5 or T7 transposition depending on direction

# Musical Context
The **N**/**P** chain is equally effective in both directions, which Harrison correlates with the authentic/plagal duality. Both directions are well represented in the literature from Bach through Schubert.

# Common Confusions
- The **N**/**P** and **L**/**R** chains both produce T5 transpositions but realize different voice-leading profiles
- The authentic/plagal duality is proper to the **N**/**P** chain specifically, not to all T5 sequences
- Both directions of the chain are equally effective, unlike some other chain types where one direction is historically more common
