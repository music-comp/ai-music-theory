# Quick Definition
The complete universe of consonant triads in twelve-tone equal temperament: 12 major triads and 12 minor triads, forming the primary harmonic objects of pan-triadic syntax.

# Formal Definition
The 24 consonant triads comprise 12 major triads (C, C#/Db, D, D#/Eb, E, F, F#/Gb, G, G#/Ab, A, A#/Bb, B) and 12 minor triads (c, c#/db, d, d#/eb, e, f, f#/gb, g, g#/ab, a, a#/bb, b). In Cohn's notation, major triads are indicated by uppercase letters (often with a plus sign: C+) and minor triads by lowercase (often with a minus sign: c-). These 24 triads constitute the complete space of triadic harmony in the twelve-tone equal-tempered system.

# Construction/Recognition
Each consonant triad:
- Contains exactly 3 distinct pitch classes
- Spans intervals of major third (4 semitones) and minor third (3 semitones)
- Is "nearly even" - dividing 12 semitones into 3+4+5 or 4+3+5

The 24 triads form:
- 4 hexatonic regions (6 triads each, based on hexatonic cycles)
- 4 Weitzmann regions (6 triads each, surrounding augmented triads)
- 12 neighborhoods (6 triads each, sharing a common tone)
- Various other systematic groupings

All 24 triads can be generated from any single triad by combinations of the basic transformations P, L, and R.

# Musical Context/Application
The 24 consonant triads serve as:
- The complete "lexicon" of pan-triadic harmony
- Nodes in geometric representations (Tonnetz, Cube Dance)
- The domain over which triadic transformations operate
- The harmonic vocabulary shared by classical and romantic syntax

Unlike classical theory, which privileges triads diatonic to the key, pan-triadic syntax treats all 24 as equally available, with relationships determined by voice-leading proximity rather than diatonic membership.

# Examples
- The 4 hexatonic cycles each contain 6 triads: {C, c, Ab, ab, E, e}, {G, g, Eb, eb, B, b}, {D, d, Bb, bb, F#, f#}, {A, a, F, f, C#, c#}
- The 4 Weitzmann regions each contain 6 triads surrounding one augmented triad
- Chromatic sequences traverse the full set of 24 triads

# Common Confusions
- The number 24 assumes enharmonic equivalence (F# = Gb as triads)
- Not all 24 triads have equal status in diatonic tonality, but they are equally available in pan-triadic contexts
- The 24 triads do not include augmented or diminished triads, which play structural roles but are not "consonant"
- Minor triads built on sharped roots (c#, d#, f#, g#, a#) require enharmonic spelling in some contexts
