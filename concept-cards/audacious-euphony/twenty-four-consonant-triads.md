# Quick Definition
The complete universe of consonant triads in twelve-tone equal temperament: 12 major and 12 minor triads, forming the primary harmonic objects and shared lexicon of both classical and pan-triadic syntax.

# Core Definition
The 24 consonant triads comprise 12 major and 12 minor triads in 12-tone equal temperament. They constitute the complete space of triadic harmony over which transformations operate and through which geometric representations are constructed. All 24 can be generated from any single triad by combinations of the basic transformations P, L, and R (synthesized from Ch. 1). Unlike classical theory, which privileges triads diatonic to the key, pan-triadic syntax treats all 24 as equally available, with relationships determined by voice-leading proximity.

# Key Properties
1. 12 major triads + 12 minor triads = 24 total (assumes enharmonic equivalence)
2. Form 4 hexatonic regions (6 triads each)
3. Form 4 Weitzmann regions (6 triads each)
4. Serve as nodes in all geometric representations (Tonnetz, Cube Dance)
5. Constitute the domain over which triadic transformations operate
6. Shared lexicon between classical and pan-triadic syntax

# Construction / Recognition
Notation in the book:
- Major: uppercase (C, G, Eb) or plus sign (C+)
- Minor: lowercase (c, g, eb) or minus sign (c-)

The 4 hexatonic cycles partition all 24:
- {C, c, Ab, ab, E, e}
- {G, g, Eb, eb, B, b}
- {D, d, Bb, bb, F#, f#}
- {A, a, F, f, C#, c#}

# Context & Application
The 24 triads form the complete harmonic vocabulary for pan-triadic analysis. Their systematic organization into hexatonic systems, Weitzmann regions, and other groupings provides the "maps" of the triadic universe that Cohn develops throughout the book.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Including augmented or diminished triads in the count
  **Correction**: Only major and minor triads are "consonant triads" in this framework

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the 24 triads have equal status in diatonic tonality
  **Clarification**: They are equally available in pan-triadic contexts but have different diatonic functions
- **Confusion**: Forgetting enharmonic equivalence
  **Clarification**: F# major and Gb major count as one triad (24 total, not 30+)
