# Quick Definition
Chromatic sequences transposing by an odd number of semitones (1, 3, 5, 7, 9, or 11), each involving three units of voice-leading work and necessarily crossing between adjacent hexatonic and Weitzmann regions.

# Core Definition
Odd-transposition sequences are distinguished from even-transposition sequences (which remain within a single region). "Transposition by semitone (T1, T11) engages all three voices in parallel motion. The remaining transpositions (T3, T5, T7, and T9) feature two voices moving in similar motion, one by semitone and the other by whole step. Thus, all odd-value transpositions involve three units of semitonal work" (p. 107). Three units of work represent a threshold: "just as a three-semitone motion in a single voice is characteristically conceived as a leap that implicitly combines and elides across two distinct steps, so we will conceive of three units of semitonal work as implicitly combining and eliding across two distinct parsimonious transformations" (pp. 107-108).

# Prerequisites
- **Chromatic sequences**: The general category
- **H-group and W-group transformations**: The two families combined to generate these sequences

# Key Properties
1. All odd transpositions involve exactly 3 units of voice-leading work
2. T1/T11: all three voices in parallel motion
3. T3/T5/T7/T9: two similar + one contrary voice
4. The threshold from 2 to 3 units marks the boundary between intra- and inter-regional motion
5. Three units = two parsimonious transformations combined

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the transpositional interval of a sequence
2. If odd: it must combine one H-group and one W-group transformation
3. Consult Table 5.1 for which pair produces the observed interval
4. Even transpositions (T2, T4, T6) stay within regions -- different analysis required

# Context & Application
The odd/even distinction is structurally fundamental: even transpositions preserve region membership; odd transpositions cross between adjacent regions. This crossing enables circumnavigation of the entire triadic universe.

# Examples
- T1/T11: N/L, S/P, H/R
- T3/T9: P/R, N/H, S/L
- T5/T7: L/R, N/P, H/S

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Three units of work = a minor third interval
  **Correction**: Three units refers to total semitonal displacement across all three voices

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: T7 and T5 are different sequence types
  **Clarification**: They are inversionally related, producing the same interval class (perfect fourth/fifth)
