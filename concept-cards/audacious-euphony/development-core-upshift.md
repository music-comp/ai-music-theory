# Quick Definition
The interior of a classical sonata-form development (the "core" or "central action zone") characteristically projects tonal instability and mounting tension through one or more sequences that sustain an upshift trajectory, a pattern extended by nineteenth-century composers through free thematic circulation.

# Formal Definition
The development core (Caplin 1998) or central action zone (Hepokoski and Darcy 2006) is the interior section of a sonata-form development that characteristically projects tonal instability and mounting tension. In classical and early Romantic practice, this section frequently features chromatic sequences sustaining an upshift trajectory. Extended upshifts function as "ingredients of the adrenal concoction" that composers inject into developments. The pattern extends from Beethoven's sequential models through Dvorak's freer thematic circulation.

# Construction/Recognition
Common features:
- One or more sequences sustaining upshift direction
- Possible flanking downshifts at beginning and end
- Acceleration or deceleration of harmonic rhythm
- Thematic fragmentation or rotation
- Climactic arrival typically at or near the retransitional dominant

# Musical Context
The development core upshift represents a continuous upshift script applied to sonata-form development sections. It provides an alternative analytical framework to Schenkerian or functional-harmonic accounts of developmental trajectories.

# Examples
- Beethoven, "Tempest" Sonata, Op. 31/2: Compressed upshift orbit between flanking downshifts (figure 6.16a)
- Beethoven, "Waldstein" Sonata, Op. 53: Broader upshift arc with varying speeds, stalling in a Weitzmann region before the retransitional dominant (figure 6.16b)
- Dvorak, "New World" Symphony: Ninety-six-measure development with relentless upshift, two complete rotations of voice-leading space (figures 6.19-6.20)

# Common Confusions
- The upshift need not be sequential; Dvorak achieves continuous upshift through free thematic circulation
- The core may include brief downshifts or stalls within the prevailing upshift trajectory
- The classical prototype (sequential repetition) and the Romantic extension (free circulation) share the same underlying voice-leading principle
