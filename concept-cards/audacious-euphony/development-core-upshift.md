# Quick Definition
The interior of a classical sonata-form development (Caplin's "core" / Hepokoski-Darcy's "central action zone") characteristically projects mounting tension through sequences sustaining an upshift trajectory -- "ingredients of the adrenal concoction" Beethoven injected into developments.

# Core Definition
"The interior of a Classical sonata-form development, which Caplin 1998 calls its core and Hepokoski and Darcy 2006 its central action zone, characteristically projects tonal instability and mounting tension, frequently through one or more sequences that sustain an upshift trajectory" (p. 150). These upshifts are "ingredients of the adrenal concoction with which Beethoven so often injected his developments" (p. 153). The pattern extends from Beethoven's sequential models through Dvorak's freer thematic circulation.

# Key Properties
1. Core = most harmonically active interior of the development
2. Typically features sequences sustaining upshift direction
3. May include flanking downshifts at beginning and end
4. Classical prototype: sequential repetition; Romantic extension: free circulation
5. Acceleration/deceleration of harmonic rhythm as dramatic device

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the development's core (most harmonically active section)
2. Track voice-leading zones through successive structural harmonies
3. Consistent increase indicates upshift trajectory
4. Note flanking downshifts, accelerations, and stalls

# Context & Application
This concept bridges formal analysis (Caplin, Hepokoski-Darcy) with voice-leading zone analysis, showing how the formal function of "developmental core" maps onto the voice-leading script of "continuous upshift."

- **Beethoven "Waldstein"**: Broader arc with acceleration and N/R stall
- **Dvorak "New World"**: Ninety-six-measure upshift through free thematic circulation
