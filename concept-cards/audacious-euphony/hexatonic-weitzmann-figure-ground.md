# Quick Definition
The hexatonic and Weitzmann regions stand in a figure-ground relation: for the hexatonic mariner, Weitzmann regions form bridges between hexatonic pools; for the Weitzmann landlubber, hexatonic regions are rivers to cross.

# Formal Definition
The four hexatonic regions and four Weitzmann regions interlock in a complementary figure-ground configuration. Each hexatonic region is a closed group of six triads connected by **L** and **P** transformations. Each Weitzmann region is a closed group of six triads connected by **R** and **N** transformations. Neither system alone can connect the full universe of twenty-four consonant triads. When combined, hexatonic and Weitzmann transformations gain the "power of perambulation," unifying the triadic universe into a connected system under efficient voice leading.

# Construction/Recognition
Starting from Weitzmann's original figure (reproduced as figure 5.1), observe that:
- Consonant triads displacing an augmented triad downward are positioned to its left
- Those displacing upward are positioned to its right
- Each cluster appears twice; pruning redundancies leaves the twenty-four triads
- Each group of consonant triads between adjacent augmented triads forms a hexatonic region
- The figure is implicitly cyclic: the same triads appear at left and right margins

These observations produce the circular figure 5.2, with augmented triads at cardinal points.

# Musical Context
The figure-ground relation explains why nineteenth-century passages that cross regional boundaries require both hexatonic and Weitzmann transformations. Schubert's Overture to *Die Zauberharfe* exemplifies motion across both systems, as charted on Cube Dance in figure 5.4.

# Common Confusions
- The hexatonic and Weitzmann regions are not hierarchically ordered; they are complementary perspectives on the same space
- Neither system alone connects all twenty-four triads; both are needed for full connectivity
- The figure-ground metaphor is perspectival: which is "figure" depends on the analyst's viewpoint
