# Quick Definition
The transition from the Lento to the agitato core of Chopin's F minor Fantasy, Op. 49, presents an **L**/**R** chain with an interpolated **N** that temporarily reverses the downshift trajectory, demonstrating how a W-group substitution within an **L**/**R** chain can create a momentary retreat before further advance.

# Formal Definition
The passage arpeggiates upward through overtone-distributed triads. An antecedent phrase downshifts through six links of an **L**/**R** chain, connecting f minor to Bb major. The goal triad acquires a seventh and resolves to its *nebenverwandt*, eb minor, from which a consequent phrase **L**/**R**-chains back to tonic f minor. The interpolated **N** (Bb major -> eb minor) functions not to continue the downshift trajectory but to temporarily reverse it: an upshift amidst a cascade of downshifts. This occurs because **N** is interpolated between two W-group progressions (**R**/**N** chain segment connecting g minor to Gb major), causing momentary tarrying within a single W-region.

# Construction/Recognition
- The **N** interpolation occurs between two W-group operations, not between an H and W operation
- This slows the rapid H/W-region turnover characteristic of chromatic sequences
- The internal six triads constitute and exhaust a pitch retention neighborhood around Bb
- On the Tonnetz (figure 5.20), the sharp angle of the jog represents temporary retreat before further advance

# Musical Context
The passage demonstrates a more nuanced use of transformational substitution than simple shortcutting: the **N** creates a dramatic turn that enriches the voice-leading narrative.

# Common Confusions
- The **N** interpolation here reverses rather than continues the trajectory, unlike the Brahms and Chopin Ballade examples
- The sharp jog angle distinguishes this from the gentler file transfers in other substitution examples
- The passage connects to Chapter 6's neighborhood concept through its exhaustion of the Bb neighborhood
