# Quick Definition
Analysis of Brahms's D major Symphony first movement (mm. 102-18) showing an L/R chain traversing nine triads from f# minor to d minor, demonstrating closure by return to original voice-leading zone radius.

# Formal Definition
The passage presents the liquidating consequent of the f# minor subordinate theme, preparing arrival of the dominant. An L/R chain exits the f# minor diatonic system at G-natural, with subsequent C-natural and F-natural creating chromatic alterations of root and fifth. The sequence terminates after nine stations at d minor, which provides closure by returning to the original radius on Cube Dance.

# Construction/Recognition
L/R chain:
f# minor --(L)--> A major --(R)--> a minor --(L)--> C major --(R)--> c minor --(L)--> Eb major --(R)--> eb minor --(L)--> Gb major --(R)--> gb/f# minor...

Actually traversing:
f# -> A -> a -> C -> c -> Eb -> eb -> Gb -> gb -> d

Cube Dance representation (Figure 5.11):
- Starts at 4:00, travels clockwise
- Returns to same radius (4:00) at d minor
- Closure through return to original zone position

Tonnetz representation (Figure 5.10):
- Leftward drift along horizontal axis
- Animated version available at Web animation 5.10

# Musical Context/Application
The analysis demonstrates how d minor brings closure to an L/R chain initiated from f# minor by returning to the original radius on Cube Dance. This "closure through return" is visually explicit on Cube Dance but requires counting on the Tonnetz. The passage exemplifies extended chromatic sequence with systematic voice-leading logic.

# Common Confusions
- The closure is by zone-radius return, not by returning to the starting triad
- The sequence spans nine triads, not a complete 24-triad cycle
- "Free fall in bass" and "free rise in upper voices" describe the voice-leading sensation
