# Quick Definition
The conceptualization of consonant triads as objects within twelve-tone chromatic pitch-class space, where relationships are determined by voice-leading proximity rather than diatonic scale membership.

# Core Definition
Viewing triads in chromatic space means measuring distances and relationships against the chromatic collection of 12 pitch classes rather than against 7-note diatonic collections. This perspective "decline[s] to interpret them in terms of the number of diatonic degrees that separate their root from some tonic" (p. 27). Triadic proximity is assessed by common-tone retention and voice-leading work. This approach adapts the conceptual framework of post-tonal pitch-class theory (1955-1980) to consonant triads, treating them as 3-element subsets of the 12-pc universe.

# Prerequisites
- **Chromatic vs. Diatonic Organization**: The two distance metrics must be understood as independent

# Key Properties
1. Distance measured in semitones (the minimal unit)
2. All 12 pitch classes are equidistant from chromatic neighbors
3. Enharmonic pairs (F#/Gb) are identical
4. The space is cyclic (modulo 12 arithmetic)
5. Triads positioned by voice-leading distances to all other triads
6. Diatonic collections have no privilege in this space

# Construction / Recognition
In chromatic space:
- Distance is measured in semitones
- Triads are positioned by their pitch-class content as 3-element subsets of mod-12 space
- Voice-leading distances and common-tone connections define proximity
- Relations to perfectly even divisions (augmented triads) are structurally significant
- Geometric representations (Tonnetz, Cube Dance, orbifolds) arise naturally

# Context & Application
The chromatic perspective on triads has been cultivated since the late 18th century by theorists dissatisfied with purely diatonic explanations. Galeazzi (1796) used common-tone relations independent of diatonic membership. Krause (1827) and Marx (1841-47) likewise prioritized common tones over diatonic membership. Hauptmann (1853) and Helmholtz (1877) were equally dedicated to common-tone proximity. Cohn adapts atonal pitch-class theory to provide systematic tools for this approach.

# Examples
- **Schubert's enharmonic passages**: triads progress smoothly in chromatic space while creating paradoxes in diatonic space
- **Chromatic sequences**: regular patterns that divide chromatic space evenly (by major thirds, minor thirds, etc.)
- **The Tonnetz**: a geometric representation displaying triads' chromatic relationships

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Chromatic space is merely the diatonic collection "with added notes"
  **Correction**: It is an independent organizing framework with fundamentally different properties

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Chromatic space is "atonal"
  **Clarification**: Triads remain consonant; their organization differs from diatonic tonality

- **Confusion**: Analyzing in chromatic space denies diatonic function
  **Clarification**: It provides an alternative for passages where diatonic analysis fails, without denying diatonic function where it applies
