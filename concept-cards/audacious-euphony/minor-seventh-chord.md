# Quick Definition
In Cohn's framework, the minor seventh chord plays a dual role: it is generated by combining R-related triads (combination strategy) and serves as a bridging chord in 4-Cube Trio, occupying even-numbered voice-leading zones between Boretz regions.

# Core Definition
The **minor seventh chord** has two roles in Cohn's framework. First, under the combination strategy, it is "formed by R-related triads straddling a major-third edge" (p. 160), producing a parallelogram on the Tonnetz. Second, in 4-Cube Trio, minor seventh chords (represented as triangular nodes) serve as bridging chords at the 12:00, 4:00, and 8:00 positions, filling even-numbered voice-leading zones where octatonic transformations leave gaps. "As both [minor seventh and French sixth] fulfill the bridging function independently, either can be removed without disconnecting the graph" (p. 175).

# Prerequisites
- **Combination strategy**: Explains the chord's generation from R-related triads
- **R transformation**: The triadic relationship generating minor seventh through combination

# Key Properties
1. Generated by union of R-related triads: e.g., D minor (D-F-A) + F major (F-A-C) = D-F-A-C
2. Shared edge: major third (F-A)
3. Tonnetz: parallelogram bisected by major-third edge
4. In 4-Cube Trio: triangular nodes at octatonic bridge positions
5. Twelve minor seventh chords exist in the chromatic universe
6. Not a member of the Tristan genus (different set class)

# Construction / Recognition
By combination: Unite two R-related triads sharing a major-third edge
- Example: D minor (D-F-A) + F major (F-A-C) = D-F-A-C

In 4-Cube Trio: Even-zone bridging nodes connecting Tristan-genus chords from adjacent Boretz regions

# Context & Application
Minor seventh chords appear in chromatic passages like the Chopin e minor Prelude, where they bridge gaps between dominant and half-diminished seventh chords, enabling continuous incremental downshifting. The e minor seventh chord at zone 0 marks a structural point in the second phrase.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Including the minor seventh in the Tristan genus
  **Correction**: The Tristan genus contains only V7 and ø7; minor seventh is a distinct set class serving a bridging function

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the minor seventh chord is structurally identical to the dominant seventh in voice-leading terms
  **Clarification**: They belong to different set classes and occupy different positions in 4-Cube Trio (even zones vs. odd zones)
