# Quick Definition
The process by which a diatonic collection becomes a bounded microecological space on the Tonnetz, activating the syntactic and semantic forces of classical tonality through the identification of syntonic images and the closing of the diatonic parallelogram.

# Formal Definition
Diatonic encapsulation occurs when the diatonic collection is represented as a parallelogram on the Tonnetz whose interior is tiled into six consonant triads. The parallelogram "encapsulates its contents into a microecological hothouse, activating the internal dynamics signified by Rings arrows." Within this encapsulated space, triads are hierarchized, intuitions about context-free proximity arise, syntax governs component ordering, and semantics (energy, gravity, attraction, magnetism, desire, leading, return, finality) become active. The blank ear takes a triad as metonym for a diatonic system, provisionally situating it inside its appropriate parallelogram, with standard confirmation through T-S-D-T progression.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Conditions for encapsulation:
- Syntonic images identified at remote corners of parallelogram
- Six consonant triads tile the interior (three major, three minor under classical tonality)
- Central triads sheltered from unstable corners can serve as tonic
- T-S-D-T progression confirms encapsulation

What activates within the capsule:
- Hierarchization of tones and chords
- Context-free proximity intuitions
- Syntax governing component ordering
- Rings arrows with full semantic charge
- The "whole suite of syntactic expectations and semantic predications of the classical microecology"

Metaphors for encapsulation:
- Entering the four walls of home
- Sprouting landing gear and coming within view of a planet
- Exiting freeway and entering fortified village or gated community

# Musical Context/Application
Encapsulation is dynamic and contingent:
- Walls can be erected through cadential confirmation
- Walls dissolve during sequential or chain progressions
- Each tonicized triad within a larger chromatic journey creates a local encapsulation
- A single triad can be encapsulated locally while participating in non-encapsulated chromatic progression globally

The convertible Tonnetz treats diatonic space as "black-box theater, whose walls can be erected, dismantled, and moved without fuss."

# Examples
- Liszt Consolation no. 3: Each hexatonic station (f minor, F major, a minor, A major) is locally encapsulated through tonicization
- At mm. 31-35, a minor is a "diatonically organized, sealed capsule that bobs on the pan-triadic sea"
- Wagner Parsifal Faith Proclamation: eb minor encapsulation halts plagal drift at m. 51
- Forces that erect walls include: reverse hemiola, accented dissonance (F-half-dim-7), flanking by syntonic images
- Brahms Symphony no. 1 finale: Thematic statements on hypermetric downbeats erect diatonic walls; liquidations dissolve them

# Common Confusions
- Encapsulation is not permanent; the same triad can be encapsulated in one phrase and released in another
- A locally encapsulated triad can simultaneously participate in a global chromatic progression
- Encapsulation activates semantic forces; it does not merely identify key membership
- The "blank ear" provisionally encapsulates any heard triad; confirmation or disconfirmation follows
