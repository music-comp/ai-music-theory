# Quick Definition
An **L**/**R** chain is a chromatic sequence generated by alternating Leittonwechsel and Relative transformations, producing transposition by perfect fifth (T5 or T7) and tracing a path along the horizontal axis of the Tonnetz.

# Formal Definition
The **L**/**R** chain alternates one H-group transformation (**L**) with one W-group transformation (**R**), generating a perfect-fifth chromatic sequence. Each pair of transformations produces three units of voice-leading work. The complete chain traverses all twenty-four triads before closing, cycling through diatonic submediants (downshifting via **L**/**R**) or diatonic mediants (upshifting via **R**/**L**). On the Tonnetz, the chain traces a horizontal leftward drift (downshifting) or rightward (upshifting). On Cube Dance, it executes multiple complete rotations.

# Construction/Recognition
Starting from any triad, alternate **L** and **R**:
- f# minor --(**R**)--> D major --(**L**)--> d minor --(**R**)--> Bb major --(**L**)--> bb minor ...
- Each triad progresses to its diatonic submediant (downshifting) or mediant (upshifting)
- The sequence transposes by T5 every two transformations

# Musical Context
The **L**/**R** chain became historically important quite early due to the classically privileged position of the fifth. A circle diagram appears in Werckmeister (1698). Carl Stein recommended piano students learn the entire twenty-four-triad cycle. Moritz Hauptmann made it central to his conception of tonal space.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Ninth Symphony, Scherzo: Traverses nineteen triads from C major to A major, nearly circumnavigating the triadic universe
- Brahms, Symphony No. 2, first movement, mm. 102-118: Downshifting **R**/**L** chain from f# minor, nine stations to d minor (figures 5.9-5.11)
- Brahms, *German Requiem*, second movement, mm. 261-271: Upshifting **L**/**R** chain from bb minor to B major, with **N**-for-**R** substitution (figure 5.15)
- Chopin, Ballade Op. 23: Downshifting **L**/**R** chain with two **N**-for-**R** substitutions

# Common Confusions
- The complete **L**/**R** chain requires all twenty-four triads to close, unlike chains that close after fewer stations
- **N**-for-**R** substitutions can shorten the chain by eight or sixteen stations while preserving voice-leading trajectory
- On the Tonnetz, the chain moves horizontally; on Cube Dance, it spirals through multiple rotations
