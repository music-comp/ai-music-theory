# Quick Definition
The key sequence of Schumann's *Dichterliebe* executes two complete upshifting rotations of voice-leading space across its sixteen songs, with continuous scalar downshift through the first eleven songs realized in triadic space as phased upshifting.

# Formal Definition
Fred Lerdahl (2001) plots *Dichterliebe*'s key sequence as a coherent path through Weber's graph of regions, noting that key signatures of songs 1-8 decrement monotonically. Cohn extends this observation: the continuous downshifting in scalar space through songs 1-11 is realized in triadic space as phased upshifting that continues through to the cycle's end. The boundary harmonies (beginning and ending triads of songs) trace two complete revolutions about Cube Dance. The initial point of cyclic renewal occurs at song 9, the exact halfway point, where A7 matches C#7 from song 1.

# Construction/Recognition
Table 6.1 documents the progression through two rotations:
- Songs 1-8: First rotation, upshifting zones 2 -> 8 with unique downshift at song 5
- Songs 9-16: Second rotation, parallel upshifting pattern
- Each half upshifts 2 -> 8 then introduces a unique downshift
- Final four songs in each group upshift 7 -> 1
- Four withdrawn songs (a, b, A, B) restore the original twenty-song conception

The cycle echoes Schumann's Op. 24 *Liederkreis* (also Heine settings), whose nine songs complete a similar upshifting rotation.

# Musical Context
The analysis reveals that *Dichterliebe*'s key sequence, often debated in terms of organic unity, has a coherent structure when viewed through voice-leading zones rather than conventional tonal analysis. E. T. A. Hoffmann's *Kreisleriana* provides a possible source for Schumann's interest in patterned upshifting.

# Common Confusions
- Scalar downshift (key signatures losing sharps/gaining flats) is opposite to triadic upshift; the two track different aspects of tonal motion
- The two rotations are not precisely pace-matched but share structural parallels
- The four withdrawn songs strengthen the parallel between the two halves
