# Quick Definition
A visual metaphor depicting a Weitzmann region as a water bug with an augmented-triad body and six consonant-triad legs (three on each side representing modally matched subregions), illustrating how all intra-regional progressions route through the augmented triad.

# Core Definition
Figure 4.1(a) presents "a water bug with augmented-triad body and six consonant-triad feet, the three on each side representing the modally matched subregions. All journeys from one foot to another are routed through the body" (p. 78). Figure 4.1(b) "reimagines the water bug as an oval hallway with three rooms on each side," where the augmented triad is the hallway through which travelers pass between rooms. "The augmented triad may or may not be marked as such: some music will linger there, some will invite passing notice of its features, and some will rush quickly through the passageway without registering any impression of it" (p. 78).

# Prerequisites
- **Weitzmann region**: The structure the metaphor represents
- **Weitzmann region structure**: The flat-terrain property that the metaphor visualizes

# Key Properties
1. Body = augmented triad; feet/legs = six consonant triads
2. Three major triads on one side, three minor triads on the other
3. All journeys between feet route through the body
4. Three journey types: same side, directly opposite, across at angle
5. All three types are structurally equivalent (2 voice-leading units)

# Construction / Recognition
Three ways of experiencing the augmented-triad passageway (p. 78):
- **Firefighter**: "Execute each [trajectory] with a single kinetic impulse" -- augmented triad not registered
- **Professor**: "Experience the hallway as a marked intermediate point" -- augmented triad noticed
- **Retiree**: "Linger in the hallway for a while" -- augmented triad prolonged

# Context & Application
The water bug/hallway metaphors make the flat-terrain structure of Weitzmann regions intuitive. They capture both the structural equality of all intra-regional progressions and the variable compositional treatment of the augmented triad intermediary.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Taking the metaphor too literally
  **Correction**: It represents voice-leading relationships, not physical distances

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The "body" must be sounded as a chord
  **Clarification**: The augmented triad may be sounded, briefly noted, or passed through imperceptibly
