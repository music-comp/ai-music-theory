# Quick Definition
A nearly even three-tone chord, either major or minor, that constitutes the primary harmonic object in both classical diatonic tonality and pan-triadic syntax.

# Formal Definition
A consonant triad (equivalent to "harmonic triad"; German: *Klang*) is a three-note chord that divides the octave nearly evenly, consisting of a major third and a minor third (in either order). The 24 consonant triads - 12 major and 12 minor - are the central harmonic objects of *Audacious Euphony*. They possess two distinct "natures": acoustic consonance (generating classical diatonic syntax) and near-evenness (generating pan-triadic syntax). Throughout the book, "triad" in its unmodified form refers restrictively to consonant triads.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Structure:
- Major triad: root + M3 (4 semitones) + m3 (3 semitones) = 0, 4, 7
- Minor triad: root + m3 (3 semitones) + M3 (4 semitones) = 0, 3, 7
- Both divide 12 semitones into 3 + 4 + 5 or 4 + 3 + 5

Properties:
- "Nearly even": as close to equal division as possible without being perfectly even
- Each consonant triad is one semitone away from exactly one augmented triad
- 24 total (12 major + 12 minor) in 12-tone equal temperament

Notation in the book:
- Major: uppercase (C, G, Eb) or plus sign (C+)
- Minor: lowercase (c, g, eb) or minus sign (c-)

# Musical Context/Application
Consonant triads serve as:
- The exclusive harmonic vocabulary of pan-triadic passages
- Nodes in all geometric representations (Tonnetz, Cube Dance)
- The domain over which transformations (P, L, R, etc.) operate
- The link between classical and romantic harmonic syntax

The book's central argument is that consonant triads have properties beyond acoustic consonance that enable a distinct mode of harmonic organization.

# Examples
- The 24 consonant triads form 4 hexatonic regions, 4 Weitzmann regions, 12 neighborhoods
- All examples in the book involve progressions of consonant triads
- Augmented and diminished triads are excluded from the "consonant triad" category

# Common Confusions
- "Consonant triad" excludes augmented and diminished triads (which are "dissonant" or have special structural roles)
- Major and minor triads are acoustically generated (first nature) AND geometrically near-even (second nature)
- The German *Klang* is used synonymously
- The term emphasizes acoustic consonance while the book explores properties beyond consonance
