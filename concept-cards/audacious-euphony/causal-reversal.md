# Quick Definition
The phenomenon where the direction of hierarchical subordination between musical elements reverses -- as when a tone changes from neighbor to main note, or a triad changes from being subordinate to tonic to being superordinate to its constituent tones -- and this reversal is cognitively opaque.

# Core Definition
Causal reversal describes the flip in hierarchical relationships accompanying syntactic switching. In diatonic syntax: chromatic (12+) -> diatonic (7) -> triad (3) -> tonic (1). In chromatic syntax: chromatic (12) <- triad (3) <- tone (1). "It is as if at one moment the person wears the garment and at the next the garment wears the person" (p. 223). The reversal explains "why double syntax encounters such a deep vein of resistance": advocates "are suggesting that all of the relations of subordination, causality, and orientation undergo a reversal" (p. 223).

# Prerequisites
- **Double syntax hypothesis**: The framework within which reversal occurs
- **Cognitive opacity**: The mechanism allowing reversal to occur unconsciously

# Key Properties
1. All relations of subordination, causality, and orientation reverse simultaneously
2. The reversal is cognitively opaque -- occurring beneath awareness
3. It explains why double syntax faces such strong resistance
4. A "cunning composer or crafty performer" can exploit this opacity
5. Quotidian examples demonstrate the ubiquity of opaque causal reversal

# Construction / Recognition
Musical manifestations (p. 224):
- G is neighbor to Ab; Ab becomes neighbor to G (Parsifal opening)
- Eb major is dominant of Ab; Ab becomes subdominant of Eb
- Arsis and thesis flip: strong beats become weak, vice versa
- Triad as acoustic object (subordinate to tonic) becomes voice-leading object (superordinate to tones)

Quotidian examples (p. 223): "On Tuesday, you drive your car to town for a tune-up... In the first case, you take the car. If the car could get to the shop without you, you would gladly send it. In the second case, the car takes you... Yet, from a cognitive standpoint, one hardly notices the difference."

# Context & Application
Causal reversal is the deeper phenomenon underlying arrow reversal. It explains not just the technical change in hierarchical direction but the psychological resistance to accepting double syntax and the cognitive resources that make it feasible despite that resistance.

# Examples
- **Parsifal opening**: G/Ab neighbor reversal; Eb/Ab dominant-subdominant reversal (p. 224)
- **Schubert Scherzo**: At m. 117, tones cease orienting toward C major and begin orienting toward chromatic collection
- **Driving analogy**: Taking car to shop vs. car taking you to barber (p. 223)
- **Power/money**: "Do you want money to get power, or power to get money?" (p. 223)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Causal reversal is audible as a discrete event

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Causal reversal is the same as functional ambiguity
  **Clarification**: It is a determinate change of direction, not ambiguity
