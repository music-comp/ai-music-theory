# Quick Definition
A melodic fulcrum is a pitch position that mediates between two diatonic interpretations, such as the semitone between a dominant's fifth and its raised fifth (augmented), upon which the tension between diatonic and chromatic syntax is balanced.

# Formal Definition
When two harmonic interpretations depend on the position of a single pitch (whether it is natural or chromatically altered), that pitch position functions as a melodic fulcrum. The fulcrum represents the point where diatonic logic (conforming to a scale) and chromatic logic (uniform transposition) diverge. Composers can treat this fulcrum as a site for motivic play, emphasizing the tension between the two interpretations.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Examples of melodic fulcrums:

1. Dominant fifth vs. augmented fifth:
- In E major: B (fifth of dominant) vs. B# (making augmented triad)
- The fulcrum is at B/B#

2. Leittonwechsel relation (C major / e minor):
- C is leading tone to C# (upper neighbor to B as dominant)
- B is leading tone up to C
- The fulcrum is at B/C

3. Nebenverwandt relation (C major / f minor):
- Two fulcrums: E/Eb and G/Ab
- E presses up (as leading tone to F in F major)
- Ab presses down (as leading tone to G in c minor)

The classroom scenario (p. 68):
Two students replicate C-E starting on E:
- One responds E-G# (raw uniformity, augmented triad)
- One responds E-G (tempered to diatonic, consonant triad)
The G/G# fulcrum balances uniformity against diatonicity.

# Musical Context/Application
Composers of the nineteenth century treated melodic fulcrums as sites for motivic play, highlighting the tension between diatonic and chromatic interpretations. Extended passages may oscillate around a fulcrum, keeping both harmonic interpretations in play.

# Examples
From the text (pp. 67-70):

Beethoven, "Appassionata" Sonata, Op. 57:
- Secondary theme features Eb/Fb motivic play (mm. 42-65)
- Development traverses hexatonic cycle featuring D#-E motion on successive downbeats
- "The entire passage prolongs Ab major by displacing its fifth Eb to its augmented fifth E and then restoring it"

Schubert, Piano Sonata D. 959, first movement:
- Exposition features B/C displacements as melodic fulcrum
- Development opens with oscillation between C major and B major
- "B thus performs the role of lower neighbor to its own upper neighbor, magnifying the melodic fulcrum introduced with the equal divisions of the exposition"

Charles Fisk's description of D. 959 development: "The music simply oscillates between C and B, achieving what [Charles] Rosen characterizes as a stasis with a 'physical effect... like nothing in music before.'"

# Common Confusions
- A melodic fulcrum is not simply any chromatic pitch; it is specifically the point where two interpretations diverge
- The fulcrum may not be audible as such; it is an analytical concept
- Multiple fulcrums may operate in a passage (as in nebenverwandt relations)
- Oscillation around a fulcrum creates ambiguity, not necessarily resolution
