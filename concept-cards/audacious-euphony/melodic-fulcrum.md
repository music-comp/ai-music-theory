# Quick Definition
A melodic fulcrum is a pitch position that mediates between two harmonic interpretations, such as the semitone between a dominant's fifth and its augmented fifth, upon which the tension between diatonic and chromatic syntax is balanced.

# Core Definition
When two harmonic interpretations depend on the position of a single pitch (whether natural or chromatically altered), that pitch position functions as a melodic fulcrum. The fulcrum represents the point where diatonic logic (conforming to a scale) and chromatic logic (uniform transposition) diverge. Cohn identifies it in the classroom scenario where "G-natural and G# displace each other across the melodic fulcrum upon which the diatonality/uniformity tension is balanced, in the same way that the same two tones constitute the modal fulcrum in the case of an E tonic, or the melodic fulcrum in a Leittonwechsel relation between c minor and Ab major, or one of two such fulcrums in the nebenverwandt relation between C major and f minor" (p. 68).

# Prerequisites
- **Consonant triad**: One side of the fulcrum produces a consonant triad (diatonic channeling)
- **Augmented triad**: The other side produces an augmented triad (uniformity)

# Key Properties
1. The fulcrum is a specific pitch position where two interpretations diverge
2. Composers can treat the fulcrum as a "site for motivic play"
3. The Leittonwechsel relation has one fulcrum (semitone); the nebenverwandt has two (both agents)
4. Extended passages may oscillate around a fulcrum, keeping both interpretations in play
5. The fulcrum concept links the diatonic/chromatic tension to specific compositional technique

# Construction / Recognition
Examples of melodic fulcrums:
1. **Diatonic/chromatic**: G vs. G# in the key of C (consonant E-G vs. augmented E-G#)
2. **Leittonwechsel** (C major / e minor): B/C fulcrum
3. **Nebenverwandt** (C major / f minor): Two fulcrums at E/Eb and G/Ab

# Context & Application
Composers of the 19th century treated melodic fulcrums as sites for motivic play, highlighting the tension between diatonic and chromatic interpretations. This technique appears prominently in Beethoven's "Appassionata" and Schubert's D. 959 Sonata.

# Examples
**Beethoven, "Appassionata" Sonata, Op. 57** (pp. 67-68): The secondary theme features Eb/Fb motivic play (mm. 42-65). "The entire passage prolongs Ab major by displacing its fifth Eb to its augmented fifth E and then restoring it."

**Schubert, Piano Sonata D. 959, 1st mvt.** (pp. 68-70): The exposition features B/C displacements. The development opens with oscillation between C major and B major. Charles Fisk describes it as "a stasis with a 'physical effect... like nothing in music before'" (quoting Rosen 1980). "B thus performs the role of lower neighbor to its own upper neighbor, magnifying the melodic fulcrum introduced with the equal divisions of the exposition" (p. 70).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Identifying any chromatic pitch as a melodic fulcrum
  **Correction**: A fulcrum is specifically the point where two interpretations diverge, not just any chromatic alteration

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The fulcrum is an audible feature
  **Clarification**: It is an analytical concept; the fulcrum may not be perceived as such without analytical attention
- **Confusion**: Oscillation around a fulcrum resolves ambiguity
  **Clarification**: Oscillation creates and sustains ambiguity rather than resolving it
