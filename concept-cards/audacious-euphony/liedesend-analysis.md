# Quick Definition
Schubert's 1816 song "Liedesend'" demonstrates a departure-return scheme realized as a palindromic motion of voice-leading zones across nine strophes, where George Grove's "inscrutable modulatory practice" reveals palindromic logic when analyzed through zones.

# Core Definition
The song is sectionalized and episodic, with each strophe having its own mood, tempo, and often key signature. "George Grove selected [it] as an example of Schubert's inscrutable modulatory practice" (Clark 2011b, cited p. 146). Figure 6.13 reveals the structure: "Minor tonics arise three times: at the song's beginning, conclusion, and palindromic center. Connecting the c minor beginning to the f minor center is a series of major triads that cascade from zone 8 down to 11; connecting the center to the e minor conclusion is a series of major triads that upshift through the same zones in reverse order" (p. 147). The palindrome is "ruffled only by the D major conclusion of the eighth stanza" (p. 147).

# Prerequisites
- **Departure-return script**: The compositional framework
- **Zonal palindrome**: The abstract level of symmetry

# Key Properties
1. Nine strophes with independent moods and tempos
2. Palindromic zonal structure: descent 8 -> 11, center at f minor, ascent 11 -> 8
3. Minor tonics at beginning (c minor), center (f minor), and end (e minor)
4. D major perturbation in stanza VIII defers but does not defeat palindrome
5. Closes in a key (e minor) first introduced at the final couplet

# Context & Application
The analysis shows that modulatory schemes can have non-monotonal logic. "The palindromic voice leading suggests that the modulatory scheme need not be deemed arbitrary even if its logic is not monotonal" (p. 148). Any attempt to organize tonics around a single center would be "tendentious to an extreme degree" (p. 148).

# Examples
- The D major perturbation at stanza VIII "reprises and ultimately reverses . . . the exact triadic succession that occurred at the conclusion of [stanza I]" (p. 147)
- Suzannah Clark's common-tone modulation analysis is extended by the zonal reading

# Common Errors
- **Error**: The song is tonally incoherent
  **Correction**: It has coherent palindromic zonal structure, just not monotonal logic

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The palindrome must be exact
  **Clarification**: Minor "rufflings" (like D major in stanza VIII) do not invalidate the overall structure
