# Quick Definition
Cohn's extended analysis of Chopin's e minor Prelude, Op. 28 no. 4, demonstrates how its chromatic voice leading circumnavigates 4-Cube Trio, engaging both odd and even voice-leading zones, with three competing analytical interpretations (Schenkerian, harmonic, hybrid).

# Core Definition
The **Chopin e minor Prelude analysis** models the Prelude as a circumnavigation of 4-Cube Trio. "Even-numbered stations are integrated more comprehensively" than in passages limited to the Tristan genus (p. 179). Three interpretive approaches are presented: **Schenkerian** (Schachter 1988: events between chromatic spans bear no harmonic value); **harmonic** (Lerdahl 2001: downshifting tritones summon unrealized tonics); **hybrid** (Gauldin 2004: E7 at m. 4 as interior structural pillar). The first phrase descends "a total of fifteen semitones: four in each lower voice and three in the cantus" (p. 180), segmented into three phases with different pacing.

# Prerequisites
- **4-Cube Trio**: The voice-leading space being circumnavigated
- **Voice-leading zones**: The metric tracking the descent
- **Circumnavigation**: The process being demonstrated

# Key Properties
1. First phrase: 15-semitone descent in three segments with different pacing
2. Engages both odd zones (Tristan-genus chords) and even zones (diminished/minor sevenths, French sixths)
3. Path self-intersects on 4-Cube Trio at the B-D-F-G# region
4. Second phrase retraces first phrase's course but leaps across ("Gulliver stride") rather than traversing chromatically
5. The "omnitonal haze" resolves when G# "remembers its monotonal responsibilities" (p. 180)

# Construction / Recognition
First phrase three segments (Figure 7.24):
1. Mm. 1-4: 4 semitones in 2 measures (moderate pace)
2. Mm. 4-5: 5 semitones in 1 measure (fast, skipping odd zones)
3. Mm. 5-8: 4 semitones in 3 measures (slow, with accelerations before cadential points)

Leading-tone behavior (Figure 7.20):
- Successive tritones summon tonics along line of fifths (E, A, D, G)
- Each leading tone resists resolution, descending instead -- "a triumph of gravity and inertia over magnetism" (Larson 1994) (p. 180)

# Context & Application
The analysis illustrates the coexistence of diatonic framing and chromatic voice leading. The denizens of 4-Cube Trio, "like those of Cube Dance, are overdetermined creatures, homophonous diamorphs through which composers and listeners can rapidly and radically exchange syntactic expectations" (pp. 182-183). This sets up the syntactic exchange theory of Chapter 8.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Privileging one analytical interpretation over others
  **Correction**: Cohn presents all three (Schenkerian, harmonic, hybrid) as valid and complementary

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the circumnavigation is the composer's conscious procedure
  **Clarification**: The circumnavigation is an analytical observation about voice-leading structure, not a claim about compositional intent
