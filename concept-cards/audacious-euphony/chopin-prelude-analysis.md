# Quick Definition
Cohn's extended analysis of Chopin's e minor Prelude, Op. 28 no. 4, demonstrates how the e minor Prelude's chromatic voice leading can be modeled on 4-Cube Trio, showing circumnavigation of tetrachordal voice-leading space through dominant, half-diminished, diminished seventh, and minor seventh chords.

# Formal Definition
The **Chopin Prelude analysis** models the e minor Prelude's first phrase as a circumnavigation of 4-Cube Trio. The analysis demonstrates:
1. Chromatic linear progressions connecting structural consonances
2. Engagement of even-numbered voice-leading zones (via diminished and minor seventh chords)
3. Multiple interpretive approaches (Schenkerian, harmonic, hybrid)
4. The relationship between Tristan-genus progressions and tonal framing

The prelude serves as an extended case study for tetrachordal voice-leading theory.

# Construction/Recognition
Structure of the first phrase:
- Total descent: 15 semitones (4 per lower voice, 3 in cantus)
- Three segments with different pacing:
  1. Mm. 1-4: 4 semitones in 2 measures (zone 6 → zone 2)
  2. Mm. 4-5: 5 semitones in 1 measure (faster, skipping odd zones)
  3. Mm. 5-8: 4 semitones in 3 measures (slower, with accelerations)

Path on 4-Cube Trio:
- Circumnavigates the graph
- Self-intersects at B-D-F-G# region
- Engages minor seventh and diminished seventh bridging chords
- Returns to tonal frame at structural points

# Musical Context
The analysis illustrates:
- How diatonic framing coexists with chromatic voice leading
- Multiple valid analytical perspectives on the same passage
- The "omnitonal haze" quality of leading tones that don't resolve
- Voice-leading zones as neutral descriptive tools

Three analytical approaches:
1. **Schenkerian**: Linear span connecting structural consonances
2. **Harmonic**: Tritones summon unrealized tonics (Lerdahl)
3. **Hybrid**: E7 at m. 4 as interior structural pillar (Gauldin)

# Examples
**First phrase (mm. 1-9)**: Chromatic descent from e minor through V7, ø7, diminished seventh, and minor seventh chords to structural subdominant. The phrase crosses zone 2 twice, creating a self-intersecting path on 4-Cube Trio.

**Second phrase (mm. 13-25)**: Retraces the first phrase's course but arrives at E7 a half-measure early, then leaps across voice-leading space (the "Gulliver stride") rather than traversing it chromatically.

**Leading tone behavior**: Each tritone summons a potential tonic (E, A, D, G, etc.); each leading tone resists resolution and descends, creating an "omnitonal haze" before the tonal reorientation.

# Common Confusions
- The analysis does not privilege one interpretation over others; all three are valid
- The chromatic passage is framed by diatonic conventions, not independent of them
- The "circumnavigation" is analytical, not the composer's conscious procedure
- The prelude demonstrates coexistence of tonal and voice-leading logics
