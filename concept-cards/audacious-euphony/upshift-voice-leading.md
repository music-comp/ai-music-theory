# Quick Definition
Voice leading in which all moving voices proceed upward under idealized voice leading, associated with transformations like Parallel (P) from minor to major.

# Formal Definition
Upshift voice leading occurs when, under idealized voice leading, all voices that move between two chords proceed in the ascending direction. Stationary voices (common tones) are permissible, but no voice moves downward. This contrasts with downshift voice leading (all movement downward) and balanced voice leading (some voices up, some down). The direction of voice motion has been connected to discussions of harmonic dualism and the phenomenology of major/minor contrast.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
For a connection from triad X to triad Y:
- Sum of directed voice-leading intervals >= 0
- Each moving voice moves by a positive (ascending) interval
- Common tones contribute 0 to the sum

Examples:
- c minor to C major (P): Eb ascends to E, C and G stationary = upshift
- e minor to E major (P): G ascends to G#, E and B stationary = upshift

Upshift voice leading characterizes:
- P transformations from minor to major triads
- Certain sequential patterns with consistent ascending motion

# Musical Context/Application
The distinction between upshift and downshift:
- Relates to Riemann's harmonic dualism (major as "upward," minor as "downward")
- Cohn reframes this as "a particular instance of a more fundamental phenomenon, the relationship between upward and downward melodic motion"
- Affects the perceived directionality of progressions
- Can characterize entire passages or sequences

The upshift/downshift distinction provides a refinement of voice-leading analysis beyond simple distance measurement.

# Examples
- c minor to C major: upshift (Eb to E)
- The P transformation from any minor triad to its parallel major is always upshift
- Sequences with consistent ascending chromatic lines exhibit upshift character
- Continuous upshift involves extended passages of unidirectional voice motion

# Common Confusions
- Upshift refers to pitch-class direction, not registral direction in actual music
- An upshift progression can be realized with descending registers in composition
- Upshift is not necessarily "brighter" or more "major" - it describes voice-leading direction
- The concept applies to the connection between chords, not to chords in isolation
