# Quick Definition
The fundamental tension between diatonic channeling (conforming to a governing scale) and the law of uniformity (exact transposition preserving interval sizes), whose interaction determines whether a passage remains diatonic or becomes chromatic.

# Core Definition
When a motivic fragment is transposed, two forces compete: diatonic channeling adjusts specific intervals to fit the governing scale while preserving generic intervals, while the law of uniformity preserves exact interval sizes at the expense of diatonic conformity. Cohn illustrates with a classroom scenario: "Two students are presented with a melodic gesture from C up to E and asked to replicate that gesture beginning on E. One responds with E up to G#, projecting an augmented triad; the second with E up to G, projecting a consonant one. Both responses are correct, but one interprets replication as raw uniformity; the other, as tempered to the diatonic collection" (p. 68).

# Prerequisites
- **Consonant triad**: Represents the diatonic channeling side of the tension (E-G)
- **Augmented triad**: Represents the uniformity side of the tension (E-G#)

# Key Properties
1. In diatonic sequences, uniformity is "kept in check" by the diatonic scale
2. In chromatic sequences, "the law of uniformity has a monolithic force" (p. 67)
3. The tension manifests whenever a motivic fragment migrates across transpositional levels
4. Later theorists formulate the same duality as diatony vs. repetition (Schenker 1906) and magnetism vs. inertia (Larson 1994)
5. G-natural and G# "displace each other across the melodic fulcrum upon which the diatonality/uniformity tension is balanced" (p. 68)

# Construction / Recognition
The classroom scenario reveals the tension at its most basic:
- C to E (major third) replicated from E
- **Diatonic channeling**: E to G (minor third, conforming to C major scale) = consonant triad
- **Law of uniformity**: E to G# (major third, preserving interval) = augmented triad
- The G/G# fulcrum is where the two logics diverge

# Context & Application
This tension underlies the relationship between consonant and augmented triads throughout the book. Passages that privilege uniformity over diatonic channeling produce chromatic sequences (hexatonic cycles, major-third divisions) where augmented triads emerge as structural centers.

# Examples
**Classroom scenario** (p. 68): Two students replicate C-E from E; one produces E-G# (uniformity), the other E-G (diatonic channeling).

**Fetis on diatonic sequences** (p. 66): "The law of uniformity is kept in check by the prior commitment to the diatonic scale."

**Chromatic sequences** (p. 67): "In these cases, the law of uniformity has a monolithic force, and the rapid turnover of chromatic pitch classes ruptures any ability of the diatonic collection to hold a focus on a particular global tonic."

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating diatonic channeling and uniformity as mutually exclusive
  **Correction**: In diatonic sequences, both operate simultaneously, with channeling constraining uniformity

- **Error**: Equating chromatic sequences with "lawlessness"
  **Correction**: Chromatic sequences follow the law of uniformity more purely than diatonic ones

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The distinction is between "tonal" and "atonal"
  **Clarification**: Both modes of organization use consonant triads; the difference is in how transposition interacts with scale
- **Confusion**: The tension is unique to 19th-century music
  **Clarification**: It is foregrounded when composers privilege uniformity, but the tension exists whenever motives are transposed
