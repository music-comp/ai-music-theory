# Quick Definition
The combination strategy generates dissonant harmonies from the union of two consonant triads, producing tetrachords that occupy determinate parallelogram positions on the Tonnetz.

# Formal Definition
**Combination** is a strategy for generating dissonant harmonies from the union of two consonant triads, each contributing some or all of its constituent tones. The strategy was developed by Rameau (1760) and elaborated by numerous nineteenth-century theorists.

Combination produces tetrachords that:
1. Unite two edge-adjacent triads on the Tonnetz
2. Form parallelograms from their two triangles
3. Have compact, determinate Tonnetz locations

Historical theorists using combination:
- Rameau (1760): major and minor seventh chords combine two complete triads
- Hauptmann (1853): major and minor sevenths
- Helmholtz: major/minor sevenths; minor triads as "clouded" combinations of three major triads
- Riemann: theories of dissonant harmony
- Capellen (1908): hybridization (Doppelklang)
- Schenker: French sixth chord analysis

# Construction/Recognition
Three tetrachords generated by combination of edge-adjacent triads:

1. **Major seventh chord** = union of L-related triads
   - Example: C major + E minor = C-E-G + E-G-B = C-E-G-B
   - Shared edge: E-G (minor third)
   - Tonnetz: parallelogram bisected by minor-third edge

2. **Minor seventh chord** = union of R-related triads
   - Example: D minor + F major = D-F-A + F-A-C = D-F-A-C
   - Shared edge: F-A (major third)
   - Tonnetz: parallelogram bisected by major-third edge

3. **Split-third chord [0347]** = union of P-related triads
   - Example: C major + C minor = C-E-G + C-Eb-G = C-Eb-E-G
   - Shared edge: C-G (perfect fifth)
   - Lendvai's "alpha" sonority in Bartok
   - Tonnetz: parallelogram bisected by fifth edge

# Musical Context
Combination is useful when:
- A tetrachord mediates between two triads sharing an edge
- The dissonance functions to connect rather than resolve
- Compact Tonnetz representation is analytically valuable

Most dissonant harmonies are represented on the Tonnetz by irregular, sprawling, or disconnected shapes. Combination-generated tetrachords are exceptions with compact parallelogram locations.

# Examples
**Schreker's Kammersymphonie (1916)**: A C# split-third chord fills a gap left by the juxtaposition of hexatonic poles A major and f minor. The split-third shares two common tones with each adjacent triad, functioning as a combinational bridge.

**Rameau's identification**: Major seventh = combination of C major and E minor (Rameau, Code pratique, 1760)

**Hauptmann's analysis**: Minor seventh chords as combinations of R-related triads, supporting the "dual" nature of the chord.

# Common Confusions
- Combination generates only three specific tetrachord types from edge-adjacent triads
- Most seventh chords do NOT have compact Tonnetz locations (dominant seventh, half-diminished, etc.)
- Combination differs from reduction: combination unites two triads; reduction isolates one from a larger chord
- The split-third chord is rare in common practice but prominent in twentieth-century music (Bartok)
