# Quick Definition
The opening of the development from Bruckner's Third Symphony connects f minor to a minor through two transpositionally related phrases using **L**/**R** chains with **N**-for-**R** substitutions and interpolated **PL** transformations, demonstrating how balanced voice leading can modify the trajectory of a chromatic sequence.

# Formal Definition
Each phrase of the development begins with slowly evolving soundsheet arpeggios that upshift across four stations of an **L**/**R** chain and ends with an **N** substitution carrying a major dominant to a minor tonic. The phrases are transpositionally related by whole step rather than tritone (as in the Chopin Ballade) because a **PL** transformation, outfitted as motion from a major tonic to its German sixth, is interpolated prior to the **N** substitution.

# Construction/Recognition
1. **L**/**R** chain upshifts through four stations
2. **PL** transformation (major tonic to German sixth) causes balanced voice leading, pausing the upshifting trajectory
3. The **PL** balanced voice leading causes the progression to linger within a Weitzmann region (e.g., Gb major to D major to g minor)
4. **N** substitution carries dominant to tonic
5. Combined phrases traverse all four major-third alleys (right to left on the Tonnetz)

# Musical Context
The passage demonstrates how interpolation of balanced voice leading (**PL**) within a predominantly unidirectional sequence can modify the net transposition between phrases. The result is a whole-step relationship rather than the tritone that would obtain without the **PL** interpolation.

# Common Confusions
- The **PL** interpolation is what causes the whole-step (rather than tritone) transposition between phrases
- The **PL** operates as "balanced" voice leading, pausing the directional trajectory momentarily
- This is retrograded on the Tonnetz relative to the Chopin Ballade because it begins with a minor rather than major triad
