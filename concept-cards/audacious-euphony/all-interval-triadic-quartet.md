# Quick Definition
A quartet of triads whose voice-leading zones form an all-interval tetrachord, distributing their six pairwise intervals evenly across all six voice-leading interval classes for maximum entropy.

# Formal Definition
An all-interval triadic quartet is an ensemble of four triads whose voice-leading zones are homologous to one of the two all-interval tetrachords: prime form [0146] or [0137]. Since the eight consonant-triad zones have octatonic structure, and all-interval tetrachords are octatonic subsets, such quartets exist within the consonant triadic system. The quartets achieve maximum variety along the conjunct/disjunct spectrum.

# Construction/Recognition
All-interval tetrachord types (Morris 1990):
- Any nonintersecting union of a tritone with a minor third
- Prime forms: [0146] and [0137]

Triadic realization:
- Two zone-diametric (T2 or T6 related) triads of one mode
- Two triads of opposite mode related by odd transposition (3 zones apart)
- Combined: one tritone + one minor third in zone space

Example [0146]:
- {g minor, Eb major, A major, d minor}
- Zones: {7, 8, 2, 4}
- Prime form: [0146]

Example [0137]:
- {eb minor, Cb major, db minor, D major}
- Zones: {7, 8, 1, 5}
- Prime form: [0137]

# Musical Context/Application
These quartets achieve maximum entropy within the triadic system, with pairwise intervals distributed across all six classes. The concept connects atonal pitch-class theory (all-interval tetrachords) with triadic voice-leading analysis, suggesting that poststructural approaches to 19th-century analysis can be undergirded by systematic theory.

# Examples
- Beethoven "Tempest" Sonata finale, mm. 8-12: {d minor, g minor, Eb major, A major} = [0146]
- Wagner Gotterdammerung opening: {eb minor, Cb major, db minor, D major} = [0137]

# Common Confusions
- The zones are homologous to all-interval tetrachords, not the pitch classes themselves
- Both tetrachord types can be realized as triadic quartets
- This is a voice-leading property, not a pitch-class set property of the triads
