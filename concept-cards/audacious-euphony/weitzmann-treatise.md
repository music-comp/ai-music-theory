# Quick Definition
Carl Friedrich Weitzmann's 1853 monograph "Der ubermassige Dreiklang" (The Augmented Triad) is the first comprehensive treatment of the augmented triad's properties and compositional potential, anticipating key concepts of Neo-Riemannian theory.

# Formal Definition
Weitzmann's treatise presents three "genesis narratives" for the augmented triad, moving from its local emergence in specific progressions, to its systematic position relative to keys, to its generative role in producing consonant triads. The treatise advocates for "granting [the augmented triad] an abiding place in the kingdom of tones" and explores its potential for enharmonic reinterpretation and voice-leading connection to consonant triads.

# Construction/Recognition
Weitzmann's three genesis narratives (using entstehen/Entstehung):

1. Chapter 2 - "Preparation, Origin, and Introduction":
How an augmented triad "comes to life" at a particular moment, "prepared through major and minor triads and their inversions." Presents 16 ways to connect a consonant triad to an augmented one via semitonal voice leading.

2. Chapter 6 - "Natural Origin of the Augmented Triad Most Important to Each Key":
Combines nebenverwandt chords (e.g., f minor and C major) into a pentachord F-Ab-C-E-G, extracting the augmented triad from its interior: F[Ab-C-E]G.

3. Chapter 7 and Figure 3.9 - Augmented triads generate consonant triads:
"From the following augmented triads... arise the [consonant] triads indicated by the letters next to them." Each augmented triad is placed at the center of a cluster showing its relationships to six consonant triads.

Table 3.1 shows Weitzmann's grouping of all 24 consonant triads as displacements of four augmented triads.

# Musical Context/Application
Weitzmann's treatise provides the historical foundation for understanding augmented triads as structural generators rather than mere passing dissonances. His figure 3.9 (translated in the text) prefigures the modern understanding of hexatonic systems, with each augmented triad at the center of a six-triad cluster.

# Examples
From the text (pp. 73-76):

Weitzmann's Table 3.1 organizing triads by augmented triad:
- {C, E, G#}: C major, E major, Ab major, a minor, c# minor, f minor
- {Db, F, A}: Db major, F major, A major, bb minor, d minor, f# minor
- {D, F#, A#}: D major, Gb major, Bb major, b minor, eb minor, g minor
- {Eb, G, B}: Eb major, G major, B major, c minor, e minor, g# minor

Figure 3.9 (translated): "Each augmented triad is presented at the center of a cluster of consonant triads... The chords placed immediately next to the augmented triad are attained through the half-step progression of one of their voices; the [chords] further away [are attained] through the half-step progression of two of their voices."

# Common Confusions
- Weitzmann's term "genesis" (Entstehung) can mean either compositional emergence or systematic generation; context determines which
- The third genesis narrative inverts the direction of the second - augmented triads generate consonant triads, not vice versa
- Weitzmann maintained the moment-to-moment priority of consonance even while recognizing the systematic priority of the augmented triad
- The treatise advocated for compositional use, not just theoretical recognition
