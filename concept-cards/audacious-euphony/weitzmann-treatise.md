# Quick Definition
Carl Friedrich Weitzmann's 1853 monograph *Der ubermassige Dreiklang* (The Augmented Triad) is the first comprehensive treatment of the augmented triad's properties and compositional potential, anticipating key concepts of neo-Riemannian theory.

# Core Definition
Weitzmann's treatise presents three "genesis narratives" for the augmented triad, moving from its local emergence in specific progressions, to its systematic position relative to keys, to its generative role in producing consonant triads. The treatise advocates for "granting [the augmented triad] an abiding place in the kingdom of tones" (Weitzmann 2004 [1853], 144; p. 61). Cohn declares that "these passages from Weitzmann's treatise are so rich in implication that they guide the work presented in the next three chapters of this book" (p. 76).

# Prerequisites
- **Augmented triad**: The subject of the treatise
- **Nebenverwandt**: A key concept Weitzmann uses to derive the augmented triad from paired consonant triads

# Key Properties
1. Published in 1853, the monograph is dedicated entirely to the augmented triad
2. It presents sixteen ways to connect a consonant triad to an augmented one via semitonal voice leading
3. It derives the augmented triad from the nebenverwandt pentachord (F-Ab-C-E-G)
4. It groups all 24 consonant triads into four clusters of six, each centered on an augmented triad
5. Figure 3.9 graphically portrays each augmented triad at the center of its consonant triad cluster
6. The treatise culminates with seven pages of examples enumerating augmented triad resolutions

# Construction / Recognition
Weitzmann's key structural insight (Table 3.1, p. 75):
- {C, E, G#}: C major, E major, Ab major, a minor, c# minor, f minor
- {Db, F, A}: Db major, F major, A major, bb minor, d minor, f# minor
- {D, F#, A#}: D major, Gb major, Bb major, b minor, eb minor, g minor
- {Eb, G, B}: Eb major, G major, B major, c minor, e minor, g# minor

# Context & Application
Weitzmann's treatise provides the historical foundation for Cohn's entire theoretical project. The four six-triad clusters of Table 3.1 become the Weitzmann regions of Chapter 4. Figure 3.9 becomes the basis for the extended Tonnetz of Chapter 5. The treatise's implications guide Chapters 4, 5, and 6.

# Examples
**Figure 3.9** (p. 75-76): Weitzmann's diagram showing each augmented triad "at the center of a cluster of consonant triads; major and minor triads are indicated by large- and small-case roots. The chords placed immediately next to the augmented triad are attained through the half-step progression of one of their voices; the [chords] further away [are attained] through the half-step progression of two of their voices."

**The "serf" metaphor** (p. 61): Weitzmann "portrayed his protagonist as a serf, scurrying in and out the rear entrance, occasionally showing his face but never intruding on the conversation in the salon," then advocated for its emancipation.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating Weitzmann as a proto-neo-Riemannian theorist
  **Correction**: Weitzmann described key relations, not triadic transformations; the transformational interpretation came from later theorists

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The treatise is purely theoretical
  **Clarification**: It includes extensive compositional examples (seven pages enumerating augmented triad resolutions)
- **Confusion**: Weitzmann resolved the tension between his genesis narratives
  **Clarification**: "The first genesis narrative nonetheless remains intact" alongside the third; Weitzmann presented both without resolution
