# Quick Definition
Interregional modulation in the tetrachordal system is the shift from one Boretz region to another, occurring when the majority diminished seventh chord complex changes, typically through an octatonic-bridge transformation.

# Core Definition
**Interregional modulation** occurs when a Tristan-genus progression shifts from one Boretz region to another. Boretz interprets the shift between majority diminished seventh chord complexes in the Tristan Prelude as "the first of several interregional modulations that, he argues, provides the Prelude with its harmonic scaffold" (p. 169). The shift is detected when a chord's spoiler tone is best understood as a displacement of a different diminished seventh than the one governing previous chords. In the Tristan Prelude, chords 1-5 displace B-D-F-G#; chord 6 (C#7) marks the modulation as B becomes "the single remnant of the former complex and now plays its own role as spoiler of a new diminished seventh chord complex centered on A-C-D#-F#" (p. 169).

# Prerequisites
- **Boretz region**: The regions between which modulation occurs
- **Octatonic region**: The bridging structure enabling modulation
- **Octatonic-group transformations**: The operations effecting the bridge

# Key Properties
1. Analogous to key modulation in tonal theory, but between Boretz regions not keys
2. Detected by change in majority diminished seventh complex
3. Typically involves octatonic-bridge transformations
4. May signal reactivation of appellative (tonal-functional) properties
5. Provides structural scaffolding analogous to tonal key schemes

# Construction / Recognition
To detect interregional modulation:
1. Track which diminished seventh chord serves as the "majority complex"
2. When a new chord's majority affiliation shifts, modulation has occurred
3. Check whether the transition involves an octatonic-bridge transformation
4. Note any change in voice-leading zone pattern (e.g., zone 3-1 oscillation breaking to zone 5)

# Context & Application
Interregional modulation provides the Tristan Prelude with its "harmonic scaffold" (Boretz). In the Brunnhilde Immolation, the consecutive upshifts Eb7 to C#ø7 to C#7 "enter a new voice-leading zone. As in Tristan, this marks the moment when the seventh chords hearken to their appellative properties after ignoring them for some measures" (p. 174). The concept thus bridges Tristan-genus analysis and tonal-functional analysis.

# Examples
- **Tristan Prelude, chords 5-6**: B (from B-D-F-G# complex) becomes spoiler of new A-C-D#-F# complex, marking first interregional modulation (p. 169)
- **Brunnhilde's Immolation**: Eb7 to C#ø7 to C#7 enters a new Boretz region, marking the moment of tonal reengagement (p. 174)
- **Boretz's structural analysis**: Multiple interregional modulations provide the Prelude's harmonic scaffold (p. 169)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Equating interregional modulation with tonal modulation
  **Correction**: This describes movement between Boretz regions (voice-leading regions), not between keys

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking interregional modulation always signals return to tonal function
  **Clarification**: It may or may not coincide with reactivation of appellative properties; in Tristan and the Immolation it does, but this is not inherent
