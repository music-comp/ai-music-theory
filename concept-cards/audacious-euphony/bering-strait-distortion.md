# Quick Definition
The Tonnetz suffers from a "Bering Strait distortion" in two dimensions: locations at the left replicate those at the right, and locations at the bottom replicate those at the top, disguising the cyclic closure that is explicit on Cube Dance.

# Formal Definition
When enharmonic equivalence is accepted (as required for chromatic sequences), the Tonnetz conceals cyclic structure. Pitch-class identities at opposite edges of the planar Tonnetz are in fact the same points. Correcting these distortions requires gluing together corresponding edges in one dimension to create a cylinder, then wrapping the cylinder into a torus (doughnut shape). This topological reality is obscured by the dimensional constraints of the printed page. The term "Bering Strait distortion" analogizes the way flat maps of the earth separate Alaska from Siberia despite their proximity.

# Construction/Recognition
Identify Bering Strait distortion when:
- A chromatic sequence traverses five hexatonic or five Weitzmann regions, which should close a cycle but appears as an open path on the Tonnetz
- Identical pitch classes appear at opposite edges of the graph
- A progression's cyclic closure is evident on Cube Dance but not on the Tonnetz

# Musical Context
The distortion becomes analytically significant whenever a passage circumnavigates the triadic universe. Many of the chromatic sequences studied in Chapter 5 traverse exactly five regions of each type, closing a cycle that the Tonnetz fails to represent visually.

# Examples
- Liszt *Lelio Fantasy*, mm. 379-439: The **N**/**L** chain returns to Db major, closing a cycle visible on Cube Dance (figure 5.23) but not on the Tonnetz (figure 5.22)
- Brahms *German Requiem*: The **L**/**R** chain with **N**-for-**R** substitution traverses all four major-third alleys
- Chopin Ballade Op. 23: Two **N**-for-**R** substitutions traverse the full cycle

# Common Confusions
- The distortion is not an error in the Tonnetz; it is an inherent limitation of representing a toroidal surface on a plane
- The Tonnetz is still analytically valuable for tracking sequential patterns and common tones despite this limitation
- Cube Dance avoids this distortion but loses other information (specific triadic progressions, common-tone tracking)
