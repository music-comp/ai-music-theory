# Quick Definition
Mehrdeutigkeit (multiple meaning) is the property of augmented triads whereby each can be enharmonically reinterpreted to function in multiple keys, first systematically explored by Georg Vogler in 1802 and expanded by Weitzmann in 1853.

# Core Definition
Because the augmented triad divides the octave into three equal major thirds, it can be spelled differently depending on harmonic context. Vogler noted that the augmented triad "appears to consist of three similar major thirds" and that "each III chord in minor... can be multiply interpreted as a III chord in three different keys" (1802, 103, 109; p. 62). Weitzmann expanded this from three keys to six, showing that the CEG# augmented triad and its enharmonic equivalents can appear as "the most important [augmented triad]" in f minor, C major, a minor, Ab major, c# minor, and E major (pp. 73-74).

# Prerequisites
- **Augmented triad**: Must understand its structure as three equal major thirds to grasp why enharmonic reinterpretation is possible

# Key Properties
1. The augmented triad's symmetric structure makes all three spellings acoustically identical
2. Vogler identified three enharmonic interpretations (as III of three minor keys)
3. Weitzmann expanded this to six keys (three major, three minor) via nebenverwandt connections
4. Mehrdeutigkeit depends on equal temperament; in just intonation, different spellings would be different pitches
5. The property enables the augmented triad to function as a "switching station" between distantly related keys

# Construction / Recognition
Enharmonic interpretations of C-E-G# (and equivalents):
- As III of a minor (C-E-G#)
- As III of c# minor (E-G#-B# = E-G#-C)
- As III of f minor (Ab-C-E = G#-C-E)

Extended to major keys via nebenverwandt (Weitzmann):
- C major (nebenverwandt of f minor)
- E major (nebenverwandt of c# minor / a minor)
- Ab major (nebenverwandt of... relative of f minor)

These six triads constitute one Weitzmann region (Table 3.1).

# Context & Application
Mehrdeutigkeit enables the augmented triad to serve as a pivot between distantly related keys and as the structural center of both hexatonic regions and Weitzmann regions. The concept is foundational for understanding how a single augmented triad connects to six consonant triads via semitonal voice leading.

# Examples
**Vogler's Handbuch (1802)** (p. 62): Figure 3.1 presents a progression whose "anacrusis/downbeat combinations form a hexatonic cycle. The third beat of each measure hosts some spelling of the CEG# augmented triad, acting successively as dominant of each triad on the following beat."

**Weitzmann's expansion** (pp. 73-74): "So we find the augmented triad Ab-C-E and its enharmonic equivalents in the nebenverwandt keys F minor and C major, further in the relative keys of each, in Ab major and A minor, finally in the nebenverwandt keys of the latter, in Db minor and E major."

**Schubert, Symphony No. 2** (pp. 63-64): "Immediately preceding each tonic is a locally appropriate spelling of the CEG# augmented triad" -- each spelling representing a different enharmonic interpretation.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Equating Mehrdeutigkeit with mere enharmonic respelling
  **Correction**: Mehrdeutigkeit is about functional reinterpretation, not just notational convenience

- **Error**: Assuming Mehrdeutigkeit works in all tuning systems
  **Correction**: The property depends on equal temperament; in just intonation, different spellings would be acoustically distinct

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Vogler and Weitzmann discovered the same thing
  **Clarification**: Vogler identified three meanings (as III of minor keys); Weitzmann expanded this to six via nebenverwandt connections
- **Confusion**: Mehrdeutigkeit requires modulation
  **Clarification**: The chord can remain in one key while possessing potential for reinterpretation
