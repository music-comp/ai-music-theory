# Quick Definition
Mehrdeutigkeit (multiple meaning) is the property of augmented triads whereby each can be enharmonically reinterpreted to function in multiple keys, first systematically explored by Georg Vogler in 1802.

# Formal Definition
Because the augmented triad divides the octave into three equal parts and is spelled differently depending on harmonic context, any augmented triad can be interpreted as belonging to multiple keys. Vogler noted that each augmented triad "can be multiply interpreted as a III chord in three different keys" (as the mediant of three different minor keys). Weitzmann later expanded this to show each augmented triad connecting to six different keys (three major and three minor).

# Construction/Recognition
Enharmonic interpretations of C-E-G# (and equivalents):

As III of minor keys:
- C-E-G# = III of a minor
- E-G#-B# (=C) = III of c# minor
- G#-B#-D## (=Ab-C-E) = III of f minor

Extended to major keys (Weitzmann):
- Related to C major (via nebenverwandt with f minor)
- Related to E major (via nebenverwandt with c# minor)
- Related to Ab major (via nebenverwandt with a minor... wait, a minor's nebenverwandt is D major)

Correction from Table 3.1: The CEG# augmented triad connects to:
- C major, E major, Ab major (major triads)
- a minor, c# minor, f minor (minor triads)

These six triads constitute one hexatonic system.

# Musical Context/Application
Mehrdeutigkeit enables the augmented triad to function as a "pivot" or "switching station" between distantly related keys. A single augmented triad can connect to six different consonant triads via single semitonal voice leading, making it a powerful tool for modulation and chromatic sequence.

# Common Confusions
- Mehrdeutigkeit is about functional reinterpretation, not just enharmonic respelling
- The three "meanings" Vogler identified were all as III of minor keys; Weitzmann expanded this
- The property depends on equal temperament; in just intonation, different spellings would be different pitches
- Mehrdeutigkeit enables but does not require modulation; the chord can remain in one key
