# Quick Definition
Triadic disjunction is the deliberate deployment of maximally distant triads in voice-leading space, privileging discontinuity over smooth voice leading, as exemplified by zone-diametric triadic progressions and aggregate-completing tetrachords of triads.

# Formal Definition
A pan-triadic model that privileges conjunct voice leading also provides the means to model its opposite: disjunction. Zone-diametric triads (those at maximum voice-leading distance of 6 zones) can be combined with hexatonic poles to create progressions with minimal pitch-class intersection. When two zone-diametric triads are combined with their hexatonic poles, the four triads together use all twelve pitch classes without overlap, exhausting the aggregate.

# Construction/Recognition
To construct maximally disjunct progressions:
1. Select a triad and locate its zone-diametric partner (6 zones away: T2 or T6 transposition)
2. Combine each with its hexatonic pole
3. The resulting four triads share no pitch classes and collectively exhaust the chromatic aggregate
4. The four-chord complex rotates through alternating voice-leading zones

# Musical Context
Disjunction serves the Romantic aesthetic fascination with the remote, unattainable, and inexplicable. Mahler's letter about the C major finale of his First Symphony describes seeking a D major chord that "had to sound as though it had fallen from heaven, as though it had come from another world" -- privileging maximal voice-leading distance.

# Examples
- J. S. Bach Prelude (Dahlhaus 1967b): Major triad followed by hexatonic pole, then transposed by zone-diametric whole step (figure 5.25a)
- Wagner, *Parsifal*: Pair of **H**-related triads transposed by tritone (figure 5.25b)
- The Bach four-chord complex appears in Mozart's C minor Fantasy, Chopin's G major Nocturne, Schumann's D minor Piano Trio, Wagner's *Parsifal*, Debussy's *Prelude to the Afternoon of a Faun*, and many others

# Common Confusions
- Disjunction is not the absence of structure but the deliberate exploitation of maximum distance within the voice-leading model
- There are exactly two ways (to within transposition and reordering) to distribute the twelve-tone aggregate among four consonant triads
- The zone-diametric intervals are whole step (T2) and tritone (T6), both producing maximum distance of 6
