# Quick Definition
When dominant sevenths are attached to major triads in an N/R cycle, complementary whole-tone scales emerge in bass and treble registers, connecting all twelve pitch classes.

# Core Definition
"The addition of the sevenths puts all twelve pitch classes in play, enabling Schubert to present complementary whole-tone scales in the treble register, chaining pungent minor-second suspensions with minor-third resolutions" (Krebs 1980, 87; p. 82). The bass traces one whole-tone collection while upper voices trace the complementary collection.

# Prerequisites
- **N/R cycle**: The underlying cyclic structure that generates the whole-tone scales

# Key Properties
1. Dominant sevenths must be attached to the major triads in the cycle
2. The seventh additions put all 12 pitch classes in play
3. Bass voices trace one whole-tone scale
4. Treble voices trace the complementary whole-tone scale
5. Minor-second suspensions chain with minor-third resolutions

# Construction / Recognition
Example: N/R cycle with sevenths: C7 -> f minor -> Ab7 -> db minor -> E7 -> a minor -> C7. The roots C, Ab, E form one whole-tone scale segment; the sevenths and remaining tones complete both whole-tone collections.

# Context & Application
Schubert exploited this property in several compositions, connecting Weitzmann regions to the broader 19th-century interest in whole-tone harmony.

# Examples
**Schubert, Ninth Symphony, 1st mvt., mm. 304-15** (p. 82): Complete N/R cycle with dominant sevenths producing whole-tone scales.
**Schubert, Octet**: N/R cycle with whole-tone prominence.
**Borodin, Second Symphony, 1st mvt., mm. 41ff** (p. 82): Similar treatment.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Attributing the whole-tone scales to the basic triadic cycle
  **Correction**: The whole-tone scales emerge from the seventh additions, not from the triads alone

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The whole-tone scales are melodic lines
  **Clarification**: They are a harmonic by-product distributed across registers, not continuous melodic lines
