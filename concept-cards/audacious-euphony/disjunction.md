# Quick Definition
The quality of being disconnected or incoherent within a given syntactic framework -- a property that is relative to the metric applied, so that a progression disjunct diatonically may be highly conjunct by voice-leading criteria.

# Formal Definition
Disjunction in Cohn's usage describes the degree to which a triadic progression fails to cohere under a particular metric of distance. A progression is diatonically disjunct when its constituent triads share few diatonic collections; it is voice-leading disjunct when the total semitonal displacement is high. The central insight is that disjunction is metric-relative: the Schubert Bb Sonata progression is "among the most entropic" diatonically, yet highly conjunct by voice leading. Interpreting such passages as simply "disjunctive, irrational, or arbitrary" mistakes a failure under one metric for a failure under all metrics.

# Construction/Recognition
Assess disjunction by specifying the metric:
- **Diatonic disjunction**: few or no triadic pairs share a diatonic collection; high "entropy"
- **Voice-leading disjunction**: high total voice-leading work between triads
- **Common-tone disjunction**: few common tones between successive triads

A passage may be disjunct on one metric and conjunct on another. The "audacious euphony" paradox arises precisely from this discrepancy.

# Musical Context
Early-nineteenth-century critics "described harmonic indirection as a kind of aimless wandering towards extraneous goals, which injected a quality of randomness and lack of plan into the music" (Shamgar 1989). The more progressive critics valued such ruptures, connecting their inexplicability to the mysterious and sublime. Cohn proposes that rather than accepting the judgment of irrationality, we should consider whether "diatonic distance is not the best metric for the situation at hand."

# Examples
- Schubert, Bb Sonata recapitulation: only 1 of 6 triadic pairs shares a diatonic collection (diatonically disjunct), but voice-leading work totals only 14 units (voice-leading conjunct)
- Warren Darcy on the *Tarnhelm* progression: "radical disjunction... the motif seems almost to have fallen in from another world"
- Abbate on Wagner's *Die Walkure*: references to "harmonic irrationality and incongruence," "unstructured harmonic improvisation," "disjunctive gap"

# Common Confusions
- Disjunction is always relative to a specified metric; there is no absolute disjunction
- Calling a passage "disjunct" without specifying the metric is incomplete
- High diatonic disjunction does not imply artistic failure; it may signal the operation of an alternative syntax
- The perception of disjunction depends on the listener's acculturated expectations and the analytical framework applied
