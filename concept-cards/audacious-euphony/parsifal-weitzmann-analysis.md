# Quick Definition
Analysis of Parsifal Act 1, mm. 404-13, showing nonsequential exploration of a complete Weitzmann region with minor triads sounded in succession followed by major triads.

# Core Definition
"Beginning at m. 405, a locally subdominant e minor spins into its Weitzmann region, sounding its minor triads in succession, followed by its major ones. An augmented sixth is attached to the final G major and resolves to the F# major dominant, reintegrating into the b minor orbit" (p. 97). The passage demonstrates that the flat-terrain structure permits any ordering, not just N/R cyclic patterns.

# Prerequisites
- **Weitzmann region**: The passage explores a complete region
- **Weitzmann region nonsequential paths**: The passage uses nonsequential modal grouping

# Key Properties
1. All six triads of the EbGB region appear
2. Minor triads: e minor, c minor, g#/ab minor (in succession)
3. Major triads: G major, Eb major, B major (in succession)
4. Augmented sixth on G major resolves to F# major dominant
5. Locally in b minor (minor dominant within e minor section)

# Construction / Recognition
The ordering groups triads by mode rather than following N/R cyclic order: all minor triads first, then all major triads.

# Context & Application
The passage shows Wagner's characteristic treatment of chromatic regions: exploratory, nonsequential, embedded within a larger tonal plan, and resolved through functional harmony (augmented sixth to dominant).
