# Quick Definition
The property of hexatonic cycles whereby each constituent triad has equal claim to serve as tonic, since the cycle lacks cadential resources and each triad contains the other's leading tone.

# Core Definition
Hexatonic cycles exhibit tonal multistability because they "abjure the cadential resources of classical tonality, such as fifth-related roots, dissonances, and diatonic coordination" (Cohn, p. 43). "The six triads are equally likely recipients of rhetorical or cadential benefaction; the progression itself is neutral with respect to its potential tonics. In this way, hexatonic progressions resemble the so-called 'standard' bell pattern (variously called *bembe* and *gagokoe*) of West Africa or the Caribbean, whose temporal 'tonic' (i.e., downbeat) can occur at several points along the cycle. The cycle itself is neutral with respect to these bestowals and is in this sense multistable (Pressing 1983)" (p. 43). Each triad "contains the other's leading tone and hence signifies the other as tonic, like the Escher hands drawing each other's sleeves" (p. 43).

# Key Properties
1. No triad can establish clear tonal dominance within the cycle
2. Each triad contains its neighbors' leading tones, creating mutual tonic implications
3. A composer can suggest a tonic "factitiously by virtue of its recurrence" but can secure it only by "recruiting external syntactic routines" (p. 43)
4. The cycle is "neutral with respect to its potential tonics" (p. 43)
5. Analogous to the metric multistability of West African bell patterns

# Construction / Recognition
Sources of tonal multistability in hexatonic progressions:
1. No fifth-related root progressions (no authentic cadences)
2. No dissonance resolution (no dominant-tonic drive)
3. No diatonic scale coordination (no key signature hierarchy)
4. Each triad contains leading tones to multiple other triads in the cycle

# Context & Application
Tonal multistability contributes to both the hexatonic trance and the uncanny semiotics of hexatonic progressions. It explains why these passages create a sense of floating or suspension, and why composers find them effective for depicting altered states.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Trying to assign a single tonic to a hexatonic cycle
  **Correction**: The cycle is inherently neutral; any tonic assignment requires external syntactic support

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking tonal multistability means the passage is atonal
  **Clarification**: The passage consists entirely of consonant triads; it is tonally ambiguous, not atonal
- **Confusion**: Assuming multistability is a perceptual claim about listeners
  **Clarification**: It is a structural property of the cyclic system; context may disambiguate for specific listeners
