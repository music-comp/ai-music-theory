# Quick Definition
Tonal multistability describes the condition of hexatonic progressions where multiple triads are equally viable candidates for tonic status, and the progression itself is neutral with respect to which one is selected.

# Formal Definition
In hexatonic progressions, "each triad contains the other's leading tone and hence signifies the other as tonic, like the Escher hands drawing each other's sleeves" (p. 43). Because hexatonic cycles "abjure the cadential resources of classical tonality, such as fifth-related roots, dissonances, and diatonic coordination," a composer "can suggest one of its constituent triads as a tonic 'factitiously by virtue of its recurrence' but can secure it only by recruiting external syntactic routines" (p. 43). The six triads are equally likely recipients of rhetorical emphasis; the progression itself is neutral.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Sources of tonal multistability in hexatonic progressions:
- No fifth-related root progressions (no authentic cadences)
- No dissonance resolution (no dominant-tonic drive)
- No diatonic scale coordination (no key signature hierarchy)
- Each triad contains leading tones to multiple other triads

Comparison to African rhythm: "Hexatonic progressions resemble the so-called 'standard' bell pattern (variously called bembe and gagokoe) of West Africa or the Caribbean, whose temporal 'tonic' (i.e., downbeat) can occur at several points along the cycle. The cycle itself is neutral with respect to these bestowals and is in this sense multistable" (Pressing 1983).

# Musical Context/Application
Tonal multistability contributes to the "hexatonic trance" effect and the association of these progressions with altered or uncanny states. The lack of clear tonal orientation creates a sense of suspension or floating, which composers exploit for expressive purposes.

# Examples
From the text (p. 43):
On the Tarnhelm progression:
"Each Tarnhelm triad contains the other's leading tone and hence signifies the other as tonic"

The text notes that tonic can be suggested "factitiously by virtue of its recurrence" (quoting Taruskin 1996) but cannot be secured without external means.

# Common Confusions
- Multistability is not the same as atonality; the progressions use consonant triads and suggest tonal centers, just multiple ones simultaneously
- The multistability is in the progression itself, not necessarily in the listener's perception (context may disambiguate)
- A composer can resolve multistability by rhetorical means (repetition, metric emphasis) without using traditional harmonic means
- Multistability is a property of the cyclic structure, not just of individual chord pairs
