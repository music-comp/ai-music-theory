# Quick Definition
A series of triads derived by alternating two distinct triadic transformations, creating characteristic sequential patterns in chromatic triadic music.

# Formal Definition
A chain is a sequence of consonant triads produced by alternately applying two different triadic transformations. For example, an LP chain alternates the Leittonwechsel (L) and Parallel (P) transformations: C - e - E - g# - G# - c - ... Chains generate cyclic patterns that traverse portions of the triadic universe in regular fashion. The properties of a chain depend on which two transformations are alternated and can produce cycles of various lengths.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Chain notation: XY chain, where X and Y are transformations

Common chains:
- LP chain: L, P, L, P... generates a hexatonic cycle of 6 triads
- LR chain: L, R, L, R... generates a chain of 8 triads
- PR chain: P, R, P, R... generates a chain of 8 triads
- RP chain: equivalent to PR in reverse

Chain length depends on the combined effect of the transformations:
- LP/PL chains: 6 triads before returning to start
- LR/RL chains: 8 triads
- PR/RP chains: 8 triads

# Musical Context/Application
Chains appear in 19th-century music as:
- Sequential passages with regular transformational patterns
- Explorations of hexatonic or other cyclic structures
- Compositional strategies for traversing chromatic space systematically
- Analytical tools for identifying underlying patterns in chromatic progressions

Different chains produce different affective qualities and traverse different portions of the triadic universe.

# Examples
- LP chain in hexatonic cycles: C - e - E - g# - Ab - c - C (returns after 6 chords)
- LR chain: C - e - G - b - D - f# - A - c# - returns to C after 8 major/minor pairs
- Brahms, Schubert, Wagner: frequent use of chain-based passages

# Common Confusions
- A chain requires exactly two alternating transformations; three or more would not be a chain proper
- The order matters: LP chain and PL chain start differently but traverse the same cycle
- Not all sequential triadic passages form chains - some use single repeated transformations
- Chains are abstractions; actual musical passages may have embellishments or deviations
