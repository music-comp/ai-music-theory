# Quick Definition
A chromatic sequence transposing by minor third, generated by P/R, N/H, or S/L alternation, with bounding axes forming diminished seventh chords that combine into octatonic collections.

# Core Definition
Minor-third (T3) sequences result from three H/W combinations: P/R (most common, classically normative), N/H (involves maverick H), and S/L (involves maverick S). On the Tonnetz, the bounding axes are generated by minor thirds, forming diminished seventh chords that combine to form an octatonic collection. "The P/R cycle thus can be considered as a primordial triadic generator for octatonic systems, analogous to the role of L and P with respect to a hexatonic system" (p. 109).

# Key Properties
1. Three generating pairs: P/R (normative), N/H, S/L (both involve one maverick)
2. Octatonic connection: P/R cycle generates octatonic collections
3. Structural parallelism: L/P : hexatonic :: P/R : octatonic
4. N/H and S/L produce the same transposition but different voice-leading profiles

# Construction / Recognition
P/R chain (downshifting): C major --(P)--> c minor --(R)--> Eb major --(P)--> eb minor --(R)--> Gb major ...
- Bass traces diminished seventh chord: C - Eb - Gb - A
- Upper voices trace complementary diminished seventh
- Combined: octatonic collection

# Context & Application
The P/R-generated minor-third sequence is one of the most historically common chromatic sequences, appearing from the Zauberharfe Overture to late Romantic works. The N/H and S/L variants appear in Schubert and Liszt.

# Examples
- **Schubert *Die Zauberharfe* Overture**: P/R chain producing T3 downshifts (p. 109)
- **Schubert *Drei Klavierstucke* no. 2, mm. 46-52** (figure 5.7): N/H chain with N within segments, H across phrase boundaries
- **Liszt *Lelio Fantasy*, mm. 195-202** (figure 5.8): S/L chain, extended downshift offset by upward registral transfers; S maps a major triad to the minor triad sharing the same third (e.g. C major to c# minor), then L maps to relative major, netting a minor third
- **Schubert Ab major Mass, Sanctus**: S/L in upshifting direction with back-relating dominants
- **Later N/H examples**: Liszt *Malediction*, Rimsky-Korsakov *Skazka*, Bruckner "Ecce Sacerdos" -- all with H within phrases and N between them (reversing Schubert's arrangement)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming only P/R generates T3 sequences
  **Correction**: N/H and S/L also produce T3, with different voice-leading profiles

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The octatonic connection applies to all T3 sequences
  **Clarification**: The octatonic bounding axes are most clearly associated with the P/R combination specifically
