# Quick Definition
A chromatic sequence transposing by minor third, generated by P/R, N/H, or S/L alternation, with octatonic implications and diminished-seventh boundary axes.

# Formal Definition
Minor-third (T3) sequences result from three H/W combinations:
- P/R (most common): Classically normative
- N/H: Involves maverick H, appears in Schubert and later
- S/L: Involves maverick S, appears in Liszt

On the Tonnetz, T3 sequences have bounding axes generated by minor thirds, forming diminished seventh chords that combine to create octatonic collections. The P/R cycle can be considered a primordial triadic generator for octatonic systems, analogous to L/P for hexatonic systems.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
P/R example (downshifting):
C major --(P)--> c minor --(R)--> Eb major --(P)--> eb minor --(R)--> Gb major ...

Octatonic connection:
- Bass motion through P/R cycle: C - Eb - Gb - A (diminished seventh)
- Upper voices trace complementary diminished seventh
- Combined: octatonic collection

Parallel structure:
- L/P : hexatonic :: P/R : octatonic

# Musical Context/Application
The P/R sequence appears in Schubert's Zauberharfe Overture (analyzed earlier). N/H sequences appear in Schubert's Drei Klavierstucke no. 2 and later in Liszt's Malediction, Rimsky-Korsakov's Skazka, and Bruckner's "Ecce Sacerdos." S/L sequences appear in Liszt's Lelio Fantasy and Schubert's Ab major Mass Sanctus.

# Examples
- Schubert Zauberharfe Overture: P/R chain producing T3 downshifts
- Schubert Drei Klavierstucke no. 2, mm. 46-52: N/H chain
- Liszt Lelio Fantasy, mm. 195-202: S/L chain (extended downshift)
- Schubert Ab major Mass, Sanctus opening: S/L with back-relating dominants

# Common Confusions
- Three different transformation pairs produce T3 sequences
- The octatonic connection comes from the P/R combination specifically
- N/H and S/L produce the same transposition but different voice-leading profiles
