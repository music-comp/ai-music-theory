# Quick Definition
The evolutionary concept (appropriated from Stephen Jay Gould via Patrick McCreless) that a structure can serve a new function without altering its form, explaining how the consonant triad developed chromatic syntactic functions while retaining its acoustic identity.

# Formal Definition
Preadaptation, in evolutionary biology, "asserts that a structure can change its function radically without altering its form as much. We can bridge the limbo of intermediate stages by arguing for a retention of old functions while new ones are developing" (Gould 1977). McCreless (1996) suggests that classical tonality is preadapted to chromatic space. Cohn identifies the specific preadapted structure: the consonant triad, whose near-evenness in chromatic 12-space enables voice-leading optimization even though the triad originally arose through acoustic consonance optimization. The triad "knew nothing" of its future chromatic role, just as fish jaws "were well designed for their respiratory role; they had been selected for this alone and 'knew' nothing of any future function."

# Construction/Recognition
What enables the triad's preadaptation:
1. Near-evenness in diatonic 7-space: Triads are step-connected (Agmon 1991)
2. Near-evenness in chromatic 12-space: Triads minimize voice-leading work between types
3. The same property (minimal perturbation of even division) operates in both spaces
4. Diatonic sequences already exploit this property (Fetis)

The preadaptation claim is stronger than McCreless's formulation:
- McCreless: "structural semitone relations," directed linear motion, sequential transposition are preadapted
- Cohn: These phenomena arise only after chromatic supplementation; the triad itself is the preadapted structure, "fully native to a nonchromaticized diatonic space"

# Musical Context/Application
Preadaptation provides a mechanism for the soft revolution:
- Composers did not invent a new harmonic language from scratch
- They discovered latent properties of familiar sounds (consonant triads)
- The discovery was gradual and did not require abandoning diatonic practice
- New syntactic possibilities emerged through experimentation with voice-leading

The fish-jaw analogy: "The intricate device was already assembled, but it was being used for breathing, not eating." Similarly, the triad was already assembled (for acoustic consonance) but could be used for voice-leading optimization.

# Examples
- Diatonic sequence: Triads exercise preadapted voice-leading capacity within diatonic space
- Baroque Fortspinnung: Arrow of orientation flips from tonic to voice-leading, exploiting preadaptation
- 19th-century chromatic progressions: Same capacity exercised in chromatic space with greater parsimony
- The emergence of hexatonic and octatonic collections as by-products of triadic preadaptation

# Common Confusions
- Preadaptation is not teleology; the triad did not develop "in order to" serve chromatic syntax
- The concept does not imply composers consciously recognized the triad's dual nature
- Preadaptation applies to the structure (triad), not to specific compositional techniques
- The fish-jaw analogy is structural, not claiming music is biological
