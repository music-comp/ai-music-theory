# Quick Definition
The evolutionary concept (from Stephen Jay Gould via Patrick McCreless) that a structure can serve a new function without altering its form, explaining how the consonant triad developed chromatic syntactic functions while retaining its acoustic identity -- like fish jaws originally designed for breathing that proved "admirably preadapted to become jaws."

# Core Definition
Preadaptation "asserts that a structure can change its function radically without altering its form as much. We can bridge the limbo of intermediate stages by arguing for a retention of old functions while new ones are developing" (Gould 1977, 108, qtd. in Cohn, p. 227). McCreless (1996, 108-10) suggests classical tonality is preadapted to chromatic space. Cohn identifies the specific preadapted structure: "it is the consonant triad, fully native to a nonchromaticized diatonic space, that plays the crucial role. Triads are preadapted to play the role of voice-leading optimizers by virtue of their near evenness, which manifests in both diatonic 7-space and chromatic 12-space" (p. 228).

# Prerequisites
- **Near-evenness**: The specific property enabling the triad's preadaptation
- **Consonant triad**: The structure that is preadapted
- **Soft revolution**: The historical process preadaptation enables

# Key Properties
1. The triad's near-evenness operates in both 7-space and 12-space
2. Preadaptation is not teleology -- the triad did not develop "in order to" serve chromatic syntax
3. The triad "knew nothing" of its future chromatic role, like fish jaws and respiration
4. Triads first assume their preadapted role in diatonic sequences (Fetis)
5. Cohn's formulation is stronger than McCreless's: the triad itself (not "structural semitone relations") is preadapted

# Construction / Recognition
**Cohn's critique of McCreless** (p. 228): McCreless identifies "structural semitone harmonic relations," directed linear motion, and sequential transposition as preadapted. But none of these "is ever intrinsic to a diatonic system, in the way that the jaw is proper to the fish. They arise only when a diatonic system is supplemented by chromatic tones, at which point the evolutionary train has already left the station."

**Cohn's alternative**: The consonant triad is "fully native to a nonchromaticized diatonic space" and exercises its preadapted voice-leading role first in diatonic sequences when triads "cease to exploit the orienting, or 'summoning,' capacity of that scale's tritone and instead exercise their capacity... for intertriadic stepwise voice leading within a quasiflat diatonic space" (p. 228).

# Context & Application
The fish-jaw analogy (Gould 1977, 108): "Fish jaws were well designed for their respiratory role; they had been selected for this alone and 'knew' nothing of any future function. In hindsight, the bones were admirably preadapted to become jaws. The intricate device was already assembled, but it was being used for breathing, not eating."

Preadaptation operates at multiple levels: the triad (acoustic -> voice-leading), the diatonic scale (generative -> modulatory), and the chromatic gamut (Pythagorean -> divisible).

# Examples
- **Diatonic sequence**: Triads first exercise preadapted voice-leading capacity within diatonic space (p. 228)
- **Baroque Fortspinnung**: Arrow of orientation flips from tonic to voice-leading, exploiting preadaptation
- **Emergence of hexatonic/octatonic collections**: By-products of triadic preadaptation (p. 229)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Preadaptation means composers consciously recognized the triad's dual nature
  **Correction**: It is about structural properties, not compositional awareness

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Preadaptation is teleology in disguise
  **Clarification**: The structure was not designed for its second function; the second function was a serendipitous consequence of pre-existing properties
