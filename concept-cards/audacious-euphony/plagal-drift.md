# Quick Definition
Extended motion through the L/R chain in the flatward (leftward) direction, creating a "perpetual downshift" that can proceed indefinitely until halted by diatonic encapsulation forces such as reverse motion, accented dissonance, or hypermetric articulation.

# Formal Definition
Plagal drift describes motion through the L/R chain where triads progress leftward on the Tonnetz (flatward in key signature terms), executing a perpetual downshift in voice-leading space. Unlike a diatonic plagal cadence (IV-I), plagal drift extends the subdominant-ward motion beyond any single key, creating a chain "that can be arrested at any point or can just as easily go on in perpetuity" (Mitchell 1962). The drift represents suspended tonal syntax: the triads are diatonic materials operating under voice-leading logic rather than functional logic.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Characteristics:
- Direction: Leftward on Tonnetz (flatward, subdominant-ward)
- Voice leading: Perpetual downshift (all moving voices descend)
- Transformations: Alternating L and R (or R and L)
- Extent: Unpredictable; no inherent stopping point

Forces that halt plagal drift:
1. Leftward motion (temporary reversal)
2. Reverse hemiola (rhythmic elongation)
3. Accented dissonance (supplementary sevenths)
4. Flanking by syntonic images (diatonic markers)
5. Thematic statement on hypermetric downbeat

Once halted, the "spring" of diatonic syntax can reload and relock into tonal position.

# Musical Context/Application
Plagal drift contrasts with dominant-ward drift:
- Rightward L/R motion also possible but less characteristic of Romantic practice
- Plagal emphasis reflects 19th-century compositional taste
- The drift exploits the L/R chain's capacity for smooth voice leading

In Wagner's Parsifal, plagal drift has hermeneutic significance: "Stopping the flow" relates to healing Amfortas's bleeding wound. The music perambulates about eb minor "without knowing how to respond to it" until forces halt the drift and establish encapsulation.

# Examples
- Wagner Parsifal Faith Proclamation (mm. 45-55): Plagal drift from Ebb major through eb minor, halted by encapsulation forces
- The drift continues after m. 56 into "even more extended plagal drift" reaching mm. 79

# Common Confusions
- Plagal drift is not a sequence of plagal cadences; it is continuous motion without cadential confirmation
- The "drift" is not aimless; it follows systematic voice-leading logic
- Halting the drift requires multiple reinforcing forces, not just a single cadential gesture
- The metaphor of "flow" and "stanching" is particularly apt for Wagner's Parsifal context
