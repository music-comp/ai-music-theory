# Quick Definition
Extended motion through the L/R chain in the flatward (leftward) direction, creating a "perpetual downshift" that can proceed indefinitely until halted by diatonic encapsulation forces.

# Core Definition
Plagal drift describes motion through the L/R chain where triads progress leftward on the Tonnetz (flatward in key-signature terms), executing a perpetual downshift in voice-leading space. Unlike a diatonic plagal cadence (IV-I), plagal drift extends subdominant-ward motion beyond any single key, creating a chain "that can be arrested at any point or can just as easily go on in perpetuity" (Mitchell 1962, qtd. p. 207). The drift represents suspended tonal syntax: diatonic materials operating under voice-leading logic rather than functional logic.

# Prerequisites
- **Cube Dance**: The space in which downshifting is tracked
- **Voice-leading Zone**: The metric for measuring downshift

# Key Properties
1. Direction: leftward on Tonnetz (flatward, subdominant-ward)
2. Voice leading: perpetual downshift (all moving voices descend)
3. Transformations: alternating L and R (or R and L)
4. Extent: unpredictable; no inherent stopping point
5. Represents suspended tonal syntax
6. Requires external forces to halt

# Construction / Recognition
Forces that halt plagal drift:
1. Leftward motion (temporary reversal)
2. Reverse hemiola (rhythmic elongation)
3. Accented dissonance (supplementary sevenths)
4. Flanking by syntonic images (diatonic markers)
5. Thematic statement on hypermetric downbeat

Once halted, the "spring" of diatonic syntax can reload and relock into tonal position.

# Context & Application
Plagal drift exploits the L/R chain's capacity for smooth voice leading. In Wagner's Parsifal, plagal drift has hermeneutic significance: "stopping the flow" relates to healing Amfortas's bleeding wound. The music perambulates without knowing how to respond until encapsulation forces halt the drift and establish tonal position. The plagal emphasis reflects 19th-century compositional taste; rightward (dominant-ward) L/R motion is also possible but less characteristic.

# Examples
- **Wagner, Parsifal, Faith Proclamation (mm. 45-55)**: Plagal drift from Ebb major through eb minor, halted by encapsulation forces
- **Parsifal, mm. 56-79**: "Even more extended plagal drift" continuing after initial halting

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Plagal drift is a sequence of plagal cadences
  **Correction**: It is continuous motion without cadential confirmation

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Plagal drift is aimless
  **Clarification**: It follows systematic voice-leading logic (alternating L and R)

- **Confusion**: A single cadential gesture can halt plagal drift
  **Clarification**: Halting requires multiple reinforcing forces working together
