# Quick Definition
Cohn's summary analysis of Schubert's C major Symphony Scherzo (mm. 93-153) demonstrating the central claims of the book: two incommensurate ways of measuring triadic distance emerging from the triad's two independent properties.

# Formal Definition
The passage begins at m. 93 with a waltz fragment presented first in the dominant, then in the tonic, and ends with a dominant prolongation preceding the Scherzo's reprise at m. 153. Its interior (from m. 109) forms a "chromatic tunnel" that recedes from C major in stages, loses contact with it entirely as hypermeter broadens to six-bar units at m. 117, and gradually reattunes to C major as four-bar units are restored at m. 141. The analysis receives two Tonnetz representations: Figure 9.2(a) maintains diatonic encapsulation around C major; Figure 9.2(b) focuses on voice-leading efficiency without encapsulation.

# Construction/Recognition
Diatonic perspective (Figure 9.2a):
- Ab and Db neighbors (mm. 109-110) receive harmonic support from f minor (m. 113) and Db major (m. 117)
- Phrygian mixture or nebenverwandt tonicization of f minor
- Exit through A major (m. 129) to d minor (m. 135) as applied dominant to supertonic
- c#/db minor at m. 123 is "inscrutable" from either diatonic portal

Voice-leading perspective (Figure 9.2b):
- C major to f minor (m. 109): Double semitonal upshift
- f minor through hexatonic alley to A major: Cumulative semitonal upshift
- A major to d minor (m. 135): Double semitonal upshift
- d minor to G major (m. 141): Larger strides in voice-leading space
- "What we initially conceived as tunneling into diatonic/tonal darkness becomes... a transient emergence into the hexatonic light"

# Musical Context/Application
The central claim: "These two incommensurate ways of measuring triadic distance emerge respectively from two independent properties of consonant triads."

Listening in terms of C major (chromatic obscurity, enharmonic crevice, reemergence): Hearing in terms of acoustic consonance, root relations to C major

Listening attuned to voice leading: Hearing elimination of chordal dissonance, reduction from two moving voices to one (m. 117), maximization of common-tone retention (mm. 123-40), restoration of voice-leading work synchronized with restoration of chordal dissonance (m. 141)

Neither perspective is prior or more correct; both operate on the same musical surface.

# Examples
- mm. 109-110: Ab and Db neighbors initiating chromatic departure
- m. 117: Db major marking six-bar hypermeter and full chromatic immersion
- m. 123: c#/db minor as crux - inscrutable diatonically, clear voice-leading-wise
- mm. 123-140: Central hexatonic passage with maximal voice-leading efficiency
- m. 141: Restoration of four-bar hypermeter and diatonic reattunement

# Common Confusions
- The two analyses are not competing interpretations; they describe different aspects of the same music
- The enharmonic notation (Db becoming C#) does not indicate syntactic change; it reflects analytical perspective
- "Chromatic tunnel" and "hexatonic light" are inverse metaphors for the same passage
- The analysis demonstrates, rather than proves, the double syntax hypothesis
