# Quick Definition
Cohn's summary analysis of Schubert's C major Symphony Scherzo (mm. 93-153) demonstrating the book's central claims: two incommensurate ways of measuring triadic distance emerging from the triad's two independent properties.

# Core Definition
The passage forms a "chromatic tunnel" receding from C major in stages, losing contact entirely as hypermeter broadens to six-bar units at m. 117, then gradually reattuning as four-bar units are restored at m. 141. Two Tonnetz representations capture the dual hearing: Figure 9.2(a) maintains diatonic orientation; Figure 9.2(b) focuses on voice-leading efficiency. "What we initially conceived as a tunneling into and out of diatonic/tonal darkness becomes, from the standpoint of voice leading, a transient emergence into the hexatonic light" (p. 216).

# Prerequisites
- **Double syntax hypothesis**: The theoretical framework being demonstrated
- **Convertible Tonnetz**: The analytical model applied
- **Hexatonic cycle**: The chromatic organizing principle at the passage's center
- **Enharmonic seam**: The c-sharp/d-flat boundary crossed at m. 123

# Key Properties
1. Two inverse hearings: diatonic darkness = voice-leading light, and vice versa
2. The c-sharp/d-flat minor at m. 123 is "inscrutable" from either diatonic portal
3. Hypermetric broadening/narrowing signals diatonic loosening/tightening
4. The passage demonstrates symmetry: NL exit (C to Db) mirrors LN reentry (c-sharp to d)

# Construction / Recognition
**Diatonic perspective** (Figure 9.2a): Ab and Db neighbors (mm. 109-110) support f minor (m. 113) and Db major (m. 117) -- phrygian mixture or nebenverwandt tonicization. A major (m. 129) to d minor (m. 135) = applied dominant to supertonic. The c-sharp/d-flat junction is connected by a "dotted arrow that seems to travel a great distance" while aurally the triads are proximate (p. 215).

**Voice-leading perspective** (Figure 9.2b): No diatonic region encapsulated. C to f minor = double semitonal upshift; f minor through hexatonic alley to A major = cumulative semitonal upshift; A to d minor = double upshift; d to G = larger strides. "A wave of voice-leading efficiency crests at the hexatonic alley, whose balanced voice leading temporarily suspends the pervasive upshift" (p. 216).

The critical point: "For chromatic triadic progressions that move through the enharmonic seam, seven-tone diatonic space provides no adequate stick for measuring distances" (p. 215). The solution: "relinquish the diatonic gauge for a chromatic one" (p. 215).

# Context & Application
This is the book's culminating analytical example, demonstrating that "these two incommensurate ways of measuring triadic distance emerge respectively from two independent properties of consonant triads" (p. 216). Listening via acoustic consonance (root relations to C major), one hears the first property. Listening via voice leading (attuned to near-evenness), one hears the second.
