# Quick Definition
The point in a chromatic progression where the passage crosses from one enharmonic spelling to another, creating a gap that diatonic logic cannot bridge -- the listener must "relinquish the diatonic gauge for a chromatic one" to maintain coherent distance measurement.

# Core Definition
The enharmonic crevice is the specific juncture where a chromatic progression passes through the enharmonic seam, creating a point where diatonic spelling cannot consistently represent the music's pitch-class relationships. Cohn describes the Schubert Scherzo as the passage "disappears into chromatic obscurity, crosses the enharmonic crevice, and reemerges back into diatonic clarity" (p. 216). The crevice embodies the fundamental incompatibility between seven-tone diatonic space and twelve-tone chromatic space: "This circle can never be squared by diatonic logic" (p. 215).

# Prerequisites
- **Double syntax hypothesis**: The framework that explains the crevice's significance
- **Enharmonic seam**: The broader concept of enharmonic boundary (from Chapter 4)

# Key Properties
1. Marks where diatonic spelling fails to capture pitch-class relationships
2. Cannot be resolved by choosing a different spelling -- the problem is structural
3. Crossing the crevice typically triggers gauge switching (diatonic to chromatic)
4. The chromatic gauge resolves the crevice by treating enharmonic equivalents as identical
5. "If Db at m. 117 becomes 'C-sharp' at 123... then 'C-sharp' at 129 is heard as Db and cannot function as the leading tone" -- an inescapable circle (p. 215)

# Construction / Recognition
The inescapable circle at the Schubert Scherzo's center: if the Db spelling is maintained from m. 117 through m. 123 to m. 129, then the tone at m. 129 cannot be C-sharp and cannot function as the dominant agent of d minor at m. 135. Conversely, if G-sharp at m. 123 is inherited from Ab at m. 117, then Ab at m. 113 is heard as G-sharp and cannot function as the flatted submediant (p. 215). The crevice is structural, not notational.

# Context & Application
The enharmonic crevice is the specific musical phenomenon that motivates the diatonic-to-chromatic gauge switch. It demonstrates why double syntax is necessary: single-syntax analysis cannot account for passages that traverse the crevice. The concept also connects to the broader theme of the book: the triad's dual nature allows it to bridge the crevice through its chromatic (voice-leading) properties even when its diatonic properties fail.

# Examples
- **Schubert C major Symphony Scherzo, m. 123**: The c-sharp/d-flat minor triad is the locus of the crevice (pp. 214-216)
- The logical circle: Db -> "C-sharp" -> Db cannot be consistently maintained

# Common Errors
- **Error**: The crevice can be resolved by choosing the right enharmonic spelling
  **Correction**: It is structural, not notational -- no consistent diatonic spelling works for both directions

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The enharmonic crevice is a problem with equal temperament
  **Clarification**: It arises from the incommensurability of 7-tone and 12-tone spaces, independent of tuning system
