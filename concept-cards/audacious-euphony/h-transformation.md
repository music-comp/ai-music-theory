# Quick Definition
The H (Hexatonic pole) transformation connects two triads diametrically opposite in a hexatonic cycle, moving all three voices simultaneously by semitone in contrary motion, with no common tones between the triads.

# Formal Definition
H is the compound transformation LP = PL, connecting triads that are three positions apart in a hexatonic cycle. It maps a major triad to the minor triad whose root is a major third plus a semitone above (or equivalently, a minor sixth below), and vice versa. As a mode-switching operation, H is an involution: H(H(X)) = X.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Voice-leading specification:
- All three voices move by semitone simultaneously
- Two voices move in one direction, one in the opposite (contrary motion)
- Common tones = 0
- Voice-leading work = 3 semitones total

Examples:
- H(C major) = ab minor: (C, E, G) -> (B, Eb, Ab)
- H(Eb major) = b minor: (Eb, G, Bb) -> (D, F#, B)
- H(ab minor) = C major: (Ab, Cb, Eb) -> (G, C, E)

Relation to L and P:
- H = LP = PL (both orderings produce the same result)
- H connects triads at maximum distance within a hexatonic system

# Musical Context/Application
The H transformation, named by Clifton Callender (1994), depicts maximal harmonic distance within a hexatonic system. In nineteenth-century music, hexatonic poles frequently accompany depictions of uncanny, supernatural, or liminal phenomena - death, transformation, magic. The progression's power derives from the paradox of maximum voice-leading activity (three simultaneous semitones) producing maximum consonance-category ambiguity.

# Examples

# Common Confusions
- H is not an independent transformation but a compound of L and P; nevertheless, it can be conceptualized as a unitary Gestalt
- Despite having no common tones, the two triads share all six pitch classes of their hexatonic collection
- H connects triads of opposite mode (major to minor), not same-mode triads
- The "pole" terminology refers to maximal distance within the cycle, not to tonal poles or magnetic poles
