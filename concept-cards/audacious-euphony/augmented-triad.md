# Quick Definition
A perfectly even three-note chord that divides the octave into three equal parts (major thirds), serving as a structural pivot in pan-triadic voice-leading space.

# Formal Definition
An augmented triad is a three-note chord consisting of two stacked major thirds, dividing the 12-semitone octave into three equal parts of 4 semitones each. Because 12 divides evenly by 3, there are only 4 distinct augmented triads (under enharmonic equivalence): {C, E, G#}, {Db, F, A}, {D, F#, A#}, {Eb, G, B}. Each augmented triad is "perfectly even" - maximally symmetric within the chromatic universe. Augmented triads play a central structural role in pan-triadic syntax even when absent from the musical surface.

# Construction/Recognition
Structure: 4 + 4 + 4 semitones (major third + major third + major third)
- Pitch-class set: [0, 4, 8] or any transposition
- Only 4 distinct forms exist (T0, T1, T2, T3)
- Each augmented triad is transpositionally equivalent to itself at T4 and T8

Relation to consonant triads:
- Each consonant triad is a single semitonal displacement from exactly one augmented triad
- Each augmented triad generates a Weitzmann region of 6 consonant triads
- Augmented triads are the "perfectly even" chords from which consonant triads ("nearly even") minimally deviate

# Musical Context/Application
Augmented triads serve as:
- Pivots between hexatonic cycles (the "centers" around which consonant triads orbit)
- Structural nodes in the Cube Dance graph
- Explanatory mechanisms even when not literally present in the music
- Historical objects of theoretical interest (Weitzmann 1853)

The centrality of augmented triads may be counterintuitive for those trained in classical harmony, where augmented triads are unstable dissonances. In pan-triadic syntax, they are structurally central precisely because of their perfect symmetry.

# Examples
- Carl Friedrich Weitzmann's 1853 treatise "The Augmented Triad" explored their voice-leading properties
- Each augmented triad is surrounded by 6 consonant triads (3 major, 3 minor) related by single semitone
- The 4 augmented triads partition the 24 consonant triads into 4 Weitzmann regions
- Augmented triads appear at transition points in hexatonic cycles

# Common Confusions
- Augmented triads are structurally important in pan-triadic syntax even when absent from the surface
- Their "perfect evenness" is the standard against which consonant triads' "near evenness" is measured
- There are only 4 distinct augmented triads, not 12 (due to their symmetry)
- In pan-triadic contexts, augmented triads function as structural pivots, not as "dominant" or "unstable" harmonies
