# Quick Definition
The union of the twelve dominant seventh and twelve half-diminished seventh chords, comprising 24 nearly even tetrachords that relate to the diminished seventh chord as consonant triads relate to the augmented triad.

# Formal Definition
The Tristan genus is defined in the Glossary as "the union of the twelve dominant and twelve half-diminished seventh chords." These 24 chords are nearly even tetrachords - minimal perturbations of the perfectly even diminished seventh chord. Just as consonant triads are single semitonal displacements of augmented triads (organizing into hexatonic and Weitzmann regions), Tristan-genus chords are single semitonal displacements of diminished seventh chords (organizing into octatonic and Boretz regions). The name references the Tristan chord from Wagner's opera, the paradigmatic example of this chord type's expressive and syntactic ambiguity.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Structure:
- 12 dominant seventh chords: [0, 4, 7, 10] and transpositions
- 12 half-diminished seventh chords: [0, 3, 6, 10] and transpositions
- Total: 24 chords
- Each is one semitone away from two diminished seventh chords

Parallel to triadic structure:
- Consonant triads : augmented triads :: Tristan-genus chords : diminished seventh chords
- Hexatonic region (6 triads around augmented) :: Boretz region (8 Tristan-genus around diminished)
- Voice-leading properties are analogous at the tetrachordal level

# Musical Context/Application
Tristan-genus chords participate in chromatic syntax similarly to consonant triads. They can function:
- As diatonic objects (dominant sevenths seeking tonic resolution; half-diminished as ii7 or vii-half-dim)
- As chromatic voice-leading objects (moving by single semitonal displacement)
- As convertible between these modes (like triads on the convertible Tonnetz)

The "omnitonal" quality of the Tristan chord exemplifies the genus's capacity to suspend diatonic orientation.

# Examples
- The Tristan chord (F-B-D#-G#): Half-diminished seventh, opening of Tristan und Isolde
- Dominant seventh chords in omnibus progressions
- Chopin e minor Prelude: G# "awakened from its omnitonal haze... remembers its monotonal responsibilities"
- Brunnhilde's Immolation: C#7 chords "hearken to their appellative properties after ignoring them"

# Common Confusions
- "Tristan genus" is Cohn's term, not Wagner's or traditional theory's
- The genus includes both dominant sevenths and half-diminished sevenths
- The Tristan chord itself is half-diminished; the genus includes both types
- Tristan-genus chords are tetrachords, not triads; they require different geometric representation
