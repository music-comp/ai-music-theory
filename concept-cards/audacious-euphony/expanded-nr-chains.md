# Quick Definition
Five-element N/R chains that extend the four-chord double-agent complex toward the complete six-element N/R cycle, representing a historical middle ground between Classical diatonic tonality and full chromatic exploration.

# Core Definition
Expanded N/R chains are "segments of five consecutive triads from an N/R cycle" (cf. p. 95). They "necessarily involve enharmonic crossing (like the full six-triad cycle)" and "embed a double-agent complex within a larger chromatic trajectory" (cf. p. 96). Two remarkable examples feature "the same set of triads, presented in the same order, although the progressions are embedded in compositions whose tonics are a tritone apart" (p. 96).

# Prerequisites
- **Double-agent complex**: The four-chord structure being expanded
- **N/R cycle**: The complete six-chord target
- **Enharmonic seam**: Five-chord chains necessarily cross it

# Key Properties
1. Five consecutive triads from an N/R cycle
2. Necessarily cross the enharmonic seam (unlike four-chord complexes which may only tease it)
3. Historical middle ground between Classical four-chord and Romantic six-chord explorations
4. The same five triads can appear in compositions with completely different tonics

# Construction / Recognition
Example five-chord chain: B major --(N)--> e minor --(R)--> G major --(N)--> c minor --(R)--> Eb major

# Context & Application
Expanded N/R chains appear in late 18th-century music as composers began exploring beyond the i-III-V# core. They trace the incremental historical expansion from four- to six-chord structures.

# Examples
**Benda, Piano Sonata in a minor (1783), mm. 19-27** (p. 96): Five-triad chain B-e-G-c-Eb over G pedal.
**Haydn, Symphony No. 99, 1st mvt. Adagio, mm. 9-19** (p. 96): Same five triads in same order, despite tonic a tritone away from Benda's.
**Schubert, Piano Sonata in c minor, D. 958, finale** (p. 97): "The sequential progression of Haydn's Symphony is compressed and extended to cover the entire six-triad Weitzmann region."

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming the five triads must serve a single tonic
  **Correction**: "The same set of triads" can appear in pieces with completely different tonics (a tritone apart)

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Five-chord chains are simply incomplete six-chord cycles
  **Clarification**: They are historically and structurally significant as an intermediate stage of chromatic exploration
