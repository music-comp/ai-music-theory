# Quick Definition
A representation of triadic space where hexatonic cycles form cross-sections of a cylinder, with perfect-fifth transpositions connecting triads horizontally; the two ends glue together with a one-third twist, forming continuous circles of fifths for major and minor triads respectively.

# Core Definition
Cohn's 1999 model positions the four hexatonic cycles as cross-sections of a cylinder and connects each triad horizontally to its perfect-fifth transpositions (p. 187). The two ends of the cylinder glue together with a one-third twist, forming two continuous circles of fifths, composed of major and minor triads respectively. The cycle containing the local tonic has tonic function; fifth relations above and below bear dominant and subdominant function. Rings's 2007 model is implicitly cylindrical and isomorphic to Cohn's (p. 188).

# Prerequisites
- **Hexatonic cycle**: The cross-sections of the cylinder
- **Tonnetz**: The basic pitch-class network being shaped into cylindrical form

# Key Properties
1. Hexatonic cycles form the circular cross-sections
2. Perfect-fifth relations connect triads along the length of the cylinder
3. One-third twist when gluing the ends creates continuous circles of fifths
4. The circular topology represents pan-triadic space (no tonic hierarchy)
5. When the space opens up (decircularizes), the cylindrical representation becomes planar

# Construction / Recognition
The cylindrical model is the closed/pan-triadic state of Hyer's convertible Tonnetz. When triads are heard relative to a tonic, the cylinder decircularizes into a plane (or Mobius strip for the diatonic case). The cylinder's circular topology means "there is no one triad that forms a tonic for the group as a whole" (Hyer 1995, 127).

# Context & Application
Three limitations of the cylindrical model motivated the convertible Tonnetz (pp. 188-189):
1. Orientation to hexatonic configuration limits applicability to octatonic-axis repertory
2. Rings arrows implicitly commit to tonal gravity whenever motion occurs along the fifth axis
3. Third relations are relegated to the chromatic axis, preventing diatonic third divisions from being modeled

## Related
- Hyer's convertible Tonnetz (the cylinder is the closed state)

## Contrasts With
- Mobius strip topology (diatonic closure involves half-twist, not full cylinder)
- Diatonic Tonnetz (planar/Mobius, not cylindrical)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: The cylindrical model fully captures syntactic interaction
  **Correction**: It has three limitations that motivate the more general convertible Tonnetz

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The cylinder is the same as the torus
  **Clarification**: The torus results from closing both axes; the cylinder closes only the hexatonic axis
