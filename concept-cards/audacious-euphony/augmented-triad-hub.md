# Quick Definition
The four augmented triads function as connection points between hexatonic and Weitzmann regions, occupying the cardinal points of Cube Dance and enabling perambulation through the entire triadic universe.

# Core Definition
Each augmented triad serves as a hub connecting its six-triad Weitzmann region to two adjacent hexatonic regions. On Cube Dance, the four augmented triads occupy the cardinal points (12:00, 3:00, 6:00, 9:00), with consonant triads arranged between them. "The Weitzmann and hexatonic regions are in a figure-ground relation. For the hexatonic mariner, the Weitzmann regions form bridges. For the Weitzmann landlubber, the hexatonic regions are rivers to cross. To circumnavigate the entire complex, one need only locate an appropriately amphibious vehicle" (p. 84). The augmented triads serve as the crossing points between these two modes of navigation.

# Prerequisites
- **Cube Dance**: The graph in which augmented triads occupy cardinal points
- **Weitzmann Region**: The six-triad region connected to each augmented hub
- **Hexatonic Region**: The six-triad region intersecting adjacent Weitzmann regions

# Key Properties
1. Four augmented triads at cardinal positions: CEG# (zone 0), C#FA (zone 3), DGbBb (zone 6), EbGB (zone 9)
2. Each connects to 6 consonant triads (its Weitzmann region)
3. Those 6 triads are divided between 2 adjacent hexatonic regions
4. Major triads counterclockwise from hub, minor triads clockwise
5. Moving between hexatonic regions requires passing through or near an augmented hub
6. Hub function is structural, not requiring prolonged augmented triads on the surface

# Construction / Recognition
On Cube Dance:
- Augmented triads are nodes at cardinal points
- Each augmented node has 6 edges to consonant triads (its Weitzmann region)
- Hexatonic cycles form the circuits of unbroken lines between cardinal points
- Weitzmann regions form "water bug" shapes centered on augmented nodes

Transitional function:
- Hexatonic-to-hexatonic travel: pass through augmented hub
- Weitzmann-to-Weitzmann travel: traverse hexatonic "river"

# Context & Application
The augmented triads' hub function explains their structural importance in 19th-century chromatic practice even when they are absent from or fleeting on the musical surface. In Schubert's Die Zauberharfe Overture, "transient augmented triads mark the transitions between regional harmonies" (p. 85). Weitzmann's 1853 treatise (*Der ubermassige Dreiklang*) was devoted precisely to this organizing function. The hub structure also explains why Cube Dance and the Tonnetz are "essentially equivalent modes for representing triadic progressions" (p. 89) with complementary advantages.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Augmented triads must be prolonged to serve as hubs
  **Correction**: The hub function is structural; even transient augmented triads mark regional transitions

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The four augmented triads are interchangeable
  **Clarification**: Each connects to a specific Weitzmann region and specific pair of hexatonic regions

- **Confusion**: Augmented triads occupy the same zones as consonant triads
  **Clarification**: They occupy their own voice-leading zones (0, 3, 6, 9), distinct from consonant triad zones
