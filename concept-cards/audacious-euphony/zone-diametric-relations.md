# Quick Definition
Triadic relationships where voice-leading zones are maximally distant (6 zones apart on the clock face), achieved by transposition by whole step (T2) or tritone (T6), representing the farthest possible leap in triadic voice-leading space.

# Core Definition
Zone-diametric relations occur between triads whose voice-leading zones differ by 6, the maximum possible distance modulo 12. On the clock-face representation (figure 5.24), these triads occupy opposite positions. Both T2 (whole step) and T6 (tritone) achieve this maximum distance of six voice-leading units. The compound paths reaching zone-diametric triads are HWHW or WHWH (four alternating transformation classes).

# Prerequisites
- **Voice-leading zones**: The metric space in which distance is measured

# Key Properties
1. Maximum distance = 6 zones (on a mod-12 scale)
2. Achieved by T2 (whole step) or T6 (tritone)
3. Both yield 6 units of voice-leading work
4. Compound path: HWHW or WHWH

# Construction / Recognition
From any triad, identify its zone, then find zone + 6 (mod 12):
- C major (zone 11): diametric zone = 5
- Options: transpose by tritone (Gb major, etc.) or by whole step (D major, etc.)
- Mahler's prescription: "No need to vainly grope" (p. 128) -- the model identifies maximum distance systematically

# Context & Application
Zone-diametric relations model the Romantic fascination with the remote and unattainable. They provide precise measurement for what composers sought intuitively: the effect of maximum tonal distance.

# Examples
- **Mahler Symphony No. 1 finale**: C major to D major (T2) described as "fallen from heaven, as though it had come from another world" (p. 127)
- Zone 5 is diametric to zone 11 (C major's zone)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: T6 (tritone) is the only zone-diametric transposition
  **Correction**: T2 (whole step) also achieves the same maximum zone distance of 6

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Zone distance is the same as root interval
  **Clarification**: Zone distance is about aggregate voice-leading displacement; T2 and T6 both yield 6 units despite different root intervals
