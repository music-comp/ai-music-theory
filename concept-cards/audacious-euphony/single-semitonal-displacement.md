# Quick Definition
The movement of exactly one voice by exactly one semitone between two triads, representing the minimal unit of voice-leading work in triadic progressions and the defining characteristic of the P and L transformations.

# Core Definition
Single semitonal displacement occurs when one and only one pitch class changes between two consonant triads, and that change is by one semitone. The resulting progression preserves two common tones and produces the smallest possible measurable change in pitch content. This is the defining characteristic of the P and L transformations and the basic building block of hexatonic cycles. Cohn notes that "motion between them thus involves a single unit of work" (p. 36).

# Prerequisites
- **minimal-work-relation** — Single semitonal displacement is the voice-leading motion that realizes the minimal-work relation

# Key Properties
1. Voice-leading work = 1 (one voice, one semitone)
2. Common tones retained = 2
3. Always connects triads of opposite mode
4. May be chromatic (P: E to Eb) or diatonic (L: C to B)
5. Total displacement in an n-step hexatonic progression = n semitones

# Construction / Recognition
- P transformation: third moves by chromatic semitone (same letter name)
- L transformation: root or fifth moves by diatonic semitone (different letter name)

Examples:
- C major to c minor: E->Eb (chromatic, P)
- C major to e minor: C->B (diatonic, L)
- c minor to Ab major: G->Ab (diatonic, L)
- ab minor to Ab major: Cb->C (chromatic, P)

# Context & Application
Single semitonal displacement is the fundamental building block of hexatonic progressions. Chaining these minimal motions produces the smooth, hypnotic voice-leading characteristic of pan-triadic harmony, allowing maximal harmonic change (mode switch) with minimal melodic disruption.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Looking for single-semitone connections between same-mode triads
  **Correction**: Single semitonal displacement always connects triads of opposite mode

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Conflating chromatic and diatonic semitones as identical operations
  **Clarification**: Both represent one unit of work, but chromatic (E-Eb, P) and diatonic (C-B, L) semitones differ in their Tonnetz representation and transformational identity
