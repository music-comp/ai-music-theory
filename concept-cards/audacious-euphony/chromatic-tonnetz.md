# Quick Definition
The Tonnetz in its closed, cylindrical/toroidal state where enharmonic and syntonic equivalences are accepted, all twenty-four consonant triads coexist without tonal hierarchy, and relations are governed by voice-leading parsimony rather than diatonic function.

# Core Definition
The chromatic Tonnetz models pan-triadic space by identifying ("gluing") enharmonically and syntonically equivalent points at opposite ends of the plane (Cohn, p. 191). In this configuration, "the [transformational] group as a whole disperses the functional 'significance' of [a single] triad among the harmonic consonances woven together to form its algebraic fabric; there is no one triad that forms a tonic for the group as a whole" (Hyer 1995, 127, qtd. p. 191). Each triad is connected to its L, P, and R associates as well as to its modally matched fifth.

# Prerequisites
- **Tonnetz**: The basic pitch-class graph with consonant-interval edges
- **Hexatonic and Weitzmann regions**: The structures organizing the chromatic space

# Key Properties
1. Enharmonic and syntonic equivalences accepted (points identified/glued)
2. No tonal hierarchy -- all triads coexist as equals
3. Relations governed by voice-leading work and common-tone retention
4. Cylindrical or toroidal topology (no half-twist, unlike the diatonic Mobius strip)
5. Hexatonic alleys, Weitzmann regions, and L/R chains provide navigational paths
6. Chromatic distance measured in semitones, not diatonic scale degrees

# Construction / Recognition
The chromatic Tonnetz is activated when "it becomes strained to hear relations between triads with respect to a given tonic triad" (Hyer 1989, 215). At that moment, "the circularized form of the lattice comes back into play" (p. 191). When triads collaborate in defining a tonic, the chromatic Tonnetz "decircularizes," extending its axes in all directions and imposing a "sense of perspective on the surrounding terrain" (Hyer 1995, 127-28).

On the convertible model: the chromatic Tonnetz is the state where diatonic walls are dissolved, Rings arrows retracted, and the listener navigates by voice-leading proximity.

# Context & Application
The chromatic Tonnetz is the default representation for the pan-triadic analyses throughout the book. It encompasses the hexatonic cycles (Chapter 2), Weitzmann regions (Chapter 4), and all transformational structures studied in Chapters 1-7. Chapter 8 shows how this chromatic space interacts with diatonic space through the convertible Tonnetz model.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: The chromatic Tonnetz is just the diatonic Tonnetz with more notes
  **Correction**: It has fundamentally different topology (cylindrical vs. Mobius) and organizing principles (voice-leading vs. functional)

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The chromatic Tonnetz eliminates tonal hearing
  **Clarification**: It represents one mode of hearing; diatonic hearing can reassert at any moment through encapsulation

- **Confusion**: Presence on the chromatic Tonnetz means a triad is atonal
  **Clarification**: It means tonal function is temporarily suspended, not absent; the triad retains its capacity for diatonic encapsulation
