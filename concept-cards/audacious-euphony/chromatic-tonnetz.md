# Quick Definition
The standard Neo-Riemannian Tonnetz optimized for chromatic relationships, where enharmonic and syntonic equivalents are identified ("glued") to create a cylindrical or toroidal topology that models pan-triadic voice-leading space.

# Formal Definition
Brian Hyer's (1989) chromatic Tonnetz positions each consonant triad as a point connected to its L, P, and R associates, as well as directly to its modally matched fifth. The Tonnetz models chromatic space by identifying enharmonically and syntonically equivalent points at opposite ends of the plane. Each such dimensional folding individually creates a cylinder. When a listener no longer hears triads in relation to a specific tonic, "the circularized form of the lattice comes back into play" (Hyer 1989, 215), and the triadic relations are measured in generic semitones rather than scale degrees.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
- Each triad occupies a unique position, connected to neighbors by L, P, R, and fifth relations
- Enharmonic identification: Db = C#, Gb = F#, etc.
- Syntonic identification: Two tones differing by syntonic comma (81:80) are treated as identical
- The resulting topology is cylindrical (or toroidal when both dimensions are closed)
- Chromatic distance is measured in semitones, not diatonic scale degrees
- The "functional 'significance' of a triad" is dispersed among all harmonies; "there is no one triad that forms a tonic for the group as a whole" (Hyer 1995, 127)

# Musical Context/Application
The chromatic Tonnetz is appropriate when:
- Music traverses pan-triadic progressions without establishing clear tonal centers
- Sequences or chains move through chromatic space via parsimonious voice leading
- Enharmonic reinterpretation occurs (crossing the "enharmonic seam")
- Hexatonic or Weitzmann-group transformations dominate

When triads collaborate in defining a tonic, the chromatic Tonnetz "decircularizes," extending its axes in all directions and imposing a "sense of perspective on the surrounding terrain" (Hyer 1995, 127-28).

# Examples
- The hexatonic cycles studied throughout Audacious Euphony are represented on the chromatic Tonnetz
- L/R chains (chapter 5) traverse the chromatic Tonnetz sequentially without establishing diatonic capsules
- Wagner's chromatic progressions in Parsifal and Tristan navigate the chromatic Tonnetz
- The interior of the Schubert C major Symphony Scherzo (mm. 117-140) operates in chromatic space where voice-leading efficiency governs, not diatonic function

# Common Confusions
- The chromatic Tonnetz does not replace the diatonic Tonnetz; both coexist as interpretive frameworks that can be activated depending on context
- Presence on the chromatic Tonnetz does not mean a triad lacks tonal function; it means that function is temporarily suspended or in abeyance
- The chromatic Tonnetz is not atonal; it represents a different organization of consonant triads based on voice-leading parsimony rather than acoustic root relations
