# Quick Definition
A passage that recedes from diatonic clarity into chromatic obscurity and then re-emerges, crossing the enharmonic seam in the process -- exemplified by the interior of the Schubert C major Symphony Scherzo.

# Formal Definition
The chromatic tunnel describes a passage that progressively loses contact with diatonic tonality, enters a zone of chromatic indeterminacy (often crossing an enharmonic seam), and then gradually reattunes to a diatonic key. The metaphor captures the listener's experience: diatonic clarity at the portals with chromatic obscurity at the center. From the diatonic perspective, the interior is dark and disorienting. But from the voice-leading perspective, the same interior represents a "transient emergence into the hexatonic light" -- maximum voice-leading efficiency and minimal chordal dissonance. The two perspectives are inverse: "the one focuses as the other blurs."

# Construction/Recognition
Typical structure:
1. Departure from diatonic clarity (increasing chromaticism, loss of cadential markers)
2. Broadening hypermeter signals loosening of diatonic grip
3. Central zone of maximum chromatic immersion (often hexatonic organization)
4. Restoration of shorter hypermetric units signals approaching diatonic re-emergence
5. Return to diatonic clarity through cadential labor

The enharmonic seam at the center presents an inscrutable junction from the diatonic standpoint: the same triad cannot be satisfactorily spelled in either the departing or arriving key. "This circle can never be squared by diatonic logic."

# Musical Context
The chromatic tunnel illustrates the central claim of the book: "two incommensurate ways of measuring triadic distance emerge respectively from two independent properties of consonant triads." Listening through the tunnel with reference to the framing key, one hears darkness and disorientation. Attuned to voice leading, one hears an "elimination of chordal dissonance and the elimination of whole steps in individual voices," a "reduction from two moving voices to one," and maximum common-tone retention at the center.

# Examples
- Schubert C major Symphony Scherzo (mm. 109-141): The passage recedes from C major, loses contact entirely at m. 117 as hypermeter broadens to six-bar units, and reattunes as four-bar units are restored at m. 141
- The c#/db minor triad at m. 123 is the crux: inscrutable from either diatonic portal, clear from the voice-leading standpoint
- The Ab and Db neighbors (mm. 109-110) initiate the chromatic departure; A major to d minor (mm. 129-135) signals re-emergence

# Common Confusions
- The "tunnel" metaphor reflects the diatonic perspective; from the voice-leading perspective, the same passage is a moment of clarity, not obscurity
- The enharmonic seam at the center is not a compositional problem but a feature that the double syntax model illuminates
- The chromatic tunnel is not a formal type but a phenomenological description of a common passage-type in chromatic tonal music
- Entering the tunnel does not require abandoning diatonic hearing; both perspectives operate simultaneously
