# Quick Definition
A passage that recedes from diatonic clarity into chromatic obscurity, crosses the enharmonic seam, and re-emerges into diatonic clarity -- appearing as darkness from the diatonic perspective but as "hexatonic light" from the voice-leading perspective.

# Core Definition
The chromatic tunnel describes a passage that progressively loses contact with diatonic tonality, enters a zone of chromatic indeterminacy often crossing an enharmonic seam, and then gradually reattunes to a diatonic key. The two perspectives are inverse: "What we initially conceived as a tunneling into and out of diatonic/tonal darkness becomes, from the standpoint of voice leading, a transient emergence into the hexatonic light" (p. 216). The interior represents maximum voice-leading efficiency: elimination of chordal dissonance, reduction from two moving voices to one (m. 117), and maximum common-tone retention (mm. 123-40) -- while the exterior portals feature greater voice-leading work synchronized with chordal dissonance.

# Prerequisites
- **Double syntax hypothesis**: The framework that makes the inversion of perspectives intelligible
- **Enharmonic seam**: The boundary crossed at the tunnel's center
- **Hexatonic cycle**: The organizing principle within the tunnel

# Key Properties
1. Diatonic clarity at portals, chromatic indeterminacy at center
2. Inverse perspectives: diatonic darkness = voice-leading light
3. The enharmonic seam at center is inscrutable from either diatonic portal
4. Hypermetric broadening typically signals loosening of diatonic grip
5. Restoration of shorter hypermetric units signals re-emergence

# Construction / Recognition
Typical structure:
1. Departure from diatonic clarity (increasing chromaticism, loss of cadential markers)
2. Broadening hypermeter signals loosening
3. Central zone of maximum chromatic immersion (often hexatonic)
4. Restoration of shorter hypermetric units
5. Return to diatonic clarity through cadential labor

The critical insight: "For chromatic triadic progressions that move through the enharmonic seam, seven-tone diatonic space provides no adequate stick for measuring distances" (p. 215).

# Context & Application
The chromatic tunnel illustrates the central claim of the book about two systems of distance measurement. It also provides the motivating example for the discussion of double syntax objections and code switching in the rest of Chapter 9.

# Examples
- **Schubert C major Symphony Scherzo** (mm. 109-141): Recedes from C major, loses contact at m. 117, reattunes at m. 141 (pp. 212-216)
- The c-sharp/d-flat minor at m. 123: inscrutable from either diatonic portal, clear from voice-leading standpoint

# Common Errors
- **Error**: The tunnel interior is disorganized or chaotic
  **Correction**: From the voice-leading perspective, it is the most organized part -- maximum efficiency

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: "Tunnel" implies the passage is inferior or problematic
  **Clarification**: It is a phenomenological description from one perspective; from the other perspective, the same passage is a moment of clarity
