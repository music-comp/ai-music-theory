# Quick Definition
The complementary relationship between the four hexatonic regions and four Weitzmann regions that enables perambulation through the entire triadic universe: individually each is a closed group, but together their transformations connect all twenty-four consonant triads under efficient voice leading.

# Core Definition
The four hexatonic regions and four Weitzmann regions are closed, mutually disconnected systems (mathematically, they are *groups*). On their own, they are "static, like a right and left leg hopping in place" (p. 101). In collaboration, Weitzmann and hexatonic transformations "gain the power of perambulation, unifying the triadic universe" (p. 101). This coordination produces a connected universe that makes good on the aphorism "any chord can go to any other chord" while affording a method of evaluating voice-leading distance, recognizing patterns of motion, and judging the coherence of progressions.

# Prerequisites
- **Hexatonic region**: Must understand the four hexatonic cycles as closed systems of six triads connected by L and P
- **Weitzmann region**: Must understand the four Weitzmann regions as closed systems of six triads connected by R and N
- **H-group transformations**: The transformations (L, P, H) that preserve hexatonic region
- **W-group transformations**: The transformations (R, N, S) that preserve Weitzmann region

# Key Properties
1. Each consonant triad belongs to exactly one hexatonic region AND one Weitzmann region
2. Neither system alone connects all twenty-four triads; both are needed for full connectivity
3. Augmented triads serve as the connection points (cardinal points) between regions
4. The interaction is perspectival (figure-ground): for the "hexatonic mariner," Weitzmann regions form bridges; for the "Weitzmann landlubber," hexatonic regions are rivers to cross
5. Alternating one H-group and one W-group transformation generates motion between adjacent regions

# Construction / Recognition
Starting from Weitzmann's original figure (reproduced as figure 5.1):
1. Observe that consonant triads displacing an augmented triad downward are positioned to its left; those displacing upward are to its right
2. Note each cluster appears twice; pruning redundancies leaves the twenty-four consonant triads
3. Each group of consonant triads separating adjacent augmented triads forms a hexatonic region
4. The figure is implicitly cyclic: same triads appear at left and right margins
5. These observations produce the circular figure 5.2, with augmented triads at cardinal points connected to their Weitzmann regions, and hexatonic regions shown as textured pools between them

# Context & Application
The interaction is the foundational insight enabling the unified model of chapter 5. It explains why typical nineteenth-century chromatic passages cross regional boundaries and why chromatic sequences alternate H-group and W-group transformations. The coordination underlies all subsequent concepts: Cube Dance, voice-leading zones, chromatic sequences, and transformational substitutions.

# Examples
- **Figure 5.2** (p. 103): Four Weitzmann "water bugs" in union with four hexatonic "pools," showing the figure-ground relation
- **Schubert, Overture to Die Zauberharfe** (1821): Crosses between regions via transient augmented triads, demonstrating how the interaction works in practice (figures 5.4-5.5)
- **The amphibious vehicle metaphor** (p. 102): "To circumnavigate the entire complex, one need only locate an appropriately amphibious vehicle"

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming hexatonic and Weitzmann regions are hierarchically ordered
  **Correction**: They are complementary perspectives on the same space; which is "figure" and which "ground" depends on the analyst's viewpoint

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking one system is more fundamental than the other
  **Clarification**: Neither system alone connects all triads; both are mathematically necessary for complete connectivity
- **Confusion**: Assuming the figure-ground metaphor is fixed
  **Clarification**: The metaphor is perspectival -- the same musical passage can be understood from either standpoint
