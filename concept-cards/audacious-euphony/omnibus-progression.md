# Quick Definition
The omnibus progression (also called the "devil's mill") is a sequential template that transposes dominant seventh chords by minor third, featuring contrary semitonal motion in two voices while two voices remain stationary, producing balanced voice leading without net directional shift.

# Core Definition
The omnibus progression is a chromatic sequence connecting dominant seventh chords related by T3 (minor third transposition). It is the first of three sequential templates Cohn presents for Tristan-genus chords (figure 7.8(a)). Unlike the T11 and T5 sequences that alternate V7 and half-diminished seventh chords, the omnibus uses only dominant seventh chords and produces balanced (non-circumnavigatory) voice leading through contrary motion. The progression's distinctive voice-leading properties derive from a principle Cohn italicizes: the minor third "divides the octave into as many equal parts as a seventh chord has tones" (p. 167).

# Key Properties
1. Only dominant seventh chords (no half-diminished)
2. Minor third transposition between successive chords (T3)
3. Two voices stationary, two move by semitone in opposite directions (contrary motion)
4. No net upshift or downshift (balanced voice leading)
5. Cannot circumnavigate 4-Cube Trio
6. Parallels the balanced voice leading of triadic T4 progressions

# Construction / Recognition
How to construct the omnibus:
1. Begin with any dominant seventh chord (e.g., C7: C-E-G-Bb)
2. Hold two voices stationary
3. Move remaining two voices by semitone in opposite directions
4. Result: dominant seventh chord a minor third away (Eb7: Eb-G-Bb-Db)

Recognition features:
- Only dominant seventh chords (no alternation with half-diminished)
- Minor third transposition between successive chords
- Contrary semitonal motion in two voices
- Chromaticized voice exchanges may embellish the progression

# Context & Application
The omnibus arises already in the 18th century and was extensively elaborated by 19th-century composers. When embellished by chromaticized voice exchanges, it becomes the traditional "omnibus" or "devil's mill" (Wason 1985, Telesco 1998). The progression shares anomalous features with triadic major-third transpositions: just as the major third divides the octave into as many equal parts as a triad has notes (producing contrary motion), the minor third divides the octave into as many parts as a seventh chord has notes. This structural parallel between T4 (triadic) and T3 (tetrachordal) is fundamental to the book's generalization from triads to seventh chords.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: "Omnibus" and "devil's mill" are different progressions
  **Clarification**: They are traditional names for the embellished version of the same T3 seventh-chord progression

- **Confusion**: The omnibus involves all Tristan-genus chord types
  **Clarification**: It uses only dominant seventh chords, unlike the T11 and T5 sequences that alternate V7 and half-diminished seventh
