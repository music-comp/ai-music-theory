# Quick Definition
Cohn's methodological invitation to temporarily suspend overlearned habits of classical diatonic theory in order to foreground the alternative voice-leading properties of consonant triads.

# Core Definition
Because classical diatonic theory is typically the first and often the only music theory one encounters, "its status as *theory*, a way of looking, easily hardens into *nature*: the way that music is" (Cohn, p. 12). Cohn occasionally invites readers to "forget" that major and minor triads are acoustic *consonances* generated from *roots* that occupy a position in a *diatonic* scale with respect to some *tonic*. This is "a cultivated and strategic denial, motivated by the desire to prevent our default habits from flooding back in and drowning those properties and relations that I wish to foreground" (p. 12).

# Key Properties
1. Involves bracketing four interrelated concepts: consonance, roots, diatonic scales, and tonic function
2. Is temporary and strategic, not a permanent rejection of classical theory
3. Compared to "one that physicians make when they bracket off the aesthetic qualities of the bodies that they are examining" (p. 12)
4. As the book progresses, readers "will increasingly find themselves on familiar ground, although their perspective on that ground will have been altered" (p. 12)

# Construction / Recognition
The technique involves bracketing:
1. That triads are acoustic **consonances**
2. That they are generated from **roots**
3. That roots occupy positions in **diatonic** scales
4. That scales relate to some **tonic**

Throughout the book, Cohn continues to use root-based naming (e.g., "C major") as heuristic convenience, while noting that "roots have no theoretical status in the theory of pan-triadic syntax" and that "heuristics are not equal to ontology" (p. 60).

# Context & Application
Strategic forgetting is necessary because the properties Cohn wishes to foreground (near evenness, voice-leading proximity, hexatonic organization) are invisible under the default lens of classical theory. The technique enables a fresh perspective on chromatic harmony without requiring rejection of classical theory.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 12): The physician analogy: just as physicians bracket aesthetic qualities of bodies being examined, Cohn brackets acoustic qualities of triads being analyzed.

**Example 2** (p. 60): Cohn will "continue to name them individually by their roots, even though roots have no theoretical status in the theory of pan-triadic syntax."

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Permanently abandoning classical theoretical concepts when analyzing pan-triadic passages
  **Correction**: The forgetting is strategic and temporary; classical concepts are reintegrated in later chapters

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking strategic forgetting is naive or ignorant
  **Clarification**: It is "cultivated and strategic" — performed by someone who knows full well what is being set aside
- **Confusion**: Believing the technique claims that consonance, roots, and keys are unreal
  **Clarification**: It claims only that they are not the only relevant properties and can occlude other important ones
