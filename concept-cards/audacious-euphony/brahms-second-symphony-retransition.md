# Quick Definition
The retransition of Brahms's Second Symphony (mm. 246-302) executes a comprehensive counterclockwise tour of two overlapping pitch retention neighborhoods centered on D and A, sounding every triad in the double neighborhood in strict four-bar hypermeter.

# Core Definition
The passage (figures 6.4-6.7) features consistent four-bar hypermeter with three rotating thematic modules (A: dyadic hemiola, B: violin theme, C: lyrical with internal progressions). Five twelve-bar hypermeasures organize the retransition around timpani pedals on D then A then D. The harmonies revolve "kaleidoscopically about the timpani pedals without privileging the tonic and dominant triads that they traditionally imply" (p. 136). The synoptic figure 6.7 reveals "a comprehensive counterclockwise tour of the space, from subdominant to subdominant" sounding "every triad in the double neighborhood, and no triads outside of it" (p. 137).

# Prerequisites
- **Pitch retention loop**: The touring mechanism
- **Double neighborhood**: The two overlapping hexagonal structures

# Key Properties
1. Strict four-bar hypermeter throughout
2. Three rotating thematic modules (A, B, C) in twelve-bar units
3. Timpani sounds only D and A, but as thirds and fifths (not roots) of various triads
4. Complete counterclockwise tour of double neighborhood
5. FA and F#A dyads (mm. 282-289) resist triadic interpretation; Tonnetz uses ovals

# Construction / Recognition
- Hypermeasure 1: D loop begins (G major -> g minor -> Bb major)
- Hypermeasure 2: Exits D neighborhood, enters A neighborhood
- Hypermeasure 3: A neighborhood tour; f# minor toggles with A major
- Hypermeasure 4: Dyadic ambiguity (FA/F#A); Tonnetz ovals
- Hypermeasure 5: D major returns; completes D loop (b minor -> G major); plagal cadence to recapitulation

# Context & Application
The analysis demonstrates the Tonnetz's ability to handle dyadic ambiguity through oval notation and shows how a conventional timpani pedal (D-A-D) can mask an adventurous harmonic surface organized by pitch retention.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: The timpani D-A-D indicates conventional tonic-dominant harmony
  **Correction**: These pitches serve as thirds and fifths of non-tonic triads

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Every harmony must have a definite triadic interpretation
  **Clarification**: The Tonnetz accommodates dyadic ambiguity through oval notation (figure 6.6b)
