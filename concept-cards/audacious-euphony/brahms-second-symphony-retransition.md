# Quick Definition
The retransition of Brahms's Second Symphony (mm. 246-302) executes a comprehensive counterclockwise tour of two overlapping pitch retention neighborhoods (centered on D and A), sounding every triad in the double neighborhood while maintaining a strict four-bar hypermeter.

# Formal Definition
The passage is structured by:
1. A consistent four-bar hypermeter articulated by harmonic changes and periodic rotation of three thematic/textural modules (A: dyadic hemiola figure, B: violin theme, C: lyrical module with internal progressions)
2. Five twelve-bar hypermeasures creating the retransition's architecture
3. Kaleidoscopic harmonizations around timpani pedals (D then A then D) that avoid privileging tonic and dominant triads
4. A counterclockwise tour of the double neighborhood from subdominant G major back to subdominant, with back-filling around dominant A

# Construction/Recognition
- Hypermeasure 1 (mm. 246-257): Tours part of D retention loop: G major to g minor to Bb major
- Hypermeasure 2 (mm. 258-269): Revisits d minor, exits D neighborhood, enters A neighborhood
- Hypermeasure 3 (mm. 270-281): Continues A neighborhood tour; f# minor established at m. 270; toggles between f# minor and A major; returns to F major
- Hypermeasure 4 (mm. 282-289): Dyadic A module, ambiguous FA/F#A dyads
- Hypermeasure 5 (mm. 290-302): D major returns, completes D retention loop through b minor to G major, plagal cadence to recapitulation

# Musical Context
The passage demonstrates how pitch retention loops provide coherent organization for an extended chromatic retransition. The timpani's conventional D-A-D pedal masks a remarkably unconventional harmonic surface. The Tonnetz's ability to assign determinate but triadically neutral locations to dyads (using ovals rather than triangles) proves analytically valuable.

# Common Confusions
- The passage sounds every triad in the double neighborhood but no triads outside it
- The timpani's D-A-D suggests conventional tonic-dominant but the harmonies are anything but conventional
- Dyadic passages (mm. 282-289) resist definitive triadic interpretation; the Tonnetz accommodates this ambiguity
