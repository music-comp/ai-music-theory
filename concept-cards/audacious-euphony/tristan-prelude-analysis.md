# Quick Definition
Cohn's analysis of the Tristan Prelude opening, following Boretz (1972), interprets the first six chords as movements within and between Boretz regions: five chords oscillating within the B-D-F-G# region, then one octatonic bridge to a new region.

# Core Definition
The **Tristan Prelude analysis** applies Boretz's (1972) observations systematically. Chords 1-5 are single semitonal displacements of a single equal division, B-D-F-G#, sharing membership in a Boretz region. Each chord contains three tones of the core diminished seventh plus one "spoiler." The progression alternates upshift and downshift, balancing between voice-leading zones 3 and 1. Chord 6 (C#7) marks an "interregional modulation" as a new majority diminished seventh complex (A-C-D#-F#) takes over. "The coast of Cornwall is littered with the detritus of ill-equipped music-theoretic vessels that perished in search of a suitable tonic at which to moor" (p. 167); Boretz approached those shores with a different conveyance.

# Prerequisites
- **Tristan genus**: The system of chords being analyzed
- **Boretz region**: The regional structure organizing the analysis
- **Spoiler tone**: The analytical tool for tracking displacement
- **Boretz-group transformations**: The operations connecting the chords

# Key Properties
1. Chords 1-5 all displace B-D-F-G# diminished seventh (Region III)
2. Balanced oscillation between zones 3 and 1
3. Successive spoilers: D# (chord 1), E (chord 2), F# (chord 3), G (chord 4), C (chord 5)
4. Chord 6 marks interregional modulation to new diminished seventh complex
5. The paradigm for understanding Tristan-genus progressions without tonal frameworks

# Construction / Recognition
Chord-by-chord (Figure 7.9, p. 167):
1. Fø7 (F-A-B-D#): spoiler D# above D
2. E7 (E-G#-B-D): spoiler E below F
3. Aø7 (A-C-Eb-F#): spoiler F# above F
4. G7 (G-B-D-F): spoiler G below G#
5. Aø7 variant: spoiler C above B
6. C#7 (C#-E#-G#-B): new complex -- interregional modulation

Transformations (Figure 7.11): S3(2) - R* - S3(2) - R* - octatonic bridge

# Context & Application
The analysis establishes the paradigm for understanding how seventh chords can be analyzed without functional theory. Wagner's initial sketch of the Prelude corroborates the virtual presence of the core diminished seventh. "Diminished seventh chords often replace Tristan-genus chords in contexts where the latter normally appear, as in m. 68 of the Prelude" (p. 170 n. 11).

# Examples
- **Chords 1-2**: D# spoiler resolves to D while F becomes E -- cross-species progression via S3(2) (pp. 168-169)
- **Chord 5-6 transition**: First interregional modulation as majority complex shifts from B-D-F-G# to A-C-D#-F# (p. 169)
- **Zone tracking**: 3-1-3-1-3-5 -- the jump to zone 5 marks the modulation (p. 172)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming this analysis denies functional hearings
  **Correction**: It provides an alternative framework, not a refutation of tonal analysis

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking "modulation" here means tonal modulation
  **Clarification**: "Interregional modulation" means moving between Boretz regions, not between keys
