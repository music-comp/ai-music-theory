# Quick Definition
Cohn's analysis of the Tristan Prelude opening, following Boretz (1972), interprets the first six chords as movements within and between Boretz regions, demonstrating balanced voice leading within a region and modulation between regions via octatonic transformation.

# Formal Definition
The **Tristan Prelude analysis** applies Boretz's observations systematically:
1. Chords 1-5 occupy a single Boretz region (centered on B-D-F-G#)
2. Each chord is a single semitonal displacement ("spoiling") of the core diminished seventh
3. Chords alternate upshift and downshift (balanced voice leading)
4. Chord 6 marks an "interregional modulation" to a new Boretz region

This analysis establishes the paradigm for understanding Tristan-genus progressions without tonal/functional frameworks.

# Construction/Recognition
Chord-by-chord analysis:
1. Fø7 (F-A-B-D#): spoiler D# (above D)
2. E7 (E-G#-B-D): spoiler E (below F)
3. Aø7 (A-C-Eb-F#): spoiler F# (above F)
4. G7 (G-B-D-F): spoiler G (below G#)
5. A-C-D#-F# = Aø7 again: spoiler C (above B)
6. C#7 (C#-E#-G#-B): new core - marks "interregional modulation"

Voice-leading zones:
- Zones alternate: 3 → 1 → 3 → 1 → 3 → 5
- Balanced oscillation between zones 3 and 1 through first five chords
- Modulation to new region at chord 6

# Musical Context/Application
The analysis demonstrates:
- How seventh chords can be analyzed without functional theory
- The "virtual presence" of diminished seventh chords
- Boretz-region membership as a structural principle
- The distinction between intra-regional and inter-regional motion

Historical significance:
- Wagner "blew open the gates" to this harmonic field
- Boretz's 1972 analysis reverses Rameau's generative arrow
- The Tristan opening became paradigmatic for voice-leading approaches to dissonance

# Examples
**Chords 1-2**: The D# spoiler of chord 1 resolves to D, but F simultaneously becomes E, creating a new spoiler. Both chords "spoil" the B-D-F-G# diminished seventh, one above, one below.

**Chords 3-4**: Same pattern with different spoilers (F# and G). The progression continues alternating within the Boretz region.

**Chord 5-6 transition**: The Aø7 (chord 5) shares only B with the new majority complex (A-C-D#-F#). This B becomes the spoiler of C#7, marking interregional modulation.

# Common Confusions
- The analysis does not deny functional hearings; it provides an alternative framework
- The diminished seventh "core" may not appear literally; its influence is virtual
- "Modulation" here means moving between Boretz regions, not tonal modulation
- Boretz's analysis predates and inspires the systematic framework Cohn develops
