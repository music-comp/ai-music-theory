# Quick Definition
Major-third transposition (T4 or T8) is the only interval of transposition that produces balanced (contrary) voice leading between triads of the same mode, because the major third divides the octave into as many equal parts as the triad has notes.

# Formal Definition
When a triad is transposed by major third (4 semitones) or its complement (8 semitones), the resulting voice leading is uniquely balanced: one voice ascends, one descends, and one remains stationary. This is because "it divides the octave into as many equal parts as the triad has notes" (p. 38). Transposition by any other interval produces uniform voice leading where all moving voices move in the same direction.

# Construction/Recognition
Mathematical basis:
- Octave = 12 semitones
- Triad = 3 notes
- 12 / 3 = 4 semitones = major third
- Therefore major-third transposition uniquely produces balanced voice leading

Voice-leading comparison (from C major):
| Target | Interval | Voice 1 | Voice 2 | Voice 3 | Motion Type |
|--------|----------|---------|---------|---------|-------------|
| E major | T4 (M3 up) | C->B (down) | E->E (hold) | G->G# (up) | Balanced |
| Ab major | T8 (m6 up) | C->C (hold) | E->Eb (down) | G->Ab (up) | Balanced |
| Eb major | T3 (m3 up) | C->Bb (down) | E->Eb (down) | G->G (hold) | Uniform |
| F major | T5 (P4 up) | C->C (hold) | E->F (up) | G->A (up) | Uniform |

# Musical Context/Application
This property explains why major-third cycles (hexatonic cycles) have distinctive voice-leading characteristics that set them apart from minor-third cycles (octatonic systems) and other chromatic sequences. The balanced voice leading contributes to the "floating" quality of hexatonic progressions.

# Examples
From the text (pp. 37-38):
"Cycles generated by major thirds exhibit balanced voice leading, alternating between up and down, whereas those generated by minor thirds lead their voices in a uniform direction."

"In transposing by major third from C major to E major, under idealized voice leading one voice moves up (G -> G#) and one moves down (C -> B), while the third voice, E, holds its place. In transposing by minor third from C major to Eb major, again under idealized voice leading, both moving voices move down (C -> Bb and E -> Eb) while G holds its place."

"The latter case is the norm. Under least-motion voice leading, recursive transposition by any interval other than major third generates uniformly directed voice leading. It is transposition by four or eight semitones that is special" (p. 37).

# Common Confusions
- T4 (up by major third) and T8 (down by major third / up by minor sixth) are complementary and both produce balanced voice leading
- The "third relation" literature often conflates major-third and minor-third cycles, obscuring this fundamental distinction
- The specialness of major-third transposition is a mathematical consequence, not a stylistic choice
- Balanced voice leading applies to same-mode triads; opposite-mode relations (L, P) have different properties
