# Quick Definition
An unordered collection of six consonant triads comprising the members of a hexatonic cycle - three major and three minor triads related by single semitonal displacement.

# Formal Definition
A hexatonic region is an unordered set containing the six consonant triads that form a hexatonic cycle. Unlike the cycle (which specifies an ordering), the region treats these triads as an unordered collection. Each hexatonic region contains three major triads (related by major third transposition) and three minor triads (also related by major third transposition). The pitch classes of all six triads combine to form the hexatonic scale. There are four hexatonic regions, partitioning the 24 consonant triads.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
The four hexatonic regions:
1. Northern: {C, c, E, e, Ab, ab} - pitch classes {C, Db, E, G, Ab, B}
2. Eastern: {Db, db, F, f, A, a} - pitch classes {Db, D, F, Ab, A, C}
3. Southern: {D, d, Gb, gb, Bb, bb} - pitch classes {D, Eb, Gb, A, Bb, Db}
4. Western: {Eb, eb, G, g, B, b} - pitch classes {Eb, E, G, Bb, B, D}

Properties:
- Each region contains exactly 6 triads (3 major, 3 minor)
- Major triads are related by T4 (major third transposition)
- Minor triads are related by T4
- Every pair of triads in a region shares membership in the same hexatonic scale

# Musical Context/Application
Hexatonic regions provide:
- A natural grouping of triads by voice-leading proximity
- The domain for H-group transformations (L, P, H)
- A partition of the 24 consonant triads into four equal groups
- Connection to the hexatonic scale (the union of the region's pitch classes)

Triads within a hexatonic region can progress smoothly via L and P transformations; the hexatonic pole (H) connects the two triads in the region that share no common tones.

# Examples
- The Northern hexatonic region {C, c, E, e, Ab, ab} contains pairs like C-c (P), C-e (L), and C-ab (H)
- Wagner's Magic Sleep music moves within a single hexatonic region
- Hexatonic regions appear in Schubert, Brahms, and other 19th-century composers

# Common Confusions
- The hexatonic region is unordered; the hexatonic cycle specifies an order
- Regions and cycles contain the same triads but emphasize different aspects
- Hexatonic regions overlap with Weitzmann regions (triads related by T4 belong to both)
- The "hexatonic scale" refers to the 6 pitch classes, not the 6 triads
