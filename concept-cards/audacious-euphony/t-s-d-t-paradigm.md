# Quick Definition
The fundamental diatonic cadential pattern (tonic to subdominant to dominant to tonic), mapped on the Tonnetz as two leftward arrows connected by a longer rightward arrow, representing the standard confirmation of diatonic encapsulation described by Gottfried Weber as "Attunement of the Ear to a Key."

# Core Definition
Standard confirmation of diatonic encapsulation comes in the form of a "tonic -> subdominant -> dominant -> tonic (abbreviated T -> S -> D -> T) progression represented on the parallelogram by two leftward arrows, one initial and one terminal, linked by a longer rightward arrow connecting its two sides" (Brower 2008, 71, discussed in Cohn, p. 194). The leftward arrows represent upshifting voice leading; the rightward arrow traverses the syntonic comma, connecting subdominant to dominant.

# Prerequisites
- **Diatonic Tonnetz**: The space on which T-S-D-T is mapped
- **Rings arrows**: The semantic forces flowing along these directed paths

# Key Properties
1. Two leftward (upshifting) arrows: T to S and D to T
2. One rightward (overshooting) arrow: S to D, crossing the syntonic comma
3. Two interpretations: planar (with disjunctive overshoot) and Mobius (continuous motion)
4. Confirms diatonic encapsulation -- the standard "attunement" progression
5. Serves as the baseline against which chromatic departures are measured

# Construction / Recognition
**Planar interpretation**: S to D involves a disjunctive overshoot across the plane. Riemann: the subdominant is "a stretched bow which slings the arrow beyond the mark" (Riemann 1893). Voice-leading correlate: "the challenge that must be negotiated without a common-tone anchor" (p. 196).

**Mobius interpretation**: S to D continues uniformly around the backside of the strip. Voice-leading correlate: perpetually upshifting motion reconstructing Rameau's *double emploi* (p. 196).

# Context & Application
T-S-D-T is the minimal expression of diatonic syntax on the Tonnetz. It appears prominently in the analysis of Liszt's Consolation no. 3, basic idea (mm. 3-6), where the progression maps onto Figure 8.7(b) with starred supplementary dissonances showing the interaction of subdominant and dominant regions.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: T-S-D-T is the only way to confirm encapsulation
  **Correction**: Other progressions can confirm; T-S-D-T is the standard or paradigmatic confirmation

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The rightward S-to-D arrow is a separate type of motion from the leftward arrows
  **Clarification**: On the Mobius strip, it is continuous with the leftward motion (around the backside)
