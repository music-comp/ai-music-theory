# Quick Definition
The opening of Liszt's Faust Symphony (1854) explicitly inverts the classical consonance/dissonance hierarchy, with augmented triads dominating the surface while consonant minor triads occupy the position of suspensions in a troped suspension figure.

# Formal Definition
The Faust opening presents "two slow rotations through three segments of material" (p. 69), with augmented triads dominating. The passage draws exclusively on two augmented triads (CEG# and FAC#) combining to form a hexatonic collection. Four boxed figures in the score show a suspension figure where "consonance and dissonance [are] inverted with respect to formal function (but not metric location): the position of preparation and resolution is occupied by dissonant augmented triads; that of the suspension, by consonant minor triads" (Morgan 1976).

# Construction/Recognition
Structure of the opening (Figure 3.4):
- Two rotations through three segments (A, B, C)
- Each segment approximately four measures
- Pitch-class pool: CEG# and FAC# augmented triads = hexatonic collection
- Exception: mm. 1 and 13 briefly exit the hexatonic collection

Inverted suspension figure:
- Traditional: consonance (preparation) -> dissonance (suspension) -> consonance (resolution)
- Faust: dissonance (preparation/augmented) -> consonance (suspension/minor triad) -> dissonance (resolution/augmented)

The metric positions are preserved (dissonance still falls on strong beat expectations), but the chord types are swapped.

# Musical Context
The Faust opening represents an extreme case of consonance/dissonance reciprocity, where the expected hierarchy is explicitly and systematically inverted. This "famous and explicit invert[sion] of the values classically accorded these sonorities" (p. 69) became influential for later chromatic composers.

# Examples
From the text (pp. 69-70):

Direct quote: "The passage consists of two slow rotations through three segments of material (marked A, B, and C in the example), each of which extends approximately four measures. Augmented triads dominate the surface. Moreover, with the exception of mm. 1 and 13, the pitch-class pool for the entire passage draws exclusively on the CEG# and FAC# augmented triads, which combine to form a hexatonic collection."

Rimsky-Korsakov's Antar (1868) likely modeled on Faust:
"Like Faust, [Antar] begins with two slow rotations through a series of three texturally differentiated segments, each approximately four measures long. The second rotation transposes the first segment by a minor sixth (down in Antar, up in Faust); the final two segments are then transposed upward by major third."

# Common Confusions
- The inversion is of functional position, not of metric placement; suspensions still occur on expected beats
- The passage is not "atonal" - it uses consonant and dissonant triads, just with reversed roles
- The hexatonic collection provides the pitch-class framework, but the structural inversion is the innovative element
- Later Romantic composers often allude to this passage without replicating its systematic inversion
