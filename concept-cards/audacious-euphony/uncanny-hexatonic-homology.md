# Quick Definition
Cohn identifies a structural homology between the hexatonic pole's musical behavior and Parsifal's dramatic content: what ought to be dissonant is unaccountably consonant, paralleling the opera's pervasive life/death ambiguity and the Freudian uncanny.

# Core Definition
Cohn identifies a "homology between the structures of hexatonic poles and of the circumstances that they portray" (p. 165). At many levels, *Parsifal* "straddles the border between life and death": transubstantiation holds blood to be alive from a dead source; Amfortas teeters on death's brink; Titurel inhabits a tomb; Kundry is shielded from death by a curse; Klingsor conjures life from desert wasteland. "This is the logic of the uncanny, as theorized by Ernst Jentsch and Freud: what ought to be dead is unaccountably living" (p. 166). The hexatonic pole produces a parallel musical uncanny: "When a consonant triad progresses to its hexatonic pole, its root is displaced down to the raised seventh degree, while its fifth is displaced upward to the flatted sixth degree. The resulting interval ought to be a dissonant diminished seventh. But if we perceive this new chord as a triad, then we are perceiving the resulting interval as a consonant major sixth. What ought to be dissonant is unaccountably consonant" (p. 166).

# Key Properties
1. Life/death duality mapped onto consonance/dissonance
2. The hexatonic pole's progression from expected dissonance to perceived consonance mirrors the uncanny
3. Alfred Lorenz: "during the lingering on the notes that are initially understood as dissonant, the chord cleanses itself, without any motion, into the most radiant beauty" (1933, 89) (p. 166)
4. The homology is structural, not merely metaphorical -- both domains involve the same logic of categorical boundary violation

# Construction / Recognition
The homology operates through:
1. **Dramatic level**: Characters and objects straddle life/death boundary
2. **Musical level**: Hexatonic pole straddles consonance/dissonance boundary
3. **Structural parallel**: "What ought to be X is unaccountably Y" (dead/living, dissonant/consonant)
4. **Freudian uncanny**: Both involve the return of what should be absent (life from death, consonance from expected dissonance)

# Context & Application
The homology provides the interpretive payoff for the technical motivic network analysis. It answers the question: "there it is again, and there it is again, and again... who cares?" The value of motivic connections is "not only what they are but what they allow the analyst to do. To see a connection is to open a door; one still needs to walk through it" (p. 164). Walking through the door reveals the homology between musical structure and dramatic meaning.

# Examples
- **Parsifal's four central elements**: Grail, Communion, blood, agony -- all depicted by hexatonic poles, all bound to the life/death theme (pp. 164-166)
- **Kundry's death**: Wagner describes it as *Entseelung* (de-souling) of a zombie; the music uses hexatonic poles (p. 166)
- **Lorenz on the Grail distortion**: The chord "cleanses itself, without any motion, into the most radiant beauty" -- consonance from expected dissonance (p. 166)

# Common Confusions

  **Clarification**: The uncanny operates at the structural/analytical level; whether listeners perceive eeriness is a separate question about reception
