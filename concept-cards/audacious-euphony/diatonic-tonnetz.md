# Quick Definition
A geometric representation of diatonic pitch relations formed as a parallelogram on the Tonnetz, encapsulating seven tones and six consonant triads, generated from a seed triad through recursive fifth transpositions and achieving closure through acceptance of the syntonic comma.

# Core Definition
The diatonic Tonnetz is generated in stages from a seed node (Brower 2008). A purely tuned triad (e.g., C major) is transposed by its most acoustically powerful interval (the perfect fifth), yielding adjacent triads. The resulting seven-tone figure forms a parallelogram whose interior is tiled into six consonant triads. The parallelogram "encapsulates its contents into a microecological hothouse, activating the internal dynamics signified by Rings arrows" (Cohn, p. 194). Closure is achieved by accepting syntonic images (tones differing by a syntonic comma) as identical, and further by accepting the pseudo-fifth (B-F) as a perfect fifth, which creates a Mobius strip topology.

# Prerequisites
- **Brower's Tonnetz genesis**: The process by which the diatonic Tonnetz is built
- **Syntonic image**: The relation between tones at remote corners that must be identified for closure
- **Consonant triad**: The building block that tiles the interior

# Key Properties
1. Parallelogram shape encapsulates the diatonic collection
2. Six consonant triads tile the interior (three major, three minor under classical tonality)
3. Only central triads (sheltered from unstable corners) can serve as tonic
4. Leftward motion (D to T to S) is entirely upshifting voice leading
5. Edge-adjacent triads differ by a single unit of voice-leading work (diatonic step)
6. The Mobius strip topology creates a continuous loop of diatonic mediants (Agmon 1995)

# Construction / Recognition
The T-S-D-T paradigm maps onto the Tonnetz as two leftward arrows (T to S, D to T) connected by a longer rightward arrow (S to D). Two interpretations exist:
- **Planar**: S-to-D involves an overshooting reversal across the plane (Riemann's "stretched bow which slings the arrow beyond the mark")
- **Mobius**: S-to-D continues uniformly around the backside of the strip

The voice-leading correlate of the Mobius conception is the perpetually upshifting figure that reconstructs Rameau's *double emploi* (Figure 8.6b, p. 196).

# Context & Application
Within the encapsulated diatonic space, "the classically attuned ear" experiences hierarchization of tones and chords, intuitions about context-free proximity, syntax governing component ordering, and Rings arrows with their full semantic charge: "energy, gravity, attraction, magnetism, discharge, orientation, center, departure and arrival, return, passing, neighboring, leading, deception, completion/incompletion, suspension, finality, stability/instability" (p. 195).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating the diatonic Tonnetz as simply a subset of the chromatic Tonnetz
  **Correction**: It has its own generative logic based on acoustic consonance, not voice-leading parsimony

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The Mobius strip topology is merely a visualization choice
  **Clarification**: It captures a genuine topological difference: how S connects to D via common tone
