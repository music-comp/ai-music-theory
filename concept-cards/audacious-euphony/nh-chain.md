# Quick Definition
An **N**/**H** chain is a chromatic sequence generated by alternating Nebenverwandt and Hexatonic Pole transformations, producing transposition by minor third (T3), involving one classically normative and one maverick transformation.

# Formal Definition
The **N**/**H** chain combines a W-group transformation (**N**, classically normative) with an H-group transformation (**H**, maverick), generating a T3 chromatic sequence. Like the **P**/**R** chain, it produces minor-third transpositions, but with a different voice-leading profile. The chain involves one rare transformation (**H**), which means that either the within-phrase or between-phrase progression employs maverick voice leading.

# Construction/Recognition
Alternate **N** and **H** transformations:
- The normative **N** (major triad to its minor subdominant) can occur within phrases
- The maverick **H** (hexatonic pole) can occur across phrase boundaries
- Or their positions can be swapped, with **H** within phrases and **N** between them

# Musical Context
The **N**/**H** chain appears less frequently than the **P**/**R** chain due to the involvement of the maverick **H** transformation. It typically emerges in music of Schubert and later composers.

# Examples
- Schubert, *Drei Klavierstucke*, D. 946, no. 2, mm. 46-52: **N** within segments, **H** across phrase boundaries (figure 5.7)
- Liszt, *Malediction* for piano and strings (seven measures after rehearsal B): **H** within phrases, **N** between them
- Rimsky-Korsakov, *Skazka*: **H** within phrases, **N** between them
- Bruckner, "Ecce Sacerdos" (from rehearsal 2): **H** within phrases, **N** between them

# Common Confusions
- The **N**/**H** and **P**/**R** chains both produce T3 transpositions but have different voice-leading profiles
- The placement of **N** vs. **H** relative to phrase boundaries is compositionally significant and can be swapped
- **H** is the "maverick" transformation that makes this chain rarer than the **P**/**R** chain
