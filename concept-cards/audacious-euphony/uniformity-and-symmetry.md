# Quick Definition
Fetis's concepts of uniformity and symmetry describe the forces that can override tonal hierarchy, allowing sequences to suspend the distinction between consonance and dissonance through systematic repetition.

# Core Definition
Francois-Joseph Fetis identified uniformity and symmetry as compositional resources that can "override the forces of tonality." In a sequence, "the succession and... movement fix the attention of the mind, which holds on to the form so strongly that any irregularity of tonality is not noticed.... The mind, absorbed in the contemplation of the progressive series, momentarily loses the feeling of tonality.... The attention of the musical sense is diverted from the feeling of tonality by symmetry of movement and succession" (Fetis 2008 [1844], 27, 30, cited pp. 65-66). In such contexts, "a diminished fifth no longer requires resolution; in this context, its behavior is indistinguishable from that of the perfect fifth" (p. 66).

# Prerequisites
- **Consonant triad**: Must understand normal tonal hierarchy to grasp how uniformity overrides it

# Key Properties
1. Sequential repetition diverts the mind from tonal feeling
2. Uniformity levels the distinction between consonance and dissonance
3. In diatonic sequences, uniformity is "kept in check" by the diatonic scale
4. In chromatic sequences, "the law of uniformity has a monolithic force" (p. 67)
5. Rapid chromatic pitch-class turnover "ruptures any ability of the diatonic collection to hold a focus on a particular global tonic" (p. 67)

# Construction / Recognition
Two types of sequence:
1. **Diatonic sequence**: Pattern iterations replicate generic intervals but conform to the diatonic scale; uniformity is constrained
2. **Chromatic sequence**: Pattern iterations replicate exact intervals; uniformity has monolithic force and can rupture diatonic tonal focus

Later theorists formulate the same duality as:
- Diatony vs. repetition (Schenker 1906)
- Magnetism vs. inertia (Larson 1994)

# Context & Application
Uniformity and symmetry explain how chromatic sequences (such as hexatonic cycles and major-third divisions) can suspend normal tonal expectations. These are the "external forces" required to level the inherently asymmetric consonance/dissonance relationship and make reciprocity possible.

# Examples
**Fetis's phenomenological description** (pp. 65-66): "The succession and... movement fix the attention of the mind, which holds on to the form so strongly that any irregularity of tonality is not noticed."

**Application to hexatonic cycles** (p. 67): "The forces that Fetis identifies become more fully unleashed in chromatic sequences, such as the hexatonic cycles explored in chapter 2 or the Schubertian third-divisions represented by figure 3.2."

**Beyond sequences proper** (pp. 67-68): The dynamic extends to any situation where "a motivic fragment migrates across a series of transpositional levels, or a fugal point of imitation is replicated on a different degree of the scale."

**Fetis on the tritone** (p. 197): Uniformity neutralizes the "appellative powers" of the pseudo-fifth (tritone) through "symmetry of movement and succession," causing "the specific identities of intervals [to] dissolve into their generic categories, and the contours of the Mobius strip disindividuate into a strangely shaped, greased racing oval." Near-evenness operates as a "silent partner" enabling smooth sequential progressions.

**Additional examples**: Bach d minor Toccata (BWV 913) demonstrates incremental migration through six flats and back without cadential confirmation. Beethoven Ninth Symphony Scherzo (mm. 159-71) as extended sequence functioning as "slippery slope." Mitchell (1962): triadic circles of fifths "can be arrested at any point or go on in perpetuity." Salzer and Schachter: "we register the equal intervallic progressions without referring them to a supposed diatonic original." Kurth extended Fetis's insight to chromatic sequences specifically, recognizing Wagner's chromatic progressions derive coherence from repetition logic rather than tonal function ("extratonal" in Kurth's sense).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Equating uniformity with "unity" in a general aesthetic sense
  **Correction**: Uniformity specifically refers to systematic repetition of exact intervallic patterns

- **Error**: Treating the suspension of tonality as permanent
  **Correction**: Sequences typically resolve back to tonal orientation; the suspension is temporary

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: "Symmetry" means inversional or retrogressive symmetry
  **Clarification**: Fetis uses "symmetry" to mean regularity of pattern repetition, not necessarily inversional symmetry
- **Confusion**: Fetis's description is prescriptive
  **Clarification**: Fetis describes compositional resources, not automatic perceptual effects
