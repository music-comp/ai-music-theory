# Quick Definition
Hexatonic poles serve as a pervasive motivic element in Wagner's Parsifal, appearing at structural, dramatic, and local levels, often with triadic constituents embedded in dissonant formations that conceal but do not eliminate the characteristic voice-leading.

# Core Definition
In Wagner's *Parsifal*, hexatonic poles function as a musical signifier that "unifies the opera and marks its individuality" (p. 164). The opera's "first chromatic progression occurs at m. 28 of the Prelude, when Ab major to e minor deforms the diatonic theme associated with the ritual of Holy Communion. Its last chromatic progression occurs eighteen measures before its final measure" with a hexatonic distortion of the Grail music (p. 162). Hexatonic poles appear at structural key relationships, dramatic climaxes, and embedded within dissonant formations where they are revealed only through the reduction strategy. The four components of Parsifal's central problem -- Grail, Communion, blood, and agony -- are "bound by a single musical signifier, the H progression" (p. 165).

# Prerequisites
- **Hexatonic pole**: The transformation being traced throughout the opera
- **Reduction strategy**: Required to reveal hexatonic poles embedded in dissonant chords
- **Under-seventh / Over-seventh**: The dissonant additions that conceal the embedded triads

# Key Properties
1. Hexatonic poles have motivic value in both senses: unifying the opera and marking its individuality
2. This abstract motive is consistent with twentieth-century usage (Schenker, Reti, Keller) rather than nineteenth-century Leitmotif tradition
3. The motivic network expands considerably when embedded triadic subsets of dissonant formations are considered
4. A homology exists between hexatonic pole structure and the dramatic content (life/death, consonance/dissonance)

# Construction / Recognition
Hexatonic poles in Parsifal appear in three forms:
1. **Surface triadic progressions**: Direct triadic hexatonic poles (Ab major to e minor, etc.)
2. **Structural key relationships**: Act-level tonal designs featuring hexatonic pole pairs
3. **Embedded in dissonances**: V7 and half-diminished chords whose unique consonant triadic subsets form hexatonic poles

To recognize embedded hexatonic poles:
- Apply reduction strategy to each dissonant chord
- Identify the unique consonant triad within
- Check whether successive embedded triads form hexatonic pole relationships

# Context & Application
The analysis demonstrates the analytical profit available from viewing dissonant harmonies in terms of their consonant-triadic subsets. This broadens the motivic network and enriches understanding of how Parsifal's musical relations organize its web of extramusical symbols. The hexatonic pole creates a homology with the opera's central life/death thematic: "When a consonant triad progresses to its hexatonic pole, its root is displaced down to the raised seventh degree, while its fifth is displaced upward to the flatted sixth degree. The resulting interval ought to be a dissonant diminished seventh. But if we perceive this new chord as a triad, then we are perceiving the resulting interval as a consonant major sixth. What ought to be dissonant is unaccountably consonant" (p. 166).

# Examples
- **Opera-level**: First chromatic progression Ab major to e minor (m. 28); last chromatic progression with Grail distortion (p. 162)
- **Act 2**: Kundry sings Parsifal's name arpeggiating cb minor (m. 739), then its hexatonic pole Eb major (m. 751); cursing "Irre" set to d minor and Gb major (p. 162)
- **Act 3 structural keys**: bb minor, D major (Good Friday), bb minor, e minor, Ab major -- retrograding the Grail-theme distortion (p. 163)
- **Pain motive (act 1)**: V4/2 in d minor displaced by iio7 in c minor; embedded triads A major and f minor form hexatonic pole (p. 163)
- **Blood motive**: First mention of Holy blood and all three act 3 blood references feature A major/f minor hexatonic pole concealed in dissonant formations (p. 164)
- **Sundenqual**: Sequence of hexatonic poles concealed behind descending fifth bass motion (p. 164)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Denying hexatonic pole status when triads are embedded in dissonances
  **Correction**: A stacked-third approach would miss the hexatonic pole in the Herzeleide motive (c# minor with under-seventh); the reduction strategy reveals it

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the motivic network requires surface triadic progressions
  **Clarification**: Many of the most significant hexatonic poles in Parsifal are embedded in dissonant formations and only visible through reduction
