# Quick Definition
Hexatonic poles serve as a pervasive motivic element in Wagner's Parsifal, appearing at structural, dramatic, and local levels, often with triadic constituents embedded in dissonant formations that conceal but do not eliminate the characteristic voice-leading.

# Formal Definition
In Wagner's *Parsifal*, **hexatonic poles** function as a musical signifier that unifies the opera and marks its individuality. The hexatonic pole (H transformation) connects triads related by contrary semitonal motion in all three voices, representing the maximally distant relationship within a hexatonic system.

Hexatonic poles appear in Parsifal:
1. At structural key relationships (act-level tonal design)
2. At dramatic climaxes and transitions
3. Embedded within dissonant formations (V7 and ø7 chords)
4. As motivic connections across the opera's Leitmotif network

# Construction/Recognition
Hexatonic pole characteristics:
- Three voices move by semitone in contrary motion
- Root moves to raised seventh degree (descending)
- Fifth moves to flatted sixth degree (ascending)
- Creates maximum voice-leading distance within hexatonic region

When embedded in dissonance:
- V7 chord embeds major triad
- ø7 chord embeds minor triad
- The embedded triads may form hexatonic poles
- Characteristic semitonal motion preserved despite dissonant context

# Musical Context/Application
Parsifal's hexatonic pole network creates motivic unity across:
- **Communion theme**: First chromatic progression (Ab major → e minor, m. 28)
- **Grail distortion**: Final chromatic progression (18 measures before end)
- **Kundry's naming**: cb minor → Eb major (act 2, mm. 739, 751)
- **Curse and spear**: d minor → Gb major, bb minor → D major
- **Large-scale key relationships**: Each act's structural keys include hexatonic pole pairs

The hexatonic pole serves as a "musical signifier" binding the opera's four central elements:
- Grail (object)
- Communion (ritual)
- Blood (fluid)
- Agony (sensation)

# Examples
**Pain motive (Parsifal, act 1)**: First textual reference to Amfortas's pain features V4/2 in d minor displaced by iio7 in c minor. The embedded triads (A major and f minor) form a hexatonic pole, creating the characteristic semitonal displacements despite the dissonant surface.

**Blood motive**: First mention of Holy blood (act 1) and all three blood references in act 3 feature the same A major/f minor hexatonic pole concealed in dissonant formations.

**Sundenqual ("pain torment")**: The progression features a sequence of hexatonic poles concealed behind descending fifth motion in the bass, with dominant and ø7 chords paired in each sequence leg.

**Act 3 structural keys**: bb minor → D major (Good Friday) → bb minor → e minor → Ab major (finale). The four structural keys retrograde the Grail-theme distortion.

# Common Confusions
- Hexatonic poles in Parsifal often appear embedded in dissonances, not as pure triadic progressions
- Recognizing the embedded triads requires the reduction strategy
- The motivic network is abstract (transformation-based), not melodic/rhythmic like traditional Leitmotifs
- Hexatonic poles create a homology between musical structure and dramatic content (life/death, consonance/dissonance)
