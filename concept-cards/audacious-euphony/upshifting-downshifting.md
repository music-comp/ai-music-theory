# Quick Definition
Terms from Lewin (1998) describing directed voice leading: an ordered pair of chords upshifts if more voices move up than down in the transition from one to the other; the same pair downshifts when the order is reversed.

# Core Definition
"Upshift and downshift, from Lewin 1998, refer to directed voice leading in pitch or pitch-class space. An ordered pair of chords upshifts if, in the transition from one to the other, more voices move up than down. The same pair downshifts when the order is reversed" (p. 103n2). The terms may apply to register-specific pitch sets (e.g., {C4, E4, G4} to {C4, F4, A4}) or to pitch-class sets under idealized voice leading (e.g., C major to F major).

# Prerequisites
- **Idealized voice leading**: Understanding how voices move by shortest path
- **Single semitonal displacement**: The unit of voice-leading motion

# Key Properties
1. Upshifting = net upward voice-leading motion; downshifting = net downward
2. On Cube Dance: clockwise = upshifting; counterclockwise = downshifting
3. On Weitzmann's figure 5.1: rightward = upshifting; leftward = downshifting
4. On the Tonnetz: rightward = downshifting (counterintuitive, retained for historical reasons)
5. Balanced voice leading (e.g., within hexatonic regions) involves neither net upshift nor downshift

# Construction / Recognition
1. Determine the idealized voice leading between two triads
2. Count voices moving up vs. down
3. If more move up: upshift. If more move down: downshift. If equal: balanced
4. On Cube Dance, simply observe clockwise (up) or counterclockwise (down) direction

# Context & Application
Upshifting and downshifting are fundamental to all analytical work in chapters 5-6. They underlie chromatic sequences (consistent direction), departure-return scripts (complementary directions), continuous upshift scripts, and the functional interpretation of voice-leading zones (upshift ~ authentic/dominant; downshift ~ plagal/subdominant).

# Examples
- **Schubert *Die Zauberharfe* Overture**: Consistent downshifting through the development (figure 5.4)
- **Cognitive blending** (p. 107): "Upshifting in pitch space is spontaneously identified with rightward motion across the page . . . and with clockwise motion about a circle," due to European orthographic convention, clock conventions, and physiological associations

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming upshift means every voice moves up
  **Correction**: It means more voices move up than down (net direction)

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Tonnetz rightward motion should be upshifting
  **Clarification**: Historical Tonnetz conventions reverse this; rightward = downshifting because early theorists conceived intervals as rising acoustic ratios projected rightward
- **Confusion**: Scalar downshift (descending key signatures) equals triadic downshift
  **Clarification**: These are independent dimensions; Schumann's Dichterliebe decrements key signatures (scalar downshift) while upshifting in triadic space
