# Quick Definition
The discredited nineteenth-century theory that major and minor triads are generated by equal but opposite forces (overtones vs. undertones), which Cohn argues is unnecessary for understanding triadic transformations because the structural equality of major and minor can be grounded in voice-leading behavior (melodic dualism) instead.

# Core Definition
Harmonic dualism, developed by Oettingen and Riemann, posits that major and minor triads are "metaphysically or physically equivalent structures," one generated upward from a fundamental (major) and one generated downward (minor). Critics note "the metaphysics is obsolete and the physics apocryphal" (Cohn, p. 50). Kopp (2002) frames Cohn's work as "A Dualist Transformation System"; Tymoczko (2009a) claims "a commitment to harmonic dualism is at the heart of neo-Riemannian theory" (p. 56). Cohn's response: "All three objections are neutralized by an appeal to voice leading, a dimension of experience that is, in principle, independent of root motion" (p. 50). The structural equality of major and minor emerges from their shared near evenness, not from harmonic generation.

# Prerequisites
- **near-evenness** — The property that actually grounds the equivalence of major and minor
- **voice-leading-equivalence** — The voice-leading basis that replaces harmonic dualism

# Key Properties
1. Harmonic dualism: major = overtone-generated, minor = undertone-generated
2. The metaphysics is "obsolete" and the physics "apocryphal" (p. 50)
3. Three objections to dualist transformation theory: (a) LP moves roots opposite directions for major vs. minor, (b) the equivalence seems merely logical, (c) it implies commitment to discredited dualism
4. All three objections are "neutralized by an appeal to voice leading" (p. 50)
5. "Concepts may be detached from the framework in which they were initially conceived" (p. 56)

# Construction / Recognition
Cohn's two-step rebuttal:
1. "Concepts may be detached from the framework in which they were initially conceived" — just as Schenkerian analysis is used without subscribing to Schenker's other beliefs (p. 56)
2. "Relations that may appear to be fundamentally dualist may arise as epiphenomena of other relations" — the inversional relation between major and minor is a consequence of near evenness, not its cause (p. 57)

# Context & Application
Understanding the critique clarifies what IS and IS NOT being claimed by neo-Riemannian/transformational theory. The theoretical apparatus can be used without subscribing to harmonic dualism. This allows scholars to employ triadic transformations without philosophical embarrassment.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 56): Klumpenhouwer's definition of harmonic dualism: "music-theoretical work that accept[s] the absolute structural equality of major and minor triads as objects derived from a single, unitary process that structurally contains the potential for twofold, or binary, articulation."

**Example 2** (p. 57): Tymoczko (2011a): "Nineteenth-century composers were not explicitly concerned with inversional relationships as such; instead, these relationships appear as necessary by-products of a deeper and more fundamental concern with efficient voice leading."
