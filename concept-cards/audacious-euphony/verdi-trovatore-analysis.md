# Quick Definition
Manrico's cantabile from Verdi's *Il Trovatore* illustrates a pitch retention loop, cycling through six triads that share a common tone using **P**, **R**, and **L** transformations in a canonical ordering.

# Formal Definition
The cantabile section of "Ah si, ben mio" (*Il Trovatore*, act 3) executes a pitch retention loop. The three quatrains modulate through: f minor to Ab major (first quatrain), ab minor to Fb major to db minor (second quatrain), and Db major (third quatrain). The transformational vehicles for such a tour are the three common-tone maximizers **P**, **R**, and **L**, placed in any order and repeated. A final **L** would return to the initial triad; instead, this transformation indicates the progress of the cantabile across its entire span.

# Construction/Recognition
The loop proceeds through six triads sharing a common tone:
- f minor --(**L**)--> Ab major --(**P**)--> ab minor --(**R**)--> Fb major --(**L**)--> db minor --(**R**)--> Db major
- A final **L** would close the loop back to f minor

# Musical Context
This passage demonstrates how pitch retention loops provide a compositional framework for organizing large-scale tonal motion while maintaining a central pitch class as an anchor of continuity.

# Common Confusions
- The loop uses all three common-tone maximizers (**P**, **R**, **L**), not just one or two
- The loop is not completed; the final **L** that would close it is indicated but not realized, marking the span's overall progress
- The order of transformations can vary; what matters is that all three are used cyclically
