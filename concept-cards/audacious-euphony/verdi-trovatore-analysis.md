# Quick Definition
Manrico's cantabile from Verdi's *Il Trovatore* (act 3) illustrates a nearly complete pitch retention loop, cycling through six triads sharing a common tone via P, R, and L transformations.

# Core Definition
Figure 6.3 models the cantabile section. The three quatrains modulate through: f minor to Ab major (first), ab minor through Fb major to db minor (second), Db major (third). "A final L would return the initial triad; instead, this transformation indicates the progress of the cantabile across its entire span" (p. 133). The loop uses all three common-tone maximizers in a canonical ordering.

# Prerequisites
- **Pitch retention loop**: The framework being demonstrated
- **Neighborhood**: The six-triad structure being toured

# Key Properties
1. Nearly complete loop (five of six transformations realized)
2. Final L implied but not realized (marks the span's progress)
3. All three common-tone maximizers (P, R, L) used cyclically

# Construction / Recognition
f minor --(L)--> Ab major --(P)--> ab minor --(R)--> Fb major --(L)--> db minor --(R)--> Db major
(A final L would return to f minor)

# Context & Application
This is the first and most straightforward example of a pitch retention loop in chapter 6, demonstrating the concept before the more complex Brahms analysis.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: The loop must be completed
  **Correction**: The final L is implied but not realized; the open loop marks the progress of the cantabile

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Only one specific ordering of P, R, L works
  **Clarification**: Any permutation repeated cyclically produces a valid loop
