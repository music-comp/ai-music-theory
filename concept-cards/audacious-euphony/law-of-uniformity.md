# Quick Definition
Fetis's principle that sequential patterns suspend tonal syntax, with the mind's attention absorbed by the logic of repetition rather than harmonic function.

# Formal Definition
The "law of uniformity" (Fetis 1844) describes how sequences create a distinct cognitive mode separate from tonal expectation. When a sequence is recognized, the "law of tonality" is placed in abeyance as cognition submits to the law of uniformity. As Fetis wrote, "The mind, absorbed in the contemplation of the progressive series, momentarily loses the feeling of tonality, and regains it only at the final cadence, where the normal order is reestablished." This principle applies to both diatonic and chromatic sequences.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
The law of uniformity operates when:
- A pattern of harmonic motion is established (minimum 2 iterations)
- The pattern creates expectation of continuation
- Tonal function becomes secondary to pattern completion
- The sequence can be "arrested at any point or go on in perpetuity" (Mitchell 1962)

Chromatic sequences governed by uniformity:
- May traverse large portions of chromatic space
- Create "suspension of tonal gravity" (Salzer & Schachter)
- Are "extratonal" in Kurth's sense
- Derive coherence from repetition, not function

# Musical Context/Application
The law of uniformity helps explain:
- How sequences function in classical and romantic music
- Why chromatic sequences are perceived as coherent despite tonal irregularity
- The cognitive shift between functional hearing and pattern-based hearing
- How composers exploit sequences to move through chromatic space

Kurth extended Fetis's insight to chromatic sequences specifically, recognizing that Wagner's chromatic progressions often derive coherence from repetition logic rather than tonal function.

# Examples
- Diatonic sequences: circle-of-fifths progressions that temporarily suspend key feeling
- Chromatic sequences in Wagner: governed by repetition, not tonal attraction
- Triadic circles of fifths: "can be arrested at any point or go on in perpetuity" (Mitchell)
- Salzer and Schachter: "we register the equal intervallic progressions without referring them to a supposed diatonic original"

# Common Confusions
- The law of uniformity does not mean sequences are outside all organization - they follow the logic of repetition
- Tonal suspension is temporary - cadences "reestablish normal order"
- The principle applies to diatonic sequences as well as chromatic ones
- Uniformity and tonality can operate at different structural levels simultaneously
