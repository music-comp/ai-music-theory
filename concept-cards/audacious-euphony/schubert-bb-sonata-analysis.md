# Quick Definition
The first movement of Schubert's Bb major Piano Sonata, D. 960, conforms to classical sonata structure but with tonally anomalous events that can be interpreted through three overlapping analytical frameworks: hexatonic constituency, Weitzmann-region constituency, and voice-leading zones.

# Formal Definition
The movement's tonal skeleton (exposition, development, recapitulation) supports anomalous tonal events: the Gb major passage, the f# minor second theme, the A major tonicization, and the development's departures from c# minor, A major, Db major, and d minor. Three analytical underlays are proposed:
1. **Hexatonic analysis**: Assigns function by hexatonic constituency; structural dominant arrives at m. 58 (A major)
2. **Weitzmann-region analysis**: Assigns function by Weitzmann constituency; dominant arrives already with f# minor second theme
3. **Voice-leading zone analysis**: Creates a hybrid; exposition is a two-phase descent (zones 5 -> 4 -> 2); development reaches nadir at zone 1; recapitulation returns through zones 4 and 5

# Construction/Recognition
Figure 6.9 presents three analytic underlays:
- Hexatonic: T (Bb hexatonic), S (Eb hexatonic), D (F hexatonic)
- Weitzmann: T (Bb Weitzmann), S, D regions defined by augmented-triad constituency
- Zones: Descent 5 -> 4 -> 2 -> 1, reversal and ascent 2 -> 4 -> 5, overshoot to 7

The exposition's two-phase descent:
- f# minor second theme displaces Bb major downward by one zone (5 -> 4)
- F major third theme displaces f# minor downward by two zones (4 -> 2)

# Musical Context
The analysis demonstrates that departure-return schemes can organize sonata-form movements where functional ascriptions are uncertain. The b minor theme in the recapitulation represents subdominant "overshoot" (zone 7), characteristic of many Schubert first movements.

# Examples
- The movement's principal thematic materials emphasize major thirds outlining augmented triads
- The retransitional d minor (m. 171): tonic in hexatonic analysis, dominant in Weitzmann analysis
- Recapitulatory b minor: tonic (hexatonic, by Bb common third), tonic (Weitzmann), subdominant overshoot (zones)

# Common Confusions
- The three analyses are not competing; they illuminate different aspects of the same structure
- The voice-leading zone analysis is the most neutral, not mandating functional interpretations
- Only one of the movement's eight non-tonic keys (d minor) is diatonic to Bb major
