# Quick Definition
A nearly even tetrachord is a four-note chord that minimally deviates from the perfectly even diminished seventh chord by single semitonal displacement, encompassing dominant seventh and half-diminished seventh chords (the Tristan genus).

# Formal Definition
**Nearly even tetrachords** are four-note chords formed by displacing one tone of a perfectly even diminished seventh chord by a single semitone. The two species are:
- **Dominant seventh** (downward displacement): one tone lowered by semitone
- **Half-diminished seventh** (upward displacement): one tone raised by semitone

Together, these two species constitute the **Tristan genus**, a tetrachordal system analogous to the system of consonant triads (which are nearly even trichords).

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Perfectly even tetrachord: diminished seventh [0369]
- Divides octave into four equal parts (minor thirds)
- Three distinct diminished seventh chords exist

Nearly even tetrachords:
- Dominant seventh [0258]: one tone lowered → interval content [4,3,3,2]
- Half-diminished seventh [0258]: one tone raised → interval content [3,3,4,2]
- Same set class, related by inversion

Relationship:
- Each diminished seventh generates 8 nearly even tetrachords (4 V7 + 4 ø7)
- 24 total Tristan-genus chords (12 V7 + 12 ø7)
- This mirrors: each augmented triad generates 6 nearly even trichords (3 major + 3 minor)

# Musical Context/Application
Nearly even tetrachords share the voice-leading efficiency of nearly even trichords because:
- They cluster around perfectly even cores
- Any two in the same region are exactly 2 voice-leading units apart
- They form systematic networks (Boretz regions, 4-Cube Trio)

The concept extends pan-triadic theory to seventh chords, showing that:
- Voice-leading parsimony principles apply beyond triads
- The special properties of triads (n=3) derive from their position in a general system
- Tetrachords (n=4) form analogous but distinct networks

# Examples
**Tristan-genus progressions**: Any passage alternating V7 and ø7 chords involves nearly even tetrachords. The Tristan Prelude opening, Brunnhilde's Immolation, and Chopin's chromatic preludes all explore this terrain.

**Comparison to triads**: Just as C major and C minor are both single semitonal displacements of C-E-G# augmented, G7 and Gø7 are both single semitonal displacements of their respective diminished seventh cores.

# Common Confusions
- "Nearly even" is defined relative to perfectly even (diminished seventh), not diatonic scale
- Minor seventh and major seventh are NOT nearly even tetrachords (they are combinations of triads)
- The dominant and half-diminished are the ONLY nearly even tetrachord species
- The special voice-leading properties derive from proximity to the perfectly even division
