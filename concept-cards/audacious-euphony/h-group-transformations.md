# Quick Definition
The three transformations that map a consonant triad within its hexatonic region but outside its Weitzmann region: Leittonwechsel (L), Parallel (P), and Hexatonic Pole (H).

# Core Definition
"A hexatonic-group (or H-group) transformation [is] any transformation that maps a consonant triad within its hexatonic region and outside of its Weitzmann region. The three H-group transformations are the Leittonwechsel (L), the parallel (P), and the hexatonic pole (H)" (Glossary, p. 229). All three change mode (major to minor or vice versa). L and P each require 1 unit of voice-leading work; H requires 3 units. H = LP = PL (decomposable into the other two). Each H-group transformation shifts voice-leading zone by 1.

# Prerequisites
- **Hexatonic Region**: The region preserved by H-group transformations
- **Weitzmann Region**: The region shifted by H-group transformations

# Key Properties
1. Preserve hexatonic region membership
2. Shift Weitzmann region membership
3. All three change mode (major to minor or vice versa)
4. L and P are classically normative; H is maverick
5. All three are involutions (applying twice returns to start)
6. Each shifts voice-leading zone by +/-1

# Construction / Recognition
For any consonant triad X in hexatonic region R:
- L(X), P(X), and H(X) all remain in R
- L(X) and P(X) share 2 common tones with X (1 unit work)
- H(X) shares 0 common tones with X (3 units work)
- The H-group generates all six triads of any hexatonic region from any starting triad

Combination with W-group (Table 5.1):
| H\W | R | N | S |
|-----|-----|-----|-----|
| L | T5 | T1 | T3 |
| P | T3 | T5 | T1 |
| H | T1 | T3 | T5 |

# Context & Application
"An odd-valued transpositional sequence is generated by alternating two transformations, one from each group. As there are three hexatonic-group transformations (L, P, and H) and three Weitzmann-group transformations (R, N, and S), there are 3 x 3 = 9 combinations that generate an odd-valued chromatic sequence through parsimonious voice leading" (p. 87). L and P combined with R and N produce the chronologically earliest sequences; combinations involving H or S are maverick.

# Examples
- **LP chain**: C - e - E - g# - Ab - c - C (traverses entire hexatonic region)
- **L/R alternation**: generates T5 sequences (circle of fifths)
- **P/R alternation**: generates T3 sequences (minor third chains)
- **H/N alternation**: generates T3 sequences (maverick variant)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: The "H" in H-group refers to the H transformation
  **Correction**: It stands for "hexatonic" -- the group preserves hexatonic region membership

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: H-group and W-group are opposites
  **Clarification**: They are complements; together they generate all transformations between consonant triads

- **Confusion**: H-group transformations preserve Weitzmann region
  **Clarification**: They preserve hexatonic region and shift Weitzmann region
