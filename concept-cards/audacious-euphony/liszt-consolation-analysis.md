# Quick Definition
Cohn's extended analysis of Liszt's Consolation no. 3 in Db major, demonstrating how the convertible Tonnetz models the interaction between diatonic function and hexatonic voice-leading, with local tonicizations creating "sealed capsules that bob on the pan-triadic sea."

# Core Definition
The Consolation consists of two sentences. The first classically continues through ii6 to a perfect cadence in Db major (expanded cadential progression). The second continues to a cadence in A minor, then returns to Db major, completing a major-third division. The analysis demonstrates: (1) diatonic progressions through T-S-D-T in the first sentence; (2) hexatonic alley sprouting from the second sentence; (3) progressive ascent through hexatonic stations, each locally tonicized while participating in pan-triadic voice-leading (pp. 189-190, 197-207).

# Prerequisites
- **Convertible Tonnetz**: The model being demonstrated
- **Diatonic encapsulation**: Each hexatonic station is locally encapsulated
- **Hexatonic cycle**: The organizing principle of the second sentence's chromatic journey

# Key Properties
1. First sentence demonstrates pure diatonic T-S-D-T on the Tonnetz
2. Second sentence demonstrates hexatonic journey with local diatonic capsules
3. Each station carries dual role: voice-leading object and acoustic object
4. The analysis reveals what earlier treatments (Figure 2.11b) could not show

# Construction / Recognition
**First sentence** (Figures 8.7a-e, pp. 197-199):
- Opening (mm. 1-3): Db major arpeggiated; diatonic collection inferred
- Basic idea (mm. 3-6): T-S-D-T with subdominant interpretation of {G, Bb, Db, F}
- Tonicization of f minor: Syntonic images shift from Eb to Bb
- Continuation: f minor transforms to diminished seventh pressing toward eb minor (ii6)
- Cadence: Expanded S-D-T progression

**Second sentence** (Figure 8.9, six stroboscopic snapshots, pp. 204-206):
Each hexatonic station locally tonicized through classical cadential means.

# Context & Application
The passage's significance: at mm. 31-35, "every event has a determinate role within the diatonic pitch space of a minor. Within that context, there exists no functional or enharmonic ambiguity... We are fully comfortable with mm. 31-35 as a diatonically determinate microcosm... What we do not understand is the diatonic function of that tonic itself, in relation to the larger Db major macrocosm: whether it is rooted on its 5-hat or 6-hat; whether it functions as subdominant by virtue of carrying the subdominant agent or as dominant by virtue of carrying the dominant agent" (p. 205).

A minor is "a diatonically organized, sealed capsule that bobs on the pan-triadic sea. Its interior is an ecological microsystem within which the fraught tensions and attractions, gravities and magnetisms of tonality are active. On its exterior, it is tethered to the broader universe by different forces and relations" (p. 205).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating the hexatonic stations as merely passing
  **Correction**: Each is genuinely tonicized with full diatonic resources

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: A minor's indeterminate function relative to Db major is a flaw in the analysis
  **Clarification**: The indeterminacy is a feature of double syntax, not a failing of analysis
