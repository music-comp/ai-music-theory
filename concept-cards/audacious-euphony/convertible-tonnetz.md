# Quick Definition
Cohn's innovation that allows the Tonnetz to switch between diatonic and chromatic representations, showing how both syntaxes coexist in 19th-century music as a "hybrid automobile" with one engine for chromatic highways and one for diatonic neighborhoods.

# Formal Definition
The convertible Tonnetz is a geometric representation that can change shape in response to how a listener hears relations among triadic objects. When triads collaborate to define a tonic, diatonic walls are erected (encapsulation), activating classical syntax with its semantic forces (gravity, attraction, leading). When triads move without establishing a tonic, the walls dissolve, and motion is governed by voice-leading parsimony on the open chromatic plane. The conversion between states is fluid; "at any moment, one may brake on a subdominant, reverse into a dominant seventh along the curb, pull forward to a tonic, and kill the engine."

# Construction/Recognition
- Diatonic mode: Parallelogram encapsulation, Rings arrows active, syntonic images define tonal boundaries
- Chromatic mode: Open plane or cylinder, voice-leading distances govern, hexatonic and Weitzmann alleys active
- Switching mechanism: Cadential confirmation erects diatonic walls; sequential or chain progressions dissolve them
- Each triad and each triadic relation occupies a unique position on the convertible Tonnetz
- The model responds dynamically to the listener's attunement to diatonic vs. chromatic syntax

Conceptual images:
- Hybrid automobile: Highway engine (chromatic) vs. neighborhood engine (diatonic)
- Landing aircraft: Sprouting landing gear as diatonic space comes into view
- Sealed capsules bobbing on pan-triadic sea: Interior activates tonal tensions; exterior tethered by voice-leading forces

# Musical Context/Application
The convertible Tonnetz models passages where:
- Music alternates between sequential/chromatic and cadential/diatonic organization
- Tonicizations provide local diatonic capsules within larger chromatic journeys
- A single triad serves dual roles: voice-leading object in hexatonic space AND acoustic object in local diatonic space
- Nineteenth-century practice intertwines both syntaxes within single phrases or movements

The model explains why "familiar tonal terms have made unmarked cameo appearances" throughout analyses of pan-triadic progressions.

# Examples

# Common Confusions
- The convertible Tonnetz is not a third kind of Tonnetz but a unified model that can present diatonic or chromatic aspects depending on musical context
- "Converting" is not a discrete switch but can be gradual: walls may "ease open" during sequences or "flip closed" approaching cadences
- The model applies to both triads and Tristan-genus seventh chords, though the latter cannot be directly traced on the two-dimensional Tonnetz
