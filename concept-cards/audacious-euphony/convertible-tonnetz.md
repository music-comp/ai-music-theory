# Quick Definition
Cohn's model unifying diatonic and chromatic representations of the Tonnetz, allowing the space to switch fluidly between encapsulated diatonic mode (with Rings arrows active) and open chromatic mode (governed by voice-leading parsimony), like "a hybrid automobile: one engine for the highway of chords, and one for touring the encapsulated neighborhoods."

# Core Definition
The convertible Tonnetz becomes a "black-box theater, whose walls can be erected, dismantled, and moved without fuss" (p. 203). One is free to establish an encapsulated diatonic space, activating Rings arrows; navigate hexatonic or octatonic alleys; slide through connected diatonic spaces; or perambulate freely across shared edges. "Adding a supplementary dissonance to any triad, one may transfer into an orthogonal space consisting of Tristan-genus objects, and wander that space as if oblivious to the semantic values of classical tonality" (p. 203).

As listeners navigate the unbounded Tonnetz, they remain "alert to the possible reassertion of classical syntax on the triadic and Tristan-genus objects that are traversed" (p. 203). Confirmation of a diatonic hypothesis reorients attention "from the far-flung space toward a well-defined sector of it, and ultimately to a particular location in that space, the cadenced triad" (p. 203).

# Prerequisites
- **Diatonic Tonnetz**: One state of the convertible model (encapsulated, arrows active)
- **Chromatic Tonnetz**: The other state (open, voice-leading governs)
- **Diatonic encapsulation**: The mechanism that activates tonal forces
- **Rings arrows**: The semantic content that activates/deactivates
- **Hexatonic and Weitzmann regions**: The structures organizing the chromatic state

# Key Properties
1. Unified model -- not a third kind of Tonnetz but one that can present either aspect
2. Conversion is fluid, not a discrete switch: walls "ease open" or "flip closed"
3. Each triad and each direct triadic relation occupies a unique position
4. The model responds dynamically to listener attunement
5. Applicable to both triads and Tristan-genus chords (the latter through "metaphorical conduit")

# Construction / Recognition
**Diatonic mode**: Parallelogram encapsulation, Rings arrows active, syntonic images define tonal boundaries, T-S-D-T governs.

**Chromatic mode**: Open plane or cylinder, voice-leading distances govern, hexatonic and Weitzmann alleys active.

**Switching mechanism**: Cadential confirmation erects diatonic walls; sequential or chain progressions dissolve them.

Conceptual metaphors (pp. 203-204):
- Hybrid automobile: highway engine (chromatic) vs. neighborhood engine (diatonic)
- Landing aircraft: sprouting landing gear as diatonic space comes into view
- Sealed capsules bobbing on pan-triadic sea: interior activates tonal tensions; exterior tethered by voice-leading forces
- "At any moment, one may brake on a subdominant, reverse into a dominant seventh along the curb, pull forward to a tonic, and kill the engine" (p. 203)

# Context & Application
Figure 8.9 (pp. 204-206) provides the most developed analytical application: six stroboscopic snapshots of Liszt's Consolation no. 3, second sentence. Each hexatonic station carries a dual role: as voice-leading object in Db hexatonic space and as acoustic object in local diatonic space. "Every event in mm. 31-35 has a determinate role within the diatonic pitch space of a minor... What we do not understand is the diatonic function of that tonic itself, in relation to the larger Db major macrocosm" (p. 205).

The convertible model is "even relevant in those passages where pan-triadic syntax does its best to cleanse our ears of the tonal semiotic" (p. 207), because every triad remains capable of diatonic encapsulation.

# Examples
- **Liszt Consolation no. 3, second sentence** (Figure 8.9, pp. 204-206): Progressive hexatonic ascent with local tonicizations at each station
- **Wagner Parsifal Faith Proclamation** (Figure 8.11, pp. 207-210): Plagal drift halted by diatonic encapsulation of eb minor
- **Brahms Symphony no. 1 finale** (Figures 8.12-8.13, pp. 209-211): Thematic statements erect walls; liquidations dissolve into chains

# Common Errors
- **Error**: The convertible Tonnetz is a third kind of Tonnetz alongside diatonic and chromatic
  **Correction**: It is a unified model that can present diatonic or chromatic aspects depending on context

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: "Converting" is a discrete switch
  **Clarification**: Walls may gradually "ease open" during sequences or gradually "flip closed" approaching cadences
