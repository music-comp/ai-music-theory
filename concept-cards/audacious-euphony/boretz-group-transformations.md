# Quick Definition
Boretz-group transformations are the voice-leading operations that connect Tristan-genus chords within a single Boretz region, analogous to Weitzmann-group transformations for triads within a Weitzmann region.

# Formal Definition
**Boretz-group transformations** (adapted from Childs 1998 and Douthett & Steinbach 1998) connect dominant seventh and half-diminished seventh chords within a single Boretz region. All such transformations involve exactly two units of voice-leading work.

The four Boretz-group transformations are:
1. **S3(2)**: Stationary interval-class 3 dyad, moving interval-class 2 dyad
2. **S3(4)**: Stationary interval-class 3 dyad, moving interval-class 4 dyad
3. **S6**: Stationary tritone (interval-class 6), moving perfect fifth dyad
4. **R***: Single voice moves by whole step (analogous to triadic R)

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Labeling convention (Childs 1998):
- "S" = Similar motion (both moving voices in same direction)
- Superscripts indicate interval classes of stationary and moving dyads
- "R*" indicates whole-step motion in one voice (Relative-like)

Cross-species transformations (V7 ↔ ø7):
- S3(2), S3(4), S6: two voices move by semitone (similar motion)
- R*: one voice moves by whole step

Same-species transformations (V7 ↔ V7 or ø7 ↔ ø7):
- Involve contrary semitonal motion
- Spoiler resolves while new spoiler emerges on same side of diminished seventh core

Voice-leading zone behavior:
- Cross-species: zones alternate (e.g., 3 → 1 → 3)
- Same-species: zones alternate with contrary voice leading

# Musical Context/Application
Boretz-group transformations characterize:
- The Tristan Prelude opening (alternating upshift and downshift)
- Any progression oscillating within a single Boretz region
- Balanced voice-leading passages among seventh chords

Combined with octatonic transformations, Boretz-group transformations enable:
- Circumnavigation of 4-Cube Trio
- Systematic analysis of seventh-chord progressions
- Understanding transformation-class substitutions

# Examples
**Tristan Prelude, chords 1-2**: The progression from Fø7 to E7 uses S3(2). The stationary dyad (B-G#, ic3) holds while the moving dyad (F-D# becomes E-D, ic2).

**Tristan Prelude, chords 2-3**: The progression from E7 to Aø7 uses R*. The E moves by whole step to F#.

**Brunnhilde's Immolation**: Similar alternation of Boretz-group transformations within the E-G-A#-C# region.

# Common Confusions
- "S" in Boretz transformations means "Similar motion," not "Slide" as in triadic S
- All Boretz-group transformations involve exactly 2 voice-leading units
- The transformations operate within a region; between-region motion requires octatonic transformations
- R* is the only single-voice motion (whole step); others involve two semitones
