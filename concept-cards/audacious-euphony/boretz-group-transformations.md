# Quick Definition
Boretz-group transformations are the four voice-leading operations connecting Tristan-genus chords within a single Boretz region, each involving exactly two units of voice-leading work, adapted from Childs 1998 and Douthett & Steinbach 1998.

# Core Definition
**Boretz-group transformations** connect dominant seventh and half-diminished seventh chords within a single Boretz region. "S" stands for similar motion (distinct from triadic S for Slide). The transformational labels in Figure 7.11 "are adapted from Childs 1998 and Douthett and Steinbach 1998" (p. 171). The four transformations are:
1. **S3(2)**: Stationary interval-class 3 dyad, moving interval-class 2 dyad
2. **S3(4)**: Stationary interval-class 3 dyad, moving interval-class 4 dyad
3. **S6**: Stationary tritone (ic6), moving perfect-fifth dyad (ic5)
4. **R***: Single voice moves by whole step (analogous to triadic R)

"The two superscripts classify the interval of the stationary and moving dyad, respectively" (p. 171).

# Prerequisites
- **Boretz region**: The regional context within which these transformations operate
- **Tristan genus**: The chords being transformed

# Key Properties
1. All four transformations involve exactly 2 voice-leading units
2. Cross-species (V7 to ø7): S3(2), S3(4), S6 move two voices by semitone; R* moves one voice by whole step
3. Same-species (V7 to V7 or ø7 to ø7): contrary motion -- spoiler resolves while new spoiler emerges on same side
4. Combined with octatonic transformations, they enable circumnavigation of 4-Cube Trio
5. Table 7.3 shows how each transpositional value (T±1, T±2, T±4, T±5) results from combining Boretz and octatonic transformations

# Construction / Recognition
Labeling convention (Childs 1998):
- "S" = similar motion (both moving voices in same direction)
- First superscript = interval class of stationary dyad
- Second superscript (in parentheses) = interval class of moving dyad
- "R*" = single voice moves by whole step (no superscripts needed)

Example from Tristan Prelude (Figure 7.11):
- Chords 1-2 (Fø7 to E7): S3(2) -- stationary B-G# (ic3), moving F-D# becomes E-D (ic2)
- Chords 2-3 (E7 to Aø7): R* -- single voice E moves by whole step to F#

# Context & Application
Boretz-group transformations characterize motion within a single Boretz region. When combined in alternation with octatonic-group transformations, they generate the sixteen distinct transformation pairs that produce the four circumnavigatory transpositional values (Table 7.3, p. 176).

# Examples
- **Tristan Prelude, chords 1-5**: Alternating Boretz-group transformations within the B-D-F-G# region (Figure 7.11, p. 171)
- **Brunnhilde's Immolation**: Similar alternation within E-G-A#-C# region (pp. 173-174)
- **S6 transformation**: Encountered in Figure 7.14 (Brunnhilde's Immolation); "holds the tritone dyad and moves the interval class 5 dyad" (p. 171)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing "S" in Boretz transformations with "S" (Slide) in triadic transformations
  **Correction**: In Boretz context, "S" means "similar motion"; in triadic context, "S" means "Slide"

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking R* involves semitonal motion like the other transformations
  **Clarification**: R* is the only single-voice transformation (whole step); the others involve two semitones distributed across two voices
