# Quick Definition
The octatonic pole is the "maverick" transformation between opposite-species Tristan-genus chords that moves all four voices by semitone (three in one direction, one opposite), using all eight tones of an octatonic scale -- the tetrachordal analogue to the hexatonic pole.

# Core Definition
Adrian Childs (1998) "dubs the particular transformation from chord (5) to chord (6) an octatonic pole because it plays the same maverick role as the hexatonic pole progression does in triadic space" (p. 172). Sigfrid Karg-Elert's 1930 treatise identified their affinity, "calling them both *Kollektivwechsel*, or 'collective exchange'" (p. 172). Figure 7.12 documents the analogy: "both progressions combine inversionally related species; involve upshifting in all-but-one voice, offset by downshifting in the remaining voice; and use all of the tones of their eponymous scales" (p. 172).

# Prerequisites
- **Octatonic-group transformations**: The octatonic pole is one of four octatonic-bridge transformations
- **Tristan genus**: The chords being transformed

# Key Properties
1. All four voices move by semitone: three in one direction, one in the opposite
2. Uses all eight tones of the relevant octatonic scale
3. Connects inversionally related species (V7 to ø7 or vice versa)
4. Maximum voice-leading distance within the octatonic region
5. Parallel to hexatonic pole: both are "collective exchanges" (Karg-Elert)

# Construction / Recognition
Voice-leading structure:
- Select a V7 or ø7 chord
- Move 3 voices by semitone in one direction (e.g., upward)
- Move 1 voice by semitone in the opposite direction (downward)
- The result is the opposite-species chord that exhausts the octatonic collection

Comparison with hexatonic pole (Figure 7.12):
- Hexatonic pole: n-1 = 2 voices one direction, 1 opposite (for n=3)
- Octatonic pole: n-1 = 3 voices one direction, 1 opposite (for n=4)

# Context & Application
The octatonic pole marks significant harmonic events in the tetrachordal system. Lendvai (1983, 1988) identifies prominent octatonic poles in the *Ring* and *Boris Godunov*. Cohn (1996) observes hexatonic and octatonic poles directly juxtaposed in Franck's Piano Quintet. The octatonic pole produces the furthest-distance connection within an octatonic region.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating the octatonic pole as the only octatonic bridge transformation
  **Correction**: It is one of four (along with S2, S4, S5); the others have two stationary voices

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming the octatonic pole has maximal voice-leading work
  **Clarification**: All four voices move by semitone (total of 4 units), but three move together making it directionally biased, not maximal in the work sense (S2, S4, S5 also involve 2 units total from the moving dyad)
