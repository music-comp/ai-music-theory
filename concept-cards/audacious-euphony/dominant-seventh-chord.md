# Quick Definition
In Cohn's framework, the dominant seventh chord is a nearly even tetrachord formed by displacing one tone of a diminished seventh chord downward by semitone, making it a member of the Tristan genus alongside the half-diminished seventh.

# Core Definition
The **dominant seventh chord** (V7) is one of two species of nearly even tetrachords in the Tristan genus. It is formed by taking a fully diminished seventh chord and displacing one of its four tones downward by semitone. There are twelve dominant seventh chords in the chromatic universe. Standard structure: major triad with minor seventh above root (e.g., G-B-D-F), interval content [4,3,3,2]. Cohn explicitly neutralizes the functional agency that classical theory bestows: "In using traditional names for seventh chords, I do not wish to take on board the functional agency that classical theory bestows on them... but only to neutralize it at this stage of theoretical development. It is not a necessary feature of Tristan-genus chords, to the same degree, in each and every one of its manifestations" (p. 168).

# Prerequisites
- **Diminished seventh chord**: The perfectly even core from which V7 is derived by downward displacement
- **Nearly even tetrachord**: V7 is the downward-displacement species of nearly even tetrachord

# Key Properties
1. Downward displacement from diminished seventh (one tone lowered by semitone)
2. Each diminished seventh generates four V7 chords
3. Two voice-leading units to any chord in the same Boretz region
4. Contrary semitonal motion to same-species chords (other V7s in region)
5. Occupies odd-numbered voice-leading zones (1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11)
6. Under reduction strategy, uniquely embeds a major triad (over-seventh analysis)

# Construction / Recognition
- From diminished seventh B-D-F-G#: lower B to Bb = Bb7, lower D to Db = Db7 (=C#7), lower F to E = E7, lower G# to G = G7
- Interval structure: [4, 3, 3, 2] (M3 + m3 + m3 + M2)
- Boretz region: determined by which diminished seventh it displaces

# Context & Application
In Tristan-genus analysis, dominant seventh chords function as voice-leading entities that may suspend their appellative (resolution-seeking) properties. They form half of the 4-Cube Trio structure. The T3 sequence of dominant sevenths produces the "omnibus" or "devil's mill" progression, featuring contrary voice motion (Fig. 7.8(a), p. 167).

# Examples
- **Tristan Prelude, chord 2**: E7 (E-G#-B-D) results from resolving D# to D in chord 1 while F is displaced to E (p. 168)
- **Sequential template Fig. 7.8(a)**: V7 chords in T3 sequence feature contrary motion -- two stationary voices, two moving by semitone in opposite directions (p. 167)
- **Chopin e minor Prelude**: V7 chords appear throughout the chromatic descent (pp. 178-182)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming V7 must resolve as a dominant in Tristan-genus analysis
  **Correction**: In this framework, V7 is defined by voice-leading structure, not by tonal function

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking V7 and ø7 are derived from each other
  **Clarification**: Both are independently derived from the diminished seventh by opposite directions of displacement
