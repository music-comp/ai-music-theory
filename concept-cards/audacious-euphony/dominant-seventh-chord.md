# Quick Definition
In Cohn's framework, the dominant seventh chord is a nearly even tetrachord formed by displacing one tone of a diminished seventh chord downward by semitone, making it a member of the Tristan genus alongside the half-diminished seventh.

# Formal Definition
The **dominant seventh chord** (V7) is one of two species of nearly even tetrachords in the Tristan genus. It is formed by:
1. Taking a fully diminished seventh chord (perfectly even tetrachord)
2. Displacing one of its four tones downward by semitone

There are twelve dominant seventh chords in the chromatic universe, each built on a different root. Each dominant seventh chord is a semitonal displacement of one of three diminished seventh chords.

Standard structure: major triad with minor seventh above root (e.g., G-B-D-F)

In Cohn's framework: nearly even tetrachord, member of Boretz region

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Interval structure:
- Standard view: [4, 3, 3, 2] (M3 + m3 + m3 + M2)
- Cohn's view: diminished seventh with one tone lowered

Boretz region membership:
- Each diminished seventh generates four V7 chords (one per displaced tone)
- Example: B-D-F-G# generates B7, D7, F7, Ab7 (by lowering B→Bb, D→Db, etc.)

Voice-leading properties:
- 2 units of work to any chord in same Boretz region
- Contrary semitonal motion to same-species chords (other V7s in region)
- Mixed motion to opposite-species chords (ø7s in region)

# Musical Context/Application
In Tristan-genus analysis, dominant seventh chords:
- Function as voice-leading entities, not necessarily as dominants
- Connect parsimoniously to other Tristan-genus chords
- May suspend their appellative (resolution-seeking) properties
- Form half of the 4-Cube Trio structure

The dominant seventh occupies odd-numbered voice-leading zones (1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11), complementary to the diminished seventh (even zones).

# Examples
**Tristan Prelude, chord 2**: The E7 chord (E-G#-B-D) results from resolving D# to D in the opening ø7 chord while displacing F to E, "spoiling" the B-D-F-G# diminished seventh in a different location.

**Sequential templates (Figure 7.8)**: V7 chords in T3 sequences feature contrary motion (two voices stationary, two moving by semitone in opposite directions), producing the "omnibus" or "devil's mill" progression.

**Chopin's e minor Prelude, Op. 28 no. 4**: V7 chords appear throughout the chromatic descent, connecting to ø7 and diminished seventh chords through semitonal voice leading.

# Common Confusions
- In this framework, V7 is not primarily defined by function but by voice-leading structure
- The "dominant" in "dominant seventh" does not require dominant function in this context
- V7 and ø7 are "inverses" with respect to displacement direction from diminished seventh
- The chord may or may not resolve according to tonal expectations
