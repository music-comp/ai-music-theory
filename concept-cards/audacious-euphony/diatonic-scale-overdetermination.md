# Quick Definition
The diatonic scale, like the consonant triad, is doubly determined: arising through fifth-generation (acoustic) but proving uniquely suited for modulatory variety (structural) due to its "unique multiplicity" property in equal-tempered chromatic space.

# Core Definition
The diatonic scale "evidently originated at a moment when modulation was inconceivable," yet once equal-tempered chromatic space arose, it had properties that "proved both unique and functionally powerful" (p. 231). Milton Babbitt observed: "The major scale is a maximal structure possessing [the property of unique multiplicity] in the usual equal-tempered division of the octave" (Babbitt 2003[1965]). Unique multiplicity means each interval class appears a different number of times: six perfect fourths, five whole steps, four minor thirds, etc. This ensures maximum diversity of common tones under transposition.

# Prerequisites
- **Overdetermination**: The general concept being applied to the scale
- **Generating interval**: The perfect fifth that generates the diatonic collection

# Key Properties
1. Fifth-generated: F-C-G-D-A-E-B
2. Unique multiplicity: different count for each interval class
3. Maximum diversity of common tones under transposition (n ranges from 2 to 6)
4. Only the chromatic heptachord shares this potential among seven-tone scales
5. The scale's overdetermination "played a central role in the new modulatory system of early-eighteenth-century harmonic tonality" (p. 231)

# Construction / Recognition
Acoustic origin: Fifth-generated collection arising through Pythagorean tuning.
Structural property (discovered later): For any diatonic collection, there exists another sharing n tones with it, where n ranges from 2 to 6. Common-tone retention under transposition is determined by interval multiplicity (p. 231, n. 7). Gamer (1967) calls this a "deep scale."

# Context & Application
The diatonic scale's overdetermination is parallel to the triad's: "just as the consonant triad's overdetermination played a central role in the early nineteenth century's discovery of an alternative to [diatonic tonality]" (p. 231). Both are homophonous diamorphs between acoustic and structural systems.

# Examples
- Six perfect fourths -> maximum common tones with fifth-related keys
- Five whole steps, four minor thirds, three major thirds, two minor seconds, one tritone
- The chromatic heptachord shares unique multiplicity but lacks acoustic motivation (p. 231, n. 7)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: The diatonic scale's modulatory properties were the reason it was originally adopted
  **Correction**: It arose through fifth-generation; modulatory properties were discovered later

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: "Unique multiplicity" is an acoustic property
  **Clarification**: It is a structural/combinatorial property of the scale's interval distribution
