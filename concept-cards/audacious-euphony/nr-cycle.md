# Quick Definition
A cycle of six triads generated by alternating N (Nebenverwandt) and R (Relative) transformations, traversing all six triads of a Weitzmann region and forming a closed loop.

# Formal Definition
An N/R cycle is a closed chain of six triads produced by alternating N and R transformations. Starting from any triad in a Weitzmann region, the cycle visits all six regional members before returning to the starting point. The cycle connects boundary triads (the S-related pair) to complete the loop. The N/R cycle is analogous to the L/P alternation that generates hexatonic cycles.

# Construction/Recognition
Example N/R cycle starting from C major (clockwise/authentic direction):
C major --(N)--> f minor --(R)--> Ab major --(N)--> db minor --(R)--> E major --(N)--> a minor --(R)--> C major

Alternative direction (counterclockwise/plagal):
C major --(R)--> a minor --(N)--> E major --(R)--> db/c# minor --(N)--> Ab major --(R)--> f minor --(N)--> C major

# Musical Context/Application
Complete N/R cycles appear in at least four Schubert instrumental works, typically in the "authentic" direction (clockwise) with major triads as local tonics and minor triads as subdominants. Liszt uses both directions: authentic in the Faust Symphony, plagal in "O Lieb, so lang du lieben kannst" and the "Vision" etude. The cycle produces whole-tone scales in the bass/treble when dominant sevenths are attached.

# Examples
- Schubert Fourth Symphony, 1st mvt., mm. 86-106: chronologically earliest complete N/R cycle
- Schubert Ninth Symphony, 1st mvt., mm. 304-15: complete cycle with dominant sevenths, producing complementary whole-tone scales
- Schubert Octet: complete N/R cycle with whole-tone prominence
- Schubert c minor Piano Sonata finale, mm. 383-87: complete six-triad cycle

# Common Confusions
- The cycle is closed because the two "boundary" triads (E major and db minor in the example) are S-related, completing the loop
- Unlike hexatonic cycles, the N/R cycle is not ordered by voice-leading proximity; it's ordered by historical/syntactic convention
- Both directions (authentic and plagal) produce valid complete cycles
