# Quick Definition
A cycle of six triads generated by alternating N (Nebenverwandt) and R (Relative) transformations, traversing all six triads of a Weitzmann region and forming a closed loop.

# Core Definition
An N/R cycle is "analogous to the L/P alternation that generates the hexatonic cycle" (p. 80). Starting from any triad, alternating N and R visits all six members of a Weitzmann region before returning. The cycle was "already suggested by Weitzmann in his initial, heuristically motivated description of the regions" (p. 80), though Weitzmann "did not indicate" the final link that "closes the chain into a cycle" since "his interest is in the contents of a region rather than its internal structure" (p. 80). The closing link connects the two boundary (S-related) triads.

# Prerequisites
- **N transformation**: One of the two alternating operations
- **R transformation**: The other alternating operation
- **Weitzmann region**: The N/R cycle traverses exactly one region

# Key Properties
1. Visits all six triads of a Weitzmann region
2. Generated by strict alternation of N and R
3. The cycle closes because the boundary triads (S-related) connect
4. Can be traversed in two directions: authentic (clockwise) and plagal (counterclockwise)
5. Unlike hexatonic cycles, the N/R ordering is based on syntactic convention, not voice-leading proximity
6. When dominant sevenths are attached to major triads, complementary whole-tone scales emerge

# Construction / Recognition
Example N/R cycle (authentic direction, from C major):
C major --(N)--> f minor --(R)--> Ab major --(N)--> db minor --(R)--> E major --(N)--> a minor --(R)--> C major

Plagal direction:
C major --(R)--> a minor --(N)--> E major --(R)--> c# minor --(N)--> Ab major --(R)--> f minor --(N)--> C major

# Context & Application
Complete N/R cycles appear in at least four Schubert instrumental passages. They represent the complete exploration of a Weitzmann region through sequential progression. The cycle provides historical and syntactic ordering even though the region's flat terrain permits any ordering.

# Examples
**Schubert, Fourth Symphony, 1st mvt., mm. 86-106** (p. 81): "The chronologically earliest of the four, and the only one to restrict its lexicon to consonant triads. Each major triad is prolonged as a local tonic, which each minor triad serves as subdominant."

**Schubert, Ninth Symphony trombone solo, mm. 304-15** (pp. 81-82): Complete cycle with dominant sevenths "producing complementary whole-tone scales in the treble register, chaining pungent minor-second suspensions with minor-third resolutions."

**Liszt, Faust Symphony** (p. 82): "Resembles figure 4.4: it moves in the authentic direction, employs no chordal dissonances, and accents the major triads as local tonics."

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming the N/R cycle represents the natural ordering of a Weitzmann region
  **Correction**: "The triads can be cyclically ordered in several ways, but there is no ordering that is more natural than the others, from the standpoint of voice leading" (p. 78)

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The N/R cycle is closed because Weitzmann described it as such
  **Clarification**: Weitzmann described a chain, not a cycle; Cohn closes it by connecting the boundary triads
- **Confusion**: Both directions are equally common
  **Clarification**: Schubert consistently uses the authentic direction; Liszt explores both
