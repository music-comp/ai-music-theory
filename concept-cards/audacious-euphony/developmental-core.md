# Quick Definition
The central, most harmonically active section of a sonata-form development, where hexatonic cycles frequently appear in late-eighteenth-century practice -- providing a structural home for pan-triadic progressions within an otherwise diatonically tonal framework.

# Formal Definition
The developmental core is the portion of a development section (in sonata or other forms) where the most intensive harmonic activity occurs, typically featuring modulation through multiple key areas and thematic fragmentation or combination. Cohn observes that the earliest hexatonic cycles in the classical repertoire characteristically appear in developmental cores, where their harmonically open, texturally diverse character suits the function of tonal exploration. These passages exhibit layered tonality: diatonic at both local and global levels, but chromatic at the middleground level of tonic succession.

# Construction/Recognition
Characteristics of hexatonic developmental cores in early examples:
- Harmonically open (not closed on the same key)
- Texturally diverse (thematic fragmentation, stretti, motivic development)
- Tonicizing dissonances buffer the stations of the hexatonic cycle
- Essential enharmonic transformation occurs within the passage
- The passage typically initiates from one key area and arrives at another via hexatonic transit

# Musical Context
The developmental core provides a natural home for hexatonic progressions because its function already demands departure from the tonic and exploration of remote harmonic territory. In early examples, hexatonic progressions are embedded within this conventional formal function; in later nineteenth-century practice, they appear more freely in various formal contexts.

# Examples
- Mozart, Symphony in Eb major, K. 543, finale, mm. 109-126: "The excerpt initiates the developmental core of a monothematic sonata-form movement. Beginning in Ab major with a partial quotation of the principal theme, the music proceeds... through a hexatonic cycle" (figure 2.5)
- Haydn, Symphony No. 98, finale, mm. 148-198: "the passage likewise constitutes the developmental core of a final movement" -- series of four-measure thematic segments traversing a hexatonic cycle (figure 2.6)

# Common Confusions
- Not all developmental cores contain hexatonic progressions; the term designates a formal function, not a harmonic requirement
- The hexatonic progression within a developmental core is typically at the middleground level, not the surface
- Later nineteenth-century composers use hexatonic progressions outside of developmental cores in various formal and textural contexts
- The concept is distinct from "development" in the general sense; it refers specifically to the most harmonically active portion
