# Quick Definition
Analysis of Richard Strauss's "Fruhling" (1949) showing how an opening stanza explores a complete Weitzmann region through nonsequential paths, with the S motive (R + LP) serving as transformational signature.

# Formal Definition
"Fruhling" from Strauss's Orchesterlieder (1949) organizes its first stanza around a Weitzmann region (GBEb center), traversed nonsequentially. The transformational signature R + LP = S appears at the opening, is retrograded at the stanza's close, fragmented in the interlude, and inverted at the second stanza's opening. The bass pitches form a whole-tone collection, and the passage features two enharmonic transformations.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Opening stanza path through GBEb region:
- c minor -> ab minor (LP) -> B major (R): exhausts half of region
- [A major, Bb major intervene outside region]
- e minor -> G major (R) -> Eb major (PL): remaining half

Transformational motive:
- R + LP = S presented at opening (c minor to B major)
- R + PL = S retrograded at close (e minor to Eb major)
- Interlude: double-agent juxtaposition (like Chopin Nocturne Op. 9 No. 1)
- Second stanza: inverted motive in CEAb region

Bass pitches: C, Ab, B, E, G, Eb = whole-tone collection

# Musical Context/Application
Composed near the end of Strauss's life (nearly two centuries after C.P.E. Bach's f minor Sonata), "Fruhling" demonstrates the continued relevance of Weitzmann regions in post-Romantic practice. The nonsequential exploration reflects Strauss's characteristically restless harmonic language while maintaining coherent voice-leading structure.
