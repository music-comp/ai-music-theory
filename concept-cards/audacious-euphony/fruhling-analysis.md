# Quick Definition
Analysis of Strauss's "Fruhling" (1949) showing how an opening stanza explores a complete Weitzmann region through the R+LP=S transformational motive, with six bass pitches forming a whole-tone collection.

# Core Definition
"Fruhling" from Strauss's *Orchesterlieder* (1949) organizes its first stanza around the EbGB Weitzmann region through nonsequential paths. The transformational signature R+LP=S "appears at the opening of the first stanza, retrograded at the close of the first stanza, fragmented into its component transformations in the interlude, and inverted at the opening of the second stanza" (cf. p. 100). The six bass pitches form a whole-tone collection (Kaplan 1994). The second stanza shifts to the CEAb region, "rupturing the boundaries of the individual Weitzmann region" (p. 100).

# Prerequisites
- **Weitzmann region**: The passage explores one, then shifts to another
- **S transformation**: The net result of the R+LP motive
- **LP=S identity**: The decomposition that structures the motive
- **Double-agent complex**: Appears in the orchestral interlude

# Key Properties
1. First stanza explores EbGB region through nonsequential paths
2. R+LP=S motive appears in original, retrograde, fragmented, and inverted forms
3. Bass pitches form a whole-tone collection: C, Ab, B, E, G, Eb
4. Interlude features double-agent juxtaposition (like Chopin Nocturne Op. 9 No. 1)
5. Second stanza shifts to CEAb region via C major -> c# minor -> E major
6. Two enharmonic transformations within the passage

# Construction / Recognition
Opening stanza path:
- c minor -> ab minor (LP) -> B major (R): exhausts half of region (= S from c minor to B major)
- [A major, Bb major intervene outside region]
- e minor -> G major (R) -> Eb major (PL): remaining half (= S from e minor to Eb major, retrograded)

# Context & Application
Composed near the end of Strauss's life (1949), "Fruhling" demonstrates the continued relevance of Weitzmann regions in post-Romantic practice, nearly two centuries after C.P.E. Bach's f minor Sonata.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Expecting sequential N/R cyclic exploration
  **Correction**: Strauss's "characteristically restless harmonic practice requires the analyst to wrestle the interpretation to the ground a bit" (p. 98)

# Common Confusions

  **Clarification**: It demonstrates interregional modulation, previewing Chapters 5-6
