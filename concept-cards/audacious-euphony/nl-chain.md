# Quick Definition
An N/L chain is a sequence alternating Nebenverwandt (N) and Leittonwechsel (L) transformations, producing semitonally ascending or descending minor tonics with intervening dominant seventh chords.

# Formal Definition
An **N/L chain** is a transformation sequence that alternates:
- **N** (Nebenverwandt): connects minor triad to its major dominant (e.g., e minor → B major)
- **L** (Leittonwechsel): connects major triad to minor triad a semitone higher (e.g., B major → e# minor, spelled as f minor)

The chain produces:
- Semitonally ascending minor triads (under upshift)
- Intervening major triads functioning as dominants
- Consistent voice-leading trajectory (upshift or downshift depending on direction)

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
N/L chain structure:
- Minor → Major (N): Weitzmann-class transformation
- Major → Minor (L): Hexatonic-class transformation
- Combined: alternating W and H classes

Voice-leading trajectory:
- N (minor to major): typically balanced or slight downshift
- L (major to minor): upshift
- Combined: net upshift

Example sequence: e- → B+ → f- → C+ → f#- → C#+ → g- ...
(Each minor triad ascends by semitone from the previous)

# Musical Context/Application
N/L chains serve compositional purposes:
- Creating directed chromatic motion
- Building developmental energy through upshifting
- Connecting distant key areas systematically
- Providing dominant preparation for each arrival

The chain is particularly effective because:
- Each minor triad has a clear dominant relationship
- Semitonal ascent creates mounting tension
- Voice leading is consistently efficient

# Examples
**Dvorak's "New World" Symphony, first movement development (mm. 233-257)**: The third paragraph executes an extended N/L chain carrying through to the fourth paragraph. Semitonally ascending minor tonics appear every eight measures: eb minor (233) → e minor (241) → f minor (249) → f# minor (257). The four-bar median of each eight-bar span is marked by arrival of a dominant seventh chord of the subsequent minor tonic.

**Beethoven's "Waldstein" Sonata, Op. 53, development (mm. 120-130)**: The upshift stalls in a Weitzmann region, connecting Bb major → b minor through an N/R chain, reaching the retransitional dominant.

# Common Confusions
- N/L differs from N/R; L produces semitonal ascent, R produces whole-step relationships
- The chain direction matters: N/L upshifts, L/N would have different trajectory
- Dominant seventh chords in the chain are passing, not structural prolongations
- Enharmonic spelling changes don't affect the chain's structure
