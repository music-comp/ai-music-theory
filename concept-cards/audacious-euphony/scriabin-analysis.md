# Quick Definition
Cohn's analysis of Scriabin's Feuillet d'Album, Op. 58 (1910), demonstrates how GWR principles extend to six-note chords, showing voice-leading among mystic chord transpositions that stay within a single hexachordal region.

# Core Definition
The **Scriabin analysis** applies GWR concepts to six-note chords. Figure 7.26 presents "the three transpositions of the mystic chord as they appear at the opening of the first three phrases" (p. 184). Each chord differs from the whole-tone collection by one "spoiler" tone. The progressions illustrated are "in and out of a single equal division and thus are analogous to transformations within a Weitzmann or Boretz system" (p. 185). Bridging progressions between whole-tone systems "would be far less parsimonious, because of the number of tones included in each chord. Two tones... would be held invariant, and each of the remaining four voices would be displaced by semitone" (p. 185).

# Prerequisites
- **Generalized Weitzmann region**: The theoretical framework
- **Mystic chord**: The chord type being analyzed
- **Spoiler tone**: The analytical tool applied to hexachords

# Key Properties
1. All three chord progressions stay within a single whole-tone GWR (intra-regional)
2. Voice-leading connections made salient through registral transfers and motivic play
3. Direction of semitonal motion echoes direction of registral displacement
4. Bridging between GWRs would require 4 moving voices (not attempted by Scriabin here)

# Construction / Recognition
Three mystic chord transpositions (Figure 7.26):
1. First chord: whole-tone except D# substitutes for D (alto voice spoiler)
2. Second chord: D# recuperated to Ebb in bass, but E to F creates new alto spoiler
3. Third chord: F recuperated to E in bass, C# becomes new alto spoiler

Scriabin's voice-leading techniques:
- Registral transfers between spoiler and soon-to-be-spoiled tones (mm. 4, 8-9)
- Voice exchanges making idealized voice leadings audible
- Semitonal motion delayed to coincide with motivic events

# Context & Application
The analysis demonstrates both the extension and the limits of GWR theory. Within a hexachordal GWR, the same 2-unit voice-leading distance applies. But bridging is impractical (4 moving voices). The analysis thus illuminates "the special capacities of triads, as selected from all possible nearly even chord types" (p. 185).

# Examples
- **Feuillet d'Album, mm. 1-4**: D# spoiler and soon-to-be-spoiled E exchange registers, triggering semitonal motion back into whole-tone collection then spoiling it again with F (p. 184)
- **Mm. 8-9**: Similar voice exchange between spoiler F and soon-to-be-spoiled C/B#; spoiling motion delayed until after remaining voices enter (p. 185)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking the Scriabin analysis demonstrates rich bridging voice leading
  **Correction**: All progressions stay within a single GWR; bridging is not attempted and would be impractical

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming the mystic chord analysis is central to Cohn's theory
  **Clarification**: It is presented to show the limits and generality of the theory, demonstrating why triads are special, not to propose hexachordal analysis as a practical tool
