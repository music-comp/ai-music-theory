# Quick Definition
Schubert's "Auf dem Flusse" from *Winterreise* exemplifies the departure-return script through multiple settings that all downshift from e minor through zones 10 to 5 and upshift back, with transformational substitutions (**P** for **L**, **H** for **L**) creating varied realizations of the same zonal trajectory.

# Formal Definition
The chromatic sections of "Auf dem Flusse" all involve a departure-return script realized as downshifting from e minor (zone 10) and upshifting back to that tonic or its zonal affiliate g# minor. The paradigmatic analysis (figure 6.8) reveals that all settings share the abstract pattern W -> H -> W for downshift (zones 10 -> 5) and the same combination for upshift. The first two stanzas use **N** -> **L** -> **N** for both legs. The final stanza introduces H-class substitutions: **P** for **L** (m. 48) and **H** for **L** (m. 66), creating major-third transpositions that require rectification.

# Construction/Recognition
Paradigmatic analysis framework (figure 6.8):
- Abstract level: W -> H -> W downshift zones 10 -> 5; same upshift back to 10
- Stanzas I, II: **N** -> **L** -> **N** both legs
- Stanza V, couplet 1: **N** -> **L** -> **N** departure, **N** -> **P** -> **N** return (P-for-L substitution)
- Stanza V, couplet 2 (mm. 55-62): Extended departure to zone 4 then 2
- Stanza V, couplet 3 (mm. 64-72): **H**-for-**L** substitution during downshift

# Musical Context
Richard Kramer calls this song's modulatory adventure "extreme" and its tonal graph the most complex in *Winterreise*. Yet the song achieves this complexity without approaching an enharmonic seam. It lives entirely "downstream" from its tonic, saturated with *nebenverwandt* relationships.

# Examples
- The **P**-for-**L** substitution at m. 48 diverts d# minor to D# major instead of B major, causing transposition up by major third
- The **H**-for-**L** substitution at m. 66 takes B major to g minor instead of d# minor ("shocking" hexatonic pole)
- The W-class transformations are always realized by **N** (nebenverwandt) without substitution
- The song is nebenverwandt-saturated: every minor triad is adjacent to its major dominant

# Common Confusions
- The song's complexity arises from H-class substitutions, not enharmonic ambiguity
- All chromatic sections share the same abstract zonal trajectory (10 -> 5 -> 10) despite surface variety
- The subdominant is almost entirely absent; the song is dominated by dominant-function motion
