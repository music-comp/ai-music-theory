# Quick Definition
A voice-leading zone is a region of triadic voice-leading space where the center of balance remains roughly constant, representing what is prolonged in hexatonic progressions rather than a traditional tonic.

# Formal Definition
When triads progress through a hexatonic cycle, the center of balance (a kind of weighted pitch center) "toggles back and forth between two pitches separated by one-third of a semitone" (p. 45). This near-stasis "coalesces around the prolongation not of a tonic, in any standard construal of the term, but rather of a zone of voice-leading space (or voice-leading zone)" (p. 45). The concept provides an alternative to tonic-based prolongation for understanding large-scale coherence in chromatic music.

# Construction/Recognition
Properties of voice-leading zones:
- Defined by balanced voice leading (aggregate upward motion = aggregate downward motion)
- Each hexatonic system occupies one voice-leading zone
- Circumnavigation of a hexatonic cycle produces no net directional motion
- The zone is characterized by the position of its central augmented triad
- Movement between zones requires leaving the hexatonic system

Analogy from the text (p. 45): "Like a walker or a waterfall, the incessant local fluctuations are underlain by a global stasis."

# Musical Context
Voice-leading zones provide a way to describe large-scale coherence in pan-triadic passages without invoking tonal hierarchy. A passage that remains within a single hexatonic system is "prolonging" its voice-leading zone, even though no single triad functions as tonic. This concept becomes central in Chapter 5, where it is developed more fully.

# Common Confusions
- A voice-leading zone is NOT a key or tonal center; it does not privilege any single triad
- The "zone" is defined by geometry (center of balance), not by tonal function
- Multiple triads can occupy the same voice-leading zone
- Do not confuse with "key area" or "tonal region" - voice-leading zones are defined differently
