# Quick Definition
The P (Parallel) transformation converts a major triad to its parallel minor or vice versa by moving the third by one chromatic semitone while preserving the root and fifth as common tones.

# Formal Definition
P is an involutory transformation (applying it twice returns to the original triad) that maps each consonant triad to the triad of opposite mode sharing the same root. The transformation preserves the perfect fifth dyad (root and fifth) and displaces the third by chromatic semitone: major third becomes minor third (e.g., E to Eb) or minor third becomes major third (e.g., Eb to E).

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Voice-leading specification:
- Root: held as common tone
- Third: moves by chromatic semitone (down for major-to-minor, up for minor-to-major)
- Fifth: held as common tone

Algebraic properties:
- P(P(X)) = X (involution)
- Voice-leading work = 1 semitone
- Common tones = 2 (the perfect fifth)

Examples:
- P(C major) = c minor: (C, E, G) -> (C, Eb, G)
- P(c minor) = C major: (C, Eb, G) -> (C, E, G)
- P(F# major) = f# minor: (F#, A#, C#) -> (F#, A, C#)

# Musical Context/Application
P transformations are ubiquitous in tonal music as mode mixture or modal mutation. In hexatonic progressions, P alternates with L to generate the complete cycle. The P transformation produces a chromatic semitone (same letter name) on the Tonnetz, appearing as vertical motion in the standard angled format.

# Examples
From the text:
- The Tarnhelm progression from Wagner's Ring begins with P transformations (p. 42)
- Beethoven's "Spring" Sonata, Adagio: Bb major mutates to bb minor at the start of the middle section (p. 44-45)
- Schubert's Piano Trio Op. 100: "The unmelodied accompaniment (or 'vamp') mutates a major chord to its parallel minor" (p. 45)

# Common Confusions
- P transformation is NOT the same as the R (relative) transformation, which connects relative major/minor (e.g., C major to a minor)
- The semitone in P is chromatic (same letter name: E-Eb), while the semitone in L is diatonic (different letter names: C-B)
- P preserves the root; do not confuse with other transformations that preserve different dyads
