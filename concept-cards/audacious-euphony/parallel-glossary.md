# Quick Definition
The relation between two consonant triads that share a root, for example C major and c minor; or the transformation that produces one of these triads from the other.

# Formal Definition
P (Parallel) is defined in the Glossary as "the relation between two consonant triads that share a root, for example, C major and c minor; or the transformation that produces one of these triads from the other." The transformation is an involution (P(P(X)) = X), involves one semitone of voice-leading work (the third moves by chromatic semitone), and preserves two common tones (root and fifth). P is one of the three H-group transformations that map triads within hexatonic regions.

# Construction/Recognition
Transformation specification:
- Input: Consonant triad
- Output: Consonant triad of opposite mode sharing same root
- Voice leading: Root held, fifth held, third moves by chromatic semitone
- Work: 1 semitone
- Common tones: 2 (the perfect fifth)

Algebraic properties:
- P is an involution: P(P(X)) = X
- P is a member of the H-group (along with L and H)
- P preserves hexatonic region membership
- P combined with L generates hexatonic cycles

# Musical Context/Application
P is the most familiar of the Neo-Riemannian transformations, corresponding to the traditional concept of mode mixture or modal mutation. In tonal music, P typically connects a triad with its parallel major or minor. In chromatic/pan-triadic contexts, P participates in hexatonic cycles (alternating with L) and can serve as part of longer transformational chains.

# Examples
- P(C major) = c minor
- P(f# minor) = F# major
- P(Ab major) = ab minor
- Mode mixture in tonal contexts
- Hexatonic cycles: L-P-L-P-L-P returns to starting triad

# Common Confusions
- P is not the same as R (Relative), which connects relative major/minor
- The semitone in P is chromatic (E to Eb), not diatonic
- P preserves the root; L preserves the minor-third dyad; R preserves the major-third dyad
- "Parallel" here means sharing a root, not parallel motion in voice leading
