# Quick Definition
Analysis of Wagner's Tarnhelm motive (1853) showing a permuted double-agent complex where the relative major follows rather than precedes the minor subdominant, producing a direct LP (hexatonic polar) progression.

# Core Definition
"The Tarnhelm swaps the order of two of Chopin's components: the relative major follows rather than precedes its minor subdominant. This produces a direct LP progression from g# minor to e minor, creating the uncanny effect discussed in chapter 2" (p. 91). The permutation creates tonal ambiguity: "g# minor: suggested by opening melodic gesture; e minor: suggested by B open fifth as half cadence; B major: activated when second phrase is omitted" (cf. pp. 91-92). Wagner often adds an under-seventh F, resolving as augmented sixth.

# Prerequisites
- **Double-agent complex**: The basic structure being permuted
- **LP transformation**: The hexatonic polar progression created by the permutation
- **Hexatonic pole**: The uncanny effect of the LP progression

# Key Properties
1. Permuted ordering: g# minor -> e minor (LP) -> B open fifth
2. Direct hexatonic polar progression (LP) creates "uncanny effect"
3. Triple tonal ambiguity: g# minor, e minor, B major
4. Under-seventh F on initial triad enables augmented-sixth resolution to B
5. B open fifth permits both e minor and B major interpretations

# Construction / Recognition
Standard: g# minor -> B major (N) -> e minor (R) -> a minor (N)
Tarnhelm permutation: g# minor -> e minor (LP) -> B (open fifth)

# Context & Application
The Tarnhelm's permuted complex enables Wagner to exploit tonal ambiguity for dramatic purposes -- the magic helmet transforms appearances, and the music's tonal instability mirrors this function.

# Examples
**Wagner, Das Rheingold** (p. 91): g# minor - e minor - B open fifth.
**Darcy 1993, 169-70** (p. 91): "The salience of the underlying i-III-V# core is sufficient to prompt a tonally well-formed hearing in g# minor."
