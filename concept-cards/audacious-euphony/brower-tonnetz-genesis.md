# Quick Definition
Candace Brower's (2008) model generating the diatonic Tonnetz in stages from a seed node, retracing pitch system development across historical time and exploring the interplay of centrifugal (acoustic/consonance) and centripetal (closure-seeking) forces.

# Formal Definition
Rather than accepting the Tonnetz as a space given all at once, Brower generates it in stages from a seed node. Her stages roughly retrace the development of pitch systems across historical time and explore the interplay of forces that precipitate and shape this development. She recognizes a centrifugal force operating on purely tuned triads, transposing them by purely tuned intervals, balanced by a centripetal force relying on psychophysiological propensity to ignore sub-threshold pitch differences, treating similarity as equivalence and inducing systemic closure through symmetric completion. This tale is "a variation on Plato's allegory of musical forces whose reconciliation provides a model for the ideal civic society."

# Construction/Recognition
Stage 1 (Figure 8.4a): C major transposed by perfect fifth yields F major (left) and G major (right); neighboring triads share single point

Stage 2 (Figure 8.4b): E's fifth relations with A and B close local triangles, creating trapezoid periphery

Stage 3 (Figure 8.4c): Adding d minor (not b minor) replicates a tone already present, creating first stage of closure (syntonic image identification)

Stage 4 (Figure 8.4d): Diatonic collection as parallelogram tiled into six consonant triads

Final closure: Accepting pseudo-fifth (B-F) creates Mobius strip topology

Brower's distinction between pure and flexible tuning recasts as tension between acoustic consonance and melodic proximity - "the tension that has been central to this book from the outset."

# Musical Context
The genesis narrative reveals:
- Near-evenness of diatonic triads in 7-tone space ensures any two species are step-connected
- Acoustic properties and voice-leading properties are inseparable ("overdetermined diatonic triad")
- The diatonic Tonnetz is "dually determined" before chromatic extensions even arise
- Sequential passages (Fetis's "law of uniformity") operate within the diatonic Tonnetz via step-connected voice leading

The narrative also traces historical expansion: Renaissance extension of fifth chains, classical-era modulatory shell, romantic-era chromatic syntax.

# Examples
- The seed triad (C major) generates F-C-G through fifth transposition (Figure 8.4a)
- The syntonic comma between the two D tones (remote corners) represents the first closure opportunity
- Brower's orientation rotates Cohn's Tonnetz 90 degrees to place dominants above tonics
- The process mirrors Hauptmann and Riemann's derivation of the diatonic scale (Harrison 1994, 44-45)

# Common Confusions
- The genesis is not merely historical narrative; it reveals the structural logic of the Tonnetz
- The stages are not arbitrary; each represents a principled decision about closure
- Centrifugal force (acoustic extension) and centripetal force (closure-seeking) are not opposed; they collaborate
- The final Mobius strip topology is not Brower's invention but follows from accepting the pseudo-fifth
