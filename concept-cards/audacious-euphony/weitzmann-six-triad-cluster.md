# Quick Definition
Weitzmann's six-triad cluster is a graphical representation (Figure 3.9 / Table 3.1) showing each augmented triad at the center of six consonant triads related to it by single or double semitonal displacement.

# Core Definition
In Weitzmann's 1853 treatise, Figure 3.9 presents "each augmented triad at the center of a cluster of consonant triads; major and minor triads are indicated by large- and small-case roots, respectively. The chords placed immediately next to the augmented triad are attained through the half-step progression of one of their voices; the [chords] further away [are attained] through the half-step progression of two of their voices" (pp. 75-76). Table 3.1 lists the four clusters exhaustively, partitioning all 24 consonant triads.

# Prerequisites
- **Augmented triad**: Each cluster is centered on one augmented triad
- **Weitzmann's treatise**: The cluster diagram is a product of the treatise's third genesis narrative
- **Weitzmann's three genesis narratives**: The cluster embodies the third narrative (augmented triads generate consonant triads)

# Key Properties
1. Four clusters, each centered on one of the four augmented triads
2. Each cluster contains 3 major and 3 minor triads
3. Inner ring (1 semitone displacement): major triads on augmented triad roots, minor triads on roots one semitone below
4. Outer ring (2 semitone displacement): modal variants of inner-ring triads
5. The four clusters exhaust all 24 consonant triads without overlap
6. "These passages from Weitzmann's treatise are so rich in implication that they guide the work presented in the next three chapters" (p. 76)

# Construction / Recognition
Per Table 3.1 (p. 75):
1. **{C, E, G#}**: C major, E major, Ab major, a minor, c# minor, f minor
2. **{Db, F, A}**: Db major, F major, A major, bb minor, d minor, f# minor
3. **{D, F#, A#}**: D major, Gb major, Bb major, b minor, eb minor, g minor
4. **{Eb, G, B}**: Eb major, G major, B major, c minor, e minor, g# minor

# Context & Application
The cluster diagram anticipates the modern understanding of Weitzmann regions. Chapter 4 formalizes these clusters as regions with specific internal structure, voice-leading properties, and transformational relationships. Chapter 5 uses Figure 3.9 to extend the Tonnetz. Chapter 6 uses the extended universe as an analytical map.

# Examples
**Weitzmann's explanatory text** (pp. 75-76): "From the following augmented triads... arise the [consonant] triads indicated by the letters next to them.... The chords placed immediately next to the augmented triad are attained through the half-step progression of one of their voices; the [chords] further away [are attained] through the half-step progression of two of their voices."

**Chapter implications** (p. 76): "Chapter 4 considers the internal structure of the six-triad pools... Chapter 5 uses figure 3.9 as a stage from which to extend the Tonnetz... Chapter 6 uses that extended universe as a playing board."

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating "cluster" as a musical chord or cluster in the compositional sense
  **Correction**: It is a spatial metaphor for the organization of triads around an augmented triad

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The "closer" and "farther" triads differ in functional relationship
  **Clarification**: The difference is in voice-leading distance (1 vs. 2 semitones), not functional hierarchy
- **Confusion**: Each consonant triad belongs to only one cluster
  **Clarification**: Correct -- each consonant triad appears in exactly one cluster (no overlap), unlike some other theoretical partitions
