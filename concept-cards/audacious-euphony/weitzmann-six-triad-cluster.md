# Quick Definition
Weitzmann's six-triad cluster is a graphical representation (Figure 3.9 in the treatise) showing each augmented triad at the center of six consonant triads that are related to it by single or double semitonal displacement.

# Formal Definition
In Weitzmann's 1853 treatise, Figure 3.9 presents each augmented triad "at the center of a cluster of consonant triads; major and minor triads are indicated by large- and small-case roots, respectively." The triads "placed immediately next to the augmented triad are attained through the half-step progression of one of their voices; the [chords] further away [are attained] through the half-step progression of two of their voices" (p. 75-76). This diagram anticipates the modern understanding of hexatonic systems.

# Construction/Recognition
Structure of each cluster:
- Center: One augmented triad
- Inner ring (1 semitone away): 6 consonant triads (3 major, 3 minor)
  - Major triads: roots on each tone of the augmented triad
  - Minor triads: roots one semitone below each tone of the augmented triad
- Outer ring (2 semitones away): 6 additional triads
  - These are the parallel triads of the inner ring

Per Table 3.1, the four clusters:
1. {C, E, G#} generates: C major, E major, Ab major, a minor, c# minor, f minor
2. {Db, F, A} generates: Db major, F major, A major, bb minor, d minor, f# minor
3. {D, F#, A#} generates: D major, Gb major, Bb major, b minor, eb minor, g minor
4. {Eb, G, B} generates: Eb major, G major, B major, c minor, e minor, g# minor

# Musical Context/Application
Weitzmann's diagram represents the first systematic visualization of what would later be called hexatonic systems. It shows that all 24 consonant triads can be organized around the four augmented triads, with each consonant triad belonging to exactly one cluster (inner ring) or two clusters (outer ring via parallel relation).

# Common Confusions
- "Cluster" is a spatial metaphor; the triads do not form a chord or cluster in the musical sense
- The "closer" and "farther" triads differ in voice-leading distance, not functional relationship
- Each consonant triad appears in one cluster's inner ring but may connect to other clusters via 2-semitone relations
- The diagram is static but represents dynamic voice-leading relationships
