# Quick Definition
The opening of Rimsky-Korsakov's Symphony No. 2 "Antar" (1868) balances consonant and augmented triads in exquisite equilibrium, with the hexatonic collection strictly observed but the relative priority of consonance and dissonance left ambiguous.

# Formal Definition
The Antar opening, likely modeled on Liszt's Faust, consists of two rotations through three segments. Unlike Faust's explicit inversion of consonance/dissonance hierarchy, Antar "balances an exquisitely fine point between its terms" (p. 70). The passage is "stricter than Faust in its hexatonicism - the passage contains not a single pitch foreign to the collection - [yet] its augmented triads are less apparent" (p. 70). The analysis can privilege either the consonant triads or the augmented triad as structural.

# Construction/Recognition
Two analytical interpretations (Figures 3.6 and 3.7):

Figure 3.6 - Consonant triads as structural:
- Opening presents f# minor -> d minor, then bb minor -> f# minor
- Augmented triad (FAC#) passes between consonant triads, distending the progression
- "The augmented triad staggers the simultaneous semitonal motions of the opening progression"

Figure 3.7 - Augmented triad as structural:
- Opening gesture unfolds an augmented triad through semitonal pairs
- Metric ambiguity leaves it unclear whether FAC# or BbDf# is emphasized
- Horn's clear commitment to FAC# provides slight disambiguation
- "The segment labeled B provides clarity, first to the bassus and then the cantus"

Structural observation: "Each of the minor triads sounded in the opening segment results from displacement of a component of the augmented triad."

# Musical Context/Application
Antar represents a subtler approach to consonance/dissonance reciprocity than Faust. Rather than explicitly inverting the hierarchy, Rimsky-Korsakov creates a situation where both interpretations (consonant triads governing vs. augmented triad governing) are analytically viable.

# Examples
From the text (pp. 70-73):

Rimsky-Korsakov's self-assessment: "Looking back two decades later at the kuchist movement of which he had been principal in the 1860s, Rimsky wrote that 'Liszt was extreme, so was Berlioz, so was Wagner. And so were we' (Taruskin 1996). The opening of Antar suggests that Rimsky was understating his capacity to nuance that extremism to artistic ends."

On the metric ambiguity: "As the metric grid does not lock in until m. 4, it is unclear whether the first or second component of each pair is the accented one, and hence whether it is the FAC# or BbDF# augmented triad that is unfolded."

On timbral clarification: "The timbral continuity of the bassoon helps to forge this connection and to project this weighting retrospectively onto the cantus of mm. 1-3."

# Common Confusions
- The two analytical interpretations are not "right" or "wrong" - the passage supports both
- The equilibrium is more subtle than Faust's explicit inversion
- The augmented triad need not sound as a chord to function structurally
- "Stricter hexatonicism" refers to pitch content, not structural clarity
