# Quick Definition
The opening of Rimsky-Korsakov's Symphony No. 2 *Antar* (1868) balances consonant and augmented triads in exquisite equilibrium, with the hexatonic collection strictly observed but the relative priority of consonance and dissonance left analytically ambiguous.

# Core Definition
Likely modeled on Liszt's *Faust*, the *Antar* opening consists of two rotations through three segments, but "balances an exquisitely fine point between its terms" (p. 70). The passage is "stricter than *Faust* in its hexatonicism -- the passage contains not a single pitch foreign to the collection -- [yet] its augmented triads are less apparent" (p. 70). Cohn presents two competing analyses: Figure 3.6, where consonant triads are structural and the augmented triad interpolates between them, and Figure 3.7, where the augmented triad is structural and consonant triads result from single-semitone displacements of its components.

# Construction / Recognition
**Figure 3.6 (consonant triads as structural)**: Opening presents f# minor -> d minor, then bb minor -> f#minor; augmented triad "staggers the simultaneous semitonal motions" between consonant triads.

**Figure 3.7 (augmented triad as structural)**: Opening gesture unfolds an augmented triad through semitonal pairs; "each of the minor triads sounded in the opening segment results from displacement of a component of the augmented triad" (p. 73).

# Context & Application
*Antar* represents a subtler approach to consonance/dissonance reciprocity than *Faust*. Rather than explicitly inverting the hierarchy, Rimsky-Korsakov creates a passage where both interpretations are analytically viable, exemplifying the analytical choice between consonant and augmented structural centers.

# Examples
**Rimsky-Korsakov's self-assessment** (p. 70): "Looking back two decades later at the *kuchist* movement... Rimsky wrote that 'Liszt was extreme, so was Berlioz, so was Wagner. And so were *we*.' The opening of *Antar* suggests that Rimsky was understating his capacity to nuance that extremism to artistic ends."

**Metric disambiguation** (p. 72): "The arrival of pedal F2 at m. 4 stakes down the metric grid clearly for the first time, conferring the accent of the F# -> F onto its second term."

**Timbral disambiguation** (p. 73): "The timbral continuity of the bassoon helps to forge this connection and to project this weighting retrospectively onto the cantus of mm. 1-3."

# Common Errors

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: "Stricter hexatonicism" means the structure is clearer
  **Clarification**: Stricter hexatonicism refers to pitch content; the structural ambiguity is actually greater than in *Faust*
- **Confusion**: The augmented triad must sound as a chord to be structural
  **Clarification**: In Figure 3.7, the augmented triad is structural despite never sounding as a vertical simultaneity before m. 4
