# Quick Definition
The **P**/**R** cycle serves as the primordial triadic generator for octatonic systems, with bass motion tracing diminished seventh chords and combined voices producing octatonic collections, analogous to the role of **L**/**P** in generating hexatonic systems.

# Formal Definition
When a **P**/**R** chain is modeled on the Tonnetz, its bounding axes are generated by minor thirds, forming diminished seventh chords that combine to produce an octatonic collection. The **P**/**R** cycle thus functions as a structural parallel to the **L**/**P** cycle's relationship with hexatonic systems: **L**/**P** generates hexatonic collections, while **P**/**R** generates octatonic collections. This generative parallelism reflects a broader structural relationship between hexatonic and octatonic organizations of chromatic space.

# Construction/Recognition
Starting from any major triad:
- Apply **P** (e.g., C major to c minor): roots form one diminished seventh chord
- Apply **R** (e.g., c minor to Eb major): fifths form complementary diminished seventh chord
- Continue the chain: Eb major --(**P**)--> eb minor --(**R**)--> Gb major...
- The bass progression C - Eb - Gb - A traces a diminished seventh chord
- Upper voices trace the complementary diminished seventh chord
- Combined pitch classes form an octatonic collection

# Musical Context
The **P**/**R** chain's octatonic connection explains the deep structural affinity between triadic chromaticism and octatonic pitch organization found across nineteenth-century music.

# Common Confusions
- The octatonic connection is specific to the **P**/**R** combination; the other T3 pairs (**N**/**H**, **S**/**L**) do not produce octatonic collections in the same way
- The parallel **L**/**P** : hexatonic :: **P**/**R** : octatonic is structural, not coincidental
- The octatonic collection emerges from combining the two diminished seventh chords traced by the chain
