# Quick Definition
Schubert's "Der Doppelganger" exemplifies a neighborhood-based compositional strategy, with the entire song organized around the gradual accumulation and completion of F#'s pitch-class neighborhood at the tonal, dyadic, and triadic levels.

# Formal Definition
The song presents a neighborhood centered on F#. The opening establishes four tones of that neighborhood (B, A#, D, C#) in dyadic partnership with a drone F#, developing into an ostinato that sometimes substitutes A for A# as a fifth tone. Each dyad admits two triadic completions, and the song engages all possibilities. The dramatic climax occurs when D# is introduced at m. 47 as root of a minor triad, completing F#'s pitch-class neighborhood. The final B major triad at the cadence completes the neighborhood at the dyadic and triadic level as well.

# Construction/Recognition
Figure 6.2 presents the narrative as cumulative Tonnetz snapshots:
- (a) m. 1: F# drone established
- (b) m. 4: Four tones of neighborhood presented as dyads
- (c) m. 7: b minor arpeggiated, filling BD dyad
- (d) mm. 10-11: A sounded, completing f# minor and D major
- (e) m. 46: D# sounded as part of d# minor, completing the neighborhood's tones
- (f) Final cadence: BD# within B major completes dyadic and triadic aggregates
- (g) Subdominant flank events: E outposted at couplet endings, C major at m. 42, e minor at m. 61

# Musical Context
The analysis showcases three virtues of the Tonnetz: it represents triads as assemblies of atomistic pitch-class components (accommodating dyads), it is neutral with respect to tonal centricity, and it facilitates tracking cumulative pitch-class accumulation independent of triadic constituency.

# Examples
- The dramatic wave crests at m. 47 with the completion of F#'s neighborhood
- Subdominant E appears at mm. 12-14, 22-24, 32-33, 42 in progressively fuller triadic contexts
- The gap between C major and the central neighborhood is filled at m. 54 (e minor over dominant pedal) and m. 61 (e minor over tonic pedal, plagal cadence)

# Common Confusions
- The analysis does not adjudicate whether the final harmonies represent minor subdominant to B major tonic or major dominant from e minor tonic; the Tonnetz is neutral on this question
- The neighborhood concept tracks pitch-class accumulation, not tonal function
- The song's dramatic arc maps onto the neighborhood's gradual completion
