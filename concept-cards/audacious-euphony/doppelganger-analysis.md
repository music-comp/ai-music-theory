# Quick Definition
Schubert's "Der Doppelganger" from *Schwanengesang* exemplifies a neighborhood-based compositional strategy, with the entire song organized around the gradual accumulation and completion of F#'s pitch-class neighborhood at tonal, dyadic, and triadic levels.

# Core Definition
The song presents a neighborhood centered on F#. It opens with four tones (B, A#, D, C#) in dyadic partnership with a drone F#, developing into an ostinato that sometimes substitutes A for A# as a fifth neighborhood tone. "Each dyad is susceptible to triadic completion in two distinct ways, and the song engages all of these possibilities" (p. 131). The dramatic climax at m. 47 introduces D# as root of d# minor, completing F#'s pitch-class neighborhood. The final B major triad "completes the neighborhood at the dyadic and triadic level as well" (p. 131).

# Prerequisites
- **Neighborhood**: The six-triad structure centered on a pitch class
- **Connected Tonnetz**: The representation used for the cumulative analysis

# Key Properties
1. Entire song organized around F# neighborhood
2. Gradual accumulation at three levels: tonal, dyadic, triadic
3. Dramatic climax = neighborhood completion (D# at m. 47)
4. Demonstrates three Tonnetz virtues: atomistic pitch-class representation, tonal neutrality, cumulative tracking

Figure 6.2 presents cumulative Tonnetz snapshots:
- (a) m. 1: F# drone
- (b) m. 4: Four neighborhood tones as dyads
- (c) m. 7: b minor arpeggiated
- (d) mm. 10-11: A sounded, completing f# minor and D major
- (e) m. 46: D# in d# minor completes neighborhood tones
- (f) Final cadence: BD# in B major completes dyadic and triadic aggregates
- (g) Subdominant flank: E at couplet endings, C major at m. 42, e minor at m. 61

# Context & Application
The analysis showcases three virtues of the Tonnetz: (1) atomistic pitch-class components accommodate dyads without forcing triadic interpretation; (2) tonal neutrality avoids arbitrating between competing tonal hearings (e.g., B major tonic vs. e minor tonic at the conclusion); (3) cumulative tracking reveals systematic pitch-class aggregation independent of triadic constituency.

# Examples
- **Tonal neutrality**: The analysis does not adjudicate whether final harmonies represent minor subdominant to B major tonic (Kramer 1986, Kurth 1997) or major dominant from e minor tonic (Kramer 1994, Code 1995, Schwarz 1997) or a liminal blend (Saslaw and Walsh 1996)
- **Subdominant gap**: E is outposted as lone representative from m. 12, gradually integrated via C major (m. 42) and e minor (m. 54, 61)
