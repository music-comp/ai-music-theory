# Quick Definition
Tetrachordal sequential templates are recurring patterns of seventh-chord progressions that use consistent transformation combinations to generate chromatic sequences, including the omnibus/devil's mill and various T11/T5 chains.

# Formal Definition
**Sequential templates** for Tristan-genus chords are recurring patterns that alternate transformations to produce transpositionally organized progressions. Three basic templates (Figure 7.8):

1. **T3 sequence (omnibus/devil's mill)**: V7 chords only, contrary motion, balanced
2. **T11 sequence**: V7 and ø7 alternate, similar motion, downshifting
3. **T5 sequence**: V7 and ø7 alternate, similar motion, downshifting

These templates can be varied through transformation-class substitutions while maintaining voice-leading trajectory.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Template characteristics:

**Figure 7.8(a) - T3 sequence**:
- Only dominant seventh chords
- Two stationary voices, two moving in contrary motion
- Balanced (no net direction)
- Known as "omnibus" or "devil's mill"

**Figure 7.8(b) - T11 sequence**:
- Alternating V7 and ø7
- Two stationary voices, two moving in similar motion
- Net downshift
- Generated by S2 + S3(2)

**Figure 7.8(c) - T5 sequence**:
- Alternating V7 and ø7
- Two stationary voices, two moving in similar motion
- Net downshift
- Generated by S4 + S3(4)

# Musical Context/Application
These templates appear in:
- Eighteenth-century chromaticism (omnibus)
- Chopin's chromatic passages extensively
- Late Romantic seventh-chord sequences
- Any passage with systematic seventh-chord motion

Templates can be:
- Extended for circumnavigation
- Varied through class substitution
- Combined with other progressions
- Accelerated or decelerated

# Examples
**Omnibus/Devil's Mill**: T3 sequence connecting V7 chords by minor third, featuring contrary voice motion. Discussed in Wason 1985, Telesco 1998.

**Chopin's Mazurka Op. 68/4 (mm. 33-40)**: Initially T5 sequence (S2 + S3(4)), then converted to T11 through S4-for-S2 substitution.

**Chopin's e minor Prelude**: The chromatic descent can be understood as varied realizations of T5 template.

**Chopin's Prelude Op. 45**: Extended T11 roulade through S4 + S3(4) alternation.

# Common Confusions
- T3 sequence is balanced (no circumnavigation); T11 and T5 are directed
- Templates describe abstract patterns; specific realizations vary
- Class substitution changes transformations but not voice-leading direction
- Sequential regularity is not required; the templates can be freely realized
