# Quick Definition
Tetrachordal sequential templates are recurring patterns of seventh-chord progressions using consistent transformation combinations, including the T3 omnibus/devil's mill (V7 only, contrary motion), T11 chains (V7/ø7 alternating, similar motion), and T5 chains (V7/ø7 alternating, similar motion).

# Core Definition
Figure 7.8 presents "three sequential templates" for Tristan-genus seventh chords (p. 167). Figure 7.8(a) involves only V7 chords in T3 sequence, arising already in the eighteenth century; "embellished by chromaticized voice exchanges, the progression becomes known as the 'omnibus' or 'devil's mill' (Wason 1985; Telesco 1998)." Figures 7.8(b) and (c), alternating V7 and ø7, "also serve as occasional late-eighteenth century templates and are frequently elaborated in music of Chopin (Tymoczko 2011b, 284-92)." Each features double semitonal motion against two stationary voices, but the T3 template features contrary motion while the others feature similar motion.

- **Tristan genus**: The chord types populating the templates

# Key Properties
1. **T3 (omnibus)**: V7 only, contrary motion, balanced (no circumnavigation)
2. **T11**: V7/ø7 alternating, similar motion, directed downshift; generated by S2 + S3(2)
3. **T5**: V7/ø7 alternating, similar motion, directed downshift; generated by S2 + S3(4)
4. All three feature two stationary voices and two moving by semitone
5. Templates can be varied through transformation-class substitutions

# Construction / Recognition
Each template specifies:
- Which chord species participate (V7 only, or V7/ø7 alternating)
- The transpositional relationship between successive chords
- Which Boretz and octatonic transformations alternate to produce the transposition

Templates can be freely combined, extended, or varied through class substitution while maintaining overall voice-leading trajectory.

# Context & Application
These templates appear in eighteenth-century chromaticism (omnibus), Chopin's chromatic passages extensively, and late Romantic seventh-chord sequences. The omnibus/devil's mill is the oldest; the T11 and T5 templates become prominent in Chopin and post-Tristan repertoire.

# Examples
- **Omnibus/devil's mill (Fig. 7.8a)**: T3 V7 sequence with contrary motion (p. 167)
- **T11 sequence (Fig. 7.8b)**: Generated by S2 + S3(2), as in Chopin Mazurka Op. 68/4 initial phrases (p. 177)
- **T5 sequence (Fig. 7.8c)**: Generated by S2 + S3(4); compare with Chopin e minor Prelude (p. 177)
- **Chopin Prelude Op. 45**: Extended T11 roulade via S4 + S3(4) alternation (Childs 1998) (p. 177)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking the T3 sequence circumnavigates
  **Correction**: T3 produces balanced motion (contrary, oscillating); only T1, T2, T4, T5 produce directed circumnavigation

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming templates must be realized exactly as shown
  **Clarification**: Templates describe abstract patterns; specific realizations vary through class substitution and other modifications
