# Quick Definition
Thematic rotation is an analytical concept describing the periodic cycling of distinct thematic or textural modules through a passage, often coordinating with hypermetric structure and harmonic design.

# Formal Definition
**Thematic rotation** refers to the systematic cycling of thematic/textural modules through a musical passage. In Cohn's analysis of Brahms's Second Symphony, three modules rotate in sequence, establishing both four-bar hypermeter and twelve-bar hypermeasures.

Rotation involves:
1. Distinct thematic/textural modules (labeled A, B, C, etc.)
2. Periodic presentation in a fixed order
3. Coordination with hypermetric structure
4. Disruption or modification as a developmental technique

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
In Brahms's Second Symphony (mm. 246-302):
- **Module A**: Dyadic figure from opening theme, heterophonic, double hemiola
- **Module B**: Metrically malleable violin theme with tremolo accompaniment
- **Module C**: Lyrical, metrically stable, with internal harmonic progressions

Rotation pattern:
- First hypermeasure: A → B → C (normal rotation)
- Second hypermeasure: A → B → C (normal rotation)
- Third hypermeasure: B → C → C (disrupted, A omitted)
- Fourth hypermeasure: A → A → A (dominance of previously missing module)

# Musical Context/Application
Thematic rotation serves analytical purposes:
- Tracking formal organization independent of key
- Understanding how composers create variety within unity
- Observing relationships between thematic and harmonic design
- Identifying disruptions as rhetorical markers

The technique relates to:
- Rotational form in sonata structures (Hepokoski and Darcy)
- Development section organization
- Strophic variation techniques

# Examples
**Brahms's Second Symphony, first movement (mm. 246-302)**: The retransition features three distinct modules rotating through five twelve-bar hypermeasures. The first two hypermeasures maintain normal A → B → C rotation. The third disrupts by omitting A. The fourth is dominated by the full-orchestra presentation of A. The fifth is ambiguously bounded as the recapitulation approaches.

**Dvorak's "New World" Symphony, first movement development**: Four paragraphs structured by hortatory thematic incipits, with the first two dominated by the pastoral third theme and the final two by the principal theme. The themes share a Hungarian choriambic rhythm and relate by contour inversion.

# Common Confusions
- Rotation is not the same as variation; rotation cycles fixed modules, variation transforms a single idea
- Disrupted rotation is itself meaningful, not a failure of the pattern
- Rotation can coordinate with or work against harmonic rhythm
- Different analytical traditions use "rotation" differently (cf. Hepokoski and Darcy's rotational sonata form)
