# Quick Definition
The periodic cycling of distinct thematic or textural modules through a passage, often coordinating with hypermetric structure and harmonic design.

# Core Definition
Thematic rotation refers to the systematic cycling of thematic/textural modules through a musical passage. It involves distinct modules (labeled A, B, C, etc.), periodic presentation in a fixed order, coordination with hypermetric structure, and disruption or modification as a developmental technique. In Cohn's analysis of Brahms's Second Symphony, three modules rotate in sequence, establishing both four-bar hypermeter and twelve-bar hypermeasures.

# Prerequisites
- Basic understanding of thematic analysis and formal structure

# Key Properties
1. Distinct thematic/textural modules cycle in fixed order
2. Periodic presentation coordinated with hypermetric structure
3. Triple rotation establishes twelve-bar hypermeter (3 modules x 4 bars each)
4. Disruption of rotation serves as developmental technique
5. Disrupted rotation is itself meaningful, not a failure of the pattern
6. Can coordinate with or work against harmonic rhythm

# Construction / Recognition
In Brahms's Second Symphony (mm. 246-302):
- **Module A**: Dyadic figure from opening theme, heterophonic, double hemiola
- **Module B**: Metrically malleable violin theme with tremolo accompaniment
- **Module C**: Lyrical, metrically stable, with internal harmonic progressions

Rotation pattern:
- First hypermeasure: A -> B -> C (normal)
- Second hypermeasure: A -> B -> C (normal)
- Third hypermeasure: B -> C -> C (disrupted, A omitted)
- Fourth hypermeasure: A -> A -> A (dominance of previously missing module)
- Fifth hypermeasure: ambiguous boundaries as recapitulation approaches

# Context & Application
Thematic rotation serves analytical purposes: tracking formal organization independent of key, understanding how composers create variety within unity, observing relationships between thematic and harmonic design, and identifying disruptions as rhetorical markers. The technique relates to rotational form in sonata structures (Hepokoski and Darcy) and to development section organization more broadly.

# Examples
- **Brahms, Second Symphony, first movement (mm. 246-302)**: Three modules rotating through five twelve-bar hypermeasures, with progressive disruption and dominance shifts
- **Dvorak, "New World" Symphony, development**: Four paragraphs structured by hortatory thematic incipits; first two dominated by pastoral third theme, final two by principal theme; themes share Hungarian choriambic rhythm and relate by contour inversion

## Contrasts With
- Variation (transforms a single idea rather than cycling distinct modules)
- Hepokoski/Darcy's rotational sonata form (a related but different usage of "rotation")

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Disrupted rotation means the pattern has failed
  **Correction**: Disrupted rotation is itself meaningful and analytically significant

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Rotation is the same as variation
  **Clarification**: Rotation cycles fixed modules; variation transforms a single idea

- **Confusion**: Different analytical traditions use "rotation" identically
  **Clarification**: Hepokoski and Darcy's rotational sonata form is a related but distinct concept
