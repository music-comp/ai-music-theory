# Quick Definition
The principle that the internal properties of consonant triads (their near evenness) generate their syntactic behavior in pan-triadic progressions, just as their acoustic properties (consonance) generate diatonic tonal syntax.

# Core Definition
"One of the most enduring features of classical tonal theory is its capacity to generate syntax from the phonological properties of its constituent objects" (Cohn, p. 57). Cohn extends this principle: "Establishing near evenness as a unique feature of consonant triads places us in a position to see that the structure of triads, as objects, is intimately related to their function, as participants in hexatonic (and, more broadly, pan-triadic) syntax" (p. 58). This responds to Daniel Harrison's critique that transformational theory treats "objects [as] inert and without tendency, and all activity and meaning are supplied by transformations applied to them" (Harrison 2011, 552, cited on p. 57).

# Key Properties
1. For diatonic tonality: acoustic consonance generates fifth-based progressions, tonic function, diatonic collections
2. For pan-triadic harmony: near evenness generates hexatonic cycles, smooth voice leading, chromatic third relations
3. The triad is not a passive object acted upon by external forces; its internal structure determines what transformations are natural
4. Steve Reich's formulation: "Material may suggest what process it should be run through (content suggests form), and processes may suggest what sort of material should be run through them (form suggests content)" (cited on p. 58)

# Construction / Recognition
The parallel:
- Classical: consonance (triadic property) -> diatonic syntax (organizational system)
- Pan-triadic: near evenness (triadic property) -> hexatonic/chromatic syntax (organizational system)

Both demonstrate that the structure of the object generates the syntax in which it participates.

# Context & Application
This principle immunizes the pan-triadic approach from Harrison's critique that transformational theory creates a "ventriloquist's dummy" where objects are "lifeless parts that are made to move by some external force" (p. 57). By showing near evenness as a unique feature of consonant triads, the approach demonstrates that "the structure of triads, as objects, is intimately related to their function" (p. 58).

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 57): Harrison's critique: "Objects are inert and without tendency, and all activity and meaning are supplied by transformations applied to them... a construction of lifeless parts."

**Example 2** (p. 58): Cohn's rebuttal: "that branch of transformational theory that takes consonant triads for its objects, and subjects those objects to transformations that minimize voice leading, is immune from it."

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Claiming that pan-triadic syntax is arbitrary or externally imposed on triads
  **Correction**: It emerges from the triads' internal property of near evenness

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking "structure generates syntax" means triadic structure DETERMINES a unique syntax
  **Clarification**: The triadic structure enables/suggests pan-triadic syntax, just as it enables/suggests diatonic syntax — both are valid
