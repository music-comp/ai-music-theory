# Quick Definition
A departure-return script is a fundamental compositional template where music moves away from a referential point (tonic) and then reverses course to return, typically realized through complementary downshifting and upshifting voice-leading trajectories.

# Formal Definition
The **departure → return** script is a fundamental template of musical form whose classical prototype moves from tonic to dominant and back. When both departure and return realize least-motion voice leading, each upper voice is structured as a palindrome. The sense of departure → return is particularly strong for performers, for whom the complementarity of downshifting and upshifting involves a kinetic aspect.

Once fifth relations lose their prototypical status, the specific case of I → V → I is absorbed into the general case of downshift/upshift complementarity. A departure → return script can be fulfilled by any triadic progression whose voices move in a uniform direction, with return realized by a progression moving through the same voice-leading zones in reverse order.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
The script operates on multiple levels:
1. **Classical prototype**: Tonic → Dominant → Tonic (zones move down then up)
2. **Generalized form**: Any uniform downshift followed by compensatory upshift
3. **Substitutional flexibility**: Same-group transformations and same-sum triads may substitute during return

A return script is fulfilled not only by literal reversal but also by a progression through the same voice-leading zones in reverse order, freely substituting:
- Same-group transformations (e.g., P for L within Hexatonic class)
- Same-sum triads (triads sharing a voice-leading zone)

# Musical Context/Application
The departure-return script provides a pan-triadic alternative to classical tonal frameworks. It allows:
- Coherent large-scale structure without strict diatonic constraints
- Functional coherence through voice-leading logic rather than root relationships
- Integration of highly chromatic passages into comprehensible formal designs

The kinetic aspect (physical sensation of upshift vs. downshift in performance) reinforces the structural relationships.

# Examples
**Schubert's "Auf dem Flusse" (Winterreise)**: The chromatic sections execute departure → return scripts with each departure as a downshift from e minor and each return as an upshift to that tonic (or its zonal affiliate, g# minor). The song uses Weitzmann-class (N) and Hexatonic-class (L, P, H) transformations with substitutional variety: P and H substitute for L at various points.

**Schubert's Bb major Piano Sonata, D. 960, first movement**: A departure → return scheme spans the movement. The exposition is a two-phase descent in voice-leading space (5 → 4 → 2). The nadir at c# minor (zone 1) is reversed when A major restores zone 2. Motion through d minor to Bb major represents compensatory upshift through zones 4 and 5.

**Liszt's Missa pro organo lectarum, Kyrie**: A brief wordless setting where each voice individually constitutes a palindrome, though the staggered firing order means the triadic progression itself is not strictly palindromic. The zonal palindrome creates departure → return through downshifting in the first two phrases and upshifting in the final phrase.

**Schubert's "Liedesend'" (1816)**: The tonal structure forms a palindromic motion through voice-leading zones, connecting c minor (beginning) to f minor (center) via descending major triads, then ascending back to e minor (conclusion).

# Common Confusions
- Departure-return is not limited to tonic-dominant-tonic; any uniform voice-leading trajectory and its reversal qualify
- The return need not be a literal reversal; same-zone substitutions maintain the script's coherence
- Functional interpretations (T-D-T) may be layered onto zonal analysis but are not required
- The script operates at multiple time scales, from phrase-level to movement-level structures
