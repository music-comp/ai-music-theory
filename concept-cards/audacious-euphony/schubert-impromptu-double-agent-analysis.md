# Quick Definition
Analysis of the codas from Schubert's Impromptus Op. 90 Nos. 2 (Eb) and 3 (Gb), showing how double-agent complexes can center on exterior rather than interior triads.

# Core Definition
The two Impromptus demonstrate "the tonal adaptability of the double-agent complex" by centering on exterior triads (p. 95). In No. 2, b minor (exterior) is tonicized through LP transposition across a caesura. In No. 3, Gb major (exterior) serves as tonic, with the complex approached from the opposite side. "The double-agent complex can in principle center around any of its four triadic constituents" (p. 95).

# Prerequisites
- **Double-agent complex**: The structure whose adaptability is demonstrated
- **LP transformation**: The transposition mechanism across the caesura

# Key Properties
1. No. 2 coda: b minor as exterior tonic, LP transposition (F# major -> Bb major) across caesura
2. No. 3 coda: Gb major as exterior tonic, approached from opposite side
3. Double agent Ebb/D appears prominently in No. 2
4. The two Impromptus approach the same complex from complementary directions
5. Fisk (2001) links the two compositions on other grounds

# Construction / Recognition
No. 2: i-V antecedent in b minor answered by V-i in eb minor; LP upward transposition across caesura, then LP downward restoring b minor.
No. 3: Gb major -> cb minor (N) -> Ebb major (R) -> augmented sixth -> Gb; second phrase extends to g minor via D major.

# Context & Application
These analyses complete the demonstration that the double-agent complex can center on any of its four constituent triads.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming the complex must center on interior triads
  **Correction**: Any of the four triads can serve as tonic

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The two Impromptus are unrelated analytically
  **Clarification**: They approach the same chromatic structure from opposite directions; Fisk (2001) links them independently
