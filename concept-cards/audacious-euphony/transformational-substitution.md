# Quick Definition
The technique of substituting T4-related triads within a voice-leading trajectory, or equivalently, substituting transformations from the same group (H or W), to introduce variety or provide shortcuts to tonal goals.

# Formal Definition
Transformational substitution operates on two levels:
1. Triadic: Any triad can be replaced by its T4-related equivalents (same voice-leading zone)
2. Transformational: Any H-group transformation can substitute for another H-group transformation; same for W-group

This maintains voice-leading trajectory while varying the specific triads or transformations. On Cube Dance, substitution continues directional motion but traverses different nodes. On Tonnetz, vectors maintain angle but crooks shift position.

# Construction/Recognition
Substitution principles:
- T4-related triads are zone-equivalent and can substitute freely
- H-group transformations (L, P, H) can substitute for each other
- W-group transformations (R, N, S) can substitute for each other

Effect on sequences:
- L/R chain: N can substitute for R, expediting arrival at tonal goal
- Chain of 24 triads reduced by 8 or 16 stations per substitution
- Voice-leading trajectory (clockwise/counterclockwise) preserved

# Musical Context
Substitutions introduce variety to sequential patterns and provide shortcuts to otherwise remote tonal goals. A composer circumnavigating the triadic universe can break strict sequence while maintaining voice-leading coherence. The technique appears extensively in 19th-century chromatic music.

# Examples
- Brahms German Requiem "Die Erloseten," mm. 261-71: N-for-R substitution in L/R chain saves 8 stations
- Chopin Ballade Op. 23: Two N-for-R substitutions save 16 stations, enabling tritone-related tonal areas
- Bruckner Symphony No. 3 development: N-for-R plus PL creates whole-step modulation
- Chopin Fantasy Op. 49: N interpolated within L/R chain creates temporary retreat

# Common Confusions
- Substitution is not random chord choice but principled zone/group equivalence
- The voice-leading trajectory continues despite substitution
- Cube Dance shows trajectory continuation; Tonnetz shows the jog/crook
