# Quick Definition
The Tristan genus is the family of nearly even tetrachords consisting of dominant seventh and half-diminished seventh chords, which together form a parsimonious voice-leading system analogous to the pan-triadic system of consonant triads.

# Formal Definition
The **Tristan genus** comprises the twenty-four dominant seventh (V7) and half-diminished (ø7) seventh chords that arise as semitonal displacements of the three fully diminished seventh chords. Together, these chords form a voice-leading system with properties analogous to the triadic system:

- **Perfectly even chord**: Diminished seventh (divides octave into four equal parts)
- **Downward displacement**: Dominant seventh chord (one tone displaced down by semitone)
- **Upward displacement**: Half-diminished seventh chord (one tone displaced up by semitone)

The term references Wagner's *Tristan und Isolde*, which "blew open the gates to a compositional field cohabited by V7 and ø7 chords whose interactions were disciplined neither by tonality nor by patterned sequential repetition."

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Nearly even tetrachord structure:
- Diminished seventh: interval content [3,3,3,3] (perfectly even)
- Dominant seventh: interval content [4,3,3,2] (nearly even)
- Half-diminished seventh: interval content [3,3,4,2] (nearly even)

Voice-leading properties:
- Any two Tristan-genus chords sharing a Boretz region are 2 voice-leading units apart
- Movement within a Boretz region: balanced (contrary semitonal motion)
- Movement between Boretz regions: directed (parallel semitonal motion)

Set-class: 4-27, prime form [0258]

# Musical Context/Application
The Tristan genus enables analysis of:
- Post-Tristan chromatic music dominated by seventh chords
- Passages where V7 and ø7 chords connect parsimoniously
- Music defying diatonic/tonal coherence
- Voice-leading logic among dissonances

The genus provides:
- Boretz regions (analogous to Weitzmann regions)
- Octatonic regions (analogous to hexatonic regions)
- 4-Cube Trio (analogous to Cube Dance)

Historical context:
- Derived from Boretz's 1972 analysis of the Tristan Prelude
- Extends Weitzmann's augmented triad approach to tetrachords
- Enables systematic study of "Tristan field" voice-leading

# Examples
**Tristan Prelude opening**: The first five chords are single semitonal displacements of the B-D-F-G# diminished seventh chord, sharing membership in a single Boretz region. Each chord contains three tones of the diminished seventh plus one "spoiler."

**Brunnhilde's Immolation (Gotterdammerung, act 3)**: Opens with alternating V7 and ø7 chords in the Boretz region centered on E-G-A#-C#.

**Chopin's Prelude Op. 45**: Extended downshifting roulade circumnavigates 4-Cube Trio multiple times through Tristan-genus progressions.

**Chopin's Mazurka Op. 68 no. 4**: T11 and T5 chains of seventh chords with class substitutions maintaining downshift trajectory.

# Common Confusions
- "Tristan genus" is Cohn's term, not standard music theory terminology
- The genus includes both V7 and ø7, not the "Tristan chord" alone
- Functional dominant/resolution behavior is suspended in Tristan-genus analysis
- The system is analogous to, but independent of, the triadic system
