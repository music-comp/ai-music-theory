# Quick Definition
The Tristan genus is the family of dominant seventh and half-diminished seventh chords, which together form a parsimonious voice-leading system analogous to the pan-triadic system, arising as semitonal displacements of the three fully diminished seventh chords.

# Core Definition
The **Tristan genus** comprises the twenty-four dominant seventh (V7) and half-diminished (ø7) seventh chords that arise as semitonal displacements of the three fully diminished seventh chords. "Together, these two species of chords... form a parsimonious voice-leading system analogous to the pan-triadic system" (p. 168). The perfectly even diminished seventh chord "may be displaced upward as well as downward, yielding a second species of nearly even tetrachord: the half-diminished as well as dominant seventh chord." The term references Wagner's *Tristan und Isolde*, which "blew open the gates to a compositional field cohabited by V7 and ø7 chords whose interactions were disciplined neither by tonality nor by patterned sequential repetition" (p. 167). Set-class 4-27, prime form [0258]; the inversional equivalence "is a by-product here... what is primary is the relationship of single semitonal displacement that this group of chords holds with respect to the equal division of its cardinality" (p. 168 n. 10).

# Prerequisites
- **Nearly even tetrachord**: The Tristan genus is the specific realization of nearly even tetrachords in 12-tone chromatic space
- **Diminished seventh chord**: The perfectly even tetrachord from which Tristan-genus chords derive
- **Substitution strategy**: The theoretical framework (Boretz's reversal of Rameau) that generates the genus

# Key Properties
1. Twenty-four members: twelve V7 chords (downward displacement) + twelve ø7 chords (upward displacement)
2. Organized into three Boretz regions (eight chords each), three octatonic regions (eight chords each)
3. Any two chords sharing a Boretz region are exactly two voice-leading units apart
4. The minor third has special status because "it divides the octave into as many equal parts as a seventh chord has tones" (p. 167, italicized in original)
5. Cohn explicitly neutralizes the functional agency classical theory bestows on these chords (p. 168)

# Construction / Recognition
Table 7.1 aligns the triadic and tetrachordal cases:
- **Perfectly even chord**: Augmented triad (n=3) / Diminished seventh (n=4)
- **Downward displacement**: Major triad / Dominant seventh
- **Upward displacement**: Minor triad / Half-diminished seventh
- **Union of both species**: Consonant triads / Tristan genus
- **Regional cluster**: Weitzmann region / Boretz region
- **Bridging region**: Hexatonic region / Octatonic region
- **Voice-leading map**: Cube Dance / 4-Cube Trio

# Context & Application
The Tristan genus enables analysis of post-Tristan chromatic music dominated by seventh chords, where V7 and ø7 chords connect parsimoniously in ways defying diatonic/tonal coherence. Succeeding generations "romped on this Tristan field for a half-century and more, using semitonal voice leading to connect ø7 chords directly to each other, and to V7 chords, in a manner that defied diatonic/tonal coherence more frequently than not" (p. 167). The exposition "develops the system of nearly even tetrachords by analogy with the trichordal case, up to the limits of that analogy's productive power" (p. 168).

# Examples
- **Tristan Prelude opening**: First five chords are single semitonal displacements of B-D-F-G# diminished seventh, sharing membership in a single Boretz region (pp. 167-168)
- **Three sequential templates (Figure 7.8)**: T3 (omnibus/devil's mill, V7 only), T11 (alternating V7 and ø7), T5 (alternating V7 and ø7) (p. 167)
- **Brunnhilde's Immolation**: Alternating V7 and ø7 in the E-G-A#-C# Boretz region (pp. 173-174)
- **Chopin's Prelude Op. 45**: Extended downshifting roulade circumnavigating 4-Cube Trio (p. 177)
- **Chopin's Mazurka Op. 68 no. 4**: T5 and T11 chains with transformation-class substitutions (pp. 177-178)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Identifying the "Tristan genus" with the "Tristan chord" alone
  **Correction**: The genus includes all 24 V7 and ø7 chords, not just the specific chord from the Tristan Prelude opening

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking functional dominant/resolution behavior is part of Tristan-genus theory
  **Clarification**: Cohn explicitly neutralizes appellative properties: "In using traditional names for seventh chords, I do not wish to take on board the functional agency that classical theory bestows on them" (p. 168)
- **Confusion**: Assuming the triadic and tetrachordal voice-leading zones communicate
  **Clarification**: Some zone labels are identical but are "false friends": the triadic and tetrachordal systems are not in communication (p. 172 n. 13)
