# Quick Definition
Lewin's directional transformations representing dominant and subdominant function: DOM (T5) indicates a chord "becomes the dominant of" its target; SUBD (T7) indicates "becomes the subdominant of" its target. Unlike L, P, R, these are not their own inverses.

# Core Definition
David Lewin (1982, 1987) defined DOM as equivalent to T5: the source chord "becomes the dominant of" the target chord. SUBD, its inverse, is equivalent to T7 = T-5: the source chord "becomes the subdominant of" the target chord (Cohn, p. 188). These transformations capture not only syntactic flow but also the "semantic charge" associated with classical tonality -- "roughly stand[ing] in for what musicians elsewhere capture through such terms as summoning, leading, gravity, magnetism, attraction, desire, and so forth" (p. 188).

# Prerequisites
- **Pan-triadic syntax**: Must understand the alternative system these transformations contrast with
- **L, P, R transformations**: Need to understand involutory transformations to grasp how DOM/SUBD differ

# Key Properties
1. DOM is equivalent to T5 (root descends by fifth / ascends by fourth)
2. SUBD is equivalent to T7 = T-5 (root ascends by fifth)
3. Single-headed arrows: not involutions (applying twice yields T10, not identity)
4. Mode-preserving: G major DOM yields C major, but G major is not the dominant of C minor per Lewin's definition
5. Carry semantic charge beyond mere transposition

# Construction / Recognition
- DOM: C major DOM yields F major (C major "becomes dominant of" F major)
- SUBD: C major SUBD yields G major (C major "becomes subdominant of" G major)
- In Rings's model, these label the vertical (tonal-functional) axis connecting triads by fifth

# Context & Application
Rings (2007) placed DOM and SUBD along the vertical axis of his model of the Schubert Eb Impromptu, where the dimensions "mark out a unified space in which we can map progressions that exploit both the triad's tonal-gravitational properties and its triadic-transformational potential, without privileging one at the expense of the other" (Rings 2007, 52, qtd. in Cohn, p. 188).

A limitation: Rings arrows implicitly commit to tonal-gravitational forces whenever motion occurs along the fifths axis, excluding the possibility that forces are slackened during sequences (p. 188).

# Examples

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating DOM as simply meaning "dominant chord"
  **Correction**: DOM is a transformation indicating the source becomes dominant of the target

- **Error**: Assuming G major is DOM of C minor
  **Correction**: DOM preserves mode; these transformations apply between modally matched triads

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: DOM/SUBD are equivalent to L, P, R in a different guise
  **Clarification**: They are fundamentally different -- directional (not involutory), mode-preserving, and semantically charged
