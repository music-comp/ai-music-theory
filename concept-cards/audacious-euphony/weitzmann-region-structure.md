# Quick Definition
The internal organization of a Weitzmann region as a flat, uniform space where all triadic pairs are exactly two voice-leading units apart, contrasting with the graded structure of hexatonic cycles.

# Core Definition
A Weitzmann region is "a flat, uniform space: all of its triadic pairs stand exactly two voice-leading units apart" (p. 78). This distinguishes it from hexatonic regions where "triadic distance correlates with voice-leading work" in a graded cycle. The five voice-leading connections from any triad to the other five in its region (Figure 4.2) all pass through the augmented triad at the center: "The first stage of each route is the same: G -> G#/Ab converts C major to C augmented. What distinguishes them from each other is what happens at the second stage: which tone moves by semitone, and in which direction" (p. 79).

# Prerequisites
- **Weitzmann region**: Must understand the six-triad composition of a region
- **Voice-leading work**: Must understand semitonal measurement to grasp the "flat terrain" property

# Key Properties
1. All triadic pairs within a region require exactly 2 units of voice-leading work
2. No cyclic ordering is more natural than others from the standpoint of voice leading
3. All intra-regional progressions route conceptually through the augmented triad
4. Same-mode connections (major to major via LP/PL) use balanced contrary motion
5. Cross-modal connections shift the center of balance by two units
6. The R connection appears shorter on the Tonnetz but requires the same 2 units as N and S

# Construction / Recognition
Voice-leading from C major to five other triads in its region (Figure 4.2):
- (a) C major -> E major (LP): G->G#, then C->B (2 units, contrary)
- (b) C major -> Ab major (PL): G->G#, then E->Eb (2 units, contrary)
- (c) C major -> a minor (R): G->A (whole step, 2 units)
- (d) C major -> f minor (N): G->Ab and E->F (2 semitones, contrary)
- (e) C major -> c# minor (S): G->G# and C->C# (2 semitones, parallel)

# Context & Application
The flat-terrain structure means composers have complete freedom in ordering regional triads. Unlike hexatonic cycles where adjacent triads connect most parsimoniously, Weitzmann regions permit any path with equal voice-leading cost. This explains why some passages are sequential (N/R cycles) while others are nonsequential (Parsifal, "Fruhling").

# Examples
**Water bug metaphor** (Figure 4.1a, p. 78): "A water bug with augmented-triad body and six consonant-triad feet... All journeys from one foot to another are routed through the body."

**Hallway metaphor** (Figure 4.1b, p. 78): "An oval hallway with three rooms on each side... A firefighter would likely execute each [journey] with a single kinetic impulse... A professor might experience the hallway as a marked intermediate point... A retiree might be inclined to linger in the hallway for a while."

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating R as closer than N or S within a Weitzmann region
  **Correction**: "R is a more distant relation than L and P, just as surely as a whole step is larger than a semitone, and is neither closer nor farther than the other four relations internal to a Weitzmann region" (p. 83)

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The N/R cycle reveals a natural ordering of the region
  **Clarification**: The N/R cycle is based on historical syntactic convention, not voice-leading proximity
- **Confusion**: All progressions literally pass through an augmented triad chord
  **Clarification**: The routing is conceptual; the augmented triad may not sound as a chord
