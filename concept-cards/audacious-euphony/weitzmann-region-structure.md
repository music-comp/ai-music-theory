# Quick Definition
The internal organization of a Weitzmann region as a flat, uniform space where all triadic pairs are exactly two voice-leading units apart, unlike the graded hexatonic cycle.

# Formal Definition
A Weitzmann region is structurally a "flat terrain" rather than a graded cycle. All pairs of triads within a region require exactly two units of voice-leading work. The triads can be cyclically ordered in several ways (via N/R alternation), but no ordering is more natural than others from voice-leading perspective. The augmented triad serves as a central hub routing all progressions.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Voice-leading connections within a Weitzmann region:
- Major to major (e.g., C to E): 2 units, via augmented triad (LP or PL)
- Major to relative minor (e.g., C to a): 2 units, one voice moves whole step (R)
- Major to nebenverwandt minor (e.g., C to f): 2 units, two voices move by semitone in contrary motion (N)
- Major to slide-related minor (e.g., C to c#): 2 units, two voices move by semitone (S)
- All cross-modal progressions shift the center of balance by two units

# Musical Context/Application
The flat structure contrasts with hexatonic regions where distance correlates with voice-leading work. In a Weitzmann region, the augmented triad functions like a hallway connecting rooms, or the body of a "water bug" with six consonant triad "feet." Composers may treat the augmented triad as a marked waypoint, a transient passage, or an imperceptible bridge.

# Common Confusions
- The structure is flat (equidistant pairs), not graded like hexatonic cycles
- All five progressions from any triad to the other five regional triads pass through the augmented triad conceptually
- R transformation appears shorter on Tonnetz but requires same voice-leading work as N and S
