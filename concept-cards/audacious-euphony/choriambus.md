# Quick Definition
The choriambus (or Hungarian choriambus) is a distinctive long-short-short-long rhythmic pattern that Cohn identifies as linking thematic materials in Dvorak's "New World" Symphony development.

# Core Definition
The choriambus is a rhythmic pattern consisting of long-short-short-long durations (in classical prosody: stressed-unstressed-unstressed-stressed). The term derives from Greek/Latin poetic meter. In Cohn's analysis of Dvorak's "New World" Symphony development, the choriambus links the pastoral third theme to the principal theme. These themes share the long-short-short-long rhythmic pattern and additionally relate by contour inversion, creating both rhythmic and melodic connections across the ninety-six-measure development.

# Prerequisites
- Basic understanding of rhythmic patterns and prosody

# Key Properties
1. Pattern: LONG - short - short - LONG
2. Duration ratio: approximately 2:1:1:2
3. Creates strong-weak-weak-strong accent pattern
4. Enables thematic connection across different melodic contexts
5. Associated with Hungarian musical idioms
6. Functions as a unifying rhythmic motive independent of pitch content

# Construction / Recognition
Choriambic rhythm:
- Pattern: LONG - short - short - LONG
- Metric notation: quarter-eighth-eighth-quarter or equivalents
- Recognizable across different melodic contexts
- Creates characteristic "rocking" or "galloping" feel

# Context & Application
In the "New World" Symphony analysis, the choriambus structures the development into four paragraphs. The "hortatory thematic incipits" share the choriambic rhythm. The first two paragraphs are dominated by the pastoral third theme, the final two by the principal theme. The choriambus provides coherence across the ninety-six-measure span, linking materials that differ in melody but share rhythmic identity and relate by contour inversion.

# Examples
- **Dvorak, "New World" Symphony, first movement development**: Hortatory thematic incipits share the choriambic rhythm; pastoral third theme and principal theme both exhibit long-short-short-long pattern, relate by contour inversion
- **Four developmental paragraphs**: first two dominated by third theme, final two by principal theme, all launched from hypermetric positions with choriambic incipits

# Common Errors
- **Error**: The choriambus is a melodic pattern
  **Correction**: It is a rhythmic pattern; different melodies can share it

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: "Hungarian choriambus" implies Hungarian origin of the pattern
  **Clarification**: The term derives from classical prosody; "Hungarian" refers to its association with Hungarian musical styles

- **Confusion**: Rhythmic identity alone cannot create thematic connection
  **Clarification**: Shared rhythmic pattern can unify thematically contrasting materials, especially when reinforced by other relationships (like contour inversion)
