# Quick Definition
A collection of chords whose pitch classes sum to a constant value; consonant triads share a voice-leading zone if transpositionally related by major third, while Tristan-genus chords share a zone if related by minor third or tritone.

# Formal Definition
Voice-leading zone is defined in the Glossary as "a collection of chords whose pitch classes sum to a constant value. Consonant triads share a voice-leading zone if they are transpositionally related by major third, in which case they share membership in both a hexatonic region and a Weitzmann region. Tristan-genus chords share a voice-leading zone if they are transpositionally related by minor third or tritone, in which case they share membership in both an octatonic region and a Boretz region."

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
For consonant triads (12 zones, 2 triads each):
- Zone determined by pitch-class sum mod 12
- Triads related by T4 or T8 share a zone
- Example: C major (0+4+7=11) and E major (4+8+11=23=11 mod 12) share zone 11
- Co-zonal triads share both hexatonic and Weitzmann region membership

For Tristan-genus chords (4 zones, 6 chords each):
- Zone determined by pitch-class sum mod 4
- Chords related by T3, T6, or T9 share a zone
- Co-zonal chords share both octatonic and Boretz region membership

Voice-leading zones measure "height" in voice-leading space; motion between zones corresponds to net upshift or downshift.

# Musical Context/Application
Voice-leading zones provide an alternative to functional analysis for tracking harmonic motion:
- Upshift voice leading moves to higher zone numbers
- Downshift voice leading moves to lower zone numbers
- Balanced voice leading stays within a zone
- Zone relationships capture voice-leading direction independent of root motion

The zones explain the "diametric" relationships in Cohn's figures: triads at opposite ends of hexatonic cycles share a zone despite maximal transformational distance.

# Examples
- C major and Ab major share a zone (both sum to 11 mod 12)
- E major and c minor share a zone (hexatonic poles)
- The four augmented triads each occupy their own zone
- Dominant seventh chords related by tritone share a zone

# Common Confusions
- Zone membership is about pitch-class sum, not transformational relationship
- Triads in the same zone may be transformationally distant (hexatonic poles)
- Zones measure "height" in an abstract sense, not register
- Voice-leading work and voice-leading zone are different concepts
