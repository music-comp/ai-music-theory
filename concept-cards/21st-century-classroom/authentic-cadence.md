# Quick Definition
An authentic cadence is a phrase ending with V resolving to I, creating the strongest sense of harmonic finality.

# Formal Definition
An authentic cadence (AC) occurs when a phrase ends with the dominant chord (V or V7) resolving to the tonic chord (I). This is the strongest and most conclusive cadence type. It can be further classified as Perfect Authentic Cadence (PAC) when both chords are in root position and ^1 is in the soprano, or Imperfect Authentic Cadence (IAC) when these conditions are not met.

# Construction/Recognition
- V - I (or V7 - I) at phrase end
- PAC: root position V to root position I, ^1 in soprano
- IAC: inverted chords or ^3/^5 in soprano
- Creates strong sense of closure
- Most common final cadence in tonal music

# Musical Context
Authentic cadences are the "gold standard" of harmonic resolution. They end most phrases and pieces in tonal music. The PAC creates the strongest possible ending; IAC creates a slightly less conclusive effect while still providing resolution.

# Examples
- "Star-Spangled Banner" ends with V-I (authentic cadence)
- "I Want to Hold Your Hand" (Beatles) uses authentic cadences
- G-C in C major = V-I = authentic cadence
- D7-G in G major = V7-I = authentic cadence

# Common Confusions
- PAC vs. IAC: PAC requires root position AND ^1 in soprano
- V7-I is still authentic (the seventh doesn't change the cadence type)
- In minor: V-i is authentic (major V to minor i)
