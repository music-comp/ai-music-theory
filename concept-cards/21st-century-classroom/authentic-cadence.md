# Quick Definition
An authentic cadence (AC) is a phrase ending with V resolving to I, creating the strongest harmonic closure; it subdivides into Perfect Authentic (PAC, with root-position chords and ^1 in soprano) and Imperfect Authentic (IAC).

# Core Definition
An authentic cadence (AC) occurs when a phrase ends with the chords V-I (or V7-I). This is the strongest and most conclusive cadence type. It can be further classified as a Perfect Authentic Cadence (PAC) when both chords are in root position and ^1 is in the soprano of the tonic chord, or an Imperfect Authentic Cadence (IAC) when these conditions are not met (inverted chords or ^3/^5 in soprano). In minor, V-i is authentic (major V to minor i, with raised ^7).

# Prerequisites
- **Cadence**: The general concept of harmonic arrival
- **Leading tone**: The ^7 in V that resolves to ^1

# Key Properties
1. V-I (or V7-I) at a phrase ending
2. Strongest and most conclusive cadence type
3. PAC requirements: both chords root position, ^1 in soprano of I
4. IAC: inverted chords, or ^3/^5 in soprano of I
5. V7-I is still authentic (the seventh does not change the cadence type)
6. In minor: V-i is authentic (major V resolves to minor i)

# Construction / Recognition
- Last two chords of phrase: V (or V7) followed by I (or i)
- Check if PAC: root position V, root position I, ^1 in soprano
- If any PAC condition is not met: IAC
- PAC creates strongest possible ending; IAC slightly less conclusive

# Context & Application
Authentic cadences are the "gold standard" of harmonic resolution. They end most phrases and pieces in tonal music. The PAC creates the strongest possible ending; IAC creates a slightly less conclusive effect while still providing resolution.

# Examples
- "Star-Spangled Banner" ends with V-I (authentic cadence) -- Francis Scott Key / John Stafford Smith
- "I Want to Hold Your Hand" (Beatles, Lennon-McCartney) uses authentic cadences
- G-C in C major = V-I = authentic cadence
- D7-G in G major = V7-I = authentic cadence
- In minor: E-Am in A minor = V-i = authentic cadence

## Contrasts With
- Half cadence (no resolution; HC ends ON V)
- Deceptive cadence (V goes to vi instead of I)
- Plagal cadence (IV-I instead of V-I)

# Common Errors
- Confusing PAC and IAC conditions (PAC requires root position AND ^1 in soprano)
- Assuming V7 changes the cadence type (it is still authentic)

# Common Confusions
- **PAC vs. IAC**: PAC requires BOTH root-position chords AND ^1 in the soprano
- **V7 is still authentic**: Adding the seventh to V does not change the cadence type
- **In minor: V is major**: V-i in minor is authentic (major V, using raised ^7, resolves to minor i)
