# Quick Definition
Perfect intervals (unison, 4th, 5th, octave) are natural-to-natural, sharp-to-sharp, and flat-to-flat, except for B-F which requires B-F# or Bb-F for perfection.

# Core Definition
Perfect intervals (unison, 4th, 5th, octave) can be perfect, augmented, or diminished but never major or minor. Perfect intervals are always natural to natural, sharp to sharp, and flat to flat except for the fourths and fifths between B and F, which involve B to F# and Bb to F. The perfect fifth is the basis for the circle of fifths.

# Prerequisites
- **Interval quality**: Understanding the perfect vs. major/minor distinction

# Key Properties
1. P1 (unison) = 0 half steps
2. P4 = 5 half steps
3. P5 = 7 half steps
4. P8 (octave) = 12 half steps
5. Natural-to-natural, sharp-to-sharp, flat-to-flat rule (except B-F)
6. Exception: B to F is a tritone (o5); B to F# = P5; Bb to F = P5

# Construction / Recognition
- Check if both notes have matching accidentals (both natural, both sharp, both flat)
- Exception: B-F pair always needs adjustment (B-F# or Bb-F for P5)
- Count half steps: P4=5, P5=7
- Use the major scale method: if upper note is in the lower note's major scale, fourths and fifths are perfect

# Context & Application
Perfect intervals are highly consonant and stable. The perfect fifth is the basis for most Western harmony and the circle of fifths. The perfect octave is the most consonant interval after the unison. Perfect consonances were the only intervals allowed in early polyphony.

# Examples
- C to C = P1 (unison, 0 half steps)
- C to F = P4 (5 half steps)
- C to G = P5 (7 half steps)
- C to C' = P8 (octave, 12 half steps)
- B to F# = P5; B to F = o5 (tritone, 6 half steps)

# Common Errors
- Assuming B to F is a perfect fifth (it is a tritone/diminished 5th)
- Calling a perfect interval "major" or "minor"

# Common Confusions
- **B-F exception**: B to F is NOT perfect; it requires adjustment (B-F# or Bb-F)
- **Never major/minor**: Perfect intervals cannot be major or minor
- **"Perfect" is a quality**: It does not mean "best" or "ideal"; it describes a specific interval size
