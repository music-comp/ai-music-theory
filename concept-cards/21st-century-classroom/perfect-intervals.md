# Quick Definition
Perfect intervals are the unison, fourth, fifth, and octave--intervals that can be perfect, augmented, or diminished, but never major or minor.

# Formal Definition
Perfect intervals (unison, 4th, 5th, octave) belong to a category that can only be perfect (P), augmented (+), or diminished (o) in quality--never major or minor. They are called "perfect" because of their acoustic purity and historical importance. Perfect intervals are natural-to-natural, sharp-to-sharp, and flat-to-flat, except for B-F, which requires B-F# or Bb-F for perfect.

# Construction/Recognition
- P1 (unison) = 0 half steps
- P4 = 5 half steps
- P5 = 7 half steps
- P8 (octave) = 12 half steps
- Perfect intervals: natural-natural, sharp-sharp, flat-flat (except B-F)
- Exception: B to F is a tritone; B to F# or Bb to F is P5

# Musical Context
Perfect intervals are highly consonant and stable. The perfect fifth is the basis for most Western harmony and the circle of fifths. The perfect octave is the most consonant interval after the unison.

# Examples
- C to C = P1 (unison)
- C to F = P4 (5 half steps)
- C to G = P5 (7 half steps)
- C to C' = P8 (octave, 12 half steps)
- B to F# = P5; B to F = diminished 5th (tritone)

# Common Confusions
- Perfect intervals CANNOT be major or minor
- B to F is NOT a perfect fifth (it is a tritone/diminished 5th)
- "Perfect" doesn't mean "best"--it describes a specific quality category
