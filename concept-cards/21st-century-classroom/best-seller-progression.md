# Quick Definition
The I-V-vi-IV progression, nicknamed the "best-seller progression," is one of the most common chord progressions in contemporary popular music, appearing in countless hit songs across decades.

# Core Definition
The I-V-vi-IV progression consists of tonic, dominant, submediant, and subdominant chords in that order. It can be rotated to vi-IV-I-V or IV-I-V-vi. When starting on vi, the progression can also be analyzed in the relative minor as i-VI-III-VII. Whether you hear it in major or minor depends on whether you hear the first chord as the tonic or the third and fourth chords as tonic and dominant.

# Prerequisites
- **Harmonic function**: Understanding of chord roles in a key

# Key Properties
1. Four chords: I-V-vi-IV
2. Rotations: vi-IV-I-V and IV-I-V-vi
3. Can be analyzed in relative minor as i-VI-III-VII
4. Has a Wikipedia page documenting songs that use it
5. Often loops continuously
6. Frequently paired with (3+3+2)+(3+3+2) rhythmic pattern

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the four-chord loop in any rotation
2. Determine whether to analyze in major or minor based on perceived tonic
3. The progression often loops continuously through entire sections
4. Listen for the characteristic shift from major (I-V) to relative minor feeling (vi-IV)

# Context & Application
This progression has become ubiquitous in 21st-century popular music. It has its own Wikipedia page, was mentioned in a New Yorker article, and the comedy group Axis of Awesome created a viral compilation of songs using it. It works in multiple rotations, giving it great versatility.

# Examples
- Adele, "Someone Like You" (2011) -- I-V-vi-IV
- Adele, "Hello" (2015) -- vi-IV-I-V rotation
- Rihanna, "Umbrella" (2007) -- IV-I-V-vi rotation
- "Despacito" (2017) -- with reggaeton beat

# Common Errors
- Confusing I-V-vi-IV with I-vi-IV-V (different order)
- Not recognizing rotations as the same progression

# Common Confusions
- **Major or minor?**: vi-IV-I-V can be analyzed as i-VI-III-VII in the relative minor
- **Hearing determines analysis**: Whether it sounds like major or minor depends on perceived tonic
- **Different from 1950s**: The chord order differs -- compare I-V-vi-IV with I-vi-IV-V
