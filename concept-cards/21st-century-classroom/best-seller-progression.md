# Quick Definition
The I-V-vi-IV progression, nicknamed the "best-seller progression," is one of the most common chord progressions in contemporary popular music, appearing in countless hit songs.

# Formal Definition
The I-V-vi-IV progression consists of tonic, dominant, submediant, and subdominant chords in that order. It can be rotated to vi-IV-I-V or IV-I-V-vi. When starting on vi, the progression can also be analyzed in the relative minor as i-VI-III-VII.

# Analysis/Application
When analyzing:
1. Identify the progression in any of its rotations
2. Determine whether to analyze in major or minor based on what sounds like tonic
3. The progression often loops continuously
4. Note the (3+3+2)+(3+3+2) rhythm frequently used with this progression

# Musical Context
This progression has become ubiquitous in 21st-century popular music. It has a Wikipedia page documenting songs that use it and was mentioned in The New Yorker. The comedy group Axis of Awesome created a viral compilation of songs using this progression.

# Examples
- Adele, "Someone Like You" (2011) - I-V-vi-IV
- Adele, "Hello" (2015) - vi-IV-I-V rotation
- Rihanna, "Umbrella" (2007) - IV-I-V-vi rotation
- Can be heard in major or relative minor (vi-IV-I-V = i-VI-III-VII)

# Related Concepts
- 1950s progression (I-vi-IV-V)
- Progression rotations
- i-bVII-bVI-bVII progression (similar in minor)
- Harmonic looping

# Common Confusions
- The vi-IV-I-V version can be analyzed as i-VI-III-VII in minor
- Whether you hear it in major or minor depends on which chord sounds like tonic
- Different from the 1950s progression (different chord order)
