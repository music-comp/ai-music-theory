# Quick Definition
A fragment (sometimes called a "germ") is a portion of a compound motive that can be extracted and developed independently.

# Formal Definition
While the motive is usually defined as the smallest identifiable melodic idea in a composition, "compound" motives can be broken into fragments. These smaller components can each be developed independently through techniques like sequence, inversion, and augmentation.

# Analysis/Application
When analyzing fragments:
1. Identify the larger compound motive
2. Determine how it can be divided into smaller units
3. Look for independent development of each fragment
4. Label fragments with lowercase letters (a, b, etc.)

# Musical Context
Fragmenting motives allows composers to develop material economically, extracting multiple ideas from a single thematic source. This technique is central to Classical and Romantic development sections.

# Examples
- J.S. Bach, Invention No. 1 in C Major - 7-note compound motive divided into two overlapping four-note fragments (a and b)
- Fragment "a" is developed in augmentation
- Fragment "b" is developed in inversion leading to a cadence in G major

# Common Confusions
- Fragments are parts of a larger compound motive
- Fragments can overlap (share common notes)
- Each fragment can be developed using different techniques
