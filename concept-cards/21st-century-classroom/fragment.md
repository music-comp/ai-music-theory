# Quick Definition
A fragment (sometimes called a "germ") is a portion of a compound motive that can be extracted and developed independently.

# Core Definition
While the motive is usually defined as the smallest identifiable melodic idea in a composition, "compound" motives can be broken into fragments (sometimes called "germs"). These smaller components can each be developed independently through techniques like sequence, inversion, and augmentation. Fragments can overlap, sharing common notes.

# Prerequisites
- **Motive**: Understanding of the melodic building block that fragments come from

# Key Properties
1. Smaller than a motive; part of a compound motive
2. Can be developed independently
3. Fragments can overlap (share common notes)
4. Labeled with lowercase letters (a, b, etc.)
5. Each fragment can be developed using different techniques

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the larger compound motive
2. Determine how it can be divided into smaller units
3. Look for independent development of each fragment elsewhere in the piece
4. Label fragments with lowercase letters (a, b, etc.)
5. Note any overlapping notes between fragments

# Context & Application
Fragmenting motives allows composers to develop material economically, extracting multiple ideas from a single thematic source. This technique is central to Classical and Romantic development sections and to Baroque inventions.

# Examples
- J.S. Bach, Invention No. 1 in C Major -- 7-note compound motive divided into two overlapping four-note fragments (a and b)
- Fragment "a" developed in augmentation
- Fragment "b" developed in inversion leading to a cadence in G major

# Common Errors
- Not recognizing fragments when they are transposed or inverted
- Missing overlapping fragments

# Common Confusions
- **Fragments are parts of motives**: The motive is the whole; fragments are its components
- **Fragments can overlap**: Two fragments may share common notes
- **Independent development**: Each fragment can be developed using different alteration techniques
