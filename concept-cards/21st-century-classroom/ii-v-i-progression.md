# Quick Definition
The ii-V-I is the most fundamental chord progression in jazz, consisting of a minor seventh on the second degree, a dominant seventh on the fifth, resolving to the tonic.

# Core Definition
The ii-V-I is one of the most common progressions in jazz. In major, the typical voicing is Dmin9-G9(13)-CΔ9. In minor, it is Dmin7(b5)-G7alt-Cm6/9. This progression combines subdominant, dominant, and tonic functions and appears in countless jazz standards (Hutchinson, Ch. 31, p. 454).

# Prerequisites
- **Jazz ninth chords** -- ii-V-I uses ninth chords and extensions
- **Altered dominant seventh chords** -- The V chord in minor is typically altered
- **Guide tones** -- Guide tones move by step through the progression

# Key Properties
1. Three-chord sequence: ii-V-I
2. Major: ii = minor 9th, V = dominant 9th/13th (possibly altered), I = major 9th or 6/9
3. Minor: ii = half-diminished 7th, V = altered dominant, i = minor 6/9 or minor-major 7th
4. Guide tones (3rds and 7ths) move by step throughout
5. Ubiquitous in jazz standards

# Construction / Recognition
**Major ii-V-I (in C):**
- ii: Dm9 (or Dm7)
- V: G9(13) or G7 with alterations
- I: CΔ9 (or CΔ7 or C6/9)

**Minor ii-V-i (in C minor):**
- ii: Dm7(b5) (half-diminished)
- V: G7alt (altered dominant)
- i: Cm6/9 (or CmΔ7)

# Context & Application
The ii-V-I is ubiquitous in jazz standards including "Autumn Leaves," "What Is This Thing Called Love," "Tune-Up," "Pent Up House," "Lady Bird," and "Firm Roots." Many jazz compositions are chains of ii-V-I progressions in various keys. Mastering this progression is essential for jazz improvisation and comping.

# Examples
- C major: Dm9-G9(13)-CΔ9 (p. 454, Figure 31.8.1)
- C minor: Dm7(b5)-G7alt-Cm6/9 (p. 455, Figure 31.8.2)
- Voice leading is very smooth, with all voices moving by step or by 3rd (p. 456)

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Circle of fifths progression** -- ii-V-I is a three-chord circle-of-fifths motion
## Related
- **Turnaround (iii-vi-ii-V)** -- Extends ii-V-I with preceding chords
- **Tritone substitution** -- bII7 can substitute for V7

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Using a major II chord instead of minor ii
  **Correction**: The ii chord is minor in both major and minor keys

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the V chord in minor is unaltered
  **Clarification**: In minor, the V chord is typically altered (G7alt), not a plain G7
- **Confusion**: Not recognizing ii-V-I in various keys
  **Clarification**: ii-V-I can occur in any key and is used to identify key centers in analysis
