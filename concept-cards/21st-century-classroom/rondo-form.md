# Quick Definition
Rondo form features a recurring refrain (A) that alternates with contrasting episodes (B, C), typically in patterns like ABACA (5-part) or ABACABA (7-part).

# Formal Definition
A rondo is a piece that begins with a refrain (an A section) that alternates with episodes (B and C). The 5-part rondo has ABACA or ABABA form. The 7-part rondo typically has ABACABA form, although other designs exist. The refrain is always in tonic; episodes are in contrasting keys.

# Construction/Recognition
5-part rondo: A B A C A (or A B A B A)
7-part rondo: A B A C A B A

Tonal plan for 7-part Classical rondo:
| A | B | retrans | A | trans | C | A | trans | B | retrans | A |
| I | V | | I | | vi,IV,i | | I | | I | | I |

(In minor: i, III, VI/iv, etc.)

Key characteristics:
- A (refrain): always in TONIC
- B (first episode): V (major) or III (minor)
- C (second episode): variety of keys (vi, IV, i, etc.)
- Retransition: prepares return of refrain

# Musical Context
- Common finale form in Classical era
- Associated with light character and quick tempo
- B section in V, then in I = sonata principle
- Often has "rondo character" (staccato, duple meter)

# Common Confusions
- The refrain is ALWAYS in tonic
- Episodes provide tonal contrast
- 7-part rondo often applies sonata principle (B in V, then in I)
- Not all rondos have "rondo character"
