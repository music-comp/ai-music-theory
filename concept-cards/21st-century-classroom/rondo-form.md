# Quick Definition

Rondo form features a recurring refrain (A) that alternates with contrasting episodes (B, C), typically in patterns like ABACA (5-part) or ABACABA (7-part).

# Core Definition

A rondo is a piece that begins with a refrain (an A section) that alternates with episodes (B and C). The 5-part rondo has ABACA or ABABA form. The 7-part rondo typically has ABACABA form, although other designs exist. The refrain is always in tonic; episodes are in contrasting keys. Retransitions (transitions returning to previously heard material) connect sections (Hutchinson, Ch. 25, p. 375).

# Prerequisites

- **Modulation** -- Episodes are in contrasting keys
- **Cadence** -- Cadences demarcate sections

# Key Properties

1. Refrain (A): always in TONIC
2. Episodes (B, C): in contrasting keys
3. 5-part: ABACA or ABABA
4. 7-part: ABACABA (most common)
5. B first in V (major) or III (minor); later returns in I (sonata principle)
6. C in various keys (i, vi, IV, etc.)
7. Retransitions connect episodes back to refrain

# Construction / Recognition

## 7-Part Rondo Diagram:
| Major: | A (I) | B (V) | retrans | A (I) | trans | C (i, vi, or IV) | A (I) | trans | B (I) | retrans | A (I) |
| Minor: | A (i) | B (III) | retrans | A (i) | trans | C (I, VI, or iv) | A (i) | trans | B (i) | retrans | A (i) |

## To Identify Rondo:
1. Listen for a recurring theme that returns in the same key
2. Identify contrasting episodes between statements of the refrain
3. Count the sections to determine 5-part or 7-part

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: Classical era final movements of sonatas, quartets, symphonies
- **Common applications**: Associated with light character and quick tempo (rondo character)
- **Historical/stylistic notes**: B section returning in tonic is an application of sonata principle; this principle occurs in many Classical-era pieces (see Rosen)

# Examples

**Example 1** (pp. 376-380): Beethoven, Pathetique Sonata, Op. 13, III (1798) -- full 7-part rondo in C minor:
- A: Refrain in C minor
- B: First episode in Eb major (III, relative major)
- A: Refrain in C minor
- C: Second episode in Ab major (VI, submediant)
- A: Abbreviated refrain
- B: Episode returns in C major (tonic major -- sonata principle)
- A: Final refrain in C minor
- Coda follows

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Placing the refrain in different keys
  **Correction**: The refrain is ALWAYS in tonic

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking all rondos have rondo character
  **Clarification**: Rondo form and rondo character are independent; a piece can have one without the other

- **Confusion**: Not recognizing that B returning in tonic is a structural feature
  **Clarification**: In 7-part rondo, the B section returning in I is an important application of sonata principle
