# Quick Definition
A neighbor tone is a non-chord tone approached by step and left by step in the opposite direction, moving away from a chord tone and returning to it.

# Core Definition
Neighbor tones move away from a note by step then return to the note by step in the opposite direction. They can be upper neighbors (step up then step down) or lower neighbors (step down then step up), and can be accented or unaccented, diatonic or chromatic.

# Prerequisites
- **Non-chord tones overview**: Understanding the classification system

# Key Properties
1. Approached by step, left by step in the opposite direction
2. The note before and after the NT is the same pitch
3. Upper neighbor: step up, step down
4. Lower neighbor: step down, step up
5. Can be accented, unaccented, diatonic, or chromatic

# Construction / Recognition
1. Look for a note that departs from and returns to the same pitch
2. Confirm stepwise motion in both directions (opposite)
3. The surrounding notes should be the same chord tone
4. Label as NT; specify UNT or LNT if needed

# Context & Application
Neighbor tones embellish a sustained or repeated pitch by briefly departing and returning. They create melodic interest without progressing to a new pitch. They are common in all styles of music.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Minuet in G, WoO 10, No. 2 -- neighbor tones embellishing the melody

# Common Confusions
- **Returns to same note**: The defining feature -- the pitch before and after the NT is the same
- **Upper vs. lower**: UNT goes up then down; LNT goes down then up
- **Incomplete neighbor**: A variant that lacks either the approach or the return
