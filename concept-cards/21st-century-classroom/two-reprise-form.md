# Quick Definition

"Two-reprise" means both the first section (A) and second section (B or A') of a binary form are repeated, as indicated by repeat signs.

# Core Definition

The term "two-reprise" indicates that both the first section (the A section) and the second section (the A' or B section) are repeated. This descriptor is used in conjunction with other form labels (Hutchinson, Ch. 24, p. 346).

# Prerequisites

- **Binary form** -- Two-reprise is a descriptor applied to binary forms

# Key Properties

1. Both sections are repeated (indicated by repeat signs)
2. Used as a descriptor alongside other terms (e.g., "two-reprise continuous rounded binary")
3. Common in Baroque and Classical binary forms
4. Sonata form also uses two-reprise structure (exposition repeats, then development+recapitulation repeat)

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify:
1. Look for repeat signs in the score
2. If both halves of a binary form have repeat signs: two-reprise
3. Note: in sonata form, the exposition repeats as one unit and the development+recapitulation repeat as another

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: Baroque dance movements, Classical minuets, themes and variations
- **Historical/stylistic notes**: Douglass Green notes that sonata form's two-reprise structure (exposition repeats, development+recap repeat) connects it to binary form origins

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 349-350): Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 284, III -- "two-reprise continuous rounded binary" because both sections repeat

**Example 2** (pp. 350-351): Schubert, 20 Minuets, D. 41, No. 18 -- two-reprise continuous rounded binary

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Confusing "two-reprise" with "two sections"
  **Correction**: "Two-reprise" means both sections REPEAT; all binary forms have two sections regardless

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking "reprise" means "section"
  **Clarification**: "Reprise" here means "repeat" (from French); two-reprise means two repeated halves
