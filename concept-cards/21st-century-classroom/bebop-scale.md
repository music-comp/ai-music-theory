# Quick Definition
The bebop scale is an eight-note scale that adds a chromatic passing tone to a seven-note scale, aligning chord tones with strong beats when playing continuous eighth notes.

# Core Definition
The bebop dominant scale is identical to the Mixolydian mode with an added natural 7 (passing tone between b7 and 8). Scale degrees: 1-2-3-4-5-6-b7-natural7. The added chromatic note occurs in descending passages (8-natural7-b7) and creates an eight-note scale where chord tones (root, 3rd, 5th, 7th) land on downbeats when playing descending eighth notes starting from a chord tone. The most rudimentary improvisation begins on the downbeat from root, 3rd, 5th, or 7th (Hutchinson, Ch. 31, pp. 457-458).

# Prerequisites
- **Intervals** -- Must understand chromatic passing tones
- **Dominant seventh chord** -- Bebop dominant scale used over dominant 7ths

# Key Properties
1. Eight-note scale (one more than standard seven-note)
2. Mixolydian + added natural 7 as chromatic passing tone
3. Scale degrees: 1-2-3-4-5-6-b7-natural7
4. Chord tones fall on downbeats in descending eighth notes
5. Most commonly used descending
6. Also: bebop major scale (1-2-3-4-5-#5-6-7)

# Construction / Recognition
**Bebop dominant:** C-D-E-F-G-A-Bb-B (over C7)
**Bebop major:** C-D-E-F-G-G#-A-B (over CΔ7)

**Descending from each chord tone (p. 458, Figure 31.9.3):**
- From root: C-B-Bb-A-G-F-E-D
- From 3rd: E-D-C-B-Bb-A-G-F
- From 5th: G-F-E-D-C-B-Bb-A
- From 7th: Bb-A-G-F-E-D-C-B

# Context & Application
The bebop scale emerged from 1940s bebop jazz. It creates strong harmonic alignment in improvisation by ensuring chord tones land on strong beats. Mark Levine's *The Jazz Theory Book* details four-part harmonizations of the bebop scale.

# Examples
- C bebop dominant: C-D-E-F-G-A-Bb-B (p. 458)
- Descending from root, 3rd, 5th, and 7th (Figure 31.9.3, p. 458)
- C bebop major: C-D-E-F-G-G#-A-B

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Using the bebop scale ascending starting on an upbeat
  **Correction**: The scale aligns chord tones with downbeats primarily in descending eighth notes starting on a downbeat

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking "bebop scale" always means bebop dominant
  **Clarification**: There are also bebop major and bebop minor scales
