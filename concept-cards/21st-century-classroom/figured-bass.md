# Quick Definition
Figured bass (thoroughbass) is a Baroque-era shorthand notation consisting of a bass line with numbers indicating intervals to be played above the bass note within the key signature.

# Formal Definition
Figured bass (also known as thoroughbass) was developed in the Baroque era (roughly 1600-1750 C.E.) as a shorthand for writing chords. It consists of a bass line notated on a staff accompanied by numbers representing intervals to be played above the bass note within the key signature. These numbers are traditionally notated below the bass line.

# Analysis/Application
Like lead-sheet symbols, figured bass allowed keyboardists freedom in choosing chord voicings. In modern music theory courses, figured bass is primarily taught as a shorthand for chord inversion symbols, placed after Roman numerals.

# Musical Context
Figured bass was standard practice in Baroque music. Original scores often show just the bass line with figures, requiring the keyboardist to "realize" the accompaniment. Modern editions typically write out realizations.

# Examples
- Handel, Recorder Sonata in A minor, HWV 362, I. Larghetto - shows figured bass in original manuscript and realized version

# Common Confusions
- Figured bass numbers represent intervals from the bass, not chord structure
- In theory class, figures are placed after Roman numerals, not below the bass
- The figures assume notes within the key signature unless marked otherwise
