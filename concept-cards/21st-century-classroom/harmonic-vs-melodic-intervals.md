# Quick Definition
Harmonic intervals are measured between two notes sounding simultaneously; melodic intervals are measured between two notes sounding in succession.

# Formal Definition
When measuring intervals vertically (both notes sounding together), we refer to harmonic intervals because the notes create harmony. When measuring horizontally (one note following another), we refer to melodic intervals because the notes create melody. Both are measured the same way but differ in musical context.

# Construction/Recognition
- Harmonic interval: notes stacked vertically, sounding together
- Melodic interval: notes written horizontally, sounding one after another
- Melodic intervals can be ascending or descending
- Same measurement method for both types
- Harmonic intervals form the basis of chords

# Musical Context
Harmonic intervals are the building blocks of chords and harmony. Melodic intervals are the building blocks of melodies and voice leading. Both are equally important in music analysis and composition.

# Examples
- Two notes in a chord played together = harmonic interval
- The distance from one melody note to the next = melodic interval
- A pianist playing C and E together creates a harmonic M3
- A singer moving from C to E creates a melodic M3

# Common Confusions
- The interval size and quality are the same whether harmonic or melodic
- Direction matters for melodic intervals (ascending vs. descending)
- Harmonic intervals occur simultaneously, not sequentially
