# Quick Definition
Harmonic intervals are measured between two notes sounding simultaneously (vertical); melodic intervals are measured between two notes sounding in succession (horizontal).

# Core Definition
When measuring vertically, we refer to harmonic intervals because the two notes sound simultaneously. When measuring horizontally, we refer to melodic intervals because the notes occur one after the other. Both types are measured the same way (same numeric size and quality) but differ in their musical context and function.

# Prerequisites
- **Intervals**: The general concept of measurement between two pitches

# Key Properties
1. Harmonic interval: notes stacked vertically, sounding together
2. Melodic interval: notes written horizontally, sounding one after another
3. Melodic intervals can be ascending or descending
4. Same measurement method for both types
5. Harmonic intervals form the basis of chords
6. Melodic intervals form the basis of melodies

# Construction / Recognition
- Harmonic: two notes aligned vertically on the staff (or sounding together)
- Melodic: two notes in sequence on the staff (one after the other)
- Both measured identically for size and quality

# Context & Application
Harmonic intervals are the building blocks of chords and harmony. Melodic intervals are the building blocks of melodies and voice leading. Both are equally important in music analysis and composition. When you measure from the tonic up to each scale degree of a major scale, all resulting intervals are either "perfect" or "major."

# Examples
- Two notes in a chord played together = harmonic interval
- The distance from one melody note to the next = melodic interval
- A pianist playing C and E together creates a harmonic M3
- A singer moving from C up to E creates an ascending melodic M3

# Common Errors
- Assuming harmonic and melodic intervals are measured differently (they are not)
- Forgetting that direction matters for melodic intervals (ascending vs. descending)

# Common Confusions
- **Same measurement**: The interval size and quality are the same whether harmonic or melodic
- **Direction**: Direction matters for melodic intervals (ascending C to E vs. descending E to C)
- **Simultaneous vs. successive**: Harmonic = simultaneous; melodic = successive
