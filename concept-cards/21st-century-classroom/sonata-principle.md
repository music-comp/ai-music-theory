# Quick Definition

Sonata principle states that material first presented in a secondary key must be restated in the tonic key to achieve tonal resolution, treating the secondary key as a "large-scale dissonance."

# Core Definition

As discussed by Charles Rosen in *Sonata Forms*, the dominant is conceived as a "dissonant tonality" in the exposition. The material played outside the tonic is dissonant with respect to the center of stability. This polarization creates a "dissonant section" that requires resolution: a simple reintroduction of the tonic key will not serve as a resolution; the section outside the tonic needs to be resolved as a whole (Rosen, *Sonata Forms*, Revised Edition, 1988, pp. 229, 244, cited by Hutchinson, Ch. 25, pp. 368-369).

# Prerequisites

- **Sonata form** -- Sonata principle is the theoretical basis for sonata form
- **Modulation** -- Tonal opposition requires understanding of key change

# Key Properties

1. Material in a secondary key is "dissonant" relative to tonic
2. This creates a "dissonant section" requiring resolution
3. Resolution requires the entire secondary section to return in tonic
4. A simple return to tonic is NOT sufficient resolution
5. The principle applies beyond sonata form (e.g., in rondo)

# Construction / Recognition

## In Sonata Form:
- Exposition: ST in V (or III) = dissonant section
- Recapitulation: ST in I = resolution

## In Rondo Form:
- B section first stated in V, later restated in I
- Demonstrates sonata principle in a rondo context

## Key Insight:
Tonal design is as important as thematic design.

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: Sonata form, rondo form, any form with secondary-key material
- **Historical/stylistic notes**: Haydn's monothematic sonatas demonstrate that tonal design can be primary even when thematic contrast is minimal; the principle applies to many Classical-era forms

# Examples

**Example 1** (pp. 375-380): Beethoven, Pathetique Sonata, Op. 13, III (rondo):
- B section first in Eb major (III)
- B section later restated in C major (tonic major)
- Demonstrates sonata principle operating in rondo form

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Applying sonata principle only to sonata form
  **Correction**: The principle operates in rondo and other forms with secondary-key material

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the "dissonance" is at the chord level
  **Clarification**: The dissonance is LARGE-SCALE -- an entire key area is "dissonant" relative to tonic

- **Confusion**: Believing a simple return to tonic resolves the opposition
  **Clarification**: The ENTIRE secondary section must return in tonic for resolution
