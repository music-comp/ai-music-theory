# Quick Definition
Secondary diminished chords can tonicize most diatonic chords except the tonic itself, with different qualities (half-diminished or fully-diminished) depending on whether the target chord is major or minor.

# Formal Definition
In major keys, secondary diminished chords can tonicize ii, iii, IV, V, and vi. In minor keys, they can tonicize III, iv, V, VI, and VII. Note that vii°/III in minor has no accidentals - it looks like ii°7 but functions differently (progressing to III rather than V).

# Analysis/Application
Quality matching:
- Minor triads tonicized by fully-diminished sevenths
- Major triads traditionally tonicized by half-diminished sevenths
- Major triads can also use fully-diminished sevenths

Secondary diminished chords in C major:
- vii°/ii = C#° or C#ø7 (tonicizes Dm)
- vii°/iii = D#° or D#ø7 (tonicizes Em)
- vii°/IV = E° or Eø7 (tonicizes F)
- vii°/V = F#° or F#ø7 (tonicizes G)
- vii°/vi = G#° or G#°7 (tonicizes Am - uses fully-dim)

# Musical Context
Secondary diminished chords provide an alternative to secondary dominants for creating tonicization. They are particularly common in sequences where the bass moves chromatically.

# Common Confusions
- vii°7/III in minor = ii°7 with same notes but different function
- Half-diminished for major targets, fully-diminished for minor targets (traditional rule)
- vii°/III is NOT included in minor because it would be ii° (no tonicization)
