# Quick Definition
An invention exposition is the opening section of a two-voice invention where the theme appears in alternating voices following a I-I-V-V-I harmonic pattern over four measures.

# Formal Definition
The invention exposition is the opening four-measure section of a two-voice invention (as exemplified in Bach's Two-Part Inventions). The theme is stated in the upper voice (m.1), then in the lower voice an octave below (m.2), then a fifth higher in the upper voice (m.3), then an octave below that in the lower voice (m.4). The underlying harmonic pattern is I-I-V-V-I, concluding with tonic harmony and typically the interval of a 10th.

# Rules/Procedures
1. **Measure 1**: Theme in upper voice (I harmony)
2. **Measure 2**: Theme in lower voice, octave below m.1 (I harmony)
   - Upper voice provides counterpoint emphasizing I
3. **Measure 3**: Theme in upper voice, P5 above m.1 (V harmony)
   - Lower voice provides counterpoint emphasizing V
4. **Measure 4**: Theme in lower voice, octave below m.3 (V harmony)
   - Upper voice provides counterpoint
5. **End of m.4/beginning of m.5**: Arrive on interval of 10th with tonic in bass and scale degree 3 in upper voice (I harmony)
6. Counterpoint must emphasize the underlying harmony of each measure

# Musical Context
Bach's Two-Part Inventions serve both as keyboard pieces and compositional models. The invention exposition teaches tonal counterpoint by combining species counterpoint skills with harmonic awareness. The I-I-V-V-I pattern provides a clear tonal framework.

# Examples
- Bach, Invention No. 1 in C major, BWV 772: Theme in RH (m.1), LH (m.2), RH up 5th (m.3), LH (m.4)
- Bach, Invention No. 7 in E minor, BWV 778: Same pattern in minor mode

# Common Confusions
- Not transposing the theme correctly (up a 5th for m.3, not up a 4th)
- Forgetting that the counterpoint must emphasize the underlying harmony
- Not ending on the 10th interval (tonic in bass, 3rd above)
- Confusing invention exposition with fugue exposition (inventions use imitation at the octave/5th, fugues use subject and answer)
