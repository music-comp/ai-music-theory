# Quick Definition
An invention exposition is the opening section of a two-voice invention where the theme appears in alternating voices following a I-I-V-V-I harmonic pattern over four measures.

# Core Definition
The invention exposition is the opening four-measure section of a two-voice invention, modeled on Bach's Two-Part Inventions. The theme is stated in the upper voice (m. 1), then in the lower voice an octave below (m. 2), then a perfect fifth higher in the upper voice (m. 3), then an octave below that in the lower voice (m. 4). The underlying harmonic pattern is I-I-V-V-I, concluding with tonic harmony at the interval of a 10th (tonic in bass, scale degree 3 in upper voice). Bach's inventions were intended as both instructional keyboard pieces and models for composition (Hutchinson, Ch. 30, pp. 429-432).

# Prerequisites
- **Species counterpoint** -- Invention counterpoint applies species skills to a tonal context
- **Harmonic function** -- Must understand the I-V-I harmonic framework

# Key Properties
1. Four measures following a I-I-V-V-I harmonic pattern
2. Theme alternates between upper and lower voices
3. Measure 1: theme in upper voice (I)
4. Measure 2: theme in lower voice, octave below m. 1 (I)
5. Measure 3: theme in upper voice, P5 above m. 1 (V)
6. Measure 4: theme in lower voice, octave below m. 3 (V)
7. Ends on interval of 10th: tonic in bass, scale degree 3 above (I)

# Construction / Recognition
**Writing an invention exposition:**
1. Copy the theme in the upper voice (m. 1)
2. Transpose down an octave for the lower voice (m. 2)
3. Transpose up a perfect 5th from m. 1 for the upper voice (m. 3)
4. Transpose down an octave from m. 3 for the lower voice (m. 4)
5. Write counterpoint in the free voice for mm. 2-4, emphasizing the harmony
6. End on the interval of a 10th (tonic in bass, 3rd above)

**Theme alterations may be needed:**
- Themes moving I to V may need contour alteration in mm. 3-4 to emphasize V leading to I
- Themes landing on scale degree 3 may need end-of-measure adjustment to reach scale degree 5

# Context & Application
Bach's Two-Part Inventions serve both as keyboard pieces and compositional models. The invention exposition teaches tonal counterpoint by combining species counterpoint skills with harmonic awareness. The I-I-V-V-I pattern provides a clear tonal framework while the theme transpositions create imitative texture.

# Examples
- **Bach, Invention No. 1 in C major, BWV 772** (p. 430): Theme in RH (m. 1), LH (m. 2), RH up P5 (m. 3), LH (m. 4)
- **Bach, Invention No. 7 in E minor, BWV 778** (p. 430): Same pattern in minor mode
- Both inventions demonstrate the I-I-V-V-I harmonic plan

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Transposing the theme up a 4th instead of a 5th for m. 3
  **Correction**: The transposition is up a perfect 5th from m. 1
- **Error**: Forgetting to end on the interval of a 10th
  **Correction**: The exposition ends with tonic in bass and scale degree 3 in the upper voice

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing invention exposition with fugue exposition
  **Clarification**: Inventions use octave/5th transpositions of the same theme; fugues use subject and answer with potential intervallic alterations
- **Confusion**: Thinking the counterpoint can ignore the underlying harmony
  **Clarification**: Unlike species counterpoint, invention counterpoint must emphasize the harmony (I or V) in each measure
