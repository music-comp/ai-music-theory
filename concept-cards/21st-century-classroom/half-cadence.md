# Quick Definition
A half cadence (HC) is a phrase ending on the V chord, creating an incomplete or suspenseful stopping point -- like a comma or question mark in language.

# Core Definition
A half cadence (HC) occurs when a phrase ends on the V chord. Unlike other cadences that resolve TO a destination chord, the half cadence ends ON the dominant, creating a sense of incompleteness or pause. It functions like a comma or question mark in language, signaling more to come. Any chord can precede V in a half cadence.

# Prerequisites
- **Cadence**: The general concept of harmonic arrival

# Key Properties
1. Phrase ends ON V (not resolving to I)
2. Any chord can precede V (I-V, ii-V, IV-V, vi-V, etc.)
3. Creates expectation for continuation
4. Sense of incompleteness or pause (comma, not period)
5. Often found at midpoints of phrase structures (e.g., end of antecedent phrase)
6. Less conclusive than any cadence that resolves to tonic

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the last chord of a phrase
- If the phrase ends on V (from any preceding chord): half cadence
- The approach chord is less important than the fact that V is the goal

# Context & Application
Half cadences create momentum and expectation, often marking the midpoint of a phrase structure (like the antecedent phrase in a period). They leave the listener waiting for resolution and are essential for creating musical drama and forward motion.

# Examples
- Mozart, Eine kleine Nachtmusik, K. 525, I (opens with a half cadence)
- "Thinking Out Loud" (Sheeran and Wadge) uses half cadences
- "Love Yourself" (Bieber, Blanco, Sheeran) includes a half cadence
- C-G in C major = I-V (half cadence)
- The antecedent phrase in a period typically ends with HC

# Common Errors
- Confusing "ending on V" with "V going somewhere" (HC ends ON V, not with V resolving)
- Assuming only certain chords can precede V in a half cadence (any chord can)

# Common Confusions
- **Ends ON V, not WITH V**: Half cadence ends with V as the goal; authentic cadence has V resolving to I
- **Still a cadence**: A half cadence is a real stopping point, just an incomplete one
- **V-I is authentic; ending on V is half**: These are different cadence types despite both involving V
