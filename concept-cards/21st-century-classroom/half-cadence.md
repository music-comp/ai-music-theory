# Quick Definition
A half cadence is a phrase ending on the V chord, creating an incomplete or suspenseful stopping point.

# Formal Definition
A half cadence (HC) occurs when a phrase ends on the dominant chord (V). Unlike other cadences that resolve TO something, the half cadence ends ON the dominant, creating a sense of incompleteness or pause. It functions like a comma or question mark in language, signaling more to come.

# Construction/Recognition
- Phrase ends ON V (not resolving to I)
- Any chord can precede V in a half cadence
- Common approaches: I-V, ii-V, IV-V, vi-V
- Creates expectation for continuation
- Often found at the midpoint of phrases or sections

# Musical Context
Half cadences create momentum and expectation, often marking the midpoint of a phrase structure (like the antecedent phrase in a period). They leave the listener waiting for resolution and are essential for creating musical drama and forward motion.

# Examples
- C-G in C major = I-V (half cadence)
- Mozart's Eine kleine Nachtmusik opens with a half cadence
- "Thinking Out Loud" (Ed Sheeran) uses half cadences
- The "antecedent" phrase in a period typically ends with HC

# Common Confusions
- Half cadence ends ON V, not WITH V going somewhere
- It is still a cadence (a stopping point), just incomplete
- V-I is authentic; ending on V (from anywhere) is half
