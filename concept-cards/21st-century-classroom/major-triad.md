# Quick Definition
A major triad is built with a major third (4 half steps) on the bottom and a minor third (3 half steps) on top, totaling a perfect fifth from root to fifth.

# Core Definition
A major triad is built with a major third (4 half steps) between the root and third, and a minor third (3 half steps) between the third and fifth. The total interval from root to fifth is a perfect fifth (7 half steps). In lead-sheet notation, major triads are represented by an uppercase letter alone (e.g., C, F, G). In Roman numeral analysis, uppercase numerals represent major triads (I, IV, V).

# Prerequisites
- **Triad**: The three-note chord concept
- **Major/minor intervals**: Distinguishing M3 from m3

# Key Properties
1. Root to third: Major 3rd (4 half steps)
2. Third to fifth: minor 3rd (3 half steps)
3. Root to fifth: Perfect 5th (7 half steps)
4. Lead-sheet symbol: uppercase letter only (C, F, G)
5. Roman numeral: uppercase (I, IV, V)
6. Sound character: "bright," "happy," "stable"

# Construction / Recognition
- M3 on the bottom, m3 on top
- Total span: P5
- Lead-sheet: just the letter (no modifier = major)
- In major keys: I, IV, V are major triads

# Context & Application
Major triads are among the most common chords in Western music. The I, IV, and V chords in major keys are all major triads. They provide stability, brightness, and resolution in harmonic progressions.

# Examples
- C major: C-E-G (M3 + m3)
- G major: G-B-D
- F major: F-A-C
- Lead-sheet: C, G, F (just the letter, no symbol)

## Contrasts With
- Minor triad (m3 on bottom, M3 on top)
- Diminished triad (m3 + m3)
- Augmented triad (M3 + M3)

# Common Errors
- Adding "M" or "maj" to the lead-sheet symbol (for triads, just the letter means major)
- Reversing the interval order (M3 is on the bottom, not the top)

# Common Confusions
- **M3 on bottom**: Major triad = M3 + m3 (opposite of minor triad's m3 + M3)
- **Lead-sheet convention**: No symbol after the letter means major for triads
- **"Major" = quality**: Refers to the arrangement of intervals, not size
