# Quick Definition
A major triad consists of a major third from root to third, and a minor third from third to fifth, totaling a perfect fifth from root to fifth.

# Formal Definition
A major triad is built with a major third (4 half steps) between the root and third, and a minor third (3 half steps) between the third and fifth. The total interval from root to fifth is a perfect fifth (7 half steps). Major triads sound "bright," "happy," or "stable."

# Construction/Recognition
- Root to third: Major 3rd (4 half steps)
- Third to fifth: minor 3rd (3 half steps)
- Root to fifth: Perfect 5th (7 half steps)
- Lead-sheet symbol: uppercase letter only (C, F, G)
- Roman numeral: uppercase (I, IV, V)

# Musical Context
Major triads are among the most common chords in Western music. The I, IV, and V chords in major keys are all major triads. They provide stability, brightness, and resolution in harmonic progressions.

# Examples
- C major: C-E-G (M3 + m3)
- G major: G-B-D
- F major: F-A-C
- Lead-sheet: C, G, F (just the letter)

# Common Confusions
- M3 is on the bottom, m3 on top (opposite of minor triad)
- Lead-sheet uses just the letter for major (no "M" or "maj" needed for triads)
- Major refers to the quality, not the size
