# Quick Definition
Each diatonic major or minor triad (except viio in major and iio/viio in minor) can be tonicized with a secondary dominant, with raised chromatic notes acting as leading tones -- except V7/IV, which uniquely uses a lowered chromatic note.

# Core Definition
In major keys, secondary dominants can tonicize ii, iii, IV, V, and vi. In minor keys, secondary dominants can tonicize III, iv, V, VI, and VII (the subtonic). Both major triads and major-minor seventh chords serve as secondary dominants. The raised notes generally act as leading tones to the root of the chord being tonicized. The only secondary dominant with a lowered chromaticism in major is V7/IV, where the lowered note acts as scale degree 4 of the chord being tonicized (analogous to how the last flat in a key signature is scale degree 4).

# Key Properties
1. In major: V/ or V7/ of ii, iii, IV, V, vi (five possible targets)
2. In minor: V/ or V7/ of III, iv, V, VI, VII (five possible targets)
3. Raised chromatics = leading tones to target root (most cases)
4. V7/IV is the exception: uses a lowered chromatic (creating scale degree 4 of target)
5. Diminished triads cannot be tonicized (viio in major; iio and viio in minor)
6. In minor, subtonic VII (major quality) can be tonicized, but viio (diminished) cannot

# Construction / Recognition
- In C major: V/ii = A(7), V/iii = B(7), V/IV = C7, V/V = D(7), V/vi = E(7)
- In C minor: V/III = Bb(7), V/iv = C(7), V/V = D(7), V/VI = Eb(7), V/VII = F(7)
- In minor, F major chord can be V/VII or IV depending on whether it progresses to Bb (VII) or to V
- Bb7 in C minor can be V7/III or VII7 -- both are correct since both progress to III

# Context & Application
V/V is the most common secondary dominant in both major and minor. In minor, the distinction between V/VII (tonicizing subtonic) and IV (pre-dominant function) depends on the chord's resolution: if it progresses to bVII, label as V/VII; if it progresses to V or viio, label as IV. The dual identity of certain chords in minor (like Bb7 as V7/III or VII7) requires attention to context and resolution.

# Common Errors
- Forgetting that V7/IV has a lowered (not raised) chromatic
- Confusing IV and V/VII in minor keys

# Common Confusions
- **V7/IV exception**: The lowered note in V7/IV acts as scale degree 4 of the target, not as a leading tone
- **Minor key ambiguity**: F major in C minor could be V/VII or IV -- resolution determines the label
- **VII vs. viio in minor**: The major subtonic VII can be tonicized; the diminished viio cannot
