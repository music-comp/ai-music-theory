# Quick Definition
Five-part rondo has the structure ABACA or ABABA, with the refrain (A) appearing three times, alternating with two contrasting episodes.

# Formal Definition
The 5-part rondo has ABACA form (two different episodes) or ABABA form (same episode returns). The refrain (A) is always in tonic, while episodes provide contrast through different keys and thematic material.

# Construction/Recognition
ABACA structure:
- A: Refrain in tonic
- B: First episode (contrasting key, often V or III)
- A: Refrain returns (tonic)
- C: Second episode (different contrasting key)
- A: Final refrain (tonic)

ABABA structure:
- Same episode (B) alternates with refrain
- Less common than ABACA

Transitions and retransitions connect sections.

# Musical Context
- Often used in slow movements
- Simpler than 7-part rondo
- Found in Mozart's Eine kleine Nachtmusik, II
- Provides clear formal structure with variety

# Examples
- Mozart, Eine kleine Nachtmusik, K. 525, II (discussed in earlier chapter)

# Common Confusions
- ABACA has two DIFFERENT episodes
- ABABA has the SAME episode returning
- The refrain is always in tonic
- Five sections, but only three are the refrain
