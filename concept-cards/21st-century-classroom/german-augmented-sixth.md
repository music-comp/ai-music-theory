# Quick Definition
The German augmented sixth (Ger+6) adds flat-3 to the Italian foundation, creating a chord that sounds identical to a dominant seventh chord but functions as a pre-dominant.

# Formal Definition
The German augmented sixth chord (labeled Ger+6) contains scale degrees flat-6, 1, flat-3, and sharp-4. It builds upon the Italian augmented sixth's flat-6, 1, and sharp-4 by adding scale degree flat-3. This creates a sonority identical to a major-minor (dominant) seventh chord, but spelled and functioning differently.

# Construction/Recognition
Scale degrees: flat-6, 1, flat-3, sharp-4
- In C major/minor: Ab, C, Eb, F# (with Ab in bass)
- When stacked in thirds: F#-Ab-C-Eb (contains diminished third F#-Ab)
- Sounds like Ab7 (Ab-C-Eb-Gb) but spelled with F# instead of Gb
- The enharmonic spelling shows voice leading: F# resolves up to G

Identification: Four-note augmented sixth chord with flat-3; sounds like Mm7.

# Musical Context
- Pre-dominant function, leading to V or cadential I6/4
- Most common augmented sixth chord type
- In jazz/pop, typically spelled as VI7 or bVI7 (dominant seventh quality)
- The Ger+6 to I6/4 avoids parallel fifths (flat-3 to 3, 1 to 1)

# Examples
- Rossini, William Tell Overture (1829) - resolves to I6/4
- John Coltrane, "Mr. P.C." (1959) - spelled as VI7 in minor
- Henry Mancini, "The Pink Panther Theme" (1963) - as VI7
- Fiona Apple, "Criminal" (1996) - F7 in key of A minor
- "Friend Like Me" from Aladdin (1992) - Am-F7-E progression

# Common Confusions
- Ger+6 SOUNDS like a dominant seventh but FUNCTIONS as pre-dominant
- Typically resolves to I6/4 (not directly to V) to avoid parallel fifths
- In jazz/pop analysis: VI7 or bVI7; in classical: Ger+6
- The "German" label has no historical justification
