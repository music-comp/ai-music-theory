# Quick Definition
The German augmented sixth (Ger+6) adds b3 to the Italian foundation, creating a chord (b6, 1, b3, #4) that sounds identical to a dominant seventh chord but is spelled differently and functions as a pre-dominant -- the most common augmented sixth type.

# Core Definition
The German augmented sixth chord (Ger+6) contains scale degrees b6, 1, b3, and #4. It builds upon the Italian augmented sixth's b6, 1, and #4 by adding scale degree b3. This creates a sonority identical to a major-minor (dominant) seventh chord, but spelled and functioning differently: the enharmonic spelling (e.g., F# instead of Gb) shows the voice leading, with #4 resolving up to 5. The Ger+6 typically resolves to the cadential I6/4 rather than directly to V, avoiding parallel fifths between b3 and b6 moving to 2 and 5.

# Key Properties
1. Scale degrees: b6, 1, b3, #4 (four notes)
2. Sounds identical to a dominant seventh chord (Mm7)
3. Spelling reflects voice leading: #4 resolves up (not Gb resolving down)
4. Most common augmented sixth chord type
5. Typically resolves to cadential I6/4 (to avoid parallel fifths)
6. In jazz/pop: spelled as VI7 or bVI7

# Construction / Recognition
- In C major/minor: Ab-C-Eb-F# (with Ab in bass)
- Sounds like Ab7 (Ab-C-Eb-Gb) but spelled with F# instead of Gb
- Stacked in thirds: F#-Ab-C-Eb (contains diminished third F#-Ab)
- Distinguished from Fr+6 by b3 (not natural 2)
- In lead-sheet: analyzed as dominant 7th on b6

# Context & Application
The Ger+6 is the most frequently encountered augmented sixth chord, appearing in both classical and popular music. In jazz and popular music, it is typically spelled and analyzed as bVI7 or VI7 (in minor), reflecting its dominant-seventh sound. The Ger+6 is also central to enharmonic modulation: because it sounds identical to a dominant seventh, it can be reinterpreted as V7 in a new key. The resolution to I6/4 (rather than directly to V) is standard in classical voice leading to avoid parallel fifths.

# Examples
- Rossini, William Tell Overture (1829) -- Ger+6 resolving to I6/4
- John Coltrane, "Mr. P.C." (1959) -- spelled as VI7 in minor
- Henry Mancini, "The Pink Panther Theme" (1963) -- as VI7
- Fiona Apple, "Criminal" (1996) -- F7 in key of A minor
- Alan Menken, "Friend Like Me" from Aladdin (1992) -- Am-F7-E (i-Ger+6-V)

# Common Errors
- Resolving directly to V when parallel fifths result (use I6/4 as intermediary)
- Spelling as a dominant 7th when classical analysis is required

# Common Confusions
- **Sound vs. function**: Sounds like V7 but functions as pre-dominant
- **Resolution to I6/4**: Standard to avoid parallel fifths (b3 to 2, b6 to 5 would create parallel 5ths with V)
- **Jazz vs. classical spelling**: VI7 in jazz = Ger+6 in classical -- same chord, different analytical traditions
