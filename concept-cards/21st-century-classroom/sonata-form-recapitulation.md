# Quick Definition

The recapitulation restates the themes from the exposition, crucially bringing the secondary theme into the tonic key, thereby resolving the tonal opposition established in the exposition.

# Core Definition

The recapitulation resolves the large-scale tonal dissonance of the exposition by restating all themes in the tonic key. As Charles Rosen explains, the section outside the tonic "needs to be resolved as a whole" -- a simple reintroduction of the tonic key will not suffice; the entire secondary section must be heard in tonic to achieve resolution (Rosen, *Sonata Forms*, cited by Hutchinson, Ch. 25, pp. 368-369).

# Prerequisites

- **Sonata form** -- The recapitulation is the third main section
- **Sonata form exposition** -- The recapitulation resolves the exposition's tonal tension
- **Sonata principle** -- Explains why the secondary theme must return in tonic

# Key Properties

1. Primary Theme: restated in tonic (as in exposition)
2. Transition: ALTERED to avoid modulation; may use tonicizations
3. Secondary Theme: NOW IN TONIC (the crucial resolution)
4. Closing Theme: in tonic; confirms home key
5. The altered transition is a key compositional challenge
6. May be followed by a coda

# Construction / Recognition

## Tonal Plan:
- PT (I) --> Transition (tonicizations, NOT modulation) --> ST (I) --> CT (I)

## Key Difference from Exposition:
- The transition is modified so the ST appears in TONIC, not the secondary key
- Everything else may be similar to the exposition, with variations

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: Final main section of sonata form movements
- **Common applications**: Resolves the tonal drama of the entire movement
- **Historical/stylistic notes**: The modified transition is where the composer's ingenuity shows; some recapitulations shorten, reorder, or vary material

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming the recapitulation is identical to the exposition
  **Correction**: The transition MUST be altered to keep the ST in tonic

- **Error**: Not recognizing that the ST key change is the main event
  **Correction**: The secondary theme appearing in tonic is the essential resolution

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking a "return to tonic" at the start of the recap is sufficient resolution
  **Clarification**: Rosen argues the ENTIRE secondary section must return in tonic -- not just the PT

- **Confusion**: Believing material cannot be shortened, reordered, or varied
  **Clarification**: Composers frequently modify recapitulation material
