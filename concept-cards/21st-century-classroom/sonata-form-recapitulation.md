# Quick Definition
The recapitulation restates the themes from the exposition, crucially bringing the secondary theme into the tonic key, thereby resolving the tonal opposition established in the exposition.

# Formal Definition
The recapitulation resolves the large-scale tonal dissonance of the exposition by restating all themes in the tonic key. As Charles Rosen explains, the section outside the tonic "needs to be resolved as a whole" - a simple return to tonic is not sufficient; the secondary theme must be heard in tonic to achieve resolution.

# Construction/Recognition
Structure of recapitulation:

1. PRIMARY THEME (PT): tonic (as in exposition)

2. TRANSITION:
   - Modified to AVOID modulation
   - May include tonicizations
   - Leads to ST in TONIC (not dominant)

3. SECONDARY THEME (ST): NOW IN TONIC
   - This is the crucial resolution
   - Material from dominant/mediant now in home key

4. CLOSING THEME (CT): tonic
   - Confirms tonic key
   - May lead to coda

Key point: The transition must be altered to keep ST in tonic.

# Musical Context
- Resolves the tonal drama of the exposition
- The modified transition is a key compositional challenge
- Secondary theme in tonic = resolution of "dissonant section"
- Often followed by a coda

# Examples
- Compare expositions and recapitulations to see transition modifications
- Mozart, Beethoven sonata movements demonstrate altered transitions

# Common Confusions
- The recapitulation is NOT an exact repeat of the exposition
- The transition MUST be altered to keep ST in tonic
- The tonal resolution is as important as the thematic return
- Material may be shortened, reordered, or varied
