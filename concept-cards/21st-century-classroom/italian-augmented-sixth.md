# Quick Definition
The Italian augmented sixth (It+6) is the simplest augmented sixth chord, containing only three notes: flat-6, 1, and sharp-4.

# Formal Definition
The Italian augmented sixth chord (labeled It+6) contains scale degrees flat-6, 1, and sharp-4. It forms the foundation upon which the French and German augmented sixth chords are built. The interval of an augmented sixth occurs between flat-6 (in bass) and sharp-4. Scale degree 1 is typically doubled.

# Construction/Recognition
Scale degrees: flat-6, 1, sharp-4
- In C major/minor: Ab, C, F# (with Ab in bass)
- When stacked in thirds: F#-Ab-C (contains diminished third F#-Ab)
- Three notes total; 1 is usually doubled
- The augmented sixth interval spans from bass to sharp-4

Identification: Look for a three-note chord with the characteristic augmented sixth interval and no additional tones.

# Musical Context
- Pre-dominant function
- Resolves to V (octave on 5) or cadential I6/4
- The augmented sixth expands outward by half steps: flat-6 down to 5, sharp-4 up to 5
- Less common than German +6 but still found in both classical and popular music

# Examples
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 5, Op. 67, I (1808) - classical spelling
- Duke Ellington, "It Don't Mean A Thing (If It Ain't Got That Swing)" (1931) - spelled enharmonically as Mm7 with omitted fifth

# Common Confusions
- The It+6 has only THREE notes (flat-6, 1, sharp-4)
- In popular music, often spelled as incomplete Mm7 chord
- Do not confuse with Fr+6 (adds 2) or Ger+6 (adds flat-3)
- The "Italian" label has no historical justification
