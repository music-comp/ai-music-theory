# Quick Definition
The Italian augmented sixth (It+6) is the simplest augmented sixth chord, containing only three notes -- b6, 1, and #4 -- forming the foundation upon which the French and German types are built, with scale degree 1 typically doubled.

# Core Definition
The Italian augmented sixth chord (It+6) contains scale degrees b6, 1, and #4. It forms the foundation upon which the French and German augmented sixth chords are built. The interval of an augmented sixth occurs between b6 (in bass) and #4. With only three notes, scale degree 1 is typically doubled in four-voice writing. When stacked in thirds, the chord contains the characteristic diminished third (#4 to b6).

# Prerequisites
- **Augmented sixth chords**: The general category and identification method

# Key Properties
1. Scale degrees: b6, 1, #4 (three notes only)
2. Forms the foundation of Fr+6 and Ger+6
3. Scale degree 1 is typically doubled in four-voice writing
4. In C major/minor: Ab-C-F# (with Ab in bass)
5. Stacked in thirds: F#-Ab-C (contains diminished third F#-Ab)
6. The augmented sixth spans from bass (b6) to #4

# Construction / Recognition
- Three-note chord with the augmented sixth interval and no additional tones
- In four voices: double scale degree 1
- Distinguished from Fr+6 (which adds 2) and Ger+6 (which adds b3)
- In lead-sheet analysis: treated as incomplete Mm7 chord (omitted 5th)

# Context & Application
The It+6 is less common than the Ger+6 but still appears in both classical and popular music. In popular music, it is often spelled enharmonically as a major-minor seventh chord with the fifth omitted. Its three-note simplicity makes it easy to use in textures where fewer chord tones are available.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 5, Op. 67, I (1808) -- It+6 with classical spelling
- Duke Ellington, "It Don't Mean A Thing (If It Ain't Got That Swing)" (1931) -- enharmonic Mm7 with omitted fifth

# Common Errors
- Adding a fourth note and still calling it Italian (Italian has only THREE notes)
- Forgetting to double scale degree 1 in four-voice writing

# Common Confusions
- **Only three notes**: It+6 has b6, 1, and #4 only
- **Foundation chord**: Fr+6 = It+6 plus 2; Ger+6 = It+6 plus b3
- **Lead-sheet vs. classical**: In lead-sheet, analyzed as incomplete dominant 7th on b6
