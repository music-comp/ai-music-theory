# Quick Definition
A phrase is the smallest section of a musical form, typically four measures long, that gives a sense of completing a formal unit, often ending with a cadence.

# Formal Definition
Musical form is full of sections, and the phrase is the smallest category of section. Unlike a motive, a phrase gives the sense of completing a formal unit. In classical music, phrases always end with cadences. In popular music, a phrase might be completed by reaching four measures or completing a line of lyric instead of by a cadence.

# Analysis/Application
When analyzing phrases:
1. Identify cadences to locate phrase endings
2. Most phrases are four or eight measures long
3. Phrases can also be 3, 5, 6, or 7 measures
4. Label phrases with lowercase letters (a, b, c, etc.)
5. Use prime marks (a', a") for variants

# Musical Context
Phrases are the fundamental building blocks of musical form. They combine to create periods, phrase groups, and larger formal structures. The four-measure phrase is most common, though other lengths occur.

# Examples
- "My Girl" (1964) - four-measure phrase with sense of completion
- "I'm Every Woman" (1978) - four-measure phrase
- "Irreplaceable" (2006) - five-measure phrase
- Mozart, Symphony No. 40, K. 550, III - six-measure phrase ending with V7-i
- Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 545, II - eight-measure phrase

# Common Confusions
- Phrases give a sense of completion; motives do not
- In popular music, phrases may end without traditional cadences
- Seven-measure phrases often involve elisions
