# Quick Definition
A phrase is the smallest section of musical form that gives the sense of completing a formal unit, typically four measures long, often ending with a cadence.

# Core Definition
Musical form is full of sections, and the phrase is the smallest category of section. Unlike a motive, a phrase gives the sense of completing a formal unit. In classical music, phrases always end with cadences; if you encounter a cadence, you have just witnessed the completion of a phrase. In popular music, a phrase might be completed by reaching four measures or by completing a line of lyric instead of by a cadence. While four- and eight-measure phrases are most common, phrases of 3, 5, 6, and 7 measures also occur.

# Prerequisites
- **Cadence**: Understanding phrase-ending chord progressions
- **Motive**: The smaller melodic building blocks within phrases

# Key Properties
1. Smallest category of formal section
2. Gives sense of completing a formal unit
3. Classical: always ends with a cadence
4. Popular: may end by reaching 4 measures or completing a lyric line
5. Most common lengths: 4 and 8 measures
6. Also possible: 3, 5, 6, 7 measures
7. Labeled with lowercase letters (a, b, c, etc.)
8. Prime marks (', ") for variants with different cadences

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify cadences to locate phrase endings
2. Most phrases are 4 or 8 measures long
3. In pop music, look for lyric completion or 4-bar units
4. Label phrases with lowercase letters
5. Use primes for similar phrases with different cadences

# Context & Application
Phrases are the fundamental building blocks of musical form. They combine to create periods, phrase groups, sentences, and larger structures. Understanding phrase structure is essential for analyzing form at all levels.

# Examples
- "My Girl" (1964) -- four-measure phrase
- "I'm Every Woman" (1978) -- four-measure phrase
- "Irreplaceable" (2006) -- five-measure phrase
- Mozart, Symphony No. 40, K. 550, III -- six-measure phrase ending with V7-i
- Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 545, II -- eight-measure phrase

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Cadence (marks phrase endings)

## Enables
- Period structure (phrases in combination)
- Sentence structure (specific phrase type)
- Larger formal structures

## Related
- subphrase, period, sentence, cadence

## Contrasts With
- Motive (does not give sense of completion)
- Subphrase (smaller, typically half a phrase)

# Common Errors
- Equating phrase with motive (phrases are larger and give completion)
- Assuming all phrases are four measures

# Common Confusions
- **Phrases give completion; motives do not**: This is the key distinction
- **Pop vs. classical**: Pop phrases may lack traditional cadences
- **Seven-measure phrases**: Often involve elisions (overlap with next phrase)
