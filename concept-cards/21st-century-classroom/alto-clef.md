# Quick Definition
The alto clef is a C clef that places middle C on the middle line of the staff, used primarily by violas.

# Formal Definition
The alto clef is a type of C clef that, when placed on a five-line staff, designates the middle line (third line from bottom) as middle C (C4). The symmetrical shape of the C clef centers on the line representing middle C.

# Construction/Recognition
- The C clef symbol centers on the middle line
- The middle line equals middle C (C4)
- Lines (bottom to top): F3-A3-C4-E4-G4
- Spaces (bottom to top): G3-B3-D4-F4

# Musical Context
The alto clef is primarily used for the viola, which has a range that sits between the treble and bass clef ranges. It allows most viola music to be written on the staff without excessive ledger lines.

# Examples
- The middle line of the alto clef staff is always C4 (middle C)
- Viola parts are almost exclusively written in alto clef
- Some trombone and other instrumental parts occasionally use alto clef

# Common Confusions
- Confusing alto clef with tenor clef (both are C clefs but place middle C on different lines)
- Trying to read alto clef as if it were treble or bass clef
- The C clef symbol looks different from treble and bass clefs
