# Quick Definition
Arpeggiated accompaniment breaks chords into individual notes played in succession, creating continuous rhythmic motion while expressing the underlying harmony.

# Core Definition
Arpeggiated accompaniments express chords rhythmically through arpeggios -- chord tones played in succession rather than simultaneously. Arpeggios can ascend, descend, or alternate direction. A bass line in octaves often accompanies the arpeggios in a lower part. The harmonious interval of a tenth (octave plus a third) frequently appears between the bass and soprano voices.

# Prerequisites
- **Texture**: Understanding of accompaniment patterns

# Key Properties
1. Chords broken into successive notes
2. Can ascend, descend, or alternate direction
3. Bass line often separate from arpeggios
4. Tenth (octave + third) between bass and soprano is common
5. Creates flowing, continuous motion
6. Can use eighth notes, sixteenth notes, or triplets

# Construction / Recognition
1. Look for chord tones played in succession rather than simultaneously
2. Identify the pattern: ascending, descending, or mixed
3. Note the bass line (often in octaves, separate from the arpeggio pattern)
4. Check for the tenth between outer voices

# Context & Application
Arpeggiated accompaniment is common across classical and popular music, from Beethoven to contemporary pop ballads. It creates flowing motion while maintaining harmonic clarity. Block chords may occasionally appear alongside arpeggios for legato effect.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Moonlight Sonata, Op. 27, No. 2, I (1802) -- triplet arpeggios with bass octaves, tenths between outer voices
- Alicia Keys, "If I Ain't Got You" (2004) -- descending arpeggios, tenths between outer voices
- "Colour My World" (Chicago, 1970) -- ascending/descending arpeggios
- Adele, "Someone Like You" (2011) -- arpeggiated piano
- "No Scrubs" (TLC, 1999) -- sixteenth-note arpeggios
- "Love on the Brain" (Rihanna, 2016) -- arpeggios with organ block chords for legato

# Common Errors
- Treating arpeggios as melody (they are accompaniment)
- Missing the separate bass line

# Common Confusions
- **Alberti bass is a subtype**: Specific low-high-middle-high pattern
- **Bass line is often separate**: The arpeggio pattern and bass line may be in different hands/instruments
- **Block chords can coexist**: As in the Rihanna example, organ block chords add legato alongside arpeggios
