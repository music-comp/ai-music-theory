# Quick Definition
A 6/9 chord contains a triad plus the 6th and 9th from the major scale, without a 7th; it serves as a common tonic chord in jazz.

# Core Definition
If a chord has both a 6th and a 9th, it is called a 6/9 chord. The major 6/9 uses the major third (C-E-G-A-D) while the minor 6/9 uses the minor third (C-Eb-G-A-D). The "6" always comes from the major scale unless otherwise specified. The 6/9 chord is a common tonic chord in jazz, particularly Cm6/9 as the resolution chord in the ii-V-i progression in minor (Hutchinson, Ch. 31, p. 447).

# Prerequisites
- **Jazz ninth chords** -- Understanding of 9th as an upper extension

# Key Properties
1. Contains root, 3rd, 5th, 6th, and 9th
2. No 7th is present
3. Major 6/9: major 3rd; Minor 6/9: minor 3rd
4. The 6th comes from the major scale unless otherwise specified
5. Common as a tonic resolution chord

# Construction / Recognition
- Major 6/9: C6/9 = C-E-G-A-D
- Minor 6/9: Cm6/9 = C-Eb-G-A-D
- No 7th; if 7th were present, the 6th becomes a 13th

# Context & Application
The 6/9 chord is a common alternative to major 7th or minor-major 7th as tonic. In the ii-V-i in minor, the resolution is typically Cm6/9. The chord's warmth without 7th tension makes it ideal for endings.

# Examples
- C6/9 = C-E-G-A-D (major 6/9, p. 447)
- Cm6/9 = C-Eb-G-A-D (minor 6/9)
- Minor ii-V-i: Dm7(b5)-G7alt-Cm6/9 (p. 454)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Including a 7th in a 6/9 chord
  **Correction**: 6/9 chords have no 7th; adding a 7th turns the 6th into a 13th

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the minor 6/9 has a minor 6th
  **Clarification**: The 6th always comes from the major scale (A natural in Cm6/9, not Ab); only the 3rd is minor
