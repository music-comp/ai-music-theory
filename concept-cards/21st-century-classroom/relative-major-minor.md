# Quick Definition
Relative major and minor keys share the same key signature but have different tonic notes; the relative minor is three half steps below the relative major.

# Formal Definition
Relative keys are major and minor keys that share the same key signature. The relative minor is found three half steps below (a minor third below) the relative major. Conversely, the relative major is three half steps above (a minor third above) the relative minor. They use the same set of pitches but center on different tonics.

# Construction/Recognition
- Same key signature, different tonic
- Relative minor = 3 half steps below major tonic
- Relative major = 3 half steps above minor tonic
- The sixth scale degree of major is the tonic of relative minor
- The third scale degree of minor is the tonic of relative major

# Musical Context
Understanding relative keys is essential for recognizing relationships between keys, analyzing modulations, and understanding how the same pitch content can create different musical effects based on which note is treated as tonic.

# Examples
- C major and A minor (no sharps/flats)
- G major and E minor (one sharp)
- F major and D minor (one flat)
- Eb major and C minor (three flats)
- "The relative minor of Eb major is C minor"

# Common Confusions
- Relative keys have the SAME key signature but DIFFERENT tonics
- Parallel keys have the SAME tonic but DIFFERENT key signatures
- Relative minor is always ^6 of the major scale, not ^3
