# Quick Definition
Relative major and minor keys share the same key signature but have different tonic notes; the relative major is three half steps above the relative minor.

# Core Definition
We use the term relative minor when referring to a minor key that has the same key signature as a major key. For example, the relative minor of Eb major is C minor because both have three flats in the key signature. Conversely, one could say the relative major of C minor is Eb major. The relative major is three half steps above the relative minor.

# Prerequisites
- **Key signature**: The shared element between relative keys
- **Natural minor scale**: The minor half of the relationship

# Key Properties
1. Same key signature, different tonic
2. Relative minor = 3 half steps below the major tonic
3. Relative major = 3 half steps above the minor tonic
4. ^6 of the major scale = tonic of the relative minor
5. ^3 of the natural minor scale = tonic of the relative major
6. They share the same pitch content but center on different tonics

# Construction / Recognition
- Same key signature, different tonic
- Relative minor tonic is on ^6 of the major scale
- Relative major tonic is on ^3 of the minor scale
- Distance: a minor third (3 half steps) apart

# Context & Application
Understanding relative keys is essential for recognizing key relationships, analyzing modulations, and understanding how the same pitch content can create different musical effects based on which note serves as tonic. Relative keys are closely related and modulation between them is common.

# Examples
- C major and A minor (no sharps/flats)
- G major and E minor (one sharp)
- F major and D minor (one flat)
- Eb major and C minor (three flats)

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Key signature, natural minor scale

## Enables
- Understanding key relationships, relative key modulation

## Related
- Parallel major/minor (same tonic, different key signature -- opposite concept)
- Circle of fifths (minor keys on the inner ring share signatures with major keys on the outer ring)

## Contrasts With
- Parallel major/minor (parallel = same tonic, different key signature; relative = different tonic, same key signature)

# Common Errors
- Confusing relative with parallel (opposite concepts)
- Saying the relative minor is ^3 of the major scale (it is ^6)

# Common Confusions
- **Relative vs. parallel**: Relative = same key signature, different tonic; Parallel = same tonic, different key signature
- **^6, not ^3**: The relative minor tonic is the 6th scale degree of the major scale
