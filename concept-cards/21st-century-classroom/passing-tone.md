# Quick Definition
A passing tone is a non-chord tone approached by step and left by step in the same direction, filling in the space between two chord tones.

# Formal Definition
Passing tones are notes that pass between chord tones. They are approached by step and left by step in the same direction (either ascending or descending). Passing tones can be accented (on the beat) or unaccented (off the beat), diatonic or chromatic.

# Analysis/Application
To identify passing tones:
1. Look for notes moving stepwise between two chord tones
2. Confirm the approach and departure are in the same direction
3. Label as PT (or P.T.)
4. Add qualifiers: accented/unaccented, chromatic if applicable

# Musical Context
Passing tones are among the most common non-chord tones, creating smooth melodic connections between chord tones. They can be diatonic (using notes from the key) or chromatic (using notes outside the key).

# Examples
- "Want to Want Me" (2015) - unaccented passing tone in bar 1, accented passing tone in bar 3
- "As Time Goes By" (1931) - two consecutive diatonic passing tones descending from root to fifth
- Thelonious Monk, "Blue Monk" (1954) - chromatic passing tones

# Common Confusions
- Two consecutive passing tones can fill in a larger interval (like root to fifth)
- Accented passing tones occur on the beat and can be quite expressive
- Chromatic passing tones add chromaticism without implying a chord change
