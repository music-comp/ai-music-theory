# Quick Definition
The cantus firmus (c.f.) is a pre-existing melody in whole notes against which counterpoint is written in species counterpoint exercises.

# Core Definition
The cantus firmus (Latin for "fixed song," abbreviated "c.f.") is a given melody used as the foundation for species counterpoint exercises. It consists entirely of whole notes and remains unchanged while the student writes counterpoint against it. The cantus firmus may be placed in either the upper or lower voice, and the rules for the added counterpoint change depending on its position (Hutchinson, Ch. 30, p. 424).

# Prerequisites
- **Intervals** -- The counterpoint is measured in intervallic distance from the cantus firmus
- **Scales and modes** -- The c.f. determines the mode (Dorian, Phrygian, Mixolydian, Aeolian, etc.)

# Key Properties
1. Written entirely in whole notes
2. Remains fixed and unaltered throughout the exercise
3. May be placed in either the upper or lower voice
4. Determines the mode of the exercise
5. Typically moves primarily by step with limited leaps
6. Begins and ends on the final (tonic) of the mode
7. Historically derived from liturgical chant; in pedagogy, composed for the exercise

# Construction / Recognition
**To identify a cantus firmus:**
1. Look for the voice written entirely in whole notes
2. It will not change throughout the exercise
3. It usually occupies one voice while the other voice provides counterpoint

**Position affects rules:**
- When c.f. is in the lower voice: write consonant intervals above
- When c.f. is in the upper voice: write consonant intervals (unison, 3rd, 5th, 6th) below

# Context & Application
Species counterpoint derived from Renaissance practice where composers added voices to existing chant melodies. The term "cantus firmus" originally referred to these chant melodies. In Fux's pedagogical method, simple melodies serve the same structural purpose. The concept of writing against a fixed element is foundational to understanding how independent voices relate to each other.

# Examples
- A c.f. in the source (p. 424) is provided as a simple whole-note melody against which students write first species counterpoint
- The c.f. determines whether leading tones must be raised (e.g., F-sharp in G Mixolydian, C-sharp in D Dorian, G-sharp in A Aeolian at the final cadence)
- A typical c.f. in D Dorian: D-E-F-G-A-G-F-E-D

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Intervals** -- C.f. exercises are measured by intervallic distance
- **Modal scales** -- C.f. establishes the mode for the exercise

## Enables
- **First through fifth species** -- All species are written against a cantus firmus

## Related
- **Species counterpoint** -- The cantus firmus is central to the species method

## Contrasts With
- **Fugue subject** -- A fugue subject is a theme that enters in different voices and keys; a c.f. is fixed in one voice throughout

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Altering the cantus firmus during the exercise
  **Correction**: The c.f. is fixed and must not be changed
- **Error**: Writing the counterpoint in the wrong relationship to the c.f. position
  **Correction**: Rules differ depending on whether c.f. is above or below; check position first

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the cantus firmus and the counterpoint have the same rules
  **Clarification**: The c.f. is given; only the counterpoint voice follows species-specific rules
- **Confusion**: Confusing cantus firmus with fugue subject
  **Clarification**: A c.f. stays in one voice throughout; a fugue subject migrates between voices and keys
