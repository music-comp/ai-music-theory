# Quick Definition
Syncopation occurs when notes on weak beats or weak parts of beats are emphasized while nearby strong beats are de-emphasized.

# Formal Definition
Syncopation is a rhythmic device where accents occur on normally weak beats or weak parts of beats, while strong beats are obscured or de-emphasized. This creates a sense of rhythmic tension and "pull" against the regular metric structure. Syncopation commonly involves ties across strong beats.

# Construction/Recognition
- Accents on weak beats or offbeats
- Strong beats (1 and 3 in 4/4) obscured by ties or rests
- Notes held across bar lines or beat boundaries
- Creates a "push" against the regular pulse
- Common in jazz, rock, pop, Latin, and African-derived music

# Musical Context
Syncopation is essential to popular music styles and creates rhythmic interest and groove. It gives music forward momentum and energy by disrupting the expected metric patterns. Syncopated rhythms often feel "catchy" or danceable.

# Examples
- A note tied from beat 4 to beat 1 of the next measure
- Accenting "and" of beat 2 instead of beat 2 itself
- The rhythm in "Eleanor Rigby" (Beatles) features beat-level syncopation
- Off-beat chord hits in reggae and ska

# Common Confusions
- Syncopation is acceptable notation even when it "hides" beats
- Common syncopation patterns don't need to be written out fully with ties
- Syncopation at the beat level differs from syncopation at the division level
