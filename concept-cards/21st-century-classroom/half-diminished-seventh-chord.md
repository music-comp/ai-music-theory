# Quick Definition
A half-diminished seventh chord consists of a diminished triad with a minor seventh above the root, also called a minor seven flat five (m7b5).

# Formal Definition
A half-diminished seventh chord is built with a diminished triad (m3 + m3) plus a minor seventh interval from root to seventh. The intervals stacked are: m3 (root to 3rd), m3 (3rd to 5th), M3 (5th to 7th). It is "half" diminished because only the triad is diminished; the seventh is a minor seventh, not a diminished seventh.

# Construction/Recognition
- Diminished triad + minor 7th from root
- Intervals: m3 + m3 + M3
- Total: 10 half steps from root to 7th
- Lead-sheet: Cm7(b5), Cø7, Cmin7(b5)
- Roman numeral: viiø7 (major), iiø7 (minor)

# Musical Context
The half-diminished seventh chord occurs diatonically on ^7 in major keys (viiø7) and on ^2 in minor keys (iiø7). In minor keys, the iiø7-V7-i progression is standard. It has a darker, more tense quality than the minor seventh chord.

# Examples
- Bø7: B-D-F-A (in C major: viiø7)
- Dø7: D-F-Ab-C (in C minor: iiø7)
- "Half" because the 7th is minor, not diminished
- Lead-sheet symbol has slashed circle (ø)

# Related Concepts

# Common Confusions
- "Half" diminished has a minor 7th; "fully" diminished has a diminished 7th
- Bø7 is different from Bo7 (different seventh)
- Also called m7(b5) or "minor seven flat five"
