# Quick Definition
Each chromatic chord type has a distinctive identifier: secondary dominants have major/Mm7 quality, secondary diminished have diminished quality, mode mixture has lowered accidentals, the Neapolitan is bII, and augmented sixth chords contain a diminished third when stacked in thirds.

# Core Definition
When analyzing chromatic harmonies, specific features help identify each chord type. Secondary dominants are identified by QUALITY (major triad or Mm7). Secondary diminished chords are identified by QUALITY (diminished, half-diminished, or fully diminished). Mode mixture is identified by ACCIDENTALS (lowered notes: b6, b3, b7). The Neapolitan is identified as bII (major triad on lowered 2). Augmented sixth chords are identified by the diminished third interval when stacked in thirds.

# Prerequisites
- All five chromatic chord categories must be understood individually

# Key Properties
| Chromatic Type | Key Identifier | Labels |
|---|---|---|
| Secondary Dominant | QUALITY: M or Mm7 | V/x, V7/x |
| Secondary Diminished | QUALITY: dim, o7, o7 | viio/x, viio7/x, viio7/x |
| Mode Mixture | ACCIDENTALS: lowered | iio, iio7, iv, viio7, bVI, etc. |
| Neapolitan | bII | N, N6 |
| Augmented Sixth | diminished 3rd | It+6, Fr+6, Ger+6, EnGer+6 |

# Construction / Recognition
Analysis process for any chromatic chord:
1. Stack the chord in thirds to identify root and quality
2. Check for a diminished third -- if present, it is an augmented sixth chord
3. If quality is major or Mm7: check P5 below root for secondary dominant
4. If quality is diminished type: check m2 above root for secondary diminished
5. Look for lowered scale degrees without secondary function: mode mixture
6. Check for bII: Neapolitan
7. Consider function and resolution to confirm

# Context & Application
This systematic approach prevents misidentification of chromatic chords. The most important first step is always stacking in thirds to determine quality. The diminished third is the most reliable single identifier (for augmented sixths). Quality alone distinguishes secondary dominants from secondary diminished chords. Mode mixture and Neapolitan share the feature of lowered accidentals but are distinguished by specific scale degrees (bII for Neapolitan vs. other lowered degrees for mixture).

# Examples
- A chromatic Mm7 chord: could be secondary dominant OR Ger+6 -- check for diminished third to distinguish
- A chord with b6: could be mode mixture (bVI, iv) or augmented sixth -- check for #4 to distinguish

# Common Errors
- Not checking for the diminished third first (missing augmented sixth chords)
- Confusing Ger+6 with a secondary dominant (both are Mm7 quality)

# Common Confusions
- **Ger+6 vs. V7**: Both are Mm7 quality -- the diminished third test distinguishes them
- **Mode mixture vs. Neapolitan**: Both have lowered accidentals -- bII specifically = Neapolitan
- **Order matters**: Check quality first, then apply the appropriate interval test
