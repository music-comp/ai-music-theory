# Quick Definition

The key to voice leading augmented sixth chords is that #4 almost always resolves upward to ^5, except when resolving to V7 where #4 moves to natural-4 (the 7th of V7). The augmented 6th interval (b6 to #4) expands outward to an octave on ^5.

# Core Definition

The salient accidental in all three augmented sixth chords is #4, which almost always resolves upward to ^5. Resolution depends on the target chord (Hutchinson, Ch. 29, pp. 419-421):
- **To V or cadential 6/4**: #4 resolves UP to ^5; b6 resolves DOWN to ^5 (augmented 6th expands to octave)
- **To V7**: #4 moves to natural-4 (becoming the 7th of V7)
- **Enharmonic German +6 to major I6/4**: both #2 and #4 resolve upward

# Key Properties

1. #4 resolves UP to ^5 (standard resolution)
2. b6 resolves DOWN to ^5
3. The augmented 6th interval (b6 to #4) expands outward to an octave on ^5
4. Exception: to V7, #4 moves to natural-4 (not up to ^5)
5. Enharmonic German: both #2 and #4 resolve upward
6. For resolutions to other chords: move smoothly, avoid parallels

# Construction / Recognition

## Standard Resolution (to V or cadential 6/4):
1. #4 goes UP to ^5
2. b6 goes DOWN to ^5
3. Other voices move smoothly

## Resolution to V7:
1. #4 goes to natural-4 (becomes 7th of V7)
2. b6 goes DOWN to ^5

## Enharmonic German to major I6/4:
1. #2 resolves UP
2. #4 resolves UP
3. Both chromatic notes move to members of I6/4

- **Historical/stylistic notes**: The outward expansion of the augmented 6th to an octave creates a satisfying resolution

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Resolving #4 DOWN instead of UP
  **Correction**: #4 almost always resolves UP (the only exception is to V7 where it moves to natural-4)

- **Error**: Forgetting the V7 exception
  **Correction**: When resolving to V7, #4 becomes natural-4, not ^5

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: The augmented 6th interval contracts inward
  **Clarification**: The augmented 6th EXPANDS outward to an octave (b6 goes down, #4 goes up, both arriving at ^5)

- **Confusion**: The enharmonic German is voice led the same as the standard German
  **Clarification**: The enharmonic German has #2 (not b3), and both #2 and #4 resolve upward to I6/4
