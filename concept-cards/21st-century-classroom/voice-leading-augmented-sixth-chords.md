# Quick Definition
The key to voice leading augmented sixth chords is that sharp-4 almost always resolves upward to scale degree 5, except when resolving to V7 where sharp-4 moves to natural-4 (the 7th of V7).

# Formal Definition
Augmented sixth chords (Italian, French, German, and Enharmonic German) all contain the characteristic interval of an augmented sixth between flat-6 and sharp-4. The sharp-4 (raised fourth scale degree) is the primary tendency tone and resolves according to the chord of resolution:
- **To V or cadential 6/4**: sharp-4 resolves UP to scale degree 5
- **To V7**: sharp-4 moves to natural-4 (becoming the 7th of V7)
- **Enharmonic German to major I-6/4**: Both sharp-2 and sharp-4 resolve upward

# Rules/Procedures
1. **Resolution to V**: sharp-4 resolves UP to 5; flat-6 resolves DOWN to 5
2. **Resolution to cadential 6/4**: Same as above - sharp-4 up to 5
3. **Resolution to V7**: Exception - sharp-4 moves to natural-4 (7th of V7)
4. **Enharmonic German (+6) to major I-6/4**:
   - Contains flat-6, 1, sharp-2, and sharp-4
   - Both sharp-2 and sharp-4 resolve upward
5. **Resolution to other chords**: Move all voices smoothly, avoid parallels, use proper doublings
6. Flat-6 typically resolves down to 5

# Musical Context
Augmented sixth chords are chromatic pre-dominant chords that create powerful drive toward the dominant. The augmented sixth interval expands outward to an octave on scale degree 5, creating a satisfying resolution.

# Examples
- Italian +6 to V in A minor: F-A-D# resolves with D# going to E, F going to E
- German +6 to V7: The D# would move to D natural (7th of V7)
- Enharmonic German +6 to A major cadential 6/4: D# and F## both resolve upward

# Common Confusions
- Forgetting the exception when resolving to V7 (sharp-4 goes to natural-4, not up to 5)
- Not recognizing that the augmented 6th interval (flat-6 to sharp-4) expands to an octave
- Confusing resolution of Enharmonic German (both chromatic notes resolve up)
- Trying to resolve sharp-4 down instead of up
