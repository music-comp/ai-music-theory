# Quick Definition
Non-chord tones are notes that do not belong to the current chord, classified by how they are approached and left (by same tone, step, or leap).

# Formal Definition
Non-chord tones (also called "embellishing tones" or "non-harmonic tones") are notes that are not members of the prevailing harmony. There are nine types, each defined by its approach and resolution: passing tone, neighbor tone, appoggiatura, escape tone, double neighbor, anticipation, pedal point, suspension, and retardation.

# Analysis/Application
To identify non-chord tones:
1. Determine the chord being played
2. Identify notes that are not members of that chord
3. Examine how the non-chord tone is approached (step, leap, or same note)
4. Examine how the non-chord tone is left (step, leap, or same note)
5. Match the pattern to the appropriate non-chord tone type

# Musical Context
Non-chord tones add melodic interest, create tension and release, and embellish the underlying harmony. They appear in all styles of Western music from Renaissance to contemporary pop.

# Examples
Qualifiers that can be applied to non-chord tones:
- Accented: occurring on the beat
- Unaccented: occurring on the weak part of the beat (offbeat)
- Chromatic: not belonging to the key
- Metrical: equal to the duration of the beat
- Sub-metrical: smaller than the beat
- Super-metrical: larger than the beat

# Common Confusions
- Distinguish between the chord tone and the embellishment - always identify the chord first
- Some notes may have multiple valid interpretations
- Chromatic non-chord tones vs. chromatic chord tones (secondary dominants)
