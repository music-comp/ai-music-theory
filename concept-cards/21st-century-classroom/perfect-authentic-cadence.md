# Quick Definition
A perfect authentic cadence (PAC) is the most conclusive type of authentic cadence, requiring three conditions: V-I motion, both chords in root position, and the tonic scale degree in the highest voice of the final chord.

# Core Definition
The Perfect Authentic Cadence must meet three requirements: (1) V-I progression; (2) Both chords in root position; (3) Tonic scale degree (1-hat) in the highest voice of the tonic chord. If any requirement is not met (inverted chord, non-tonic in soprano, or viio instead of V), the cadence is an Imperfect Authentic Cadence (IAC).

# Prerequisites
- **Cadence**: Understanding of phrase-ending chord progressions

# Key Properties
1. V-I progression (not viio-I)
2. Both V and I in root position
3. Tonic (1-hat) in the highest voice of I chord
4. Most conclusive cadence type
5. If any condition fails, it is an IAC instead

# Construction / Recognition
1. Confirm V-I (not viio-I)
2. Check that both V and I are in root position
3. Verify that 1-hat is in the soprano/highest voice of the I chord
4. If all three conditions are met, label as PAC; otherwise IAC

# Context & Application
The PAC is the most conclusive cadence in tonal music. It typically ends the consequent phrase in periods and concludes larger formal sections. Understanding PAC vs. IAC is essential for analyzing period structure -- periods require a "less conclusive then more conclusive" cadential relationship.

# Examples
- Mozart, K. 331, I -- PAC at the end of the consequent phrase
- Haydn, Piano Sonata Hob. XVI:9, III -- IAC then PAC creating period
- Beethoven, "Fur Elise" -- IAC then PAC creating period structure

# Common Errors
- Labeling viio-I as PAC (it is always IAC)
- Not checking the soprano note of the I chord

# Common Confusions
- **viio-I is always IAC**: Even if both chords are in root position with tonic in soprano
- **Inverted V or I = IAC**: Both must be in root position
- **Soprano must have 1-hat**: Having 3-hat or 5-hat in soprano makes it IAC
