# Quick Definition
A perfect authentic cadence (PAC) is the most conclusive type of authentic cadence, requiring V-I motion with both chords in root position and the tonic scale degree (^1) in the highest voice of the final chord.

# Formal Definition
The Perfect Authentic Cadence must meet three requirements: (1) V-I progression; (2) Both chords in root position; (3) Tonic scale degree (^1) in the highest voice of the tonic chord. If any requirement is not met, the cadence is an Imperfect Authentic Cadence (IAC).

# Analysis/Application
To identify a PAC:
1. Confirm V-I (not vii°-I)
2. Check that both V and I are in root position
3. Verify that ^1 is in the soprano/highest voice of the I chord
4. If all three conditions are met, label as PAC

# Musical Context
The PAC is the most conclusive cadence in tonal music. It typically ends phrases, periods, and larger formal sections. Understanding PAC vs. IAC is essential for analyzing period structure.

# Examples
- Mozart, K. 331, I - PAC at the end of the antecedent phrase
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in F Major, Hob. XVI:9, III - IAC followed by PAC
- Beethoven, "Fur Elise" - IAC then PAC creating period structure

# Common Confusions
- vii°-I is an IAC, not a PAC (even if other conditions are met)
- Inverted V or I creates an IAC
- Having ^3 or ^5 in the soprano creates an IAC
