# Quick Definition
Strophic form is a song structure where the same music repeats for each verse of text, with only the lyrics changing between repetitions.

# Formal Definition
In strophic form (also called verse-repeating form or AAA form), the same musical section repeats multiple times with different lyrics for each repetition. This is one of the simplest formal structures and was common in folk music and art song before the development of more complex forms.

# Analysis/Application
When analyzing strophic form:
1. Identify the repeating musical material
2. Note how many strophes (verses) occur
3. Look for any variations between strophes
4. The form can be labeled AAA... (one A for each strophe)

# Musical Context
Strophic form is common in folk songs, hymns, and some art songs (Lieder). While simpler than verse-chorus or AABA forms, it allows focus on text-setting and lyrical content.

# Examples
- Many folk songs and hymns
- Some art songs (Lieder)
- Represents the simplest form of repeating verse structure

# Related Concepts
- Verse-chorus form
- AABA form
- Modified strophic form
- Through-composed form

# Common Confusions
- Different from verse-chorus (no contrasting chorus section)
- Modified strophic form has slight variations between strophes
- Through-composed is the opposite (no repeated sections)
