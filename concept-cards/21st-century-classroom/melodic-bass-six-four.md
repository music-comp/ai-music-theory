# Quick Definition
A melodic bass six-four occurs when the bass voice carries the melody and the fifth of a chord happens to appear in this melodic bass line, resulting in a second-inversion chord as a byproduct of melodic motion rather than voice-leading convention.

# Core Definition
The melodic bass six-four is the fourth approved context for second-inversion chords in classical music. In this situation, the bass voice has the melody; when the fifth of the chord occurs in this bass voice melody, the result will be a 6/4 chord. Unlike the cadential, passing, and pedal types, which are defined by specific bass-line patterns, the melodic bass 6/4 is defined by the melodic function of the bass voice. The 6/4 chord arises as a natural consequence of the bass carrying thematic material.

# Prerequisites
- **Six-four chord types**: The broader category of approved 6/4 contexts

# Key Properties
1. Bass voice carries the melody (thematic material)
2. The fifth of a chord appears in the melodic bass line
3. Results in a 6/4 chord as a byproduct of melody
4. Not defined by a specific bass-line pattern (unlike the other three types)
5. The 6/4 is incidental to the melodic function
6. Less common than the other three types

# Construction / Recognition
- The bass voice is clearly carrying melodic/thematic material
- A 6/4 chord appears because the melody passes through the fifth of a chord
- The 6/4 is not approached by stepwise motion (passing) or repeated notes (pedal)
- Context of the bass as melody-carrier is essential for identification

# Context & Application
The melodic bass six-four is the least common and least rule-bound of the four types. It occurs when compositional priorities (placing the melody in the bass) override the normal restrictions on 6/4 chords. This situation arises in passages where the bass has thematic material, such as in cello or bassoon melodies accompanied by upper voices.

# Examples
- Any passage where the bass voice carries the melody and the fifth of a chord occurs melodically in the bass

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Six-four chord restrictions with melodic exception

## Enables
- Freedom for bass to carry melodic material without inversion restrictions

## Related
- passing-six-four, pedal-six-four (the other embellishing 6/4 types)

## Contrasts With
- cadential-six-four (which has dominant function)
- The other three types (which have specific bass-line patterns)

# Common Errors
- Using "melodic bass" as justification for any 6/4 chord (the bass must genuinely carry a melody)
- Confusing with passing 6/4 when the bass moves stepwise in a melodic passage

# Common Confusions
- **Melody in the bass**: The bass must be carrying recognizable thematic/melodic material
- **Incidental**: The 6/4 is a byproduct of the melody, not a voice-leading goal
- **Least rule-bound**: Unlike the other three types, this one is defined by context rather than pattern
