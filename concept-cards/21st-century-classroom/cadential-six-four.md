# Quick Definition
The cadential six-four (I6/4) is a second-inversion tonic chord that regularly precedes the V chord in cadences, having dominant function rather than tonic function.

# Formal Definition
The "cadential six-four" (cadential 6/4) refers to the I6/4 that regularly proceeds the V chord in cadences. Despite having a tonic triad, it has dominant function, just like the V chord that follows it. Previously called "I/5th" in modified slash notation.

# Analysis/Application
The cadential 6/4:
1. Appears immediately before V in cadences
2. Has the fifth of the I chord (^5) in the bass
3. Functions as dominant preparation, not tonic
4. Often labeled as cad. 6/4 or I6/4 with dominant function
5. Some analysts label it V6/4-5/3 to emphasize its dominant function

# Musical Context
The cadential six-four is one of the most common and important uses of the second-inversion chord in tonal music. It creates a distinctive sound before the dominant at phrase endings.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 331, I - cadential 6/4 before V
- "The Star-Spangled Banner" - cadential 6/4 near the end

# Common Confusions
- Despite containing a I chord, the cadential 6/4 has DOMINANT function
- This is one of four approved uses of 6/4 chords in classical music
- The bass note (^5) is shared with the following V chord
