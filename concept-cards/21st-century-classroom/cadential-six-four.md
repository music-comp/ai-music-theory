# Quick Definition
The cadential six-four (I6/4) is a second-inversion tonic triad that regularly precedes V at cadences, functioning as a dominant preparation rather than as a tonic chord despite containing tonic chord tones.

# Core Definition
The "cadential six-four" (or cadential 6/4) refers to the I6/4 chord that regularly precedes the V chord in cadences. Despite containing tonic triad pitches, the cadential 6/4 has dominant function, just like the V chord that follows it. The fifth of the I chord (scale degree 5) is in the bass, which is shared with the following V chord. Previously called "I/5th" in modified slash notation, this chord is now labeled using figured bass terminology throughout analysis.

# Prerequisites
- **Figured bass inversion symbols**: Understanding that 6/4 means second inversion
- **Triad inversions**: Recognizing second inversion with the fifth in the bass
- **Cadences**: The phrase-ending context where this chord appears

# Key Properties
1. Second-inversion tonic triad (I6/4)
2. Has DOMINANT function, not tonic function
3. Scale degree 5 in the bass (shared with following V)
4. Appears immediately before V in cadences
5. Previously labeled "I/5th" in slash notation
6. Some analysts label it V6/4-5/3 to emphasize dominant function

# Construction / Recognition
- Located at a cadence point, immediately before V
- Bass note is scale degree 5 (same as following V chord)
- Upper voices contain scale degrees 1 and 3 (tonic triad tones)
- The upper voices resolve: 6 moves to 5, 4 moves to 3 (creating V)
- Functions as dominant preparation despite its tonic triad content

# Context & Application
The cadential six-four is one of the most important and common chord usages in tonal music. It creates a distinctive, expectant sound before the dominant at phrase endings. The chord exemplifies how bass position and context override chord content in determining function. It is one of four approved uses of 6/4 chords in classical music, and by far the most frequently encountered.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 331, I -- cadential 6/4 before V
- Francis Scott Key and John Stafford Smith, "The Star-Spangled Banner" -- cadential 6/4 near the end

- Analyzing the cadential 6/4 as having tonic function

# Common Confusions
- **Function vs. content**: Despite containing I chord tones, the cadential 6/4 has DOMINANT function
- **Labeling debate**: Some theorists write V6/4-5/3 instead of I6/4 to show dominant function
- **Bass note**: Scale degree 5 in bass connects the I6/4 to the V that follows
