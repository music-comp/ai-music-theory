# Quick Definition
Spread voicings are five-note jazz chord voicings with root, 3rd, and 7th as the lowest notes, with upper extensions voiced above, often in fourths or fifths.

# Formal Definition
Spread voicings (also called "open" voicings) are a category of jazz chord voicings featuring five notes with the root always as the lowest note. The 3rd and 7th appear among the lowest three voices (with the 3rd either above or below the 7th). The remaining chord members (9th, 11th, 13th, or alterations) are voiced above, often using intervals of a fourth or fifth between upper voices.

# Construction/Calculation
1. Place the root as the bass note
2. Voice the 3rd and 7th next (order: R-3-7 or R-7-3)
3. Add two upper extension notes from the chord symbol
4. Upper voices often span 4ths or 5ths for an open sound
5. The 5th may be omitted to maintain five parts
6. For sus chords: use R-4-7; for 6th chords: use R-3-6

# Musical Context
Spread voicings create the characteristic "big band" or orchestral jazz piano sound. The wide spacing between notes allows each voice to be heard clearly and provides power without muddiness. These voicings work well for comping (accompanying) and for arranging horn sections. The smooth voice leading between spread voicings creates professional-sounding jazz progressions.

# Examples
- Dm9 spread: D (root) - F (3rd) - C (7th) - E-A (9th-5th in 4ths above)
- G13 spread: G (root) - F (7th) - B (3rd) - D-E (5th-13th above)
- CΔ9 spread: C (root) - E (3rd) - B (7th) - D-G (9th-5th above)

# Common Confusions
- Root is always the lowest note in spread voicings (not necessarily in close voicings)
- The 3rd can be above or below the 7th in the lower voices
- Omitting the 5th is common and acceptable to maintain five voices
- Different from "open position" in classical harmony
