# Quick Definition
Eleventh chords extend beyond the ninth by adding the 11th (the 4th scale degree an octave higher), creating a six-note chord structure common in jazz.

# Formal Definition
An eleventh chord includes the root, 3rd, 5th, 7th, 9th, and 11th. All numbers up to the highest number are assumed present when a chord symbol shows "11." The 11th comes from the major scale unless otherwise specified (i.e., it is a perfect 11th - the same as a perfect 4th an octave higher).

# Construction/Calculation
1. Build the complete ninth chord (root, 3rd, 5th, 7th, 9th)
2. Add the 11th (perfect 4th + octave above root)
3. In practice, the unaltered 11th often clashes with the major 3rd (they are a minor 9th apart)
4. Composers/arrangers often omit the unaltered 11th or alter it to #11 when the chord has a major 3rd
5. For analysis, specify which notes are included: C9(11) indicates C-E-G-Bb-D-F with the 11th but without the 13th

# Musical Context
Eleventh chords appear frequently in jazz, though the natural 11th is often avoided on major and dominant chords due to its clash with the major 3rd. The #11 is more commonly used on these chord types. Minor 11th chords (where the 11th doesn't clash) are very common in jazz.

# Examples
- C11 = C-E-G-Bb-D-F (all chord members up to 11th)
- Cm11 = C-Eb-G-Bb-D-F (minor 11th - no clash between 11th and minor 3rd)
- CΔ11 = C-E-G-B-D-F (major 11th - though the natural 11th clashes)
- C7(#11) = C-E-G-Bb-D-F# (altered 11th avoids clash)

# Common Confusions
- "11" vs "sus": label the 4th as "sus" only if the 3rd is NOT present; if the 3rd IS present, label the 4th as "11"
- C7sus has C-F-G-Bb (no 3rd), while C11 has C-E-G-Bb-D-F (includes 3rd)
- The unaltered 11th clashing with the major 3rd is a key reason many jazz musicians avoid C11 and use C7(#11) instead
