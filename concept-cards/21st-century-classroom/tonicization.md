# Quick Definition
Tonicization means treating a chord other than the I chord like a tonic by approaching it with its dominant, creating a momentary sense of a new key without actually modulating.

# Formal Definition
Tonicization means treating a chord other than the I chord like a tonic by approaching it with its dominant. Unlike modulation, tonicization is temporary - the music quickly returns to the original key. Secondary dominants and secondary diminished chords are the primary tools for tonicization.

# Analysis/Application
Tonicization process:
1. The secondary dominant creates expectation for a new "tonic"
2. The chromatic note acts as leading tone to the tonicized chord's root
3. Resolution to the target chord completes the tonicization
4. The original key reasserts itself shortly after

# Musical Context
Tonicization adds harmonic color and creates stronger motion to important chords without the commitment of a full modulation. It's especially common for tonicizing V and ii.

# Examples
- V/V (tonicizing the dominant) - extremely common
- V/ii (tonicizing the supertonic) - strengthens ii-V-I motion
- V/vi (tonicizing the submediant) - common in sequences

# Common Confusions
- Tonicization is temporary; modulation is more permanent
- Diminished chords cannot be tonicized (vii° and ii°)
- The chromaticism intensifies the drive to the target chord
