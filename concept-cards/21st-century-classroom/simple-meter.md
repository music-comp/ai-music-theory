# Quick Definition
Simple meter is any meter where the beats naturally divide into two equal parts.

# Formal Definition
In simple meter, each beat divides naturally into two equal parts. The top number of a simple time signature indicates the number of beats per measure (2, 3, or 4), and the bottom number indicates which note value receives one beat. The division of the beat is twice as fast as the beat note value.

# Construction/Recognition
- Top numbers: 2, 3, or 4
- Beats divide into two parts
- Bottom number = the beat unit
- 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 are common simple meters
- 2/2, 3/2, 4/2 and 2/8, 3/8, 4/8 are also simple

# Musical Context
Simple meter is the most common type of meter in Western music. The two-part division of the beat feels natural and is found in most popular music, marches, and much classical music.

# Examples
- 4/4 (simple quadruple): 4 quarter-note beats, each divides into 2 eighths
- 3/4 (simple triple): 3 quarter-note beats, each divides into 2 eighths
- 2/4 (simple duple): 2 quarter-note beats, each divides into 2 eighths
- 2/2 (simple duple): 2 half-note beats, each divides into 2 quarters

# Common Confusions
- The bottom number shows the beat in simple meter (but NOT in compound meter)
- Simple doesn't mean "easy"--it means beats divide by two
- 3/4 is simple triple, not compound
