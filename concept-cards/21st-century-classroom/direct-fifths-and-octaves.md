# Quick Definition
Direct (or hidden) fifths and octaves occur when two voices approach a perfect 5th or 8ve by similar motion; in species counterpoint, these are forbidden at barlines.

# Core Definition
Direct fifths and direct octaves (also called "hidden fifths" and "hidden octaves") occur when two voices move in similar motion (same direction) to arrive at a perfect fifth or octave. In species counterpoint, perfect consonances must be approached by contrary motion across barlines, which effectively prohibits direct 5ths and 8ves at these structural points. In third species specifically, direct 5ths and 8ves "are not allowed and occur when an 8ve or 5th on a downbeat is approached through similar motion" (Hutchinson, Ch. 30, p. 428).

# Key Properties
1. Two voices approach a perfect 5th or 8ve by similar motion (same direction)
2. Less severe than parallel 5ths/8ves (which require the same interval before and after)
3. Forbidden when crossing barlines in species counterpoint
4. Perfect consonances must be approached by contrary motion at structural points
5. Imperfect consonances (3rds, 6ths) are not subject to this restriction
6. The rule applies in all species when moving to a downbeat

# Construction / Recognition
**To identify direct 5ths/8ves:**
1. Find any perfect 5th or 8ve on a downbeat
2. Check the direction of both voices approaching that interval
3. If both voices move in the same direction (similar motion), it is a direct 5th or 8ve
4. This is prohibited in species counterpoint

**To avoid direct 5ths/8ves:**
- Use contrary motion when approaching any perfect consonance across a barline
- Imperfect consonances on downbeats may be approached freely

# Context & Application
The prohibition of direct fifths and octaves varies by style period. In strict Renaissance counterpoint (species), they are forbidden when approaching structural points. In later SATB style, the rule is often relaxed for inner voices but maintained between outer voices (soprano and bass). The rule preserves voice independence by preventing voices from "arriving together" at acoustically prominent intervals.

# Examples
- Direct 5th: both voices ascending to arrive at a perfect 5th on the downbeat (p. 428)
- Direct 8ve: both voices moving in similar motion to reach an octave on the downbeat
- Third species (p. 428): explicitly forbidden when approaching downbeats by similar motion

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Approaching a perfect 5th or 8ve on a downbeat by similar motion
  **Correction**: Use contrary motion to approach all perfect consonances at barlines

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing direct with parallel 5ths/8ves
  **Clarification**: Parallel requires the same interval type before AND after; direct only requires similar motion approaching the interval
- **Confusion**: Applying the rule too broadly to all contexts
  **Clarification**: In species counterpoint, the rule primarily applies when crossing barlines; in SATB, it varies by voice pairing and style
