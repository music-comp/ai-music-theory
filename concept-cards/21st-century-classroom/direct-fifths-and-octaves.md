# Quick Definition
Direct (or hidden) fifths and octaves occur when two voices approach a perfect 5th or 8ve by similar motion; in species counterpoint, these are forbidden when approaching perfect consonances across barlines.

# Formal Definition
Direct fifths and direct octaves (also called "hidden fifths" and "hidden octaves") occur when two voices move in similar (same direction) motion to arrive at a perfect fifth or octave. While not as severe as parallel fifths and octaves (which require the same interval before AND after), direct fifths and octaves can still weaken voice independence. In species counterpoint, perfect consonances must be approached by contrary motion across barlines, effectively prohibiting direct 5ths and 8ves at these structural points.

# Rules/Procedures
1. Direct 5th: Two voices approach a P5 by similar motion
2. Direct 8ve: Two voices approach an 8ve (or unison) by similar motion
3. **In species counterpoint**:
   - Perfect consonances (1, 5, 8, 12) must be approached by CONTRARY motion
   - This rule applies when crossing barlines
   - Effectively prohibits direct 5ths and 8ves at barlines
4. **In SATB voice leading**:
   - Rules vary by style; often allowed in inner voices
   - Usually prohibited in outer voices (soprano-bass)
5. Imperfect consonances (3rds, 6ths) may be approached any way

# Musical Context
The prohibition of direct fifths and octaves varies by style period. In strict Renaissance counterpoint (species), they are forbidden when approaching structural points. In later SATB style, the rule is often relaxed for inner voices but maintained between soprano and bass.

# Examples
- Direct 5th: Soprano moves C to D while bass moves F to G (both up, arriving at 5th)
- Direct 8ve: Alto moves E to G while bass moves G to G (alto up, arriving at 8ve)
- Both would violate the contrary motion rule at barlines

# Common Confusions
- Confusing direct with parallel 5ths/8ves (parallel requires same interval before AND after)
- Applying the rule too strictly to all contexts (varies by style)
- Forgetting that the rule primarily applies when crossing barlines in species
- Not checking for direct intervals in all voice pairings
