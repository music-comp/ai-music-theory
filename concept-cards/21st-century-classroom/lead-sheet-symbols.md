# Quick Definition
Lead-sheet symbols are shorthand chord notations using letters and symbols to indicate the root and quality of a chord, commonly used in jazz and popular music.

# Formal Definition
Lead-sheet symbols (also called chord symbols or lead-sheet notation) communicate chord root and quality using capital letters for the root, optional accidentals, and quality indicators. These symbols allow performers to create their own voicings rather than reading specific notes. The system is standard in jazz, pop, rock, and commercial music.

# Construction/Recognition
- Root: uppercase letter (C, F#, Bb)
- Major triad: letter only (C, F, G)
- Minor triad: letter + "m" (Cm, Am, F#m)
- Diminished: letter + "o" or "dim" (Co, Bdim)
- Augmented: letter + "+" or "aug" (C+, Gaug)

# Musical Context
Lead-sheet symbols are essential for jazz musicians and pop/rock performers who need to read chord changes quickly and create their own voicings. They appear in fake books, chord charts, and above melody lines in sheet music.

# Examples
- C = C major triad (C-E-G)
- Gm = G minor triad (G-Bb-D)
- Do = D diminished triad (D-F-Ab)
- E+ = E augmented triad (E-G#-B#)
- F#m = F# minor triad (F#-A-C#)

# Common Confusions
- No symbol after the letter means major (not "M")
- Lowercase "m" means minor (not the root letter)
- The root letter is always uppercase, even for minor chords
