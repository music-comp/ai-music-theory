# Quick Definition
Verse-chorus form is the most common structure in contemporary popular music, alternating between verses (with changing lyrics) and choruses (containing the memorable "hook" or refrain).

# Formal Definition
In verse-chorus form, the chorus contains the "hook" - the memorable refrain - while the verse typically contains less memorable melodic material with changing lyrics. Common additions include pre-chorus, post-chorus, bridge, introduction, and interlude sections.

# Analysis/Application
When analyzing verse-chorus form:
1. Identify the chorus by its memorable hook and repeated lyrics
2. Identify verses by changing lyrics and typically less prominent melody
3. Note additional sections (pre-chorus, bridge, etc.)
4. Chart the form with time stamps and bar counts

# Musical Context
Verse-chorus form became dominant in rock and pop music from the 1960s onward, largely replacing AABA form. It remains the standard form for contemporary popular music.

# Examples
- "We Are The World" (1985) - clear verse-chorus with bridge: Intro-V1-V2-Chorus-V3-Chorus-Bridge-Chorus-Chorus (key change)-Fade
- "Smells Like Teen Spirit" (1991) - includes pre-chorus and post-chorus sections: Intro-V1-Pre-Chorus-Chorus-Post-Chorus-Interlude-V2-etc.

# Common Confusions
- There may be disagreement about section labels (pre-chorus vs. "verse part b")
- Simple verse-chorus uses same progression for verse and chorus
- Contrasting verse-chorus has different progressions for verse and chorus
