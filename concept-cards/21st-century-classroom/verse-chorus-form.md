# Quick Definition
Verse-chorus form is the most common structure in contemporary popular music, alternating between verses (with changing lyrics) and choruses (containing the memorable "hook" or refrain).

# Core Definition
In verse-chorus form, the chorus contains the "hook" -- the memorable refrain -- while the verse typically contains less memorable melodic material with changing lyrics. Common additions include pre-chorus, post-chorus, bridge, introduction, and interlude. A simple verse-chorus form uses the same harmonic progression for verse and chorus. A contrasting verse-chorus form has different progressions for verse and chorus.

# Prerequisites
- **Phrase**: Understanding of musical sections
- **Cadence**: Understanding of section endings

# Key Properties
1. Chorus contains the "hook" (memorable refrain)
2. Verses have changing lyrics, less prominent melody
3. Simple verse-chorus: same progression for verse and chorus
4. Contrasting verse-chorus: different progressions
5. Optional sections: pre-chorus, post-chorus, bridge, introduction, interlude
6. Sections typically 4, 8, 12, or 16 bars

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the chorus by its memorable hook and repeated lyrics
2. Identify verses by changing lyrics
3. Note additional sections (pre-chorus, bridge, etc.)
4. Chart the form with time stamps and bar counts
5. Determine if simple or contrasting

# Context & Application
Verse-chorus form became dominant in rock and pop from the 1960s onward, largely replacing AABA form. It remains the standard for contemporary popular music. There may be disagreement about labeling sections ("pre-chorus" vs. "verse part b").

# Examples
- "We Are The World" (1985) -- contrasting verse-chorus with bridge
- "Smells Like Teen Spirit" (1991) -- includes pre-chorus, post-chorus, and interlude
- "All About That Bass" -- simple verse-chorus (same progression for verse and chorus)

# Common Errors
- Assuming all verse-chorus forms have bridges (many do not)
- Confusing simple and contrasting types

# Common Confusions
- **Labeling disagreement**: One person's "pre-chorus" may be another's "verse part b"
- **Simple vs. contrasting**: Simple uses same progression; contrasting uses different progressions
- **Bridge vs. pre-chorus**: Different structural roles -- bridge provides contrast after choruses; pre-chorus builds tension before the chorus
