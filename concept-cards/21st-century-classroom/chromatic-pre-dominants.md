# Quick Definition
Chromatic pre-dominant chords expand the pre-dominant function category beyond diatonic ii and IV to include V/V, viio/V, iv, iio7, N6, and the three augmented sixth chords -- all sharing the same basic role of leading to dominant function.

# Core Definition
The study of chromatic harmonies reveals the chromatic expansion of the pre-dominant harmonic function category. In addition to the diatonic pre-dominants (IV, ii), chromatic pre-dominants include: V/V (secondary dominant of V), viio/V (secondary diminished of V), iv (borrowed from minor), iio7 (borrowed from minor), N6 (Neapolitan), and the augmented sixth chords (It+6, Fr+6, Ger+6). All share the same fundamental function: leading to dominant-function chords (V, viio, cadential I6/4).

# Prerequisites
- **Harmonic function**: The concept of tonic, pre-dominant, dominant categories
- **Secondary dominants**: V/V as pre-dominant
- **Mode mixture**: iv and iio7 as borrowed pre-dominants
- **Neapolitan chord**: N6 as chromatic pre-dominant
- **Augmented sixth chords**: It+6, Fr+6, Ger+6 as chromatic pre-dominants

# Key Properties
1. Diatonic pre-dominants: IV, ii
2. Secondary pre-dominants: V/V, viio/V
3. Borrowed pre-dominants: iv, iio7
4. Neapolitan: N6
5. Augmented sixth: It+6, Fr+6, Ger+6
6. All lead to dominant function (V, viio, cad. I6/4)
7. Choice of pre-dominant affects color and intensity
8. Basic phrase model unchanged: Ton. - Pre-Dom. - Dom. - Ton.

# Construction / Recognition
The expanded harmonic flowchart:
- Tonic: I
- Tonic Prolongation: iii, vi
- Pre-Dominant: IV, ii, V/V, viio/V, iv, iio7, N6, It+6, Fr+6, Ger+6
- Dominant: viio, V
- Tonic: I

# Context & Application
All these chords share the same harmonic function despite their different structures and origins. The choice among them affects the color and intensity of the progression without changing the underlying phrase model. Multiple chromatic pre-dominants can appear in sequence, intensifying the approach to the dominant. Understanding their shared function helps organize what might otherwise seem like an overwhelming variety of chromatic chords.

# Examples
- Diatonic: ii-V-I (neutral)
- Neapolitan: N6-V-I (dramatic, Romantic)
- Augmented sixth: Ger+6-I6/4-V-I (intense, chromatic)
- Combined: iv-Ger+6-I6/4-V-I (multiple chromatic pre-dominants)

# Common Errors
- Thinking chromatic pre-dominants change the basic phrase model (they do not)
- Treating augmented sixths as dominant function (they are pre-dominant)

# Common Confusions
- **Same function**: All these chords share pre-dominant function despite different structures
- **V/V dual identity**: V/V is both a secondary dominant and a chromatic pre-dominant
- **Augmented sixth resolution**: +6 chords can resolve to I6/4 (which then resolves to V), maintaining the pre-dominant-to-dominant flow
