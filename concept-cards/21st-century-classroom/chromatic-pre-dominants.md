# Quick Definition
Chromatic pre-dominant chords expand the pre-dominant harmonic function category beyond diatonic ii and IV to include secondary dominants of V, borrowed chords, Neapolitan, and augmented sixth chords.

# Formal Definition
The study of chromatic harmonies reveals the chromatic expansion of the pre-dominant harmonic function category. In addition to the diatonic pre-dominants (IV, ii), chromatic pre-dominants include: V/V (secondary dominant of V), vii°/V (secondary diminished of V), iv (borrowed from minor), ii-half-dim7 (borrowed from minor), N6 (Neapolitan), and augmented sixth chords (It+6, Fr+6, Ger+6).

# Construction/Recognition
Pre-dominant function chords (expanded list):

Diatonic:
- IV (subdominant)
- ii (supertonic)

Chromatic:
- V/V (secondary dominant)
- vii°/V (secondary diminished)
- iv (borrowed subdominant)
- ii-half-dim7 (borrowed supertonic seventh)
- N6 (Neapolitan sixth)
- It+6, Fr+6, Ger+6 (augmented sixth chords)

# Musical Context
- All pre-dominants lead to dominant function chords
- Chromatic pre-dominants intensify the drive to the dominant
- The choice of pre-dominant affects the color and intensity of the progression
- Multiple chromatic pre-dominants can be combined in sequence

# Examples
Harmonic flowchart progression:
Tonic - Tonic Prolongation - Pre-Dominant - Dominant - Tonic
  I         iii, vi           [all above]    vii°, V     I

Pre-dominant choices create different effects:
- ii - V - I (diatonic, neutral)
- N6 - V - I (dramatic, Romantic)
- Ger+6 - V - I (intense, chromatic)

# Common Confusions
- All these chords share the same FUNCTION (pre-dominant) despite different structures
- Chromatic pre-dominants do not change the basic phrase model
- The augmented sixth chords can resolve to I6/4 (which then resolves to V)
- V/V is both a secondary dominant AND a chromatic pre-dominant
