# Quick Definition
The vii diminished seventh chord (fully or half-diminished) consists entirely of tendency tones, requiring special voice leading: when the 7th is above the 3rd, resolve the 3rd (scale degree 2) UP instead of down to avoid parallel fifths.

# Formal Definition
The vii-diminished-7 (vii-fully-diminished-7) and vii-half-diminished-7 chords contain scale degrees 7, 2, 4, and 6, all of which are tendency tones. If all tendency tones resolve normally (7 up, 2-4-6 down), parallel fifths can result depending on the voicing. The solution is: when the 7th of the chord (scale degree 6) is voiced above the 3rd of the chord (scale degree 2), resolve the 3rd UP by step while all other tendency tones resolve normally.

# Rules/Procedures
1. Identify the voicing: Is the 7th (scale degree 6) above or below the 3rd (scale degree 2)?
2. **If 7th is ABOVE the 3rd**:
   - Resolve scale degree 2 (3rd of chord) UP by step to scale degree 3
   - Resolve scale degree 4 down to 3
   - Resolve scale degree 6 down to 5
   - Resolve scale degree 7 up to 8
3. **If 7th is BELOW the 3rd**:
   - Resolve all tendency tones normally (7 up; 2, 4, 6 all down)
4. Alternative solution: Revoice the chord so 7th is below 3rd, then use normal resolution
5. Parallel 4ths are acceptable as an alternative solution

# Musical Context
This special resolution prevents the parallel 5ths that would occur if scale degree 2 resolved down to 1 while scale degree 6 resolved down to 5. The chord's function as a dominant substitute means it resolves to tonic, making careful voice leading essential.

# Examples
- In C major, vii-half-dim-7 = B-D-F-A; if A is above D, resolve D up to E
- vii-dim-7/V resolving to V: Same principle applies to secondary diminished seventh chords
- Exception: vii-dim-7/V to V may use unequal fifths instead of doubled leading tone

# Common Confusions
- Resolving all tendency tones down without checking the voicing (causes parallels)
- Forgetting that the voicing determines which procedure to use
- Not recognizing that scale degree 2 is the 3rd of the chord (not the 2nd)
- Applying this rule to all seventh chords (it only applies to vii-dim-7 types)
