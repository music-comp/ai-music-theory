# Quick Definition

Six-four chords (second-inversion triads) are restricted to three specific contexts: pedal 6/4 (bass stays on same note), passing 6/4 (bass moves by step through the chord), and cadential 6/4 (dominant embellishment resolving to V).

# Core Definition

Three situations where a 6/4 chord is appropriate (Hutchinson, Ch. 26, pp. 398-399):
1. **Pedal 6/4**: bass stays on the same note (I-IV6/4-I or V-I6/4-V)
2. **Passing 6/4**: bass moves by step through the chord (I-V6/4-I6 or IV-I6/4-IV6, and retrogrades)
3. **Cadential 6/4**: functions as dominant embellishment resolving to V or V7

# Prerequisites

- **Second-inversion voice leading** -- 6/4 chords are second-inversion triads
- **Cadential six-four** -- The most important type; reviewed from earlier chapter

# Key Properties

1. Pedal 6/4: bass note = same as surrounding chords
2. Passing 6/4: bass moves stepwise connecting root position to first inversion (or reverse)
3. Cadential 6/4: bass on ^5, resolves to V with 6-5 and 4-3 motion
4. Always double the bass (5th of chord) in all 6/4 types
5. Random use of 6/4 chords sounds stylistically incorrect

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify Six-Four Type:
1. **Pedal**: Does the bass stay on the same note before and after? (e.g., I-IV6/4-I)
2. **Passing**: Does the bass move by step through the 6/4? (e.g., I-V6/4-I6)
3. **Cadential**: Is the bass on ^5, resolving to V at a cadence?
4. If none of these: the 6/4 chord is likely inappropriate

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: Chorale harmonizations, composition
- **Historical/stylistic notes**: The cadential 6/4 is the most important; creates strong dominant preparation at phrase endings

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Using 6/4 chords freely without a specific context
  **Correction**: Every 6/4 must be pedal, passing, or cadential

- **Error**: Forgetting to double the bass in 6/4 chords
  **Correction**: Always double the bass (5th) in all three types

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: The cadential 6/4 is a I chord
  **Clarification**: It functions as a dominant embellishment (the 6 and 4 are suspensions/appoggiaturas resolving to V)
