# Quick Definition
Augmented sixth chords are chromatic pre-dominant chords containing flat-6 and sharp-4, which approach the dominant from a half step above and below simultaneously.

# Formal Definition
Augmented sixth chords are a special class of pre-dominant chords with notes that approach the dominant (5) from a half-step below (sharp-4) and from a half-step above (flat-6) simultaneously. The interval between flat-6 and sharp-4 is an augmented sixth, which resolves outward to an octave on scale degree 5. In minor keys, flat-6 is diatonic and does not require an accidental.

# Construction/Recognition
All augmented sixth chords contain:
- flat-6 (in bass)
- sharp-4 (resolves up to 5)

Recognition principle: When you encounter a chord with a diminished third (when stacked in thirds), it is an augmented sixth chord. The diminished third is the inversion of the augmented sixth.

Three main types:
- Italian (It+6): flat-6, 1, sharp-4
- French (Fr+6): flat-6, 1, 2, sharp-4
- German (Ger+6): flat-6, 1, flat-3, sharp-4

# Musical Context
- Pre-dominant function, intensifying drive to V
- The augmented sixth resolves outward by half steps to an octave on 5
- Common in Classical and Romantic era music
- Also found in jazz and popular music (often spelled as dominant 7th)

# Examples
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 5, Op. 67, I (1808) - Italian +6
- Schubert, Winterreise, "Der Wegweiser" (1823) - French +6
- Rossini, William Tell Overture (1829) - German +6
- John Coltrane, "Mr. P.C." (1959) - German +6 as VI7

# Common Confusions
- The geographic labels (Italian, French, German) have no known historical reasoning
- In lead-sheet analysis, treat as Mm7 chord built on flat-6 (shows sonic quality)
- Classical spelling shows resolution direction (with sharp-4)
- The diminished third (when stacked in thirds) identifies augmented sixth chords
