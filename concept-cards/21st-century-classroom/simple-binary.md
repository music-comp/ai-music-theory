# Quick Definition

Simple binary form lacks both the similar endings of balanced binary and the return of opening material found in rounded binary, often developing initial ideas without literal return.

# Core Definition

"Simple" binary is a term used to describe a binary form that does not have the similar endings of balanced binary or the return of opening material found in rounded binary. The second section often features development of the primary idea from the first section. Both sections can begin similarly, resulting in the large-scale form AA' (Hutchinson, Ch. 24, p. 351).

# Prerequisites

- **Binary form** -- Simple binary is one of the three binary subtypes

# Key Properties

1. No similar endings (unlike balanced binary)
2. No return of opening material after contrast (unlike rounded binary)
3. Second section often develops first section's ideas
4. Both sections may begin similarly (AA')
5. Common in Baroque and early Classical eras

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify Simple Binary:
1. Confirm the piece is in binary form
2. Verify the second section does NOT end with material similar to the first section's ending (rules out balanced)
3. Verify there is no return of opening material after a contrasting passage (rules out rounded)
4. If neither balanced nor rounded features are present: simple binary

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: Baroque dance movements, early Classical pieces
- **Common applications**: Dance suites by Bach, Handel, and contemporaries
- **Historical/stylistic notes**: The development in the second section anticipates later sonata-form development sections

# Examples

**Example 1** (pp. 352-353): J.S. Bach, English Suite No. 3 in G minor, BWV 808, Gavotte I -- simple binary where both sections begin similarly (AA') but the second section develops rather than literally restates

# Relationships

## Builds Upon
- **Binary form** -- Simple binary is the most basic binary subtype

## Enables
- **Sonata form development** -- The developmental quality of the second section in simple binary anticipates sonata-form development

## Related
- **Binary principle** -- Many simple binary forms follow binary principle (tonal journey away and back)

## Contrasts With
- **Rounded binary** -- Rounded has return of opening material; simple does not
- **Balanced binary** -- Balanced has similar endings; simple does not

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Classifying as rounded binary because both sections begin similarly
  **Correction**: Similar beginnings in AA' are different from return after contrast; rounded binary requires contrast THEN return

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking "simple" means the piece is easy or short
  **Clarification**: "Simple" is a technical term distinguishing this subtype from rounded and balanced

- **Confusion**: Believing development in the second section means it cannot be binary
  **Clarification**: Development of first-section ideas is characteristic of simple binary; it does not make the form something else
