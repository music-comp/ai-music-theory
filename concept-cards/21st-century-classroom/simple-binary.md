# Quick Definition
Simple binary form lacks both the similar endings of balanced binary and the return of opening material found in rounded binary, often developing initial ideas without literal return.

# Formal Definition
"Simple" binary is a term used to describe a binary form that does not have the similar endings of balanced binary or the return of opening material found in rounded binary. The second section often features development of the primary idea from the first section, and the sections can begin similarly (resulting in AA' large-scale form).

# Construction/Recognition
Characteristics:
- No similar endings (unlike balanced binary)
- No return of opening material (unlike rounded binary)
- Second section may develop first section's ideas
- Both sections may begin similarly (AA')

Diagram:
| A (or a a') | A' (development of A material) |

Note: Even though sections begin similarly, the lack of a true RETURN after contrast distinguishes this from rounded binary.

# Musical Context
- Found throughout Baroque era
- Common in early Classical era
- The "development" in second section anticipates later sonata form
- Less common than rounded binary in high Classical period

# Examples
- J.S. Bach, English Suite No. 3 in G minor, BWV 808, Gavotte I
- Many Baroque dance movements

# Related Concepts
- Binary form
- Rounded binary
- Balanced binary
- Development (motivic)
- Baroque dance suite

# Common Confusions
- Simple binary CAN have similar beginnings (but no return AFTER contrast)
- The second section develops but does not literally restate
- The term "simple" distinguishes it from rounded and balanced types
- Do not confuse similar beginnings with the "return" of rounded binary
