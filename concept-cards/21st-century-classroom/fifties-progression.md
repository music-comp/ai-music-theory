# Quick Definition
The I-vi-IV-V progression, also called the "'50s progression" or "'50s doo-wop progression," follows the Tonic-Tonic Prolongation-PreDominant-Dominant functional pattern with a characteristic descending-thirds bass line.

# Core Definition
The I-vi-IV-V progression realizes the functional sequence Tonic-Tonic Prolongation-PreDominant-Dominant. The bass line characteristically descends in thirds then rises a step (in C: C-A-F-G). It can be rotated to IV-V-I-vi. A closely related variant, I-vi-ii/3rd-V, produces the same bass line but uses ii instead of IV. This progression represents the full harmonic function cycle.

# Prerequisites
- **Harmonic function**: Understanding of T, TP, PD, D categories
- **Harmonic flowchart**: The diagram governing typical chord progressions

# Key Properties
1. Functional pattern: Tonic - Tonic Prolongation - PreDominant - Dominant
2. Bass line descends in thirds (C-A-F) then rises (F-G)
3. Can be rotated to IV-V-I-vi
4. Variant: I-vi-ii/3rd-V produces the same bass line
5. Typically uses half-note or whole-note harmonic rhythm
6. Often loops continuously

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the I-vi-IV-V pattern or its rotations
2. Listen for the characteristic descending-thirds bass line
3. The progression may appear with half-note or whole-note harmonic rhythm
4. Check for the variant with ii in first inversion replacing IV

# Context & Application
Extremely prevalent in 1950s rock and roll and doo-wop, though the progression was also widely used in the 1930s and 1940s. It remains common in contemporary pop music. In classical music, the variant I-vi-ii/3rd-V appears in works like Beethoven's Pathetique Sonata.

# Examples
- "Heart and Soul" (Carmichael/Loesser) -- C major
- "Earth Angel" (1954) -- Ab major
- Dolly Parton, "I Will Always Love You" (1974) -- A major
- Ramones, "Rock 'n' Roll High School" (1978) -- C major
- John Legend, "All of Me" (2013) -- Ab major, whole-note harmonic rhythm
- MAGIC!, "Rude" (2014) -- rotated to IV-V-I-vi
- Beethoven, Pathetique Sonata, Op. 13, I -- I-vi-ii/3rd-V variant

# Common Errors
- Confusing I-vi-IV-V (50s) with I-V-vi-IV (best-seller) -- different chord orders
- Not recognizing rotations as the same progression

# Common Confusions
- **Different rotations are the same progression**: IV-V-I-vi is a rotation of I-vi-IV-V
- **I-vi-ii/3rd-V has the same bass line**: But uses ii instead of IV
- **Compare with best-seller**: I-vi-IV-V (50s) vs. I-V-vi-IV (best-seller) -- different orders
