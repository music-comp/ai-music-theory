# Quick Definition
Sonatina form is sonata form without the development section, consisting only of exposition and recapitulation.

# Formal Definition
While "sonatina" is sometimes understood to mean a short sonata or an easy sonata for beginners, in terms of form, sonatina form is sonata form without the development section. The exposition is followed directly by the recapitulation. This form is sometimes encountered in slow movements of larger works and in overtures.

# Construction/Recognition
Structure:
| EXPOSITION | RECAPITULATION |
| PT - trans - ST - (CT) | PT - trans - ST - (CT) |
| I --> V | I --> I |

No development section between exposition and recapitulation.

Characteristics:
- All elements of sonata form EXCEPT development
- Still has tonal opposition in exposition
- Still has tonal resolution in recapitulation
- Shorter overall duration

# Musical Context
- Found in slow movements of symphonies
- Common in overtures
- Suitable for lighter or shorter pieces
- Maintains sonata principle without developmental complexity

# Examples
- Tchaikovsky, Nutcracker, "Miniature Overture" (1892):
  - Exposition: PT (0:00), ST (0:49), CT (1:28)
  - Recapitulation: PT (1:40), ST (2:30), CT (3:09)
  - No development section

# Common Confusions
- "Sonatina" as a piece title doesn't always mean sonatina FORM
- Sonatina form still has full exposition and recapitulation
- The absence of development is the defining feature
- The form still follows sonata principle (tonal opposition and resolution)
