# Quick Definition

Sonatina form is sonata form without the development section, consisting only of exposition and recapitulation.

# Core Definition

While "sonatina" is sometimes understood to mean a short sonata or an easy sonata for beginners, in terms of form, sonatina form is sonata form without the development section. It is sometimes encountered in the second, slow movement of a larger work like a symphony, as well as in overtures (Hutchinson, Ch. 25, p. 368).

# Prerequisites

- **Sonata form** -- Sonatina form is defined by its relationship to sonata form (minus the development)

# Key Properties

1. Consists of exposition followed directly by recapitulation
2. No development section
3. Still has tonal opposition in exposition and tonal resolution in recapitulation
4. Still follows sonata principle
5. Shorter overall duration than full sonata form

# Construction / Recognition

## Structure:
| EXPOSITION | RECAPITULATION |
| PT - transition - ST - (CT) | PT - transition - ST - (CT) |
| I --> V | I --> I |

## To Identify:
1. Look for a sonata-form-like structure
2. If there is no development section between exposition and recapitulation: sonatina form

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: Slow movements of symphonies, overtures
- **Historical/stylistic notes**: Maintains sonata principle without developmental complexity

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 368): Tchaikovsky, *The Nutcracker*, "Miniature Overture" (1892):
- Exposition: PT (0:00), ST (0:49), CT (1:28)
- Recapitulation: PT (1:40), ST (2:30), CT (3:09)
- No development section

# Relationships

## Builds Upon
- **Sonata form** -- Sonatina form is sonata form minus the development

## Related
- **Sonata principle** -- Tonal opposition and resolution still apply

## Contrasts With
- **Sonata form** -- Full sonata form includes a development section

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming any short piece called "sonatina" is in sonatina form
  **Correction**: "Sonatina" as a title does not always indicate sonatina FORM; the formal definition is specific

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking sonatina form lacks tonal drama
  **Clarification**: The tonal opposition (I to V) and resolution (all in I) are fully present
