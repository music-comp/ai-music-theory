# Quick Definition
Normal form represents the notes of a pitch-class set in their most compact ascending arrangement, providing a standardized way to compare sets.

# Formal Definition
Normal form represents the pitch classes of a set as they occur in the music, arranged in their most compact ascending order. It is the ordering that spans the smallest interval from lowest to highest pitch class. Normal form preserves the actual pitch classes found in the music (unlike prime form, which transposes to 0).

# Construction/Calculation
Steps to determine normal form (Rahn method):
1. Arrange pitch classes in ascending numeric order, eliminate duplicates
2. Examine every possible ascending "rotation" of the notes
3. Find the most compact form (smallest span from first to last)
4. Tie-breaker: if spans are equal, choose the set with smaller interval from first to penultimate note
5. Absolute tie: choose the set beginning on the smaller number
6. Write in square brackets with commas: [11, 2, 3, 7]

Example: {3, 11, 2}
- Orderings: [2, 3, 11], [3, 11, 2], [11, 2, 3]
- Spans: 9, 11, 4
- Most compact: [11, 2, 3] (span of 4)

# Musical Context
Normal form is an intermediate step toward prime form and provides a consistent representation of pitch-class sets. It shows the actual pitches in the music while arranging them systematically for comparison. Normal form is essential for the subsequent calculation of prime form.

# Examples
- {3, 11, 2} in normal form: [11, 2, 3] (span of 4 semitones)
- {8, 0, 9} in normal form: [8, 9, 0] (span of 4 semitones)
- Four-note example: [2, 3, 8, 9] (tie resolved by smaller starting number)
- Written with square brackets and commas

# Common Confusions
- Normal form uses the ACTUAL pitch classes, not transposed to 0 (that's prime form)
- The Rahn method measures first to penultimate for tie-breaking; Forte measures first to second
- "Most compact" means smallest outer interval, not smallest inner intervals
- Normal form is written in square brackets []; prime form uses parentheses ()
