# Quick Definition
Afterbeats are repeated chords that occur after the downbeat, while offbeats are chords that consistently occur on upbeats, avoiding downbeats entirely, as in polka and reggae styles.

# Formal Definition
"Afterbeats" (term from Schoenberg's Fundamentals of Musical Composition) are repeated chords (usually eighth or quarter notes) that occur after the downbeat. "Offbeats" are chords that occur regularly on upbeats, avoiding downbeats, characteristic of polka and reggae styles.

# Analysis/Application
Afterbeats:
- Bass or melody on downbeat
- Repeated chords fill the remaining beats
- Creates forward motion while grounding the downbeat

Offbeats:
- Chords consistently on upbeats
- Downbeats may be silent or have bass only
- Creates characteristic lift and groove

# Musical Context
Afterbeats are common in Classical keyboard music. Offbeat patterns define specific genres: polka (Central European dance) and reggae (Jamaican music). Reggae's offbeat pattern has influenced rock and pop.

# Examples
Afterbeats:
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata Op. 2, No. 1, IV - classic afterbeat pattern
- Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 279, III - afterbeats with passing-tone figures

Offbeats (Polka):
- "Beer Barrel Polka" (1927)

Offbeats (Reggae):
- Bob Marley, "Could You Be Loved" (1980)
- The Police, "Walking on the Moon" (1979)
- "Red Red Wine" (UB40, 1983)
- "I'm Yours" (Jason Mraz, 2007)
- "Rude" (MAGIC!, 2013)

# Related Concepts
- Block chord accompaniment
- Syncopation
- Reggae style
- Polka style

# Common Confusions
- Afterbeats still have strong downbeat emphasis; offbeats avoid the downbeat
- Reggae offbeats are played on electric guitar typically
- These patterns define genre identity
