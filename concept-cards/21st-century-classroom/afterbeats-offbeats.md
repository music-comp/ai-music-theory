# Quick Definition
Afterbeats are repeated chords occurring after the downbeat (Schoenberg's term), while offbeats are chords consistently placed on upbeats avoiding downbeats, characteristic of polka and reggae styles.

# Core Definition
"Afterbeats" (term from Schoenberg's Fundamentals of Musical Composition) are repeated chords (usually eighth or quarter notes) that occur after the downbeat, with the bass or melody on the downbeat itself. "Offbeats" are chords that occur regularly on upbeats, avoiding downbeats. Offbeat patterns define specific genres: polka (Central European) and reggae (Jamaican). Afterbeats may not always be repeated chords -- they can be passing-tone figures harmonized in thirds.

- **Block chord accompaniment**: Understanding of chordal rhythm patterns

# Key Properties
1. Afterbeats: chords after the downbeat; strong downbeat emphasis maintained
2. Offbeats: chords on upbeats, avoiding downbeats entirely
3. Afterbeats common in Classical keyboard music
4. Offbeats define polka and reggae styles
5. Reggae offbeats typically played on electric guitar
6. Reggae influence spread to British bands (Police, UB40) by late 1970s

# Construction / Recognition
1. Afterbeats: bass/melody on downbeat, chords fill remaining beats
2. Offbeats: chords consistently on upbeats, downbeats silent or bass-only
3. Listen for the characteristic "lift" of offbeat patterns

# Context & Application
Afterbeats are a standard Classical keyboard accompaniment. Offbeat patterns define genre identity in polka and reggae. Reggae's offbeat texture has influenced rock, pop, and contemporary music globally, from The Police to Jason Mraz.

# Examples
- Afterbeats: Beethoven Op. 2 No. 1 IV; Mozart K. 279 III (with passing-tone figures)
- Polka: "Beer Barrel Polka" (1927)
- Reggae: Bob Marley "Could You Be Loved" (1980), The Police "Walking on the Moon" (1979), UB40 "Red Red Wine" (1983), Jason Mraz "I'm Yours" (2007), MAGIC! "Rude" (2013)

# Common Errors
- Confusing afterbeats with offbeats (afterbeats keep the downbeat; offbeats avoid it)

# Common Confusions
- **Afterbeats have strong downbeats**: The chords fill after the bass/melody on beat 1
- **Offbeats avoid the downbeat**: Creating the characteristic reggae/polka lift
- **Reggae influence**: Spread from Jamaica to British rock (Police, UB40) and modern pop
