# Quick Definition
Binary form is a two-section musical structure (A and B, or A and A') with each section typically repeated, common in Baroque dance movements and Classical themes.

# Formal Definition
Binary form consists of two main sections. Three types of binary form are distinguished: balanced binary (first and second sections end with similar cadential material, transposed), rounded binary (opening material returns after contrasting material), and "simple" binary (no return of opening material, no similar endings). The descriptor "two-reprise" indicates both sections repeat.

# Construction/Recognition
Section labels: uppercase letters (A, B, or A')
Phrase labels: lowercase letters (a, b, a', etc.)

Types of binary:
1. Simple Binary: Two different sections (AA' or AB)
2. Rounded Binary: Opening returns (ABA' structure within binary frame)
3. Balanced Binary: Similar endings in both sections (transposed)

Formal descriptors:
- Sectional: A section ends on tonic
- Continuous: A section does NOT end on tonic
- Two-reprise: Both sections repeat

# Musical Context
- Predominant form in Baroque dance suites
- Common in Classical era themes (minuets, variations)
- Each section usually repeats (indicated by repeat signs)
- The two sections are roughly proportional in length

# Examples
- J.S. Bach, Invention No. 8 in F major, BWV 779 - balanced binary
- J.S. Bach, French Suite No. 6, Courante - balanced binary (similar endings)
- J.S. Bach, English Suite No. 3, Gavotte I - simple binary

# Common Confusions
- Binary has TWO sections, but the second may recall the first (rounded binary)
- Use uppercase for sections, lowercase for phrases
- "Two-reprise" is not the same as "two sections" (refers to repeats)
- The distinction between rounded binary and ternary can be subtle
