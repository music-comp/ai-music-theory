# Quick Definition

Binary form is a two-section musical structure (A and B, or A and A') with each section typically repeated, common in Baroque dance movements and Classical themes.

# Core Definition

Binary form consists of two main sections. Three types of binary form are distinguished: balanced binary (first and second sections end with similar cadential material, transposed), rounded binary (opening material returns after contrasting material in the second section), and "simple" binary (no return of opening material, no similar endings). The text uses uppercase letters (A, B) for sections and lowercase letters (a, b) for phrases (Hutchinson, Ch. 24, p. 344).

# Prerequisites

- **Phrase** -- Binary form is built from phrases grouped into sections; understanding phrase structure is needed to identify sections
- **Cadence** -- Cadences demarcate sections and determine whether a form is sectional or continuous
- **Modulation** -- Understanding key change is essential for binary principle and continuous binary forms

# Key Properties

1. Consists of exactly two main sections
2. Sections are labeled with uppercase letters (A, B or A, A')
3. Three subtypes: simple binary, rounded binary, balanced binary
4. Can be sectional (A ends on tonic) or continuous (A does not end on tonic)
5. Often "two-reprise" (both sections repeat)
6. Second section may begin similarly to the first (AA')
7. Predominant form in Baroque dance suites

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify Binary Form:
1. Determine how many large sections the piece has (should be two)
2. Check for repeat signs dividing the piece into two halves
3. Examine what happens at the end of the first section (tonic = sectional; non-tonic = continuous)
4. Check if opening material returns in the second section (rounded binary)
5. Check if both sections end similarly (balanced binary)
6. If neither, classify as simple binary

## Naming Convention:
- Place "sectional" or "continuous" before "binary"
- Add "two-reprise" if both sections repeat
- Add "rounded" or "balanced" if applicable
- Example: "two-reprise continuous rounded binary"

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: Baroque dance suites (gavottes, courantes, sarabandes), Classical-era minuets, theme-and-variations themes
- **Common applications**: Short instrumental movements, themes that serve as basis for variation sets
- **Historical/stylistic notes**: Predominant in the Baroque era (Bach, Handel, Scarlatti); also common in early Classical era; evolves into sonata form

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 352): J.S. Bach, English Suite No. 3 in G minor, BWV 808, Gavotte I -- simple binary form where both sections begin similarly (AA')

**Example 2** (p. 347): J.S. Bach, Invention No. 8 in F major, BWV 779 -- balanced binary form with similar cadential endings

**Example 3** (p. 349): Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 284, III -- two-reprise continuous rounded binary form

# Relationships

## Builds Upon
- **Phrase** -- Binary form organizes phrases into two large sections
- **Cadence** -- Cadences at section boundaries define the form's character

## Enables
- **Sonata form** -- Evolved from binary form, particularly rounded and balanced types
- **Binary principle** -- The tonal plan of modulating away and back

## Related

## Contrasts With
- **Ternary form** -- Three sections (ABA) vs. two sections
- **Rounded binary** -- Often confused with ternary due to the return of opening material

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Labeling sections with lowercase letters instead of uppercase
  **Correction**: Use uppercase (A, B) for sections, lowercase (a, b) for phrases

- **Error**: Forgetting to specify sectional/continuous in the form name
  **Correction**: Always state sectional or continuous before binary in the full name

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking binary means the two sections must be contrasting
  **Clarification**: The second section may develop or recall first-section material (AA' is still binary)

- **Confusion**: Equating "two-reprise" with "two sections"
  **Clarification**: "Two-reprise" means both sections are repeated; all binary forms have two sections whether or not they repeat

- **Confusion**: Believing rounded binary is the same as ternary
  **Clarification**: Rounded binary has two sections (with internal return); ternary has three independent sections
