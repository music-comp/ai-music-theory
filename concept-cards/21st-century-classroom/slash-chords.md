# Quick Definition
A slash chord notation shows a chord symbol followed by a slash and bass note, indicating a specific note should be in the bass (e.g., C/E means C major with E in the bass).

# Formal Definition
In lead-sheet notation, a slash chord places a triad or chord symbol before the slash and the bass note after it. The first letter represents the root of the chord; the letter after the slash indicates the lowest note (bass). Slash chords are used for inversions and for chords with non-chord tones in the bass.

# Construction/Recognition
- Format: Chord/Bass (e.g., C/E, Dm/A, G/B)
- First letter = chord root
- Letter after slash = bass note
- Can indicate inversions: C/E = C major first inversion
- Can indicate non-chord tone bass: C/F# = C major over F# bass

# Musical Context
Slash chords are essential for specifying bass notes in lead sheets and chord charts. They allow precise bass line notation while maintaining the flexibility of chord symbols. Common uses include inversions, pedal tones, and descending bass lines.

# Examples
- C/E = C major with E in bass (first inversion)
- C/G = C major with G in bass (second inversion)
- Dm/A = D minor with A in bass (second inversion)
- F/G = F major triad over G bass (common as dominant substitute)

# Common Confusions
- The letter after the slash is the BASS note, not another chord
- The first symbol is always the chord/triad, not the key
- Slash chords can have bass notes that are not chord tones
