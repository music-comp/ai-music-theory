# Quick Definition
When voice leading root position triads, the procedure depends on the melodic interval in the bass line: 3rd/6th, 4th/5th, 2nd, or repeated note each have specific rules for moving the upper voices.

# Formal Definition
Voice leading root position triads systematically follows the melodic interval between consecutive bass notes:
- **Bass moves by 3rd or 6th**: Retain two common tones, move one voice by step
- **Bass moves by 4th or 5th**: Either retain one common tone and move two voices by step, OR move all upper voices in the same direction
- **Bass moves by 2nd**: Move all upper voices in contrary motion to the bass
- **Bass repeats**: Repeat or arpeggiate the upper voices while maintaining proper doubling

# Rules/Procedures
1. **3rd or 6th**: Keep two common tones in place; move the remaining voice by step
2. **4th or 5th** (two options):
   - Option A: Hold the one common tone; move other two voices by step
   - Option B: Move all three upper voices in the same direction to nearest chord tones
3. **2nd**: Move all three upper voices in contrary motion to the bass
   - Exception: Deceptive progression has special rules (see separate card)
4. **Repeated note**: Either repeat all upper voices or arpeggiate to different chord members (keep doubled root, one 3rd, one 5th)

# Musical Context
These procedures ensure smooth voice leading with minimal motion and no parallel 5ths/8ves. The common tone approach maximizes connection between chords, while contrary motion when the bass moves by step prevents parallels.

# Examples
- Bass C to E (3rd): G and E stay; the voice on C moves to B
- Bass C to G (5th): Either keep E and move others, or move all voices down
- Bass C to D (2nd): If bass rises, all upper voices fall
- Bass C to C (same): Upper voices can repeat or exchange positions

# Common Confusions
- Forgetting that bass movement of 4th/5th has two valid options
- Not recognizing that the deceptive progression is an exception to the 2nd rule
- Moving voices unnecessarily when common tones are available
- Confusing the bass interval with the chord quality
