# Quick Definition
The tritone is an interval of exactly six half steps, spelled either as an augmented fourth (+4) or diminished fifth (o5).

# Formal Definition
The tritone (literally "three whole tones") equals six half steps, exactly half an octave. It can be spelled as an augmented fourth (e.g., C-F#) or a diminished fifth (e.g., C-Gb). These two spellings are enharmonically equivalent in sound but differ in notation and harmonic function. The tritone is the only interval that inverts to itself.

# Construction/Recognition
- 6 half steps (3 whole steps)
- Augmented fourth: C-F# (letter names span 4)
- Diminished fifth: C-Gb (letter names span 5)
- Divides the octave exactly in half
- Inverts to itself (+4 inverts to o5, both 6 half steps)

# Musical Context
The tritone is historically significant as the "diabolus in musica" (devil in music) due to its dissonant sound. It is the unstable interval in dominant seventh chords that resolves to the tonic. The tritone between ^4 and ^7 drives harmonic motion.

# Examples
- B to F = tritone (o5)
- F to B = tritone (+4)
- In C major, the tritone F-B appears in the G7 chord
- The tritone resolves inward (F-B to E-C) or outward (B-F to C-E)

# Common Confusions
- Tritone is a generic term for both +4 and o5
- Same sound, different spelling and voice-leading implications
- The only interval that inverts to itself (still 6 half steps)
