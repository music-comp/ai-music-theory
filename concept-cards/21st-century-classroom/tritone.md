# Quick Definition
The tritone is an interval of exactly six half steps (three whole tones), spelled either as an augmented fourth (+4) or diminished fifth (o5); it is the only interval that inverts to itself.

# Core Definition
A "tritone" is a generic name for an augmented fourth (+4) or diminished fifth (o5). These two intervals are enharmonic -- they sound the same (6 half steps) but are spelled differently. The tritone literally means "three whole tones" and divides the octave exactly in half. It is the only interval that inverts to itself in terms of half-step size.

# Key Properties
1. 6 half steps (3 whole steps)
2. Augmented fourth (+4): letter names span 4 (e.g., C-F#)
3. Diminished fifth (o5): letter names span 5 (e.g., C-Gb)
4. Divides the octave exactly in half
5. Inverts to itself (+4 inverts to o5, both 6 half steps)
6. Historically called "diabolus in musica" (the devil in music)

# Construction / Recognition
- Count 6 half steps from any note
- +4: spelled across 4 letter names (C to F#)
- o5: spelled across 5 letter names (C to Gb)
- Same sound, different notation

# Context & Application
The tritone is historically significant due to its dissonant sound and was called "diabolus in musica." It is the unstable interval within dominant seventh chords that creates the drive to resolve. The tritone between ^4 and ^7 of a major scale is the most harmonically charged interval, resolving inward (to ^3 and ^1) or outward.

# Examples
- B to F = tritone (o5, diminished fifth)
- F to B = tritone (+4, augmented fourth)
- In C major, the tritone F-B appears in the G7 chord (V7)
- The tritone resolves inward (F-B to E-C) or outward (B-F to C-E)

# Common Errors
- Treating +4 and o5 as different-sounding intervals (they are enharmonic)
- Forgetting the tritone's role in dominant harmony

# Common Confusions
- **"Tritone" is a generic term**: It covers both +4 and o5
- **Same sound, different function**: The spelling determines whether it is +4 or o5, which affects voice leading
- **Self-inverting**: The only interval whose inversion has the same number of half steps (6)
