# Quick Definition
In original Baroque figured bass notation, chromatic alterations to notes above the bass are indicated by accidentals placed before or through the numbers, or by a standalone accidental that always refers to the third above the bass.

# Core Definition
Figured bass numbers in actual Baroque music (but not in modern Roman numeral labeling) may be altered chromatically using several conventions: a sharp, flat, or natural placed before a number alters that specific interval above the bass; a slash through a number raises that interval by a half step; and a standalone accidental with no accompanying number alters the third above the bass note. These symbols allowed Baroque keyboardists to read chromatic modifications quickly while realizing the bass line in performance.

# Prerequisites
- **Figured bass**: The basic notational system being modified
- **Figured bass inversion symbols**: Standard abbreviations that can receive alterations
- **Accidentals**: Understanding of sharps, flats, and naturals

# Key Properties
1. Accidental before a number: alters that specific interval
2. Slash through a number: raises the interval by a half step
3. Standalone accidental (no number): alters the third above the bass
4. These appear in original Baroque notation and urtext editions
5. Modern Roman numeral analysis uses quality indicators (M, diminished, half-diminished) instead
6. Full figured bass shows every interval above the bass, not just the abbreviated symbols

# Construction / Recognition
- In original manuscripts: look for accidentals attached to or near figured bass numbers
- A sharp before "6" means the sixth above the bass is raised
- A slash through any number means raise that interval
- A lone sharp or flat with no number refers to the third above the bass
- In modern analysis: these alterations are absorbed into Roman numeral quality symbols

# Context & Application
These chromatic alteration symbols are encountered when studying original Baroque manuscripts, urtext editions, and when learning to realize figured bass at the keyboard. While modern Roman numeral analysis does not use these symbols directly, understanding them is essential for reading historical sources and for keyboard realization courses. The full figured bass notation showing every interval above the bass (not just abbreviated symbols) also appears in historical sources.

# Examples
- A slash through the number 6 raises the sixth above the bass by a half step
- A standalone sharp indicates a major (raised) third above the bass
- Full figured bass: root position triad shows 5/3, first inversion shows 6/3 (modern abbreviation: 6)

# Common Errors
- Applying these symbols in Roman numeral analysis (they belong only in original figured bass)
- Forgetting that a standalone accidental always refers to the third above bass

# Common Confusions
- **Historical vs. modern**: These symbols appear in Baroque scores, not in Roman numeral analysis
- **Standalone accidental**: Always modifies the third above the bass, even without a "3"
- **Slash vs. sharp**: A slash through a number raises it; a sharp before a number also raises it
