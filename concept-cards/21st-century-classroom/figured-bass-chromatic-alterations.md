# Quick Definition
In original Baroque figured bass notation, chromatic alterations to notes above the bass are indicated by accidentals before or through the numbers, or by a standalone accidental for the third above the bass.

# Formal Definition
Figured bass numbers in actual music may be altered chromatically with the following symbols: a sharp, flat, or natural placed before the number alters that interval; a slash through a number raises it by a half step; a standalone accidental (with no number) alters the third above the bass.

# Analysis/Application
Chromatic alteration symbols:
- Sharp/flat/natural before number: alters that specific interval
- Slash through number: raises the interval by half step
- Standalone accidental: alters the third above bass

# Musical Context
These symbols allowed Baroque keyboardists to read chord symbols quickly while playing from a figured bass part. In modern analysis, we use Roman numerals with quality indicators instead.

# Examples
- A slash through the number 6 raises the sixth above the bass
- A standalone sharp indicates a major third above the bass
- These appear in original Baroque manuscripts and urtext editions

# Common Confusions
- These symbols appear in original notation, not in Roman numeral analysis
- In Roman numeral analysis, we indicate quality with M, diminished symbol, etc.
- A standalone accidental always refers to the third above bass
