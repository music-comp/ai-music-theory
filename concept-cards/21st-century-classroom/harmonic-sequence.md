# Quick Definition
A harmonic sequence is a musical idea (usually involving both melody and harmony) that is repeated at a different pitch level, creating predictable motion through harmonic space.

# Formal Definition
A sequence is a musical idea repeated at a different pitch level. In a harmonic sequence, the bass line acts as a "melody" that is sequenced, and the harmonies above it follow the same pattern at the new pitch level. Sequences can be short or long, and they create a sense of inevitability in harmonic progression.

# Analysis/Application
To identify harmonic sequences:
1. Look for repeated patterns in the bass line
2. Check if the harmonic pattern repeats at different pitch levels
3. Sequences often explain unusual chord progressions that violate the harmonic flowchart
4. Label the sequential units to show the pattern

# Musical Context
Harmonic sequences appear throughout Western music from the Baroque era to contemporary pop. They can create dramatic builds, provide structural predictability, and justify otherwise unusual chord progressions.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 5, Op. 67, I (short sequential motive)
- Mozart, Symphony No. 40, I (four-bar ideas sequenced down a step)
- "Love You Like A Love Song" - bass line as a two-note descending sequence
- "Hotel California" - unusual progressions explained by sequences
- "Flashdance... What A Feeling" - sequence creates exceptions to normal function

# Common Confusions
- Sequences explain exceptions to the harmonic flowchart - when progressions seem unusual, check for sequential patterns
- The roots may follow a sequential pattern even when chords are inverted
- Harmonic sequences involve both the bass line and the chords above
