# Quick Definition
Second species writes two half notes against each whole note of the cantus firmus, introducing the passing tone as the one allowable dissonance.

# Core Definition
In second species counterpoint, one writes two half notes against a cantus firmus in whole notes. The rules are generally the same as first species, with one critical addition: the passing tone is allowed as a simple dissonance. The first half note of each measure must always be a consonance; the second half note may be a dissonance only if it functions as a passing tone (approached and left by step). Second species can also include writing three half notes against a dotted whole note in 3/2 time (Hutchinson, Ch. 30, p. 426).

# Prerequisites
- **First species counterpoint** -- Second species builds on first species consonance rules
- **Cantus firmus** -- Written against a given c.f.
- **Passing tone** -- The only dissonance allowed in second species; must be approached and left by step

# Key Properties
1. Two half notes per whole note in the cantus firmus
2. First half note of each measure must be consonant
3. Second half note may be a passing tone (dissonant, step-step) or must be consonant
4. Same melodic leap restrictions as first species
5. No repeated notes across barlines
6. Three distinct concluding formulas depending on c.f. position and mode
7. Consecutive 8ves or 5ths on downbeats are avoided unless a leap of a 4th follows

# Construction / Recognition
**Beginning:**
1. Start with a half note at the octave or unison on beat 1 or beat 3
2. If starting on beat 3, place a half rest at the beginning

**Ending (concluding formulas):**
1. C.f. in lower part: approach final octave with intervals 5th-6th (scale degrees 6-7 against 2 in c.f.)
2. C.f. in upper part: approach final octave/unison with intervals 5th-3rd (scale degrees 5-7 against 2 in c.f.)
3. Phrygian exception: c.f. in upper part, approach with 6th-3rd (scale degrees 4-flat-7 against flat-2 in c.f.)
4. End with a whole note on octave or unison

**Barline rules:**
- Imperfect consonances: may be approached any way across barlines
- Perfect consonances: contrary motion only across barlines
- No repeated notes across barlines

# Context & Application
Second species introduces the concept of metrical hierarchy in counterpoint -- strong beats must be consonant while weak beats can include carefully controlled dissonance. The passing tone becomes the gateway to understanding how dissonance creates forward motion within a consonant framework. This is the first step toward the rhythmic complexity of free counterpoint.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Placing a dissonance on the first half note of a measure
  **Correction**: The first half note must always be consonant
- **Error**: Using a neighbor tone instead of a passing tone
  **Correction**: Only passing tones (approached and left by step in the same direction) are allowed
- **Error**: Repeating notes across barlines
  **Correction**: Do not repeat notes across barlines in second species

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking any dissonance is allowed on weak beats
  **Clarification**: Only passing tones (approached and left by step) serve as dissonances; neighbor tones and other non-chord tones are not permitted
- **Confusion**: Forgetting the Phrygian exception for concluding formulas
  **Clarification**: Phrygian mode has a unique ending formula due to its half-step above the final
