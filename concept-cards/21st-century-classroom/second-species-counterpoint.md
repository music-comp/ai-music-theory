# Quick Definition
Second species writes two half notes against each whole note of the cantus firmus; the first half note must be consonant, while the second may be a dissonant passing tone.

# Formal Definition
Second species counterpoint introduces the first allowable dissonance: the passing tone. Two half notes are written against each whole note in the cantus firmus. The first half note (on the downbeat) must always be a consonance. The second half note may be either consonant or a passing tone (approached and left by step). The rules for approaching perfect consonances apply when crossing barlines.

# Rules/Procedures
1. **Begin** with a half note at the octave or unison on beat 1 or beat 3
   - If starting on beat 3, place a half rest on beat 1
2. **End** with a whole note on an octave or unison
   - C.F. in lower voice: Approach with intervals 5-6 (scale degrees 6-7 against 2 in c.f.)
   - C.F. in upper voice: Approach with intervals 5-3 (scale degrees 5-7 against 2 in c.f.)
   - **Phrygian exception**: C.F. in upper, approach with 6-3 (4-flat7 against flat2)
3. **First half note of each measure**: Must be consonant
4. **Second half note**: May be consonant OR a passing tone (step-step)
5. **Crossing barlines**:
   - Imperfect consonances: Approached any way
   - Perfect consonances: Must be approached by contrary motion
   - Do not repeat notes across barlines
6. **Avoid consecutive 8ves or 5ths on downbeats** unless a 4th leap follows

# Musical Context
Second species introduces rhythmic variety and the controlled use of dissonance (passing tones). It teaches how dissonance can create forward motion when properly handled.

# Examples
- C.F. in bass, counterpoint has half notes: First beat consonant, second beat may pass through dissonance
- Concluding formula with C.F. below: ...5-6-8 (intervals 5 then 6 resolving to octave)

# Common Confusions
- Placing a dissonance on the first half note (must be consonant)
- Using neighbor tones (only passing tones are allowed in second species)
- Repeating notes across barlines (forbidden)
- Approaching perfect consonances across barlines by parallel or similar motion
- Having consecutive 5ths or 8ves on downbeats without intervening leap of 4th
