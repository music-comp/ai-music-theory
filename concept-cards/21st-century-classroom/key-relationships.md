# Quick Definition
Closely related keys have key signatures one accidental sharper or flatter than the home key, yielding five closely related keys for any major or minor key -- all other keys are "foreign" or "distantly related."

# Core Definition
"Closely related" keys have key signatures one degree "sharper" or "flatter" than the starting key. For any major or minor key, there are five closely related keys, including the relative major or minor of the home key. Key signatures that are not closely related are said to be "foreign" or "distantly related." The tonal distance between keys affects the perceived drama of modulation and determines which modulatory techniques are available.

# Prerequisites
- **Key signatures**: Understanding accidental counts
- **Circle of fifths**: The spatial relationship of keys
- **Relative major/minor**: One of the five closely related keys

# Key Properties
1. Five closely related keys for any major or minor key
2. Related by one accidental difference in key signature
3. The five are: relative, dominant, subdominant, relative of dominant, relative of subdominant
4. All other keys = foreign (distantly related)
5. Parallel major/minor are NOT closely related (different key signatures)
6. Baroque/Classical: typically modulate to closely related keys
7. Romantic era: exploration of foreign key relationships

# Construction / Recognition
For A major (3 sharps), the five closely related keys:
- f# minor (relative minor, 3 sharps)
- E major (dominant, 4 sharps)
- D major (subdominant, 2 sharps)
- c# minor (relative of dominant, 4 sharps)
- b minor (relative of subdominant, 2 sharps)

For C major: closely related = G, F, a, e, d
For a minor: closely related = C, G, d, e, F

# Context & Application
Baroque and Classical composers primarily modulated to closely related keys. Romantic-era composers explored foreign key relationships using chromatic pivot chords, common-tone modulations, and enharmonic modulation. The "tonal distance" (how many accidentals separate two keys) correlates with the perceived drama of the modulation. Foreign key modulation requires special techniques (chromatic pivots, Neapolitan pivot, common-tone, enharmonic).

# Examples
- C major to G major: closely related (one more sharp)
- C major to Db major: foreign (different by 5 flats)
- C major to Ab major: foreign (different by 4 flats)
- a minor to C major: closely related (relative major)

# Common Errors
- Assuming parallel major/minor are closely related (they are NOT)
- Counting more than five closely related keys

# Common Confusions
- **Parallel vs. relative**: Relative keys are closely related; parallel keys are NOT (different key signatures)
- **One accidental**: Closely related keys differ by exactly ONE accidental
- **Five, always five**: Every major or minor key has exactly five closely related keys
