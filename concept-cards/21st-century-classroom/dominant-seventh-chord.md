# Quick Definition
A dominant seventh chord (also called a major-minor seventh chord) consists of a major triad with a minor seventh above the root, creating the characteristic tension that drives resolution to the tonic.

# Core Definition
The dominant seventh chord is built from a major triad plus a minor seventh from root to seventh. It is called "major-minor" because the triad is major and the seventh interval is minor. In Roman numeral analysis, it is notated as V7. Its intervallic structure is M3 + m3 + m3. It naturally occurs on scale degree 5 in major keys and on the raised 5th degree in harmonic minor. It contains a tritone between the third and seventh of the chord, which creates its characteristic tension.

- **Seventh chord**: General concept of four-note chords

# Key Properties
1. Major triad + minor seventh from root to seventh (10 half steps)
2. Third-stacking pattern: M3 + m3 + m3
3. Contains the tritone between the third and seventh of the chord
4. Naturally occurs as V7 in major and harmonic minor
5. Roman numeral: uppercase with superscript 7 (V7)
6. Lead-sheet symbols: C7, G7, D7

# Construction / Recognition
1. Build a major triad on the root
2. Add a note a minor third above the fifth (or a minor seventh above the root)
3. The tritone between chord third and seventh is the hallmark sound
4. In a key context, look for V7 -- the dominant chord with added seventh

# Context & Application
The dominant seventh is the most important seventh chord in tonal harmony. Its tritone creates an instability that naturally resolves to the tonic triad: the tritone between scale degrees 7 and 4 resolves to 1 and 3 of the tonic chord. It is fundamental in classical, popular, jazz, and blues music.

# Examples
- G7 in C major: G-B-D-F (V7), tritone B-F resolves to C-E
- D7 in G major: D-F#-A-C
- C7: C-E-G-Bb

# Common Errors
- Confusing dominant seventh (M3+m3+m3) with major seventh (M3+m3+M3)
- Writing a major seventh interval instead of minor seventh

# Common Confusions
- **"Dominant" has two meanings**: The chord built on scale degree 5, and also a chord quality (major triad + minor seventh) that can be built on any pitch
- **C7 vs. Cmaj7**: C7 has Bb (minor seventh); Cmaj7 has B natural (major seventh)
