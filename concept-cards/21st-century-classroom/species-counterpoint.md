# Quick Definition
Species counterpoint is a pedagogical method from Fux's *Gradus ad Parnassum* (1725) that teaches contrapuntal composition by progressively adding rhythmic complexity against a given melody (cantus firmus).

# Core Definition
Species counterpoint is a systematic approach to teaching counterpoint developed by Johann Joseph Fux in his treatise *Gradus ad Parnassum* (1725). The method uses simple rhythmic values and teaches composers to write one, two, three, or four notes against a longer note value, usually a whole note. Counterpoint itself is "the simultaneous sounding of two or more melodic lines," producing textures called "contrapuntal" (as opposed to "homophonic" melody-accompaniment textures). The pedagogical method focuses on two-voice writing, though semester-long courses extend to four voices (Hutchinson, Ch. 30, p. 423).

# Prerequisites
- **Consonance and dissonance** -- Species counterpoint rules are built on the distinction between consonant and dissonant intervals
- **Intervals** -- Each species defines permitted intervals between the cantus firmus and counterpoint voice

# Key Properties
1. Based on 16th-century Renaissance style (Palestrina)
2. Five progressively complex species, each isolating specific contrapuntal problems
3. All species are written against a cantus firmus (c.f.) in whole notes
4. Primarily taught in two-voice texture
5. Rules derive from observations of note-by-note details in Renaissance compositions
6. Each species introduces one new rhythmic relationship to the cantus firmus

# Construction / Recognition
**The five species:**
1. **First Species**: Whole note against whole note (note against note)
2. **Second Species**: Two half notes against each whole note
3. **Third Species**: Four quarter notes against each whole note
4. **Fourth Species**: Syncopations and suspensions (tied half notes)
5. **Fifth Species (Florid)**: Free mixture of all four preceding species

**To recognize species counterpoint:**
1. Identify the cantus firmus (whole notes, single melodic line)
2. Identify the counterpoint voice and its rhythmic relationship
3. Check for adherence to intervallic rules governing consonance and dissonance

# Context & Application
Species counterpoint prepares students for free composition by isolating specific contrapuntal problems. While the style is based on Renaissance practice, the skills transfer to Baroque (fugue, invention), Classical, and later styles. Bach's Well-Tempered Clavier demonstrates advanced contrapuntal mastery built on these foundations. In a typical college curriculum, species counterpoint precedes the study of tonal counterpoint (inventions, fugue).

# Examples
- **Bach, Well-Tempered Clavier, Book I, Fugue 3 in C-sharp major, BWV 848** (p. 423): Demonstrates advanced two-voice counterpoint
- **Orlande de Lassus, *Expectatio Justorum*** (p. 429): Shows florid (fifth species) counterpoint with all species observable in the opening
- Palestrina's masses represent the stylistic model that Fux's rules codify

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Using parallel 5ths or 8ves when approaching perfect consonances
  **Correction**: Perfect consonances must always be approached by contrary motion
- **Error**: Including dissonant intervals in first species
  **Correction**: Only consonances (1, 3, 5, 6, 8, and compounds) are permitted in first species
- **Error**: Outlining a tritone melodically at turning points
  **Correction**: Change direction to avoid tritone outlines between two melodic turning points

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking species counterpoint rules are arbitrary
  **Clarification**: They derive from systematic observation of Renaissance practice (Palestrina)
- **Confusion**: Expecting species counterpoint to sound like Bach
  **Clarification**: It models Palestrina-era style; Baroque counterpoint has different conventions
- **Confusion**: Believing the method is outdated or irrelevant
  **Clarification**: The isolation of specific problems (consonance, dissonance, suspension) trains compositional awareness applicable to any style
