# Quick Definition
An augmented triad is built with two major thirds (M3 + M3), totaling an augmented fifth from root to fifth; it is the rarest of the four triad types in diatonic music.

# Core Definition
An augmented triad is built with a major third (4 half steps) between the root and third, and another major third (4 half steps) between the third and fifth. The total interval from root to fifth is an augmented fifth (8 half steps). Augmented triads are rare in diatonic music. The lead-sheet symbol uses the plus sign (+) after the root. In Roman numerals, uppercase with plus sign (III+, V+).

# Prerequisites
- **Triad**: The three-note chord concept
- **Augmented/diminished intervals**: Understanding the augmented fifth

# Key Properties
1. Root to third: Major 3rd (4 half steps)
2. Third to fifth: Major 3rd (4 half steps)
3. Root to fifth: augmented 5th (8 half steps)
4. Lead-sheet symbol: root + "+" or "aug" (C+, Gaug)
5. Roman numeral: uppercase + "+" (III+, V+)
6. Rarest of the four triad types; tonally ambiguous due to symmetry

# Construction / Recognition
- M3 + M3 (both intervals identical)
- Total span: +5 (augmented fifth)
- Symmetrical structure divides the octave into three equal parts
- Lead-sheet: letter + "+" or "aug"

# Context & Application
Augmented triads occur naturally in harmonic minor (III+) and can be used as altered dominants (V+). Their symmetric structure (three equal M3 intervals) makes them tonally ambiguous -- any note could be heard as the root. They are the rarest of the four triad types.

# Examples
- C augmented: C-E-G# (M3 + M3)
- G augmented: G-B-D#
- Ab augmented: Ab-C-E
- Lead-sheet: C+, G+, Caug

# Common Errors
- Confusing the augmented triad with a major triad with raised fifth (they are the same thing)
- Forgetting the symmetric property (each note could serve as root of a different augmented triad)

# Common Confusions
- **Both intervals identical**: Both internal intervals are major thirds (like diminished has two minor thirds)
- **Symmetrical**: Augmented triads divide the octave into three equal parts, making them tonally ambiguous
- **Very rare**: The rarest triad quality in diatonic music
