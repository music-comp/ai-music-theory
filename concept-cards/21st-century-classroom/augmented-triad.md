# Quick Definition
An augmented triad consists of two major thirds stacked together, totaling an augmented fifth from root to fifth.

# Formal Definition
An augmented triad is built with a major third (4 half steps) between the root and third, and another major third (4 half steps) between the third and fifth. The total interval from root to fifth is an augmented fifth (8 half steps). Augmented triads sound "stretched" or "expectant" and are relatively rare in tonal music.

# Construction/Recognition
- Root to third: Major 3rd (4 half steps)
- Third to fifth: Major 3rd (4 half steps)
- Root to fifth: augmented 5th (8 half steps)
- Lead-sheet symbol: root + plus sign (C+, G+)
- Roman numeral: uppercase + plus sign (III+, V+)

# Musical Context
Augmented triads are the rarest of the four triad types in diatonic music. They occur naturally in harmonic minor (III+) and can be used as altered dominants (V+). Their symmetric structure makes them tonally ambiguous.

# Examples
- C augmented: C-E-G# (M3 + M3)
- G augmented: G-B-D#
- Ab augmented: Ab-C-E
- Lead-sheet: C+, G+, Caug

# Common Confusions
- Both internal intervals are major thirds
- The fifth is augmented, not perfect
- Augmented triads divide the octave into three equal parts (symmetrical)
