# Quick Definition
The tenor clef is a C clef that places middle C on the fourth line from the bottom, used for higher passages in cello, bassoon, and trombone.

# Formal Definition
The tenor clef is a type of C clef that, when placed on a five-line staff, designates the fourth line from the bottom as middle C (C4). The symmetrical shape of the C clef centers on the line representing middle C.

# Construction/Recognition
- The C clef symbol centers on the fourth line from the bottom
- The fourth line equals middle C (C4)
- Lines (bottom to top): D3-F3-A3-C4-E4
- Spaces (bottom to top): E3-G3-B3-D4

# Musical Context
The tenor clef is used for higher passages in instruments that normally read bass clef, such as cello, bassoon, and trombone. It allows these instruments to read notes in a higher register without excessive ledger lines.

# Examples
- The fourth line from the bottom of the tenor clef staff is always C4 (middle C)
- Cello music often switches to tenor clef for higher passages
- Bassoon and trombone parts may use tenor clef for upper register music

# Common Confusions
- Confusing tenor clef with alto clef (middle C is on different lines)
- Assuming all C clefs are the same
- Forgetting which instruments commonly use tenor clef
