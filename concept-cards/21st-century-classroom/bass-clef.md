# Quick Definition
The bass clef is a musical symbol that indicates lower-pitched notes, with the F below middle C located on the fourth line from the bottom.

# Formal Definition
The bass clef, also called the F clef, is a clef symbol that, when placed on a five-line staff with its two dots surrounding the second line from the top, designates that line as F3 (the F below middle C). It is used for lower-pitched instruments and voices.

# Construction/Recognition
- The two dots of the bass clef surround the F line (fourth line from bottom)
- Lines (bottom to top): G-B-D-F-A ("Good Boys Do Fine Always")
- Spaces (bottom to top): A-C-E-G ("All Cows Eat Grass")
- Middle C (C4) is on the first ledger line above the staff

# Musical Context
The bass clef is used for instruments and voices in the lower register, including bass, cello, bassoon, trombone, bass voices, and the left hand of piano music.

# Examples
- The note on the fourth line from the bottom is always F3
- The note in the second space from the bottom is always C3
- The note on the first ledger line above the staff is middle C (C4)

# Common Confusions
- Confusing bass clef line/space names with treble clef line/space names
- Middle C location: it is above the bass staff, not on it
- The two dots indicate F, not where to start reading
