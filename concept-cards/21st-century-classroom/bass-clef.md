# Quick Definition
The bass clef (F clef) designates the fourth line from the bottom as F3 (the F below middle C), with its two dots surrounding that line.

# Core Definition
The F clef, when placed on a five-line staff with the dots surrounding the second line from the top (fourth line from bottom), creates the bass clef. In bass clef, the notes on the lines are G-B-D-F-A from lowest to highest. The spaces are A-C-E-G from lowest to highest. Middle C (C4) is notated on the first ledger line above the staff.

# Prerequisites
- **Staff**: The five-line framework on which notes are written

# Key Properties
1. Based on the F clef symbol, with two dots surrounding the F line
2. Fourth line from the bottom = F3
3. Lines (bottom to top): G-B-D-F-A
4. Spaces (bottom to top): A-C-E-G
5. Middle C is on the first ledger line above the staff
6. Used for lower-pitched instruments and voices

# Construction / Recognition
- F clef symbol with two dots surrounding the fourth line from the bottom
- Lines: G-B-D-F-A ("Good Boys Do Fine Always")
- Spaces: A-C-E-G ("All Cows Eat Grass")
- Middle C sits on the first ledger line above

# Context & Application
The bass clef is used for instruments and voices in the lower register, including bass, cello, bassoon, trombone, bass voices, and the left hand of piano music. Together with the treble clef, it forms the grand staff.

# Examples
- The note on the fourth line from the bottom is always F3
- The note in the second space from the bottom is always C3
- Middle C (C4) is on the first ledger line above the staff

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Staff

## Enables
- Reading bass lines, left-hand piano notation, lower-register instruments

## Related
- Treble clef (higher register counterpart)
- Grand staff (treble + bass clef joined by a bracket)
- Ledger lines (for notes beyond the staff)

## Contrasts With
- Treble clef (orients to G instead of F; used for higher pitches)

# Common Errors
- Confusing bass clef line/space names with treble clef line/space names
- Misidentifying the meaning of the two dots (they indicate F, not a starting point)

# Common Confusions
- **Middle C location**: It is above the bass staff on a ledger line, not on the staff itself
- **The two dots**: They surround the F line to orient the clef, not to indicate where to start reading
