# Quick Definition
A cadence is a harmonic arrival point at the end of a phrase, functioning like punctuation in language; the four basic types are authentic (V-I), plagal (IV-I), deceptive (V-vi), and half (ending on V).

# Core Definition
A cadence is a harmonic arrival point, a harmonic moment of stasis. A cadence can be compared to a comma or period in written language -- the ear gets a moment to process a short passage of music, then the music continues. The four basic cadence types are: (1) Authentic Cadence (AC): a phrase ending with the chords V-I; (2) Plagal Cadence (PC): a phrase ending with the chords IV-I; (3) Deceptive Cadence (DC): a phrase ending with the chords V-vi; (4) Half Cadence (HC): a phrase ending on the V chord.

# Prerequisites
- **Roman numeral analysis**: Needed to label cadence chords

# Key Properties
1. Occurs at the end of a phrase
2. Creates a sense of pause, arrival, or conclusion
3. Comparable to punctuation in language
4. Four basic types: AC, PC, DC, HC
5. AC (V-I): strongest closure
6. PC (IV-I): softer closure
7. DC (V-vi): unexpected, delays resolution
8. HC (ending on V): incomplete, expectant

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the last two chords (or last chord for HC) of a phrase
- V-I = authentic; IV-I = plagal; V-vi = deceptive; ending on V = half
- Must occur at a phrase ending, not just any chord change

# Context & Application
Cadences are essential for musical form, marking phrase boundaries and creating the sense of musical "breathing." Strong cadences (authentic) create finality; weak cadences (half) create expectation. Cadences are crucial in both classical and popular music analysis.

# Examples
- "Star-Spangled Banner" ends with an authentic cadence (V-I)
- "Amen" in hymns is a plagal cadence (IV-I)
- Mozart's "Voi che sapete" includes a deceptive cadence
- Mozart's Eine kleine Nachtmusik opens with a half cadence
- Half cadences create a "comma" effect; authentic cadences create a "period" effect

# Common Errors
- Identifying any chord change as a cadence (cadences occur only at phrase endings)
- Confusing half cadence (ends ON V) with authentic cadence (V goes TO I)

# Common Confusions
- **Phrase endings only**: A cadence is at the END of a phrase, not just any chord change
- **HC ends ON V**: Half cadence ends on V (from anywhere); authentic cadence starts on V and goes to I
- **DC = "V to not-I"**: Deceptive cadence means V resolves to something other than I (usually vi)
