# Quick Definition
Two notes are enharmonic when they sound the same but are written with different note names, such as C# and Db.

# Core Definition
When two notes sound the same but are written as two different notes on the staff, the written notes are said to be enharmonic. This occurs with all the black keys on the piano (C#=Db, D#=Eb, F#=Gb, G#=Ab, A#=Bb). Notes on the white keys of the piano can be written enharmonically as well (C=B#, E=Fb, D=Cx, etc.).

# Prerequisites
- **Accidentals**: Understanding sharps, flats, and their effects on pitch

# Key Properties
1. Enharmonic notes sound identical (same pitch frequency)
2. They are notated differently on the staff
3. All black keys have two common enharmonic names
4. White keys can also be spelled enharmonically (e.g., C=B#, E=Fb)
5. Double sharps and flats create additional enharmonic possibilities (e.g., D=Cx=Ebb)
6. The choice of spelling depends on musical context

# Construction / Recognition
- Black key pairs: C#=Db, D#=Eb, F#=Gb, G#=Ab, A#=Bb
- White key examples: C=B#=Dbb, E=Fb=D##, F=E#=Gbb, B=Cb=A##
- Same key on the piano, different notation on the staff

# Context & Application
Enharmonic spelling is chosen based on musical context, key signature, and voice-leading conventions. The choice of spelling affects how a note functions harmonically even though it sounds the same. Enharmonic equivalence is also important for understanding enharmonic key pairs at the bottom of the circle of fifths (B/Cb, F#/Gb, C#/Db).

# Examples
- C# and Db are enharmonic (same key on piano, different notation)
- E and Fb are enharmonic (same white key)
- G and Fx (F double sharp) are enharmonic
- B and Cb are enharmonic

# Common Errors
- Assuming only black keys can be spelled enharmonically (white keys can too)
- Using the wrong enharmonic spelling for a given key context

# Common Confusions
- **Sound vs. function**: Enharmonic notes sound the same but may have different musical functions depending on context
- **Context determines spelling**: The key, chord, and voice leading determine the correct spelling
- **All notes are enharmonic**: Not just black keys -- every pitch can be spelled multiple ways
