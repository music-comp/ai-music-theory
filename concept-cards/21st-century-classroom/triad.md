# Quick Definition
A triad consists of three notes stacked in consecutive thirds; the lowest note when stacked in thirds is the root, the middle note is the third, and the top note is the fifth.

# Core Definition
A triad consists of three notes stacked in consecutive thirds. A triad is also called a chord as well as a harmony. (Harmony also refers to chord progressions.) The lowest note of a triad when it is stacked in thirds is called the root. The middle note is the third and the highest note of the triad is the fifth. There are four qualities of triads -- augmented, major, minor, and diminished. Major and minor triads are common, diminished triads are less common, and augmented triads are rare.

# Prerequisites
- **Intervals**: Understanding thirds and fifths
- **Major/minor intervals**: Distinguishing M3 from m3

# Key Properties
1. Three notes stacked in consecutive thirds
2. Root: the foundation note (lowest when stacked in thirds)
3. Third: the middle note (a 3rd above the root)
4. Fifth: the top note (a 5th above the root)
5. Four qualities: major, minor, diminished, augmented
6. Frequency in tonal music: major and minor (common), diminished (less common), augmented (rare)

# Construction / Recognition
- Stack notes on consecutive lines or consecutive spaces
- Root = bottom note when stacked in thirds
- Quality determined by the combination of intervals (M3+m3, m3+M3, m3+m3, M3+M3)
- Distinguish root from bass (root = lowest when in thirds; bass = lowest sounding note)

# Context & Application
Triads are the building blocks of harmony and form the basis of chord progressions, harmonic analysis, and accompaniment patterns. Most tonal music can be analyzed in terms of triads and their extensions. A triad is also called a chord as well as a harmony.

# Examples
- C major triad: C-E-G (M3 + m3)
- A minor triad: A-C-E (m3 + M3)
- B diminished triad: B-D-F (m3 + m3)
- C augmented triad: C-E-G# (M3 + M3)

# Common Errors
- Confusing root with bass (root is lowest when stacked in thirds; bass is lowest sounding note in any voicing)
- Assuming a triad must always appear in root position

# Common Confusions
- **Root vs. bass**: The root is the bottom note when stacked in thirds; the bass is the lowest note in any voicing
- **Three pitch classes**: A triad always has exactly three distinct pitch classes, even if doubled in multiple octaves
- **"Chord" and "harmony"**: Both terms can refer to a triad; "harmony" also refers to chord progressions
