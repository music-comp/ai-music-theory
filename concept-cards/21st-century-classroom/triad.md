# Quick Definition
A triad is a three-note chord built by stacking two consecutive thirds, consisting of a root, third, and fifth.

# Formal Definition
A triad consists of three notes stacked in consecutive thirds. The lowest note when stacked in thirds is the root, the middle note is the third (a third above the root), and the top note is the fifth (a third above the third, or a fifth above the root). Triads are the fundamental harmonic unit in Western tonal music.

# Construction/Recognition
- Three notes stacked in thirds (on three consecutive lines or spaces)
- Root: the foundation note (lowest when in root position)
- Third: the middle note (a 3rd above the root)
- Fifth: the top note (a 5th above the root)
- Four qualities: major, minor, augmented, diminished

# Musical Context
Triads are the building blocks of harmony. They form the basis of chord progressions, harmonic analysis, and accompaniment patterns. Most tonal music can be analyzed in terms of triads and their extensions.

# Examples
- C major triad: C-E-G (root-third-fifth)
- A minor triad: A-C-E
- B diminished triad: B-D-F
- D augmented triad: D-F#-A#

# Common Confusions
- Root vs. bass: root is the bottom note when stacked in thirds; bass is the lowest note in any voicing
- A triad always has exactly three distinct pitch classes
- Doubled notes (e.g., 4-voice harmony) are still the same triad
