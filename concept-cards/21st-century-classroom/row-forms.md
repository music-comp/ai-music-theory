# Quick Definition
The four row forms are Prime (P), Retrograde (R), Inversion (I), and Retrograde Inversion (RI) - transformations that generate the 48 possible versions of a twelve-tone row.

# Formal Definition
The four basic row forms in twelve-tone technique are: Prime (P) - the original row; Retrograde (R) - the prime form backward; Inversion (I) - the prime with all intervals inverted (opposite direction); and Retrograde Inversion (RI) - the inversion played backward. Each form can be transposed to begin on any of the 12 pitch classes, yielding 48 total row forms (4 x 12).

# Construction/Calculation
The four row forms:
- P (Prime): The original row as composed
- R (Retrograde): Prime form read backward (note 12 to note 1)
- I (Inversion): Each interval goes in the opposite direction
- RI (Retrograde Inversion): Inversion read backward

Transposition labeling:
- P and I: labeled by their STARTING note (P0 starts on C, I3 starts on Eb)
- R and RI: labeled by their ENDING note (R0 ends on C, RI7 ends on G)

# Musical Context
The four row forms and their transpositions provide 48 versions of the series for compositional use. Composers choose row forms based on musical needs - melodic contour, harmonic implications, voice-leading possibilities, or structural plans. The row matrix displays all 48 forms for analytical reference.

# Examples
Given P0: C-Db-Gb-G-F-D-E-Eb-Bb-A-B-G#
- R0: G#-B-A-Bb-Eb-E-D-F-G-Gb-Db-C
- I0: C-B-F#-F-G-Bb-Ab-A-D-Eb-Db-E
- RI0: E-Db-Eb-D-A-Ab-Bb-G-F-F#-B-C

P3 would transpose P0 up 3 semitones (starting on Eb)

# Common Confusions
- "Retrograde Inversion" is the inversion retrograded (not retrograde inverted)
- R and RI are labeled by ENDING note, not starting note
- RI is NOT the same as IR (inversion of retrograde)
- All row forms preserve the row's intervallic structure in some form
