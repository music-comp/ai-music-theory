# Quick Definition
The i-bVII-bVI-bVII progression is a minor-key chord loop with a descending bass line (1-b7-b6) similar to the Andalusian progression, commonly heard in rock and pop music. It can also be analyzed in relative major as vi-V-IV-V.

# Core Definition
The i-bVII-bVI-bVII (e.g., Am-G-F-G) progression is similar to the descending 1-b7-b6-5 bass line of the "Andalusian progression" (Am-G-F-E) in flamenco music, except the last chord is bVII instead of V. It can be rotated to bVI-bVII-i-bVII, and the fourth chord is sometimes eliminated (bVI-bVII-i-i). The progression can also be analyzed in the relative major key as vi-V-IV-V.

# Key Properties
1. Minor-key loop: i-bVII-bVI-bVII
2. Bass line descends: 1-b7-b6, then returns to b7
3. Similar to Andalusian progression but last chord differs
4. Rotation: bVI-bVII-i-bVII
5. Simplified version: bVI-bVII-i-i (fourth chord eliminated)
6. Relative major analysis: vi-V-IV-V

# Construction / Recognition
1. Listen for the descending minor bass line (1-b7-b6)
2. Check whether the last chord is bVII or V (distinguishes from Andalusian)
3. May appear in rotation starting on bVI
4. In major-key analysis, look for vi-V-IV-V

# Context & Application
This progression is common in rock, pop, and hip-hop music. Philip Glass used it in Metamorphosis Two, demonstrating its cross-genre appeal. The descending bass line creates a characteristic melancholy or dramatic effect.

# Examples
- Led Zeppelin, "Stairway to Heaven" (1970) -- i-bVII-bVI-bVII
- Adele, "Rolling In The Deep" (2010) -- i-bVII-bVI-bVII
- "All I Do Is Win" (2010) -- bVI-bVII-i-i rotation (fourth chord eliminated)
- Philip Glass, Metamorphosis Two (1989) -- bVI-bVII-i-i

# Common Errors
- Confusing with the Andalusian cadence (Am-G-F-E), which ends on V
- Not recognizing the rotation bVI-bVII-i

# Common Confusions
- **Same notes, different analysis**: vi-V-IV-V in major = i-bVII-bVI-bVII in relative minor
- **Hearing determines key**: Whether it sounds minor or major depends on perceived tonic
- **Andalusian vs. this progression**: The Andalusian cadence ends on V (E in A minor); this progression ends on bVII (G in A minor)
