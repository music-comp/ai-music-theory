# Quick Definition
A tetrachord is a four-note segment of a scale spanning a perfect fourth.

# Formal Definition
A tetrachord is a four-note scale segment. In the context of the major scale, each tetrachord follows the pattern W-W-H (whole step, whole step, half step). The major scale consists of two identical tetrachords connected by a whole step.

# Construction/Recognition
- Four consecutive scale notes
- Major scale tetrachord pattern: W-W-H
- Lower tetrachord: ^1-^2-^3-^4
- Upper tetrachord: ^5-^6-^7-^8
- Connected by a whole step between ^4 and ^5

# Musical Context
Understanding tetrachords makes it easier to construct and remember scales. The upper tetrachord of one major scale becomes the lower tetrachord of the major scale a fifth higher, which explains the circle of fifths relationship.

# Examples
- In C major: Lower tetrachord C-D-E-F, Upper tetrachord G-A-B-C
- In G major: Lower tetrachord G-A-B-C, Upper tetrachord D-E-F#-G
- The upper tetrachord of C major (G-A-B-C) is the lower tetrachord of G major

# Common Confusions
- A tetrachord is specifically four notes, not just any scale segment
- Both tetrachords in a major scale have the same W-W-H pattern
- Tetrachords are connected by a whole step, not a half step
