# Quick Definition
A tetrachord is a four-note scale segment; in the major scale, each tetrachord follows the pattern W-W-H.

# Core Definition
A tetrachord is a four-note scale segment. In the context of the major scale, the lower tetrachord consists of the pattern whole step, whole step, half step (W-W-H). The upper tetrachord duplicates the pattern in the lower one. A whole step joins the lower tetrachord to the upper tetrachord, creating the complete major scale.

# Prerequisites
- **Half step, whole step**: The interval components of the tetrachord pattern

# Key Properties
1. Four consecutive scale notes
2. Major scale tetrachord pattern: W-W-H
3. Lower tetrachord: ^1-^2-^3-^4
4. Upper tetrachord: ^5-^6-^7-^8
5. Connected by a whole step between ^4 and ^5
6. Both tetrachords are identical in interval pattern

# Construction / Recognition
1. Four notes in stepwise succession
2. In major: W-W-H pattern
3. The upper tetrachord of one major key becomes the lower tetrachord of the key a fifth higher

# Context & Application
Understanding tetrachords makes it easier to construct and remember scales. The upper tetrachord of one major scale becomes the lower tetrachord of the major scale a fifth higher, which explains the circle of fifths relationship between keys.

# Examples
- In C major: lower tetrachord C-D-E-F, upper tetrachord G-A-B-C
- In G major: lower tetrachord G-A-B-C, upper tetrachord D-E-F#-G
- The upper tetrachord of C major (G-A-B-C) is the lower tetrachord of G major

# Common Errors
- Forgetting that both tetrachords have the same W-W-H pattern in major
- Connecting the tetrachords with a half step instead of a whole step

# Common Confusions
- **Specifically four notes**: A tetrachord is specifically four notes, not just any scale segment
- **Connection**: The two tetrachords in a major scale are connected by a whole step, not a half step
- **Sharing**: The upper tetrachord of one key = the lower tetrachord of the key a fifth higher
