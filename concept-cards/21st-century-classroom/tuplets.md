# Quick Definition
A tuplet is a grouping of notes that divides the beat differently than normal, such as triplets (3 notes in the space of 2) or duplets (2 notes in the space of 3).

# Formal Definition
Tuplet is a generic term for a rhythmic grouping that substitutes an irregular number of notes for the normal division of the beat. A triplet places three notes in the space normally occupied by two; a quintuplet places five notes in the space of four; a duplet places two notes in the space normally occupied by three (in compound meter).

# Construction/Recognition
- Triplet: 3 notes in the time of 2 (common in simple meter)
- Duplet: 2 notes in the time of 3 (common in compound meter)
- Quintuplet: 5 notes in the time of 4
- Sextuplet: 6 notes in the time of 4
- Indicated by a number (and often a bracket) over the notes

# Musical Context
Tuplets add rhythmic variety and complexity. Triplets are extremely common for creating a flowing, rolling feel in simple meter. Duplets in compound meter create a straighter, more march-like feel within the prevailing triple division.

# Examples
- Three eighth-note triplet = one quarter note duration in 4/4
- Quarter-note triplet in 4/4 = 3 quarter notes in the space of 2 beats
- Duplet eighth notes in 6/8 = 2 eighths filling one dotted-quarter beat
- Quintuplet sixteenths = 5 sixteenths in the space of one quarter note

# Common Confusions
- If music naturally has triplet divisions, use compound meter instead of constant triplets
- Tuplets are exceptions to normal beat division, not the rule
- The number indicates how many notes fit in the grouping
