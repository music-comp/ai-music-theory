# Quick Definition
Consonance refers to intervals that sound stable (P1, P5, P8, M3, m3, M6, m6); dissonance refers to intervals that sound tense and want to resolve (m2, M2, P4 in some contexts, tritone, m7, M7).

# Core Definition
Consonance and dissonance describe the relative stability or tension of intervals. Perfect consonances (P1, P5, P8) are the most stable. Imperfect consonances (M3, m3, M6, m6) are pleasant but less stable. Dissonances (m2, M2, +4/o5, m7, M7) create tension requiring resolution. The perfect fourth (P4) is contextually consonant or dissonant -- consonant within a chord but potentially dissonant against the bass.

# Key Properties
1. Perfect consonances: P1 (unison), P5, P8 -- most stable
2. Imperfect consonances: M3, m3, M6, m6 -- pleasant, less stable
3. Dissonances: m2, M2, P4 (in some contexts), +4/o5 (tritone), m7, M7
4. P4 is contextually consonant or dissonant
5. Dissonances create tension that typically resolves to consonances
6. These categories have evolved throughout music history

# Construction / Recognition
- Perfect consonances sound open and stable
- Imperfect consonances sound pleasant and full
- Dissonances sound tense, harsh, or expectant
- The tritone is the most characteristically dissonant interval

# Context & Application
The interplay between consonance and dissonance creates tension and release, a fundamental element of musical expression. Dissonances make music interesting; consonances provide resolution. Different style periods treat dissonance differently -- what was dissonant in medieval music may be consonant in jazz.

# Examples
- P5 (C-G) sounds open and stable (perfect consonance)
- M3 (C-E) sounds pleasant and full (imperfect consonance)
- m2 (C-Db) sounds harsh and wants to resolve (dissonance)
- Tritone (B-F) is highly dissonant and drives cadential resolution

# Common Errors
- Assuming dissonance is "bad" (it creates musical interest and expression)
- Treating P4 as always consonant or always dissonant (it depends on context)

# Common Confusions
- **P4 is contextual**: Consonant within a chord but potentially dissonant against the bass
- **Dissonance is not "bad"**: It creates musical interest and forward motion
- **Historical change**: What counts as dissonant has evolved throughout music history
