# Quick Definition
An episode in a fugue is a section that does not contain a complete statement of the subject but develops material through fragmentation and sequential treatment.

# Formal Definition
Episodes are developmental sections in a fugue that occur between expositions. They do not contain complete statements of the subject. Instead, episodes develop motivic material drawn from the subject, answer, and countersubject through techniques such as fragmentation (using only the "head" or "tail" of a theme) and sequence. Episodes often modulate between key areas, connecting one exposition's key to the next.

# Rules/Procedures
1. Episodes contain NO complete statement of the fugue subject
2. Material sources for episodes:
   - Subject head (beginning portion)
   - Subject tail (ending portion)
   - Countersubject fragments
   - Sometimes new material (less common)
3. Developmental techniques:
   - Sequence (most common)
   - Fragmentation
   - Imitation of fragments
   - Motivic combination
4. Episodes typically modulate to set up the next exposition
5. In analysis, identify the source of episodic material and the key journey

# Musical Context
Episodes provide contrast to the formal stability of expositions. The sequential treatment creates momentum, and modulations set up new key areas. Episodes demonstrate the developmental potential of the subject material.

# Examples
- Bach, Fugue 2 in C minor, Episode 1: Uses fragments of subject and countersubject 1
- Typical episode: Subject head in sequence, descending by step through circle of 5ths

# Common Confusions
- Mistaking an incomplete subject statement for an episode (partial subjects can occur in expositions)
- Not recognizing the source of episodic material
- Thinking episodes are unrelated to the subject (they usually derive from it)
- Confusing "episode" (fugue) with "episode" (sonata-rondo form)
