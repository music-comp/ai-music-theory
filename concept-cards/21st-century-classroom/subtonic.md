# Quick Definition
The subtonic is ^7 when it lies a whole step below the tonic, occurring naturally in the natural minor scale and lacking the leading tone's strong pull to resolve.

# Core Definition
The subtonic is a whole step below the tonic, while the leading tone is only a half step below the tonic. The subtonic occurs naturally in natural minor and requires no accidentals in minor keys. The subtonic triad (VII) is built on the lowered ^7 and is a major triad. It regularly occurs in circle-of-fifths progressions in minor and in rock and pop music.

# Prerequisites
- **Scale degree names**: Understanding the ^7 position
- **Natural minor scale**: The scale form where the subtonic occurs

# Key Properties
1. Always a whole step below the tonic
2. Occurs naturally in natural minor (no accidental needed)
3. Does not create the strong upward pull of the leading tone
4. The VII chord (subtonic triad) built on it is major
5. Common in rock, pop, and folk styles
6. "Sub-" means "below" -- the subtonic is simply a tone below the tonic

# Construction / Recognition
- ^7 that is a whole step (not a half step) below ^1
- Found in natural minor without accidentals
- The VII chord is a major triad built on this degree

# Context & Application
The subtonic is common in modal music, rock, pop, and folk styles where the natural minor scale predominates. The VII chord (subtonic triad) is frequently used in these styles, particularly in progressions like VII-i. Both VII (subtonic triad) and viio (leading-tone triad) are included among the most commonly used chords in minor.

# Examples
- In A natural minor: G is the subtonic (whole step below A)
- In E natural minor: D is the subtonic
- In C natural minor: Bb is the subtonic
- Rock progression: G-Am (VII-i in A minor)

# Common Errors
- Confusing subtonic with leading tone
- Raising ^7 when the subtonic (natural ^7) is intended

# Common Confusions
- **Subtonic vs. leading tone**: Subtonic = whole step below tonic; leading tone = half step below
- **Both can appear in minor**: Minor keys use both subtonic and leading tone depending on context
- **VII vs. viio**: The subtonic triad (VII) is major; the leading-tone triad (viio) is diminished
