# Quick Definition
The subtonic is the seventh scale degree when it is a whole step below the tonic, occurring naturally in the natural minor scale.

# Formal Definition
The subtonic is ^7 when it lies a whole step (whole tone) below ^1. It occurs in the natural minor scale and does not have the strong upward pull of the leading tone. The term "subtonic" emphasizes that it is simply a step below the tonic, without the leading function implied by "leading tone."

# Construction/Recognition
- Always a whole step below the tonic
- Occurs naturally in natural minor (no accidentals needed)
- Does not create strong pull to tonic
- The VII chord (subtonic triad) is built on this degree
- Different function from leading tone

# Musical Context
The subtonic is common in modal music, rock, pop, and folk styles where the natural minor scale predominates. The VII chord (subtonic triad) is frequently used in these styles, particularly in progressions like VII-i.

# Examples
- In A natural minor: G is the subtonic (whole step below A)
- In E natural minor: D is the subtonic
- In C natural minor: Bb is the subtonic
- Rock progression example: G-Am (VII-i in A minor)

# Common Confusions
- Subtonic (whole step below tonic) vs. leading tone (half step below tonic)
- The subtonic does not "lead" as strongly to tonic
- In minor keys, both subtonic and leading tone may appear depending on context
