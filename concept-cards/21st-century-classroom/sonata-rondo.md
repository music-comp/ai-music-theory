# Quick Definition
Sonata rondo form replaces the second episode (C) with a development section, creating a hybrid structure: A-B-A-Development-A-B-A.

# Formal Definition
Sonata rondo form is a rondo in which the second episode (the C section) is replaced by a development section, resulting in a design of A-B-A-Dev.-A-B-A. This combines the recurring refrain principle of rondo with the developmental procedures of sonata form.

# Construction/Recognition
Structure: A - B - A - Development - A - B - A

Comparison to 7-part rondo:
- 7-part rondo: A B A C A B A
- Sonata rondo: A B A Dev A B A

Characteristics:
- A (refrain): tonic, expository
- B (episode): secondary key, then tonic (sonata principle)
- Development: fragmentation, sequencing, modulation
- Development replaces what would be the C episode

# Musical Context
- Combines best of both forms
- Common Classical finale form
- Has rondo's accessibility with sonata's drama
- The development adds depth to the rondo structure

# Examples
- Many Beethoven and Mozart finales use this hybrid form
- Combines the light character of rondo with developmental complexity

# Related Concepts
- Rondo form
- Sonata form
- Seven-part rondo
- Development section
- Sonata principle

# Common Confusions
- The "C" section is replaced by DEVELOPMENT, not just omitted
- This is different from simply cutting the C episode
- The form still applies sonata principle (B returns in tonic)
- May be difficult to distinguish from 7-part rondo with developmental C
