# Quick Definition
A riff is a short repeated melodic/rhythmic pattern, usually in the bass or guitar, that defines a song's identity and provides the foundation for its groove.

# Core Definition
Sometimes the identity of a song is in its repeated bass line, known as a "riff" -- a short repeated pattern. Distinctive guitar riffs often work in conjunction with bass to create a song's signature sound. Riffs serve as primary accompaniment, hook, or both.

# Prerequisites
- **Texture**: Understanding of accompaniment types

# Key Properties
1. Short, repeated pattern
2. Usually in bass or guitar (or both)
3. Defines the song's identity and groove
4. Can imply harmony without stating full chords
5. May include subtle variations
6. Bass and guitar riffs often work together

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the repeating pattern
2. Note the rhythmic character
3. Observe the melodic contour and intervals
4. Consider how the riff relates to the underlying harmony
5. Note any variations or developments

# Context & Application
Riffs are central to rock, funk, R&B, and hip-hop music. A memorable riff can define a song and become immediately recognizable. This chapter encourages close listening to texture and accompaniment as a source of compositional ideas.

# Examples
- Bass riffs: "Little Brown Jug" (1869), "Day Tripper" (Beatles, 1965), "Good Times" (Chic, 1979), "Another One Bites the Dust" (Queen, 1980), "Super Freak" (Rick James, 1981), "Under Pressure" (Bowie/Queen, 1981), "Fancy" (2014), "Downtown" (Macklemore, 2015)
- Guitar riffs: "You Really Got Me" (Kinks, 1964), "Immigrant Song" (Led Zeppelin, 1970), "Walk This Way" (Aerosmith, 1975), "Back in Black" (AC/DC, 1980)

# Common Errors
- Assuming riffs must be exactly the same each time (subtle variations are common)

# Common Confusions
- **Riff vs. ostinato**: Similar concepts; "riff" is more common in popular music terminology
- **Guitar and bass together**: Often work as a unit to create the signature sound
- **Riffs imply harmony**: They suggest chords without playing full chords
