# Quick Definition
A riff is a short repeated pattern, usually in the bass or guitar, that defines a song's identity and provides the foundation for its groove.

# Formal Definition
Sometimes the identity of a song is in its repeated bass line, known as a "riff." A riff is a short repeated pattern that serves as the primary accompaniment or hook. Distinctive guitar riffs often work in conjunction with bass to create the song's signature sound.

# Analysis/Application
When analyzing riffs:
1. Identify the repeating pattern
2. Note the rhythmic character
3. Observe the melodic contour
4. Consider how the riff relates to the harmony
5. Note any variations or developments

# Musical Context
Riffs are central to rock, funk, and R&B music. A memorable riff can define a song and become immediately recognizable to listeners.

# Examples
Bass riffs:
- "Little Brown Jug" (1869) - early example
- "Day Tripper" (Beatles, 1965)
- "Good Times" (Chic, 1979)
- "Another One Bites the Dust" (Queen, 1980)
- "Super Freak" (Rick James, 1981)
- "Under Pressure" (Queen/David Bowie, 1981)

Guitar riffs (with bass):
- "You Really Got Me" (The Kinks, 1964)
- "Immigrant Song" (Led Zeppelin, 1970)
- "Walk This Way" (Aerosmith, 1975)
- "Back in Black" (AC/DC, 1980)

# Common Confusions
- Riffs repeat but may include subtle variations
- Guitar and bass riffs often work together
- Riffs can imply harmony without stating full chords
