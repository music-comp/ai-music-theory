# Quick Definition
The fugue subject is the primary melodic idea of a fugue, stated by each voice in turn during the first exposition and returning in various keys throughout the piece.

# Formal Definition
The fugue subject is the main theme upon which the entire fugue is built. It is first stated by one voice alone, then imitated by each subsequent voice during the first exposition. The subject is typically in the tonic key when stated by the first voice and by odd-numbered entries. It establishes the melodic and rhythmic character of the entire fugue and returns (often in different keys) in subsequent expositions.

# Rules/Procedures
1. The subject is the first melodic idea presented in the fugue
2. Each voice states the subject in turn during the first exposition
3. The subject typically:
   - Establishes the key (tonic) clearly
   - Has distinctive melodic/rhythmic character
   - Is of moderate length (usually 1-4 measures)
4. Later appearances may be in different keys
5. The subject can be fragmented in episodes (subject head, subject tail)
6. Some fugues have more than one subject (double fugue, triple fugue)

# Musical Context
The subject is analogous to a theme in sonata form - it's the essential melodic material. Bach's fugue subjects are remarkable for their distinctive character and developmental potential. Each voice's entry with the subject contributes to the contrapuntal texture.

# Examples
- Bach, Fugue 2 in C minor: Subject in first voice (m.1), imitated by second voice (m.3), third voice (m.7)
- The subject can be identified by finding the first complete melodic idea before another voice enters

# Common Confusions
- Confusing subject with answer (subject is in tonic, answer is in dominant)
- Not recognizing the full extent of the subject (where does it end?)
- Thinking the subject must appear exactly the same each time (it can be ornamented or altered)
- Missing subject entries in inner voices
