# Quick Definition
The fugue subject is the primary melodic idea of a fugue, stated by each voice in turn during the first exposition and returning in various keys throughout the piece.

# Core Definition
The fugue subject is the primary melodic idea and is stated by each voice in turn in the first exposition. It establishes the melodic and rhythmic character of the entire fugue and returns in various keys in subsequent expositions. Subjects can be fragmented into head motives and tail motives for development in episodes. An answer (the subject transposed to the dominant) is considered equivalent to a subject for formal analysis purposes (Hutchinson, Ch. 30, pp. 433-434).

# Prerequisites
- **Fugue** -- The subject exists within the fugal form

# Key Properties
1. First melodic idea presented in the fugue (single voice)
2. Each voice enters with the subject in turn during the first exposition
3. Typically establishes the key clearly
4. Has distinctive melodic and rhythmic character
5. Can be divided into head motive and tail motive
6. Returns in different keys in later expositions
7. Fragments used in episodes for development

# Construction / Recognition
**Identifying the subject:**
1. Find the first melodic idea stated by a single voice at the opening
2. The subject ends when the second voice enters
3. Note the intervallic profile (especially the first few intervals)
4. The same intervallic profile identifies subject entries throughout the piece

**Subject can be fragmented:**
- Head motive: opening portion of the subject
- Tail motive: closing portion of the subject
- Both are used in episodes and bridges

# Context & Application
The subject is analogous to a theme in other forms -- it is the essential melodic material that generates the entire composition. Bach's fugue subjects are remarkable for their distinctive character and developmental potential. The intervals within the subject become the DNA of the fugue.

# Examples
- **Bach, Fugue 2 in C minor, WTC Book I** (p. 434): Subject in soprano (m. 1), imitated by alto (m. 3 as answer), then soprano countersubject begins; third voice enters at m. 7

## Contrasts With

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Not identifying where the subject ends
  **Correction**: The subject typically ends when the second voice enters with the answer

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing subject with answer
  **Clarification**: The subject is in the tonic key; the answer is the transposition to the dominant
- **Confusion**: Thinking the subject must appear exactly the same each time
  **Clarification**: Subjects can be slightly ornamented or altered in later appearances
