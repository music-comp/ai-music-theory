# Quick Definition
A double neighbor (or "neighbor group") occurs when both the upper and lower neighbor tones appear before returning to the original pitch.

# Formal Definition
The double neighbor (sometimes called a "neighbor group") occurs when both the upper and lower neighbor occur before the return to the starting tone. The order can be upper-lower-return or lower-upper-return.

# Analysis/Application
To identify double neighbors:
1. Look for a chord tone that moves to both its upper and lower neighbor before returning
2. The pattern can go up-down-return or down-up-return
3. All notes except the outer chord tones are non-chord tones
4. Label as DN or D.N.

# Musical Context
Double neighbors create more elaborate melodic embellishment than single neighbor tones. They are common in Classical and Romantic music, often as ornamental figures.

# Examples
- C major arpeggio embellished with double neighbors (illustrative example)
- Kuhlau, Sonatina in C Major, Op. 55, No. 1, I (1823)
- Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 333, I (1784) - double neighbor on beat 4 of first measure

# Common Confusions
- The double neighbor is sometimes confused with escape tones and appoggiaturas
- Keep track of chord tones when analyzing - the outer notes should be the same chord tone
- Incomplete double neighbor figures occur in jazz and late Romantic music
