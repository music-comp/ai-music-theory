# Quick Definition
A polychord is the simultaneous sounding of two or more triads or seventh chords, creating complex harmonic structures; notated with a horizontal line between chord symbols.

# Core Definition
A polychord typically consists of two triads sounding simultaneously, though it could also comprise two seventh chords, a seventh chord and a triad, or more than two chords. Polychords are notated using a horizontal line between the chord symbols (not a slash, which is reserved for slash chords). Inversions of the component chords need not be specified (Hutchinson, Ch. 32, pp. 472-474).

# Prerequisites
- **Triads** -- Component chords are typically triads or seventh chords
- **Seventh chords** -- May also serve as polychord components

# Key Properties
1. Two or more triads/7th chords sounding simultaneously
2. Notated with horizontal line (not slash)
3. Component chord inversions need not be specified
4. Each chord maintains its identity
5. Creates complex harmony from simpler components

# Construction / Recognition
- Select two triads or seventh chords
- Voice in separate registers
- Notate: upper chord / horizontal line / lower chord

# Context & Application
The most famous polychord is the "Rite of Spring chord" by Stravinsky. Copland also used polychords extensively.

# Examples
- Stravinsky, *Rite of Spring*, "Dance of the Adolescents" (p. 473, Figure 32.4.1): Eb dominant 7th over Fb major ("Rite of Spring chord")
- Copland, *Appalachian Spring* (p. 474, Figure 32.4.2): A major below E major
- Stravinsky, *Petrushka*, Fourth Tableau (p. 474, Figure 32.4.3): Chromatically ascending dom7ths against repeating G-F-C cycle

# Relationships
## Related
- **Bitonality** -- Sustained two-key passages; polychords are more localized
## Contrasts With
- **Slash chords** -- Slash chords specify bass note under a single chord; polychords have two independent harmonies

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Using a slash instead of a horizontal line for polychord notation
  **Correction**: Polychords use a horizontal line; slashes indicate slash chords (bass note)

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking polychords and slash chords are the same
  **Clarification**: Polychords combine two independent harmonies; slash chords specify a single chord over a bass note
