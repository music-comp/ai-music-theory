# Quick Definition
An appoggiatura is a non-chord tone approached by leap and left by step, typically accented (on the beat), making it a particularly expressive embellishment.

# Formal Definition
The appoggiatura is approached by leap and left by step. It is usually accented (occurring on the beat), which makes it a particularly expressive non-chord tone. Both the leap to and step away from an appoggiatura can be from any direction (ascending or descending).

# Analysis/Application
To identify an appoggiatura:
1. Look for a non-chord tone that is approached by leap
2. Confirm it resolves by step
3. Note that it is typically on a strong beat
4. Label as App. or APP

# Musical Context
Appoggiaturas create strong melodic tension because they are accented dissonances that resolve by step. They are common in expressive, lyrical passages in both classical and popular music.

# Examples
- "My Heart Will Go On" (James Horner, 1997) - appoggiatura creating emotional expression
- Bruce Springsteen, "Born to Run" (1975)
- Mozart, Symphony No. 40, K. 550, II - appoggiaturas resolving upward
- "Want to Want Me" (2015) - appoggiatura in bar 2

# Related Concepts

# Common Confusions
- Unlike suspensions, appoggiaturas are approached by leap, not by same note
- Appoggiaturas can resolve either up or down by step
- The leap can be from above or below
