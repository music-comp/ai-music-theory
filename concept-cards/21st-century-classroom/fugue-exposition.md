# Quick Definition
A fugue exposition is a section containing at least one complete statement of the subject; the first exposition features entries of all voices, while later expositions may have fewer entries.

# Core Definition
A fugue exposition is a section that contains at least one full statement of the subject of the fugue. The first exposition is unique: all voices state the subject at least once, alternating between subject (tonic) and answer (dominant). Later expositions (sometimes called "middle entries") feature subject statements in various related keys but do not necessarily include all voices. An answer is considered equivalent to a subject because the intervallic alterations are minimal (Hutchinson, Ch. 30, pp. 433-434).

# Prerequisites
- **Fugue** -- Must understand the overall fugal form
- **Fugue subject** -- Expositions are defined by subject statements

# Key Properties
1. Contains at least one complete statement of the fugue subject
2. First exposition: all voices enter in turn with subject or answer
3. Later expositions: at least one complete subject statement, possibly in different keys
4. Entries alternate subject (tonic) and answer (dominant)
5. Expositions establish clear key areas
6. Separated by episodes

# Construction / Recognition
**Identifying expositions:**
1. Look for complete statements of the subject
2. First exposition: all voices enter one by one
3. Later expositions: at least one full subject statement in a new key
4. Expositions provide formal stability between developmental episodes
5. Note the key area of each exposition for tonal analysis

# Context & Application
The alternation of expositions and episodes gives fugue its formal shape. Expositions provide thematic stability while episodes develop material. The key areas of later expositions create the fugue's tonal journey, typically visiting related keys before returning to the tonic for the final exposition.

# Examples
- **Bach, Fugue 2 in C minor** (pp. 434-437): First exposition (mm. 1-8) with Subject-Answer-Subject entries; second, third, fourth, and fifth (final) expositions in related keys, returning to tonic

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking only the first section is an "exposition"
  **Correction**: Later subject groups are also expositions (or "middle entries")

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing expositions with episodes
  **Clarification**: Expositions contain at least one complete subject statement; episodes develop fragments without complete statements
- **Confusion**: Thinking all voices must participate in every exposition
  **Clarification**: Only the first exposition requires all voices; later expositions need only one complete subject
