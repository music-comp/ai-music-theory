# Quick Definition
A fugue exposition is a section containing at least one complete statement of the subject; the first exposition features entries of all voices, while later expositions may have fewer entries.

# Formal Definition
A fugue exposition is a structural section containing at least one complete statement of the fugue subject. The first exposition is special: all voices enter in succession with either the subject (tonic key) or answer (dominant key). Later expositions (sometimes called "middle entries") feature subject statements in various keys, but not necessarily in all voices. Expositions typically establish clear key areas and provide formal stability between the developmental episodes.

# Rules/Procedures
1. **First exposition**: All voices enter with subject or answer
   - Voices typically alternate: Subject - Answer - Subject (or S-A-S-A for 4 voices)
   - Establishes tonic key
   - All voices must participate
2. **Later expositions**: At least one complete subject statement
   - May occur in related keys (relative major/minor, subdominant, etc.)
   - Not all voices need participate
   - Some theorists call these "middle entries"
3. In analysis, identify the key area of each exposition
4. Expositions are separated by episodes

# Musical Context
The alternation of expositions and episodes gives fugue its formal shape. Expositions provide thematic stability while episodes develop material sequentially. The key areas of later expositions help create the fugue's tonal journey.

# Examples
- Bach, Fugue 2 in C minor: First exposition (mm. 1-8) has S-A-S entries
- Second exposition begins around m. 11 in relative major (Eb)
- Fifth (final) exposition returns to tonic

# Common Confusions
- Thinking only the first section is an "exposition" (later subject groups are also expositions)
- Not distinguishing expositions from episodes (expositions have complete subjects)
- Missing the key areas of later expositions
- Confusing exposition with entire fugue form
