# Quick Definition

Classical symphonies, sonatas, and quartets typically follow a four-movement structure: sonata form (I), slow movement form (II), minuet and trio (III), and rondo or sonata form (IV).

# Core Definition

The forms commonly encountered in various movements of Classical symphonies, string quartets, and sonatas follow a conventional pattern (Hutchinson, Ch. 25, p. 382):
1. First movement: Sonata form
2. Second movement: Ternary form (ABA), sonatina form, or five-part rondo
3. Third movement: Minuet and Trio (compound ternary)
4. Fourth movement: Rondo form, sonata form, or sonata rondo form

# Prerequisites

- **Sonata form** -- Standard first-movement form
- **Ternary form** -- Option for second movements
- **Rondo form** -- Option for fourth movements
- **Compound ternary** -- Standard third-movement form (minuet and trio)

# Key Properties

1. First movement: sonata form, usually fast (Allegro)
2. Second movement: ternary, sonatina, or five-part rondo, usually slow
3. Third movement: minuet and trio (compound ternary), moderate tempo
4. Fourth movement: rondo, sonata, or sonata rondo, usually fast
5. Three-movement works often omit the minuet

# Construction / Recognition

## Standard Four-Movement Plan:
1. **Movement I**: Sonata form -- fast, most substantial
2. **Movement II**: Ternary / Sonatina / Five-part rondo -- slow
3. **Movement III**: Minuet and Trio -- moderate, dance-like
4. **Movement IV**: Rondo / Sonata / Sonata rondo -- fast, often rondo character

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven symphonies, quartets, sonatas
- **Historical/stylistic notes**: Three-movement sonatas omit the minuet; Beethoven sometimes used scherzo instead of minuet

# Examples

**Example 1**: Haydn, Mozart, and Beethoven symphonies follow this plan
**Example 2**: Classical string quartets use the same structure
**Example 3**: Classical piano sonatas (often three movements, omitting the minuet)

# Relationships

## Related
- **Sonatina form** -- Option for slow movements
- **Sonata rondo** -- Option for finales
- **Five-part rondo** -- Option for slow movements

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Expecting all Classical works to have exactly four movements
  **Correction**: Three-movement sonatas are common (omit the minuet)

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the plan is rigid and absolute
  **Clarification**: This is a common pattern, not a rule; individual works may vary
