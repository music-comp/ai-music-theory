# Quick Definition
Seventh chords have four possible positions: root position, first inversion (3rd in bass), second inversion (5th in bass), and third inversion (7th in bass).

# Formal Definition
Because seventh chords have four notes, they have four possible positions based on which note is in the bass. Root position has the root in bass. First inversion has the third in bass. Second inversion has the fifth in bass. Third inversion has the seventh in bass. Figured bass symbols distinguish these positions.

# Construction/Recognition
- Root position: root in bass (figured bass: 7)
- First inversion: 3rd in bass (figured bass: 6/5)
- Second inversion: 5th in bass (figured bass: 4/3)
- Third inversion: 7th in bass (figured bass: 4/2 or just 2)
- The root is always determined by stacking in thirds

# Musical Context
Inversions allow for smoother bass lines and voice leading. Third inversion (with the seventh in bass) creates particularly strong voice-leading pull, as the seventh typically resolves down by step.

# Examples
- G7 root position: G-B-D-F
- G7 first inversion: B-D-F-G (G7/B or G6/5)
- G7 second inversion: D-F-G-B (G7/D or G4/3)
- G7 third inversion: F-G-B-D (G7/F or G4/2)

# Common Confusions
- "Third inversion" has the 7th in bass, not the 3rd
- Seventh chords have one more inversion than triads
- Figured bass numbers indicate intervals above the bass, not chord tones
