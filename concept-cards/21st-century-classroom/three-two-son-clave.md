# Quick Definition
The 3-2 son clave is an Afro-Cuban rhythmic pattern with three attacks in the first bar and two in the second; its first bar features the 3+3+2 subdivision that has become pervasive in popular music from the 1950s to the present.

# Core Definition
The 3-2 son clave (and 2-3 son clave) are essential rhythmic elements in Afro-Cuban music. The first bar has three attacks following a 3+3+2 grouping (3+3+2 = 8 eighth notes). This rhythm, also called the "Bo Diddley Beat," has influenced music from 1950s rock to contemporary pop. The 3+3+2 pattern can appear in eighth notes or sixteenth notes (the sixteenth-note version is called tresillo). The reggaeton beat is built from this rhythm and relates to the habanera pattern. Extensions include: (3+3+2)+(3+3+2), 3+3+3+3+2+2, and 8 groups of 3 plus 4 groups of 2.

# Key Properties
1. 3-2 son clave: 3 attacks in bar 1, 2 attacks in bar 2
2. First bar = 3+3+2 grouping of 8 pulses
3. Creates syncopation against regular meter
4. Can appear in eighth notes or sixteenth notes (tresillo)
5. Extensions: (3+3+2)+(3+3+2), 3+3+3+3+2+2, 8x3+4x2
6. Reggaeton beat built from this pattern
7. Related to habanera (Bizet, Carmen)
8. Other combinations: 3+3+4+3+3

# Construction / Recognition
1. Listen for groups of 3+3+2 against the regular meter
2. Count: 1-2-3, 1-2-3, 1-2 (within 8 pulses)
3. The pattern creates accent on beats 1, the "and" of 2, and 4 (in 4/4)
4. May repeat: (3+3+2)+(3+3+2) fills two bars

# Context & Application
The 3+3+2 pattern appears across genres from Afro-Cuban music to 1950s rock (Bo Diddley) to contemporary pop, EDM, and reggaeton. It crosses cultural boundaries from Bizet's habanera to Ligeti's chamber music. It is one of the most important rhythmic patterns in global popular music.

# Examples
- Son clave / Bo Diddley Beat: "Pretty Thing," "I Want Candy," "Faith" (George Michael), U2 "Desire"
- 3+3+2: "Eye of the Tiger" (intro, 1982), "Girls Just Want to Have Fun" (1983)
- (3+3+2)+(3+3+2): "All of Me" (John Legend, 2013), "Shape of You" (Ed Sheeran, 2017), "Clocks" (Coldplay, 2003, in 8th notes), Ligeti Trio for Violin Horn Piano II (1982)
- Reggaeton: "Dem Bow" (1990), "One Dance" (Drake, 2016), "Despacito" (2017), "Sorry" (Bieber)
- Habanera: Bizet Carmen (1870)
- 3+3+3+3+2+2: U2 "Beautiful Day," "Hold My Hand" (Glynne, 2015)
- 3+3+4+3+3: "Party Rock Anthem" (2011), "Starships" (2012), "Problem" (Ariana Grande, 2014), "Tik Tok" (2009)

# Common Errors
- Confusing 3-2 clave with 2-3 clave (order of bars differs)
- Not recognizing the 3+3+2 at different rhythmic levels (8th vs. 16th notes)

# Common Confusions
- **3-2 vs. 2-3 clave**: Which bar has 3 attacks and which has 2
- **Eighth notes vs. sixteenth notes**: The pattern can appear at different levels (16th-note version = tresillo)
- **Many extensions exist**: (3+3+2)+(3+3+2), 3+3+3+3+2+2, 3+3+4+3+3, etc.
- **Reggaeton connection**: The reggaeton beat derives from 3+3+2 and relates to the habanera
