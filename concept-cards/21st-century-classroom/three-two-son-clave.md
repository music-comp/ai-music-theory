# Quick Definition
The 3-2 son clave is an Afro-Cuban rhythmic pattern with three attacks in the first bar and two in the second, featuring the characteristic 3+3+2 subdivision that appears throughout popular music.

# Formal Definition
The 3-2 son clave is essential to Afro-Cuban music. The first bar contains three attacks following a 3+3+2 grouping (three eighth notes + three eighth notes + two eighth notes = 8 eighth notes). This rhythm has influenced popular music from Bo Diddley to contemporary pop. The pattern can appear in eighth notes or sixteenth notes (tresillo).

# Analysis/Application
The 3+3+2 rhythm:
- Groups 8 pulses as 3+3+2 instead of 4+4
- Creates syncopation against the regular meter
- Can be extended: (3+3+2)+(3+3+2), 3+3+3+3+2+2, or other variations
- Related to habanera and reggaeton rhythms

# Musical Context
The 3+3+2 pattern (also called "Bo Diddley Beat") appears across genres from 1950s rock to contemporary pop. It creates rhythmic interest through displacement against the regular meter. The reggaeton beat is built from this rhythm.

# Examples
3-2 Son Clave / Bo Diddley Beat:
- "Pretty Thing" (Willie Dixon)
- "I Want Candy" (1965)
- "Faith" (George Michael)
- U2, "Desire"

3+3+2 rhythm:
- "Eye of the Tiger" (1982) - introduction
- "Girls Just Want to Have Fun" (1983)

(3+3+2)+(3+3+2):
- "All of Me" (John Legend, 2013)
- "Shape of You" (Ed Sheeran, 2017)
- "Clocks" (Coldplay, 2003) - in eighth notes

Reggaeton (related pattern):
- "Despacito" (2017)
- "One Dance" (Drake, 2016)

# Common Confusions
- 3-2 clave refers to which bar has 3 vs. 2 attacks (also exists as 2-3 clave)
- The 3+3+2 pattern can appear at different rhythmic levels (eighth notes or sixteenth notes)
- Many variations and extensions of the basic pattern exist
