# Quick Definition
Tritone substitution replaces a dominant seventh chord with another dominant seventh whose root is a tritone away, exploiting their shared guide tones.

# Core Definition
A tritone substitution replaces a dominant seventh chord with the dominant seventh chord built a tritone away. This works because the two chords share the same guide tones (3rd and 7th), which are simply exchanged. The bII7 can substitute for V7 because they have identical guide tones. In C, Db9(13) is often substituted for G7(#9/#5); their voicings (not counting roots) are identical (Hutchinson, Ch. 31, p. 455).

# Prerequisites
- **Guide tones** -- Tritone substitution is based on shared guide tones
- **Altered dominant seventh chords** -- Altered dominants have the same voicings as their tritone subs

# Key Properties
1. Replaces V7 with bII7 (roots a tritone apart)
2. Both chords share the same guide tones (3rds and 7ths swap)
3. Creates chromatic bass motion (e.g., Dm7-Db7-C instead of Dm7-G7-C)
4. Voicings for G7(#9/#5) and Db9(13) are identical (minus roots)
5. Only applies to dominant 7th chords

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify a dominant 7th chord (e.g., G7)
2. Find the root a tritone away (G to Db)
3. Build a dominant 7th on that root (Db7)
4. Guide tones: G7 (B and F) = Db7 (F and Cb/B) -- same pitches swapped

# Context & Application
Tritone substitution is fundamental to bebop and modern jazz. It creates chromatic bass motion adding sophistication to standard progressions. The A7(#9/#5) and Eb9(13) voicings are identical in the blues progression example (p. 456), as are G7(#9/#5) and Db9(13).

# Examples
- G7 replaced by Db7 (both resolve to C, p. 455)
- ii-V-I becomes ii-bII-I: Dm7-Db7-CΔ7
- Turnaround with subs: E7-Eb7-D7-Db7
- Blues: A7(#9/#5) and Eb9(13) have identical voicings (p. 456)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Applying tritone substitution to non-dominant chords
  **Correction**: Tritone substitution works only for dominant 7th chords

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the sub chord and original share all notes
  **Clarification**: They share guide tones (3rd and 7th) only; roots and other notes differ
