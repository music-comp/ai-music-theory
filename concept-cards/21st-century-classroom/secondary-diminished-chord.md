# Quick Definition
A secondary diminished chord is a diminished triad or seventh chord that functions as vii° of a chord other than the tonic, resolving up by half step to tonicize that chord.

# Formal Definition
The vii° chord has dominant function and typically resolves to chords of tonic function. Diminished triads, half-diminished seventh chords, and fully-diminished seventh chords can all function as secondary diminished chords, tonicizing diatonic chords other than I. The root of a secondary diminished chord is a minor second below the root of the chord being tonicized.

# Analysis/Application
Secondary diminished chords contain chromaticism:
- The raised note acts as leading tone to the target chord
- Minor triads are tonicized by fully-diminished seventh chords
- Major triads are traditionally tonicized by half-diminished seventh chords (but can use fully-diminished)

# Musical Context
Secondary diminished chords are common in sequences and provide chromatic color similar to secondary dominants. They often appear in circle-of-fifths progressions.

# Examples
- J.S. Bach, "Jesu, Joy of Man's Desiring" (1723) - secondary diminished chords in sequence
- Tchaikovsky, The Nutcracker, "Trepak" (1892)
- Billy Joel, "Just the Way You Are" (1977)
- "Friends in Low Places" (1990)
- Queen, "We Are the Champions" (Freddie Mercury, 1977)

# Common Confusions
- The root of vii°/x is a minor second BELOW the target root (not a P5 above)
- vii°/III in minor is the same as ii° (no accidentals) but functions differently
- Fully-diminished chords tonicize minor triads; half-diminished traditionally tonicize major triads
