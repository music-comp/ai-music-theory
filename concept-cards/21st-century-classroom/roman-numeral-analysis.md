# Quick Definition
Roman numerals represent chords by the scale degree of their root: uppercase for major triads (I, IV, V), lowercase for minor (ii, iii, vi), with symbols for diminished (viio) and augmented (III+).

# Core Definition
We will use Roman numerals to represent chords within a specified key. The Roman numeral "I" represents a triad built on ^1, or the 1st note of the scale. Uppercase Roman numerals represent major triads and lowercase Roman numerals (e.g., "i") represent minor triads. Uppercase Roman numerals with a "+" are augmented (e.g., "III+"), and lowercase Roman numerals with an "o" are diminished (e.g., "viio"). Thinking of music in terms of numbers is helpful with transposition (moving melodies and/or chord progressions from one key to another) and helps us see patterns between many different pieces in different keys.

# Key Properties
1. Numeral indicates scale degree of the chord root (I through vii)
2. Uppercase = major triad; lowercase = minor triad
3. Uppercase + "+" = augmented; lowercase + "o" = diminished
4. Key-independent: same numerals apply in any key
5. Inversions: I/3rd (third in bass), I/5th (fifth in bass) before figured bass
6. Related to the Nashville Number System (Arabic numbers for chords)

# Construction / Recognition
- Determine the key
- Find the scale degree of the chord root
- Assign uppercase or lowercase based on quality
- Add "+" for augmented, "o" for diminished
- Note inversions with /3rd or /5th

# Context & Application
Roman numeral analysis allows musicians to understand chord progressions independent of key and to transpose music easily. It reveals common patterns like I-IV-V-I that occur across countless pieces in different keys. Thinking of music in terms of numbers helps analyze it and spot similar patterns.

# Examples
- In C major: C=I, Dm=ii, Em=iii, F=IV, G=V, Am=vi, Bo=viio
- In G major: G=I, Am=ii, Bm=iii, C=IV, D=V, Em=vi, F#o=viio
- In g minor: V/3rd means the dominant chord with its third in the bass (D/F# in G minor)
- The progression I-IV-V-I works in any major key

# Common Errors
- Forgetting that the numeral indicates scale degree, not position in a progression
- Using uppercase for a minor chord or vice versa

# Common Confusions
- **Uppercase/lowercase = quality**: Uppercase means major, lowercase means minor -- not loudness or importance
- **Function vs. specific notes**: Roman numerals show function (transferable across keys); lead-sheet symbols show specific notes
- **Nashville Number System**: Uses Arabic numbers (1, 4, 5) instead of Roman numerals for the same purpose
