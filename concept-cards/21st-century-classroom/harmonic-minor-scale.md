# Quick Definition
The harmonic minor scale raises ^7 of natural minor by a half step to create a leading tone, producing a characteristic augmented second between ^6 and ^7.

# Core Definition
The harmonic minor scale modifies the natural minor by raising ^7 by a half step. This creates a leading tone (half step below tonic) essential for strong cadences and dominant function in minor keys. The raised ^7 produces an augmented second interval between ^6 and raised ^7, giving the scale its distinctive sound. Writing harmonic minor when using minor key signatures requires raising ^7 with an accidental.

# Prerequisites
- **Natural minor scale**: The unmodified form from which harmonic minor is derived

# Key Properties
1. Raises ^7 from natural minor by one half step
2. Creates a leading tone (half step below tonic)
3. Characteristic augmented second (A2) between ^6 and ^7
4. Half steps between ^2-^3, ^5-^6, and ^7-^8
5. The raised ^7 requires an accidental (not in key signature)
6. Provides the major V chord and viio chord in minor keys

# Construction / Recognition
- Start with natural minor, raise ^7 by a half step
- Or: use minor key signature, add accidental to raise ^7
- The A2 between ^6 and ^7 is the audible giveaway

# Context & Application
The harmonic minor scale provides the dominant chord (V) with a major quality and leading tone, essential for creating strong harmonic progressions and cadences in minor keys. The augmented second gives it a distinctive "exotic" sound that composers sometimes avoid melodically.

# Examples
- A harmonic minor: A-B-C-D-E-F-G#-A (G raised to G#)
- E harmonic minor: E-F#-G-A-B-C-D#-E
- C harmonic minor: C-D-Eb-F-G-Ab-B-C (Bb raised to B natural)

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Natural minor scale

## Enables
- Major V chord in minor, viio chord in minor, strong authentic cadences in minor

## Related
- Melodic minor scale (also raises ^7, and additionally raises ^6 to smooth the A2)
- Leading tone (the raised ^7)
- Diatonic chords in minor (V and viio come from harmonic minor)

## Contrasts With
- Natural minor scale (which has lowered ^7, no leading tone)
- Melodic minor scale (which also raises ^6 to avoid the A2)

# Common Errors
- Forgetting to notate the raised ^7 as an accidental (it is not in the key signature)
- Confusing the A2 interval with a minor third

# Common Confusions
- **Accidental required**: The raised ^7 must be notated with an accidental; it is not in the key signature
- **A2 interval**: The augmented second (^6 to raised ^7) sounds like a minor third but is spelled differently
- **Harmonic purpose**: This scale explains harmony (V-i cadences) but composers often avoid the A2 in melodies
