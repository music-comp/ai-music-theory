# Quick Definition
The harmonic minor scale raises the seventh scale degree of natural minor by a half step, creating a leading tone that pulls strongly to the tonic.

# Formal Definition
The harmonic minor scale modifies the natural minor by raising ^7 by a half step. This creates a leading tone (half step below tonic) and results in an augmented second interval between ^6 and ^7. The scale pattern is W-H-W-W-H-A2-H (where A2 = augmented second).

# Construction/Recognition
- Start with natural minor, raise ^7 by a half step
- Contains a raised ^7 (leading tone)
- Characteristic augmented second between ^6 and ^7
- Half steps between ^2-^3, ^5-^6, and ^7-^8
- The raised ^7 requires an accidental in notation

# Musical Context
The harmonic minor scale provides the dominant chord (V) with a major quality and leading tone, essential for creating strong harmonic progressions and cadences in minor keys. The augmented second interval gives it a distinctive "exotic" sound.

# Examples
- A harmonic minor: A-B-C-D-E-F-G#-A (G raised to G#)
- E harmonic minor: E-F#-G-A-B-C-D#-E
- C harmonic minor: C-D-Eb-F-G-Ab-B-C (Bb raised to B natural)
- The V chord in minor comes from harmonic minor

# Common Confusions
- The raised ^7 must be notated with an accidental (not in key signature)
- The augmented second (^6 to ^7) is a melodic "bump" composers often avoid
- Harmonic minor explains harmony but composers often avoid the A2 melodically
