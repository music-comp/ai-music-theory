# Quick Definition
Chromatic mediants are chords with roots a third apart that share only one common tone and have the same quality (both major or both minor).

# Formal Definition
Chromatic mediants are chords with roots a third apart (mediant relationship) that share only one common tone and have the same quality. This distinguishes them from diatonic mediants, which may share two common tones or have different qualities. Any major or minor triad has four chromatic mediants.

# Construction/Recognition
Criteria for chromatic mediant relationship:
1. Root of second chord is a third lower or higher
2. Both chords have the SAME quality (both major or both minor)
3. The two chords share ONLY ONE common tone

Four chromatic mediants of C major:
- E major (share E)
- Eb major (share G)
- A major (share E)
- Ab major (share C)

Four chromatic mediants of C minor:
- E minor (share E)
- Eb minor (share G)
- A minor (share E)
- Ab minor (share C)

# Musical Context
- Common in Romantic-era music and film scores
- Creates dramatic harmonic color without modulation
- Often used in common-tone modulations
- The single common tone provides connection while maximizing harmonic contrast

# Examples
Chromatic mediant progressions (not modulation):
- Wagner, Das Rheingold, Scene 3 (1854) - i to bVI (Cm to Abm)
- John Williams, Star Wars: Return of the Jedi, "Main Title" (1983)
- John Williams, The Phantom Menace, "Duel of the Fates" (1999)

# Common Confusions
- Diatonic mediants may share TWO common tones (e.g., C major and A minor)
- CHROMATIC mediants share only ONE common tone
- Both chords must have the same quality
- Chromatic mediants can be used progressively (without modulation) or in modulation
