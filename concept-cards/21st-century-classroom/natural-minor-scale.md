# Quick Definition
The natural minor scale uses only the notes of the minor key signature (no accidentals), with the pattern W-H-W-W-H-W-W and lowered ^3, ^6, and ^7 compared to parallel major.

# Core Definition
There are three minor scales: the natural minor scale, the harmonic minor scale, and the melodic minor scale. The natural minor scale uses only the notes found in the minor key signature without any alterations. Its intervallic pattern is W-H-W-W-H-W-W. Compared to the parallel major, the natural minor has lowered ^3, ^6, and ^7. Minor key signatures agree with the notes of the natural minor scale.

# Prerequisites
- **Major scale**: The reference point for understanding lowered degrees

# Key Properties
1. Pattern: W-H-W-W-H-W-W
2. Half steps between ^2-^3 and ^5-^6
3. Uses notes exactly as written in the minor key signature (no extra accidentals)
4. Lowered ^3, ^6, and ^7 compared to parallel major
5. The descending melodic minor is identical to natural minor
6. ^7 is a whole step below ^1 (subtonic, not leading tone)

# Construction / Recognition
- Apply the minor key signature with no additional accidentals
- Or: take parallel major and lower ^3, ^6, ^7
- Pattern: W-H-W-W-H-W-W

# Context & Application
Natural minor provides the foundation for minor key signatures and is commonly used in popular music, rock, folk, and modal compositions. Compositions in minor typically do not strictly use only one of the three minor scales; the three minor scales are distillations of composers' actual practice.

# Examples
- A natural minor: A-B-C-D-E-F-G-A (all white keys)
- E natural minor: E-F#-G-A-B-C-D-E
- C natural minor: C-D-Eb-F-G-Ab-Bb-C

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Major scale

## Enables
- Minor key signatures, understanding of harmonic and melodic minor as modifications

## Related
- Harmonic minor scale (raises ^7 for leading tone)
- Melodic minor scale (raises ^6 and ^7 ascending)
- Relative major/minor (same key signature)
- Subtonic (^7 in natural minor)

## Contrasts With
- Major scale (brighter; half steps at ^3-^4 and ^7-^8)
- Harmonic minor scale (raises ^7 to create leading tone)

# Common Errors
- Adding a leading tone (raised ^7) when natural minor is intended
- Confusing the key signature with the scale (natural minor matches the key signature exactly)

# Common Confusions
- **Subtonic vs. leading tone**: Natural minor has a subtonic (whole step below tonic), not a leading tone
- **Key signature basis**: Natural minor is the form that agrees with the minor key signature
- **Compositional practice**: Real music freely mixes all three minor scale forms
