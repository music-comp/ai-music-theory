# Quick Definition
The natural minor scale is a seven-note scale following the pattern W-H-W-W-H-W-W, with half steps between ^2-^3 and ^5-^6.

# Formal Definition
The natural minor scale (also called the pure minor or Aeolian mode) uses only the notes found in the minor key signature without any alterations. Its intervallic pattern is W-H-W-W-H-W-W. Compared to the parallel major, the natural minor has lowered ^3, ^6, and ^7.

# Construction/Recognition
- Pattern: W-H-W-W-H-W-W
- Half steps occur between ^2-^3 and ^5-^6
- Uses notes exactly as written in the minor key signature
- Lowered ^3, ^6, and ^7 compared to parallel major
- The descending melodic minor is identical to natural minor

# Musical Context
Natural minor is one of three common forms of the minor scale. It provides the foundation for minor key signatures and is commonly used in popular music, rock, folk, and modal compositions. It creates a darker, more melancholic sound than major.

# Examples
- A natural minor: A-B-C-D-E-F-G-A (all white keys)
- E natural minor: E-F#-G-A-B-C-D-E
- C natural minor: C-D-Eb-F-G-Ab-Bb-C
- Half steps: B-C and E-F in A natural minor

# Common Confusions
- Natural minor has a subtonic (whole step below tonic), not a leading tone
- The ^7 in natural minor does not strongly pull to the tonic
- Natural minor is the basis for minor key signatures
