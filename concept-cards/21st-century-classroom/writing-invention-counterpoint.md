# Quick Definition
Counterpoint accompanying theme statements in inventions must emphasize the underlying harmony, which may require altering the theme itself or creating "microharmonies" within measures.

# Formal Definition
When writing counterpoint for invention expositions, the accompanying line must support the harmonic function of each measure (I or V). This differs from species counterpoint because tonal considerations take precedence. Themes may need alteration when transposed to fit the harmonic context, and individual measures may contain "microharmonies" (brief subsidiary harmonic motions like I-IV-I or I-vii-6-I) even when the overriding harmony is stable.

# Rules/Procedures
1. **Counterpoint must emphasize the harmony** in each measure
2. **Altering themes for harmony**:
   - When theme moves from I to V, it may need alteration at end of m.4 to emphasize V and lead to I
   - When theme lands on scale degree 3 instead of 5, alter end of m.2 to reach scale degree 5 at start of m.3
3. **Minor key themes with 2 and 3**: When transposed up a 5th (mm. 3-4), become raised-6 and raised-7
   - Use melodic minor scale for mm. 3-4
   - Raised-7 is the 3rd of V; raised-6 is typically a passing tone
4. **Microharmonies**: Brief harmonic motions within a measure (e.g., I-IV-I, I-vii-6-I)
   - Overriding harmony remains I or V
   - Common in Bach's inventions

# Musical Context
These techniques bridge strict species counterpoint with the harmonic demands of tonal music. Bach's C major invention demonstrates microharmonies in mm. 1-2 where brief IV and vii-6 chords appear within predominant I harmony.

# Examples
- Theme ending on scale degree 3: Alter last note to lead to 5 for next measure
- Minor key theme with D-E: When transposed to V area, becomes A-B (raised-6 and raised-7)
- Microharmony: C major invention, m.1: I-IV-I within overall I

# Common Confusions
- Writing species-style counterpoint that ignores the underlying harmony
- Not altering themes when the transposition creates harmonic problems
- Forgetting to raise 6 and 7 in minor when theme moves to V area
- Mistaking microharmonies for actual harmonic changes
