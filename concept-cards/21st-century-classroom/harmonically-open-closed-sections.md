# Quick Definition
Harmonically closed sections end on the tonic chord (I), providing resolution, while harmonically open sections end on a non-tonic chord (typically V or IV), creating forward momentum.

# Formal Definition
A harmonically closed section ends on the tonic chord (I). A harmonically open section typically ends on a non-tonic chord like V, but could also end on IV. The placement of open and closed sections is crucial to the flow of music in both AABA and verse-chorus forms.

# Analysis/Application
Typical patterns:
- In AABA: A sections are usually closed (end on I); B section is usually open (ends on V)
- In verse-chorus: Verses, pre-choruses, and bridges are usually open; choruses can be open or closed
- Harmonically open sections create momentum toward the next section

# Musical Context
Understanding harmonic openness and closure is essential for composing effective song forms. Open sections propel the music forward while closed sections provide resolution.

# Examples
- "We Are the World" - verse and bridge are harmonically open (end on V); chorus is harmonically closed (ends on I)

# Common Confusions
- "Open" doesn't mean "incomplete" - it means ending on a non-tonic chord
- Choruses can be either open or closed depending on the song
- Harmonically open sections naturally lead into the following section
