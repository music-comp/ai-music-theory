# Quick Definition
Harmonically closed sections end on the tonic chord (I), providing resolution, while harmonically open sections end on a non-tonic chord (typically V or IV), creating forward momentum into the next section.

# Core Definition
A harmonically closed section ends on the tonic chord (I). A harmonically open section typically ends on a non-tonic chord like V, but could also end on IV. Placing harmonically open and closed sections appropriately is essential to musical flow in both AABA and verse-chorus forms.

# Prerequisites
- **Cadence**: Understanding of phrase endings
- **Verse-chorus form**: Structural context for open/closed sections

# Key Properties
1. Closed = ends on I (tonic)
2. Open = ends on V, IV, or other non-tonic chord
3. In AABA: A sections usually closed; B usually open
4. In verse-chorus: verses, pre-choruses, bridges usually open; choruses can be either
5. Open sections create forward momentum
6. Closed sections provide resolution

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the final chord of each section
2. If it ends on I, the section is harmonically closed
3. If it ends on V, IV, or another non-tonic chord, it is harmonically open
4. Map the pattern of open and closed sections across the entire form

# Context & Application
Understanding harmonic openness and closure is essential for composing effective song forms. Open sections propel the music forward into the next section, while closed sections provide arrival points. Composers strategically place open and closed sections to control formal flow.

# Examples
- "We Are the World" -- verse and bridge are open (end on V); chorus is closed (ends on I)
- AABA general pattern: A sections closed (I); B section open (V)

# Common Errors
- Assuming all choruses are harmonically closed (they can be open)

# Common Confusions
- **"Open" does not mean "incomplete"**: It means ending on a non-tonic chord, which creates forward motion
- **Choruses can be open or closed**: It depends on the song
- **Compositional tool**: Placing open sections before choruses creates momentum into the hook
