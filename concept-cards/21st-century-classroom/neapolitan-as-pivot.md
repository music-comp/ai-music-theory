# Quick Definition
The Neapolitan chord can serve as a pivot chord in modulation, bridging the gap between two distantly related keys by functioning as N in one key and V or another chord in the new key.

# Formal Definition
A particularly adventurous and imaginative pivot is the Neapolitan, which can bridge the tonal distance between two foreign (distantly related) keys. Because the Neapolitan is a major triad built on flat-2, it can be reinterpreted as a dominant chord or other function in a key a tritone or other distant interval away.

# Construction/Recognition
- Identify the Neapolitan in the first key (N or N6)
- Reinterpret the same pitches as a functional chord in the new key
- The Neapolitan can sometimes appear with dominant-seventh quality (N with added 7th)
- Common reinterpretation: N in first key = V or V7 in new key

# Musical Context
- Used for dramatic modulations to distant keys
- Allows modulation to keys a tritone away (maximum tonal distance)
- Part of the Romantic era expansion of modulatory techniques
- Creates a sense of harmonic surprise and adventure

# Examples
- Schubert, Schwanegesang, D. 957, "Fruhlingssehnsucht" (1828):
  - Modulates from D minor to Ab minor (tritone relation)
  - N6/5 (with dominant-seventh quality) in D minor becomes V6/5 in Ab minor

# Common Confusions
- The Neapolitan pivot is for DISTANT modulations, not closely related keys
- This is different from diatonic common chord modulation
- The Neapolitan may need to be respelled enharmonically in the new key
- The dominant-seventh form of Neapolitan (N7) facilitates this pivot
