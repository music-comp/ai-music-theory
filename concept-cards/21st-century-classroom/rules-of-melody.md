# Quick Definition
Rules of melody guide the construction of singable melodic lines in each voice, ensuring tendency tones resolve properly, avoiding awkward intervals, and maintaining smooth contour.

# Formal Definition
The rules of melody are principles for constructing singable melodic lines in voice leading, derived from historical practice (particularly J.S. Bach's 371 Chorales). Each voice part in SATB writing is considered a melody, so these rules apply to all four parts. The rules address tendency tone resolution, interval quality, leap recovery, and consecutive leap patterns.

# Rules/Procedures
1. **Tendency tones should resolve**:
   - Scale degree 7 resolves up to 8
   - Scale degree 4 resolves down to 3 (less strictly observed)
   - Scale degrees 6 and 2 resolve down by step
2. **Avoid augmented intervals** melodically
3. **Leaps larger than a 4th and diminished intervals** should change direction after the leap
4. **Consecutive leaps** should outline a triad (not a dissonant interval)

# Musical Context
These rules create smooth, vocal-friendly melodic lines. While based on Baroque practice, exceptions occur throughout music history. Breaking these rules often results in difficult-to-sing or awkward-sounding melodic lines.

# Examples
- Scale degree 7 (B in C major) should move to 8 (C), not leap down to G
- An augmented second (e.g., Ab to B natural) should be avoided
- After leaping up a 5th, the melody should step back down
- Two ascending leaps of a 3rd (C-E-G) outline a C major triad and are acceptable

# Common Confusions
- Thinking tendency tone resolution is absolute - it is a guideline with exceptions
- Not recognizing augmented intervals when accidentals are involved
- Confusing the direction change rule - it applies after large leaps AND diminished intervals
- Forgetting that each voice part must follow these rules, not just the soprano
