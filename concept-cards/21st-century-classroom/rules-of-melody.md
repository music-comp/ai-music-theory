# Quick Definition

Rules of melody guide the construction of singable melodic lines, requiring that tendency tones resolve properly, augmented intervals be avoided, large leaps change direction, and consecutive leaps outline triads.

# Core Definition

The purpose of the rules of melody is to aid in the construction of singable melodies, reflecting a summary of melodic practice based on J.S. Bach's 371 Chorales. Each part in an SATB composition is considered a melody, so these rules apply to all four parts (Hutchinson, Ch. 26, p. 391):

1. **Tendency tones** (^2, ^4, ^6, ^7) should resolve: ^7 goes to ^8, ^4 goes to ^3 (less strictly), ^6 and ^2 resolve down by step
2. **Avoid augmented intervals** melodically
3. **Leaps larger than a 4th** and **leaps of diminished intervals** should change direction after the leap
4. **Consecutive leaps** should outline a triad

# Prerequisites

- **Voice leading** -- These rules apply to each voice in SATB writing
- **Leading tone** -- ^7 is the strongest tendency tone

# Key Properties

1. ^7 --> ^8 (strongest tendency)
2. ^4 --> ^3 (less strictly observed)
3. ^6 and ^2 resolve down by step
4. No augmented melodic intervals
5. Large leaps and diminished intervals change direction
6. Consecutive leaps outline a triad

# Construction / Recognition

## To Apply:
1. Check every voice individually for tendency tone resolution
2. Scan for augmented intervals (especially in minor keys with raised ^7)
3. After any leap > P4 or diminished interval, ensure the next motion changes direction
4. If two consecutive leaps occur, verify they outline a triad

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: All four-part voice leading exercises
- **Historical/stylistic notes**: Based on Bach chorale practice; exceptions occur throughout music history

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Forgetting that ALL four parts must follow melody rules, not just soprano
  **Correction**: Check alto, tenor, and bass lines for violations too

- **Error**: Writing an augmented second in minor (^6 to ^7) without recognizing it
  **Correction**: In minor keys, watch for augmented intervals when the leading tone is raised

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking tendency tone resolution is absolute
  **Clarification**: These are guidelines; exceptions exist, especially for ^4 and inner voices
