# Quick Definition
Ternary form (ABA) consists of three sections: a statement (A), a contrasting digression (B), and a restatement of the opening (A), with each section typically self-contained.

# Formal Definition
Ternary form is usually diagrammed as ABA and described as "statement, digression, restatement." Each section is typically more substantial and self-contained than in rounded binary form. Character pieces from the Romantic era (nocturnes, intermezzos, songs without words) are often in ternary form.

# Construction/Recognition
Basic structure: A B A
- A: Statement (establishes main material and key)
- B: Digression (contrasting material, often different key)
- A: Restatement (return of opening, may be varied as A')

Section characteristics:
- Each section is substantial enough to stand alone
- B section has genuinely contrasting melody (not just development)
- Clear sectional divisions

Can be sectional or continuous:
- Sectional: A ends on tonic
- Continuous: A modulates or ends on half cadence

# Musical Context
- Common in Romantic character pieces
- Composers: Schubert, Chopin, Mendelssohn, Schumann, Brahms
- The B section provides contrast and relief
- The return of A provides satisfying closure

# Examples
- Mozart, Andante in E-flat major, K. 15mm:
  - A: a a' (HC, PAC in Eb)
  - B: b b (HC, HC)
  - A: a a' (HC, PAC in Eb)

- Rachmaninoff, Prelude in C-sharp minor, Op. 3, No. 2:
  - Larger ternary with extended sections

# Common Confusions
- Ternary has THREE sections; rounded binary has TWO sections with internal return
- In ternary, B is substantial and contrasting; in rounded binary, contrast is brief
- Use proportion, nature of contrast, and era to distinguish from rounded binary
- The final A may be varied (written out or da capo)
