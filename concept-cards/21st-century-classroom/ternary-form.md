# Quick Definition

Ternary form (ABA) consists of three sections: a statement (A), a contrasting digression (B), and a restatement of the opening (A), with each section typically self-contained.

# Core Definition

Ternary form is usually diagrammed as ABA and described as "statement, digression, restatement." Each section is typically more substantial and self-contained than in rounded binary form. The B section features genuinely contrasting melody, not just development of A material. Character pieces from the Romantic era with titles such as "Nocturne," "Intermezzo," and "Song Without Words" are often in ternary form (Hutchinson, Ch. 24, pp. 353-357).

# Prerequisites

- **Phrase** -- Ternary form organizes phrases into three large sections
- **Cadence** -- Cadences demarcate the boundaries between sections

# Key Properties

1. Three sections: A (statement), B (digression), A (restatement)
2. B section has genuinely contrasting melody
3. Each section is substantial enough to stand alone
4. Can be sectional or continuous
5. The return of A provides closure
6. Can range from small (Mozart K. 15mm) to large (Rachmaninoff Prelude)

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify Ternary Form:
1. Determine the piece has three large sections
2. Verify the first and third sections share material (ABA)
3. Confirm the B section has genuinely contrasting melody (not just development)
4. Check that the B section is substantial enough to be a section, not just a phrase

## Diagram Example (Mozart K. 15mm):
| A | B | A |
| a a' (HC, PAC) | b b (HC, HC) | a a' (HC, PAC) |

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: Romantic character pieces (nocturnes, intermezzos, songs without words), some Classical movements
- **Common applications**: Schubert, Chopin, Mendelssohn, Schumann, Brahms piano works; also found in larger orchestral pieces
- **Historical/stylistic notes**: Romantic composers favored ternary form; Baroque/Classical composers more often used rounded binary

# Examples

**Example 1** (pp. 354-355): Mozart, Andante in E-flat major, K. 15mm -- small ternary with clear ABA structure, each section containing two phrases

**Example 2** (pp. 355-357): Rachmaninoff, Prelude in C-sharp minor, Op. 3, No. 2 -- larger ternary where each section is extended; demonstrates that ternary form can accommodate substantial sections

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Labeling a rounded binary as ternary because material returns
  **Correction**: Apply the three criteria (proportion, nature, era) from Section 24.8 to distinguish

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking ternary means any piece with three parts
  **Clarification**: The B section must be genuinely contrasting and substantial; brief contrast within a binary section does not make ternary

- **Confusion**: Believing the final A must be identical to the first A
  **Clarification**: The return may be varied (A'); it can also be a da capo (exact repeat)
