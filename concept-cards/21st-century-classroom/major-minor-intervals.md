# Quick Definition
Major and minor intervals are 2nds, 3rds, 6ths, and 7ths--intervals that can be major, minor, augmented, or diminished, but never perfect.

# Formal Definition
Intervals of the second, third, sixth, and seventh can be major (M) or minor (m) but never perfect. Major intervals occur naturally from the tonic to each note of the major scale. Minor intervals are one half step smaller than major intervals. These intervals can also be augmented (one half step larger than major) or diminished (one half step smaller than minor).

# Construction/Recognition
- m2 = 1 half step; M2 = 2 half steps
- m3 = 3 half steps; M3 = 4 half steps
- m6 = 8 half steps; M6 = 9 half steps
- m7 = 10 half steps; M7 = 11 half steps
- All notes in major scale above tonic are major or perfect

# Musical Context
Major and minor thirds are essential for determining chord quality (major vs. minor triad). Major and minor seconds define scale types. Sixths and sevenths are important for chord extensions and voice leading.

# Examples
- C to D = M2 (2 half steps)
- C to Db = m2 (1 half step)
- C to E = M3 (4 half steps)
- C to Eb = m3 (3 half steps)
- C to A = M6; C to Ab = m6

# Common Confusions
- Major/minor intervals CANNOT be perfect
- Minor is always one half step smaller than major (same numeric size)
- "Major" doesn't mean "bigger"--it describes quality
