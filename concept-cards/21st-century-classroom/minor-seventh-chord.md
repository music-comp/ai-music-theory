# Quick Definition
A minor seventh chord consists of a minor triad with a minor seventh above the root, producing a soft, mellow, relaxed sonority.

# Core Definition
The minor seventh chord is built from a minor triad plus a minor seventh interval from root to seventh (10 half steps). Its intervallic structure is m3 + M3 + m3. In Roman numeral notation, a lowercase numeral with superscript 7 indicates a minor seventh chord (e.g., ii7, vi7). It occurs naturally on scale degrees ii, iii, and vi in major keys, and on i and iv in minor keys.

# Prerequisites
- **Seventh chord**: General concept of four-note chords
- **Minor triad**: The triad type on which this chord is built

# Key Properties
1. Minor triad + minor seventh from root to seventh (10 half steps)
2. Third-stacking pattern: m3 + M3 + m3
3. Occurs on ii, iii, and vi in major keys; i and iv in minor keys
4. Roman numeral: lowercase with 7 (ii7, iii7, vi7)
5. Lead-sheet symbols: Cm7, Cmin7, C-7
6. Both the triad and the seventh interval are minor quality

# Construction / Recognition
1. Build a minor triad on the root
2. Add a note a minor third above the fifth (or a minor seventh above the root)
3. Distinguish from half-diminished by checking that the fifth is perfect (not diminished)

# Context & Application
The minor seventh chord is one of the most common seventh chords in tonal music. It serves as ii7 in the ubiquitous ii7-V7-I jazz progression. In popular music, it appears frequently in circle-of-fifths segments. It has a warm, relaxed quality.

# Examples
- Dm7: D-F-A-C (ii7 in C major)
- Am7: A-C-E-G (vi7 in C major)
- Em7: E-G-B-D (iii7 in C major)
- ii7-V7-I = Dm7-G7-Cmaj7 in C major

## Contrasts With
- Dominant seventh chord (major triad instead of minor)
- Half-diminished seventh chord (diminished triad instead of minor)
- Minor-major seventh chord (major seventh instead of minor seventh)

# Common Errors
- Confusing minor seventh chord with half-diminished (the fifth differs: perfect vs. diminished)
- Confusing Dm7 with D7 (D7 is dominant, with F# instead of F)

# Common Confusions
- **m7 vs. m7b5**: Minor seventh has a perfect fifth; half-diminished has a diminished fifth
- **Both intervals are minor**: Both the triad quality and the seventh interval are minor
