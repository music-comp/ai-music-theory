# Quick Definition
A minor seventh chord consists of a minor triad with a minor seventh above the root, creating a mellow, relaxed sound.

# Formal Definition
A minor seventh chord is built with a minor triad (m3 + M3) plus a minor seventh interval from root to seventh. The intervals stacked are: m3 (root to 3rd), M3 (3rd to 5th), m3 (5th to 7th). It occurs diatonically on ^2, ^3, and ^6 in major keys, and on ^1 and ^4 in minor keys.

# Construction/Recognition
- Minor triad + minor 7th from root
- Intervals: m3 + M3 + m3
- Total: 10 half steps from root to 7th
- Lead-sheet: Cm7, Am7, Dm7
- Roman numeral: ii7, iii7, vi7 (in major)

# Musical Context
Minor seventh chords have a warm, relaxed quality. They are extremely common in jazz and popular music. The ii7-V7-I progression is the most important chord progression in jazz, with ii7 being a minor seventh chord.

# Examples
- Dm7: D-F-A-C (in C major: ii7)
- Am7: A-C-E-G (in C major: vi7)
- Em7: E-G-B-D (in C major: iii7)
- ii7-V7-I = Dm7-G7-C in C major

# Common Confusions
- Both the triad AND the seventh interval are minor
- Not the same as minor-major seventh (which has a major 7th)
- Dm7 is different from D7 (which is dominant)
