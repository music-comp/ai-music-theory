# Quick Definition
Impressionism is a late 19th/early 20th century musical style characterized by modal harmony, extended chords, and parallelism, primarily associated with Debussy and Ravel.

# Formal Definition
Impressionism in music refers to a compositional approach associated primarily with Claude Debussy and Maurice Ravel in France, along with Ottorino Respighi (Italy), Charles Tomlinson Griffes (America), and Frederick Delius (England). Key characteristics include: (1) use of church modes as an alternative to chromatic late-Romantic harmony, (2) use of upper extensions (9ths, 11ths, 13ths) in tertian harmonies, and (3) parallelism (planing) where chord voicings move in parallel motion.

# Construction/Calculation
Three main techniques of Impressionism:
1. Modal writing: Use church modes (Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, etc.) instead of major/minor
2. Extended tertian harmony: Build chords with 9ths, 11ths, 13ths (sometimes with chromatic alterations)
3. Parallelism/planing: Move entire chord voicings in parallel motion, abandoning traditional voice leading rules

# Musical Context
Impressionism emerged as a reaction against the heavily chromatic music of Richard Wagner (particularly the Tristan chord and its influence). Composers sought new sounds through modes, whole-tone scales, and non-functional harmony. The term comes from the visual art movement, though Debussy disliked the label. The style influenced later 20th-century harmony and jazz.

# Examples
- Debussy, String Quartet in G minor, Op. 10, I: Opens in Phrygian mode
- Debussy, Danses, II "Danse profane": Lydian mode
- Ravel, Piano Trio, I: Opens in Dorian mode, later transforms to Lydian
- Debussy, "Clair de lune": Uses Ebm9 chord with 9th in melody

# Related Concepts
- Church modes
- Parallelism (planing)
- Extended tertian harmony
- Pandiatonicism
- Whole-tone scale

# Common Confusions
- Impressionism is not merely "pretty" or "atmospheric" - it has specific technical characteristics
- Modal does not mean atonal - impressionist music often has tonal centers
- Parallelism abandons traditional voice leading but maintains coherent harmony
- Not all French music from this period is impressionist
