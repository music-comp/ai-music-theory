# Quick Definition
Thirteenth chords are the largest extension of tertian harmony, containing up to seven notes built in thirds from the root through the 13th (the 6th scale degree an octave higher).

# Formal Definition
A thirteenth chord theoretically includes the root, 3rd, 5th, 7th, 9th, 11th, and 13th. All numbers up to the highest number are assumed present when a chord symbol shows "13." The 13th comes from the major scale unless otherwise specified (i.e., it is a major 13th - the same as a major 6th an octave higher).

# Construction/Calculation
1. Build the complete eleventh chord (root, 3rd, 5th, 7th, 9th, 11th)
2. Add the 13th (major 6th + octave above root)
3. In practice, the 5th and unaltered 11th are often omitted
4. When the 11th clashes with the 3rd, it is typically omitted or raised to #11
5. For precise notation, use parentheses: C9(13) specifies C-E-G-Bb-D-A (9th and 13th, no 11th)

# Musical Context
Thirteenth chords represent the full harmonic vocabulary of jazz. They are particularly common on dominant chords resolving in ii-V-I progressions. The 13th adds color and richness while maintaining the chord's function. In real-world jazz, C13 is understood to omit the 11th because experienced musicians know it clashes with the 3rd.

# Examples
- C13 = C-E-G-Bb-D-F-A (complete, though 11th often omitted in practice)
- C9(13) = C-E-G-Bb-D-A (specifies 9th and 13th without 11th)
- Cm13 = C-Eb-G-Bb-D-F-A (minor 13th)
- C7(b13) = dominant 7th with lowered 13th (enharmonic equivalent of #5)

# Common Confusions
- "6" vs "13": label the 6th as "6" only if the 7th is NOT present; if the 7th IS present, label the 6th as "13"
- C6 has C-E-G-A (no 7th), while C13 implies C-E-G-Bb-D-(F)-A (includes 7th)
- b13 is the enharmonic equivalent of #5; b13 implies the unaltered 5th is also present, while #5 does not
