# Quick Definition
Thirteenth chords include all members up to the 13th (the 6th an octave higher); "13" implies a 7th is present while "6" does not.

# Core Definition
The 13th is the 6th scale degree an octave higher, coming from the major scale unless otherwise specified. C13 technically means all members through the 13th (C-E-G-Bb-D-F-A), though the unaltered 11th is omitted in practice because it clashes with the major 3rd. The "6 vs. 13" rule: label the 6th as "6" only if the 7th is absent; if the 7th is present, label it "13." The textbook uses C9(13) to specify C-E-G-Bb-D-A without the 11th (Hutchinson, Ch. 31, pp. 447-448).

# Key Properties
1. "13" is "6" an octave higher
2. 13th comes from the major scale unless otherwise specified
3. C13 implies all members through 13th (11th often omitted)
4. "6 vs. 13" rule: "6" = no 7th; "13" = 7th present
5. b13 is enharmonically #5; b13 implies unaltered 5th also present

# Construction / Recognition
- C6 = C-E-G-A (no 7th; A labeled "6")
- C13 = C-E-G-Bb-D-F-A (with 7th; A labeled "13")
- C9(13) = C-E-G-Bb-D-A (textbook notation, omitting 11th)

# Context & Application
Thirteenth chords represent the fullest extension of tertian harmony. In jazz practice, not all members are sounded; the 11th is typically omitted with major-3rd chords. The 6 vs. 13 distinction is essential for accurate chord symbol interpretation.

# Examples
- C13 = C-E-G-Bb-D-F-A (complete, p. 447)
- C9(13) = C-E-G-Bb-D-A (textbook notation)
- C6 = C-E-G-A (6th without 7th)
- C7(b13) = dominant 7th with lowered 13th (enharmonic of #5)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Using "6" when the 7th is present
  **Correction**: If the 7th is present, the 6th must be labeled "13"

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking C13 contains only root, 3rd, 5th, 7th, and 13th
  **Clarification**: C13 implies all members through the 13th; the 11th is commonly omitted
- **Confusion**: Confusing b13 with #5
  **Clarification**: b13 implies the unaltered 5th is also present; #5 does not
