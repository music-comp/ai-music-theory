# Quick Definition
Accidentals are five types of symbols placed before notes that raise or lower their pitch: sharp, flat, double sharp, double flat, and natural.

# Core Definition
There are five types of accidentals; accidentals are characters that can be placed before notes to raise or lower them. The sharp symbol (#) raises a pitch a half step. The flat symbol (b) lowers a pitch a half step. The double sharp symbol (x) raises a pitch two half steps, or a whole step. The double flat symbol (bb) lowers a pitch two half steps, or a whole step. The natural symbol cancels a previous accidental or key signature alteration.

# Prerequisites
- **Pitch**: Understanding notes as highness/lowness
- **Half step**: The smallest interval, needed to understand what accidentals do

# Key Properties
1. Sharp (#): raises pitch one half step
2. Flat (b): lowers pitch one half step
3. Double sharp (x): raises pitch one whole step (two half steps)
4. Double flat (bb): lowers pitch one whole step (two half steps)
5. Natural: cancels any previous accidental
6. Accidentals apply to all occurrences of that note within the same measure

# Construction / Recognition
- Placed immediately before the notehead
- Sharp: grid-like symbol (#)
- Flat: lowercase-b-like symbol (b)
- Natural: box with upward and downward extensions
- Accidentals carry through the measure but are canceled by the bar line

# Context & Application
Accidentals allow composers to use notes outside a given key signature or to temporarily alter pitches within a musical passage. An accidental applies to all occurrences of that note within the same measure unless canceled by another accidental. Bar lines cancel accidentals.

# Examples
- C# is one half step higher than C
- Bb is one half step lower than B
- Fx (F double sharp) sounds the same as G
- Bbb (B double flat) sounds the same as A

# Common Errors
- Forgetting that accidentals apply only within the same measure (bar lines cancel them)
- Applying an accidental to a different octave of the same note name

# Common Confusions
- **Scope of accidentals**: They apply only within the same measure; the bar line cancels them
- **Octave specificity**: Accidentals apply only to the specific octave where written
- **Natural sign**: Cancels both sharps and flats (not just one type)
