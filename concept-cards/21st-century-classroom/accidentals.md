# Quick Definition
Accidentals are symbols placed before notes that raise or lower their pitch by half steps or whole steps.

# Formal Definition
Accidentals are five types of symbols that modify the pitch of a note: sharp (#) raises a pitch by a half step, flat (b) lowers a pitch by a half step, double sharp (x) raises a pitch by a whole step, double flat (bb) lowers a pitch by a whole step, and natural cancels a previous accidental or key signature alteration.

# Construction/Recognition
- Sharp (#): raises pitch one half step
- Flat (b): lowers pitch one half step
- Double sharp (x): raises pitch two half steps (one whole step)
- Double flat (bb): lowers pitch two half steps (one whole step)
- Natural: cancels any previous accidental

# Musical Context
Accidentals allow composers to use notes outside a given key signature or to temporarily alter pitches within a musical passage. An accidental applies to all occurrences of that note within the same measure unless canceled by another accidental.

# Examples
- C# is one half step higher than C
- Bb is one half step lower than B
- Fx (F double sharp) sounds the same as G
- Bbb (B double flat) sounds the same as A

# Common Confusions
- Accidentals apply only within the same measure (bar line cancels them)
- Accidentals apply only to the specific octave where written
- Natural sign cancels sharps AND flats (not just one type)
