# Quick Definition
A sentence is a phrase with a specific melodic construction: an initial melodic idea (motive or subphrase) is either repeated or sequenced, then followed by related or unrelated material leading to a cadence.

# Core Definition
A sentence in music is a phrase with a specific melodic construction: a melodic idea (motive 1 or subphrase a) is either repeated or sequenced then followed by either related or unrelated material leading to a cadence. The standard structure is presentation (idea + repetition/sequence) followed by continuation (development + cadential material). Sentences can be part of periods, since a sentence is a type of phrase and periods combine phrases.

# Prerequisites
- **Phrase**: The formal unit that a sentence is a specific type of
- **Motive**: The melodic idea that begins a sentence
- **Subphrase**: The two-measure units within a sentence

# Key Properties
1. A single phrase with specific melodic construction
2. Presentation: initial idea + repetition or sequence (typically 2+2 bars)
3. Continuation: development and cadential material (typically 4 bars)
4. Standard length: 8 bars (4+4), but can be 4 bars (2+2)
5. The repeated idea can be exact, sequenced, or varied
6. Continuation can use related or unrelated material

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the opening melodic idea (motive or subphrase)
2. Note if it is repeated exactly, sequenced, or varied
3. Observe how the continuation leads to the cadence
4. Standard: 2+2+4 bar structure

# Context & Application
The sentence is one of the fundamental phrase types in Classical music, alongside the period. A sentence is a single phrase; a period requires at least two phrases. Sentences can be embedded within periods.

# Examples
- Mozart, K. 331, I -- motive sequenced down a step in m. 2, related material and cadence
- Beethoven, Op. 2, No. 1, I -- subphrase a varied in mm. 3-4, motive extracted in mm. 5-6, leads to HC
- Mozart, K. 333, I -- unrelated material follows subphrase repetition

# Common Errors
- Confusing sentence (one phrase) with period (multiple phrases)
- Assuming all phrases are sentences

# Common Confusions
- **Sentence is one phrase; period is two or more**: This is the key distinction
- **Sentences can be part of periods**: A period may contain a sentence as its antecedent or consequent
- **Not all phrases are sentences**: Only those with the specific presentation-continuation structure
