# Quick Definition
A sentence is a phrase with a specific melodic construction: an initial melodic idea is either repeated or sequenced, then followed by related or unrelated material leading to a cadence.

# Formal Definition
A sentence in music is a phrase with a specific melodic construction: a melodic idea (motive 1 or subphrase a) is either repeated or sequenced, then followed by either related or unrelated material leading to a cadence. Sentences are typically 8 bars (4+4) but can be 4 bars (2+2) or other lengths.

# Analysis/Application
Standard sentence structure:
1. Presentation: Initial idea (2 bars) + repetition/sequence (2 bars)
2. Continuation: Development and cadential material (4 bars)

When analyzing:
1. Identify the opening melodic idea
2. Note if it's repeated exactly, sequenced, or varied
3. Observe how the continuation leads to the cadence

# Musical Context
The sentence is one of the fundamental phrase types in Classical music. It differs from the period in being a single phrase rather than a combination of phrases. A sentence can be part of a period.

# Examples
- Mozart, K. 331, I - motive sequenced down a step in m. 2, then related material and cadence
- Beethoven, Op. 2, No. 1, I - subphrase a varied in mm. 3-4, motive extracted in mm. 5-6, leads to HC
- Mozart, K. 333, I - unrelated material follows subphrase repetition

# Common Confusions
- A sentence is a single phrase; a period requires at least two phrases
- Sentences can be part of larger period structures
- Not all phrases are sentences - only those with the specific presentation-continuation structure
