# Quick Definition
A period consists of at least two phrases where the final phrase ends with a more conclusive cadence than the first phrase, creating an antecedent ("question") and consequent ("answer") relationship.

# Core Definition
In music, a period consists of at least two phrases with the final phrase ending in a more conclusive cadence than the first phrase. The phrase with the less conclusive cadence is the "antecedent" (question); the phrase with the more conclusive cadence is the "consequent" (answer). Conclusive cadences end on tonic (AC, PC); inconclusive cadences do not (HC, DC). PAC is more conclusive than IAC.

# Prerequisites
- **Phrase**: Understanding of musical sections
- **Cadence**: All four cadence types
- **Perfect authentic cadence**: PAC vs. IAC distinction

# Key Properties
1. At least two phrases
2. Final phrase has more conclusive cadence
3. Antecedent = less conclusive (question)
4. Consequent = more conclusive (answer)
5. Common patterns: HC then PAC; HC then IAC; IAC then PAC
6. A repeated phrase (same cadence twice) is NOT a period

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify cadences at phrase endings
2. Determine conclusiveness: PAC > IAC > HC; DC is inconclusive
3. If final cadence is more conclusive, it is a period
4. Label antecedent and consequent
5. Determine if parallel or contrasting

# Context & Application
The period is one of the fundamental formal structures in Classical music. The antecedent-consequent relationship creates satisfying formal balance. Sentences can be part of periods, since sentences are phrases with specific structure.

# Examples
- Mozart, K. 331, I, mm. 1-8 -- HC (antecedent) then PAC (consequent)
- Haydn, Piano Sonata Hob. XVI:9, III -- IAC then PAC
- Beethoven, "Fur Elise" -- IAC then PAC
- Bill Withers, "Lean on Me" -- less then more conclusive cadence

# Common Errors
- Calling a repeated phrase a period (cadences must differ in conclusiveness)
- Forgetting that sentences can be embedded within periods

# Common Confusions
- **Repeated phrase is not a period**: Same cadence twice does not create antecedent-consequent
- **Sentences within periods**: A sentence is a single phrase; it can serve as antecedent or consequent
- **Conclusiveness hierarchy**: PAC > IAC > HC (and DC is inconclusive)
