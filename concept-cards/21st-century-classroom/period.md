# Quick Definition
A period consists of at least two phrases where the final phrase ends with a more conclusive cadence than the first phrase, creating a "question and answer" relationship.

# Formal Definition
In music, a period consists of at least two phrases with the final phrase ending in a more conclusive cadence than the first phrase. The phrase ending with the less conclusive cadence is the "antecedent" (question), and the phrase ending with the more conclusive cadence is the "consequent" (answer).

# Analysis/Application
Cadential patterns in periods:
- HC then PAC (most common)
- HC then IAC
- IAC then PAC
- DC then AC

To analyze:
1. Identify cadences at phrase endings
2. Determine if the second is more conclusive
3. Label antecedent and consequent
4. Note if parallel or contrasting

# Musical Context
The period is one of the fundamental formal structures in Classical music, appearing throughout the common-practice era and continuing in popular music. The antecedent-consequent relationship creates satisfying formal balance.

# Examples
- Mozart, K. 331, I, mm. 1-8 - HC (antecedent) then PAC (consequent)
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in F Major, Hob. XVI:9, III - IAC then PAC
- Beethoven, "Fur Elise" - IAC then PAC
- Bill Withers, "Lean on Me" - less conclusive then more conclusive cadence

# Common Confusions
- Periods require different cadences, with the second more conclusive
- A repeated phrase (same cadence twice) is not a period
- Sentences can be part of periods (a sentence is a phrase, periods combine phrases)
