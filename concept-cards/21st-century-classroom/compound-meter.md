# Quick Definition
Compound meter is any meter where the beats naturally divide into three equal parts; compound time signatures have a top number of 6, 9, or 12, and the beat is a dotted note value.

# Core Definition
If the beats are normally divided into three parts, the meter is described as compound. With compound meters the bottom number specifies the division of the beat, not the beat itself. The beat value is a dotted note. Compound time signatures have a top number greater than four that is divisible by 3 (6, 9, 12). We say 6/8 is "compound duple meter" because it has two beats. The time signature 6/16 is also compound duple.

# Prerequisites
- **Meter**: The concept of beat grouping and division
- **Time signature**: The notation expressing compound meter
- **Dots and ties**: Understanding dotted notes (the beat unit in compound meter)

# Key Properties
1. Beats divide naturally into three equal parts
2. Top numbers: 6 (duple), 9 (triple), or 12 (quadruple)
3. Top number / 3 = number of beats per measure
4. Bottom number = the division note value, NOT the beat
5. Beat = dotted version of the bottom number (e.g., dotted quarter in 6/8)
6. Creates a lilting, swinging, or rolling feel

# Construction / Recognition
- Top number divisible by 3 and greater than 4
- Divide top number by 3 for the number of beats
- Beat unit = dotted note of the bottom number value
- 6/8: 2 dotted-quarter beats; 9/8: 3 dotted-quarter beats; 12/8: 4 dotted-quarter beats

# Context & Application
Compound meter creates a lilting, swinging, or rolling feel. It is common in dance music like jigs, many ballads, and songs with a triplet feel. Blues shuffles often feel like compound meter. If music naturally has triplet division, use 6/8, 9/8, or 12/8 rather than writing constant triplets in simple meter.

# Examples
- 6/8 (compound duple): 2 dotted-quarter beats, each divides into 3 eighths
- 9/8 (compound triple): 3 dotted-quarter beats, each divides into 3 eighths
- 12/8 (compound quadruple): 4 dotted-quarter beats, each divides into 3 eighths
- 6/4 (compound duple): 2 dotted-half beats, each divides into 3 quarters
- 6/16 (compound duple): 2 dotted-eighth beats

# Common Errors
- Counting 6/8 as having six beats (it has two)
- Treating the bottom number as the beat unit (it is the division)

# Common Confusions
- **6/8 has TWO beats**: The 6 indicates divisions, not beats; divide by 3
- **Bottom number is NOT the beat**: In compound meter, the beat is a dotted note
- **"Compound" vs. "complicated"**: Compound means beats divide by three, not that the music is complex
