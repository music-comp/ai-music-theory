# Quick Definition
Compound meter is any meter where the beats naturally divide into three equal parts, with the beat being a dotted note value.

# Formal Definition
In compound meter, each beat divides naturally into three equal parts. The top number indicates the number of divisions (not beats) and is always divisible by 3 (6, 9, or 12). The bottom number indicates the division note value, and the beat is the dotted version of that division (e.g., in 6/8, the division is an eighth note, so the beat is a dotted quarter).

# Construction/Recognition
- Top numbers: 6, 9, or 12 (divisible by 3)
- Top number / 3 = number of beats (6=2 beats, 9=3 beats, 12=4 beats)
- Bottom number = the division, not the beat
- Beat = dotted note of the bottom number value
- Beats divide into three parts

# Musical Context
Compound meter creates a lilting, swinging, or rolling feel. It is common in dance music like jigs, many ballads, and songs with a triplet feel. Blues shuffles often feel like compound meter.

# Examples
- 6/8 (compound duple): 2 dotted-quarter beats, each divides into 3 eighths
- 9/8 (compound triple): 3 dotted-quarter beats, each divides into 3 eighths
- 12/8 (compound quadruple): 4 dotted-quarter beats, each divides into 3 eighths
- 6/4 (compound duple): 2 dotted-half beats, each divides into 3 quarters

# Common Confusions
- 6/8 has TWO beats, not six (the 6 indicates divisions)
- The bottom number is NOT the beat in compound meter
- Compound doesn't mean "complicated"--it means beats divide by three
