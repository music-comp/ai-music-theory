# Quick Definition
Third species writes four quarter notes against each whole note of the cantus firmus; the downbeat must be consonant, with passing tones and the cambiata figure as allowable dissonances.

# Core Definition
Third species counterpoint creates flowing melodic lines with four quarter notes per whole note in the cantus firmus. Each downbeat must be consonant. Allowable dissonances are passing tones (never on the first beat) and the cambiata figure (a specific melodic pattern where only the second note of the measure is the dissonant interval). Direct 5ths and 8ves are not allowed when approaching by similar motion to a downbeat (Hutchinson, Ch. 30, p. 427).

# Prerequisites
- **Second species counterpoint** -- Third species extends the rhythmic subdivision further
- **Cantus firmus** -- Written against a given c.f.
- **Cambiata figure** -- A specific dissonance treatment unique to third species

# Key Properties
1. Four quarter notes per whole note in the cantus firmus
2. Each downbeat must be consonant
3. Passing tones allowed but never on beat 1
4. Cambiata figure: only the second note of the measure may be dissonant
5. Direct 5ths and 8ves forbidden (similar motion to 5th or 8ve on downbeats)
6. No repeated notes across barlines
7. Three distinct concluding formulas for the penultimate measure

# Construction / Recognition
**Beginning and ending:**
1. Begin on an octave or unison; write four quarter notes per measure except the final
2. End on a whole note at octave or unison

**Concluding formulas (penultimate measure):**
- C.f. in lower voice, option 1: scale degrees 4-5-6-7
- C.f. in lower voice, option 2: scale degrees 2-1-6-7 (the cambiata)
- C.f. in upper voice: scale degrees 7-5-6-7

**Barline rules:**
- Imperfect consonances across barlines: approached any way
- Perfect consonances across barlines: contrary motion only
- No repeated notes across barlines

**Dissonance rules:**
- Passing tones: allowed but never on beat 1
- Cambiata: second note of measure is the only dissonant note in this figure

# Context & Application
Third species develops flowing melodic lines and teaches how to weave dissonance into faster-moving passages. The cambiata figure is a characteristic Renaissance idiom found throughout 16th-century polyphony. The four-quarter-note texture provides greater melodic freedom while maintaining strict consonance/dissonance control.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Placing a passing tone on the first beat of the measure
  **Correction**: Passing tones in third species are never on beat 1; the downbeat must always be consonant
- **Error**: Using the cambiata with dissonance on a note other than the second
  **Correction**: In the cambiata figure, only the second note of the measure may be dissonant
- **Error**: Creating direct 5ths or 8ves by similar motion to a downbeat
  **Correction**: Perfect consonances on downbeats must be approached by contrary motion

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking any beat other than beat 1 can be freely dissonant
  **Clarification**: Non-beat-1 dissonances must still be proper passing tones or part of the cambiata figure
- **Confusion**: Confusing the cambiata with a neighbor tone
  **Clarification**: The cambiata has a specific melodic contour (step-leap-step) unique to third species
