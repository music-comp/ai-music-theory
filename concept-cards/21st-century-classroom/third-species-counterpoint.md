# Quick Definition
Third species writes four quarter notes against each whole note of the cantus firmus; the downbeat must be consonant, with passing tones and the cambiata figure as allowable dissonances.

# Formal Definition
Third species counterpoint creates flowing melodic lines with four quarter notes per whole note in the cantus firmus. Each downbeat must be consonant. Allowable dissonances are passing tones (never on the first beat) and the cambiata figure (a specific melodic pattern where only the second note is dissonant). The increased rhythmic activity requires careful attention to direct fifths and octaves across barlines.

# Rules/Procedures
1. **Begin** on an octave or unison with four quarter notes per measure (except final)
2. **End** on a whole note at octave or unison
   - **C.F. in lower voice**: Penultimate measure uses 4-5-6-7 or 2-1-6-7 (cambiata)
   - **C.F. in upper voice**: Penultimate measure uses 7-5-6-7
3. **Each downbeat** must be a consonance
4. **Crossing barlines**:
   - Imperfect consonances: Approached any way
   - Perfect consonances: Contrary motion only (avoid direct 5ths and 8ves)
   - No repeated notes across barlines
5. **Allowable dissonances**:
   - Passing tones (never on beat 1)
   - Cambiata: Second note may be dissonant in this specific figure
6. **Direct 5ths and 8ves**: Forbidden when approaching by similar motion to downbeat

# Musical Context
Third species develops flowing melodic lines and teaches how to weave dissonance into faster-moving passages. The cambiata figure is a characteristic Renaissance idiom.

# Examples
- Concluding formula (C.F. below): ...G-A-B-C resolving to C whole note (4-5-6-7-8)
- Cambiata: D-C-A-B where C is dissonant but approached/left properly

# Common Confusions
- Placing a passing tone on the first beat (forbidden)
- Using the cambiata with dissonance on any note other than the second
- Creating direct 5ths or 8ves by similar motion to downbeats
- Not using specific concluding formulas for the penultimate measure
- Repeating notes across barlines
