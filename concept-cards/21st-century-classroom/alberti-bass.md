# Quick Definition
Alberti bass is an arpeggiated accompaniment pattern that follows a "low-high-middle-high" sequence, commonly used in Classical-era keyboard music.

# Formal Definition
Alberti bass accompaniment patterns involve arpeggios that do not arpeggiate chords in a simple upward or downward motion, but in a "low-high-middle-high" pattern. Named after Domenico Alberti (1710-1746), this pattern became a signature of Classical keyboard style.

# Analysis/Application
The Alberti bass pattern:
1. Low note (bass)
2. High note (top of chord)
3. Middle note (middle of chord)
4. High note (repeat)

This creates constant eighth-note or sixteenth-note motion while outlining the harmony.

# Musical Context
The Alberti bass is characteristic of Classical-era piano music, especially Mozart and Haydn. It provides rhythmic drive and harmonic clarity while remaining relatively simple to execute.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 545, I (1788) - classic Alberti bass accompaniment
- "Pipeline" (1962) - Alberti pattern transposed to E minor in lower register

# Common Confusions
- The pattern is specifically low-high-middle-high, not just any arpeggio
- Can occur in any register, not just bass
- The pattern creates constant motion even when the harmony is static
