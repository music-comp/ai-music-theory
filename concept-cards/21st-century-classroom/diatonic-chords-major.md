# Quick Definition
The diatonic chords in major follow the pattern I-ii-iii-IV-V-vi-viio (major-minor-minor-major-major-minor-diminished).

# Formal Definition
Diatonic chords are triads built using only the notes of a given key signature. In major keys, triads built on each scale degree produce a consistent pattern of qualities: I (major), ii (minor), iii (minor), IV (major), V (major), vi (minor), viio (diminished). This M-m-m-M-M-m-o pattern holds for all major keys.

# Construction/Recognition
- I = major (on ^1)
- ii = minor (on ^2)
- iii = minor (on ^3)
- IV = major (on ^4)
- V = major (on ^5)
- vi = minor (on ^6)
- viio = diminished (on ^7)
- Pattern: M-m-m-M-M-m-o

# Musical Context
Understanding diatonic chords allows quick identification of chord qualities in any major key. This knowledge is essential for harmonization, composition, and analysis. The primary triads (I, IV, V) are all major; the secondary triads (ii, iii, vi) are all minor.

# Examples
- C major: C-Dm-Em-F-G-Am-Bo
- G major: G-Am-Bm-C-D-Em-F#o
- F major: F-Gm-Am-Bb-C-Dm-Eo
- The I, IV, and V are called "primary" triads

# Common Confusions
- The pattern is consistent in ALL major keys
- viio is diminished, not minor
- iii and vi are minor, not major
