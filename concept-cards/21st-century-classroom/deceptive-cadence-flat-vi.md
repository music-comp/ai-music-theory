# Quick Definition
The V to flat-VI deceptive cadence substitutes the borrowed flat-VI chord for the expected tonic, creating a dramatic "epic" effect often followed by flat-VII to I.

# Formal Definition
The V to flat-VI deceptive cadence is a deceptive realization of the deceptive progression using a borrowed chord. This cadence often connects to the flat-VI - flat-VII - I progression to produce an emphatic, "epic" (though somewhat cliched) ending. The progression exploits the half-step motion from flat-6 to 5 and from flat-7 to 1.

# Construction/Recognition
- V resolves to flat-VI instead of I or vi
- Often followed by flat-VII - I to complete the phrase
- Full progression: V - flat-VI - flat-VII - I
- The flat-VI is a major triad built on the lowered 6th scale degree

# Musical Context
- Creates dramatic, triumphant endings
- Particularly associated with "epic" performances of national anthems
- Common in film scores and dramatic musical moments
- The flat-VI - flat-VII - I motion provides strong voice leading to tonic

# Examples
- Whitney Houston's "Star-Spangled Banner" at 1991 Super Bowl
- Beyonce's "Star-Spangled Banner" at 2013 Presidential Inauguration
- Lady Gaga's "Star-Spangled Banner" at 2016 Super Bowl
- Beauty and the Beast (1991) - ending of the animated film

# Common Confusions
- This is distinct from the diatonic V-vi deceptive cadence
- The flat-VI is major quality, not minor like diatonic vi
- The "epic" quality can become cliched if overused
- flat-VII is also a borrowed chord in this context
