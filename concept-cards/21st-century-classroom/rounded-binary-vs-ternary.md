# Quick Definition
Rounded binary and ternary can be distinguished by examining the proportion and nature of the contrasting section, and considering the historical era of the composition.

# Formal Definition
When analyzing five or six phrase pieces that could be rounded binary or ternary, three criteria help distinguish between them: proportion (is the contrasting section substantial enough to stand alone?), nature (is the contrast developmental or genuinely new material?), and era (Baroque/Classical composers favor rounded binary; Romantic composers favor ternary).

# Construction/Recognition
Three criteria for distinction:

1. PROPORTION:
   - Rounded binary: contrasting section too small to stand alone
   - Ternary: contrasting section proportionally substantial

2. NATURE of contrasting section:
   - Rounded binary: melody built from first section's motives; harmony often dominant pedal or V-I alternation
   - Ternary: genuinely contrasting melody in contrasting section

3. ERA:
   - Baroque/Classical (Bach, Handel, Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven): likely rounded binary
   - Romantic (Schubert, Schumann, Chopin, Mendelssohn, Brahms): likely ternary

Note: Beethoven bridges Classical and Romantic eras.

# Musical Context
- Both forms feature return of opening material
- The distinction affects how we understand the piece's structure
- All three criteria may not agree; weigh the evidence
- Context and style inform the analysis

# Examples
Beethoven, Eleven Bagatelles, Op. 119, No. 9:
- Proportion: bars 9-12 contrast for 4 bars vs. 8 bars opening (half as long) --> rounded binary
- Nature: melody from first measure; V-I6/4 alternation --> rounded binary
- Era: Beethoven (Classical/Romantic boundary) --> likely rounded binary
- Conclusion: rounded binary

- Not all pieces fit neatly into one category
- The criteria may give conflicting answers
- Written-out repeats still count as repeats in the diagram
- Beethovens era placement can be debated
