# Quick Definition

Rounded binary and ternary can be distinguished by examining three criteria: the proportion of the contrasting section, the nature of the contrasting material, and the historical era of the composition.

# Core Definition

When analyzing five- or six-phrase pieces that could be rounded binary or ternary, three criteria help distinguish between them: (1) proportion -- is the contrasting section substantial enough to stand alone? (2) nature -- is the contrast developmental or genuinely new material? and (3) era -- Baroque/Classical composers favor rounded binary, Romantic composers favor ternary. These criteria may not be unanimous, requiring the analyst to weigh the evidence (Hutchinson, Ch. 24, pp. 357-359).

# Key Properties

1. Three criteria: proportion, nature, era
2. Proportion: small contrasting section suggests rounded binary; substantial section suggests ternary
3. Nature: developmental motives and V-pedal harmony suggest rounded binary; genuinely contrasting melody suggests ternary
4. Era: Baroque/Classical likely rounded binary; Romantic likely ternary
5. Criteria may conflict; analyst must weigh evidence
6. Beethoven is a borderline figure between Classical and Romantic

# Construction / Recognition

## The Three Criteria:

### 1. PROPORTION
- Rounded binary: contrasting section too small to stand alone
- Ternary: contrasting section proportionally substantial

### 2. NATURE of Contrasting Section
- Rounded binary indicators: (a) melody built from first-section motives, (b) harmony consists of dominant pedal or V alternating with I or I6/4
- Ternary indicators: genuinely contrasting melody

### 3. ERA
- Baroque/Classical (Bach, Handel, Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven): likely rounded binary
- Romantic (Schubert, Schumann, Chopin, Mendelssohn, Brahms): likely ternary

- Conclusion: rounded binary

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Applying only one criterion and ignoring the others
  **Correction**: Always consider all three criteria and weigh the evidence

- **Error**: Treating the criteria as absolute rules
  **Correction**: The criteria are guidelines that may conflict; analytical judgment is required

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking every piece must fit neatly into one category
  **Clarification**: Some pieces are genuinely ambiguous; the criteria may give conflicting answers

- **Confusion**: Placing Beethoven definitively in one era
  **Clarification**: Beethoven bridges Classical and Romantic; generally considered Classical for form analysis
