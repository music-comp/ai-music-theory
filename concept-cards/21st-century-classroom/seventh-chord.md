# Quick Definition
A seventh chord is a four-note chord built by adding a note a third above the fifth of a triad, creating root, third, fifth, and seventh.

# Formal Definition
A seventh chord adds to a triad another note a third higher than the fifth. The four notes are the root, third, fifth, and seventh. Because chords are built in thirds, the intervals from the root are a third (to the 3rd), a fifth (to the 5th), and a seventh (to the 7th). There are five common types of seventh chords in classical and popular music.

# Construction/Recognition
- Four notes: root, third, fifth, seventh
- Built by stacking three thirds
- Five common types: major 7th, dominant 7th, minor 7th, half-diminished 7th, fully-diminished 7th
- Chord extensions (9th, 11th, 13th) continue the pattern of stacked thirds

# Musical Context
Seventh chords are richer and more colorful than triads. The dominant seventh chord is essential for tonal harmony, creating strong pull toward resolution. Jazz makes extensive use of all seventh chord types.

# Examples
- Cmaj7: C-E-G-B (major seventh chord)
- G7: G-B-D-F (dominant seventh chord)
- Am7: A-C-E-G (minor seventh chord)
- Chord members: root (C), 3rd (E), 5th (G), 7th (B)

# Common Confusions
- The "seventh" refers to the interval from root, not the scale degree
- Five different seventh chord qualities exist (don't assume all 7ths are the same)
- Seventh chord = triad + one more third
