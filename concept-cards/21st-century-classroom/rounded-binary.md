# Quick Definition
Rounded binary form features the return of opening material (often shortened) after a contrasting phrase at the beginning of the second section, creating an ABA' structure within a two-section frame.

# Formal Definition
In rounded binary form, the material at the beginning of the first section returns, often shortened, after a contrasting phrase at the beginning of the second section. The phrase diagram typically shows: First Section (phrases 1-2) || Second Section: contrasting material (phrase 3), return of opening (phrases 4-5) ||.

# Construction/Recognition
Generic phrase diagram:
```
First Section: a a' (or a b)
Second Section: c (contrasting) | a a' (return, often shortened)
```

Full name example: "two-reprise continuous rounded binary"
- Two-reprise: both sections repeat
- Continuous: first section doesn't end on tonic
- Rounded: opening material returns in second section

Characteristics:
- Opening material RETURNS after contrast
- Return often abbreviated or varied
- Second section has TWO parts: contrast + return

# Musical Context
- Extremely common in Classical era (Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven)
- Found in: themes from variations, minuet sections, short character pieces
- The return provides satisfying closure
- Often used as the form for individual sections in larger forms

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 284, III (Theme) - two-reprise continuous rounded binary
- Schubert, 20 Minuets, D. 41, No. 18 in F major - two-reprise continuous rounded binary

# Related Concepts
- Binary form
- Ternary form
- Simple binary
- Balanced binary
- Sonata form (evolved from rounded binary)

# Common Confusions
- The "rounding" is the RETURN of opening material
- Rounded binary can be difficult to distinguish from ternary
- The contrasting section is often short (one phrase)
- Different from balanced binary (which has similar endings, not beginnings)
