# Quick Definition
Quintal harmony builds chords by stacking perfect fifths instead of thirds, creating an open, resonant sonority associated with 20th-century music.

# Formal Definition
Quintal harmony refers to chords constructed entirely (or mostly) from stacked fifths, providing an alternative to tertian (third-based) harmony. Quintal chords have an open, resonant quality due to the strong acoustic properties of the perfect fifth. Because a perfect fifth inverts to a perfect fourth, quintal and quartal harmonies are closely related.

# Construction/Calculation
1. Select a root note
2. Stack perfect fifths above it: P5 + P5 + P5...
3. Three-note quintal chord: root - P5 - P5 (e.g., C-G-D)
4. Four-note quintal chord: root - P5 - P5 - P5 (e.g., C-G-D-A)
5. Can mix perfect and diminished fifths for variety
6. Quintal chords span very wide ranges quickly

# Musical Context
Quintal harmony appears throughout 20th-century music, particularly in works seeking an archaic or "open" sound. Bartok used extended quintal passages in his Piano Concerto No. 2. The technique creates a hollow, spacious quality reminiscent of medieval organum. In some contexts, quintal sonorities evoke pastoral or folk-like qualities.

# Examples
- Bartok, Piano Concerto No. 2, II: Extended quintal harmony passage
- Stacked fifths: C-G-D-A-E (pentatonic collection)
- Open fifths in orchestration create quintal texture
- Folk-influenced 20th-century music frequently uses quintal voicings

# Related Concepts
- Quartal harmony
- Secundal harmony
- Tertian harmony
- Power chords (root + fifth in rock music)

# Common Confusions
- Quintal is inversionally related to quartal (P5 inverts to P4)
- Power chords (root-fifth-octave) are a simple quintal structure
- Stacked fifths quickly produce wide-ranging chords
- Quintal harmony is not the same as "open spacing" in traditional harmony
