# Quick Definition
Quintal harmony builds chords by stacking fifths, creating an open, resonant sonority; it is the inversional equivalent of quartal harmony.

# Core Definition
Quintal harmony refers to chords stacked entirely (or mostly) in fifths. Because a perfect fifth inverts to a perfect fourth, quintal and quartal harmonies are closely related. Quintal chords span wider ranges quickly and have an open, resonant quality from the acoustic strength of the perfect fifth (Hutchinson, Ch. 32, p. 471).

# Prerequisites
- **Intervals** -- Must understand perfect fifths

# Key Properties
1. Chords built from stacked perfect fifths
2. Open, resonant quality
3. Inversionally related to quartal (P5 inverts to P4)
4. Spans wide ranges quickly
5. Associated with archaic, pastoral, or folk qualities

# Construction / Recognition
- Three-note quintal: C-G-D
- Four-note quintal: C-G-D-A
- Five stacked fifths produce a pentatonic collection

# Context & Application
Bartok used extended quintal passages. Quintal sonorities evoke pastoral or folk qualities and appear in film scoring for "open" or spacious textures.

# Examples
- Bartok, Piano Concerto No. 2, II (p. 472, Figure 32.3.4): Extended quintal harmony passage

# Relationships
## Related
- **Quartal harmony** -- Inversional equivalent (P4 inverts to P5)
- **Secundal harmony** -- Another alternative to tertian construction

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing quintal with open-spaced tertian harmony
  **Correction**: Quintal chords are specifically built from stacked 5ths, not just widely spaced

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking quintal and quartal produce the same sound
  **Clarification**: While inversionally equivalent, register placement creates different sonic qualities
