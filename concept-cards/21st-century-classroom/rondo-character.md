# Quick Definition
Rondo character refers to a quick tempo in duple meter with light, playful style, typically achieved through staccato articulation - common in Classical finales but not required for rondo form.

# Formal Definition
Rondo character is characterized by quick tempo in duple meter with light character, typically achieved through the use of staccato articulation. This duple meter could be either simple (2/4) or compound (6/8). During the Classical era, the final movement of multi-movement compositions was often in rondo character. However, not all pieces in rondo form have rondo character.

# Construction/Recognition
Characteristics of rondo character:
- Quick tempo (Allegro, Presto)
- Duple meter: simple (2/4) or compound (6/8)
- Light, playful quality
- Staccato articulation prominent
- Often dance-like rhythms

Note: Rondo CHARACTER is distinct from rondo FORM
- A piece can be in rondo form without rondo character
- A piece can have rondo character without being in rondo form

# Musical Context
- Common in Classical era finale movements
- Provides contrast to serious first movements
- The light character suits the "final statement" role
- Associated with entertainment and accessibility

- Mozart, Symphony No. 39 in E-flat major, K. 543, IV

# Common Confusions
- Rondo character does NOT require rondo form
- Rondo form does NOT require rondo character
- The term describes CHARACTER/STYLE, not structure
- Compound duple (6/8) is still "duple" for this purpose
