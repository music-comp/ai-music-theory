# Quick Definition
Secondary chords follow the same voice leading principles as seventh chords: resolve the 7th down by step, alternate complete and incomplete voicings in chains, and apply the vii-dim-7 resolution rules to secondary diminished sevenths.

# Formal Definition
Voice leading secondary dominant and secondary diminished seventh chords uses the same principles as their diatonic counterparts:
1. The 7th of any secondary seventh chord resolves down by step
2. In chains of root position secondary sevenths, alternate complete and incomplete voicings
3. Secondary vii-dim-7 and vii-half-dim-7 chords follow the special resolution rules for diminished seventh chords
4. Chromatic alterations (typically raised notes) tend to resolve in the direction of their alteration

# Rules/Procedures
1. **Secondary dominants with 7th**: Resolve the 7th down by step
2. **Successive secondary 7ths in root position**: Alternate complete and incomplete voicings
   - The 3rd of one chord (often a raised note) resolves down by half step to the 7th of the next chord
   - This chromaticism "cancels out" as raised note moves to diatonic note
3. **Secondary vii-dim-7 or vii-half-dim-7 chords**: Follow Principle 27.4.1
   - When 7th is above 3rd, resolve 3rd up; others resolve normally
4. **Exception for vii-dim-7/V to V**: If voicing requires 7th above 3rd, parallel unequal 5ths are preferred over doubling the leading tone

# Musical Context
Secondary chords are chromatic embellishments that tonicize diatonic chords. Their voice leading maintains the same principles as diatonic chords while accommodating the chromatic alterations.

# Examples
- V7/V to V: The 7th of V7/V resolves down to become the 3rd of V
- Chain of secondary dominants: V7/vi - V7/ii - V7/V - V7 - I with alternating voicings
- vii-dim-7/V to V: Special care needed to avoid parallel 5ths or doubled leading tone

# Common Confusions
- Treating secondary chords as fundamentally different from diatonic chords (same principles apply)
- Forgetting the special resolution for secondary diminished sevenths
- Not recognizing that the chromatic alteration affects tendency (raised notes want to continue rising)
- Missing the exception for vii-dim-7/V where unequal 5ths are preferred
