# Quick Definition

Secondary chords follow the same voice leading principles as their diatonic counterparts: resolve the 7th down by step, alternate voicings in chains, and apply viio7 resolution rules -- with chromatic alterations resolving in the direction of their alteration.

# Core Definition

Voice leading secondary dominant and secondary diminished seventh chords uses the same principles as diatonic counterparts (Hutchinson, Ch. 29, pp. 417-418):
1. Resolve the 7th of a chord down by step
2. Alternate complete and incomplete voicings in chains of root position secondary 7ths; the 3rd (typically raised) resolves down by half step to the 7th of the next chord
3. Follow viio7/viiø7 resolution principles for secondary viio7 and viiø7 chords
4. Exception: when viio7/V or viiø7/V resolves to V with 7th above 3rd, prefer parallel unequal 5ths over doubling the leading tone

# Key Properties

1. Same voice leading rules as diatonic counterparts
2. Resolve 7th down by step
3. In chains: alternate complete/incomplete voicings; raised 3rd resolves to diatonic 7th of next chord
4. Secondary viio7/viiø7: follow Principle 27.4.1
5. Exception for viio7/V to V: unequal 5ths preferred over doubled leading tone
6. Chromatic alterations (raised notes) tend to resolve in the direction of alteration

# Construction / Recognition

## Procedure:
1. Identify the secondary chord and its target
2. Apply the same voice leading rules as the diatonic equivalent
3. Resolve the 7th down by step
4. For secondary viio7: check 7th-above-3rd voicing
5. For viio7/V to V: unequal 5ths are acceptable if voicing requires it

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating secondary chords as fundamentally different from diatonic chords
  **Correction**: Same voice leading principles apply; just watch the chromatic alterations

- **Error**: Doubling the leading tone when viio7/V resolves to V
  **Correction**: Prefer unequal 5ths over doubled leading tone in this situation

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Chromatic alterations have no tendency
  **Clarification**: Raised notes tend to continue rising; the 3rd of a secondary chord (a raised note) resolves down to the 7th of the next chord, canceling the chromaticism
