# Quick Definition
An escape tone is a non-chord tone approached by step and left by leap in the opposite direction.

# Formal Definition
An escape tone is approached by step and left by leap in the opposite direction. It "escapes" from the expected stepwise resolution by leaping away. Escape tones are less common than passing tones and neighbor tones.

# Analysis/Application
To identify an escape tone:
1. Look for a non-chord tone approached by step
2. Confirm it leaves by leap in the opposite direction
3. The escape tone moves away from the expected resolution
4. Label as ET or E.T.

# Musical Context
Escape tones create a sense of surprise by leaping away from an expected resolution. They often occur at phrase endings or in sequential passages.

# Examples
- "The Star-Spangled Banner" - the word "of" in "home of the brave" is a classic escape tone example
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in C Major, Hob. XVI:35, III (1780) - escape tones used in melodic sequence
- Paul McCartney, "Silly Love Songs" (1976) - escape tone in popular music

# Related Concepts
- Appoggiatura (opposite: leap to step)
- Non-chord tones
- Melodic embellishment

# Common Confusions
- Escape tone = step then leap; Appoggiatura = leap then step
- The leap and step must be in opposite directions
- Don't confuse with neighbor tones (which return to the original note)
