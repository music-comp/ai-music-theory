# Quick Definition
The Neapolitan chord typically resolves to V or a cadential 6/4 chord, with flat-2 moving down to the leading tone and 4 often remaining as a common tone or moving to 5.

# Formal Definition
As a pre-dominant chord, the Neapolitan (N6) progresses to dominant-function chords. The standard resolution is N6 to V or N6 to cadential I6/4 to V. The lowered second scale degree (flat-2) typically resolves down by diminished third (or augmented sixth in contrary motion) to the leading tone (7). Scale degree 4 in the bass commonly moves to 5.

# Construction/Recognition
Typical voice leading from N6:
- flat-2 (root of N) resolves down to 7 (leading tone)
- 4 (bass note in N6) moves to 5 (root of V)
- flat-6 (5th of N) moves to 5 (root of V) or remains if going to cad. 6/4
- The interval flat-2 to 7 is a diminished third (enharmonic to major second)

Common progressions:
- N6 - V - i
- N6 - i6/4 - V - i
- N6 - V7 - i

# Musical Context
- The diminished third motion (flat-2 to 7) creates distinctive chromatic color
- First inversion places 4 in bass for smooth motion to 5
- Often precedes the cadential 6/4 in authentic cadences
- The dramatic quality makes it suitable for climactic moments

# Examples
- Beethoven, Moonlight Sonata - N6 resolving to V in C# minor
- Compare the effect of N6 - V with iv - V or ii diminished 6 - V

# Common Confusions
- The flat-2 to 7 motion is a diminished third, not a major second (different spelling)
- Root position Neapolitan is rare; first inversion is standard
- Do not double flat-2 (it's a tendency tone)
- The Neapolitan is NOT a dominant-function chord despite its dramatic quality
