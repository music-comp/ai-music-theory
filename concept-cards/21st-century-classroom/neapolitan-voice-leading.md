# Quick Definition
The Neapolitan chord (N6) resolves to V or cadential I6/4, with b2 moving down by a diminished third to the leading tone (7), scale degree 4 in the bass moving to 5, and b6 resolving to 5.

# Core Definition
As a pre-dominant chord, the Neapolitan (N6) progresses to dominant-function chords. The standard resolution is N6 to V, or N6 to cadential I6/4 to V. The lowered second scale degree (b2) typically resolves downward by a diminished third to the leading tone (scale degree 7). Scale degree 4 in the bass (the bass note of N6) moves to scale degree 5 (the root of V). The interval from b2 to 7 is a diminished third, which is enharmonically equivalent to a major second but spelled differently to reflect the chromatic voice leading.

# Prerequisites
- **Neapolitan chord**: The chord being resolved
- **Cadential six-four**: A common intermediate step in the resolution

# Key Properties
1. b2 (root of N) resolves down to 7 (leading tone) -- a diminished third
2. Scale degree 4 (bass of N6) moves to 5 (root of V)
3. b6 (5th of N) resolves to 5 (root of V) or stays if going to cad. 6/4
4. Common progressions: N6-V-i, N6-I6/4-V-i, N6-V7-i
5. First inversion (N6) provides smooth bass motion (4 to 5)
6. Do not double b2 (it is a tendency tone)

# Construction / Recognition
- N6 followed by V: b2 descends to leading tone, 4 in bass moves to 5
- N6 followed by cadential I6/4: b2 can move to 1 (common tone with I6/4) or to 7
- The diminished third (b2 to 7) is the most distinctive voice-leading interval
- Compare the effect of N6-V with iv-V or iio6-V to hear the difference

# Context & Application
The diminished third motion from b2 to 7 creates the distinctive chromatic color of the Neapolitan resolution. First inversion (N6) is standard because it places scale degree 4 in the bass, allowing smooth half-step or whole-step motion to scale degree 5. The cadential I6/4 often intervenes between N6 and V, providing an even stronger cadential approach.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Moonlight Sonata, Op. 27, No. 2, I -- N6 resolving to V in C# minor
- Standard comparison: try N6-V, iv-V, and iio6-V in succession to hear differences

# Common Errors
- Doubling b2 (it is a tendency tone that must resolve)
- Moving b2 up instead of down to the leading tone

# Common Confusions
- **Diminished third vs. major second**: b2 to 7 is a diminished third (e.g., Db to B-natural), not a major second -- spelling matters
- **N6, not root position**: Root position Neapolitan is rare; N6 puts 4 in bass for smooth motion
- **Not dominant function**: The Neapolitan leads TO dominant, it does not replace it
