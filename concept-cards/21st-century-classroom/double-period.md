# Quick Definition
A double period consists of at least four phrases organized into an antecedent group (first two phrases) and a consequent group (final two phrases), with the consequent group ending more conclusively.

# Formal Definition
A double period consists of at least 4 phrases and is comprised of an antecedent group and a consequent group. The first two phrases form the antecedent group and the final two phrases form the consequent group, which ends with a cadence that "answers" the less conclusive cadence that ended the antecedent group.

# Analysis/Application
Typical cadential schemes for double periods:
- Scheme 1: HC - HC - HC - PAC
- Scheme 2: IAC - IAC - IAC - PAC
- Scheme 3: IAC - HC - IAC - PAC

Common melodic scheme: a b a b' (parallel double period)

# Musical Context
Double periods extend the antecedent-consequent relationship over a larger span, creating balanced 16-bar structures. They are common in Classical themes and song forms.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata Op. 10, No. 1, II - a b a b' melodic scheme with HC-HC-HC-PAC

# Common Confusions
- Double period vs. repeated period: in a double period, the antecedent group ends inconclusively; in a repeated period, each period is complete
- The second phrase of the antecedent group can end with HC or IAC
- Parallel double period: both groups begin similarly (a b a b')
