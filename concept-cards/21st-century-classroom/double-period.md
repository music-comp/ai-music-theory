# Quick Definition
A double period consists of at least four phrases organized into an antecedent group (first two phrases) and a consequent group (final two phrases), with the consequent group ending more conclusively.

# Core Definition
A double period consists of at least 4 phrases comprised of an antecedent group and a consequent group. The first two phrases form the antecedent group; the final two form the consequent group, ending with a cadence that "answers" the less conclusive cadence at the end of the antecedent group. Common melodic scheme: a b a b' (parallel double period). A repeated period is different: it has two complete periods repeated (e.g., HC-PAC-HC-PAC).

# Prerequisites
- **Period**: Understanding of antecedent-consequent structure

# Key Properties
1. At least four phrases
2. Antecedent group (first two phrases) + consequent group (final two)
3. Consequent group ends more conclusively
4. Common cadential schemes: HC-HC-HC-PAC; IAC-IAC-IAC-PAC; IAC-HC-IAC-PAC
5. Common melodic scheme: a b a b' (parallel double period)
6. Distinct from repeated period

# Construction / Recognition
1. Count phrases (at least four)
2. Divide into antecedent group (first half) and consequent group (second half)
3. Check that the antecedent group ends inconclusively
4. Check that the consequent group ends with the most conclusive cadence
5. Compare to repeated period: in repeated period, each pair is a complete period

# Context & Application
Double periods extend the antecedent-consequent relationship over a larger span, creating balanced 16-bar structures. They are common in Classical themes. The key distinction from repeated periods is that the antecedent group ends without full closure.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata Op. 10, No. 1, II -- a b a b' melodic scheme, HC-HC-HC-PAC
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata Op. 53, I -- repeated period (HC-PAC-HC-PAC), NOT double period

# Common Errors
- Confusing double period with repeated period (check the second phrase's cadence)

# Common Confusions
- **Double vs. repeated period**: In a double period, the antecedent group ends inconclusively (HC-HC); in a repeated period, the first period is complete (HC-PAC)
- **IAC-HC possible in antecedent**: The first phrase can end IAC and the second HC within the antecedent group (Scheme 3)
