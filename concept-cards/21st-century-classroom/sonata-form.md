# Quick Definition
Sonata form is a large-scale ternary structure consisting of exposition (presenting themes in tonic and secondary key), development (fragmenting and modulating), and recapitulation (restating themes in tonic).

# Formal Definition
Sonata form, also known as "first-movement form," is "the most important principle of musical form, or formal type, from the Classical period well into the 20th century" (Grove Music Online). It consists of three main sections: exposition (presenting primary and secondary themes in different keys), development (fragmenting and sequencing themes through various keys), and recapitulation (restating themes, now all in tonic).

# Construction/Recognition
Three main sections:

EXPOSITION:
- Primary Theme (PT): tonic key
- Transition: modulating
- Secondary Theme (ST): V (major) or III (minor)
- Closing Theme (CT): secondary key (optional)

DEVELOPMENT:
- Fragmentation and sequencing of themes
- Modulations through various keys
- Retransition: V pedal leading back to tonic

RECAPITULATION:
- Primary Theme: tonic
- Transition: may be altered (tonicizations, not modulation)
- Secondary Theme: NOW IN TONIC
- Closing Theme: tonic

# Musical Context
- Standard first movement form in Classical symphonies, sonatas, quartets
- Evolved from rounded and balanced binary forms
- Repeat signs often indicate two-reprise structure
- The tonal drama of exposition is resolved in recapitulation

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 545, I
- Mozart, Eine kleine Nachtmusik, K. 525, I
- Tchaikovsky, Nutcracker, "Miniature Overture" (sonatina form - no development)

# Common Confusions
- "Sonata form" is not the same as "sonata" (a multi-movement work)
- Multiple secondary themes are common (ST1, ST2)
- The recapitulation brings ALL themes to tonic
- Development may include new material
