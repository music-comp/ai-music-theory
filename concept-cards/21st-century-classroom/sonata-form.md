# Quick Definition

Sonata form is a large-scale ternary structure consisting of exposition (presenting themes in tonic and secondary key), development (fragmenting and modulating), and recapitulation (restating themes in tonic).

# Core Definition

Sonata form, also known as "first-movement form," is "the most important principle of musical form, or formal type, from the Classical period well into the 20th century" (Grove Music Online, cited by Hutchinson, Ch. 25, p. 367). It consists of three main sections: exposition, development, and recapitulation. Douglass Green notes sonata form evolved from and synthesizes rounded binary and balanced binary: it begins the return with a restatement of the opening (rounded binary) and closes with the secondary material transposed to tonic (balanced binary).

# Prerequisites

- **Binary form** -- Sonata form evolved from binary form
- **Modulation** -- Tonal movement is fundamental to sonata form
- **Cadence** -- Cadences demarcate sections and themes

# Key Properties

1. Three main sections: exposition, development, recapitulation
2. Exposition presents themes in contrasting keys (tonic + secondary key)
3. Development fragments, sequences, and modulates themes
4. Recapitulation restates all themes in tonic
5. Multiple secondary themes (ST1, ST2) are common
6. Repeat signs create a two-reprise structure
7. Evolved from rounded binary and balanced binary

# Construction / Recognition

## Generalized Diagram:
| | EXPOSITION | | | DEVELOPMENT | | RECAPITULATION | | |
| Major: | PT (I) | transition | ST (V) | (CT: V) | Fragmentation, sequencing, retransition (V pedal) | PT (I) | transition (tonicizations) | ST (I) | (CT: I) |
| Minor: | PT (i) | transition | ST (III) | (CT: III) | Development | PT (i) | transition | ST (i) | (CT: i) |

PT = Primary Theme, ST = Secondary Theme, CT = Closing Theme

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: Standard first-movement form in Classical symphonies, sonatas, quartets
- **Common applications**: Most important formal type from Classical era through 20th century
- **Historical/stylistic notes**: The exposition typically repeats; development and recapitulation may also repeat as a unit (two-reprise). Some sonatas include new material in the development. Not all sonatas have closing themes.

# Relationships

## Builds Upon
- **Rounded binary** -- Sonata form begins the return with restatement of opening
- **Balanced binary** -- Sonata form closes with secondary material transposed to tonic

## Enables
- **Sonata rondo** -- Hybrid combining rondo and sonata principles
- **Sonatina form** -- Sonata form without the development

## Related
- **Sonata principle** -- The theoretical basis for tonal opposition and resolution
- **Structural functions** -- Expository, transitional, developmental, terminative

## Contrasts With
- **Sonatina form** -- Lacks the development section
- **Rondo form** -- Alternating refrain/episodes vs. three-section tonal drama

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming the recapitulation is an exact repeat of the exposition
  **Correction**: The transition must be altered so the ST appears in tonic, not the secondary key

- **Error**: Expecting exactly one secondary theme
  **Correction**: Multiple secondary themes (ST1, ST2) are common

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: "Sonata form" is the same as "sonata"
  **Clarification**: "Sonata form" is a formal structure for a single movement; "sonata" is a multi-movement work

- **Confusion**: Thinking the development must only use existing themes
  **Clarification**: Some developments introduce new material
