# Quick Definition
The French augmented sixth (Fr+6) adds scale degree 2 to the Italian augmented sixth foundation, creating a four-note chord with a distinctive whole-tone color.

# Formal Definition
The French augmented sixth chord (labeled Fr+6) contains scale degrees flat-6, 1, 2, and sharp-4. It builds upon the Italian augmented sixth's flat-6, 1, and sharp-4 by adding scale degree 2. This creates two tritones within the chord (flat-6 to 2, and 1 to sharp-4), giving it a whole-tone quality.

# Construction/Recognition
Scale degrees: flat-6, 1, 2, sharp-4
- In C major/minor: Ab, C, D, F# (with Ab in bass)
- When stacked in thirds: F#-Ab-C-D (contains diminished third F#-Ab)
- Contains two tritones: flat-6 to 2, and 1 to sharp-4
- All four notes belong to a whole-tone scale

Identification: Four-note augmented sixth chord with scale degree 2.

# Musical Context
- Pre-dominant function
- Intensifies the drive toward the V chord
- The whole-tone quality creates a distinctive, unstable color
- Resolves to V with both tritones resolving

# Examples
- Schubert, Winterreise, D. 911, "Der Wegweiser" (1823)

# Common Confusions
- Fr+6 has scale degree 2 (not flat-2 or sharp-2)
- The two tritones give it a whole-tone quality distinct from other +6 chords
- Do not confuse with Ger+6 which has flat-3 instead of 2
- The "French" label has no historical justification
