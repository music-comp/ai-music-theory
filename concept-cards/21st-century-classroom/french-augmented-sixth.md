# Quick Definition
The French augmented sixth (Fr+6) adds scale degree 2 to the Italian foundation, creating a four-note chord (b6, 1, 2, #4) with two tritones and a distinctive whole-tone color.

# Core Definition
The French augmented sixth chord (Fr+6) contains scale degrees b6, 1, 2, and #4. It builds upon the Italian augmented sixth's b6, 1, and #4 by adding scale degree 2. This creates two tritones within the chord (b6 to 2, and 1 to #4), giving it a distinctive whole-tone quality. All four notes of the Fr+6 belong to a whole-tone scale.

# Prerequisites
- **Augmented sixth chords**: The general category
- **Italian augmented sixth**: The foundation that Fr+6 extends

# Key Properties
1. Scale degrees: b6, 1, 2, #4 (four notes)
2. Built on the Italian foundation (b6, 1, #4) plus scale degree 2
3. Contains two tritones: b6 to 2, and 1 to #4
4. All four notes belong to a whole-tone scale
5. In C major/minor: Ab-C-D-F# (with Ab in bass)
6. Stacked in thirds: F#-Ab-C-D (contains diminished third F#-Ab)

# Construction / Recognition
- Four-note augmented sixth chord with scale degree 2 (not b3)
- Distinguished from Ger+6 by the presence of natural 2 instead of b3
- The whole-tone quality is distinctive when heard
- The two tritones both resolve when the chord moves to V

# Context & Application
The Fr+6 has a distinctive, unstable sound due to its two tritones and whole-tone content. It intensifies the drive toward the V chord with both tritones resolving simultaneously. Less common than the Ger+6, it nonetheless creates a unique harmonic color in the pre-dominant position.

# Examples
- Schubert, Winterreise, D. 911, "Der Wegweiser" (1823) -- Fr+6 intensifying the drive to V

# Common Errors
- Confusing scale degree 2 with b2 or #2
- Mistaking Fr+6 for Ger+6 (check for 2 vs. b3)

# Common Confusions
- **2, not b2 or #2**: Fr+6 uses natural scale degree 2
- **Whole-tone connection**: All four notes belong to a whole-tone scale, giving a distinct color
- **Two tritones**: b6-2 and 1-#4 both resolve to scale degree 5 and its octave
