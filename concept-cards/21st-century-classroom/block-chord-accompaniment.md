# Quick Definition
Block chord accompaniment involves playing complete chords in rhythmic patterns, including the "1 (2) &" pattern, the "Barbara Ann" rhythm, repeated eighth-note chords, and repeated quarter-note chords.

# Core Definition
Block chord accompaniments feature complete chords played rhythmically rather than arpeggiated. Four common patterns: (1) The "1 (2) &" rhythm -- chord on beat 1, chord on the upbeat of beat 2. (2) The "Barbara Ann" rhythm -- "1 2 3 (4) & (1) & (2) & 3" pattern. (3) Repeated eighth-note chords. (4) Repeated quarter-note chords. The specific rhythm chosen significantly affects the feel and genre association.

# Prerequisites
- **Texture**: Understanding of accompaniment types

# Key Properties
1. Complete chords played rhythmically (not arpeggiated)
2. "1 (2) &": two chords per measure, beat 1 and upbeat of 2
3. "Barbara Ann": syncopated pattern from 1960s pop
4. Repeated eighth notes: constant motion, common in classical and rock
5. Repeated quarter notes: steady pulse, common across genres
6. Rhythm pattern can appear in diminution (doubled speed)

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify that chords are played as complete units
2. Determine the specific rhythmic pattern
3. The "1 (2) &" has the distinctive syncopated second chord
4. Repeated notes create constant rhythmic drive

# Context & Application
Block chord accompaniments are ubiquitous in both popular and classical music. The specific rhythm chosen defines the groove and genre feel. These patterns can be used on piano, guitar, or any chordal instrument.

# Examples
- "1 (2) &": "Let's Get It On" (1973), "Thinking Out Loud" (2014), "Don't You (Forget About Me)" (1985), "Love Yourself" (Bieber, 2015, in diminution)
- "Barbara Ann": "Barbara Ann" (1961), "You Can't Hurry Love" (1966), "Walking On Sunshine" (1985), "Smoke on the Water" (1973, slower version)
- Repeated 8th notes: Mozart K. 310 I, Schubert "Gute Nacht," Schumann "Ich grolle nicht," "We Are Young" (2011)
- Repeated quarter notes: Handel "Ombra mai fu," Billy Joel "She's Got a Way," "Roxanne" (1978), "Love Song" (Bareilles, 2007)

# Common Confusions
- **Many patterns exist**: The four listed here are common but not exhaustive
- **Diminution**: A pattern can appear at doubled speed (as in "Love Yourself")
- **Genre association**: The rhythm chosen strongly affects the stylistic feel
