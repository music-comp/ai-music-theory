# Quick Definition
Block chord accompaniment involves playing chords as complete units in rhythmic patterns, including specific rhythms like the "1 (2) &" pattern, the "Barbara Ann" rhythm, repeated eighth notes, and repeated quarter notes.

# Formal Definition
Block chord accompaniments feature complete chords played rhythmically rather than arpeggiated. Common patterns include: the "1 (2) &" rhythm (chord on beat 1, chord on the "and" of beat 2); the "Barbara Ann" rhythm (a more complex syncopated pattern); repeated eighth-note chords; and repeated quarter-note chords.

# Analysis/Application
Common block chord rhythms:
1. "1 (2) &" - first chord on beat 1, second on upbeat of beat 2
2. "Barbara Ann" - "1 2 3 (4) & (1) & (2) & 3" pattern
3. Repeated eighth notes - constant eighth-note chords
4. Repeated quarter notes - steady quarter-note chords

# Musical Context
Block chord accompaniments are ubiquitous in popular music and appear throughout classical music. The specific rhythm chosen significantly affects the feel and genre association.

# Examples
"1 (2) &" rhythm:
- "Let's Get It On" (Marvin Gaye, 1973)
- "Thinking Out Loud" (Ed Sheeran, 2014)
- "Don't You (Forget About Me)" (1985)

"Barbara Ann" rhythm:
- "Barbara Ann" (1961)
- "You Can't Hurry Love" (1966)
- "Walking On Sunshine" (1985)

Repeated eighth notes:
- Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 310, I
- Schubert, "Gute Nacht"
- "We Are Young" (Fun., 2011)

# Common Confusions
- Block chords can use many different rhythmic patterns
- The rhythm chosen significantly affects style and feel
- Some patterns can appear in diminution (doubled speed)
