# Quick Definition

Voice leading (or part writing) refers to the rules governing melodic motions of voices in harmonic progressions, prioritizing independence of parts, proper chord voicing, and smooth melodic motion.

# Core Definition

Voice leading, also known as part writing, refers to rules about melodic motions of voices involved in harmonic progressions. The primary concerns behind voice leading rules are independence of parts, voicing of chords, and economy of melodic motion for the sake of singable parts (Hutchinson, Ch. 26, p. 389).

# Prerequisites

- **Triad** -- Voice leading requires understanding of chord structures
- **Triad inversions** -- Different inversions have different voice leading rules

# Key Properties

1. Three primary concerns: independence of parts, voicing of chords, economy of motion
2. "SATB" = soprano, alto, tenor, bass (four-part writing)
3. "Chorale-style": S+A on treble staff, T+B on bass staff
4. "Piano-style": three upper parts in treble, bass alone in bass clef
5. Based primarily on J.S. Bach's 371 Chorales

# Construction / Recognition

## Chorale-Style Layout:
- Treble staff: Soprano (stems up), Alto (stems down)
- Bass staff: Tenor (stems up), Bass (stems down)

## Piano-Style Layout:
- Treble staff (right hand): Soprano, Alto, Tenor
- Bass staff (left hand): Bass only

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: Chorale harmonizations, composition exercises, arranging
- **Common applications**: Bach chorales as primary model; principles apply to all multi-part writing
- **Historical/stylistic notes**: Rules based on Renaissance through Romantic practice, particularly J.S. Bach's 371 Chorales

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating all four voices as a block that moves together
  **Correction**: Each voice must be treated as an independent melody

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Voice leading is the same as harmony
  **Clarification**: Voice leading is about HOW voices move; harmony is about WHICH chords are used

- **Confusion**: Piano-style and chorale-style produce different results
  **Clarification**: The same rules apply; only the staff layout differs
