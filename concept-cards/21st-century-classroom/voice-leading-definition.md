# Quick Definition
Voice leading (or part writing) refers to the rules governing melodic motions of voices in harmonic progressions, prioritizing independence of parts, proper chord voicing, and smooth melodic motion.

# Formal Definition
Voice leading encompasses the principles that guide how individual voices move from one chord to the next in multi-part writing. The three primary concerns are: (1) independence of parts, ensuring each voice has its own melodic identity; (2) voicing of chords, ensuring proper doubling and spacing; and (3) economy of melodic motion, creating singable lines with minimal leaps.

# Rules/Procedures
1. SATB (chorale-style) voice leading uses four parts: soprano, alto, tenor, and bass
2. In chorale style, soprano and alto share the treble staff; tenor and bass share the bass staff
3. Piano-style voice leading places all three upper parts in the right hand (treble clef)
4. Each voice should be treated as an independent melody
5. Voices should move smoothly, preferring stepwise motion over leaps

# Musical Context
Voice leading applies to any multi-part composition, from Bach chorales to modern arrangements. It is essential for writing singable choral parts and creating smooth harmonic progressions. The principles are based on Renaissance and Baroque practice, particularly J.S. Bach's 371 Chorales.

# Examples
- J.S. Bach's 371 Chorales serve as the primary model for voice leading practice
- Any SATB hymn or choral arrangement demonstrates these principles

# Common Confusions
- Confusing voice leading with harmony - voice leading is about how voices move, not which chords are used
- Treating all voices as one block rather than independent melodic lines
- Forgetting that piano-style and chorale-style have different voice distributions on the staff
