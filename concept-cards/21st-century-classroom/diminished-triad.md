# Quick Definition
A diminished triad is built with two minor thirds (m3 + m3), totaling a diminished fifth (tritone) from root to fifth.

# Core Definition
A diminished triad is built with a minor third (3 half steps) between the root and third, and another minor third (3 half steps) between the third and fifth. The total interval from root to fifth is a diminished fifth (6 half steps, or tritone). Diminished triads are less common than major and minor triads. The lead-sheet symbol uses the degree symbol (o) after the root. In Roman numerals, lowercase with degree symbol (viio, iio).

# Prerequisites
- **Triad**: The three-note chord concept
- **Augmented/diminished intervals**: Understanding the diminished fifth

# Key Properties
1. Root to third: minor 3rd (3 half steps)
2. Third to fifth: minor 3rd (3 half steps)
3. Root to fifth: diminished 5th (6 half steps, tritone)
4. Lead-sheet symbol: root + degree symbol (Bo, Co, Do)
5. Roman numeral: lowercase + degree symbol (viio, iio)
6. Less common than major and minor triads; inherently unstable

# Construction / Recognition
- m3 + m3 (both intervals are the same)
- Total span: o5 (diminished fifth = tritone)
- The tritone creates instability and drive to resolve
- Lead-sheet: letter + "o" or "dim"

# Context & Application
Diminished triads occur naturally on ^7 in major keys (viio) and on ^2 in minor keys (iio). The viio triad has dominant function because it contains the leading tone and the tritone. Diminished triads are less common than major and minor triads due to their inherent instability.

# Examples
- B diminished: B-D-F (m3 + m3, tritone B-F)
- C diminished: C-Eb-Gb
- F# diminished: F#-A-C
- Lead-sheet: Bo, Cdim, F#o

# Common Errors
- Confusing diminished triad with diminished seventh chord (triad has 3 notes; seventh chord has 4)
- Forgetting that the outer interval is a diminished fifth, not a perfect fifth

# Common Confusions
- **Both intervals identical**: Both internal intervals are minor thirds (unlike major and minor triads)
- **Tritone content**: The diminished fifth is a tritone, giving the chord its unstable quality
- **Relatively rare**: Diminished triads are less common than major and minor triads
