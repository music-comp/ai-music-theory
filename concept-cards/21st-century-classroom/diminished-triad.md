# Quick Definition
A diminished triad consists of two minor thirds stacked together, totaling a diminished fifth from root to fifth.

# Formal Definition
A diminished triad is built with a minor third (3 half steps) between the root and third, and another minor third (3 half steps) between the third and fifth. The total interval from root to fifth is a diminished fifth (6 half steps, or tritone). Diminished triads sound unstable and want to resolve.

# Construction/Recognition
- Root to third: minor 3rd (3 half steps)
- Third to fifth: minor 3rd (3 half steps)
- Root to fifth: diminished 5th (6 half steps, tritone)
- Lead-sheet symbol: root + degree symbol (Co, Bo)
- Roman numeral: lowercase + degree symbol (viio, iio)

# Musical Context
Diminished triads occur naturally on ^7 in major keys (viio) and on ^2 in minor keys (iio). They are less common than major and minor triads due to their inherent instability. The diminished triad has dominant function when built on the leading tone.

# Examples
- B diminished: B-D-F (m3 + m3)
- C diminished: C-Eb-Gb
- F# diminished: F#-A-C
- Lead-sheet: Bo, Cdim, F#o

# Common Confusions
- Both internal intervals are minor thirds
- The fifth is diminished, not perfect
- Diminished triads are relatively rare; don't confuse with diminished seventh chords
