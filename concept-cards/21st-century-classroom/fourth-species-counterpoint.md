# Quick Definition
Fourth species deals with suspensions and syncopations using tied half notes; if the note after the tie is dissonant (4th or 7th), it must resolve down by step as a suspension.

# Core Definition
Fourth species counterpoint focuses on suspensions and syncopations. Half notes are tied across barlines, creating rhythmic displacement. When the note after the tie is dissonant (forming a 4th or 7th against the cantus firmus), it is a suspension and must resolve downward by step. When the note after the tie is consonant (unison, 3rd, 5th, 6th, 8ve), it is a syncopation and may be left by step or leap in any direction. In this introductory text, the cantus firmus is only in the lower voice for fourth species (Hutchinson, Ch. 30, p. 428).

# Key Properties
1. Half note on beat 3 ties across barline to half note on beat 1
2. Beat 3 half note is always consonant (serves as preparation)
3. Tied note on beat 1: if dissonant (4th or 7th), it is a suspension resolving down by step
4. Tied note on beat 1: if consonant, it is a syncopation (free to leap or step any direction)
5. Allowed suspensions in upper voice: 4-3 and 7-6 only
6. Forbidden suspensions: 9-8 and 2-1
7. Penultimate measure must contain a 7-6 suspension
8. Begin with a half rest, then half note an octave above c.f.

# Construction / Recognition
**Setup:**
1. Start with a half rest, then write a half note an octave above the c.f.
2. The half note on beat 3 of every measure (except the penultimate) ties across the barline

**Identifying suspensions vs. syncopations:**
1. Check the interval on beat 1 (after the tie)
2. If dissonant (4th or 7th): suspension -- must resolve down by step
3. If consonant (1, 3, 5, 6, 8): syncopation -- may be left in any manner

**Required elements:**
- Penultimate measure: must contain a 7-6 suspension
- Final measure: whole note at the octave

**Label all suspensions and syncopations in analysis; circle all dissonant intervals.**

# Context & Application
Fourth species isolates the suspension figure, one of the most important expressive devices in Renaissance and Baroque counterpoint. Suspensions create tension through rhythmic displacement of dissonance, with the resolution providing a sense of release. The preparation-suspension-resolution pattern (consonant-dissonant-consonant) is fundamental to tonal music.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Using 9-8 or 2-1 suspensions
  **Correction**: Only 4-3 and 7-6 suspensions are allowed in the upper voice in this style
- **Error**: Resolving a suspension upward
  **Correction**: Suspensions must always resolve downward by step
- **Error**: Forgetting the 7-6 suspension in the penultimate measure
  **Correction**: The penultimate measure requires a 7-6 suspension

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Treating all tied notes as suspensions
  **Clarification**: If the interval after the tie is consonant, it is a syncopation (not a suspension) and can be left freely
- **Confusion**: Thinking suspensions can resolve in any direction
  **Clarification**: In species counterpoint, suspensions must resolve downward by step
