# Quick Definition
Fourth species deals with suspensions and syncopations using tied half notes; if the note after the tie is dissonant (4th or 7th), it must resolve down by step as a suspension.

# Formal Definition
Fourth species counterpoint focuses on suspensions and syncopations. Half notes are tied across barlines, creating rhythmic displacement. When the note after the tie is dissonant (forming a 4th or 7th against the cantus firmus), it is a suspension and must resolve downward by step. When the note after the tie is consonant, it is a syncopation and may be left by step or leap in any direction.

# Rules/Procedures
1. **Begin** with a half rest, then half note an octave above the c.f.
2. **Each measure** (except penultimate): Half note on beat 3 ties to half note on beat 1 of next measure
3. **End** on a whole note at the octave
4. **Penultimate measure**: Must contain a 7-6 suspension
5. **Beat 3**: Always consonant (preparation)
6. **After the tie (beat 1)**:
   - **Dissonant (4 or 7)**: It's a suspension - resolve DOWN by step
   - **Consonant (1, 3, 5, 6, 8)**: It's a syncopation - leave any way
7. **Allowed suspensions in upper voice**: 4-3 and 7-6
   - 9-8 and 2-1 are NOT allowed in this style
8. All suspensions and syncopations should be labeled in analysis

# Musical Context
Fourth species isolates the suspension figure, one of the most important expressive devices in Renaissance and Baroque counterpoint. Suspensions create tension through rhythmic displacement of dissonance.

# Examples
- 7-6 suspension: E tied across barline against C.F. D, resolves to D (7-6-5 if continuing)
- 4-3 suspension: F tied against C.F. C, resolves to E
- Syncopation: G tied against C.F. C (consonant 5th), may leap to any note

# Common Confusions
- Using 9-8 or 2-1 suspensions (not allowed in this style)
- Resolving suspensions upward (must resolve down)
- Treating all tied notes as suspensions (consonant ties are syncopations)
- Forgetting the required 7-6 suspension in the penultimate measure
- Starting on beat 1 instead of with a half rest
