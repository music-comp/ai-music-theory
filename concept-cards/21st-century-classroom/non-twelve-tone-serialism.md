# Quick Definition
Non-twelve-tone serialism uses series of fewer than twelve pitch classes while still employing serial transformations like inversion, retrograde, and transposition.

# Formal Definition
Non-twelve-tone serialism refers to serial techniques applied to pitch series containing fewer than all twelve pitch classes. These shorter series still maintain fixed order and employ transformations (P, R, I, RI), but pitch classes may repeat before all twelve appear. This approach allows serial organization while permitting tonal references or other pitch emphases impossible in strict twelve-tone technique.

# Construction/Calculation
Characteristics of non-twelve-tone serialism:
1. Series contains fewer than 12 pitch classes
2. Some pitch classes may repeat within the series
3. Certain pitch classes are emphasized by repetition
4. Standard serial transformations still apply (P, R, I, RI)
5. Can imply tonal centers or modes
6. Not all compositions using the technique are strictly serial throughout

# Musical Context
Stravinsky adopted serial techniques in his later works but often used series shorter than twelve notes. His Septet (1953) uses a 16-note series containing only 8 distinct pitch classes, with A, G, and G# each appearing three times. This approach allowed Stravinsky to maintain his characteristic sound while engaging with serial procedures.

# Examples
- Stravinsky, Septet (1953): 16-note series with 8 distinct pitch classes
- Series contains repeated pitches: B, A, G, F#, G# appear multiple times
- The series implies E or A as potential tonal centers
- Rotation techniques applied to serial music (as in Stravinsky's later works)

# Common Confusions
- "Serial" does not require all 12 pitch classes
- Non-twelve-tone series can still generate many row forms
- Repetitions within the series are compositionally significant
- The technique represents a middle ground between strict serialism and free atonality
