# Quick Definition
Non-twelve-tone serialism uses series of fewer than twelve pitch classes while still employing serial transformations (P, R, I, RI).

# Core Definition
Serialism also includes music that is not twelve-tone but uses a series of notes maintaining its order and employing inversion, retrograde, and retrograde inversion. Stravinsky's Septet (1953) uses a 16-note series containing only 8 distinct pitch classes, with some pitches repeated multiple times. Such a series can imply tonal centers (E or A in the Septet). The work is not strictly serial but contains serial elements (Hutchinson, Ch. 34, pp. 497-498).

# Prerequisites
- **Serialism** -- Non-twelve-tone is a variant of serialism
- **Twelve-tone technique** -- Must understand what is being modified

# Key Properties
1. Series contains fewer than 12 distinct pitch classes
2. Some pitch classes may repeat within the series
3. Standard transformations (P, R, I, RI) still apply
4. Can imply tonal centers or modes
5. Not necessarily strictly serial throughout

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the series (may have repeated pitch classes)
- Note which pitch classes are emphasized by repetition
- Check for serial transformations
- Look for tonal implications

# Examples
- Stravinsky, Septet (1953) (p. 498, Figures 34.6.1-34.6.4): 16-note series with 8 distinct pitch classes
- Repeated pitches: B, A, G, F#, G# appear multiple times
- Normal form of the 8 pitches implies E or A as tonal center

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Dismissing a work as "not serial" because it uses fewer than 12 pitches
  **Correction**: Serial techniques can apply to series of any length

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking non-twelve-tone serialism is the same as free atonality
  **Clarification**: It maintains ordered series and transformations, unlike free atonal writing
