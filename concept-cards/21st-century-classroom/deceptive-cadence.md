# Quick Definition
A deceptive cadence (DC) is a phrase ending where V resolves to vi (or another non-tonic chord) instead of the expected I; more broadly, "V to not-I."

# Core Definition
A deceptive cadence (DC) occurs when a phrase ends with the chords V-vi. A deceptive cadence means V did not go to I. This means that "V to not-I" is technically a more correct description for a deceptive cadence than V-vi, which is the most common realization. The term "deceptive" reflects that the listener expects V to resolve to I but is "deceived" by the unexpected resolution. V can go to IV/3rd, IV, or other chords and still be deceptive.

# Prerequisites
- **Cadence**: The general concept of harmonic arrival
- **Authentic cadence**: The expected V-I that the DC subverts

# Key Properties
1. V-vi is the most common form
2. More broadly: V to "not-I" at a phrase ending
3. Creates surprise and delays expected tonic arrival
4. The leading tone often still resolves to ^1 even in the deceptive resolution
5. Can involve V going to IV/3rd or other unexpected chords
6. Prolongs the phrase by delaying closure

# Construction / Recognition
- Last chords of phrase: V followed by something other than I
- V-vi is the most common realization
- Creates a surprise that extends the phrase
- Look for the dominant chord resolving unexpectedly

# Context & Application
Deceptive cadences create surprise and extend phrases by delaying the expected tonic arrival. They are used for dramatic effect. The V-vi progression in the Prelude to Act I of Wagner's Tristan und Isolde is "arguably one of the most famous deceptive cadences in the history of classical music." Mozart's Ave Verum Corpus features V going to IV/3rd as a non-standard deceptive cadence.

# Examples
- G-Am in C major = V-vi (most common deceptive cadence)
- Mozart, "Voi che sapete" from The Marriage of Figaro (deceptive cadence)
- Wagner, Tristan und Isolde, Prelude to Act I (famous deceptive cadence)
- Kelly and Steinberg, "True Colors" (deceptive cadence)
- Mozart, Ave Verum Corpus: V to IV/3rd (non-standard deceptive)

# Common Errors
- Assuming DC must always be V-vi (V to any "not-I" is deceptive)
- Confusing deceptive cadence with half cadence

# Common Confusions
- **V-vi is most common but not the only form**: V to any "not-I" at a phrase ending is deceptive
- **The "deception" is harmonic**: The leading tone (^7) can still resolve to ^1 even in the deceptive chord
- **Delays, not avoids**: A deceptive cadence delays the eventual tonic arrival; the music typically resolves to I soon after
