# Quick Definition
Middle C (C4) is the C located near the center of the piano keyboard, serving as the primary reference point between treble and bass clefs.

# Core Definition
The note C4 is known as "middle C" and is an important reference point. In treble clef, middle C is notated on the ledger line below the staff. In bass clef, middle C is notated on the ledger line above the staff. The other two commonly used clefs (alto and tenor) each use a C clef that designates the placement of middle C on the staff itself. Middle C is always clearly notated in either the upper or lower staff of the grand staff and never floats between the two staves.

# Prerequisites
- **Octave registers**: The numbering system for identifying specific octaves
- **Staff**: The five-line framework for notation

# Key Properties
1. Designated as C4 in octave register notation
2. Located approximately in the center of the piano keyboard
3. Boundary point between treble and bass clef territories
4. In treble clef: first ledger line below the staff
5. In bass clef: first ledger line above the staff
6. In alto clef: middle line of the staff
7. In tenor clef: fourth line from the bottom

# Construction / Recognition
- On piano: the C closest to the center of the keyboard
- In treble clef: ledger line below the staff
- In bass clef: ledger line above the staff
- In alto clef: the middle line
- In tenor clef: the fourth line

# Context & Application
Middle C is the universal reference point for pitch in Western music. It connects the treble and bass clef systems and is essential for reading the grand staff used in piano music. Every clef is defined in part by its relationship to middle C.

# Examples
- On a standard 88-key piano, middle C is the 4th C from the left
- In treble clef, middle C is one ledger line below the staff
- In bass clef, middle C is one ledger line above the staff
- Middle C never "floats" between staves on the grand staff

# Common Errors
- Placing middle C between the two staves of the grand staff rather than clearly in one or the other
- Confusing the location of middle C across different clefs

# Common Confusions
- **Grand staff notation**: Middle C is always clearly notated in either the upper or lower staff, never floating between them
- **Same pitch, different positions**: The location of middle C differs for each clef but represents the same pitch (C4)
