# Quick Definition
Middle C (C4) is the C note located near the center of the piano keyboard, serving as the primary reference point between treble and bass clefs.

# Formal Definition
Middle C, designated as C4 in octave register notation, is the C located approximately in the middle of the piano keyboard and standard musical range. It serves as the boundary point between treble and bass clef territories and is an essential reference for reading music in any clef.

# Construction/Recognition
- On piano: the C closest to the center of the keyboard
- In treble clef: notated on the first ledger line below the staff
- In bass clef: notated on the first ledger line above the staff
- In alto clef: notated on the middle line
- In tenor clef: notated on the fourth line from the bottom

# Musical Context
Middle C is the universal reference point for pitch in Western music. It connects the treble and bass clef systems and is essential for reading the grand staff used in piano music.

# Examples
- On a standard 88-key piano, middle C is the 4th C from the left
- In treble clef, middle C is one ledger line below the staff
- In bass clef, middle C is one ledger line above the staff
- Middle C never "floats" between staves on the grand staff

# Common Confusions
- Middle C is always clearly notated in either the upper or lower staff of the grand staff, never floating between them
- The location of middle C differs for each clef but represents the same pitch
- Middle C (C4) is not the same as "the middle of the C notes on the piano"
