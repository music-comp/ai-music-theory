# Quick Definition
Pandiatonicism uses all notes of a diatonic scale freely without traditional harmonic function or voice-leading conventions.

# Core Definition
Pandiatonicism refers to the use of all diatonic notes without the need for scale degrees or harmonies to progress or function tonally (V does not need to progress to I, scale degree 7 does not need to resolve to 8, etc.). It often manifests as a "wash" of notes from the major scale, or as chords made of non-traditional combinations of diatonic notes, often with at least one interval of a 2nd in chord voicings (Hutchinson, Ch. 32, p. 470).

# Prerequisites
- **Scales and modes** -- Pandiatonicism uses diatonic collections

# Key Properties
1. All seven diatonic notes used freely
2. No functional harmonic progressions required
3. No voice-leading resolutions required
4. Intervals of 2nds common in chord voicings
5. Creates a "wash" of diatonic sound
6. Non-traditional chord combinations

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify a diatonic collection
- Observe absence of functional progression (V does not resolve to I)
- Note freely combined scale degrees forming non-traditional chords
- Look for seconds in chord voicings

# Context & Application
Pandiatonicism emerged in early 20th-century music. Stravinsky and Copland are particularly associated with it. The "Americana" sound of Copland often derives from pandiatonic writing. It creates consonance (all diatonic notes) combined with harmonic stasis.

# Examples
- Stravinsky, *Petrushka*, Fourth Tableau (p. 470, Figure 32.2.1): Wash of major-scale notes
- Copland, *Appalachian Spring* (p. 470, Figure 32.2.2): Non-traditional pandiatonic chord shapes
- Copland, *Appalachian Spring* (p. 471, Figure 32.2.3): Diatonic wash with dissonant counterpoint

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Analyzing pandiatonic passages with functional Roman numerals
  **Correction**: Pandiatonicism abandons functional progressions; describe the diatonic collection and registral distribution instead

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking pandiatonicism is the same as atonality
  **Clarification**: Pandiatonic music uses a single diatonic collection (not chromatic); it is consonant but non-functional
- **Confusion**: Thinking dissonances in pandiatonic music are "errors"
  **Clarification**: 2nds and 7ths are allowed freely without resolution
