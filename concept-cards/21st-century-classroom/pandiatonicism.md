# Quick Definition
Pandiatonicism uses all notes of a diatonic scale freely without the need for traditional harmonic function or voice-leading conventions.

# Formal Definition
Pandiatonicism refers to the use of all diatonic notes (typically from a major scale) without requiring scale degrees or harmonies to progress or function tonally. The dominant need not resolve to the tonic; the leading tone need not resolve to the tonic note. It often manifests as a "wash" of notes from the major scale, or as chords made of non-traditional combinations of diatonic notes, frequently including at least one interval of a second in chord voicings.

# Construction/Calculation
Pandiatonic techniques:
1. Use all seven notes of a diatonic collection freely
2. Abandon functional harmonic progressions (V need not go to I)
3. Abandon melodic voice-leading conventions (scale degree 7 need not resolve to 8)
4. Create chords from non-traditional note combinations
5. Include seconds/ninths in chord voicings for characteristic color
6. Create washes of diatonic sound without functional direction

# Musical Context
Pandiatonicism emerged in the early 20th century as composers sought alternatives to chromatic Romanticism while avoiding atonality. Stravinsky and Copland are particularly associated with the technique. It creates a sense of consonance (all notes are diatonic) combined with harmonic stasis (no functional progression). The "Americana" sound of Copland often derives from pandiatonic writing.

# Examples
- Stravinsky, Petrushka, Fourth Tableau: Wash of notes from major scale
- Copland, Appalachian Spring: Non-traditional pandiatonic chord shapes in non-functional progression
- Copland, Appalachian Spring: Wash of diatonic sound regardless of dissonant intervals in counterpoint
- Characteristic "open" American sound

# Common Confusions
- Pandiatonicism is NOT the same as atonality - all notes come from one diatonic collection
- It is NOT traditional tonal harmony - functional progressions are abandoned
- Dissonances (2nds, 7ths) are allowed to occur freely without resolution
- The technique creates a "white-key" sound even when transposed to other keys
