# Quick Definition
The bridge in a fugue is a brief modulatory passage that occurs within the first exposition, typically connecting the answer to the subsequent subject entry.

# Formal Definition
The bridge (sometimes called "link" or "codetta") is a short passage that occurs only within the first exposition of a fugue. Its purpose is to modulate from the dominant key (established by the answer) back to the tonic key for the next subject entry. The bridge often contains motivic fragmentation of the subject. Not all fugues have bridges - they appear only when the modulation back to tonic requires a transitional passage.

# Rules/Procedures
1. The bridge occurs ONLY within the first exposition
2. It connects the answer (dominant key) to the next subject (tonic key)
3. It modulates: typically from V back to I
4. Material often derived from the subject (especially subject head)
5. Bridge is typically brief - a few beats to a measure or two
6. Not all fugues have a bridge; it depends on the subject and harmonic plan

# Musical Context
The bridge smooths the return to tonic after the answer's dominant emphasis. In Bach's Fugue 2 in C minor, the bridge in m.5 uses the subject head while modulating from G minor back to C minor for the third entry.

# Examples
- Bach, Fugue 2 in C minor, m.5: Bridge contains subject head fragment
- The bridge appears between m.3 answer and m.7 subject entry

# Common Confusions
- Looking for a bridge outside the first exposition (bridges only occur in the first exposition)
- Confusing the bridge with an episode (episodes are between expositions; bridges are within the first exposition)
- Thinking all fugues have bridges (not all do)
- Not recognizing that bridge material derives from the subject
