# Quick Definition
The answer is the statement of the fugue subject in the dominant key by the second voice to enter; it may be "real" (exact transposition) or "tonal" (with altered intervals to begin in tonic before modulating).

# Formal Definition
The answer (sometimes called "response") is the second voice's statement of the fugue subject, transposed to the key of the dominant. A "real answer" transposes all intervals exactly to the dominant. A "tonal answer" alters certain intervals, typically at the beginning, to start on the tonic pitch before modulating to the dominant. Tonal answers are used when the subject begins with a prominent 1-5 or 5-1 motion; these intervals are answered with 5-1 or 1-5 respectively.

# Rules/Procedures
1. **Real answer**: Exact transposition of subject to dominant key
   - All intervals preserved
   - Used when subject does not prominently feature 1-5 or 5-1 motions
2. **Tonal answer**: Intervals altered to accommodate tonic-dominant relationship
   - Subject's 1-5 answered by 5-1
   - Subject's 5-1 answered by 1-5
   - Used when subject begins with prominent 1-5 or 5-1
3. The answer appears when the second voice enters
4. In the first exposition, answer entries alternate with subject entries
5. An answer is considered equivalent to a subject for formal analysis

# Musical Context
The tonal answer developed as a convention to smooth the tonic-dominant relationship at the beginning of fugues. If a subject leaps prominently from tonic to dominant, an exact transposition would create a jarring motion from dominant to supertonic; the tonal answer corrects this.

# Examples
- Bach, Fugue 2 in C minor: Subject descends a P4 (note 3 to note 4); answer descends a P5
  - This makes the answer "tonal" because the interval was altered
- Real answer: If subject is C-D-E-F-G, answer would be G-A-B-C-D (exact intervals)

# Common Confusions
- Thinking all answers are exact transpositions (tonal answers have alterations)
- Not recognizing when an answer is tonal vs. real
- Confusing answer with countersubject (answer IS the subject transposed)
- Thinking a tonal answer is "wrong" (it's a deliberate compositional choice)
