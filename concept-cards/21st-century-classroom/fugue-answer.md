# Quick Definition
The answer is the statement of the fugue subject in the dominant key by the second voice to enter; it may be "real" (exact transposition) or "tonal" (with altered intervals).

# Core Definition
The answer, called "response" in some texts, refers to the statement of the subject in the key of the dominant by the second voice to enter in a fugue. When the intervals are an exact transposition, it is called a "real answer." When intervals are altered -- typically to start in the tonic before modulating to the dominant -- it is called a "tonal answer." An answer is considered equivalent to a subject for formal analysis because the intervallic alterations are minimal (Hutchinson, Ch. 30, pp. 433-434).

# Prerequisites
- **Fugue subject** -- The answer is a transposition of the subject

# Key Properties
1. Subject transposed to the key of the dominant
2. Stated by the second voice to enter
3. Real answer: exact transposition of all intervals
4. Tonal answer: certain intervals altered (typically first few notes)
5. Considered equivalent to a subject for formal purposes
6. In the first exposition, answer entries alternate with subject entries

# Construction / Recognition
**Identifying the answer:**
1. Find the second voice entry after the subject
2. Compare intervals to the original subject
3. If all intervals match: real answer
4. If some intervals differ (typically at the beginning): tonal answer

**Tonal answer alterations:**
- Subject's scale degree 1-5 motion answered by 5-1
- Subject's 5-1 motion answered by 1-5
- Typically only the first few notes are altered

# Context & Application
The answer establishes the tonic-dominant polarity that is fundamental to fugal structure. The distinction between real and tonal answers reflects the composer's sensitivity to tonal implications. Tonal answers prevent awkward emphasis on the supertonic when subjects prominently feature the tonic-dominant interval.

# Examples
- **Bach, Fugue 2 in C minor** (p. 434): Subject's third note descends a P4 to the fourth note; the answer descends a P5 instead, making this a tonal answer
- Real answer: if subject is C-D-E-F-G, answer is G-A-B-C-D (exact intervals)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Not recognizing a tonal answer (thinking the different interval is a mistake)
  **Correction**: Compare the first few intervals carefully; a tonal answer deliberately alters the 1-5 / 5-1 relationship

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing the answer with the countersubject
  **Clarification**: The answer IS the subject transposed; the countersubject is different material accompanying it
- **Confusion**: Thinking a tonal answer is "wrong"
  **Clarification**: Tonal answers are a deliberate compositional convention, not errors
