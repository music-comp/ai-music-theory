# Quick Definition
A fully diminished seventh chord consists of a diminished triad with a diminished seventh above the root, built entirely of stacked minor thirds.

# Formal Definition
A fully diminished seventh chord (often just called "diminished seventh") is built with a diminished triad (m3 + m3) plus a diminished seventh interval from root to seventh. All intervals are minor thirds: m3 + m3 + m3. This symmetric construction means the chord divides the octave into four equal parts and can resolve to multiple keys.

# Construction/Recognition
- Diminished triad + diminished 7th from root
- Intervals: m3 + m3 + m3 (all minor thirds)
- Total: 9 half steps from root to 7th
- Lead-sheet: Co7, Cdim7
- Roman numeral: viio7 (in minor with raised ^7)

# Musical Context
The fully diminished seventh chord is highly unstable and wants to resolve. It occurs naturally as viio7 in minor keys (built on raised ^7). Due to its symmetry, the same chord can be spelled four different ways and resolve to four different keys. It is common in Romantic-era music for its dramatic quality.

# Examples
- Bo7: B-D-F-Ab (in C minor: viio7)
- Co7: C-Eb-Gb-Bbb (enharmonic: A)
- Any note can be respelled as root (symmetrical chord)
- The enharmonic dim7 on Ab is Ab-Cb-Ebb-Gbb = same notes as Bo7

# Common Confusions
- The double-flat seventh (Bbb = A) looks strange but is correct
- All four inversions sound identical due to symmetry
- "Fully" or just "diminished seventh" (vs. "half-diminished")
