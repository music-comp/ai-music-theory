# Quick Definition
Minimalism is a compositional approach emerging in the 1960s characterized by extreme repetition, gradual processes, steady pulse, and limited harmonic and melodic material.

# Formal Definition
Minimalism is a musical style that arose in the 1960s as a reaction against the complexity of serialism and the indeterminacy of chance music. Key characteristics include: repetition of short musical patterns, gradual transformation processes, steady pulse, diatonic or limited pitch collections, and extended duration. The four primary minimalist composers are La Monte Young, Terry Riley, Philip Glass, and Steve Reich.

# Construction/Calculation
Characteristics of minimalism:
1. Repetition of short patterns (cells, modules)
2. Gradual, perceptible processes of change
3. Steady, motoric pulse
4. Diatonic or restricted pitch materials
5. Consonant harmonies (compared to serialism)
6. Extended time scale
7. Specific techniques: additive process, phase shifting, rotation

# Musical Context
Minimalism emerged in New York and California in the 1960s, influenced by non-Western music, jazz, and rock. It represented a radical departure from the academic complexity of post-war modernism. The style has profoundly influenced film music, popular music, and contemporary classical composition.

# Examples
- La Monte Young, The Well-Tuned Piano (1964-)
- Terry Riley, In C (1964)
- Philip Glass, Two Pages (1969), Einstein on the Beach (1976)
- Steve Reich, Piano Phase (1967), Clapping Music (1972)

# Common Confusions
- "Minimal" doesn't mean simple or easy - the processes can be intricate
- Not all repetitive music is minimalist
- Each minimalist composer has distinct techniques
- Post-minimalism and minimalism are related but different
