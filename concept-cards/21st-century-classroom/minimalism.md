# Quick Definition
Minimalism is a 1960s compositional approach characterized by extreme repetition, gradual processes, and limited material, associated with La Monte Young, Terry Riley, Philip Glass, and Steve Reich.

# Core Definition
The rise of minimalism in the 1960s was a significant development in art music. Four composers are typically associated with minimalism: La Monte Young (b. 1935), Terry Riley (b. 1935), Philip Glass (b. 1937), and Steve Reich (b. 1936). The textbook focuses on two techniques: (1) additive minimalism, associated with Philip Glass, and (2) phase shifting, associated with Steve Reich (Hutchinson, Ch. 35, p. 501).

# Prerequisites
This is a foundational concept for 20th-century music study with no prerequisites within this source.

# Key Properties
1. Emerged in the 1960s
2. Four primary composers: Young, Riley, Glass, Reich
3. Extreme repetition of short patterns
4. Gradual, perceptible processes of change
5. Steady pulse
6. Limited pitch/harmonic material
7. Two main techniques: additive process (Glass) and phase shifting (Reich)

# Construction / Recognition
**Identifying minimalist music:**
1. Extensive repetition of short patterns
2. Gradual transformation audible to the listener
3. Steady, motoric pulse
4. Diatonic or limited pitch collections
5. Extended duration

# Context & Application
Minimalism represented a radical departure from post-war academic complexity (serialism). It profoundly influenced film music, popular music, and contemporary classical composition.

# Examples
- La Monte Young, *The Well-Tuned Piano* (1964-)
- Terry Riley, *In C* (1964)
- Philip Glass, *Two Pages* (1969), *Einstein on the Beach* (1976)
- Steve Reich, *Piano Phase* (1967), *Clapping Music* (1972)

# Relationships
## Related
- **Additive process** -- Glass's technique
- **Phase shifting** -- Reich's technique
- **Rotation** -- Underlies phase shifting
- **Process music** -- Aesthetic framework for minimalism
## Contrasts With
- **Serialism** -- Opposite aesthetic: repetition vs. non-repetition, consonance vs. dissonance

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking minimalist music is simple to compose or perform
  **Correction**: The processes can be intricate and performances demanding

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking all repetitive music is minimalist
  **Clarification**: Minimalism specifically uses systematic processes with perceptible gradual change
- **Confusion**: Treating all four composers as interchangeable
  **Clarification**: Each has distinct techniques (Glass: additive; Reich: phase; Riley: cells; Young: drones)
