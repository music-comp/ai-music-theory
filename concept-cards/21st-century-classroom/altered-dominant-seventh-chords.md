# Quick Definition
Altered dominant seventh chords have either or both the 5th and 9th chromatically raised or lowered, creating tension that intensifies the resolution to tonic.

# Core Definition
A dominant seventh chord is considered "altered" when either or both the 5th or 9th are chromatically raised or lowered. The root, 3rd, and 7th remain unchanged. Possible alterations: #5, b5, #9, b9, or combinations. The chord symbol "G7alt" indicates an altered dominant but is vague about which specific alterations are used. Altered dominants are typically used when the chord has dominant function (root resolving down by a fifth); unaltered dominants are often used when they have tonic function (e.g., the first chord in a blues) (Hutchinson, Ch. 31, pp. 448-449).

# Prerequisites
- **Jazz ninth chords** -- Altered chords modify the 9th

# Key Properties
1. Root, 3rd, and 7th remain unchanged
2. 5th may be raised (#5) or lowered (b5)
3. 9th may be raised (#9) or lowered (b9)
4. Both 5th and 9th may be altered simultaneously
5. #9 often spelled as b10 for ease of reading (appears as both major and minor 3rd)
6. b13 is enharmonic equivalent of #5; b5 is preferred to b13 in this text
7. Enharmonic respelling is common for readability

# Construction / Recognition
**Building an altered dominant:**
1. Start with dominant 7th (root, M3, P5, m7)
2. Raise or lower the 5th: #5 or b5
3. Raise or lower the 9th: #9 or b9
4. Both alterations may occur simultaneously

**When to use altered dominants:**
- When chord has dominant function (root resolving down by 5th)
- Unaltered dominants preferred for tonic function (I7 in blues)

# Context & Application
Altered dominants are fundamental to bebop and modern jazz harmony. They create maximum tension before resolution and provide chromatic voice leading. The corresponding scale for C7alt is the diminished-whole tone (or altered) scale.

# Examples
- C7(#5) = C-E-G#-Bb (p. 449)
- C7(b9) = C-E-G-Bb-Db
- C7(#9) = C-E-G-Bb-D# (often spelled C-E-G-Bb-Eb)
- C7(#9/#5) = both alterations simultaneously
- G7alt in ii-V-i: Dm7(b5)-G7alt-Cm6/9

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Altering the root, 3rd, or 7th
  **Correction**: Only the 5th and 9th are altered; root, 3rd, and 7th are fixed

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking #9 means the chord has both major and minor 3rds
  **Clarification**: The #9 (spelled as b10 for reading ease) looks like a minor 3rd but functions as the raised 9th
- **Confusion**: Confusing b5 with #11
  **Clarification**: #11 implies the natural 5th is also present; b5 does not
