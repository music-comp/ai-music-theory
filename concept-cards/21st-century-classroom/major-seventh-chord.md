# Quick Definition
A major seventh chord consists of a major triad with a major seventh above the root, creating a warm, jazzy sound.

# Formal Definition
A major seventh chord is built with a major triad (M3 + m3) plus a major seventh interval from root to seventh. The intervals stacked are: M3 (root to 3rd), m3 (3rd to 5th), M3 (5th to 7th). It occurs diatonically on ^1 and ^4 in major keys.

# Construction/Recognition
- Major triad + major 7th from root
- Intervals: M3 + m3 + M3
- Total: 11 half steps from root to 7th
- Lead-sheet: Cmaj7, CM7, C triangle-7
- Roman numeral: IM7, IVM7

# Musical Context
The major seventh chord has a lush, sophisticated sound common in jazz, bossa nova, and pop ballads. It occurs naturally on the tonic (I) and subdominant (IV) in major keys. It is less dissonant and more stable than the dominant seventh.

# Examples
- Cmaj7: C-E-G-B (C major triad + B)
- Fmaj7: F-A-C-E
- In C major: Cmaj7 = IM7, Fmaj7 = IVM7
- Common in jazz standards and pop ballads

# Common Confusions
- Cmaj7 has a MAJOR seventh (B natural), not a minor seventh
- The "maj" refers to the seventh interval, not the triad (which is already major)
- Different from C7 (dominant seventh, which has Bb)
