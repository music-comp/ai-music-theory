# Quick Definition
A major scale is a seven-note scale following the pattern of whole and half steps: W-W-H-W-W-W-H, creating the familiar "do-re-mi" sound.

# Formal Definition
The major scale consists of seven notes following a specific succession of whole and half steps. It can be understood as two tetrachords (four-note segments) each following the pattern W-W-H, connected by a whole step. The complete pattern is W-W-H-W-W-W-H. All major scales use consecutive letter names without skipping or repeating any note name.

# Construction/Recognition
- Pattern: W-W-H-W-W-W-H (Whole-Whole-Half-Whole-Whole-Whole-Half)
- Lower tetrachord: W-W-H
- Whole step connection between tetrachords
- Upper tetrachord: W-W-H
- Each letter name appears exactly once

# Musical Context
The major scale is the foundation of Western tonal music. It establishes the tonic key and provides the basis for diatonic harmony. Its characteristic sound is often described as "happy" or "bright."

# Examples
- C major scale: C-D-E-F-G-A-B-C (all white keys)
- G major scale: G-A-B-C-D-E-F#-G
- D major scale: D-E-F#-G-A-B-C#-D
- The half steps always occur between ^3-^4 and ^7-^8

# Common Confusions
- All letter names must be used in order (D-E-F#-G, not D-E-Gb-G)
- Half steps occur only between ^3-^4 and ^7-^8
- "Major" refers to the quality of the scale, not the size
