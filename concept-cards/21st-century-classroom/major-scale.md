# Quick Definition
A major scale is a seven-note scale built from two identical tetrachords (W-W-H) connected by a whole step, producing the complete pattern W-W-H-W-W-W-H.

# Core Definition
A major scale contains a specific succession of whole and half steps. It is helpful to think of the pattern as consisting of two tetrachords. The lower tetrachord consists of the pattern whole step, whole step, half step. A whole step joins the lower tetrachord to the upper tetrachord. The upper tetrachord duplicates the pattern in the lower one: whole step, whole step, half step. The complete pattern is W-W-H-W-W-W-H. All major scales use the notes of the musical alphabet in order; no notes are skipped and no notes occur twice.

# Prerequisites
- **Half step**: Needed to understand the H positions in the pattern
- **Whole step**: Needed to understand the W positions in the pattern

# Key Properties
1. Pattern: W-W-H-W-W-W-H
2. Two identical tetrachords (W-W-H) connected by a whole step
3. Half steps occur only between ^3-^4 and ^7-^8
4. Each letter name appears exactly once (no skipping, no repeating)
5. Seven distinct notes plus the octave

# Construction / Recognition
1. Start on any note (the tonic)
2. Lower tetrachord: W-W-H (^1-^2-^3-^4)
3. Whole step connection between ^4 and ^5
4. Upper tetrachord: W-W-H (^5-^6-^7-^8)
5. Verify each letter name appears exactly once

# Context & Application
The major scale is the foundation of Western tonal music. It establishes the tonic key and provides the basis for diatonic harmony, key signatures, and chord construction. Its characteristic sound is often described as "bright" or "happy."

# Examples
- C major: C-D-E-F-G-A-B-C (all white keys)
- G major: G-A-B-C-D-E-F#-G
- D major: D-E-F#-G-A-B-C#-D (the source's primary example)
- Incorrect spelling: D-E-Gb-G has G appearing twice and skips F

# Common Errors
- Using the wrong enharmonic spelling (e.g., Gb instead of F# in D major, causing G to appear twice)
- Confusing the location of half steps (they occur only between ^3-^4 and ^7-^8)

# Common Confusions
- **Correct spelling**: All letter names must be used in order -- D-E-F#-G, not D-E-Gb-G
- **Half step placement**: Half steps occur only between ^3-^4 and ^7-^8 in major
- **"Major" as quality**: Refers to the scale type, not size
