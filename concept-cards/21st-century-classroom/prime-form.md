# Quick Definition
Prime form is the most reduced representation of a pitch-class set, transposed to start on 0 and inverted if necessary for maximum compactness; written in parentheses without commas.

# Core Definition
Prime form is usually a transposition and possibly an inversion of the normal form to its most essential form, much as an Eb major triad in second inversion belongs to the category "major triad." All prime forms are transposed to start on C (pitch integer 0). Written in parentheses without commas: (014). Use T for 10 and E for 11 (Hutchinson, Ch. 33, pp. 480-482).

# Prerequisites
- **Normal form** -- Prime form is calculated from normal form

# Key Properties
1. Always starts on 0
2. Most compact form (comparing original and inverted)
3. Written in parentheses without commas: (014)
4. Use T for 10, E for 11: (013568T)
5. Categorizes sets into equivalence classes (set classes)
6. 208 distinct prime forms for 3-9 note sets

# Construction / Recognition
**Steps (Rahn method):**
1. Transpose normal form to start on 0
2. Invert the transposed form (intervals go opposite direction)
3. Put inverted form in ascending order
4. If ties existed in normal form, recheck inverted orderings
5. Compare transposed and inverted: most compact is prime form
6. Write in parentheses without commas

**Example:** Normal form [11, 2, 3]
- Transpose to 0: [0, 3, 4]
- Invert: [0, 8, 9] -> reorder: [0, 1, 4]
- Compare: [0, 3, 4] vs [0, 1, 4] -> (014) is more compact

# Context & Application
Prime form reveals deep structural relationships. Two passages using different pitches may share the same prime form, indicating structural similarity.

# Examples
- {3, 11, 2}: normal [11, 2, 3] -> prime (014) (p. 481)
- {8, 0, 9}: normal [8, 9, 0] -> prime (014) -- same as above! (p. 482)
- All major and minor triads share prime form (037)
- (0148) requires full comparison of transposed and inverted versions (p. 480)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Using commas in prime form notation
  **Correction**: Prime form uses no commas: (014) not (0, 1, 4)

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking prime form preserves the original pitches
  **Clarification**: Prime form is an abstraction; it transposes to 0 and may invert
- **Confusion**: Not realizing major and minor triads share the same prime form
  **Clarification**: Inversion relates major to minor; both are (037)
