# Quick Definition
Pitch refers to the highness or lowness of a musical note; on a piano, pitches sound higher moving to the right and lower moving to the left.

# Core Definition
Pitch has to do with notes. On the piano there are 88 notes. As you move to the right on a piano keyboard, the notes sound higher; notes sound lower as you move to the left. Pitch is the fundamental perceptual property that allows notes to be ordered from low to high.

(none -- this is the first concept introduced)

# Key Properties
1. Pitch describes the highness or lowness of a sound
2. The piano keyboard has 88 distinct pitches
3. Higher position on the keyboard or staff corresponds to higher pitch
4. The seven letter names A-B-C-D-E-F-G (the musical alphabet) are the basic pitch names

# Construction / Recognition
- Higher placement on a staff = higher pitch
- Moving right on a piano keyboard = higher pitch
- Moving left on a piano keyboard = lower pitch
- The musical alphabet (A-B-C-D-E-F-G) repeats across registers

# Context & Application
Pitch is the fundamental building block of melody and harmony. Every note in music has a specific pitch, and the relationships between pitches create the intervals, scales, and chords that form the basis of music theory.

# Examples
- Moving right on a piano keyboard produces higher pitches
- Moving left produces lower pitches
- The musical alphabet A-B-C-D-E-F-G represents seven basic pitch classes

# Common Errors
- Confusing pitch (high/low) with dynamics (loud/soft)
- Confusing pitch class (e.g., C) with a specific pitch (e.g., C4)

# Common Confusions
- **Pitch vs. volume**: Pitch refers to high/low, not soft/loud
- **Pitch class vs. specific pitch**: C is a pitch class; C4 is a specific pitch in a specific octave register
