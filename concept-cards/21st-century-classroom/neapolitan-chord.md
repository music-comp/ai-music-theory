# Quick Definition
The Neapolitan chord is a major triad built on the lowered second scale degree (flat-II), most commonly found in first inversion, functioning as a chromatic pre-dominant chord.

# Formal Definition
The Neapolitan chord is labeled "N" instead of flat-II in classical music analysis. Being a chromatically altered ii chord, the Neapolitan has pre-dominant harmonic function. When it occurs in its most common inversion (first inversion), it is labeled N6. The chord consists of flat-2, 4, and flat-6 (in root position) and contains scale degree 4 in the bass when in first inversion.

# Construction/Recognition
- Major triad built on lowered 2nd scale degree
- Most common inversion: first inversion (N6) with scale degree 4 in bass
- In C major/minor: Db-F-Ab (root position) or F-Ab-Db (first inversion)
- Use "N" label instead of flat-II in Roman numeral analysis
- Quality is always major

# Musical Context
- Pre-dominant function (replaces ii or IV)
- Often accompanied by changes in texture, register, and dynamics
- Creates a distinctive, dramatic color before dominant
- Common in both major and minor keys
- Particularly prominent in Classical and Romantic era music

# Examples
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 7, Op. 92, I (1812) - with notable texture/dynamic changes
- Beethoven, Moonlight Sonata, Op. 27, No. 2, I (1802)
- John Williams, Raiders of the Lost Ark, "Raiders March" (1981)
- Michael Giacchino, Star Trek, "End Credits" (2009)
- Hans Zimmer, Batman v Superman, "The Red Capes are Coming" (2016)

# Related Concepts
- Pre-dominant function
- Chromatic harmony
- Mode mixture

# Common Confusions
- Always use "N" label, not flat-II, in classical analysis
- The Neapolitan is almost always in first inversion (N6)
- Compare the effect of N6 with other pre-dominants (iv, ii diminished 6) to hear the distinctive color
- In minor keys, flat-6 is diatonic, but flat-2 still requires an accidental
