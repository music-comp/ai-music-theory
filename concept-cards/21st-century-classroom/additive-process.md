# Quick Definition
Additive process is a minimalist technique, associated with Philip Glass, where patterns are gradually lengthened by systematically adding or subtracting notes.

# Formal Definition
Additive process (or additive minimalism) is a compositional technique where musical patterns are incrementally extended or shortened through systematic addition or subtraction of elements. In Glass's version, a pattern is repeated with the final note subtracted, then that version is added to the original, creating growth through accumulation. The process creates gradual change that the listener can perceive and follow.

# Construction/Calculation
Glass's additive process (as in Two Pages):
1. State the original pattern
2. Repeat the pattern, subtracting the final note
3. Each subsequent repetition subtracts one more note from the previous version
4. These shortened versions accumulate, lengthening the total pattern
5. The process can work in reverse (subtractive)

Alternative additive approach (Einstein on the Beach):
1. Begin with a short rhythmic value per chord
2. Gradually add subdivisions to each chord
3. Chords grow progressively longer

# Musical Context
Philip Glass developed additive process in his early works like Two Pages (1969) and Music in Fifths (1969). The technique creates hypnotic, gradually evolving textures. In Einstein on the Beach, a five-chord progression grows as each chord receives progressively more rhythmic subdivisions.

# Examples
- Glass, Two Pages (1969): Pattern grows through subtraction-addition process
- Glass, Music in Fifths (1969): Clear display of additive process
- Glass, Einstein on the Beach, Act 1 Scene 1 "Train": Chords lengthen by adding subdivisions
- Each step in the process is audible to the listener

# Common Confusions
- "Additive" can mean adding notes OR adding repetitions
- The process is systematic, not arbitrary
- Subtractive process is the reverse technique
- Different from augmentation (proportional lengthening)
