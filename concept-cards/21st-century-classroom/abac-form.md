# Quick Definition
ABAC form is a 32-bar song structure where the A section returns after the B section but is followed by a new C section that provides a different ending, creating asymmetry.

# Formal Definition
Many popular songs from the 1930s-1950s were in 32-bar ABAC form. Like AABA, each section is typically 8 bars. The A section appears twice, but instead of returning after B as in AABA, it is followed by a contrasting C section. The form may also occur as ABAB' where B' begins like B but ends differently.

# Analysis/Application
When analyzing ABAC form:
1. Identify the returning A section
2. Note how B and C sections differ
3. Listen for whether a verse precedes the refrain
4. Each section is typically 8 bars

# Musical Context
ABAC was common in the golden age of American popular song (1930s-1950s). It offers slightly more variety than AABA while maintaining the memorable return of the A melody.

# Examples
- "White Christmas" (Irving Berlin)
- "Someday My Prince Will Come" (from Snow White)
- "When I Fall In Love"
- Hoagy Carmichael, "Star Dust" (1927) - often performed with a verse preceding the ABAC refrain

# Common Confusions
- ABAC may also appear as ABAB' (where B' is a variant of B)
- A "verse" may precede the refrain in performance
- The distinction between ABAC and ABAB' can be subtle
