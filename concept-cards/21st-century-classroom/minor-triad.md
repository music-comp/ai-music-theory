# Quick Definition
A minor triad is built with a minor third (3 half steps) on the bottom and a major third (4 half steps) on top, totaling a perfect fifth from root to fifth.

# Core Definition
A minor triad is built with a minor third (3 half steps) between the root and third, and a major third (4 half steps) between the third and fifth. The total interval from root to fifth is a perfect fifth (7 half steps). Minor triads are represented with the root in uppercase followed by a lowercase "m" (e.g., Cm, Am, F#m). In Roman numeral analysis, lowercase numerals represent minor triads (i, ii, iii, iv, vi).

# Prerequisites
- **Triad**: The three-note chord concept
- **Major/minor intervals**: Distinguishing M3 from m3

# Key Properties
1. Root to third: minor 3rd (3 half steps)
2. Third to fifth: Major 3rd (4 half steps)
3. Root to fifth: Perfect 5th (7 half steps)
4. Lead-sheet symbol: uppercase root + lowercase "m" (Cm, Am, F#m)
5. Roman numeral: lowercase (i, ii, iii, iv, vi)
6. Sound character: "dark," "sad," "serious"

# Construction / Recognition
- m3 on the bottom, M3 on top (opposite of major triad)
- Total span: P5 (same as major triad)
- Lead-sheet: letter + "m"
- In major keys: ii, iii, vi are minor triads

# Context & Application
Minor triads are essential for both minor keys (tonic chord is minor) and the ii, iii, and vi chords in major keys. They create contrast with major triads and provide the darker color palette in tonal music.

# Examples
- C minor: C-Eb-G (m3 + M3)
- A minor: A-C-E
- D minor: D-F-A
- Lead-sheet: Cm, Am, Dm, F#m

## Contrasts With
- Major triad (M3 on bottom, m3 on top)
- Diminished triad (m3 + m3, diminished fifth)
- Augmented triad (M3 + M3, augmented fifth)

# Common Errors
- Reversing the interval order (m3 is on the bottom, M3 on top)
- Using lowercase root letter in lead-sheet notation (the root is always uppercase: Cm, not cm)

# Common Confusions
- **m3 on bottom**: Minor triad = m3 + M3 (opposite of major's M3 + m3)
- **Root letter always uppercase**: Lead-sheet writes Cm, not cm (lowercase "m" is the quality indicator)
- **Same outer interval**: Both major and minor triads have a P5 from root to fifth
