# Quick Definition
A minor triad consists of a minor third from root to third, and a major third from third to fifth, totaling a perfect fifth from root to fifth.

# Formal Definition
A minor triad is built with a minor third (3 half steps) between the root and third, and a major third (4 half steps) between the third and fifth. The total interval from root to fifth is a perfect fifth (7 half steps). Minor triads sound "dark," "sad," or "serious."

# Construction/Recognition
- Root to third: minor 3rd (3 half steps)
- Third to fifth: Major 3rd (4 half steps)
- Root to fifth: Perfect 5th (7 half steps)
- Lead-sheet symbol: uppercase letter + lowercase "m" (Cm, Fm, Am)
- Roman numeral: lowercase (i, ii, iii, iv, vi)

# Musical Context
Minor triads are essential for both minor keys and the ii, iii, and vi chords in major keys. They create contrast with major triads and provide the darker color palette in tonal music.

# Examples
- C minor: C-Eb-G (m3 + M3)
- A minor: A-C-E
- D minor: D-F-A
- Lead-sheet: Cm, Am, Dm

# Common Confusions
- m3 is on the bottom, M3 on top (opposite of major triad)
- Lead-sheet uses lowercase "m" after the root (Cm, not CM or C-)
- The root is still uppercase in lead-sheet notation (Cm, not cm)
