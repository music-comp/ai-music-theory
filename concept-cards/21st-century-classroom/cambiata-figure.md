# Quick Definition
The cambiata is a specific melodic figure in third species counterpoint where the second note is dissonant, approached by step and left by leap of a third in the opposite direction.

# Formal Definition
The cambiata (also known as "nota cambiata" or "changing note") is an ornamental melodic figure permitted in third species counterpoint. It consists of four notes with a specific contour: the first note is consonant, the second note is dissonant (approached by step), the third note is consonant (approached by leap of a third in the opposite direction), and the fourth note continues in the original direction. Only the second note may be dissonant.

# Rules/Procedures
1. The figure consists of four notes in third species
2. **First note**: Consonant with the cantus firmus
3. **Second note**: May be dissonant; approached by step from first note
4. **Third note**: Consonant; leap of a third in opposite direction from the step
5. **Fourth note**: Step in original direction (the direction of the initial step)
6. Only use cambiata where the second note is the only dissonance
7. Common in concluding formulas: 2-1-6-7-8 (where 1 is dissonant against 2 in the c.f.)

# Musical Context
The cambiata is a characteristic Renaissance melodic ornament. It provides variety in third species while maintaining controlled dissonance treatment. The figure appears frequently in Palestrina and other 16th-century composers.

# Examples
- In C (c.f. has D): Counterpoint D-C-A-B where:
  - D is consonant (octave)
  - C is dissonant (7th against D)
  - A is consonant (5th against D) - leap down a 3rd
  - B continues upward
- Concluding cambiata: 2-1-6-7-8 at the final cadence

# Common Confusions
- Using cambiata with dissonance on a note other than the second
- Confusing cambiata with escape tone (escape tone leaps in same direction as approach)
- Thinking any four-note figure is a cambiata (specific contour required)
- Forgetting that the leap must be a third in the opposite direction
