# Quick Definition
The cambiata is a specific melodic figure in third species counterpoint where the second note is dissonant, approached by step and left by leap of a third in the opposite direction.

# Core Definition
The cambiata (also known as "nota cambiata" or "changing note") is an ornamental melodic figure permitted in third species counterpoint. It consists of four notes with a specific contour: the first note is consonant, the second note may be dissonant (approached by step from the first), the third note is consonant (reached by a leap of a third in the opposite direction), and the fourth note continues. Only the second note of the measure may be the dissonant interval in a cambiata. The cambiata also appears as a concluding formula in third species with the pattern scale degrees 2-1-6-7 (Hutchinson, Ch. 30, p. 427).

# Prerequisites
- **Third species counterpoint** -- The cambiata is a dissonance treatment specific to third species

# Key Properties
1. Four-note melodic figure with a specific contour
2. First note: consonant
3. Second note: may be dissonant (the only dissonant note in the figure)
4. Third note: consonant, reached by leap of a third in opposite direction from initial step
5. Fourth note: continues the line
6. Only permitted in third species counterpoint
7. Also used in concluding formulas: scale degrees 2-1-6-7

# Construction / Recognition
**The cambiata contour:**
1. Step from first to second note (may create dissonance)
2. Leap of a third in the opposite direction from second to third note (consonant)
3. Step or continuation from third to fourth note

**As a concluding formula (c.f. in lower voice):**
- Penultimate measure: scale degrees 2-1-6-7 (where 1 is dissonant against 2 in the c.f.)
- Followed by whole note on scale degree 8 (octave)

# Context & Application
The cambiata is a characteristic Renaissance melodic ornament found frequently in Palestrina and other 16th-century composers. It provides variety in third species while maintaining controlled dissonance treatment. The figure enriches melodic lines beyond what simple passing tones allow.

# Examples
- Concluding cambiata (p. 427): 2-1-6-7-8 at the final cadence where scale degree 1 is dissonant against the c.f.
- Third species example (p. 428): the cambiata appears in the third measure, with the second note as the only dissonant interval

# Relationships
## Related
- **Passing tone** -- Both are allowable dissonances in third species, but with different contours
- **Escape tone** -- Similar in having a step-leap pattern, but differs in direction

## Contrasts With
- **Escape tone** -- The escape tone leaps in the same direction as the approach; the cambiata leaps in the opposite direction

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Using the cambiata with dissonance on a note other than the second
  **Correction**: Only the second note of the figure may be dissonant
- **Error**: Leaping in the same direction as the initial step
  **Correction**: The leap must be a third in the opposite direction from the step

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking any four-note figure with a dissonance is a cambiata
  **Clarification**: The cambiata has a specific contour: step, leap of third in opposite direction
- **Confusion**: Confusing the cambiata with a double neighbor
  **Clarification**: The cambiata's leap is always a third, and the dissonance is always the second note
