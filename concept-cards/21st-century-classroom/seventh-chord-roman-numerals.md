# Quick Definition
Roman numeral notation for seventh chords uses uppercase/lowercase for triad quality, with specific suffixes indicating the seventh type: "7" for minor seventh, "M7" for major seventh, "O/7" for half-diminished, and "o7" for fully-diminished.

# Core Definition
When notating seventh chords with Roman numerals: an uppercase numeral means a major triad; a lowercase numeral means a minor triad. Adding "7" means there is a minor seventh between root and seventh (e.g., V7 = major triad + minor seventh). Adding "M7" indicates a major seventh chord (e.g., IM7). The half-diminished symbol (O/7) indicates a diminished triad with a minor seventh. The diminished symbol (o7) indicates a diminished triad with a diminished seventh.

# Key Properties
1. Major key diatonic 7ths: IM7, ii7, iii7, IVM7, V7, vi7, viiO/7
2. Minor key diatonic 7ths (harmonic): iM7, iiO/7, IIIM7, iv7, V7, VIM7, viio7
3. "7" suffix = minor seventh interval from root
4. "M7" suffix = major seventh interval from root
5. "O/7" suffix = diminished triad + minor seventh
6. "o7" suffix = diminished triad + diminished seventh
7. Uppercase/lowercase still indicates triad quality

# Construction / Recognition
1. Determine the triad quality (uppercase = major, lowercase = minor)
2. Determine the seventh quality and add the appropriate suffix
3. In major: I and IV get M7; V gets plain 7; ii, iii, vi get plain 7; vii gets O/7
4. In minor (harmonic): V gets 7; vii gets o7; ii gets O/7

# Context & Application
Understanding diatonic seventh chord Roman numerals is essential for harmonic analysis in both classical and popular music. The system concisely communicates both the chord quality and its position within the key. The ii7-V7-I progression is the most common chord progression in jazz.

# Examples
- C major diatonic 7ths: IM7=Cmaj7, ii7=Dm7, iii7=Em7, IVM7=Fmaj7, V7=G7, vi7=Am7, viiO/7=Bm7b5
- C minor diatonic 7ths: iM7=Cm(maj7), iiO/7=Dm7b5, IIIM7=Ebmaj7, iv7=Fm7, V7=G7, VIM7=Abmaj7, viio7=Bdim7

# Common Errors
- Forgetting that plain "7" means minor seventh, not major seventh
- Confusing O/7 (half-diminished) and o7 (fully-diminished) symbols

# Common Confusions
- **V7 does not mean "major seventh"**: V7 is a major triad with a minor seventh (dominant seventh)
- **IM7 is not the same as I7**: IM7 is a major seventh chord; I7 would imply a dominant seventh quality on the tonic
- **"7" always means minor seventh interval**: This is the default; major seventh requires the "M7" designation
