# Quick Definition
A time signature is two stacked numbers at the beginning of a piece indicating how many beats are in each measure and what note value receives one beat.

# Formal Definition
A time signature consists of two numbers placed at the beginning of a piece. The top number indicates "how many" beats (or divisions in compound meter) per measure, and the bottom number indicates "of what" note value. In simple meter, the bottom number shows the beat unit; in compound meter, it shows the division unit.

# Construction/Recognition
- Two numbers stacked vertically (e.g., 4/4, 3/4, 6/8)
- Top number: how many beats (simple) or divisions (compound)
- Bottom number: which note value (4=quarter, 8=eighth, 2=half)
- Common time (C) = 4/4
- Cut time (C with line) = 2/2

# Musical Context
Time signatures establish the metric framework for a piece, defining how beats are grouped into measures and how rhythms relate to the underlying pulse. They are essential for performers to understand the rhythmic organization of music.

# Examples
- 4/4: four quarter-note beats per measure (simple quadruple)
- 3/4: three quarter-note beats per measure (simple triple)
- 6/8: six eighth notes per measure, grouped as two dotted-quarter beats (compound duple)
- 2/2: two half-note beats per measure (simple duple)

# Common Confusions
- In compound meter, the bottom number is NOT the beat; the beat is a dotted note
- 6/8 has two beats, not six (it is compound duple)
- The time signature applies until a new one appears
