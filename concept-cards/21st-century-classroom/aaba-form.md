# Quick Definition
AABA form is a 32-bar song form associated with Broadway musicals and the Great American Songbook, where three A sections contain the main melody and the B section (bridge or "middle eight") provides contrast.

# Core Definition
The AABA form was dominant in popular music from the 1930s to the 1950s. Each section (A or B) is typically 8 measures long, creating a 32-bar structure. The A sections contain the primary melody while the B section provides contrast and is often called the "bridge" or "middle eight." The entire 32-bar AABA form is sometimes called a "refrain" or "chorus," and some songs have a "verse" (also called "prelude" or "introduction") before the refrain.

- **Phrase**: Understanding of musical sections

# Key Properties
1. Typically 32 bars: 8+8+8+8
2. A sections: primary melody
3. B section: contrasting "bridge" or "middle eight"
4. One or more A sections may be instrumental
5. Form may repeat (AABA AABA) with variations
6. A sections may be eliminated on repeat (e.g., AABAABA)
7. The 32-bar form may be preceded by a "verse"

# Construction / Recognition
1. Each section is typically 8 bars
2. A sections contain the main melodic material
3. B section provides contrast
4. Look for the return of A after B
5. Note any verse preceding the 32-bar form

# Context & Application
AABA was dominant for Broadway and Tin Pan Alley songs from the 1930s through the 1950s. It remained common through the 1960s (Beatles) and occasionally appears today. The Great American Songbook -- Gershwin, Porter, Berlin, Kern -- is built on AABA form.

# Examples
- Gershwin, "I Got Rhythm" (1930) -- classic AABA with 10-bar A3
- Gershwin: "Let's Call the Whole Thing Off," "Someone to Watch Over Me"
- Cole Porter: "Anything Goes," "Love for Sale," "I Get A Kick Out of You"
- Irving Berlin: "Blue Skies," "Puttin' on the Ritz"
- Beatles: "Yesterday," "Norwegian Wood," "Hey Jude"
- The Cure, "Friday, I'm in Love" (1992)
- Norah Jones, "Don't Know Why" (1999)

# Common Errors
- Confusing the "verse" before AABA with verse-chorus "verse"
- Not recognizing when A sections are varied or instrumental

# Common Confusions
- **Terminology overlap**: The 32-bar AABA may be called "refrain" or "chorus"; the section before it may be called "verse," "prelude," or "introduction"
- **A section variations**: A3 may be slightly different (extra bars, varied ending)
- **Not just 1930s**: AABA continues through Beatles era and occasionally appears today
