# Quick Definition
Phrase groups and phrase chains are combinations of phrases that end with a half cadence (HC) rather than concluding with a more conclusive cadence, distinguishing them from periods.

# Formal Definition
A phrase group consists of at least two phrases whose melodies begin similarly and ends with a half cadence. A phrase chain also ends in a half cadence but consists of at least two phrases with contrasting melodies. Neither is a period because they do not end with a more conclusive cadence than their opening phrase.

# Analysis/Application
To distinguish:
- Phrase group: similar melodic beginnings (a a'), ends with HC
- Phrase chain: contrasting melodic beginnings (a b), ends with HC
- Period: ends with more conclusive cadence than opening phrase

# Musical Context
Phrase groups and chains often function as antecedent-like material that requires a following consequent section for completion. They create forward momentum toward resolution.

# Examples
- Francois Couperin, 5 Pieces de Clavecin, 4. Le Petit-Rien - phrase group (similar melodies, ends HC)
- Beethoven, Sonatina in F, Anh. 5 No. 2, II. Rondo - phrase chain (contrasting melodies, ends HC)

# Common Confusions
- Phrase groups have similar melodic beginnings; phrase chains have contrasting beginnings
- Both end with half cadence (unlike periods which end more conclusively)
- These forms often occur as the first half of a larger structure
