# Quick Definition
Phrase groups and phrase chains are combinations of phrases that end with a half cadence (HC) rather than a more conclusive cadence -- phrase groups have similar melodic beginnings; phrase chains have contrasting beginnings.

# Core Definition
Phrase groups and phrase chains are not periods. Both end with a half cadence. A phrase group consists of at least two phrases whose melodies begin similarly and has the final phrase ending in a half cadence. A phrase chain also ends in a half cadence but consists of at least two phrases with contrasting melodies. The terminology draws from Douglass Green's Form in Tonal Music.

# Prerequisites
- **Period**: Understanding what phrase groups/chains are NOT

# Key Properties
1. Both end with a half cadence (HC)
2. Phrase group: similar melodic beginnings (a a'), ends HC
3. Phrase chain: contrasting melodic beginnings (a b), ends HC
4. Neither is a period (periods end more conclusively)
5. Can have 2 or 3 phrases
6. Often function as the first half of a larger structure

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify that the final cadence is a half cadence
2. If HC at end: not a period
3. Compare melodic beginnings: similar = phrase group; contrasting = phrase chain
4. Map as: phrase group (a a' = HC) or phrase chain (a b = HC)

# Context & Application
Phrase groups and chains often require a following consequent section for completion. They create forward momentum toward resolution and are common in Classical music transitional passages.

# Examples
- Couperin, 5 Pieces de Clavecin, 4. Le Petit-Rien -- phrase group (similar melodies, ends HC)
- Beethoven, Sonatina in F, Anh. 5 No. 2, II. Rondo -- phrase chain (contrasting melodies, ends HC)

# Common Errors
- Calling a phrase group a period (check the final cadence -- HC means not a period)

# Common Confusions
- **Phrase group = similar beginnings; phrase chain = contrasting beginnings**: Same as parallel/contrasting distinction in periods
- **Both end with HC**: This is what distinguishes them from periods
- **Can function as "antecedent"**: Often the first half of a larger formal structure
