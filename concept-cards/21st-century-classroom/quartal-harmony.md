# Quick Definition
Quartal harmony builds chords by stacking fourths instead of thirds, creating an open, ambiguous sonority characteristic of 20th-century music.

# Core Definition
Quartal harmony refers to chords stacked entirely (or mostly) in fourths. Quintal harmony refers to chords stacked in fifths. Secundal harmony refers to chords stacked in seconds. These contrast with the tertian (third-based) harmony that dominates most of Western music. Alternative stacking methods became more common after 1900 as composers sought to innovate and break with the past (Hutchinson, Ch. 32, p. 471).

# Prerequisites
- **Intervals** -- Must understand fourth, fifth, and second intervals

# Key Properties
1. Chords built from stacked perfect fourths
2. Open, hollow, or suspended quality
3. Absence of clear major/minor identity
4. Became common after 1900
5. Quartal and quintal are inversionally related (P4 inverts to P5)

# Construction / Recognition
- Three-note quartal: root-P4-P4 (e.g., C-F-Bb)
- Four-note quartal: root-P4-P4-P4 (e.g., C-F-Bb-Eb)
- Can mix perfect and augmented fourths

# Context & Application
Quartal harmony appears in early 20th-century concert music, jazz (particularly modal jazz), and television/film music. Schoenberg, Parker (*CHiPs* theme), and Griffin (*Jeopardy!* theme) all used quartal structures.

# Examples

- John Parker, *CHiPs* Theme (p. 471, Figure 32.3.2)
- Merv Griffin/Julann Wright, *Jeopardy!* Theme (p. 472, Figure 32.3.3)

# Relationships
## Related
- **Quintal harmony** -- Stacked fifths (inversional equivalent)
- **Secundal harmony** -- Stacked seconds (another alternative to tertian)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Labeling any chord containing a 4th as "quartal"
  **Correction**: Quartal chords are built entirely or mostly from stacked 4ths, not just containing one

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking quartal and quintal are fundamentally different
  **Clarification**: They are inversionally related (P4 inverts to P5); the sonic difference is in register placement
