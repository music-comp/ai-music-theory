# Quick Definition
Combinatoriality is a property where a hexachord of one row form combines with a hexachord of a related form to produce all twelve pitch classes.

# Core Definition
Combinatoriality describes the relationship between hexachords of related row forms where two hexachords together contain all twelve pitch classes without duplication. A row is hexachordally combinatorial if its first hexachord, combined with the first hexachord of a specific transposition or inversion, produces the complete chromatic aggregate. This allows twelve-tone aggregates in the vertical dimension (concept synthesized from serialism context; advanced topic touched on in row analysis).

# Prerequisites
- **Twelve-tone technique** -- Combinatoriality is a property of twelve-tone rows
- **Row forms** -- Must understand P, R, I, RI relationships

# Key Properties
1. Property of hexachords (first or second half of a row)
2. Two complementary hexachords together = all 12 pitch classes
3. Types: I-combinatorial, R-combinatorial, RI-combinatorial, all-combinatorial
4. Enables twelve-tone aggregates in the vertical dimension

# Construction / Recognition
1. Take first 6 notes of P0
2. Find which I or transposed P has complementary pitch classes in its first hexachord
3. If complement is found: the row is combinatorial at that transposition

# Context & Application
Schoenberg utilized combinatorial properties. The technique became especially important for Babbitt and post-war serialists.

# Examples
- Schoenberg's rows often exhibit I-combinatoriality
- All-combinatorial rows (rare, highly symmetric) can combine with any transformation
- 4-Z15 (0146) and 4-Z29 (0137) with vector 111111 are the "all-interval tetrachords"
