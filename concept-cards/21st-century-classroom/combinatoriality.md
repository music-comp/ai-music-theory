# Quick Definition
Combinatoriality is a property of certain twelve-tone rows where a hexachord (half of the row) combines with a hexachord from a related row form to complete all twelve pitch classes.

# Formal Definition
Combinatoriality describes the relationship between hexachords of related row forms where two hexachords together contain all twelve pitch classes without duplication. A row is hexachordally combinatorial if its first hexachord, combined with the first hexachord of a specific transposition or inversion, produces the complete chromatic aggregate. This allows composers to create twelve-tone aggregates vertically when combining row forms.

# Construction/Calculation
Types of combinatoriality:
1. I-combinatorial: First hexachord of P combines with first hexachord of specific I form
2. R-combinatorial: First hexachord of P combines with second hexachord of specific P transposition
3. RI-combinatorial: First hexachord of P combines with second hexachord of specific I form
4. All-combinatorial: Row exhibits all types (rare, highly symmetric rows)

Testing for combinatoriality:
- Take first 6 notes of P0
- Find which I or transposed P has complementary notes in its first hexachord

# Musical Context
Schoenberg discovered and utilized combinatorial properties to create twelve-tone aggregates in the vertical dimension. When two combinatorial row forms are combined contrapuntally, each half-row vertical slice contains all twelve pitch classes. This technique became important in the music of Babbitt and other post-war serialists.

# Examples
- Schoenberg's rows often exhibit I-combinatoriality
- P0 hexachord: {0,1,4,5,6,8} + I5 hexachord: {2,3,7,9,10,11} = aggregate
- All-combinatorial rows are highly constrained (few possibilities)
- Webern's Symphony row is all-combinatorial

# Common Confusions
- Combinatoriality is about hexachords, not complete rows
- Not all rows are combinatorial
- Different types of combinatoriality involve different row form pairings
- Combinatoriality enables twelve-tone harmony, not just melody
