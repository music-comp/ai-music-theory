# Quick Definition
Ninth chords are seventh chords with an added ninth (the 2nd an octave higher); in lead-sheet notation, "9" implies the 7th is also present.

# Core Definition
A ninth chord consists of a seventh chord (root, 3rd, 5th, 7th) with the addition of the 9th above the root. In lead-sheet notation, adding "9" to a chord symbol means the 7th is also present. The 9th comes from the major scale unless otherwise specified (i.e., it is a major 9th above the root by default). Four of the five common seventh chord types can have ninths added; the exception is the fully diminished seventh chord. The half-diminished seventh (ø7) is also labeled as m7(b5) in jazz (Hutchinson, Ch. 31, p. 446).

# Key Properties
1. "9" in a chord symbol implies the 7th is present below the 9th
2. The 9th is the 2nd scale degree an octave higher
3. The 9th comes from the major scale unless otherwise specified
4. Four seventh chord types accept ninths: dominant, major, minor, half-diminished
5. The fully diminished seventh chord does not typically have a 9th added
6. If no 7th is wanted, use "add 9" or "add 2" instead

# Construction / Recognition
**Building ninth chords:**
1. Build the underlying seventh chord
2. Add the 9th from the major scale (major 2nd + octave above root)
3. Symbol conventions: "maj" or delta before 9 means major 7th (not major 9th); "m" or "-" means minor 3rd

**Common ninth chord types:**
- C9 = C-E-G-Bb-D (dominant ninth)
- CΔ9 = C-E-G-B-D (major ninth)
- Cm9 = C-Eb-G-Bb-D (minor ninth)
- Cø9 or Cm9(b5) = C-Eb-Gb-Bb-D (half-diminished ninth)

# Context & Application
Ninth chords are fundamental to jazz harmony. They appear in virtually all jazz styles from swing to bebop to modern jazz. The added 9th creates the characteristic richness of jazz sonority. In the ii-V-I progression, typical voicings use ninths: Dm9-G9(13)-CΔ9.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Writing a 9th without the 7th and labeling it "C9"
  **Correction**: "C9" implies the 7th is present; use "C(add9)" or "C(add2)" for 9th without 7th

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking "maj9" means the 9th is major
  **Clarification**: "maj" (or Δ) before 9, 11, or 13 means the 7th is major, not the upper extension
- **Confusion**: Confusing C9 with CΔ9
  **Clarification**: C9 has a minor 7th (dominant); CΔ9 has a major 7th
