# Quick Definition
Ninth chords are seventh chords with an added ninth (the 2nd scale degree an octave higher), commonly used as a foundation of jazz harmony.

# Formal Definition
A ninth chord consists of a seventh chord (root, 3rd, 5th, 7th) with the addition of the 9th above the root. In lead-sheet notation, adding "9" to a chord symbol implies the 7th is also present. The 9th comes from the major scale unless otherwise specified (i.e., it is a major 9th above the root by default).

# Construction/Calculation
1. Build the underlying seventh chord (e.g., Mm7, MM7, mm7, half-dim7)
2. Add the 9th from the major scale (major 2nd + octave above root)
3. Four of five common seventh chord types can have 9ths added; the fully diminished seventh is the exception
4. The half-diminished seventh chord (ø7) is also labeled as m7(b5) in jazz notation

# Musical Context
Ninth chords are fundamental to jazz harmony. They appear in virtually all jazz styles from swing to bebop to modern jazz. The rich sonority of the added 9th creates the characteristic "jazz sound" that distinguishes jazz harmony from classical or popular music harmony.

# Examples
- C9 = C-E-G-Bb-D (dominant ninth)
- Cmaj9 (CΔ9) = C-E-G-B-D (major ninth)
- Cm9 = C-Eb-G-Bb-D (minor ninth)
- Cø9 = C-Eb-Gb-Bb-D (half-diminished ninth)

# Common Confusions
- "9" always implies the 7th is present; use "add 9" or "add 2" if only the 9th is added without the 7th
- The Δ (delta) symbol means major 7th, not major 9th - CΔ9 has a major 7th and major 9th
- Do not confuse C9 (dominant 9th with minor 7th) with Cmaj9 (major 9th with major 7th)
