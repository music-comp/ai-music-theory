# Quick Definition
Ledger lines are short horizontal lines added above or below the staff to extend its range for notes that fall outside the five-line staff.

# Core Definition
Staves are extended by ledger lines. These supplementary short lines extend the staff when notes are too high or too low to fit within the standard five lines. Each ledger line represents one additional pitch step, following the same line-space pattern as the main staff.

# Prerequisites
- **Staff**: The five-line framework that ledger lines extend

# Key Properties
1. Short horizontal lines drawn above or below the staff
2. Notes can be placed on ledger lines or in spaces between them
3. Multiple ledger lines can be stacked for very high or low notes
4. Lines should be only slightly wider than the notehead
5. Follow the same line-space pattern as the main staff

# Construction / Recognition
- Short horizontal lines extending the staff above or below
- Notes placed on ledger lines or in spaces between them
- Same pitch logic as the main staff lines and spaces

# Context & Application
Ledger lines allow composers to write notes beyond the standard staff range while maintaining the same clef. They are essential for indicating extreme registers. Middle C, for example, requires a ledger line in both treble clef (below) and bass clef (above).

# Examples
- Middle C in treble clef sits on the first ledger line below the staff
- Middle C in bass clef sits on the first ledger line above the staff
- High C (C6) in treble clef requires two ledger lines above the staff

# Common Errors
- Miscounting ledger lines when reading high or low notes
- Forgetting to count spaces between ledger lines as valid pitch positions

# Common Confusions
- **Same pitch, different clef**: The first ledger line below treble clef and the first ledger line above bass clef are both middle C
- **Readability**: Too many ledger lines become hard to read; clef changes or 8va markings are preferred for extreme ranges
