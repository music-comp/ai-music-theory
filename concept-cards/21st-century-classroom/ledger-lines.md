# Quick Definition
Ledger lines are short horizontal lines added above or below the staff to extend its range for notes that fall outside the five-line staff.

# Formal Definition
Ledger lines are supplementary lines that extend the staff when notes are too high or too low to fit within the standard five lines. Each ledger line represents one additional pitch step, following the same line-space pattern as the main staff.

# Construction/Recognition
- Short horizontal lines drawn above or below the staff
- Notes can be placed on ledger lines or in the spaces between them
- Multiple ledger lines can be stacked for very high or low notes
- Lines should be only slightly wider than the notehead

# Musical Context
Ledger lines allow composers to write notes beyond the standard staff range while maintaining the same clef. They are essential for indicating extreme registers of instruments and voices.

# Examples
- Middle C in treble clef sits on the first ledger line below the staff
- Middle C in bass clef sits on the first ledger line above the staff
- High C (C6) in treble clef requires two ledger lines above the staff

# Common Confusions
- Miscounting ledger lines when reading high or low notes
- Forgetting to count spaces between ledger lines
- Confusing the first ledger line below treble clef with the first ledger line above bass clef (both are middle C)
