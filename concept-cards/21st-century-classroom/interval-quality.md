# Quick Definition
Interval quality classifies the exact half-step distance for a given numeric interval size: unison/4th/5th/octave can be perfect (never major/minor), while 2nd/3rd/6th/7th can be major or minor (never perfect).

# Core Definition
Intervals such as the unison, fourth, fifth, and octave can be classified as "perfect" but never "major" or "minor." Conversely, the intervals of the second, third, sixth, and seventh can be major or minor but never perfect in quality. Additionally, any interval can be augmented (one half step larger than perfect or major) or diminished (one half step smaller than perfect or minor).

# Prerequisites
- **Intervals**: Understanding numeric size
- **Major scale**: Reference for determining quality (all scale degrees above tonic are perfect or major)

# Key Properties
1. Perfect intervals: unison (P1), 4th (P4), 5th (P5), octave (P8)
2. Major/minor intervals: 2nd, 3rd, 6th, 7th
3. Perfect can NEVER be major or minor
4. Major/minor can NEVER be perfect
5. Any interval can be augmented (+) or diminished (o)
6. Minor = one half step smaller than major
7. Augmented = one half step larger than perfect or major
8. Diminished = one half step smaller than perfect or minor

# Construction / Recognition
- Method 1: Think of lower note as tonic; determine if upper note is in that major scale (if so, interval is perfect or major)
- Method 2: Count half steps and compare to the half-step table
- If upper note is one half step lower than major, the interval is minor

# Context & Application
Interval quality affects consonance, dissonance, and overall musical color. Understanding quality is essential for building chords, analyzing harmony, and ear training. The two quality families (perfect vs. major/minor) never overlap.

# Examples
- C to E = M3 (4 half steps); C to Eb = m3 (3 half steps)
- C to G = P5 (7 half steps); C to G# = +5 (8 half steps)
- C to Gb = o5 (6 half steps)
- Half-step table: m2=1, M2=2, m3=3, M3=4, P4=5, tritone=6, P5=7, m6=8, M6=9, m7=10, M7=11, P8=12

# Common Errors
- Calling a fourth "major" or a third "perfect" (wrong quality family)
- Forgetting that any interval size can be augmented or diminished

# Common Confusions
- **Perfect CANNOT be major/minor**: Unison, 4th, 5th, 8ve are never M or m
- **Major/minor CANNOT be perfect**: 2nd, 3rd, 6th, 7th are never P
- **Any interval can be augmented or diminished**: Both quality families allow + and o
