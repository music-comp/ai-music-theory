# Quick Definition
Interval quality describes the specific size of an interval in half steps, classified as perfect, major, minor, augmented, or diminished.

# Formal Definition
Interval quality specifies the exact half-step distance for a given numeric interval size. Perfect intervals (unison, 4th, 5th, octave) can be perfect, augmented, or diminished. Major/minor intervals (2nd, 3rd, 6th, 7th) can be major, minor, augmented, or diminished. Augmented is one half step larger than perfect or major; diminished is one half step smaller than perfect or minor.

# Construction/Recognition
- Perfect intervals: P1 (unison), P4, P5, P8 (octave)
- Major/minor intervals: 2nd, 3rd, 6th, 7th
- Minor = one half step smaller than major
- Augmented (+) = one half step larger than perfect or major
- Diminished (o) = one half step smaller than perfect or minor

# Musical Context
Interval quality affects consonance, dissonance, and the overall sound of music. Major intervals sound "bright," minor intervals sound "dark," augmented intervals sound "stretched," and diminished intervals sound "compressed."

# Examples
- C to E = M3 (4 half steps)
- C to Eb = m3 (3 half steps)
- C to G = P5 (7 half steps)
- C to G# = +5 (8 half steps)
- C to Gb = o5 (6 half steps)

# Common Confusions
- Perfect intervals CANNOT be major or minor
- Major/minor intervals CANNOT be perfect
- Any interval can be augmented or diminished
