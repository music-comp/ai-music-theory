# Quick Definition
Intervals are a measurement between two pitches, consisting of a numeric size (2nd, 3rd, etc.) and a quality (perfect, major, minor, etc.); they are the building blocks of scales, chords, and melodies.

# Core Definition
Intervals are the building blocks of scales, chords (or harmonies), and melodies. Intervals are a measurement between two pitches, either vertically or horizontally. When measuring vertically, we refer to harmonic intervals because the two notes sound simultaneously. When measuring horizontally, we refer to melodic intervals because the notes occur one after the other. Every interval has two components: the numeric size and the quality.

# Prerequisites
- **Half step, whole step**: The basic units of interval measurement
- **Major scale**: Reference framework for interval identification

# Key Properties
1. Two components: numeric size (how many letter names) and quality (exact half-step content)
2. Harmonic interval: notes sound simultaneously (vertical)
3. Melodic interval: notes sound in succession (horizontal)
4. Odd-numbered intervals: line to line, or space to space
5. Even-numbered intervals: line to space, or space to line
6. All intervals from tonic to each degree of a major scale are either perfect or major

# Construction / Recognition
1. Count letter names inclusively to determine numeric size: C to E = 1-2-3 = 3rd
2. Determine quality by half-step content or comparison to major scale
3. Odd intervals: line-to-line or space-to-space on the staff
4. Even intervals: line-to-space or space-to-line on the staff

# Context & Application
Intervals are essential for constructing triads, analyzing harmony, recognizing melodic patterns, and ear training. Understanding intervals is the foundation for chord construction and voice leading analysis.

# Examples
- C to E = Major 3rd (M3, 4 half steps)
- C to G = Perfect 5th (P5, 7 half steps)
- C to Bb = minor 7th (m7, 10 half steps)
- Harmonic intervals create chords; melodic intervals create melodies

# Common Errors
- Forgetting to count both notes (C to E = 1, 2, 3 = third, not second)
- Specifying only number without quality (or vice versa)

# Common Confusions
- **Inclusive counting**: The numeric size counts BOTH the starting and ending notes
- **Two components required**: Both quality and number are needed for complete identification (e.g., "major third," not just "third")
- **Same number, different qualities**: The same numeric size can have different qualities (M3, m3, +3, o3)
