# Quick Definition
Process music is composition where the musical structure is determined by a gradual, audible process that unfolds over time, making the compositional procedure perceptible to listeners.

# Formal Definition
Process music refers to compositions structured by systematic procedures that are audible to the listener. Unlike hidden serial procedures, the processes in minimalist works (additive process, phase shifting) are designed to be heard. Steve Reich articulated this aesthetic in his 1968 essay "Music as a Gradual Process," advocating for music where compositional processes are perceptually accessible.

# Construction/Calculation
Characteristics of process music:
1. A clear, systematic procedure generates the music
2. The process is audible, not hidden
3. Change occurs gradually enough to be perceived
4. Once set in motion, the process runs its course
5. The outcome is determined by the initial conditions
6. Personal expression occurs in choosing the process and materials

# Musical Context
Steve Reich's essay "Music as a Gradual Process" (1968) became a manifesto for process music. Reich distinguished his approach from serial music (where processes are inaudible) and chance music (where processes are indeterminate). Process music influenced subsequent generations of composers and connects to systems music and algorithmic composition.

# Examples
- Reich, Piano Phase: Phase shifting process
- Reich, Clapping Music: Rotation process
- Glass, Two Pages: Additive process
- Riley, In C: Indeterminate process (players choose repetitions)

# Common Confusions
- Process music is not improvised - the process determines outcomes
- The process must be AUDIBLE, not just theoretically present
- "Process" doesn't mean "procedure" - it implies gradual, perceptible change
- Process music is a subset of minimalism, not synonymous with it
