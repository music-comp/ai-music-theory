# Quick Definition
The minor-major seventh chord combines a minor triad with a major seventh, creating a distinctive jazz sonority often used as a tonic minor chord.

# Core Definition
The minor-major seventh chord (mΔ7) consists of a minor triad (root, minor 3rd, perfect 5th) with a major 7th added. While not one of the five standard classical seventh chord types, it is essential to jazz harmony and commonly has a ninth added (mΔ9). The Δ symbol is used primarily in jazz; popular music typically uses "maj" instead (Hutchinson, Ch. 31, p. 447).

# Prerequisites
- **Seventh chords** -- Must understand basic seventh chord construction

# Key Properties
1. Minor triad + major 7th
2. Structure: root-m3-P5-M7 (semitones: 0-3-7-11)
3. Commonly has 9th added (mΔ9)
4. Δ symbol means major 7th (not major 3rd)
5. "m" or "-" means minor 3rd

# Construction / Recognition
- CmΔ7 = C-Eb-G-B
- CmΔ9 = C-Eb-G-B-D
- The "minor" refers to the 3rd; "major" refers to the 7th

# Context & Application
The mΔ7 chord appears in jazz as a tonic minor chord, associated with the melodic minor scale. It has a distinctive "mysterious" quality. It commonly functions as i in minor key jazz harmony.

# Examples
- CmΔ7 = C-Eb-G-B (p. 447)
- CmΔ9 = C-Eb-G-B-D
- Chord-scale relationship: C melodic minor ascending

## Contrasts With
- **Minor seventh chord** -- Cm7 has minor 7th (Bb); CmΔ7 has major 7th (B)
- **Major seventh chord** -- CΔ7 has major 3rd (E); CmΔ7 has minor 3rd (Eb)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing CmΔ7 with Cm7
  **Correction**: CmΔ7 has a major 7th (B); Cm7 has a minor 7th (Bb)

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking Δ means major 3rd
  **Clarification**: Δ (or "maj") always refers to the quality of the 7th, not the 3rd
