# Quick Definition
The minor-major seventh chord combines a minor triad with a major seventh, creating a distinctive jazz sonority often associated with mystery or tension.

# Formal Definition
A minor-major seventh chord (mΔ7 or m(maj7)) consists of a minor triad (root, minor 3rd, perfect 5th) with a major 7th added above the root. This chord type, while less common than the five standard seventh chord types, is an essential part of the jazz harmonic vocabulary and frequently has a ninth added as well.

# Construction/Calculation
1. Build a minor triad (root, minor 3rd, perfect 5th)
2. Add the major 7th (half step below the octave)
3. Result: root - m3 - P5 - M7 (semitones: 0-3-7-11)
4. Often includes the 9th: mΔ9 = root - m3 - P5 - M7 - M9

# Musical Context
The minor-major seventh chord appears frequently in jazz, often as a tonic minor chord or on the iv chord. It has a characteristic "James Bond" or mysterious quality. The chord is associated with the ascending melodic minor scale and serves as the i chord in minor key jazz harmony. The Δ (delta) symbol is primarily used in jazz; popular music typically uses "maj" instead.

# Examples
- CmΔ7 = C-Eb-G-B
- CmΔ9 = C-Eb-G-B-D
- AmΔ7 = A-C-E-G#
- The tonic chord in jazz minor: im(Δ7)

# Common Confusions
- Do not confuse with minor seventh (m7) which has a minor 7th (Cm7 = C-Eb-G-Bb)
- Do not confuse with major seventh (Δ7) which has a major 3rd (CΔ7 = C-E-G-B)
- The Δ symbol refers to the quality of the 7th, not the 3rd
- "Minor-major" refers to the 3rd being minor and the 7th being major
