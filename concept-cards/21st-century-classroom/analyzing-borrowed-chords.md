# Quick Definition
Analyzing borrowed chords involves stacking in thirds to find root and quality, then labeling with the altered Roman numeral -- writing them requires careful attention to the flat convention (bVI in A major = F major, not Ab major, because the root is lowered by m2 from the diatonic 6th degree).

# Core Definition
Unlike secondary chords, you merely note the root, quality, and inversion of a borrowed chord in your Roman numeral analysis -- no slash notation is needed. To analyze: stack the notes in thirds, determine the root and quality, and identify the Roman numeral based on the (lowered) scale degree. To write a borrowed chord from a Roman numeral, pay close attention to the flat convention: flats before Roman numerals mean to lower a root a minor second from its diatonic position in the key signature, not to literally put a flat accidental in front of the note.

# Key Properties
1. No slash notation -- just root, quality, and inversion
2. Stack in thirds to find root and quality (same as diatonic analysis)
3. Flats before Roman numerals = lower by m2 from diatonic position
4. Example: bVI in A major -- diatonic 6th = F#; lower by m2 = F-natural; build major triad = F-A-C
5. Quality is determined by the parallel minor's chord on that degree
6. Always check: does the quality match the Roman numeral given?

# Construction / Recognition
Analyzing example -- chord with A-C-Eb-Gb in G major:
1. Stack in thirds: A-C-Eb-Gb = half-diminished seventh
2. Root is A; A = scale degree 2 in G major
3. Quality is half-diminished
4. Analysis: iio7 (borrowed supertonic seventh)

Writing example -- bVI in A major:
1. Diatonic 6th in A major = F#
2. Lower by m2 = F-natural
3. Build major triad (capital VI) on F: F-A-C
4. Result: F major triad

# Context & Application
The flat-before-Roman-numeral convention is one of the most common sources of confusion in mode mixture analysis. Students in keys with sharps often make errors because "flat" does not literally mean "add a flat" -- it means "lower by m2." In A major, bVI is F major (F-A-C), not Ab major. Understanding this convention is essential for both analyzing and writing borrowed chords correctly.

# Examples
- iio7 in G major: A-C-Eb-Gb (half-diminished; A = scale degree 2)
- bVI in A major: F-A-C (F# lowered by m2 = F-natural; build major triad)
- iv in D major: G-Bb-D (diatonic IV = G-B-D; lower 3rd from B to Bb for minor quality)

# Common Errors
- Interpreting bVI as "Ab major" in A major (it is F major)
- Using slash notation for borrowed chords (no V/x or viio/x)

# Common Confusions
- **Flat = lower by m2**: Not literally "add a flat accidental"
- **No slash notation**: Borrowed chords are labeled simply by root, quality, inversion
- **Quality matches the label**: iio means diminished quality on lowered-quality 2nd degree
