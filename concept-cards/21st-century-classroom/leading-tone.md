# Quick Definition
The leading tone is the seventh scale degree when it is a half step below the tonic, creating a strong pull toward resolution to the tonic.

# Formal Definition
The leading tone is ^7 when it lies a half step (semitone) below ^1. It is called "leading" because it has a strong tendency to resolve upward to the tonic. In major keys, ^7 is always a leading tone. In minor keys, the leading tone requires raising the natural ^7 by a half step (as in harmonic and ascending melodic minor).

# Construction/Recognition
- Always a half step below the tonic
- In major: naturally occurs in the scale
- In minor: requires raising ^7 with an accidental
- Creates strong melodic pull toward tonic
- Essential for authentic cadences and dominant function

# Musical Context
The leading tone is crucial for establishing tonality and creating strong cadences. The half-step relationship creates tension that resolves satisfyingly to the tonic. The leading tone is the defining note of the dominant chord and diminished vii chord.

# Examples
- In C major: B is the leading tone (B→C)
- In G major: F# is the leading tone (F#→G)
- In A minor: G# is the leading tone (raised from G natural)
- The V chord contains the leading tone, which resolves to ^1

# Common Confusions
- Leading tone vs. subtonic: leading tone is a half step below tonic; subtonic is a whole step below
- In minor, the leading tone must be raised with an accidental
- The leading tone "leads" to tonic by half step motion
