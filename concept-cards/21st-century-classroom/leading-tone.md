# Quick Definition
The leading tone is ^7 when it lies a half step below the tonic, creating a strong melodic pull upward to ^1.

# Core Definition
The leading tone is ^7 when it lies a half step (semitone) below ^1. It is called "leading" because it has a strong tendency to resolve upward to the tonic. In major keys, ^7 is always a leading tone. In minor keys, the leading tone requires raising the natural ^7 by a half step (as in harmonic and ascending melodic minor).

# Prerequisites
- **Scale degree names**: Understanding the ^7 position
- **Half step**: The interval that defines the leading tone relationship

# Key Properties
1. Always a half step below the tonic
2. In major: naturally occurs in the scale (no accidental needed)
3. In minor: requires raising ^7 with an accidental
4. Creates strong melodic pull toward tonic
5. Essential component of the dominant chord (V) and viio chord
6. Fundamental to authentic cadences

# Construction / Recognition
- ^7 that is a half step below ^1
- In major: the seventh note of the scale
- In minor: the raised seventh note (needs an accidental)

# Context & Application
The leading tone is crucial for establishing tonality and creating strong cadences. The half-step relationship creates tension that resolves satisfyingly to the tonic. The leading tone is present in both the V chord and the viio chord, giving them dominant function.

# Examples
- In C major: B is the leading tone (B -> C)
- In G major: F# is the leading tone (F# -> G)
- In A minor: G# is the leading tone (raised from G natural)
- The V chord contains the leading tone, which resolves to ^1

# Relationships
## Builds Upon

- Authentic cadences, dominant function, strong tonal resolution

## Related
- Subtonic (the whole-step version of ^7)
- Harmonic minor scale (provides the leading tone in minor)
- Authentic cadence (requires the leading tone)
- Diatonic chords in minor (V and viio require raised ^7)

## Contrasts With
- Subtonic (whole step below tonic, lacks the strong pull to resolve)

# Common Errors
- Forgetting to raise ^7 in minor keys when a leading tone is needed
- Confusing leading tone with subtonic

# Common Confusions
- **Leading tone vs. subtonic**: Leading tone = half step below tonic; subtonic = whole step below
- **Minor key accidental**: In minor, the leading tone must be raised with an accidental (it is not in the key signature)
- **Name origin**: Called "leading" because it "leads" to tonic by half step
