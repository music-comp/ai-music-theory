# Quick Definition
A whole step is an interval equal to two half steps; on a piano keyboard, there is always exactly one key between two notes a whole step apart.

# Core Definition
A whole step is made of two half steps. On a piano keyboard, moving a whole step means skipping exactly one key (black or white). Most adjacent white keys are a whole step apart, with the exceptions of E-F and B-C, which are half steps.

# Prerequisites
- **Half step**: The component unit (two half steps = one whole step)

# Key Properties
1. Equals two half steps
2. One key is always skipped between whole-step notes on the piano
3. Most adjacent white keys are whole steps: C-D, D-E, F-G, G-A, A-B
4. E to F and B to C are NOT whole steps (they are half steps)
5. On a guitar, two frets = one whole step

# Construction / Recognition
- Two half steps combined
- Skip one key on the piano to move a whole step
- C to D, D to E, F to G, G to A, A to B are all whole steps between white keys

# Context & Application
Whole steps are essential components of scales and melodies. The major scale pattern consists of whole steps and half steps in a specific arrangement: W-W-H-W-W-W-H. Understanding whole and half steps is fundamental to constructing and recognizing all scale types.

# Examples
- C to D is a whole step (C# is skipped)
- D to E is a whole step (D#/Eb is skipped)
- E to F# is a whole step (F is skipped)
- Bb to C is a whole step (B is skipped)

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Half step

## Enables
- Scale construction, tetrachord building, interval measurement

## Related
- Major scale (built from whole and half step patterns)
- Tetrachord (W-W-H pattern)

## Contrasts With
- Half step (which is half the size)

# Common Errors
- Calling E to F a whole step (it is a half step)
- Calling B to C a whole step (it is a half step)

# Common Confusions
- **E-F and B-C exceptions**: These are the only adjacent white-key pairs that are half steps, not whole steps
- **Always skip one key**: A whole step always skips exactly one piano key (black or white)
