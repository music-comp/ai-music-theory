# Quick Definition
A whole step is an interval equal to two half steps, or the distance of two adjacent keys on the piano keyboard.

# Formal Definition
A whole step (also called a whole tone) equals the distance of two half steps. On a piano keyboard, there is always exactly one key (black or white) between two notes that are a whole step apart.

# Construction/Recognition
- Two half steps combined
- Skip one key on the piano to move a whole step
- Examples: C to D, D to E, F to G, G to A, A to B
- On a guitar, two frets equal one whole step

# Musical Context
Whole steps are essential components of scales and melodies. The major scale pattern consists of whole steps and half steps in a specific arrangement. Understanding whole and half steps is fundamental to constructing and recognizing scales.

# Examples
- C to D is a whole step (C# is skipped)
- D to E is a whole step (D#/Eb is skipped)
- E to F# is a whole step (F is skipped)
- Bb to C is a whole step (B is skipped)

# Common Confusions
- E to F is NOT a whole step (it is a half step)
- B to C is NOT a whole step (it is a half step)
- Whole step always skips exactly one piano key
