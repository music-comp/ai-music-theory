# Quick Definition
Scale degrees have specific names: ^1 tonic, ^2 supertonic, ^3 mediant, ^4 subdominant, ^5 dominant, ^6 submediant, ^7 leading tone (or subtonic).

# Core Definition
Scale degrees, though often referred to by numbers, also have names. These names are used throughout the text to refer to scale degrees, chords built on these scale degrees, and keys associated with these scale degrees. The names reflect each degree's functional position: tonic (^1), supertonic (^2), mediant (^3), subdominant (^4), dominant (^5), submediant (^6), and leading tone or subtonic (^7). The subtonic is a whole step below the tonic, while the leading tone is only a half step below the tonic.

# Prerequisites
- **Major scale**: The scale framework for these names
- **Natural minor scale**: Where the subtonic (vs. leading tone) distinction applies

# Key Properties
1. ^1: Tonic -- the home note, tonal center
2. ^2: Supertonic -- one step above tonic
3. ^3: Mediant -- midway between tonic and dominant
4. ^4: Subdominant -- a fifth below tonic (or fourth above)
5. ^5: Dominant -- the most important note after tonic
6. ^6: Submediant -- midway between subdominant and upper tonic
7. ^7: Leading tone (half step below tonic) or Subtonic (whole step below)

# Construction / Recognition
- "Sub-" prefix means "below": subdominant = fifth below tonic, submediant = third below upper tonic
- Mediant = midpoint between tonic and dominant
- Submediant = midpoint between subdominant and tonic (from below)
- Leading tone = half step below tonic; subtonic = whole step below tonic

# Context & Application
These names are used throughout music theory to refer to scale degrees, chords built on those degrees, and keys associated with those degrees. Understanding these names is essential for harmonic analysis, chord function discussions, and communication about tonal music.

# Examples
- In C major: C=tonic, D=supertonic, E=mediant, F=subdominant, G=dominant, A=submediant, B=leading tone
- In C natural minor: Bb is the subtonic (whole step below C)
- In C harmonic minor: B natural is the leading tone (half step below C)

# Common Errors
- Confusing subtonic (whole step below) with leading tone (half step below)
- Misunderstanding the "sub-" prefix as meaning "lesser" rather than "below"

# Common Confusions
- **Subtonic vs. leading tone**: Subtonic is a whole step below tonic (natural minor ^7); leading tone is a half step below (major ^7 or raised ^7 in minor)
- **"Sub-" prefix meaning**: Means "below," not "lesser" -- subdominant = fifth below tonic, submediant = third below the upper tonic
- **Mediant and submediant symmetry**: Mediant is equidistant between tonic and dominant; submediant mirrors this from below
