# Quick Definition
The grand staff is a combination of treble and bass clef staves joined by a bracket, used primarily for piano music.

# Formal Definition
The grand staff (also called great staff) consists of a treble clef staff and a bass clef staff connected by a brace or bracket on the left side. This notation system spans a wide pitch range and is the standard format for notating keyboard music.

# Construction/Recognition
- Treble clef staff on top
- Bass clef staff on bottom
- Connected by a bracket or brace on the left
- Bar lines typically extend through both staves
- Middle C can appear on ledger lines in either staff

# Musical Context
The grand staff is essential for piano, organ, harp, and other keyboard instruments. It allows notation of both hands simultaneously and covers the full range of the keyboard.

# Examples
- All piano music is written on the grand staff
- The right hand typically plays from the treble clef staff
- The left hand typically plays from the bass clef staff
- Middle C is notated on a ledger line in either staff, never floating between

# Common Confusions
- Middle C never floats between the two staves; it is always clearly notated in one or the other
- The staves are connected but each maintains its own clef and pitch references
- Notes can cross between staves (e.g., left hand playing in treble clef range)
