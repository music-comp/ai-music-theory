# Quick Definition
The grand staff is a treble and bass clef joined together by a bracket, used for piano music and other keyboard instruments.

# Core Definition
The grand staff, which is a treble and bass clef joined together by a bracket, is how piano music is written. The treble clef staff is on top and the bass clef staff is on the bottom. Middle C is always clearly notated in either the upper or lower staff and never floats between the two staves.

# Prerequisites
- **Treble clef**: The upper staff
- **Bass clef**: The lower staff
- **Middle C**: The reference point connecting both staves

# Key Properties
1. Treble clef staff on top, bass clef staff on bottom
2. Connected by a bracket or brace on the left
3. Bar lines typically extend through both staves
4. Middle C can appear on ledger lines in either staff
5. Standard notation format for keyboard instruments

# Construction / Recognition
- Two staves joined by a bracket on the left side
- Upper staff: treble clef; lower staff: bass clef
- Middle C on a ledger line in either staff, never floating between

# Context & Application
The grand staff is essential for piano, organ, harp, and other keyboard instruments. It allows notation of both hands simultaneously and covers the full range of the keyboard. The right hand typically plays from the treble clef staff and the left hand from the bass clef staff.

# Examples
- All piano music is written on the grand staff
- The right hand typically reads the treble clef staff
- The left hand typically reads the bass clef staff
- Middle C is notated on a ledger line in either staff, never floating between

# Common Errors
- Placing middle C between the two staves (it must be clearly in one or the other)
- Confusing which staff corresponds to which hand

# Common Confusions
- **Middle C placement**: Never floats between the two staves; it is always clearly notated in one
- **Crossover**: Notes can cross between staves (e.g., left hand playing in treble clef range), so hand assignment is not absolute
