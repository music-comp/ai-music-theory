# Quick Definition
A retardation is an accented non-chord tone approached by the same note and resolving upward by step - essentially an upward-resolving suspension.

# Formal Definition
A retardation is usually an accented non-chord tone, meaning it occurs on a downbeat. A retardation is approached by the same note and resolves up by step. It has the same structure as a suspension (preparation, retardation, resolution) but resolves in the opposite direction.

# Analysis/Application
To identify retardations:
1. Look for a note held from the previous beat that is dissonant
2. Confirm it resolves upward by step
3. The preparation, dissonance, and resolution follow the same pattern as suspensions
4. Label as Ret. or R

# Musical Context
Retardations are less common than suspensions but appear frequently at cadences, especially in Classical period slow movements. They often occur alongside suspensions for expressive effect.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C major, K. 545, II (1788) - retardation at cadence
- J.S. Bach, French Suite No. 1 in D Minor, Sarabande - "ret." appears in tenor part alongside suspension
- Classical composers often used retardations at cadences in slow movements

# Related Concepts

# Common Confusions
- Retardation = resolves UP; Suspension = resolves DOWN
- Both are approached by same note and are typically accented
- Retardations often occur at cadences where the leading tone is held and resolves up to tonic
