# Quick Definition
The melodic minor scale raises both ^6 and ^7 when ascending (avoiding the augmented second of harmonic minor) and descends as natural minor.

# Core Definition
The melodic minor scale has an ascending version and a descending version. The ascending form raises both ^6 and ^7 from natural minor, eliminating the augmented second of harmonic minor while retaining the leading tone. The descending version is the same as the natural minor scale. Both raised notes require accidentals in notation.

# Prerequisites
- **Natural minor scale**: The descending form and the basis
- **Harmonic minor scale**: Understanding why ^7 is raised and why the A2 is avoided

# Key Properties
1. Ascending: raises both ^6 and ^7 from natural minor
2. Descending: identical to natural minor (lowered ^7 and ^6)
3. Ascending upper tetrachord matches the major scale
4. Eliminates the augmented second of harmonic minor
5. Retains the leading tone for resolution to tonic
6. Both raised notes require accidentals

# Construction / Recognition
- Ascending: W-H-W-W-W-W-H (raised ^6 and ^7)
- Descending: same as natural minor W-W-H-W-W-H-W
- Ascending top four notes match the major scale

# Context & Application
The melodic minor scale was developed to create smoother melodic lines when approaching the tonic from below, avoiding the awkward augmented second of harmonic minor. Compositions in minor freely mix all three scale forms. In jazz, the ascending form is often used in both directions ("jazz minor").

# Examples
- A melodic minor ascending: A-B-C-D-E-F#-G#-A
- A melodic minor descending: A-G-F-E-D-C-B-A (= natural minor)
- C melodic minor ascending: C-D-Eb-F-G-A-B-C

## Contrasts With
- Harmonic minor (retains the A2; melodic minor removes it)
- Natural minor (has lowered ^6 and ^7 in both directions)

# Common Errors
- Using the ascending form when descending (in classical style)
- Forgetting that both raised notes need accidentals

# Common Confusions
- **Classical vs. jazz**: Classical melodic minor differs ascending and descending; jazz uses the ascending form in both directions
- **Accidentals**: Both raised ^6 and ^7 require accidentals (they are not in the key signature)
- **Compositional freedom**: Real music freely mixes all three minor scale forms
