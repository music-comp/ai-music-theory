# Quick Definition
Serialism is a compositional technique organizing music according to a fixed series of elements, most commonly the twelve pitch classes; it encompasses twelve-tone technique and related approaches.

# Core Definition
Serialism encompasses the twelve-tone technique of Schoenberg, Webern, and Berg, who were the major figures associated with expressionism and atonality. Twelve-tone serialism uses all twelve pitch classes in a fixed order; serialism also includes music that uses fewer than twelve pitch classes while maintaining serial procedures (Hutchinson, Ch. 34, p. 492).

# Prerequisites
- **Set theory** -- Serialism grew from the same atonal context as set theory analysis

# Key Properties
1. Uses a predetermined series (row) to structure composition
2. Twelve-tone: all 12 pitch classes in fixed order
3. Four transformations: P, R, I, RI
4. Each transposable to 12 levels = 48 row forms
5. Row order is fixed (no reordering)
6. Can also apply to fewer than 12 pitches

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the row (series of pitch classes)
2. Trace transformations (P, R, I, RI) and transpositions
3. Every note should be accountable to a row form

# Context & Application
Serialism arose as systematic organization for atonal music. Schoenberg's Suite for Piano, Op. 25 is a foundational work. Later composers (Stravinsky) adopted serial techniques with fewer than 12 pitches.

# Examples
- Schoenberg, Suite for Piano, Op. 25 (p. 492, Figure 34.1.1)
- Stravinsky, Septet (1953): Non-twelve-tone serial elements (p. 497)

# Relationships
## Related
- **Twelve-tone technique** -- The primary form of serialism
- **Tone row** -- The ordered pitch series
- **Non-twelve-tone serialism** -- Serial techniques with fewer pitch classes
## Contrasts With
- **Minimalism** -- Opposite aesthetic: repetition vs. non-repetition

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Equating serialism exclusively with twelve-tone technique
  **Correction**: Serialism is broader; it includes non-twelve-tone approaches

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking serial music must sound "ugly" or "difficult"
  **Clarification**: Serial technique is a structural principle; the resulting music varies widely in character
