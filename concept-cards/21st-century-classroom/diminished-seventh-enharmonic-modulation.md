# Quick Definition
Each fully diminished seventh chord can resolve to four different keys by treating any of its four notes as the leading tone (scale degree 7), enabling modulation to keys a minor third apart.

# Formal Definition
Each diminished seventh sonority implies four different keys because each note can be treated as scale degree 7 (leading tone), resolving up by half step to the tonic of a new key. This creates four possible resolutions for every fully diminished seventh chord. Additionally, each note can function as the 7th of the chord, resolving down to the root of a dominant seventh chord.

# Construction/Recognition
For any diminished seventh chord (e.g., G#-B-D-F):

Each note as leading tone (7):
- G# as 7: resolves to A (minor or major)
- B as 7: resolves to C (minor or major)
- D as 7: resolves to Eb (minor or major)
- F (respelled E#) as 7: resolves to F# (minor or major)

Each note as chord 7th (resolves down to V7):
- G# down to G: V7 in C
- B down to Bb: V7 in Eb
- D down to Db: V7 in Gb
- F down to E: V7 in A

The four keys are a minor third apart (related by the symmetry of diminished 7th chord).

# Musical Context
- Enables rapid modulation to distant keys
- All four keys are equidistant (minor 3rd apart)
- You must imagine the alternative spellings
- Like homophones: two/to/too or there/their/they're

# Examples
- Beethoven, Pathetique Sonata, Op. 13, I (1798):
  - F#dim7/C in G minor (vii-dim4/3) reinterpreted as vii-dim4/2 in E minor (D#dim7/C)
  - Resolves to V7 in E minor

- Beethoven, Symphony No. 5, II (1808):
  - E dim7 in C (vii-dim7/IV) reinterpreted as vii-dim4/2 in Ab (G dim7/Fb)
  - Unusual resolution to Ger+6

- Schubert, Schwanegesang, "Der Atlas" (1828):
  - G#dim7 in G minor reinterpreted as E#dim7 (vii-dim6/5/V) in B minor

# Common Confusions
- You must visualize the enharmonic respelling
- All four potential resolutions are equally valid theoretically
- Context and the actual resolution determine the analysis
- Diminished triads are not tonicized (only major/minor chords)
