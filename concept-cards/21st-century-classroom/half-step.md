# Quick Definition
A half step is the smallest interval in Western music, equal to the distance between any two adjacent keys on a piano keyboard.

# Formal Definition
A half step (also called a semitone) is the distance from one note to the next nearest note on a piano keyboard. This is the smallest standard interval in the Western twelve-tone system. Two half steps equal one whole step.

# Construction/Recognition
- The distance between any key and the very next key (black or white) on the piano
- Examples of natural half steps: E to F, B to C (no black key between them)
- Examples of chromatic half steps: C to C#, F# to G
- On a guitar, one fret equals one half step

# Musical Context
Half steps are fundamental building blocks of scales, intervals, and harmony. The placement of half steps within a scale determines its quality (major, minor, etc.). The half step creates tension and pull, especially between the leading tone and tonic.

# Examples
- E to F is a half step (adjacent white keys)
- B to C is a half step (adjacent white keys)
- C to C# is a half step
- G to Ab is a half step
- F# to G is a half step

# Related Concepts
- Whole step
- Semitone
- Major scale (W-W-H-W-W-W-H pattern)
- Leading tone
- Chromatic scale

# Common Confusions
- Not all adjacent white keys are half steps (most are whole steps)
- Only E-F and B-C are natural half steps (white key to white key)
- Half step is the same as semitone (different terminology, same concept)
