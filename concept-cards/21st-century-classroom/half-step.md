# Quick Definition
A half step is the distance from one note to the next nearest note on a piano keyboard -- the smallest standard interval in Western music.

# Core Definition
A half step on a piano keyboard is the distance from one note to the next nearest note. This is the smallest standard interval in the Western twelve-tone system. Two half steps equal one whole step.

# Prerequisites
- **Pitch**: Understanding that notes have highness/lowness

# Key Properties
1. Smallest standard interval in Western music
2. Distance between any key and the very next key (black or white) on the piano
3. Two half steps = one whole step
4. Natural half steps occur between E-F and B-C (adjacent white keys)
5. All other adjacent white keys are a whole step apart

# Construction / Recognition
- Distance from any piano key to the immediately adjacent key
- Natural half steps: E to F, B to C (no black key between them)
- Chromatic half steps: C to C#, F# to G, etc.
- On a guitar, one fret = one half step

# Context & Application
Half steps are fundamental building blocks of scales, intervals, and harmony. The placement of half steps within a scale determines its quality (major, minor, etc.). The half step creates tension and pull, especially between the leading tone and tonic.

# Examples
- E to F is a half step (adjacent white keys, no black key between)
- B to C is a half step (adjacent white keys, no black key between)
- C to C# is a half step (white key to adjacent black key)
- G to Ab is a half step

## Contrasts With
- Whole step (which spans two half steps)

# Common Errors
- Assuming all adjacent white keys are half steps (most are whole steps)
- Confusing half step with whole step in scale construction

# Common Confusions
- **Natural half steps**: Only E-F and B-C are half steps between white keys; all other adjacent white keys are whole steps apart
- **Terminology**: Half step and semitone are the same concept (different terminology traditions)
