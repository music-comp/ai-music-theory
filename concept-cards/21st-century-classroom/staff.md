# Quick Definition
A staff is a set of five horizontal lines and four spaces on which musical notes are written.

# Formal Definition
The staff (plural: staves) is the fundamental framework for Western musical notation, consisting of five parallel horizontal lines and four spaces between them. Each line and space represents a specific pitch, determined by the clef placed at the beginning of the staff.

# Construction/Recognition
- Five horizontal lines
- Four spaces between the lines
- Notes placed on lines or in spaces
- Higher placement = higher pitch
- Extended with ledger lines above or below when needed

# Musical Context
The staff is the canvas for all standard Western music notation. It provides a visual representation of pitch, with rhythm indicated by note shapes. Nearly all written music uses the staff system.

# Examples
- Treble clef staff: lines are E-G-B-D-F (bottom to top)
- Treble clef staff: spaces are F-A-C-E (bottom to top)
- Bass clef staff: lines are G-B-D-F-A (bottom to top)

# Related Concepts

# Common Confusions
- "Staff" vs. "staves": Staff is singular, staves is plural
- Lines vs. spaces: Both represent distinct pitches
- Confusing which line/space represents which note without a clef reference
