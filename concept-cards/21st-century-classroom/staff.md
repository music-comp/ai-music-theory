# Quick Definition
A staff is a set of five horizontal lines on which musical notes are written; a clef orients the lines to specific pitches.

# Core Definition
Notes are written on a five-line staff. A clef orients the lines to a reference point. The plural of "staff" in musical terminology is "staves." Staves are extended by ledger lines when notes fall above or below the five lines. Each line and space represents a specific pitch, determined by the clef placed at the beginning of the staff.

# Prerequisites
- **Pitch**: Understanding that notes have highness/lowness

# Key Properties
1. Five parallel horizontal lines
2. Four spaces between the lines
3. Notes placed on lines or in spaces
4. Higher placement = higher pitch
5. Requires a clef to assign specific pitch names
6. Extended with ledger lines when needed

# Construction / Recognition
- Five horizontal lines with four spaces
- Notes placed on lines or in spaces
- Treble clef staff lines: E-G-B-D-F ("Every Good Boy Does Fine")
- Treble clef staff spaces: F-A-C-E
- Bass clef staff lines: G-B-D-F-A
- Higher placement = higher pitch

# Context & Application
The staff is the fundamental framework for Western musical notation, providing a visual representation of pitch. Nearly all standard written music uses the staff system, with rhythm indicated by note shapes and duration symbols.

# Examples
- Treble clef staff: lines are E-G-B-D-F (bottom to top)
- Treble clef staff: spaces are F-A-C-E (bottom to top)
- Bass clef staff: lines are G-B-D-F-A (bottom to top)

# Common Errors
- Confusing lines and spaces as the same thing (each represents a distinct pitch)
- Forgetting that the staff is meaningless without a clef

# Common Confusions
- **"Staff" vs. "staves"**: Staff is singular, staves is plural
- **Lines vs. spaces**: Both represent distinct pitches; notes sit on lines or between them
- **Without a clef**: The staff alone does not specify which pitches the lines represent
