# Quick Definition
Augmented intervals are one half step larger than perfect or major; diminished intervals are one half step smaller than perfect or minor.

# Formal Definition
Augmented intervals (+) are one half step larger than perfect or major intervals of the same numeric size. Diminished intervals (o) are one half step smaller than perfect or minor intervals. Any interval size can be augmented or diminished. The most common are the tritone (+4/o5) and the augmented second in harmonic minor.

# Construction/Recognition
- Augmented = perfect + half step, OR major + half step
- Diminished = perfect - half step, OR minor - half step
- +4 = 6 half steps (same as o5, enharmonically)
- +2 = 3 half steps (same as m3 enharmonically)
- Doubly augmented/diminished also possible

# Musical Context
The augmented fourth (tritone) is historically important as the "devil in music" and is essential to dominant seventh chord function. Augmented seconds appear in harmonic minor. Diminished fifths occur in diminished triads and seventh chords.

# Examples
- C to F# = +4 (augmented fourth, 6 half steps)
- C to Gb = o5 (diminished fifth, 6 half steps)
- F to G# = +2 (augmented second, 3 half steps) in A harmonic minor
- C to G# = +5 (augmented fifth, 8 half steps)
- C to Dbb = o2 (diminished second, 0 half steps)

# Common Confusions
- +4 and o5 are enharmonic (same sound, different spelling and function)
- Augmented second sounds like minor third but functions differently
- "Augmented" means larger; "diminished" means smaller (not missing)
