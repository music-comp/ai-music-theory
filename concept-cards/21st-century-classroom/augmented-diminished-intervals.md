# Quick Definition
Augmented intervals are one half step larger than perfect or major; diminished intervals are one half step smaller than perfect or minor. Any interval size can be augmented or diminished.

# Core Definition
Augmented intervals are one half step larger than perfect or major intervals and diminished intervals are one half step smaller than perfect or minor intervals. Remember that perfect intervals (unisons, fourths, fifths, and octaves) can never be major or minor, and major and minor intervals (seconds, thirds, sixths, and sevenths) can never be perfect in quality. However, any size of interval can be augmented or diminished.

# Prerequisites
- **Interval quality**: The perfect and major/minor families that + and o modify
- **Perfect intervals**: The P family (P + half step = +; P - half step = o)
- **Major/minor intervals**: The M/m family (M + half step = +; m - half step = o)

# Key Properties
1. Augmented = perfect + half step, OR major + half step
2. Diminished = perfect - half step, OR minor - half step
3. Any interval size can be augmented or diminished
4. +4 = 6 half steps (same as o5, enharmonically)
5. +2 = 3 half steps (enharmonic with m3 but spelled differently)
6. The continuum: o - m - M - + (for 2nds, 3rds, 6ths, 7ths) or o - P - + (for unisons, 4ths, 5ths, 8ves)

# Construction / Recognition
- For perfect intervals: augmented = P + 1 half step; diminished = P - 1 half step
- For major/minor intervals: augmented = M + 1 half step; diminished = m - 1 half step
- The "o" symbol represents diminished; the "+" symbol represents augmented

# Context & Application
The augmented fourth (tritone) is historically important as the "diabolus in musica" and is essential to dominant seventh chord function. Augmented seconds appear in harmonic minor between ^6 and raised ^7. Diminished fifths occur in diminished triads and seventh chords.

# Examples
- C to F# = +4 (augmented fourth, 6 half steps)
- C to Gb = o5 (diminished fifth, 6 half steps)
- F to G# = +2 (augmented second, 3 half steps) in A harmonic minor
- C to G# = +5 (augmented fifth, 8 half steps)

# Common Errors
- Confusing augmented second with minor third (same sound, different spelling and function)
- Forgetting that +4 and o5 are enharmonic (same sound, different interval names)

# Common Confusions
- **+4 and o5 are enharmonic**: Same sound (6 half steps), different spelling and function
- **+2 sounds like m3**: An augmented second sounds identical to a minor third but functions differently
- **"Augmented" = larger; "diminished" = smaller**: Not "missing" or "extra"
