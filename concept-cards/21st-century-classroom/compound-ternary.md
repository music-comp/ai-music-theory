# Quick Definition
Compound ternary is a ternary form in which one or more sections (A or B) is itself a complete binary or ternary form, creating a nested hierarchical structure.

# Formal Definition
A compound ternary is a ternary form in which one of the sections (the A or the B) is itself a binary or ternary form. This creates a hierarchical structure where smaller forms are nested within the larger ternary framework. Common examples include the minuet and trio and the da capo aria.

# Construction/Recognition
Hierarchical structure example (Minuet and Trio):
```
LARGE SCALE: A (Minuet) - B (Trio) - A (Minuet da capo)

A (Minuet): itself a rounded binary or simple binary
B (Trio): itself a rounded binary or simple binary
A (Minuet): repeat of first A
```

The "Minuet and Trio" form:
- Minuet: binary or rounded binary form
- Trio: binary or rounded binary form (usually in contrasting key)
- Minuet da capo: return of entire minuet section

# Musical Context
- Minuet and Trio: standard third movement of Classical symphonies/sonatas
- Da capo aria: Baroque vocal form (A section repeated after B)
- Creates extended forms with internal variety
- The nested structure provides multiple levels of contrast and return

# Examples
- Classical minuet and trio movements (Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven symphonies)
- Baroque da capo arias

# Related Concepts
- Ternary form
- Binary form

# Common Confusions
- "Compound" refers to the nested structure, not the meter
- Both A and B can be binary forms within the ternary frame
- The da capo return is usually exact (not rewritten)
- The trio is called "trio" historically (originally for three instruments)
