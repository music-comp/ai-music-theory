# Quick Definition

Compound ternary is a ternary form in which one or more sections (A or B) is itself a complete binary or ternary form, creating a nested hierarchical structure.

# Core Definition

A compound ternary is a ternary form in which one of the sections (the A or the B) is itself a binary or ternary form. Examples can be found in the minuet and trio as well as the da capo aria (Hutchinson, Ch. 24, p. 357).

# Prerequisites

- **Ternary form** -- Compound ternary is a ternary form at the largest level
- **Binary form** -- The individual sections may themselves be binary forms

# Key Properties

1. Large-scale ABA structure
2. One or more sections is itself a binary or ternary form
3. Creates hierarchical nesting of forms
4. Common examples: minuet and trio, da capo aria

# Construction / Recognition

## Hierarchical Structure (Minuet and Trio):
```
LARGE SCALE: A (Minuet) - B (Trio) - A (Minuet da capo)
  A (Minuet): itself a rounded binary or simple binary
  B (Trio): itself a rounded binary or simple binary (often contrasting key)
  A (Minuet): repeat of entire first A section
```

## To Identify:
1. Confirm the piece has an ABA large-scale structure
2. Analyze each section independently for its own internal form
3. If any section is itself a complete binary or ternary: compound ternary

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: Third movement of Classical symphonies and sonatas (minuet and trio), Baroque vocal music (da capo aria)
- **Common applications**: Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven symphonies and quartets; Baroque opera
- **Historical/stylistic notes**: The trio originally referred to a contrasting section for three instruments; the da capo ("from the head") indicates returning to the beginning

# Examples

**Example 1**: Classical minuet and trio movements -- the minuet is typically a rounded binary, the trio is a binary or rounded binary in a contrasting key, and the minuet returns da capo

**Example 2**: Baroque da capo arias -- the A section repeats after the B section

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Analyzing only the large-scale form and ignoring the internal forms
  **Correction**: Always analyze both the large-scale ABA and the internal form of each section

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking "compound" refers to compound meter
  **Clarification**: "Compound" here means nested or hierarchical form structure

- **Confusion**: Believing the trio must be for three instruments
  **Clarification**: "Trio" is a historical name; it no longer implies three instruments
