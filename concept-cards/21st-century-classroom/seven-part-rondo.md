# Quick Definition
Seven-part rondo has the structure ABACABA, with the refrain appearing four times and the first episode (B) returning later in the tonic key, often applying sonata principle.

# Formal Definition
The 7-part rondo typically has ABACABA form, although other designs exist. The refrain (A) appears four times, always in tonic. Significantly, the B episode first appears in the dominant (or mediant in minor), then returns later in tonic - an application of sonata principle.

# Construction/Recognition
ABACABA structure:
- A: Refrain (tonic)
- B: First episode (V in major, III in minor)
- A: Refrain (tonic)
- C: Second episode (variety: i, vi, IV, etc.)
- A: Refrain (tonic) - often abbreviated
- B: First episode NOW IN TONIC (sonata principle!)
- A: Final refrain (tonic), often followed by coda

Transitions connect A to episodes
Retransitions lead back to A

# Musical Context
- Common Classical finale form
- The return of B in tonic = sonata principle
- Greater tonal journey than 5-part rondo
- Often in rondo character (light, quick, staccato)

# Examples
- Beethoven, Pathetique Sonata, Op. 13, III:
  - A: Refrain in C minor
  - B: First episode in Eb major (III)
  - A: Refrain in C minor
  - C: Second episode in Ab major (VI)
  - A: Abbreviated refrain, leads to B
  - B: Episode returns in C major (tonic major!)
  - A: Final refrain in C minor
  - Coda

# Common Confusions
- B returning in tonic is crucial (sonata principle)
- The third A is often abbreviated
- The second B is in TONIC, not dominant
- The form may include retransitions between sections
