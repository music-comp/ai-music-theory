# Quick Definition

Seven-part rondo has the structure ABACABA, with the refrain appearing four times and the first episode (B) returning later in the tonic key, applying sonata principle.

# Core Definition

The 7-part rondo typically has ABACABA form, although other designs exist. The refrain (A) appears four times, always in tonic. Significantly, the B episode first appears in the dominant (or mediant in minor), then returns later in tonic -- an application of sonata principle (Hutchinson, Ch. 25, p. 375).

# Prerequisites

- **Rondo form** -- Seven-part rondo is the full Classical rondo type
- **Sonata principle** -- B returning in tonic is an application of sonata principle

# Key Properties

1. ABACABA: refrain appears four times
2. B first in V (major) or III (minor), later returns in I (sonata principle)
3. C in various keys: i, vi, IV, VI, etc.
4. Third A often abbreviated
5. Transitions and retransitions connect sections
6. Often followed by a coda

# Construction / Recognition

## ABACABA Structure:
- A: Refrain (tonic)
- B: First episode (V in major, III in minor)
- A: Refrain (tonic)
- C: Second episode (various keys)
- A: Refrain (tonic, often abbreviated)
- B: First episode NOW IN TONIC (sonata principle)
- A: Final refrain (tonic), often with coda

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: Common Classical finale form
- **Historical/stylistic notes**: The return of B in tonic provides greater tonal journey than 5-part rondo; often in rondo character

# Examples

**Example 1** (pp. 376-380): Beethoven, Pathetique Sonata, Op. 13, III:
- A: Refrain in C minor
- B: First episode in Eb major (III)
- A: Refrain in C minor
- C: Second episode in Ab major (VI)
- A: Abbreviated refrain
- B: Episode returns in C major (tonic major)
- A: Final refrain in C minor, followed by Coda

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Forgetting that B returns in TONIC, not in its original key
  **Correction**: The second B statement is in tonic -- this is the whole point of sonata principle in rondo

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the third A must be full-length
  **Clarification**: The third A is often abbreviated, leading directly to the B return
