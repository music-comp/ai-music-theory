# Quick Definition
A monothematic sonata uses the primary theme (or material derived from it) in place of a contrasting secondary theme, emphasizing tonal design over thematic contrast.

# Formal Definition
Haydn was especially fond of restating the Primary Theme in the dominant where the Secondary Theme would normally occur. This monothematic approach reinforces the idea that the tonal design of a sonata was as important as thematic design. The "secondary theme area" exists tonally even when it lacks new thematic material.

# Construction/Recognition
Structure:
- Exposition: PT in I, "ST" area uses PT material in V
- Recapitulation: PT in I, "ST" area uses PT material in I

Characteristics:
- Primary theme appears in both tonic and secondary key
- The key change is more important than thematic contrast
- Transition and closing sections provide variety
- Reinforces sonata principle (tonal over thematic)

# Musical Context
- Particularly associated with Haydn
- Demonstrates that sonata form is fundamentally about tonal drama
- The contrast comes from KEY, not theme
- The recapitulation still resolves the tonal dissonance

# Examples
- Many Haydn piano sonatas and symphonies
- The practice demonstrates the primacy of tonal design

# Related Concepts
- Sonata form

# Common Confusions
- Monothematic does not mean "no contrast" - key provides contrast
- The form still has a "secondary theme AREA" (defined by key)
- This is not a deficiency but a compositional choice
- Reinforces that sonata form is about tonal journey
