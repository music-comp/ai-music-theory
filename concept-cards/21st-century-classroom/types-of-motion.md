# Quick Definition
The four types of motion describe how two voices move in relation to each other: contrary, oblique, similar, and parallel motion.

# Formal Definition
Types of motion classify the relative melodic direction of any two voices in a multi-part texture:
- **Contrary motion**: Two voices move in opposite directions (one up, one down) by step or leap
- **Oblique motion**: One voice stays on the same pitch while the other moves up or down
- **Similar motion**: Both voices move in the same direction (both up or both down) by step or leap; also called "direct motion"
- **Parallel motion**: A specific type of similar motion where both voices move in the same direction by the same interval size

# Rules/Procedures
1. Identify the direction each voice moves (up, down, or static)
2. Identify the interval size each voice moves (step, leap, or none)
3. Compare the directions:
   - Opposite directions = Contrary motion
   - One static = Oblique motion
   - Same direction, different intervals = Similar motion
   - Same direction, same interval = Parallel motion

# Musical Context
Understanding types of motion is essential for avoiding objectionable parallels (parallel 5ths and 8ves) and for achieving voice independence. Contrary and oblique motion generally promote independence, while parallel motion can undermine it (depending on the interval).

# Examples
- Contrary motion: Soprano rises C to D while bass falls G to F
- Oblique motion: Alto stays on E while tenor rises A to B
- Similar motion: Soprano rises C to E (3rd) while alto rises G to A (2nd)
- Parallel motion: Soprano rises C to D while alto rises E to F (both move up by step)

# Common Confusions
- Confusing similar motion with parallel motion - parallel is a subset of similar motion requiring the same interval size
- Forgetting that parallel motion is acceptable for 3rds and 6ths but not for 5ths and 8ves
- Not recognizing that "direct motion" is another term for similar motion
