# Quick Definition
Stravinsky's late twelve-tone practice (1960--1966) uses only four untransposed series forms, derives rotational arrays from their hexachords, traces melodic pathways through arrays, and uses columns for harmonies -- emphasizing common tones and centricity rather than aggregate formation.

# Core Definition
"When Stravinsky started writing serial and twelve-tone music late in his life, he brought to bear the full force of his vigorous and highly individual musical personality" (Straus, p. 345). He "normally used only four untransposed forms of the series: a prime (P), an inversion starting on the same first note (I), the retrograde of the prime (R), and the inversion of the retrograde, starting on the same first note (IR)" (p. 345). He derived rotational arrays from each hexachord of each form (8 arrays total).

His twelve-tone music is "about as different from the twelve-tone music of Schoenberg and Webern as it can be" (p. 349): intensive melodic focus, common-tone emphasis, pitch centricity, and non-aggregate-based organization.

# Key Properties
1. Only 4 untransposed forms (not 48)
2. 8 rotational arrays (one per hexachord of each form)
3. Common tones between rows create melodic focus
4. First note of each row is constant, creating centricity
5. Non-aggregate-based: "Stravinsky's late music often traces melodic pathways through his rotational arrays" (p. 348)
6. Verticals (columns) used for harmonies, often with inversional balance

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the four forms (P, I, R, IR)
- Derive rotational arrays from each hexachord
- Look for melodic cycles through array rows
- Check verticals for harmonic structures

# Context & Application
Stravinsky represents the third of five approaches, distinguished by its non-aggregate focus, intensive melodic concentration, and characteristic pitch doublings and centricity. "His twelve-tone music, very unlike the Schoenbergian variety, is not generally based on the aggregate" (p. 348).

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 345--348): Stravinsky, A Sermon, a Narrative, and a Prayer -- three simultaneous cycles through a rotational array in alto, tenor, and chorus. Eb recurs as the first note of each row, serving as pitch center.

**Example 2** (pp. 348--349): Stravinsky, Requiem Canticles -- verticals from the array create harmonies balanced around A# as axis of symmetry. "Frequent pitch-class doublings... impart a sense of weight and focus" (p. 349).

# Common Errors
- Expecting aggregate formation (Stravinsky often avoids it)
- Assuming Stravinsky uses all 48 forms (he uses only 4)
- Not recognizing the role of common tones and centricity

# Common Confusions
- **IR vs. RI**: Stravinsky uses IR (inversion of retrograde starting on same note as R), which differs from the standard RI labeling
