# Quick Definition
Stravinsky's late twelve-tone practice (1960-1966) is based on rotational arrays derived from series hexachords, using melodic pathways through arrays and verticals for harmonies, with characteristic pitch focus and common-tone emphasis.

# Formal Definition
When Stravinsky started writing serial and twelve-tone music late in his life, he brought a vigorous and highly individual musical personality. Beginning with *Movements* (1960) and ending with *Requiem Canticles* (1966), he settled on a practice based on rotational arrays. He normally used only four untransposed series forms (P, I, R, IR starting on the same note) and derived arrays from their hexachords. His music often traces melodic pathways through these arrays and uses the verticals (columns) for harmonic structures.

# Musical Context/Application
Characteristics of Stravinsky's twelve-tone approach:
- **Limited transposition**: Only four untransposed forms, not all 48
- **Hexachordal focus**: Arrays derived from hexachords, not complete series
- **Common tones**: First note of each array row is constant, creating centricity
- **Non-aggregate approach**: Unlike Schoenberg, Stravinsky often avoids systematic aggregate formation
- **Intensive melodic focus**: Relatively small group of notes in circulation
- **Verticals**: Array columns used for harmonic structures with inversional balance

# Examples
- Stravinsky, A Sermon, a Narrative, and a Prayer:
  - Three cycles through rotational array (alto, tenor, chorus)
  - Eb recurs as first note of each row, serving as pitch center
  - Within each cycle, transpositions compose out the inversion of the hexachord
- Stravinsky, Requiem Canticles:
  - Verticals from array create harmonies
  - A# serves as axis of symmetry for verticals
  - Frequent pitch-class doublings create weight and focus

# Common Confusions
- Expecting Stravinsky's twelve-tone music to sound like Schoenberg's—it's very different
- Assuming aggregate formation is central—Stravinsky often avoids it
- Overlooking the role of common tones and pitch centricity
- Not recognizing that Stravinsky uses only four forms, not 48
