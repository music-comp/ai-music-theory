# Quick Definition
The characteristic smaller sets contained within the octatonic collection, including major and minor triads, diminished seventh chords, and many other familiar formations, all of which can be transposed within the collection at T0, T3, T6, and T9.

# Formal Definition
The octatonic collection contains many familiar formations among its subsets. Because of the collection's transpositional symmetry (T0, T3, T6, T9), any subset can be transposed at these intervals without introducing notes foreign to the collection. Conversely, the octatonic collection can be generated by combining any subset with its T0, T3, T6, and T9 transpositions.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Key subsets of the octatonic collection (using OCT0,1 as example):

Triads (4 major, 4 minor):
- Major: C+, D#+, F#+, A+ (roots form dim7)
- Minor: C#-, E-, G-, Bb- (roots form other dim7)

Seventh chords:
- Diminished seventh: two complete (the defining ones)
- Dominant seventh: four (on C, D#, F#, A)
- Half-diminished seventh: four

Other characteristic subsets:
- (0134): Contains chromatic trichord + major/minor third
- (0235): "Dorian tetrachord"
- (0167): The "Z-cell" (favorite of Bartók)
- (0236): Minor-major combination

Generation principle: Any subset x 4 transpositions (at T0, T3, T6, T9) = octatonic collection

# Musical Context/Application
Octatonic subsets allow:
1. Quasi-tonal passages within octatonic contexts (using triads/seventh chords)
2. Recognition of octatonic structure through characteristic subsets
3. Understanding how octatonic collections arise from transposition schemes
4. Analysis of octatonic music through its subset structure

# Common Confusions
- The octatonic contains 4 major AND 4 minor triads (rich triadic content)
- Triadic music can still be octatonic, not just chromatic or diatonic
- T3/T6/T9 transposition of any subset stays within the collection
- Recognition of subsets helps identify octatonic contexts
