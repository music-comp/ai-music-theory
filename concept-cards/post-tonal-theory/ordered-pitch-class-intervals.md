# Quick Definition
Ordered pitch-class intervals in a series are the directed intervals between consecutive pitch classes, typically represented as positive integers 1-11, defining the intervallic identity of the series.

# Formal Definition
Because we are dealing with an ordered succession of pitch classes, when discussing its intervals, we usually refer to the *ordered pitch-class intervals*. These are usually represented as positive integers, 1-11, but sometimes negative numbers are used for 7 and above (7 = -5; 8 = -4; 9 = -3; 10 = -2; 11 = -1). A twelve-tone series has eleven ordered pitch-class intervals (one fewer than the number of pitch classes). This particular interval succession is what defines the prime ordering of a series.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
- For consecutive pitch classes x and y: ordered pc interval = (y - x) mod 12
- Positive representation: intervals 1-11
- Negative representation (optional): 7 = -5, 8 = -4, 9 = -3, 10 = -2, 11 = -1
- A series has 11 ordered pitch-class intervals
- The interval succession defines series identity under transformation

# Musical Context/Application
- The interval succession remains invariant under transposition (defines the "P" identity)
- Under retrograde: intervals are reversed and complemented
- Under inversion: intervals are complemented but maintain order
- Under retrograde-inversion: intervals maintain values but reverse order
- Interval content often reveals structural properties (recurring intervals, excluded intervals)

# Examples
- Schoenberg, String Quartet No. 4, P2: intervals are <11, 8, 1, 7, 10, 1, 8, 8, 11, 11, 5>
- Notable: predominance of intervals 1, 4, 8, and 11; complete exclusion of 3 and 9
- This intervallic profile is shared by all 48 forms in the series class
- Webern's series often use only a few different intervals intensively (especially ic 1)

# Common Confusions
- Confusing ordered pitch-class intervals with unordered interval classes
- Forgetting that retrograde complements intervals as well as reversing them
- Not recognizing that the interval succession is the defining characteristic of the series ordering
