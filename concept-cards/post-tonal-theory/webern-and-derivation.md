# Quick Definition
Webern's twelve-tone music is characterized by derived series, where intensive use of a single trichord or tetrachord set class creates extreme motivic concentration across all structural levels.

# Formal Definition
Webern's music is highly concentrated motivically, making intensive use of just a few intervals or sets. In writing twelve-tone music, Webern often guaranteed a high degree of motivic concentration by employing *derived series*—series where all discrete segmental trichords or tetrachords are members of the same set class. This approach creates three simultaneous levels of structure: note-to-note, trichord-to-trichord (or tetrachord-to-tetrachord), and series-to-series, all shaped by the same intervallic relationships.

# Musical Context/Application
- Economy and concentration are hallmarks of Webern's style
- Webern's works are short partly because generating materials are so restricted
- The generating set class shapes all dimensions of the music
- Transformations at the note level (T_n, I_n) are reflected at the series level
- The generator influences instrumentation, register, articulation—not just pitch content
- The serial approach enabled Webern to shape motivic flow at multiple structural levels

# Examples
- Webern, Concerto for Nine Instruments, op. 24:
  - Series derived from (014)
  - Four discrete trichords ordered as P7, RI6, R1, I0
  - Intervals: <+4,-1>, <-1,+4>, <+1,-4>, <-4,+1>
  - Every circled trichord in the music is (014)
  - The violin's four notes form (014); the viola's four notes form (014)
  - Melodic trichords linked by T4 and I6—same as within (014)
  - First two series of movement related by T4; first two notes related by T4

# Common Confusions
- Thinking derivation is purely mechanical—it enables profound musical expression
- Overlooking how the generator influences non-serial aspects (register, timbre)
- Not recognizing the multi-level projection of the same intervals
- Assuming Webern's brevity indicates simplicity—it reflects concentration
