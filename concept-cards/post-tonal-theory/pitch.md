# Quick Definition
A pitch is a specific note with a particular frequency, located at a unique position in the range from lowest to highest audible tones.

# Formal Definition
A pitch is a note with a specific frequency occupying a unique location in pitch space. Unlike pitch class (an abstraction), a pitch can be precisely notated on a musical staff. Pitches are designated using octave-register notation, where middle C is C4. For example, the lowest note on the cello is C2, representing a specific pitch two octaves below middle C.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Pitches can be represented numerically using various systems:
- Octave designation: C4 = middle C, C2 = two octaves below middle C
- MIDI numbers: Middle C = 60, each semitone = 1
- Frequency: A4 = 440 Hz (standard tuning)

Pitches are located in linear pitch space, extending from the lowest to highest audible tones.

# Musical Context/Application
When we refer to a specific pitch, we mean a particular note at a particular register. The traditional grand staff provides distinct positions for all pitches, including the 88 pitches represented by piano keys. Pitch relationships involve considerations of register, contour, and actual distance in semitones.

# Examples
**Example 1-5** (Feldman, Durations III, No. 3): A passage where each of three instruments plays a series of notes. The listener hears many different pitches as the instrumental lines leap about. The tuba, for example, plays five different pitches, most of which are repeated. However, the passage as a whole contains only three pitch classes: F#, G, and Ab.

# Common Confusions
Pitch and pitch class are fundamentally different concepts. A pitch is a specific, concrete note at a particular frequency; a pitch class is an abstract category containing all pitches with the same (or enharmonically equivalent) letter name. When we say "the lowest note on the cello is C," we refer to a specific pitch (C2). When we say "the tonic of Beethoven's Fifth Symphony is C," we refer to pitch class C.
