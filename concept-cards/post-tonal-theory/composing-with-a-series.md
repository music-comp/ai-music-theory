# Quick Definition
Composing with a series involves numerous creative decisions beyond the series order itself—including register, rhythm, articulation, instrumentation, and vertical vs. horizontal presentation—making it a process of "endless reshaping of a basic shape."

# Formal Definition
There is nothing mechanical about either the construction of the series or its musical development in a composition. A composer of twelve-tone music must construct their own basic materials, embedding preferred intervals and subsets within the series. Once constructed, many compositional decisions remain: Should notes sound one at a time or simultaneously? In what registers? With what durations and articulations? Played by what instruments? A good composer doesn't just lay series forms end-to-end any more than Mozart simply strings scales together. As Schoenberg said, music involves "the endless reshaping of a basic shape."

# Musical Context/Application
Compositional decisions include:
- **Horizontal vs. vertical**: Notes can form melodies or chords
- **Register**: The same pitch class can appear in different octaves
- **Duration and rhythm**: The series specifies pitch classes, not rhythmic values
- **Articulation**: Legato, staccato, accents, etc.
- **Instrumentation**: Series can be distributed among multiple instruments
- **Voice distribution**: A single series can be shared among voices
- **Spacing and doubling**: Chord voicing choices
- **Tempo and dynamics**: Affect expression and character

# Examples
- Schoenberg, String Quartet No. 4, first movement: P2 is presented in three very different ways:
  1. As a singing melody in the first violin (mm. 1-6)
  2. Distributed among instruments with different rhythms
  3. As chords with melodic fragments
- The musical idea is recognizable in each case but "wonderfully varied"
- Each presentation makes independent compositional choices while using the same series

# Common Confusions
- Thinking twelve-tone composition is "mechanical" or purely systematic
- Assuming the series dictates all musical parameters—it only orders pitch classes
- Overlooking the creative freedom in register, rhythm, texture, and other dimensions
- Not recognizing that series construction itself involves compositional choices
