# Quick Definition
Composing with a series involves numerous creative decisions beyond pitch-class order -- including register, rhythm, articulation, instrumentation, and vertical vs. horizontal presentation -- making it a process of "endless reshaping of a basic shape."

# Core Definition
"There is nothing mechanical about either the construction of the series or its musical development in a composition" (Straus, p. 322). A twelve-tone composer must construct their own basic materials (unlike tonal composers who work with shared scales and triads). Once a series is constructed, "just think how many compositional decisions are still required to turn it into music. Should the notes be sounded one at a time or should some of them be heard simultaneously? In what registers should they occur? Played by what instruments? With what durations? What articulations? It is like being given a C-major scale and told to compose some music. There are certain restrictions, but a great deal of freedom as well" (p. 322).

# Prerequisites
- **Twelve-tone series** -- the material being composed with
- **Series class** -- the available forms for compositional use

# Key Properties
1. The series specifies pitch-class order only; all other parameters are freely chosen
2. Construction of the series itself is a compositional decision
3. Choice of series forms is a compositional decision
4. Presentation of the series (the most important decision) is a compositional decision
5. "A good composer doesn't just lay series forms end-to-end any more than Mozart simply strings scales together" (p. 322)

# Construction / Recognition
Compositional decisions include:
- Horizontal (melodic) vs. vertical (harmonic) presentation
- Register and octave placement
- Rhythm, duration, tempo
- Articulation (legato, staccato, accents)
- Instrumentation and voice distribution
- Spacing, doubling, dynamics

# Context & Application
The series provides a framework within which enormous creative freedom exists. As Schoenberg said, music involves "the endless reshaping of a basic shape" (p. 323). The compositional choices are based on "hearable musical relationships" -- they serve musical and expressive purposes, not mere systematic requirements.

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 322--323, Exx. 6-1 and 6-15): Schoenberg, String Quartet No. 4, first movement -- three different statements of P2: (a) as a singing melody in the first violin, (b) distributed among instruments, (c) as chords with melodic fragments. "The musical idea is recognizable in each case, but wonderfully varied" (p. 323).

# Common Errors
- Thinking twelve-tone composition is "mechanical" or purely systematic
- Assuming the series dictates all musical parameters (it only orders pitch classes)
- Overlooking the creative freedom in register, rhythm, texture, and other dimensions

# Common Confusions
- **Restriction vs. freedom**: The series constrains pitch-class order but leaves all other musical dimensions open
- **Series construction vs. series use**: Both involve creative decision-making; neither is automatic
