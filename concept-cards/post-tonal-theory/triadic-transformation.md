# Quick Definition
A class of operations on major and minor triads that combine common-tone-preserving contextual inversion with voice-leading parsimony, always converting a major triad to a minor triad or vice versa.

# Formal Definition
Triadic transformations involve two interrelated properties: (1) contextual inversion, meaning the triad inverts around one or two of its own notes, which always changes the mode (major becomes minor, minor becomes major); and (2) voice-leading parsimony, meaning the voices move as little as possible. The most parsimonious voice leading involves two common tones and one voice moving by semitone; slightly less parsimonious involves two common tones and one voice moving by whole tone, or one common tone and two voices each moving by semitone.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Six primary triadic transformations:
| Name | Common Tones | Voice Leading | Contextual Inversion |
|------|--------------|---------------|---------------------|
| P (Parallel) | 2 (the fifth) | 1 voice by 1 semitone | Invert around shared P5 |
| L (Leading-tone) | 2 (the m3) | 1 voice by 1 semitone | Invert around shared m3 |
| R (Relative) | 2 (the M3) | 1 voice by 2 semitones | Invert around shared M3 |
| P' (SLIDE) | 1 (the third) | 2 voices by 1 semitone each | Invert around the 3rd alone |
| L' | 1 | 2 voices by 1 semitone each | Invert around non-M3 tone |
| R' | 1 | 2 voices by 2 semitones each | Invert around non-m3 tone |

P', L', and R' are "obverses" of P, L, R: they retain a singleton and flip the dyad.

# Musical Context/Application
Triadic transformations are fundamental to:
1. Neoclassical, neotonal, and minimalist styles
2. Analysis of late-Romantic chromaticism (neo-Riemannian theory)
3. Understanding non-functional triadic progressions
4. Creating coherent progressions without traditional harmonic functions

# Common Confusions
- These transformations always change mode (major to minor or vice versa)
- P, L, R retain two common tones; P', L', R' retain only one
- "Parallel" here means parallel major/minor (same root), not parallel motion
- These differ from functional harmonic relationships (V-I, etc.)
