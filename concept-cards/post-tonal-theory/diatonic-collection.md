# Quick Definition
Any transposition of the seven "white notes" of the piano, sc(013568T), used in post-tonal music without functional harmony and traditional voice leading, often with non-triadic harmonies.

# Formal Definition
The diatonic collection is any transposition of the seven "white notes" of the piano, set class (013568T) (Forte-name 7-35). While this collection is the basic referential source for all Western tonal music (all major scales, natural minor scales, and church modes are diatonic collections), in post-tonal music it is used without functional harmony and traditional voice leading. The diatonic collection acts as a harmonic field from which smaller musical shapes are drawn.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Properties:
- 7 pitch classes
- Set class (013568T), Forte-name 7-35
- 12 distinct transpositions
- 7 modal orderings per transposition

Interval vector: [254361]
- Rich in perfect fifths and major seconds
- Contains one tritone

Structure: Alternating pattern of whole steps and half steps with asymmetric half-step placement.

# Musical Context/Application
Post-tonal diatonic music features:
1. Non-triadic harmonies (especially (0257) and (027) in Stravinsky)
2. Non-functional chord progressions
3. Centricity without tonality
4. Modal orderings without traditional scale-degree functions

# Examples
Example 5-13: Stravinsky's Petrushka, Russian Dance - Uses only "white notes" for first 8 measures (DIA0#), then shifts to 1-sharp collection (DIA1#) when F# replaces F. The modal ordering shifts from G Mixolydian to A Dorian.

Example 5-15: Stravinsky's The Rake's Progress - Diatonic passage based on DIA3# (A Ionian) features (027) on virtually every beat: A-B-E and A-D-E. Non-triadic despite being diatonic.

Example 5-16: Reich's Piano Phase - Uses five notes of DIA2# [B, C#, D, E, F#]. The collection never changes, but rhythmic phase-shifting creates variety.

# Common Confusions
- Diatonic does not mean tonal; post-tonal diatonic music lacks functional harmony
- The collection is distinct from its scalar orderings
- Post-tonal diatonic music often features non-triadic subsets
- 12 collections but 84 possible scales (12 x 7 modes)
