# Quick Definition
The twelve-tone series serves as the fundamental source of structural relations in a piece, shaping the music from the immediate surface to the deepest structural level, and functioning as theme, scale, and motive repository simultaneously.

# Formal Definition
The series is the source of structural relations in a twelve-tone piece. It functions simultaneously as:
1. A **theme**: a recognizable melodic idea that recurs in various ways throughout a piece
2. A **scale**: the basic referential collection from which harmonies and melodies are drawn
3. A **repository of motives**: a large design within which are embedded numerous smaller designs

The series plays a more fundamental role in twelve-tone music than theme, scale, or motive play in tonal music. In tonal music, scales, themes, and motives are part of common musical property shared across pieces and composers. In twelve-tone music, virtually no two pieces use the same series, making each composition contextually unique.

# Musical Context/Application
- The series shapes all dimensions of musical structure: melody, harmony, rhythm, form
- From piece to piece and composer to composer in twelve-tone music, relatively little material is shared
- Twelve-tone music is "contextual" rather than "communal"—each piece defines its own referential world
- The series influences not just note-to-note motion but also trichord-to-trichord and series-to-series relationships
- Composers build specific structures and relationships into the series, which then permeate all 48 forms

# Examples
- Schoenberg's String Quartet No. 4: The series shapes the motivic surface, the harmonic vocabulary, the voice leading, and the large-scale form
- Webern's Concerto, op. 24: The (014) trichord that generates the derived series permeates instrumentation, register, and articulation
- Berg's Violin Concerto: The series embeds major and minor triads, giving the work a quasi-tonal sound

# Common Confusions
- Treating the series as merely a sequence to be "laid out" rather than understanding it as shaping all musical dimensions
- Assuming the series functions identically to tonal scales—it is more fundamental and all-encompassing
- Not recognizing that the series's subset structure (dyads, trichords, tetrachords) is often more audible than the series as a whole
