# Quick Definition
A cycle of six triads (alternating major and minor) created by alternating L and P transformations, containing triads built on three pitch classes related by major third, and using only six pitch classes that form a hexatonic collection.

# Formal Definition
An LP-cycle (also called a hexatonic system) is produced by alternating L (Leading-tone) and P (Parallel) transformations starting from any major or minor triad. The cycle consists of six triads: three major and three minor, built on roots related by major thirds (forming an augmented triad). Each move around the cycle involves changing a single note by one semitone, making the voice leading maximally parsimonious. The six pitch classes used by all triads in the cycle together form one of the four hexatonic collections, sc(014589).

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Starting from C+:
C+ --P--> C- --L--> Ab+ --P--> Ab- --L--> E+ --P--> E- --L--> C+ (returns)

The four LP-cycles/hexatonic systems contain triads on these roots:
1. C, E, Ab (major and minor on each)
2. Db, F, A (major and minor on each)
3. D, F#, Bb (major and minor on each)
4. Eb, G, B (major and minor on each)

Each LP-cycle uses exactly 6 pitch classes = one hexatonic collection (014589).

# Musical Context/Application
LP-cycles are fundamental to:
1. Understanding non-functional triadic progressions
2. Analyzing minimalist opera (Adams, Glass)
3. Late-Romantic chromatic harmony
4. Connecting the hexatonic collection to triadic harmony

# Common Confusions
- Each LP-cycle contains only 6 of the 24 major/minor triads
- The cycle connects triads whose roots form an augmented triad
- LP-cycles and hexatonic collections are closely related but distinct concepts (one is triadic, the other is pitch-class based)
- There are exactly four LP-cycles, corresponding to the four hexatonic collections
