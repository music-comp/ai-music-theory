# Quick Definition
A pitch interval (pi) is the distance between two pitches, measured in semitones within pitch space.

# Core Definition
A pitch interval (abbreviated pi) is the distance between two pitches, measured by the number of semitones between them. A pitch interval is created when we move from pitch to pitch in pitch space. It can be as large as the range of our hearing or as small as a semitone. Pitch intervals may be understood as ordered (indicating direction via +/- signs) or unordered (indicating only absolute distance).

# Prerequisites
- **Pitch** — pitch intervals measure distance between specific pitches
- **Intervals Calculated in Semitones** — the unit of measurement

# Key Properties
1. Measured in pitch space (linear), not pitch-class space (circular)
2. Can be any positive integer (not limited to 0-11)
3. Can be ordered (directed: +/-) or unordered (absolute distance)
4. Preserves compound intervals (a minor ninth is 13, not 1)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Identify two specific pitches (with registers)
2. Count the semitones between them
3. For ordered: prepend + (ascending) or - (descending)
4. For unordered: state the absolute number of semitones

## To Recognize:
1. An interval expressed as a number of semitones between two specific pitches
2. May include a +/- sign (ordered) or may be unsigned (unordered)

# Context & Application
Whether we consider an interval ordered or unordered depends on our analytical interests. Ordered pitch intervals capture the contour of a melodic line (its balance of rising and falling motion). Unordered pitch intervals ignore contour and concentrate on the spaces between pitches. Both are measured in pitch space and therefore preserve registral information.

# Examples
**Example 1-11** (p. 25): A melodic passage from Schoenberg, String Quartet No. 3, first movement, analyzed with both ordered and unordered pitch intervals. The ordered pitch intervals (+1, -11, -1, +3, -13, +1) focus on contour. The unordered pitch intervals (1, 11, 1, 3, 13, 1) focus on spacing.

**From the text**: "When we say that C4 ascends four semitones to E4, we are talking about an ordered pitch interval (opi = +4); when we say that there are four semitones between C4 and E4, we are talking about an unordered pitch interval (upi = 4)."

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Reducing a pitch interval by mod 12
  **Correction**: Pitch intervals preserve compound intervals. A pitch interval of 13 remains 13; it is only when converting to a pitch-class interval that mod 12 applies.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Not specifying whether an interval is ordered or unordered
  **Clarification**: Always specify. opi = +4 (ascending major third) is distinct from opi = -4 (descending major third), while upi = 4 in both cases.
