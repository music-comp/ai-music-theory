# Quick Definition
Aggregate formation refers to the various methods by which all twelve pitch classes are collected, including single series statements, hexachordal combination, trichordal combination, and vertical arrangements in arrays.

# Formal Definition
*Aggregate formation* encompasses the various techniques composers use to complete collections of all twelve pitch classes. In twelve-tone music, the aggregate is the basic harmonic unit. Methods include:
- **Serial statement**: A single presentation of the twelve-tone series
- **Hexachordal combinatoriality**: Two complementary hexachords from different series forms
- **Trichordal combinatoriality**: Four complementary trichords from array columns
- **Vertical aggregates**: Pitch classes aligned harmonically from multiple sources
- **Multiple layers**: Simultaneous aggregate formation in different dimensions

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
**Methods of forming aggregates:**
1. Series = 12 consecutive pcs = 1 aggregate
2. H1 + H2 (from combinatorial pair) = 1 aggregate
3. T1 + T2 + T3 + T4 (from array column) = 1 aggregate
4. Multiple simultaneous methods in Babbitt's arrays:
   - Within rows: 12 pcs linearly
   - Between row-pairs: hexachordal combination
   - Within columns: trichordal combination

# Musical Context/Application
- Aggregate completion often marks structural boundaries
- Different formation methods create different textures
- Schoenberg emphasizes hexachordal formation
- Babbitt emphasizes multi-dimensional formation
- Stravinsky often avoids systematic aggregate formation
- Rate and manner of aggregate completion affects pacing

# Examples
- Schoenberg, String Quartet No. 4: Hexachordal aggregates between P0 and RI5
- Babbitt, String Quartet No. 2: Aggregates in rows, between row-pairs, and in columns simultaneously
- Stravinsky, Requiem Canticles: Often focuses on common tones rather than aggregate completion

# Common Confusions
- Assuming all twelve-tone music emphasizes aggregate formation equally
- Not recognizing multiple simultaneous aggregates
- Thinking aggregate completion is always audible—it's often subtle
- Confusing aggregate formation with series statement—they can differ
