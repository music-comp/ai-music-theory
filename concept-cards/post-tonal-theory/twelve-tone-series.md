# Quick Definition
A twelve-tone series (or row) is an ordered succession of all twelve pitch classes, with each pitch class occurring exactly once, serving as the basic referential structure for twelve-tone music.

# Formal Definition
A *series* is a line (ordered succession) of pitch classes. Unlike an unordered pitch-class set, which retains its identity regardless of how its elements are arranged, a series has a fixed order—changing the order changes the identity of the series. A *twelve-tone series* specifically contains one of each of the twelve pitch classes (with each occurring exactly once). Music that uses such a series as its basic, referential structure is known as *twelve-tone music*.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
- A twelve-tone series is an ordered 12-tuple containing each integer 0-11 exactly once
- Example: (2, 1, 9, 10, 5, 3, 4, 0, 8, 7, 6, 11) - Schoenberg's String Quartet No. 4
- Recognition: Look for a melody or succession presenting all 12 pitch classes without repetition
- The series is usually presented explicitly at the beginning of a piece

# Musical Context/Application
In twelve-tone music, the series plays multiple roles:
- Like a **theme**: a recognizable "tune" that recurs in various ways throughout a piece
- Like a **scale**: the basic referential collection from which harmonies and melodies are drawn
- Like a **repository of motives**: a large design within which are embedded numerous smaller designs

The series plays a more fundamental role in twelve-tone music than theme, scale, or motive play in tonal music. Virtually no two pieces use the same series, making twelve-tone music highly contextual rather than communal.

# Examples
- Schoenberg, String Quartet No. 4, first movement: The melody in the first violin presents all twelve pitch classes in a clear, forthright way beginning on D (pitch-class 2), designated P2
- Webern, "Wie bin ich froh!": The melody in measures 1-5 presents the twelve-tone series for the piece, beginning on G (P7)
- The series shapes the music from the immediate surface to the deepest structural level

# Common Confusions
- Confusing series (ordered) with set (unordered): A set retains identity regardless of order; a series changes identity if order changes
- Assuming the series must appear melodically: It can be distributed among multiple voices or presented harmonically
- Thinking the series is just "the notes" rather than understanding it as an ordering that shapes all musical dimensions
