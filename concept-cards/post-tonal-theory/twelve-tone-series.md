# Quick Definition
A twelve-tone series (or row) is an ordered succession of all twelve pitch classes, each occurring exactly once, that serves as the basic referential structure for twelve-tone music.

# Core Definition
A *series* is a line (ordered succession) of pitch classes. Unlike an unordered pitch-class set, which retains its identity regardless of how its elements are arranged, a series has a fixed order -- changing the order changes the identity of the series. A *twelve-tone series* specifically contains one of each of the twelve pitch classes. Music that uses such a series as its basic, referential structure is known as *twelve-tone music* (Straus, p. 310).

A series can be any length, but "by far the most common is a series consisting of all twelve pitch classes (with one of each)" (p. 310). Shorter series are also possible, as in Stravinsky's five-note series for *In Memoriam Dylan Thomas*.

# Prerequisites
- **Pitch class** -- the series is an ordering of pitch classes, not pitches
- **Ordered vs. unordered sets** -- the distinction between set (unordered) and series (ordered) is foundational

# Key Properties
1. Contains each of the twelve pitch classes exactly once
2. Order is essential to identity: reordering creates a different series form
3. Serves simultaneously as theme, scale, and motive repository
4. Virtually no two pieces share the same series, making twelve-tone music contextual rather than communal
5. Shapes music "from the immediate surface to the deepest structural level" (p. 311)

# Construction / Recognition
- A twelve-tone series is an ordered 12-tuple containing each integer 0--11 exactly once
- Example: P2 = (2, 1, 9, 10, 5, 3, 4, 0, 8, 7, 6, 11) -- Schoenberg's String Quartet No. 4
- Look for a melody or succession presenting all 12 pitch classes without repetition, usually at the beginning of a piece
- The first explicit presentation is typically designated the prime form

# Context & Application
The series plays multiple, overlapping roles in twelve-tone music:
- Like a **theme**: a recognizable succession that recurs in various transformations
- Like a **scale**: the basic referential collection for harmonies and melodies
- Like a **repository of motives**: smaller designs embedded within the larger ordering

The series is more fundamental than any of these individual roles in tonal music, because tonal music is "relatively communal" (shared scales, triads) while twelve-tone music is "relatively contextual" (each piece has its own series).

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 311): Schoenberg, String Quartet No. 4, first movement -- the melody in the first violin presents all twelve pitch classes clearly, beginning on D (pitch-class 2), designated P2. Notes are numbered 1--12 in order of occurrence.

**Example 2** (p. 319): Webern, "Wie bin ich froh!" -- the melody in measures 1--5 presents the series beginning on G, designated P7, then repeats its first four notes.

# Common Errors
- Treating series and set as interchangeable: a set is unordered, a series is ordered
- Assuming the series must appear as a single melodic line: it can be distributed across voices and registers

# Common Confusions
- **Series vs. set**: A set retains identity regardless of ordering; a series changes identity if reordered
- **Series as "the notes" vs. series as ordering**: The series is not just twelve pitch classes (every series contains the same twelve); it is a specific *ordering* that defines structural relationships
