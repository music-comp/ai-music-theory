# Quick Definition

Patterns generated by repeatedly transposing a pitch class by a single interval, creating six distinct cycle types (C1-C6) that underlie many melodic and harmonic structures in post-tonal music.

# Formal Definition

An **interval cycle** is the sequence of pitch classes produced when starting from any pitch class and moving repeatedly by a single interval (measured in semitones). There are six interval cycles, designated C1 through C6, corresponding to intervals 1 through 6 semitones. Each cycle type has characteristic properties:

- **C1** (semitone cycle): Moves through all 12 pitch classes; only one distinct cycle
- **C2** (whole-tone cycle): Two distinct cycles (even and odd pitch classes)
- **C3** (minor third cycle): Three distinct cycles (the three diminished seventh chords)
- **C4** (major third cycle): Four distinct cycles (the four augmented triads)
- **C5** (perfect fourth/fifth cycle): Moves through all 12 pitch classes; only one distinct cycle
- **C6** (tritone cycle): Six distinct cycles (the six tritone dyads)

Moving clockwise around the cycle involves the stated interval; counterclockwise involves the complement (e.g., C1 clockwise = i1, counterclockwise = i11).

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition

**Number of distinct cycles for interval i:**
Number of cycles = GCD(i, 12)

**Size of each cycle (number of pitch classes):**
Cycle size = 12 / GCD(i, 12)

| Interval | GCD(i,12) | Number of Cycles | PCs per Cycle |
|----------|-----------|------------------|---------------|
| 1 (C1)   | 1         | 1                | 12            |
| 2 (C2)   | 2         | 2                | 6             |
| 3 (C3)   | 3         | 3                | 4             |
| 4 (C4)   | 4         | 4                | 3             |
| 5 (C5)   | 1         | 1                | 12            |
| 6 (C6)   | 6         | 6                | 2             |

Subscript notation identifies pitch-class content:
- C2_0 contains pitch-class 0 (even integers)
- C2_1 contains pitch-class 1 (odd integers)
- C3_0, C3_1, C3_2 distinguish the three diminished seventh chords

# Musical Context/Application

Interval cycles provide:
- **Melodic frameworks**: Guiding linear motions in post-tonal music
- **Harmonic foundations**: Generating cyclic sets and maximally even harmonies
- **Referential collections**: The large collections of Chapter 5 derive from cycles:
  - Chromatic scale = C1
  - Whole-tone scales = two C2s
  - Octatonic = combination of two C3s
  - Hexatonic = combination of two C4s
  - Diatonic = seven-note segment of C5
- **Compositional resources**: Systematic means for organizing pitch material

Interval cycles are "generic shapes" (unlike piece-specific motives in composing-out) that guide motion across many post-tonal works.

# Examples

**Bartok, String Quartet No. 6, fourth movement** (Example 4-6):
- Cadential approach to C-minor triad
- Cello: descending C1 (E-Eb-D-Db-C)
- First violin: ascending C2 (A-B-C#-Eb)
- Main theme shaped by C3

**Varese, Density 21.5** (Example 4-7):
- First ten measures: principal melodic boundary tones follow C3_1 (C#-E-G-Bb)
- Melody gradually rises an octave from C#4 to Db5
- Arrival on D5 (fff) signals harmonic shift

**Ives, "The Cage"** (Example 4-8):
- Melody: five-note segments of C2 (whole-tone cycles)
- Accompanying chords: five-note segments of C5 (circle of fifths)
- Registral arrangement reveals cyclic origins

**Bartok, String Quartet No. 3** (Example 4-9):
- Opening chord: four-note segment of C1 (C#-D-D#-E)
- Opening melody: complementary eight-note segment of C1
- Final chord: four-note segment of C5 (C#-G#-D#-A#)

# Common Confusions

- **C5 vs. C7**: Both traverse all 12 pitch classes; C5 (fourths) and C7 (fifths) are inverses of each other and generate the same cycle
- **Subscript notation**: The subscript identifies which pitch class is contained in the cycle, not the starting point
- **Cycles vs. scales**: A cycle is a theoretical construct; a scale is a realized, ordered pitch collection (though they may coincide)
- **Single interval vs. alternating intervals**: Interval cycles use one repeated interval; combination cycles alternate two intervals
