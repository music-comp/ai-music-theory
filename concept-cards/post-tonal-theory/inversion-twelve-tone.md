# Quick Definition
The inversion (I) of a series inverts each pitch class in the series (0 inverts to 0, 1 to 11, 2 to 10, etc.), producing an interval succession where each interval is replaced by its complement mod 12 while maintaining the same order.

# Formal Definition
The *inversion* of the series involves inverting each pitch class: pitch-class 0 inverts to 0, 1 inverts to 11, 2 inverts to 10, 3 inverts to 9, and so on. The interval succession is the same as that of the prime ordering, but each interval is replaced by its complement mod 12. The intervals are the same as in the retrograde, but in reverse order. The twelve resulting series forms are called I0, I1, I2, ... I11, where I_n is the inverted ordering beginning on pitch-class n.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
- I_n(x) = n - x (mod 12) for each pitch class x in the series
- I_n begins on pitch-class n
- Intervals: same order as P, but each interval i becomes (12 - i) mod 12
- Example: If P has interval 11 between notes 1-2, I has interval 1 between notes 1-2
- I_n is used both as an operation and as a series name (context clarifies meaning)

# Musical Context/Application
- One of the four basic orderings (P, R, I, RI) of the twelve-tone series
- Series related by inversion (P and I, R and RI) have complementary intervals in the same order
- Inversionally related series often share segmental subsets (invariants)
- Combinatoriality frequently involves pairing P and I forms
- The I-form can create balance and symmetry in twelve-tone compositions

# Examples
- Schoenberg, String Quartet No. 4: I7 is the inversion starting on pitch-class 7 (G)
- Webern, "Wie bin ich froh!": I7 is the inversion of P7, and both are used in the song
- The ordered pitch-class intervals in I are the complements (mod 12) of those in P, in the same order

# Common Confusions
- Confusing the operation I_n with the series name I_n—the former maps pitch classes, the latter names a series form
- Expecting the inverted series to have "opposite" contour—it's pitch-class inversion, which may or may not produce pitch inversion
- Forgetting that inversion changes intervals to their complements (1↔11, 2↔10, etc.)
