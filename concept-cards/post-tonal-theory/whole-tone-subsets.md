# Quick Definition
The limited set of smaller collections contained within the whole-tone collection, featuring only three trichord types, three tetrachord types, and one pentachord type, all containing exclusively even intervals.

# Formal Definition
Due to the whole-tone collection's high symmetry and restricted interval content (only ic2, ic4, and ic6), its subset structure is predictably limited and redundant. The collection contains only three different trichord-types, three different tetrachord-types, and a single pentachord-type. These subsets form an inclusion lattice showing how smaller sets are contained within larger ones.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Complete subset structure of the whole-tone collection:

Trichords (3 types):
- (024): Three M2s stacked
- (026): M2 + tritone
- (048): Augmented triad

Tetrachords (3 types):
- (0246): Four-note whole-tone segment
- (0248): Three M2s + M3
- (0268): Two tritones, M2 apart

Pentachord (1 type):
- (02468): Five-note whole-tone segment

Hexachord:
- (02468T): The complete whole-tone collection itself

All subsets contain only even intervals (ic2, ic4, ic6).

# Musical Context/Application
Understanding whole-tone subsets helps:
1. Identify whole-tone contexts through characteristic harmonies
2. Recognize the augmented triad (048) as a whole-tone subset
3. Understand the limited harmonic vocabulary available
4. Analyze the distinctive "floating" quality of whole-tone music

# Common Confusions
- The subset structure is very limited compared to diatonic or octatonic
- No triads except augmented (no major or minor triads)
- No half-steps in any subset
- The limited variety is a compositional constraint and characteristic sound
