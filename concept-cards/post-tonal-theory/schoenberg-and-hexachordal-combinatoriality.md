# Quick Definition
Schoenberg's mature twelve-tone practice uses I-combinatorial series to create aggregates between paired series forms, organizing large-scale structure through "twelve-tone areas" that function analogously to tonal keys.

# Core Definition
"In his mature twelve-tone music, Schoenberg always constructs series in which the two hexachords are related by inversion" (Straus, p. 338). Combinatoriality provides three organizational functions: (1) local connections between series forms through aggregate formation, (2) common-tone links from phrase to phrase, and (3) large-scale harmonic structure through "twelve-tone areas." Each area contains four series forms (P, I, R, RI) sharing hexachordal content, analogous to a tonal key.

# Prerequisites
- **Hexachordal combinatoriality** -- the foundational technique
- **Twelve-tone areas** -- the large-scale organizational principle

# Key Properties
1. Series always constructed with I-related hexachords
2. Aggregates form between paired series forms
3. Common-tone connections link consecutive forms
4. 48 forms divided into 12 areas of 4 forms each
5. Areas function like keys for large-scale harmonic motion
6. "Modulation" from area to area creates structural drama

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the combinatorial pair (P_m and I_n)
- Group forms into areas sharing hexachordal content
- Track area motion through a composition

# Context & Application
Schoenberg represents the second of five major approaches surveyed in the chapter. His combinatorial approach creates both local (aggregate/common-tone) and global (area modulation) organization.

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 338--344): Schoenberg, String Quartet No. 4 -- P2 and I7 are combinatorial. The first movement opens in A2, modulates to A4, reaches climactic A8 at m. 165.

**Example 2** (pp. 363--369): Schoenberg, Piano Piece, op. 33a -- uses only P10, I3, R10, RI3 (area A10) for first 23 measures. Large-scale progression: A10-A0-A5-A10, resembling I-II-V-I.

# Common Errors
- Expecting area modulation to sound like tonal key change
- Not recognizing that areas share all 12 pcs

# Common Confusions
- **Area vs. key**: Both organize large-scale structure, but areas differ in hexachordal content, not total pitch-class content
