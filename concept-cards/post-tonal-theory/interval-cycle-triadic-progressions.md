# Quick Definition
Progressions of triads whose roots follow an interval cycle, creating systematic, non-functional triadic motion through pitch-class space.

# Formal Definition
Beyond the triadic transformations (P, L, R, etc.), progressions of triads in post-tonal music may be organized by interval cycles, where the roots of successive triads follow a consistent intervallic pattern. These progressions create coherent motion without traditional harmonic function, often traversing all twelve pitch classes or a complete cycle before returning to the starting point.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Common interval-cycle triadic progressions:

1-cycle (chromatic): Roots descend/ascend by semitone
- Example: C- to B- to Bb- to A-... (all 12 minor triads)

3-cycle: Roots move by minor third
- Creates diminished-seventh-related progression
- 4 triads complete the cycle

4-cycle: Roots move by major third
- Creates augmented-triad-related progression
- 3 triads complete the cycle
- Related to LP-cycles

5-cycle (fifths): Roots move by perfect fifth
- Traditional "cycle of fifths" without functional implications

# Musical Context/Application
Interval-cycle triadic progressions provide:
1. Systematic organization without functional harmony
2. Large-scale directional motion
3. Complete traversal of triadic space
4. Alternative to transformation-based organization

# Examples
Example 4-35: Crumb's Makrokosmos, "Primeval Sounds (Genesis I)" - All twelve minor triads arranged by 1-cycle. Right-hand triad roots descend chromatically through the entire octave. Left-hand grace-note triads parallel at the tritone, also tracing a 1-cycle.

Example 4-36: Britten's Serenade, "Sonnet" - Major triads arranged symmetrically around D, creating both interval-cycle and inversional-symmetry organization.

# Common Confusions
- Interval-cycle progressions differ from transformation-based progressions (though they may overlap)
- The cycle concerns root motion, not voice leading
- Triads of the same quality (all major or all minor) create clearer cycle progressions
- The cycle may be complete or partial
