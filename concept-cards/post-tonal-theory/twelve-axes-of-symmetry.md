# Quick Definition
The complete set of twelve possible inversional axes in pitch-class space, identified either by the sum of balanced pitch-class pairs or by the axis poles themselves.

# Formal Definition
There are exactly twelve axes of inversional symmetry in pitch-class space, corresponding to the twelve possible index sums (0-11) that balanced pitch-class pairs can produce. Each axis passes through two poles a tritone apart. For even sums, the axis passes through two pitch classes; for odd sums, the axis passes between two pairs of adjacent pitch classes.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
The twelve axes identified by sum n (poles at n/2 and n/2 + 6):

| Sum | Axis Poles | Notation |
|-----|-----------|----------|
| 0 | C-F# | 0-6 |
| 1 | C/Db-F#/G | 0/1-6/7 |
| 2 | Db-G | 1-7 |
| 3 | Db/D-G/Ab | 1/2-7/8 |
| 4 | D-Ab | 2-8 |
| 5 | D/Eb-Ab/A | 2/3-8/9 |
| 6 | Eb-A | 3-9 |
| 7 | Eb/E-A/Bb | 3/4-9/10 |
| 8 | E-Bb | 4-10 |
| 9 | E/F-Bb/B | 4/5-10/11 |
| 10 | F-B | 5-11 |
| 11 | F/F#-B/C | 5/6-11/0 |

Even sums produce axes through single pitch classes; odd sums produce axes between pitch-class pairs.

# Musical Context/Application
The twelve inversional axes have the potential to function like the twelve major/minor keys of traditional tonality:
1. Music can be organized around a particular axis
2. Music can "modulate" from one axis to another
3. Axis changes can articulate form
4. Motion between axes can be motivically related to surface intervals

# Examples
Example 5-10 illustrates all twelve axes of inversional symmetry on pitch-class clockfaces.

Example 5-11: Bartók's Mikrokosmos no. 141 "Subject and Reflection" - The first phrase is symmetrical around pitch-axis E4 (pc axis E-Bb). At measure 15, the axis shifts to B5 (seven semitones higher), composing-out the initial melodic idea (T7).

Example 5-12: Bartók's Sonata for Two Pianos and Percussion - The axis shifts from 0-6 (C-F#) to 1-7 (Db-G), moving up one semitone like the initial melodic gesture.

# Common Confusions
- Six of the twelve axes pass through pitch classes; six pass between pitch classes
- The axis poles are always a tritone apart
- The twelve axes exhaust all possibilities; there are no more
- Axes are related to each other by transposition (T1 shifts the axis by half a semitone in each direction)
