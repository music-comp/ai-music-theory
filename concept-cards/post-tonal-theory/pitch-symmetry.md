# Quick Definition
An arrangement of pitch classes in register such that the pitch intervals form a palindrome; a realization of inversional symmetry in concrete pitch space rather than abstract pitch-class space.

# Formal Definition
Pitch symmetry occurs when an inversionally symmetrical pitch-class set is arranged in register so that the pitch intervals (measured in semitones) reading from bottom to top are identical to those reading from top to bottom. This makes the symmetry audible and visually apparent on the staff.

Any inversionally symmetrical pitch-class set can be arranged pitch-symmetrically, but composers often choose not to do so, leaving the symmetry to exist only in the more abstract pitch-class space.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
For a chord to be pitch-symmetrical:
- Measure pitch intervals (not pitch-class intervals) from lowest to highest note
- The sequence of intervals must be a palindrome
- There will be a central pitch (for odd cardinality) or central interval (for even cardinality) that serves as the axis

Example: A pitch-symmetrical arrangement of [C, D, E]:
- C4 - E4 - G#4 (intervals: 4 - 4) is pitch-symmetrical
- C4 - E5 - G#4 would NOT be pitch-symmetrical

# Musical Context/Application
Pitch symmetry creates:
- Audible balance and stability
- A clearly perceivable center or axis
- Strong registral architecture

Composers may choose pitch symmetry for dramatic effect, or deliberately avoid it to keep the symmetry abstract. The choice between pitch-symmetrical and pitch-class-symmetrical arrangements is a significant compositional decision affecting how listeners perceive the harmony.

# Examples
From Example 3-15b: Three hypothetical pitch-symmetrical arrangements of sc(01348):
- All five notes arranged so pitch intervals form palindromes
- E serves as the center of symmetry in each arrangement
- Each arrangement realizes the same pitch-class symmetry in different registral configurations

From Example 3-17: Varese, Hyperprism - [C, C#, D] arranged with C# in the middle register:
- D enters 23 semitones below middle C#
- C enters 23 semitones above middle C#
- Creates vast pitch space articulated symmetrically
- Grace notes reinforce symmetry: F (4 semitones above C#) balanced by A (4 semitones below C#)

# Common Confusions
- Confusing pitch symmetry (specific registral arrangement) with pitch-class symmetry (abstract property)
- Thinking all inversionally symmetrical sets must be arranged pitch-symmetrically (they can be, but need not be)
- Not distinguishing between the axis of symmetry in pitch space (a specific pitch) vs. pitch-class space (a pitch class or axis between pitch classes)
