# Quick Definition
A naming system for the two whole-tone collections: WT0 (containing pitch-class C, the "even" collection) and WT1 (containing pitch-class C#, the "odd" collection).

# Formal Definition
WT nomenclature identifies the two whole-tone collections by their lowest pitch-class member: WT0 contains pitch-class 0 (C) and consists entirely of even pitch-class integers; WT1 contains pitch-class 1 (C#) and consists entirely of odd pitch-class integers. These are sometimes called the "even" and "odd" collections based on the parity of their pitch-class integers.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
The two whole-tone collections:

| Name | Alternative | Contains PC | All Pitch Classes |
|------|------------|-------------|-------------------|
| WT0 | "Even" | 0 (C) | 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 |
| WT1 | "Odd" | 1 (C#) | 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 |

Quick identification:
- If collection contains C (pc 0): WT0
- If collection contains C# (pc 1) but not C: WT1

Relationship: WT0 and WT1 are complements and related by T1

# Musical Context/Application
WT nomenclature provides:
1. Simple, unambiguous identification (only two choices)
2. Clear indication of collection change (WT0 to WT1)
3. Recognition of parity relationships
4. Parallel structure to OCT and HEX nomenclature

# Common Confusions
- The subscript (0 or 1) indicates which collection, not a starting note
- WT0 is not "the whole-tone scale on C"; it is the collection containing C
- Any note of either collection can function as centric
- The two collections are pitch-class complements (together they exhaust all 12 pcs)
