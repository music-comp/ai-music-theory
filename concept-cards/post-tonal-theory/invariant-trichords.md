# Quick Definition
Invariant trichords are three-note collections that remain intact when the series is transformed -- either by mapping onto each other (two equivalent trichords) or by mapping onto themselves (inversionally symmetrical trichords).

# Core Definition
The same principles that apply to invariant dyads extend to trichords. If two trichords in a series are related by I_n, inverting the series by I_n causes them to map onto each other. If a trichord is inversionally symmetrical, "at some level of inversion it will remain intact by mapping onto itself" (Straus, p. 328). Trichordal invariance creates motivic continuity across series forms, linking melodic statements that might otherwise seem unrelated.

# Prerequisites
- **Invariant dyads** -- the same principles extended to larger collections
- **Index number** -- determines which transformation preserves the trichords

# Key Properties
1. Two equivalent trichords (related by I_n) swap positions under I_n
2. Inversionally symmetrical trichords (e.g., (048)) can map onto themselves
3. The inversional relationship extends to pitch space (not just pitch-class space)
4. Provides "a nice way of hearing a path through twelve-tone music" (p. 331)

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify trichords in the series (especially segmental subsets)
2. Check if any two trichords are related by inversion
3. Calculate the index number relating them
4. For inversionally symmetrical trichords, find the I_n level for self-mapping
5. Apply that I_n to the series: invariant trichords are preserved

# Context & Application
Trichordal invariance links melodic statements associated by inversion, providing both an inversional relationship and shared segmental subsets. The augmented triad (048), being maximally symmetrical, always maps onto itself or an equivalent trichord.

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 330--331, Exx. 6-22 through 6-24): Berg, Violin Concerto -- the series contains two augmented triads (048) and one (024). Under I2, the two (048)s exchange positions and the (024) maps onto itself. P7 and I7 (related by I2, since 7+7=14=2 mod 12) share these trichords. The rising figure (P7) and descending figure (I7) are linked by their shared segmental subsets.

# Common Errors
- Expecting trichords to remain in the same order position (they swap)
- Confusing set-class invariance (always preserved) with pitch-class invariance (requires specific I_n)

# Common Confusions
- **Self-mapping vs. mutual mapping**: Inversionally symmetrical sets can map onto themselves; non-symmetrical sets map onto equivalent sets
- **Pitch inversion vs. pc inversion**: In Berg's example, the I-form is a pitch inversion as well as a pc inversion, with ordered pitch intervals simply reversed
