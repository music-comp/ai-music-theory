# Quick Definition
An ordered pitch interval (opi) measures the directed distance between two pitches, with a plus sign for ascending and minus sign for descending motion.

# Formal Definition
An ordered pitch interval (abbreviated opi) specifies both the size and direction of an interval between two pitches. A plus sign indicates an ascending interval, and a minus sign indicates a descending interval. Ordered pitch intervals capture the contour of a melodic line by indicating whether each successive interval rises or falls.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Calculation of ordered pitch interval:
- opi = pitch2 - pitch1 (using semitone values)
- Positive result = ascending interval
- Negative result = descending interval

Example calculations:
- C4 to E4: opi = +4 (ascending major third)
- E4 to C4: opi = -4 (descending major third)
- C4 to C5: opi = +12 (ascending octave)

# Musical Context/Application
Ordered pitch intervals are useful when analyzing melodic contour and the specific shape of a musical line. They distinguish between ascending and descending motion of the same size and preserve compound intervals (those larger than an octave) without reduction.

# Examples
**Example 1-11** (Schoenberg, String Quartet No. 3, first movement): A melodic line analyzed as ordered pitch intervals: <+1, -11, -1, +3, -13, +1>. These intervals focus attention on the contour, showing alternation between small ascending intervals and larger descending intervals.

# Common Confusions
Ordered pitch intervals preserve both direction and absolute size. An opi of +4 is different from an opi of -4, and both are different from an opi of +16 (ascending compound major third). This is more specific than ordered pitch-class intervals, which reduce to mod 12.
