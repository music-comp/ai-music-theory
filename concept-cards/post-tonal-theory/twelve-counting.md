# Quick Definition
Twelve-counting is the analytical technique of identifying which series form is being used at each point in a twelve-tone piece by numbering notes according to their order position in the series.

# Formal Definition
*Twelve-counting* is a method for identifying the series forms in a twelve-tone work, providing a low-level map of the composition. The first step is to identify the series, which is usually presented explicitly at the beginning of the piece (though sometimes detective work is needed). The usual procedure involves identifying the order position each pitch occupies in the series form (1 is first, 2 is second, etc.). This can be done by constructing a matrix and locating forms, or more directly by analyzing intervallic relationships between known forms.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
**Identification Process:**
1. Identify the series (usually from the opening statement)
2. Construct a 12×12 matrix (optional but helpful)
3. Look at the first few notes of an unknown passage
4. Compare with the 48 possible forms to find a match
5. Number each note according to its order position (1-12)

**Using intervallic relationships:**
- If intervals match P but in reverse: it's an R-form
- If intervals are complements of P in same order: it's an I-form
- If intervals match P in reverse order: it's an RI-form
- Add corresponding pitch classes to find the index number

# Musical Context/Application
- Provides a structural outline of the piece
- Reveals how the composer moves from series form to series form
- Shows how notes may overlap between forms (one note serving as both last of one form and first of next)
- Gives context for understanding intervallic and motivic relationships
- Essential first step in detailed twelve-tone analysis

# Examples
- Webern, "Wie bin ich froh!": The melody presents P7, starting on G; twelve-count shows order positions 1-12
- To identify the accompaniment, compare its intervals to P7's intervals
- If intervals match P7 in reverse order, starting from the end, it's an RI-form
- Calculate index number: 6 + 8 = 14 → 2 (mod 12), so accompaniment is RI7 (since 7 + 7 = 14 → 2)

# Common Confusions
- Assuming every note belongs to exactly one series form—notes can overlap between forms
- Forgetting that multiple series forms may be sounding simultaneously
- Not recognizing when the series is presented incompletely or with notes distributed among voices
- Thinking twelve-counting alone reveals musical meaning—it's just a structural outline
