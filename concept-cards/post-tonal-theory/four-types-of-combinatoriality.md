# Quick Definition
The four types of hexachordal combinatoriality are I-combinatoriality (hexachords related by inversion to each other), P-combinatoriality (hexachords related by transposition to each other), R-combinatoriality (hexachords that map onto themselves by transposition), and RI-combinatoriality (hexachords that map onto themselves by inversion).

# Formal Definition
The four kinds of hexachordal combinatoriality are determined by the capacity of a hexachord for transposing or inverting onto its complement or onto itself:

1. **I-combinatoriality**: H1 and H2 are I-related to each other (Schoenberg's primary method)
2. **P-combinatoriality**: H1 and H2 are T-related to each other
3. **R-combinatoriality**: H1 and H2 are T-related to themselves
4. **RI-combinatoriality**: H1 and H2 are I-related to themselves

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
**I-combinatoriality**: If I_n(H1) = H2, then P combines with I_(index) to form aggregates
**P-combinatoriality**: If T_n(H1) = H2, then P combines with P_n to form aggregates
**R-combinatoriality**: If T_n(H1) = H1, then P combines with R_n to form aggregates (trivial at T0)
**RI-combinatoriality**: If I_n(H1) = H1, then P combines with RI_n to form aggregates

# Musical Context/Application
- Not all hexachords possess all types of combinatoriality
- Schoenberg primarily used I-combinatoriality
- P-combinatoriality is rarer (only one hexachord type)
- RI-combinatoriality appears in Z-related hexachord pairs
- The combinatoriality chart (Example 6-37) lists all combinatorial hexachords

# Examples
- I-combinatoriality: Most of Schoenberg's mature twelve-tone works
- P-combinatoriality: Hexachord (013458) has this property at one level
- R-combinatoriality: Trivial at T0 for all hexachords; (013679) has it at non-trivial level
- RI-combinatoriality: Z-related hexachord pairs like (012348)/(012456)

# Common Confusions
- Confusing the four types—each involves different mappings (complement vs. self, T vs. I)
- Thinking all hexachords are combinatorial—only specific hexachord types have these properties
- Forgetting R-combinatoriality at T0 is trivial (any hexachord maps onto itself)
- Not recognizing that a hexachord can have multiple types of combinatoriality
