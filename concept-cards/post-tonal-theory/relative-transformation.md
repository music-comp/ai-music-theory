# Quick Definition
A triadic transformation relating major and minor triads that share a major third, where the root of the major triad becomes the third of the minor triad (and vice versa), with one voice moving by whole step.

# Formal Definition
The R (Relative) transformation relates a major triad and a minor triad that contain the same major third. The root of the major triad becomes the third of the minor triad, and vice versa. The transformation is a contextual inversion around the shared major third, with one voice moving by two semitones (a whole step). R is its own inverse: R(R(x)) = x.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
R transformation properties:
- Common tones: 2 (the notes forming the major third)
- Voice leading: 1 voice moves by 2 semitones
- Mode change: major <-> minor

Examples:
- R(C+) = A- [C and E are common; G moves to A]
- R(A-) = C+ [A and C are common; E moves up to G... wait, that's backwards]

Correction - R(A-) = C+: [C and E are common in the result]

Voice-leading diagram for C+ to A-:
```
C -> C (common)
E -> E (common)
G -> A (up 2 semitones)
```

The name "Relative" refers to the relative major/minor relationship.

# Musical Context/Application
The R transformation is important in:
1. PLR-cycles (cycles sharing a common tone)
2. Relative major/minor relationships outside functional tonality
3. Chains of triadic transformations
4. Neo-Riemannian analysis

# Common Confusions
- R relates triads whose roots are a minor third apart (C+ and A-)
- R involves whole-step motion (2 semitones), making it slightly less parsimonious than P or L
- R corresponds to the traditional relative major/minor relationship (C major and A minor share a key signature)
