# Quick Definition

The interval cycle produced by moving repeatedly by tritone (interval 6), generating six distinct cycles, each containing only two pitch classes (a tritone dyad).

# Formal Definition

The **6-cycle (C6)** is the interval cycle generated by repeated transposition by interval class 6 (the tritone). Because GCD(6, 12) = 6, there are exactly six distinct 6-cycles, each containing only two pitch classes:

- **C6_0**: {0, 6} = {C, F#/Gb}
- **C6_1**: {1, 7} = {C#, G}
- **C6_2**: {2, 8} = {D, G#/Ab}
- **C6_3**: {3, 9} = {D#/Eb, A}
- **C6_4**: {4, 10} = {E, A#/Bb}
- **C6_5**: {5, 11} = {F, B}

Each C6 corresponds to one of the six tritone dyads. The tritone is unique as the only interval that is its own complement (6 + 6 = 12 mod 12).

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition

**Properties of C6:**
- Interval: 6 semitones (tritone)
- Number of distinct cycles: GCD(6, 12) = 6
- Pitch classes per cycle: 12/6 = 2
- Set class of complete cycle: sc(06) = tritone dyad

**The six cycles:**
- C6_0: 0 - 6 - (0) [C - F# - (C)]
- C6_1: 1 - 7 - (1) [C# - G - (C#)]
- C6_2: 2 - 8 - (2) [D - Ab - (D)]
- C6_3: 3 - 9 - (3) [Eb - A - (Eb)]
- C6_4: 4 - 10 - (4) [E - Bb - (E)]
- C6_5: 5 - 11 - (5) [F - B - (F)]

**Relationship to other collections:**
- C6s can combine to produce whole-tone or octatonic collections
- Three non-adjacent C6s = whole-tone scale (e.g., C6_0 + C6_2 + C6_4)
- Various combinations of C6s relate to larger symmetrical collections

**Relationship to C3:**
Every other note in a C3 produces a C6:
- C3_0: C-Eb-Gb-A → every other: C-Gb (C6_0) or Eb-A (C6_3)

# Musical Context/Application

The 6-cycle provides:
- **Tritone dyads**: The most unstable/dissonant dyad in traditional harmony
- **Maximally even dyad**: The tritone divides the octave exactly in half
- **Symmetric division**: sc(06) is the only dyad that maps onto itself under T6
- **Combinatorial building block**: C6s combine to form larger collections

The C6 is central to:
- Tritone relationships in tonal and post-tonal music
- Symmetrical pitch structures
- Combination with other cycles to form larger collections
- Bartok's "axis" system based on tritone relationships

# Examples

**Maximal evenness** (Example 4-10):
- The C6 harmony sc(06) is the maximally even dyad
- If placing two notes as far apart as possible on the pitch-class clock, they will be a tritone apart
- The "nearly even" version is sc(05), the perfect fourth/fifth

**Derivation of larger collections:**
- C6_0 + C6_2 + C6_4 = {0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10} = whole-tone scale (C2_0)
- C6_1 + C6_3 + C6_5 = {1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11} = whole-tone scale (C2_1)
- Various combinations can also produce octatonic collections

**Crumb, Makrokosmos, "Primeval Sounds"** (Example 4-35):
- Right-hand minor triads descend chromatically
- Left-hand grace-note triads follow a parallel C1 a tritone below
- The tritone relationship (C6) structures the parallel motion between hands

# Common Confusions

- **Smallest cycles**: C6 cycles contain only 2 pitch classes each, making them the smallest interval cycles
- **Self-complementary interval**: The tritone is unique in being its own inversion (6 semitones up = 6 semitones down)
- **Six cycles, not one**: There are six distinct tritone dyads, each forming its own C6
- **Direction irrelevant**: Unlike other cycles, moving by +6 or -6 is identical (both arrive at the same pitch class)
