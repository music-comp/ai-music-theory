# Quick Definition
Notes with different spellings but the same pitch (like Bb and A#) are considered functionally equivalent in post-tonal music.

# Formal Definition
Enharmonic equivalence is the principle that notes spelled differently but sounding at the same pitch (on equal-tempered instruments) are treated as functionally identical in post-tonal music. Unlike common-practice tonal music, where Bb and A# have different functions and meanings based on their scale-degree roles, post-tonal music largely abandons these distinctions.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Enharmonically equivalent notes map to the same pitch-class integer:
- B# = C = Dbb = pitch class 0
- C# = Db = pitch class 1
- D# = Eb = pitch class 3
- And so on for all twelve pitch classes

# Musical Context/Application
In post-tonal music, the notation of pitches is generally functionally arbitrary, determined by convenience and legibility rather than tonal function. Composers may occasionally notate pitches in ways that suggest function (such as sharps for ascending motion and flats for descending), but this is not systematic or required.

# Examples
**Example 1-4** (Stockhausen, Klavierstuck III): A passage involving three pitch repetitions where Ab returns three octaves higher spelled as G#. The Ab and G# are enharmonically equivalent, representing the same pitch class.

# Common Confusions
In tonal music, enharmonic spelling carries functional meaning (e.g., in G major, Bb is flat-3 while A# is sharp-2, with very different musical roles). Students with strong tonal training may initially resist treating Bb and A# as equivalent. In post-tonal analysis, however, these distinctions are typically abandoned.
