# Quick Definition

A compositional technique where a motive from the musical surface is projected over larger spans of music, creating structural coherence through concealed repetition at deeper levels.

# Formal Definition

**Composing-out** is a technique borrowed from Schenkerian analysis and adapted for post-tonal music, in which a motivic idea (a set of pitches, intervals, or a set class) is enlarged and projected over significant musical distances. The term describes how melodic or harmonic ideas from the foreground are replicated at background structural levels, binding together passages that may span phrases, sections, or even entire compositions. Related terms include "enlargement," "concealed repetition," "motivic parallelism," "nesting," and "self-similarity."

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition

To identify composing-out:
1. Identify a prominent motive at the surface level (usually a small pitch-class set or interval pattern)
2. Look for the same set class or interval succession at structural points:
   - First notes of phrases
   - Last notes of phrases
   - Melodic high points or low points
   - Bass line structural notes
   - Notes sharing register or articulation
3. The composed-out motive need not preserve exact pitch order but must preserve the set class or interval content

# Musical Context/Application

Composing-out serves several functions in post-tonal music:
- Creates long-range coherence without tonal harmonic functions
- Provides motivic unity across disparate surface events
- Establishes hierarchical structure through motivic parallelism
- Can span from small phrases to entire compositions

Unlike Schenkerian analysis in tonal music (which reveals underlying tonal voice-leading structures), post-tonal composing-out focuses on motivic and set-class relationships rather than harmonic prolongation.

# Examples

**Webern, Five Songs, op. 3, no. 1, "Dies ist ein Lied fur dich allein"** (Example 4-1):
- The first four notes of the melody, D-Db-Eb-Gb, return as the first notes of the four vocal phrases
- The last four notes of the first phrase, F-Ab-E-Bb, return (reordered, T6) as the last notes of the four phrases
- Boundary notes at phrase beginnings and endings compose-out surface motives

**Schoenberg, Three Piano Pieces, op. 11, no. 1** (Example 4-3):
- The first three melody notes B-G#-G are composed-out as the three melodic high points
- The same motive appears in the bass line: Gb-Bb-G, connected by shared register and articulation
- Each statement is a member of sc(014)

**Stravinsky, Les Noces** (Example 4-4):
- Opening melody uses [B, D, E]
- Third scene: same set transposed T2 to [C#, E, F#]
- Final coda: transposed T-3 to [G#, B, C#]
- The large-scale progression composes-out the intervallic shape of the original motive across the entire ballet

**Musgrave, Mary Queen of Scots** (Example 4-2):
- Accompaniment chord Eb-A-D elaborated by neighboring motion
- Same three notes appear as long notes in the vocal line
- Additional statements of sc(016) within the vocal line compose-out the harmony melodically

# Common Confusions

- **Composing-out vs. simple repetition**: Composing-out involves projection at a deeper structural level, not merely literal surface repetition
- **Composing-out vs. Schenkerian prolongation**: In tonal music, composing-out prolongs harmonies; in post-tonal music, it projects motivic/set-class relationships without implying harmonic function
- **Exact pitch preservation**: The composed-out motive need not preserve exact pitches or order; it typically preserves the set class or interval content
- **Scale of application**: Composing-out can occur at any level, from phrase to section to entire work
