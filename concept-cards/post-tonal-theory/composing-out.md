# Quick Definition
A compositional technique where a motive from the musical surface is projected over larger spans of music, creating structural coherence through concealed repetition at deeper levels.

# Core Definition
**Composing-out** is a technique adapted from Schenkerian analysis for post-tonal music, in which a motivic idea (a set of pitches, intervals, or a set class) is enlarged and projected over significant musical distances. The term describes how melodic or harmonic ideas from the foreground are replicated at background structural levels, binding together passages that may span phrases, sections, or entire compositions. Related terms include "enlargement," "concealed repetition," "motivic parallelism," "nesting," and "self-similarity" (Straus, Ch. 4, p. 176).

# Prerequisites
- **Set class** -- needed to identify the motivic identity that is projected across levels
- **Transposition (Tn)** -- composed-out motives are often transposed forms of surface motives
- **Interval class** -- the interval content of the surface motive must be recognized at deeper levels

# Key Properties
1. A surface motive (identified by set class or interval content) reappears at a deeper structural level
2. The composed-out motive need not preserve exact pitch order; set-class identity suffices
3. Structural notes are identified by position: first/last notes of phrases, melodic extremes, registrally prominent notes, shared articulation
4. Composing-out can span from phrases to entire works

# Construction / Recognition
To identify composing-out:
1. Identify a prominent motive at the surface level (usually a small pitch-class set or interval pattern)
2. Look for the same set class or interval succession at structural points:
   - First notes of phrases
   - Last notes of phrases
   - Melodic high points or low points
   - Bass line structural notes
   - Notes sharing register or articulation
3. The composed-out motive preserves set class or interval content, not necessarily exact pitches or order

# Context & Application
Composing-out serves several functions in post-tonal music:
- Creates long-range coherence without tonal harmonic functions
- Provides motivic unity across disparate surface events
- Establishes hierarchical structure through motivic parallelism
- Unlike Schenkerian prolongation (which reveals underlying voice-leading structures), post-tonal composing-out focuses on motivic and set-class relationships rather than harmonic prolongation

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 176): Webern, *Five Songs*, op. 3, no. 1 -- The first four melody notes D-Db-Eb-Gb return as the first notes of the four vocal phrases. The last four notes of the first phrase, F-Ab-E-Bb, return (reordered, T6) as the last notes of the four phrases.

**Example 2** (p. 178): Schoenberg, *Three Piano Pieces*, op. 11, no. 1 -- The first three melody notes B-G#-G (sc(014)) are composed-out as the three melodic high points; the same set class appears in the bass line: Gb-Bb-G.

**Example 3** (p. 179): Stravinsky, *Les Noces* -- Opening melody uses [B, D, E]; third scene transposes T2 to [C#, E, F#]; final coda transposes T-3 to [G#, B, C#]. The large-scale progression composes-out the intervallic shape across the entire ballet.

**Example 4** (p. 177): Musgrave, *Mary Queen of Scots* -- Accompaniment chord Eb-A-D (sc(016)) is elaborated by neighboring motion, and the same three notes appear as long notes in the vocal line.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Set class** -- the composed-out identity
- **Transposition** -- the mechanism connecting surface and deep levels

## Enables
- **Analytical levels** -- composing-out implies multiple structural levels
- **Motivic enlargement** -- a closely related concept

## Related
- **Direct presentation** -- surface-level motivic statement (what gets composed-out)
- **Indirect presentation** -- another way of presenting motivic material

## Contrasts With
- **Simple repetition** -- composing-out involves projection at a deeper structural level, not merely literal surface repetition

# Common Errors
- Confusing composing-out with simple surface repetition
- Requiring exact pitch preservation (set-class identity suffices)
- Applying Schenkerian prolongational logic (harmonic prolongation) to post-tonal composing-out

# Common Confusions
- **Composing-out vs. Schenkerian prolongation**: In tonal music, composing-out prolongs harmonies; in post-tonal music, it projects motivic/set-class relationships without implying harmonic function
- **Scale of application**: Can occur at any level from phrase to entire work
