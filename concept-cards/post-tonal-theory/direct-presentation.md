# Quick Definition
Direct presentation occurs when pitch classes that are contiguous within the series appear together in the music, maintaining their serial adjacency.

# Core Definition
In direct presentation, segmental subsets of the series -- "groups of pitch classes that are contiguous within the series" (Straus, p. 324) -- appear with their serial adjacency preserved in the musical surface. When the series is stated as a melody, all contiguous groups are in direct presentation. The discrete subsets (non-overlapping divisions) are a special case: "There are four discrete trichords, three discrete tetrachords, and two discrete hexachords in every series" (p. 255).

# Prerequisites
- **Segmental subsets** -- the subsets being directly presented
- **Twelve-tone series** -- the source of the subsets

# Key Properties
1. Pitch classes are contiguous within the series
2. Serial adjacency is preserved in the musical surface
3. Discrete subsets divide the series into non-overlapping portions
4. The same set class appears at corresponding positions across all 48 forms

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify groups of notes that are adjacent in the series ordering
- Check whether they appear together in the music (melodically or harmonically)
- Note recurring set classes in corresponding positions

# Context & Application
Direct presentation makes the series structure audible. In Schoenberg's String Quartet No. 4, the opening melody presents P2 linearly, and the music divides the series into its four discrete trichords, with each trichord appearing four times (once in the melody, three times in the accompaniment).

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 324--325, Exx. 6-16 and 6-17): Schoenberg, String Quartet No. 4, first movement -- the first trichord of P2 (D-C#-A) is a member of sc(015), which recurs at other positions in the series. The music divides the series into four discrete trichords; each trichord sounds four times in the passage.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Segmental subsets** -- direct presentation is one way of presenting them

## Contrasts With
- **Indirect presentation** -- where non-contiguous notes are aligned to create subset associations

# Common Errors
- Assuming all presentations must be direct (indirect presentation is equally important)
- Conflating "direct" with "melodic" (harmonic statements can also be direct)

# Common Confusions
- **Direct vs. indirect**: Direct preserves serial adjacency; indirect creates set-class associations through vertical alignment of non-adjacent notes
