# Quick Definition
Direct presentation occurs when a segmental subset of the series appears in its series order, with the pitch classes presented in the same sequence as they occur in the row.

# Formal Definition
*Direct presentation* is when a segmental subset of the series is presented with its pitch classes occurring in the same order as they appear in the series. The subset appears "directly" as an ordered segment of the series, maintaining its internal ordering. This is the most straightforward way of presenting the subsets embedded in a series, making the connection between the musical surface and the underlying series structure explicit.

# Musical Context/Application
- Direct presentation makes the series structure audible in the music
- Melodic statements often use direct presentation
- When the series is stated as a melody, all contiguous groups are in direct presentation
- Listeners can potentially recognize the series ordering through direct presentations
- Analysts can identify series forms by examining directly presented segments

# Examples
- Schoenberg, String Quartet No. 4, mm. 1-6: The first violin presents all 12 notes of P2 in order
- Each discrete trichord in the melody is a direct presentation
- The (015) trichords at positions 1-3 and 6-8 appear with their serial ordering preserved
- When the music "divides the series into its four discrete trichords," each is directly presented

# Common Confusions
- Assuming all presentations must be direct—indirect presentation is equally common
- Conflating "direct" with "melodic"—harmonic statements can also be direct
- Forgetting that direct presentation preserves order within the subset, not necessarily between subsets
- Not recognizing that register and rhythm are independent of direct/indirect distinction
