# Quick Definition
A voice-leading pattern where notes radiate symmetrically outward from a central axis pitch, establishing that pitch as centric through balanced divergent motion.

# Core Definition
An **expanding wedge** occurs when notes radiate outward from a central tone by gradually increasing distances, with paired voices moving away from the axis at equal rates in opposite directions. The central tone serves as the axis of symmetry from which the wedge expands. This technique establishes centricity through the systematic, balanced radiation of pitch material from the axis (Straus, Ch. 5, p. 250).

# Prerequisites
- **Inversional axis** -- the center around which the wedge expands
- **Inversional symmetry in pitch space** -- the balanced pitch-space relationship

# Key Properties
1. Paired voices move outward from the axis by equal intervallic distances
2. The axis is the arithmetic mean of any balanced pair at any moment
3. The pattern typically proceeds: axis, then +/-1, +/-2, +/-3 semitones, etc.
4. The axis pitch may or may not be sounded before expansion begins
5. Establishes the axis as the registral center

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify a starting pitch or pair of pitches near a central point
2. Track whether subsequent entries balance above and below
3. Verify that the distances increase symmetrically
4. The midpoint of any balanced pair should equal the axis pitch

# Context & Application
Expanding wedges are a common device for establishing or reinforcing inversional centricity. They make the axis of symmetry audible through systematic voice-leading motion. The technique is especially clear when multiple instruments participate in the expansion simultaneously.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 250): Ligeti, *Ten Pieces for Wind Quintet*, no. 9 -- Three instruments in canon share a canonic line beginning on Eb. A wedge of notes expands outward by gradually increasing distances from Eb as axis.

**Example 2** (p. 252): Bartok, *String Quartet No. 5*, fifth movement -- C-Db dyad expands symmetrically: to B-D, then Bb-Eb, etc. When the wedge reaches G-F#, both notes are doubled at the octave. The axis is C/Db-F#/G.

**Example 3** (p. 253): Bartok -- Ab and Bb repeated in right hand, with left hand adding expanding wedge: B and G (1 semitone each side), C and Gb (2), Db and F (3), D and Fb (4), Eb (5 semitones both above and below). The only unheard pc is A -- a "silent center."

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Inversional axis** -- the center from which expansion radiates

## Enables
- **Centricity** -- expanding wedges establish axis tones as centers

## Contrasts With
- **Contracting wedge** -- converges inward rather than expanding outward

# Common Errors
- Expecting perfectly strict expansion (approximate wedges are common)
- Confusing pitch-space and pitch-class-space wedge properties

# Common Confusions
- **Expanding vs. contracting**: Expanding moves outward from the axis; contracting converges inward toward it
- The wedge operates in pitch space (specific registers), not abstract pitch-class space
