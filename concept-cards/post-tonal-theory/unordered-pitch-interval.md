# Quick Definition
An unordered pitch interval (upi) measures the absolute distance in semitones between two pitches without indicating direction.

# Formal Definition
An unordered pitch interval (abbreviated upi) expresses only the absolute space between two pitches, without regard to the order (ascending or descending) of the pitches. It is always expressed as a positive number representing the number of semitones separating the two pitches.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Calculation of unordered pitch interval:
- upi = |pitch2 - pitch1| (absolute value of the difference)
- Always a positive integer
- Preserves compound intervals (unlike interval class)

Example calculations:
- Between C4 and E4: upi = 4
- Between E4 and C4: upi = 4 (same result)
- Between C4 and E5: upi = 16 (compound major third)

# Musical Context/Application
Unordered pitch intervals are useful when analyzing the spacing or distribution of pitches without concern for direction. They describe the distances between notes in a chord or the registral span of a musical passage.

# Examples
**Example 1-11** (Schoenberg, String Quartet No. 3, first movement): The same melodic passage analyzed for unordered pitch intervals: (1, 11, 1, 3, 13, 1). These intervals ignore the contour and focus only on the distances between adjacent notes.

# Common Confusions
Unordered pitch intervals differ from interval classes. An upi of 13 (minor ninth) remains 13, whereas the corresponding interval class is 1. Unordered pitch intervals preserve information about compound intervals and actual registral distance.
