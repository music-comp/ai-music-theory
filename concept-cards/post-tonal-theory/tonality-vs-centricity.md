# Quick Definition
Tonality involves functional harmonic relationships (dominant to tonic, etc.) while centricity simply indicates that a particular note functions as central, without requiring traditional functional progressions.

# Formal Definition
Tonality and centricity are related but distinct concepts for organizing music around focal pitches. In traditional tonality, the tonic is established through multiple intertwined means: as the root of the tonic triad, as the first scale degree, and as the goal of functional harmonic progressions (pre-dominant to dominant to tonic). Centricity, by contrast, refers simply to the sense that a particular note is central or focal, achieved through emphasis, repetition, registral prominence, or inversional symmetry, without requiring functional harmony or traditional voice leading.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Tonality requires:
1. Triadic harmony
2. Functional progressions (T-PD-D-T paradigm)
3. Scale-degree relationships
4. Leading-tone resolution
5. Voice-leading conventions

Centricity requires only:
1. A sense of one or more tones as central
2. Any means of establishing that centrality

Centricity is the more general concept; tonality is a specific type of centricity with additional requirements.

# Musical Context/Application
The distinction matters because:
1. Post-tonal music can have centricity without tonality
2. Analysis can acknowledge centric effects without forcing tonal interpretations
3. Pieces can be "in" or "on" a note without being "in the key of" that note
4. The spectrum from atonal to centric to tonal describes different degrees of pitch-class hierarchy

# Examples
Example 5-1: Stravinsky's Serenade in A - The piece is "in A" but not "in A major" or "in A minor." A is established through inversional convergence, not V-I progressions.

Example 5-2: Stravinsky's Symphony in C - C is present but contested; the music provides "contexts for and resistance to C as centric tone."

Example 5-3: Stravinsky's Concerto in D - D-major, D-minor, and B-minor triads confront each other statically without traditional tonal resolution.

Schoenberg's op. 11, no. 1 - Tonality operates "like a ghost" that cannot be pinned down; different analysts find different keys.

# Common Confusions
- Centric music is not necessarily tonal
- Atonal music is not necessarily non-centric
- The presence of triads does not guarantee tonality
- Traditional tonal analysis may be inadequate for centric but non-tonal music
