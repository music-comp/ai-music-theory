# Quick Definition
The two possible scalar arrangements of the octatonic collection: the 1-2 ordering (beginning with a semitone, alternating semitone-whole tone) and the 2-1 ordering (beginning with a whole tone, alternating whole tone-semitone).

# Formal Definition
When written out as a scale, the octatonic collection consists of alternating intervals of 1 and 2 semitones. Unlike the diatonic collection (which has 7 modes), the octatonic collection has only two distinct interval orderings: 1-2-1-2-1-2-1-2 (beginning with a semitone) or 2-1-2-1-2-1-2-1 (beginning with a whole tone). Each of the three octatonic collections can be presented in either ordering, starting on any of four notes for each ordering type.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
The two orderings:

1-2 ordering (half-whole scale):
- Interval pattern: 1-2-1-2-1-2-1-2
- Example (OCT0,1): C-C#-D#-E-F#-G-A-Bb-C

2-1 ordering (whole-half scale):
- Interval pattern: 2-1-2-1-2-1-2-1
- Example (OCT0,1): C#-D#-E-F#-G-A-Bb-C-C#

For each collection and ordering, there are 4 possible starting notes (related by T3):
- OCT0,1, 1-2: can start on C, D#, F#, or A
- OCT0,1, 2-1: can start on C#, E, G, or Bb

Total: 3 collections x 2 orderings x 4 starting points = 24 scales

# Common Confusions
- There are only 2 orderings (compared to 7 for diatonic)
- The ordering is about interval pattern, not which collection is in use
- "Half-whole" and "whole-half" (jazz terminology) refer to these same two orderings
- Starting note can vary independently of ordering type
