# Quick Definition
A six-note collection, sc(014589), constructed from alternating semitones and minor thirds, both transpositionally and inversionally symmetrical at three levels, existing in four distinct transpositions.

# Formal Definition
The hexatonic collection, set class (014589) (Forte-name 6-20), occurs frequently in post-tonal music and has many striking properties. It is both transpositionally and inversionally symmetrical at three different levels (T0, T4, T8). As a result, there are only four distinct members of this set class. The collection can be thought of as two augmented triads related by T1. When written as a scale, it alternates intervals of 1 and 3 semitones.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
The four hexatonic collections:

| Name | Pitch Classes | Augmented Triads |
|------|---------------|------------------|
| HEX0,1 | [0, 1, 4, 5, 8, 9] | C-E-G# and C#-F-A |
| HEX1,2 | [1, 2, 5, 6, 9, 10] | C#-F-A and D-F#-A# |
| HEX2,3 | [2, 3, 6, 7, 10, 11] | D-F#-A# and D#-G-B |
| HEX3,4 | [3, 4, 7, 8, 11, 0] | D#-G-B and E-G#-C |

Interval content: Emphasizes ic1, ic3, and ic4
Symmetry: Maps onto itself at T0, T4, T8

# Musical Context/Application
The hexatonic collection:
1. Underlies LP-cycles (hexatonic systems) in triadic transformations
2. Contains major and minor triads with traditional "feel"
3. Features augmented triads and major seventh chords
4. Creates characteristic hexatonic sound world

# Common Confusions
- Only 4 hexatonic collections exist (between the 2 whole-tone and 3 octatonic)
- Hexatonic ≠ "six notes"; it specifically means sc(014589)
- The connection to LP-cycles and triadic transformations is fundamental
- Each collection contains two (not one) augmented triads
