# Quick Definition
The semitone is the smallest interval in equal-tempered music and serves as the basic unit for measuring all intervals in post-tonal theory.

# Formal Definition
In post-tonal music, intervals are measured by the number of semitones they contain, rather than by diatonic steps or traditional quality designations (major, minor, diminished, augmented). The semitone provides a neutral, consistent unit of measurement that does not depend on diatonic context. This approach treats enharmonically equivalent intervals (such as major third and diminished fourth) as the same interval because both contain four semitones.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Interval measurement in semitones:
| Semitones | Traditional Names |
|-----------|-------------------|
| 0 | unison |
| 1 | minor 2nd |
| 2 | major 2nd, diminished 3rd |
| 3 | minor 3rd, augmented 2nd |
| 4 | major 3rd, diminished 4th |
| 5 | perfect 4th |
| 6 | augmented 4th, diminished 5th (tritone) |
| 7 | perfect 5th |
| 8 | minor 6th, augmented 5th |
| 9 | major 6th |
| 10 | minor 7th, augmented 6th |
| 11 | major 7th |
| 12 | octave |

# Musical Context/Application
Measuring intervals in semitones is essential for post-tonal analysis because post-tonal music does not necessarily reference diatonic scales, making traditional interval names (which assume diatonic context) potentially misleading. The semitone-based system allows precise, unambiguous description of intervallic relationships.

# Examples
**Example 1-9** (Carter, String Quartet No. 3): A series of seven harmonic intervals played in rhythmic unison. The first six intervals are spelled as major thirds while the seventh is spelled as a diminished fourth, but in this musical context all seven are understood as enharmonically equivalent: all are 4s (or compound/octave-equivalent 4s).

# Common Confusions
When converting from traditional interval names, remember that semitone counting measures the actual acoustic distance, not diatonic steps. A major third and a diminished fourth are both 4 semitones, even though they span different numbers of letter names.
