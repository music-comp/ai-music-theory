# Quick Definition
A three-dimensional geometric representation showing all tetrachord-classes positioned according to their voice-leading distances, where each edge represents one semitone of voice-leading adjustment.

# Formal Definition
The voice-leading space for tetrachords is a three-dimensional rendering in which tetrachord set classes are arranged according to their semitonal offset from one another. As with trichords, adjacent set classes in the space require adjustment of only one voice by one semitone. The increased dimensionality (compared to the trichordal map) reflects the greater complexity of four-note set relationships.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
The offset numbers in transformational voice leading correspond exactly to distances in the voice-leading space:
- Movement from one chord to another via T* with offset of 2 means the set classes are "two clicks away" on the map
- Larger offsets correspond to larger leaps in the space
- Offset of 1 indicates adjacent positions in the space

The space has similar structural properties to the trichordal space:
- (0123) (chromatic tetrachord) at one extreme
- (0369) (diminished seventh chord) at the opposite extreme

# Musical Context/Application
The tetrachordal space permits:
1. Visualization of four-note chord progressions
2. Interpretation of harmonic motion in terms of distance and direction
3. Understanding the relationship between transformational voice leading and spatial proximity
4. Analysis of whether progressions move toward chromaticness or evenness

# Common Confusions
- The three-dimensional nature makes visualization more challenging than the trichordal map
- Not all tetrachord relationships can be easily traced due to the higher dimensionality
- The space represents abstract set classes, not actual voiced chords
