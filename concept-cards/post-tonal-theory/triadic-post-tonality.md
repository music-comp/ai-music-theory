# Quick Definition
Music that uses major and minor triads as basic harmonies but without the functional relationships (pre-dominant, dominant, tonic) of traditional tonality, characteristic of neoclassicism, neotonality, and minimalism.

# Formal Definition
Triadic post-tonality describes extended progressions of major and minor triads that are not constrained by the norms of traditional tonality. The triads do not relate to each other functionally as pre-dominants, dominants, or tonics. Instead, they may be connected by contextual inversions (like P, L, and R), interval cycles, inversional symmetry, or motivic processes. The music is triadic but distinctively post-tonal.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Characteristics of triadic post-tonality:
1. Triads (major and minor) as primary harmonies
2. Absence of functional progressions (no V-I, ii-V-I patterns)
3. Organization through:
   - Triadic transformations (P, L, R, SLIDE, etc.)
   - LP-cycles and PLR-cycles
   - Interval cycles
   - Inversional symmetry
   - Motivic processes

# Musical Context/Application
Triadic post-tonality appears in:
1. Neoclassicism (Stravinsky)
2. Neotonality (various 20th/21st century composers)
3. Minimalism (Adams, Glass, Reich)
4. Late-Romantic chromatic music (analyzed retrospectively)
5. Film and popular music influenced by these styles

# Examples
Example 4-29: Adams's Harmonium uses SLIDE to connect tonally distant triads.

Example 4-30: Adams's Nixon in China uses L-progressions within hexatonic systems.

Example 4-31: Glass's Einstein on the Beach combines hexatonic-system progressions with a brief tonally normal cadence (IV-V-I).

Example 4-34: Stravinsky's Symphony in C creates tension between L-related triads (C+ and E-) without resolution.

Example 4-35: Crumb's Makrokosmos arranges all 12 minor triads following the 1-cycle.

Example 4-36: Britten's Serenade arranges major triads symmetrically around D.

# Common Confusions
- Triadic post-tonality is not "atonal" - it uses triads prominently
- The absence of functional harmony does not mean the music is random or unstructured
- Triadic post-tonality can coexist with moments of functional tonality
- Centric effects may exist without traditional tonic function
