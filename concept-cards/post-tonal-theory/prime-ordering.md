# Quick Definition
The prime ordering (P) is the designated original or reference form of a twelve-tone series, typically the first statement in the piece, from which retrograde, inversion, and retrograde-inversion are calculated.

# Formal Definition
The series is traditionally used in four different orderings: prime (P), retrograde (R), inversion (I), and retrograde-inversion (RI). Some statement of the series, usually the very first one in the piece, is designated the *prime*, and the rest are calculated in relation to it. The prime ordering is labeled with a subscript indicating its starting pitch class: P0 begins on pitch-class 0, P1 on pitch-class 1, P2 on pitch-class 2, and so on. There are twelve different forms of the prime ordering: P0, P1, P2, ... P11.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
- P_n = prime ordering beginning on pitch-class n
- P0, P1, P2, ... P11 = the twelve transpositions of the prime ordering
- The ordered pitch-class intervals define the prime ordering
- Example (Schoenberg, String Quartet No. 4): P2 = D-C#-A-Bb-F-Eb-E-C-Ab-G-F#-B
- Ordered intervals: 11, 8, 1, 7, 10, 1, 8, 8, 11, 11, 5

# Musical Context/Application
- Usually designated from the first clear, forthright statement of the series in a piece
- Notes are numbered 1-12 according to their order position
- All P-forms share the same interval succession
- The choice of which form to call P0 is somewhat arbitrary; what matters is consistency
- Sometimes the subscript indicates the first note; sometimes analysts normalize to P0

# Examples
- Schoenberg, String Quartet No. 4, first movement: The melody in the first violin (D-C#-A-Bb-F-Eb-E-C-Ab-G-F#-B) is designated P2 because it begins on D (pitch-class 2)
- Webern, "Wie bin ich froh!": P7 is designated from the melody beginning on G
- Each note is numbered according to its order position (1-12)

# Common Confusions
- Assuming P0 is always "the" prime form—the subscript merely indicates starting pitch class
- Confusing the prime ordering subscript with set-theory prime form notation
- Not realizing that different analysts may label the same series differently depending on which statement they designate as prime
