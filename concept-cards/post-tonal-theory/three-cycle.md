# Quick Definition

The interval cycle produced by moving repeatedly by minor third (interval 3), generating three distinct cycles that correspond to the three diminished seventh chords.

# Formal Definition

The **3-cycle (C3)** is the interval cycle generated by repeated transposition by interval class 3 (the minor third). Because GCD(3, 12) = 3, there are exactly three distinct 3-cycles, each containing four pitch classes:

- **C3_0**: {0, 3, 6, 9} = {C, Eb, Gb, A} = one diminished seventh chord
- **C3_1**: {1, 4, 7, 10} = {C#, E, G, Bb} = another diminished seventh chord
- **C3_2**: {2, 5, 8, 11} = {D, F, Ab, B} = the third diminished seventh chord

Each C3 corresponds to one of the three diminished seventh chords. Combining any two of the three C3s produces the octatonic collection.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition

**Properties of C3:**
- Interval: 3 semitones (minor third)
- Number of distinct cycles: GCD(3, 12) = 3
- Pitch classes per cycle: 12/3 = 4
- Set class of complete cycle: sc(0369) = diminished seventh chord

**The three cycles:**
- C3_0: 0 - 3 - 6 - 9 - (0) [C - Eb - Gb - A - (C)]
- C3_1: 1 - 4 - 7 - 10 - (1) [C# - E - G - Bb - (C#)]
- C3_2: 2 - 5 - 8 - 11 - (2) [D - F - Ab - B - (D)]

**Relationship to C6:**
Every other note in a 3-cycle produces a 6-cycle (tritone):
- From C3_0: C-Gb (tritone) or Eb-A (tritone)

**Relationship to octatonic:**
C3_0 + C3_1 = octatonic collection (one of three)
C3_0 + C3_2 = different octatonic collection
C3_1 + C3_2 = third octatonic collection

# Musical Context/Application

The 3-cycle provides:
- **Diminished seventh chords**: The foundational tetrachord for C3
- **Minor-third melodic motion**: Systematic motion by ic3
- **Octatonic collections**: Combining two C3s yields octatonic material
- **Maximally even tetrachord**: sc(0369) is the most evenly distributed four-note set

The C3 is central to:
- Late Romantic chromatic harmony (diminished seventh chords)
- Bartok's symmetrical structures
- Octatonic-based compositions
- Music organized around minor-third relationships

# Examples

**Varese, Density 21.5** (Example 4-7):
- Principal melodic boundary tones follow C3_1: C#-E-G-Bb
- The melody gradually rises an octave within this C3 framework
- Arrival on D5 signals a shift away from this cyclic orientation

**Bartok, String Quartet No. 6, fourth movement** (Example 4-6):
- The 3-cycle shapes the main theme of the movement and the quartet as a whole
- Minor-third relationships provide structural coherence

**Stravinsky, The Rite of Spring, Introduction** (Model Analysis 4.1):
- The voice-leading reduction reveals motion through a diminished seventh chord: C-A-F#-D#
- The C3 unifies melodic motion across the passage

**General application:**
- The three diminished seventh chords partition all twelve pitch classes
- Any two C3s together form one of the three octatonic collections
- The C3 relates to the traditional use of diminished seventh chords as symmetrical, omnibus harmonies

# Common Confusions

- **Three cycles, not one**: C3 produces three distinct diminished seventh chords, not a single twelve-tone cycle
- **C3 vs. octatonic**: A single C3 is a diminished seventh (4 notes); combining two C3s produces an octatonic collection (8 notes)
- **Minor third vs. major sixth**: Motion by i3 and i9 (its complement) traverse the same cycle in opposite directions
- **C3 subscript**: The subscript indicates which pitch class (0, 1, or 2 mod 3) is contained in the cycle
