# Quick Definition
A pitch-class interval (pci) is the distance between two pitch classes, measured in modular pitch-class space and never exceeding 11 semitones.

# Formal Definition
A pitch-class interval (abbreviated pci) is the distance between two pitch classes, created when moving from pitch class to pitch class in modular pitch-class space. Unlike pitch intervals, pitch-class intervals can never be larger than eleven semitones because no two pitch classes are more than eleven semitones apart in pitch-class space. Pitch-class intervals may be ordered or unordered.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Pitch-class interval calculations operate mod 12:
- Maximum size: 11 semitones
- For ordered pitch-class intervals: count clockwise on the clockface
- For unordered pitch-class intervals: take the shorter path (maximum 6)

Relationship to pitch intervals:
- Pitch-class interval = pitch interval mod 12
- Compound intervals reduce to their within-octave equivalents

# Musical Context/Application
Pitch-class intervals provide an abstract measure of distance between pitch classes that removes registral information. This abstraction allows analysts to identify relationships between musical events in different registers. The pitch-class clockface is the most useful tool for calculating pitch-class intervals.

# Examples
Analysis often involves determining which type of interval (pitch or pitch-class, ordered or unordered) best captures a particular musical relationship. The four types offer a spectrum from most concrete to most abstract.

# Common Confusions
Pitch-class intervals always reduce compound intervals to their within-octave equivalents via mod 12. A pitch interval of 15 (minor ninth) becomes pitch-class interval 3. This abstraction is useful for identifying relationships across registers but removes information about actual registral distance.
