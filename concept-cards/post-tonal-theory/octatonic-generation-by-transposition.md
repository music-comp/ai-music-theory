# Quick Definition
The principle that any subset of the octatonic collection can be transposed at T0, T3, T6, and T9 without leaving the collection, and conversely, combining any subset with its T3/T6/T9 transpositions generates the entire octatonic collection.

# Core Definition
The octatonic collection's internal transpositional symmetry (mapping onto itself at T0, T3, T6, T9) means that any of its subsets can be transposed at these four levels while remaining within the collection. Conversely, combining any subset with its transpositions at T3, T6, and T9 produces the complete octatonic collection. This generative principle explains why octatonic collections frequently emerge as by-products of compositional schemes involving minor thirds (T3) and tritones (T6) (Straus, Ch. 5, pp. 266-267).

# Prerequisites
- **Octatonic collection** -- the 8-note collection itself
- **Octatonic subsets** -- the familiar formations contained within
- **Transposition (Tn)** -- the operation that generates the collection

# Key Properties
1. Any subset transposed at T0, T3, T6, T9 stays within the collection
2. Any subset combined with its T3/T6/T9 transpositions generates the complete collection
3. Octatonic passages often arise as by-products of T3/T6 transposition schemes
4. This explains how triads, seventh chords, and other familiar harmonies can generate octatonic contexts
5. Taking a major triad and combining it with T3, T6, T9 produces an octatonic collection

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify a subset that recurs at T3 or T6 transposition levels
2. Combine the transposed forms: the resulting collection should be octatonic
3. If a passage features T3/T6 transpositional schemes, suspect octatonic origin
4. Verify by checking pitch-class content against OCT0,1, OCT1,2, or OCT2,3

# Context & Application
This generative principle is central to understanding how composers arrive at octatonic collections. Rather than consciously choosing an octatonic scale, many composers create octatonic contexts by systematically transposing motives at T3 or T6. The Stravinsky "Petrushka chord" (two tritone-related major triads) is a classic example.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 267): Bartok, *Mikrokosmos*, no. 101 -- Combines one form of (0235) in the left hand with its tritone transposition in the right hand. Result: complete OCT2,3. In m. 12, tritone-related forms of (025) create incomplete OCT0,1.

**Example 2** (p. 267): Stravinsky, *Petrushka* -- Combines two major triads a tritone apart (C+ and F#+), creating incomplete OCT0,1. The famous "Petrushka chord" is a byproduct of T6 transposition.

**Example 3** (p. 268): Messiaen, *Quartet for the End of Time* -- Uses T3 and T6 transpositions of subsets like (013), generating octatonic context.

# Common Errors

# Common Confusions
- **Generation vs. selection**: The octatonic collection can emerge from transpositional processes rather than being selected as a referential scale
