# Quick Definition
The midpoint around which pitches or pitch classes balance in an inversionally symmetrical set or passage, potentially functioning as a pitch or pitch-class center.

# Formal Definition
An inversional axis is the point (in pitch space) or pair of points (in pitch-class space) around which all notes of an inversionally symmetrical set balance. An inversionally symmetrical set has an axis of symmetry, and this axis may function as a pitch or pitch-class center in post-tonal music. In pitch space, the axis is a single pitch (which may or may not be sounded); in pitch-class space, the axis consists of two pitch classes (or two pairs of pitch classes) a tritone apart, called the poles of the axis.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
In pitch space:
- The axis is the arithmetic mean of balanced pitch pairs
- Example: If D4 and F4 balance, the axis is E4 (or Eb4/E4 boundary)

In pitch-class space:
- For each sum n (of balanced pairs), the axis passes through n/2 and n/2 + 6
- If balanced notes sum to 8, the axis is 4-10 (E-Bb)
- If the sum is odd (e.g., 7), the axis passes between pitch classes: 3/4-9/10 (Eb/E-A/Bb)
- Axis notation: single pcs separated by dash (4-10) or pc pairs separated by slash (3/4-9/10)

# Musical Context/Application
Inversional axes function in post-tonal music analogously to keys in tonal music:
1. They provide orientation and centricity
2. Music can "modulate" from one axis to another
3. The twelve axes parallel the twelve major/minor keys
4. Axes can be established, departed from, and returned to

# Examples
Example 5-4: Gubaidulina's String Trio - B4 is the axis of symmetry, literally the central tone.

Example 5-7: Bartók's String Quartet No. 5 - the axis of symmetry is C/Db-F#/G in pitch-class space.

Example 5-8: Bartók's piece uses axis A-Eb, with A as the pitch axis but A-Eb as the pitch-class axis.

Example 5-9: Finney's Fantasy for Solo Violin maintains axis D-Ab through the passage.

# Common Confusions
- In pitch space, the axis is one point; in pitch-class space, it is two points (or pairs) a tritone apart
- The axis may or may not be actually sounded
- Axes in pc space are notated differently depending on whether the sum is even or odd
- Both poles of a pc axis have centric potential
