# Quick Definition

Pitch-class sets that approximate maximal evenness by adjusting one note of a maximally even set by one semitone, producing historically significant harmonies like consonant triads and dominant seventh chords.

# Formal Definition

A **nearly even set** is a pitch-class set derived from a maximally even set by adjusting one pitch class by one semitone. This minimal alteration moves the set away from perfect or maximal evenness while retaining much of its balanced distribution. Nearly even sets have played crucial roles throughout music history, often serving as the most consonant or functionally significant harmonies at their cardinality.

The relationship between maximally even and nearly even sets reveals why certain chord types have been favored across different musical traditions.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition

**Nearly even sets by cardinality:**

| Cardinality | Maximally Even | Nearly Even | Traditional Name |
|-------------|----------------|-------------|------------------|
| 2 | (06) Tritone | (05) | Perfect fourth/fifth |
| 3 | (048) Augmented triad | (037) | Major/minor triad |
| 4 | (0369) Diminished seventh | (0258) | Dominant/half-dim. seventh |
| 6 | (02468T) Whole-tone | (013579) | Scriabin's "Mystic" chord |
| 7 | (013568T) Diatonic | (013468T) or (0134689) | Melodic minor / Harmonic minor |

**Derivation process:**
1. Start with a maximally even set
2. Move one pitch class by one semitone (either direction)
3. The result is a nearly even set

**Example - trichord:**
- Maximally even (048): C-E-G# (augmented triad)
- Adjust G# down by 1: C-E-G = (037) major triad
- Adjust E down by 1: C-Eb-G# = (037) minor triad (in different voicing)

# Musical Context/Application

Nearly even sets explain why certain harmonies have been historically favored:

- **Perfect fourth/fifth (05)**: The most consonant dyad; "nearly" a tritone
- **Major/minor triad (037)**: The foundational consonant harmony of tonal music; "nearly" an augmented triad
- **Dominant seventh (0258)**: The primary functional dissonance of tonal music; "nearly" a diminished seventh
- **Mystic chord (013579)**: Scriabin's signature harmony; "nearly" whole-tone
- **Melodic/harmonic minor scales**: Common minor-mode variants; "nearly" diatonic

This framework suggests that the most functionally important harmonies in tonal music are slight deviations from the maximally even structures generated by interval cycles.

# Examples

**From Example 4-10:**

**Dyad:**
- Maximally even: (06) tritone - C to F#
- Nearly even: (05) perfect fourth/fifth - C to F or C to G
- The perfect fifth, the most consonant interval, is one semitone removed from the tritone

**Trichord:**
- Maximally even: (048) augmented triad - C-E-G#
- Nearly even: (037) major triad - C-E-G or minor triad - C-Eb-G
- The consonant triads that dominate Western music are nearly even

**Tetrachord:**
- Maximally even: (0369) diminished seventh - C-Eb-Gb-A
- Nearly even: (0258) dominant seventh - C-E-G-Bb or half-diminished - C-Eb-Gb-Bb
- Traditional functional seventh chords are nearly even

**Hexachord:**
- Maximally even: (02468T) whole-tone - C-D-E-F#-G#-A#
- Nearly even: (013579) Scriabin's "Mystic" chord - C-F#-Bb-E-A-D (in Scriabin's voicing)
- Scriabin's signature harmony is a slightly adjusted whole-tone collection

**Heptachord:**
- Maximally even: (013568T) diatonic/major scale
- Nearly even: (013468T) ascending melodic minor / acoustic scale
- Nearly even: (0134689) harmonic minor scale
- The common minor-scale variants are slight adjustments of the major scale

# Common Confusions

- **Nearly even vs. slightly dissonant**: "Nearly even" describes structural distribution, not necessarily perceptual consonance
- **One semitone adjustment**: By definition, a nearly even set differs from a maximally even set by exactly one semitone in one voice
- **Multiple nearly even versions**: A maximally even set may have multiple nearly even variants (e.g., dominant seventh and half-diminished are both nearly even tetrachords)
- **Historical significance**: The concept explains rather than defines which harmonies are important; not all nearly even sets have been historically significant
