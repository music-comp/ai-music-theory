# Quick Definition
Notes that function as focal points or centers in post-tonal music, established through emphasis, repetition, registral prominence, or inversional symmetry rather than through functional harmonic relationships.

# Formal Definition
Centric tones are pitches or pitch classes that assume a central, focal role in post-tonal music. The term is more accurate than "tonic" for post-tonal contexts because it does not imply the functional harmonic relationships (V-I progressions, pre-dominant to dominant to tonic motion) associated with traditional tonality. Centric tones may be established through various means: duration, dynamics, frequency, registral position, metric placement, and especially through inversional symmetry.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Methods of establishing centric tones:
1. Duration: longer notes attract attention
2. Dynamics: louder notes are perceived as more important
3. Frequency: repeated notes gain emphasis
4. Register: extreme high or low notes are prominent
5. Metric position: downbeats carry weight
6. Convergence: melodic lines arriving at the tone
7. Inversional symmetry: axis notes function as centers
8. Framing: notes at beginnings and endings of phrases

# Musical Context/Application
Centric tones allow post-tonal music to have orientation and hierarchy without traditional tonal function. A piece may be organized around a centric tone that:
- Is sounded prominently
- Is implied but rarely sounded (silent center)
- Competes with other centric tones
- Shifts during the course of the piece

# Examples
Example 5-1: In Stravinsky's Serenade in A, A is established as the centric tone through inversional convergence - the bass and soprano converge symmetrically on A without any V-I relationship.

Example 5-4: Gubaidulina's String Trio establishes B4 as a central tone, with all new notes balanced around it (A# balanced by C, A3 by C#6, etc.).

Example 5-8: Bartók creates an unsounded centric tone A - it lies in the middle of a repeated figure, "a kind of silent center around which everything balances."

# Common Confusions
- Centric tones need not be triadic roots or scale degrees
- A centric tone can be implied without being sounded
- Multiple centric tones can coexist or compete
- Centric tone is not the same as "tonic" (which implies functional relationships)
