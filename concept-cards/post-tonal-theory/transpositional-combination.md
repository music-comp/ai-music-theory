# Quick Definition
The process of combining a set with one or more transpositions of itself to create a larger set; notated as n*m, meaning interval-class n transposed by interval m.

# Formal Definition
Transpositional combination (TC) is the combination of a set with one or more transpositions of itself to create a larger set. The larger set is said to have the TC property if it can be divided into two or more subsets related by transposition.

The notation n*m indicates that interval (or set) n is combined with its transposition by m semitones. For example, 3*1 means two instances of ic3, a semitone apart.

While inversional symmetry relates to combining sets with their inversions, TC relates to combining sets with their transpositions. All sets with the TC property are also inversionally symmetrical (TC is the more exclusive property).

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
TC notation: n*m
- n = the interval (or generating set) being combined
- m = the interval of transposition between instances
- Result: 2n-note set (assuming no common tones)

For tetrachords with TC property:
- (0123) = 1*2 (semitones transposed by 2)
- (0134) = 1*3 or 3*1
- (0167) = 1*6 or 5*6
- (0369) = 3*6

Larger TC structures can be built recursively: (3*1)*6 means "combine 3*1 tetrachords at T6"

# Musical Context/Application
TC is valuable for:
- Understanding how larger structures are built from smaller cells
- Analyzing motivic development through transposition
- Creating balanced, symmetrical harmonic structures
- Composing with systematically related materials

The TC property creates audible relationships: listeners can perceive the transpositionally related subsets as variants of each other.

# Examples
From Example 3-34: Stravinsky, Symphony of Psalms, first movement
- Bass begins with pi3 (F-Ab), then E-G (another pi3, a semitone lower)
- This is 3*1: two ic3s, transposed by semitone
- Also can be thought of as 1*3: two semitones (E-F and G-Ab) transposed by ic3
- Result: sc(0134) - [E, F, G, Ab]

- Another (0134) appears: [Bb, B, C#, D]
- These two tetrachords combine at T6
- Full structure: (3*1)*6

# Common Confusions
- Confusing TC (combining with transpositions) with inversional symmetry (combining with inversions)
- Not recognizing that some tetrachords have multiple TC analyses (like (0167) = 1*6 or 5*6)
- Thinking TC applies to all inversionally symmetrical sets (it's more restrictive)
- Forgetting that the combined sets must not share pitch classes (or the result has fewer notes)
