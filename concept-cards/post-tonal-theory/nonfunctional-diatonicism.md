# Quick Definition
The use of the diatonic collection as a referential source in post-tonal music, without functional harmony or traditional voice leading, often featuring non-triadic harmonies such as (0257) and (027).

# Core Definition
**Nonfunctional diatonicism** describes the use of diatonic collections in post-tonal music where the collection acts as a "harmonic field" from which smaller musical shapes are drawn, without the functional harmonic progressions (T-PD-D-T) or traditional voice-leading conventions of tonal music. Triads may appear but do not function in traditional relationships; more characteristically, non-triadic sonorities like (0257) and (027) serve as primary harmonies. Centricity is established through emphasis and repetition rather than functional resolution (Straus, Ch. 5, pp. 260-265).

# Prerequisites
- **Diatonic collection** -- the pitch-class set being used
- **Centricity** -- how centers are established without functional harmony

# Key Properties
1. The diatonic collection functions as a harmonic field, not a key
2. Non-triadic harmonies (especially (027) and (0257)) replace functional chords
3. Centricity is established through emphasis, not harmonic function
4. Modal ordering may shift within the same collection
5. Collection changes create large-scale harmonic motion (like modulation)
6. Minimalist music often uses diatonic collections in this way

# Construction / Recognition
1. Verify that all or most pitches belong to a single diatonic collection
2. Check for absence of functional harmonic progressions
3. Look for characteristic non-triadic sonorities (stacked fourths, (0257))
4. Identify the centric tone through non-functional means
5. Track collection changes across formal boundaries

# Context & Application
Nonfunctional diatonicism is characteristic of Stravinsky's neoclassical works, Copland's "Americana" style, and much minimalist music (Reich, Glass, Adams). The familiar diatonic sound provides accessibility while the absence of functional harmony creates a distinctly post-tonal character.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 261): Stravinsky, *Petrushka*, Russian Dance -- Uses only "white notes" for 8 measures, centered on G (G Mixolydian). At m. 9, F# replaces F (DIA1# = A Dorian). The collection change conveys harmonic shift.

**Example 2** (p. 263): Stravinsky, *The Rake's Progress* -- DIA3# collection (A Ionian). On virtually every beat: A-B-E or A-D-E (both (027)). "Stravinsky fills in that fifth not with the traditional third, but with seconds and fourths." Diatonic but neither triadic nor tonal.

**Example 3** (p. 264): Reich, *Piano Phase* -- Five notes of DIA2# [B, C#, D, E, F#] throughout the entire piece. The collection never changes, but rhythmic phase-shifting creates variety. The harmonic dyads alternate between consonant and dissonant stages as the phasing progresses.

# Common Errors
- Analyzing nonfunctional diatonic music with Roman numerals
- Assuming diatonic means tonal

# Common Confusions
- **Diatonic vs. tonal**: Diatonic describes pitch content; tonal describes harmonic function. Post-tonal music can be diatonic without being tonal.
