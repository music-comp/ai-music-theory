# Quick Definition
A pitch or pitch class that functions as the axis of inversional symmetry and thus as a center of the music, despite never actually being sounded in the passage.

# Core Definition
A **silent center** is a centric tone that is implied by the inversional symmetry of the surrounding pitches but is itself absent from the musical texture. All sounding pitches balance around this unsounded point, which functions as the center of the passage despite its absence. The concept demonstrates that centricity in post-tonal music can be structural rather than acoustically present (Straus, Ch. 5, pp. 253-254).

# Prerequisites
- **Inversional axis** -- the structural point around which notes balance
- **Centricity** -- the sense of a note functioning as focal

# Key Properties
1. The center is defined by the inversional symmetry of surrounding notes
2. Every sounding note has a partner at equal distance on the opposite side
3. The center is structurally present but acoustically absent
4. Later in a piece, the silent center may become sounded (or vice versa)
5. Both poles of a pitch-class axis can function as silent centers

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify inversional symmetry in the passage
2. Calculate the axis of symmetry
3. Check whether the axis pitch(es) are actually sounded
4. If the axis is unsounded, it functions as a silent center

# Context & Application
Silent centers demonstrate that post-tonal centricity need not rely on acoustic emphasis (loudness, duration, repetition). Instead, centricity can emerge from purely structural relationships. The concept is particularly relevant in music with strict inversional symmetry, where the center is defined by the balanced arrangement of surrounding pitches.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 253): Bartok -- Repeated Ab and Bb in the right hand, with expanding wedge in the left hand. Every pitch class is heard except A, which lies at the center of the repeated figure. A is "a kind of silent center around which everything balances." A-Eb is the pitch-class axis. Later in the piece, the music returns at T6, and now Eb (the other pole) is emphasized while A remains silent.

## Enables
- **Structural centricity** -- centricity through structure rather than emphasis

## Contrasts With

# Common Errors
- Assuming all centric tones must be acoustically prominent
- Missing silent centers when analyzing for centricity

# Common Confusions
- **Silent center vs. absent pitch**: Not every absent pitch is a silent center; the center must be defined by the inversional symmetry of the surrounding notes
