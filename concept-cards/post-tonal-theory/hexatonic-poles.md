# Quick Definition
Pairs of triads that lie opposite each other in an LP-cycle (hexatonic system), sharing no common tones and together exhausting all six pitch classes of the hexatonic collection.

# Formal Definition
Hexatonic poles are the two triads positioned diametrically opposite each other within an LP-cycle. These triads have no notes in common (they are pitch-class complements within the hexatonic collection), and together they account for all six pitch classes of the hexatonic collection (014589). The relationship between hexatonic poles involves maximum voice-leading distance within the LP-cycle (three transformations apart via L and P).

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Within each LP-cycle, hexatonic pole pairs:
- LP-cycle 1: C+ and G#- (or Ab-), E+ and C-, Ab+ and E-
- LP-cycle 2: Db+ and A-, F+ and Db-, A+ and F-
- LP-cycle 3: D+ and Bb-, F#+ and D-, Bb+ and F#-
- LP-cycle 4: Eb+ and B-, G+ and Eb-, B+ and G-

Properties of hexatonic poles:
- Zero common tones
- Combined pitch content = complete hexatonic collection
- Maximum distance (3 steps) within the LP-cycle
- One is major, one is minor

# Musical Context/Application
Hexatonic poles create striking harmonic effects:
1. Maximum contrast within a coherent system
2. Sense of harmonic distance bridged by shared collection
3. Dramatic juxtaposition in post-tonal and late-Romantic music
4. Structural markers in large-scale form

# Examples
Example 4-27: The four hexatonic systems show hexatonic poles opposite each other (e.g., C+ and G#-).

Example 4-31: Glass's Einstein on the Beach - the first and third chords of the progression are hexatonic poles.

Example 4-32: Shostakovich's String Quartet No. 4, second movement - A major is juxtaposed with F minor, which are hexatonic poles. Together they exhaust HEX0,1. The movement ends on F major, another triad from the same LP-cycle.

Example 4-33: Zwilich's Chamber Concerto - the progression reaches hexatonic pole E- from Ab+.

# Common Confusions
- Hexatonic poles are always one major and one minor triad
- They share no common tones (unlike most triadic transformations)
- The term "poles" refers to their opposite positions in the LP-cycle, not to inversional poles
- Hexatonic poles are specific to LP-cycles, not to PLR-cycles
