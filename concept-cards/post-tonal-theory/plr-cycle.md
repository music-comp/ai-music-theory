# Quick Definition
A cycle of six triads (three major and three minor) that all share a single pitch class, connected by P, L, and R transformations, with each triad differing from its neighbors by only one or two semitones.

# Formal Definition
A PLR-cycle consists of the six triads (three major and three minor) that all contain a single shared pitch class. Unlike the LP-cycle which cycles through L and P only, the PLR-cycle involves all three primary transformations (P, L, R) and their obverses. The triads lying opposite each other in the cycle are related by the obverse transformations P' (SLIDE), L', or R'.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
For the PLR-cycle containing pitch-class C:
- C+ (C is the root)
- C- (C is the root)
- Ab+ (C is the third)
- F- (C is the fifth)
- F+ (C is the fifth)...

All six triads share pitch-class C. Adjacent triads are connected by P, L, or R. Opposite triads are connected by P', L', or R'.

Structure: Each pitch class defines a unique PLR-cycle of 6 triads.

# Musical Context/Application
PLR-cycles reveal:
1. All triadic contexts for a given pitch class
2. Relationships between primary and obverse transformations
3. Maximum connectivity of triads sharing a common tone
4. Alternative to LP-cycles for organizing triadic progressions

# Common Confusions
- PLR-cycles are organized around a shared pitch class, not a shared collection
- There are 12 PLR-cycles (one for each pitch class), compared to 4 LP-cycles
- PLR-cycles include all three primary transformations plus their obverses
- LP-cycles share a collection; PLR-cycles share a pitch class
