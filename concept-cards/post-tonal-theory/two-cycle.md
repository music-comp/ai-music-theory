# Quick Definition

The interval cycle produced by moving repeatedly by whole tone (interval 2), generating two distinct cycles that correspond to the two whole-tone scales (even and odd pitch classes).

# Formal Definition

The **2-cycle (C2)** is the interval cycle generated by repeated transposition by interval class 2 (the whole tone). Because GCD(2, 12) = 2, there are exactly two distinct 2-cycles, each containing six pitch classes:

- **C2_0**: Contains the even pitch classes {0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10} = {C, D, E, F#, G#, A#}
- **C2_1**: Contains the odd pitch classes {1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11} = {C#, D#, F, G, A, B}

These two cycles are complementary and together exhaust all twelve pitch classes. Each C2 corresponds to one of the two whole-tone collections.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition

**Properties of C2:**
- Interval: 2 semitones (whole tone)
- Number of distinct cycles: GCD(2, 12) = 2
- Pitch classes per cycle: 12/2 = 6
- Set class of complete cycle: sc(02468T) = whole-tone collection

**The two cycles:**
- C2_0: 0 - 2 - 4 - 6 - 8 - 10 - (0) [C - D - E - F# - G# - A# - (C)]
- C2_1: 1 - 3 - 5 - 7 - 9 - 11 - (1) [C# - D# - F - G - A - B - (C#)]

**Relationship to C4:**
Every other note in a 2-cycle produces a 4-cycle (augmented triad):
- From C2_0: taking alternate notes gives C-E-G# (C4_0) or D-F#-A# (C4_2)

# Musical Context/Application

The 2-cycle provides:
- **Whole-tone collections**: The foundational material for whole-tone harmony
- **Melodic resources**: Scalewise motion by whole tone
- **Hexatonic saturation**: Six pitch classes with maximum dispersal
- **Affinity with C5**: Every other note of a 5-cycle produces a 2-cycle

The C2 is central to:
- Debussy's whole-tone passages
- Impressionist harmonic vocabulary
- Symmetrical scalar structures
- Maximally even hexachords

The complete C2 produces the maximally even hexachord sc(02468T).

# Examples

**Ives, "The Cage"** (Example 4-8):
- Melody consists of five-note segments from the two C2 cycles
- Cyclic origins clearly audible in the whole-tone melodic motion

**Bartok, String Quartet No. 6, fourth movement** (Example 4-6):
- First violin approaches cadence with ascending C2: A-B-C#-Eb
- Whole-tone motion provides systematic approach to cadential goal

**Affinity with C5:**
In Ives's "The Cage," the accompanying chords are five-note segments of C5. Taking every other note of these C5 chords produces C2 segments, demonstrating the affinity between the cycles:
- C5: G#-C#-F#-B-E (five-note segment)
- Every other note: G#-F#-E = segment of C2_0

# Common Confusions

- **Two cycles, not one**: Unlike C1 and C5, C2 does not pass through all 12 pitch classes; there are two distinct complementary cycles
- **C2_0 vs. C2_1**: The subscript identifies the pitch-class content (even or odd), not the starting note
- **C2 vs. whole-tone scale**: C2 is the abstract cyclic structure; the whole-tone scale is its musical realization
- **Segments vs. complete cycles**: Partial C2 traversals are common; complete six-note cycles produce the whole-tone collection
