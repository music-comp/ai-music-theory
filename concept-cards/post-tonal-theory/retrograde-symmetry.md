# Quick Definition
Retrograde symmetry occurs when a series's interval succession reads the same forward and backward, reducing the 48 series forms to 24 distinct orderings because each P-form equals some RI-form.

# Core Definition
A series has retrograde symmetry when "the intervals are the same reading from right to left as they are reading from left to right. This means that for every P-form of the series, there will be an RI-form that presents the twelve pitch classes in exactly the same order. Similarly, for every I-form there will be an identical R-form" (Straus, p. 360). As a result, "there are only twenty-four rather than the usual forty-eight distinct orderings" (p. 360).

# Prerequisites
- **Series class** -- understanding the 48-form family
- **Retrograde and retrograde-inversion** -- the equivalences created by symmetry

# Key Properties
1. Interval succession is palindromic
2. P_n = RI_m for some m
3. I_n = R_m for some m
4. Only 24 distinct series forms instead of 48
5. "Retrograde symmetry, like intervallic concentration, is a characteristic of Webern's style" (p. 360)
6. Often reflected in the formal structure of pieces (ABA forms)

# Construction / Recognition
- List the 11 ordered pc intervals
- Check if the sequence is a palindrome
- If so, the series is retrograde-symmetrical
- Construct the matrix: identical P and RI forms will be apparent

# Context & Application
Retrograde symmetry is particularly associated with Webern's aesthetic of economy and concentration. It applies "not only to the construction of the series but... to the organization of musical phrases and sections as well" (p. 360). Webern's formal designs often mirror the palindromic structure of his series.

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 360--363): Webern, String Quartet, op. 28 -- all discrete dyads are ic1, with ic3 and ic4 connecting them. The interval succession is retrograde-symmetrical. The formal layout of Variation 4 (ABA) mirrors the series symmetry. Writing out a matrix reveals only 24 distinct orderings.

# Common Errors
- Assuming all series have 48 distinct forms (retrograde-symmetrical ones have 24)
- Confusing retrograde symmetry of the series with retrograde symmetry of musical form (though Webern often aligns both)

# Common Confusions
- **Series symmetry vs. formal symmetry**: Retrograde symmetry of the series is about the interval succession; formal symmetry is about phrase and section structure. In Webern, these often coincide.
