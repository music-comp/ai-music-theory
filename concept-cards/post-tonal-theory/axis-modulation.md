# Quick Definition
The process by which music shifts from one inversional axis to another, analogous to tonal modulation, often with the interval of axis change composing-out a surface motivic interval.

# Core Definition
**Axis modulation** describes how the twelve inversional axes can function analogously to the twelve major/minor keys of traditional tonality. Music can be organized around a particular axis, "modulate" to another axis, and shape large-scale form through axis changes. Crucially, the interval of transposition between successive axes often replicates surface motivic intervals, creating coherence between small-scale melodic ideas and large-scale structural motion (Straus, Ch. 5, pp. 257-260).

# Prerequisites
- **Twelve axes of symmetry** -- the twelve possible inversional axes
- **Motion from axis to axis** -- the basic concept of shifting axes

# Key Properties
1. Twelve inversional axes parallel twelve major/minor keys
2. Axis changes can articulate formal boundaries
3. The interval of axis change may mirror surface melodic intervals (composing-out)
4. T6 transposition preserves the same pitch-class axis (both poles swap)
5. Music may alternate between axes rather than moving directionally

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the inversional axis for a passage (calculate the sum of balanced pairs)
2. When the sum changes, the axis has modulated
3. Compare the interval of axis change to prominent surface intervals
4. Track axis changes to understand large-scale form

# Context & Application
Axis modulation provides a post-tonal analogue to tonal modulation for organizing large-scale form. Just as tonal music moves from key to key to create formal structure, post-tonal music can move from axis to axis. The technique is especially prominent in Bartok.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 257): Bartok, *Mikrokosmos*, no. 141, "Subject and Reflection" -- First phrase balances around unsounded E4 (pc axis E-Bb). At m. 15, axis shifts to B5, seven semitones higher. The T7 axis motion composes-out the initial right-hand melody (Bb rising to F). Later, the axis alternates B4 and F4 (same pc axis B-F).

**Example 2** (p. 258): Bartok, *Sonata for Two Pianos and Percussion* -- Opening motive is symmetrical and rises by one semitone. First statements symmetrical around F# (axis 0-6). Later statements around G then Db (axis 1-7). The axis shifts up one semitone, mirroring the motivic semitone ascent.

# Common Errors
- Assuming axis motion must be directional (alternation is equally valid)
- Confusing axis modulation with transposition of pitch material

# Common Confusions
- **Axis change vs. transposition**: Transposing the music changes the axis, but changing the axis does not necessarily involve transposition of all material
