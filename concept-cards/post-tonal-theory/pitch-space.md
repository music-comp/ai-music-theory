# Quick Definition
Pitch space is the linear, extended space ranging from the lowest to highest audible tones, where each pitch occupies a unique position.

# Formal Definition
Pitch space is an extended linear space ranging in equal-tempered semitones from the lowest to the highest audible tone. Unlike the cyclical pitch-class space, pitch space provides distinct positions for all individual pitches. The traditional grand staff illustrates pitch space by providing distinct positions for all pitches, including the 88 pitches represented by piano keys.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Pitch space can be measured in semitones from a reference point:
- Middle C (C4) = reference point 0 (or MIDI 60)
- Each semitone up = +1
- Each semitone down = -1
- The space extends linearly in both directions

Contrast with pitch-class space:
- Pitch space: linear, infinite (bounded only by audibility)
- Pitch-class space: circular, contains only 12 elements

# Musical Context/Application
Music unfolds simultaneously in both pitch space and pitch-class space. While pitch-class analysis reveals abstract relationships between equivalent pitches in different registers, pitch-space analysis addresses specific registral placement, contour, and actual distances between notes. Both perspectives are valuable for understanding post-tonal music.

# Examples
The text uses the analogy of time: "As our lives unfold in time, each hour and each day are uniquely located in linear time, never to be repeated." Similarly, each pitch in a piece occupies a unique location in pitch space, even though it belongs to a pitch class that may be represented elsewhere.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes conflate pitch space with pitch-class space. Pitch space is linear and potentially infinite (limited only by human hearing), while pitch-class space is circular and contains exactly twelve elements. A melody that ascends through an octave travels through pitch space but returns to its starting point in pitch-class space.
