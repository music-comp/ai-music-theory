# Quick Definition
Pitch space is the linear, extended space ranging in equal-tempered semitones from the lowest to the highest audible tone, where each pitch occupies a unique position.

# Core Definition
Pitch space is an extended linear space ranging in equal-tempered semitones from the lowest to the highest audible tone. The traditional grand staff illustrates pitch space by providing distinct positions for all pitches, including the 88 pitches represented by the piano keyboard. In pitch space, each pitch has a unique location that is never repeated. Pitch intervals are measured in this space.

# Prerequisites
- **Pitch** — the individual elements located in pitch space

# Key Properties
1. Linear (not cyclical): extends from low to high without wrapping
2. Each pitch occupies a unique position
3. Measured in semitones from any reference point
4. Can accommodate intervals of any size, including compound intervals
5. The grand staff is a good illustration of pitch space

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Arrange all audible pitches in order from lowest to highest
2. Space them equally by semitone

## To Recognize:
1. A representation where each specific pitch has its own unique location
2. No repetition or cycling of positions

# Context & Application
Music unfolds simultaneously in both pitch space and pitch-class space. Pitch-space analysis captures register, contour, and actual distances between notes. Pitch intervals (ordered and unordered) are measured in pitch space. The contrast between linear pitch space and circular pitch-class space is fundamental: "Just as our lives unfold simultaneously in linear and modular time, music unfolds simultaneously in pitch and pitch-class space" (Straus).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Applying mod 12 to pitch-space calculations
  **Correction**: Mod 12 applies to pitch-class space. In pitch space, a minor ninth is 13 semitones, not 1.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking pitch space and pitch-class space are interchangeable
  **Clarification**: Pitch space is linear and infinite (bounded only by audibility); pitch-class space is circular and contains exactly 12 elements. A melody ascending through an octave traverses pitch space but returns to its starting point in pitch-class space.
