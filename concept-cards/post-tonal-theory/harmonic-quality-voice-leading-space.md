# Quick Definition
The correlation between a set class's position in voice-leading space and its traditional harmonic character, with chromatic/tense harmonies at one end and even/consonant harmonies at the other.

# Formal Definition
Voice-leading spaces for both trichords and tetrachords exhibit a consistent structural layout where the most compact, compressed, chromatic set classes lie at one extreme of the space, while the most dispersed, open, evenly-spaced set classes lie at the opposite extreme. This layout enables measurement of both the distance between harmonies and the direction of harmonic progression in terms of tension and resolution.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
For trichords:
- Most chromatic extreme: (012) - the chromatic trichord
- Most even extreme: (048) - the augmented triad (maximally even trichord)
- Maximum distance between them: 6 semitones of offset

For tetrachords:
- Most chromatic extreme: (0123) - the chromatic tetrachord
- Most even extreme: (0369) - the diminished seventh chord (maximally even tetrachord)

The concept of "maximally even" refers to the most equal way of arranging notes around the 12-pitch-class clockface.

# Musical Context/Application
Harmonic quality in voice-leading space enables analysts to:
1. Interpret progressions as moving toward tension (chromaticness) or relaxation (evenness)
2. Understand traditional consonance/dissonance distinctions in spatial terms
3. Trace the harmonic trajectory of a passage
4. Identify structural arrival points based on position in the space

# Common Confusions
- "Consonant" and "dissonant" here refer to traditional perceptions, not absolute acoustic properties
- The spatial relationship describes voice-leading distance, not functional harmonic relationships
- Maximally even sets (augmented triad, diminished seventh) are considered consonant in this spatial sense despite their traditional dissonance in tonal contexts
