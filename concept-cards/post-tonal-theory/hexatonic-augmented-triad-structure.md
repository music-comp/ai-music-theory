# Quick Definition
Each hexatonic collection is composed of exactly two augmented triads related at T1, and each collection contains two of the four possible augmented triads while excluding the other two.

# Core Definition
The **hexatonic collection** can be understood as the combination of two augmented triads related at T1. Since there are four augmented triads in total (C4_0 through C4_3), each hexatonic collection contains a specific pair. This construction parallels the octatonic collection's construction from two diminished seventh chords (C3s). The augmented triad pairing determines the hexatonic collection's identity and connects it to the LP-cycle (hexatonic system) of triadic transformation theory (Straus, Ch. 5, p. 273).

# Prerequisites
- **Hexatonic collection** -- the 6-note set
- **Four-cycle (C4)** -- augmented triads as C4 cycles

# Key Properties
1. Each hexatonic = two augmented triads related at T1
2. Each collection contains exactly 2 of the 4 augmented triads
3. The defining semitone pair connects one note of each augmented triad
4. The four hexatonic collections partition the augmented triad pairs:
   - HEX0,1: C-E-G# plus C#-F-A
   - HEX1,2: C#-F-A plus D-F#-A#
   - HEX2,3: D-F#-A# plus D#-G-B
   - HEX3,4: D#-G-B plus E-G#-C
5. When written as a scale, alternates intervals 1 and 3

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify two augmented triads related at T1 in the passage
2. Combine their pitch-class content
3. The result is one of the four hexatonic collections
4. Alternatively, look for the alternating 1-3-1-3-1-3 interval pattern

# Context & Application
The augmented triad structure explains why the hexatonic collection contains major and minor triads (each augmented triad can be "resolved" by semitone to produce major or minor triads), connecting it to the LP-cycle where P and L transformations move single notes by semitone between augmented triad tones and their semitone neighbors.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 274): Schoenberg, *Little Piano Pieces*, op. 19, no. 2 -- Six-note chord represents HEX2,3. The highest three notes form one augmented triad, the lowest three form another.

**Example 2** (p. 274): Bartok, *String Quartet No. 2*, first movement -- Upper three parts arpeggiate augmented triad F-A-C#. Far below, cello centers on A-minor triad A-C-E. Together: HEX0,1.

**Example 3** (p. 275): Babbitt, *String Quartet No. 2* -- First violin and viola play only HEX0,1; second violin and cello play only HEX2,3 (the complementary hexatonic). Both feature combinations of i3 and i4.

# Common Errors
- Confusing which augmented triad pairs produce hexatonic vs. whole-tone collections (two C4s with even subscripts or two with odd subscripts produce whole-tone, not hexatonic)

# Common Confusions
- **Hexatonic vs. whole-tone from augmented triads**: C4_0 + C4_1 = hexatonic; C4_0 + C4_2 = whole-tone. The key is the T1 relationship between the augmented triads.
