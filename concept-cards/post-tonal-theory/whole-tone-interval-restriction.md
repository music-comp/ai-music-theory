# Quick Definition
The whole-tone collection contains only even interval classes (ic2, ic4, ic6), entirely lacking semitones, minor thirds, and perfect fourths/fifths, which produces its characteristic floating, directionless quality.

# Core Definition
The **whole-tone collection** contains only even intervals: interval-classes 2, 4, and 6. It completely lacks ic1 (semitone), ic3 (minor third), and ic5 (perfect fourth/fifth). This maximal intervallic restriction -- the most restricted of any of the four referential collections -- produces the collection's characteristic sound: floating, non-directional, without the leading-tone pull of semitones or the grounding effect of perfect fifths (Straus, Ch. 5, pp. 268-269).

# Key Properties
1. Contains ONLY ic2, ic4, ic6 (all even)
2. Contains NO ic1, ic3, ic5 (no semitones, minor thirds, or perfect fifths)
3. Interval vector: [060603]
4. No leading tones (no semitone approach to any pitch)
5. No perfect consonances (no P4/P5)
6. Maximum transpositional symmetry (maps onto itself at T0, T2, T4, T6, T8, T10)
7. Only one possible scalar ordering: 2-2-2-2-2-2

# Construction / Recognition
1. Check for absence of semitones in the passage
2. Verify that all intervals are even (M2, M3, tritone, or their compounds)
3. Confirm pitch-class content matches WT0 or WT1
4. Listen for the characteristic "floating" quality

# Context & Application
The absence of semitones and perfect fifths removes two of the most powerful means of creating directed motion in music. Without leading tones and without dominant-tonic relationships, whole-tone music tends toward stasis and ambiguity. This quality made it attractive to Debussy and other impressionists, and it contributes to the dreamlike, floating quality of whole-tone passages.

**Example 1** (p. 269): The inclusion lattice shows only 3 trichord types (024, 026, 048), 3 tetrachord types (0246, 0248, 0268), and 1 pentachord type (02468). Compare this to the diatonic collection's much richer subset structure.

# Common Confusions
- **Absence vs. presence**: The whole-tone collection is defined as much by what it lacks (ic1, ic3, ic5) as by what it contains
