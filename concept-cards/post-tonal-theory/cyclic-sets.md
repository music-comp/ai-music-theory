# Quick Definition

Pitch-class sets that consist of an entire interval cycle or a contiguous segment of a cycle, forming harmonies and melodies with characteristic cyclic properties.

# Formal Definition

A **cyclic set** is a pitch-class set that comprises either a complete interval cycle or a contiguous segment of one. Cyclic sets inherit the structural properties of their generating cycle and play a prominent role in shaping both harmony and melody in post-tonal music. Complete cyclic sets are inversionally symmetrical and transpositionally limited (they map onto themselves under certain transpositions).

Types of cyclic sets:
- **Complete cycles**: The entire cycle (e.g., complete C3 = diminished seventh chord)
- **Cyclic segments**: Contiguous portions of a cycle (e.g., five notes of C5 = pentatonic)

The large referential collections of post-tonal theory have cyclic origins:
- Chromatic scale = C1
- Whole-tone collection = C2 (complete)
- Octatonic collection = combination of two C3s
- Hexatonic collection = combination of two C4s
- Diatonic/pentatonic = segments of C5

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition

To identify cyclic sets:
1. Arrange the pitch classes in ascending order
2. Calculate the intervals between consecutive pitch classes
3. If all intervals are equal (or equal to a single value and its complement mod 12), the set is cyclic
4. Identify the generating interval

**Complete cyclic sets (set classes):**
| Cycle | Set Class | Name |
|-------|-----------|------|
| C1 | (0123456789TE) | Chromatic aggregate |
| C2 | (02468T) | Whole-tone scale |
| C3 | (0369) | Diminished seventh |
| C4 | (048) | Augmented triad |
| C5 | (full cycle) | Circle of fifths (all 12 pcs) |
| C6 | (06) | Tritone |

**Common cyclic segments:**
- 5-note C5 segment: sc(02479) - pentatonic
- 7-note C5 segment: sc(013568T) - diatonic
- 4-note C1 segment: sc(0123) - chromatic tetrachord

# Musical Context/Application

Cyclic sets provide:
- **Harmonic vocabulary**: Chords built from cyclic segments
- **Melodic resources**: Scales and lines following cyclic patterns
- **Referential collections**: Large-scale pitch organization
- **Transpositional relationships**: Cyclic sets often relate by T at the generating interval

Applications:
- Quartal/quintal chords (C5 segments)
- Whole-tone passages (C2)
- Diminished-based harmony (C3)
- Augmented triad usage (C4)
- Chromatic clusters (C1 segments)

# Examples

**Ives, "The Cage"** (Example 4-8):
- Melody: five-note segments of C2 (whole-tone segments)
- Accompanying chords: five-note segments of C5 (quartal harmony)
- Both melody and harmony are cyclic sets from different cycles
- Chords arranged registrally to reveal their cyclic (stacked-fourth) origins

**Bartok, String Quartet No. 3** (Example 4-9):
- Opening chord: four-note segment of C1 (C#-D-D#-E) - chromatic cluster
- Opening melody: eight-note complementary segment of C1 (F-F#-G-G#-A-A#-B-B#)
- Together, chord and melody complete the aggregate through C1 segmentation
- Final chord: four-note segment of C5 (C#-G#-D#-A#) - quartal harmony

**Derivation of larger collections** (referenced in text):
- Octatonic: combination of two C3s (e.g., C3_0 + C3_1)
- Hexatonic: combination of one even and one odd C4 (e.g., C4_0 + C4_1)
- Whole-tone: combination of three non-adjacent C6s (e.g., C6_0 + C6_2 + C6_4)

# Common Confusions

- **Cyclic set vs. interval cycle**: The interval cycle is the abstract pattern; a cyclic set is a concrete pitch-class set derived from it
- **Complete vs. segment**: A segment of a cycle is still a cyclic set, but with fewer pitch classes than the complete cycle
- **Combination collections**: Octatonic and hexatonic are not single cycles but combinations of two cycles
- **Cyclic vs. symmetrical**: All complete cyclic sets are symmetrical, but not all symmetrical sets are cyclic
