# Quick Definition
Progressions of triads whose roots trace a motivic pattern from elsewhere in the piece, creating coherence between melodic motives and harmonic succession.

# Formal Definition
Motivic triadic progressions organize the roots of successive triads according to a motivic pattern derived from melodic material in the composition. The root succession forms the same set class as a prominent melodic motive, creating structural unity between surface and background, between melody and harmony. This technique composes-out motivic ideas at the level of harmonic progression.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Identification process:
1. Identify prominent melodic motives (set classes)
2. Examine root progressions of triadic passages
3. Compare root succession to melodic set classes
4. Recognize motivic connections at multiple structural levels

Common patterns:
- Melodic trichord composed-out as three triad roots
- Tetrachordal motive reflected in four-triad progression
- Melodic intervals reflected in root intervals

# Musical Context/Application
Motivic triadic progressions create:
1. Unity between melodic and harmonic dimensions
2. Multi-level structural coherence
3. Non-functional but highly organized progressions
4. Connection between foreground motives and middleground structure

# Examples
Example 4-37: Shostakovich's String Quartet No. 12, second movement - A twelve-note cello melody ends with D, Eb, F. These notes return as the highest notes of subsequent triads. Additionally, the melody embeds sc(0137), and the succession of triadic roots (G, Ab, Bb, D) describes another member of (0137). The triads harmonize the melody in a nonfunctional, parallel way while the root progression follows a motivic path.

# Common Confusions
- The motivic connection is in the root succession, not necessarily in voice leading
- Triads may be major, minor, or mixed within the progression
- The melodic motive may be transposed or inverted in the root progression
- Motivic triadic progressions differ from both functional and transformation-based progressions
