# Quick Definition
The four orderings of a series (P, R, I, RI) have systematic intervallic relationships: P and RI share the same intervals in reverse order, P and I have complementary intervals in the same order, and P and R have complementary intervals in reverse order.

# Formal Definition
Series that have the same ordering (P and P, I and I, R and R, or RI and RI) are said to be *prime-related* and have the same intervals in the same order. The relationships between different ordering types are:
- **P and I**: complementary intervals in the same order (inversion-related)
- **P and RI**: same intervals in reverse order (retrograde-inversion-related)
- **P and R**: complementary intervals in reverse order (retrograde-related)
- **I and R**: same intervals in reverse order
- **I and RI**: complementary intervals in reverse order
- **R and RI**: complementary intervals in same order

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Given interval sequence of P = <a, b, c, d, e, f, g, h, i, j, k>:
- **P-related forms**: <a, b, c, d, e, f, g, h, i, j, k> (same intervals, same order)
- **I-related forms**: <12-a, 12-b, 12-c, ...> (complementary, same order)
- **R-related forms**: <12-k, 12-j, 12-i, ...> (complementary, reverse order)
- **RI-related forms**: <k, j, i, h, g, f, e, d, c, b, a> (same intervals, reverse order)

Complement of interval n = (12 - n) mod 12

# Musical Context/Application
- These relationships explain why series forms sound related despite different orderings
- All 48 forms share common intervallic features (some intervals may predominate or be excluded)
- Understanding these relationships helps identify series forms during analysis
- Composers exploit these relationships for unity and variety
- The close relationship between P and RI (same intervals reversed) is particularly audible

# Examples
Schoenberg, String Quartet No. 4 intervals:
| Ordering | Intervals |
|----------|-----------|
| Prime | 11, 8, 1, 7, 10, 1, 8, 8, 11, 11, 5 |
| Retrograde | 7, 1, 1, 4, 4, 11, 2, 5, 11, 4, 1 |
| Inversion | 1, 4, 11, 5, 2, 11, 4, 4, 1, 1, 7 |
| Retrograde-Inversion | 5, 11, 11, 8, 8, 1, 10, 7, 1, 8, 11 |

Notice: intervals 1, 4, 8, 11 predominate; intervals 3, 9 are excluded in all forms.

# Common Confusions
- Expecting all four orderings to sound completely different—they share intervallic profiles
- Forgetting that "complementary" means (12 - n), not "opposite" in some other sense
- Not recognizing the close audible relationship between P and RI forms
