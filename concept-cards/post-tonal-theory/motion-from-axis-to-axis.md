# Quick Definition
The large-scale organization of post-tonal music through changes in inversional axis, analogous to modulation between keys in tonal music, often motivically related to surface pitch intervals.

# Formal Definition
Motion from axis to axis describes how post-tonal music can be shaped by changes in the governing inversional axis, similar to how tonal music is shaped by modulation between keys. The interval of transposition between successive axes can relate motivically to surface melodic intervals, creating coherence between small-scale melodic ideas and large-scale harmonic structure. Each axis change represents a shift in the pitch-class center around which the music balances.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
When the axis changes:
1. The index sum changes
2. The poles shift by the transposition interval (divided by 2 on each side)
3. The interval of axis change may mirror surface motives

Example: If a melody emphasizes T7 (rising P5), the axis might also shift by 7 semitones (from E4 axis to B4 axis).

Axis relationships:
- T1 on the music shifts the axis by a "half semitone" (moves between adjacent axes)
- T6 preserves the axis (transposition by tritone keeps the same poles)

# Musical Context/Application
Motion from axis to axis provides:
1. Large-scale structural organization
2. Parallel to tonal modulation
3. Motivic connection between surface and structure
4. Formal articulation through axis changes

# Examples
Example 5-11: Bartók's Mikrokosmos no. 141 "Subject and Reflection" - The first phrase (mm. 1-14) balances around E4 (unsounded). At measure 15, the tempo changes and the axis shifts to B5, seven semitones higher. This T7 motion composes-out the initial melodic idea in the right hand (Bb rising to F). Later the axis alternates between B4 and F4 (same pc axis: B-F).

Example 5-12: Bartók's Sonata for Two Pianos and Percussion - The opening motive is symmetrical and rises by one semitone. The first statements are symmetrical around F# (axis 0-6), the later statements around G then Db (axis 1-7). The axis shifts up one semitone, mirroring the motivic semitone ascent.

# Common Confusions
- Axis motion is not the same as transposition of pitch material (though they may coincide)
- The interval of axis shift can be conceptualized in two ways (up or down by complementary intervals)
- Motion between axes can be gradual or abrupt
- Some passages may alternate between axes rather than moving directionally
