# Quick Definition
Nodes and arrows are the basic components of transformational networks, where nodes contain musical elements (notes or sets) and arrows show the operations connecting them.

# Formal Definition
In transformational analysis, transpositional and inversional relationships are represented using networks consisting of nodes and arrows. Nodes are circles containing musical elements such as individual notes or pitch-class sets. Arrows indicate the operations (Tn or In) that connect the nodes, showing how the music moves from one element to another. When two networks have the same arrow structure, they are called isographic.

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Network components:
- **Nodes**: Circles or boxes containing musical content
  - May contain single pitch classes, sets, or set names
  - Represent "objects" in the musical space
- **Arrows**: Lines with direction showing transformations
  - Labeled with operations (T0, T3, I5, etc.)
  - Direction indicates which element transforms to which
  - Double-headed arrows used for inversions (since In is its own inverse)

# Musical Context/Application
Networks provide a visual representation of musical relationships and transformational processes. They show not just what sets are present but how the music moves between them. Comparing networks reveals structural similarities between passages or pieces, even when the actual pitch content differs.

# Examples
**Example 2-12** (Stravinsky, Agon): Two isographic networks showing:
- (a) Note-level relationships: Bb --T3--> Db
- (b) Set-level relationships: [G, Ab, Bb, B] --T3--> [Bb, B, Db, D]

The same T3 operation moves between notes and between sets, demonstrating how small-scale and large-scale relationships mirror each other.

# Common Confusions
Networks represent relationships, not chronological order. While arrows often follow the temporal order of the music, they primarily show transformational connections. Two networks are isographic when their arrows have the same labels, regardless of what's in the nodes.
