# Quick Definition
Twelve-tone music encompasses many individual approaches; Straus surveys five: Webern (derivation), Schoenberg (hexachordal combinatoriality), Stravinsky (rotational arrays), Crawford Seeger (multilevel rotation), and Babbitt (trichordal arrays).

# Core Definition
"Twelve-tone music is not a uniform or monolithic enterprise. Twelve-tone composers share a premise -- that interesting and expressive music can be written with reference to a precomposed ordering of the twelve pitch classes -- but when they actually get down to writing music, they do so in unique and individual ways. Twelve-tone composition is a world of musical possibilities, and within that world, each composer has discovered or created a new country or province with its own distinctive landscape" (Straus, p. 334).

# Key Properties
The five approaches surveyed:
1. **Webern**: Derived series; motivic concentration; multi-level projection of a single set class
2. **Schoenberg**: Hexachordal combinatoriality; twelve-tone areas; large-scale harmonic motion
3. **Stravinsky**: Rotational arrays; four untransposed forms; common tones; centricity; non-aggregate-based
4. **Crawford Seeger**: Multilevel rotation; triple passacaglia; sub-twelve-tone series
5. **Babbitt**: Trichordal arrays; multi-dimensional aggregates; gradual series revelation

# Construction / Recognition
When analyzing a twelve-tone piece, determine which approach is being used:
- Derived series with concentrated motives = Webernian
- Combinatorial hexachords with area organization = Schoenbergian
- Rotational arrays with centricity = Stravinskian
- Multilevel rotation with short series = Crawford Seeger
- Trichordal arrays with multi-dimensional aggregates = Babbittian

# Context & Application
Each approach produces a distinctive sound world. Understanding these five approaches provides a framework for analyzing most twelve-tone music from the Second Viennese School through mid-century American serialism.

# Common Errors
- Treating twelve-tone music as monolithic or uniform
- Applying Schoenberg's methods to analyze Stravinsky's music (or vice versa)

# Common Confusions
- **Shared premise, different practice**: All five composers use ordered pitch-class successions, but their organizational methods differ profoundly
