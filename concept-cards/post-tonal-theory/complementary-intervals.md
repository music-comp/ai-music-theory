# Quick Definition
Complementary intervals are pairs of ordered pitch-class intervals that add up to 12 (or 0 mod 12), representing the same interval class traversed in opposite directions.

# Formal Definition
In pitch-class space, two ordered pitch-class intervals are complements mod 12 if they add up to 12 (or equivalently, 0). When you reverse the order of two pitch classes, the resulting ordered pitch-class intervals are complements of each other. Traditional tonal theory calls these pairs inversions (like minor thirds and major sixths, or perfect fourths and perfect fifths). Each interval class comprises a pair of complementary ordered pitch-class intervals (except interval class 0 and 6, which are their own complements).

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Complementary pairs (mod 12):
| Pair 1 | Pair 2 | Interval Class |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| 0 | 12 (= 0) | 0 |
| 1 | 11 (= -1) | 1 |
| 2 | 10 (= -2) | 2 |
| 3 | 9 (= -3) | 3 |
| 4 | 8 (= -4) | 4 |
| 5 | 7 (= -5) | 5 |
| 6 | 6 | 6 |

Property: If opci from x to y is n, then opci from y to x is 12 - n (= -n mod 12).

# Musical Context/Application
Complementary intervals arise naturally when analyzing directed motion. Ascending by 4 semitones has the same endpoint as descending by 8 semitones. Understanding complementation helps in recognizing inversional relationships and calculating transposition and inversion operations efficiently.

# Common Confusions
Complementary intervals belong to the same interval class but have opposite directional implications. An opci of 11 (ascending major seventh) and opci 1 (ascending semitone) are complements; both belong to interval class 1, but they move in opposite directions around the pitch-class clockface.
