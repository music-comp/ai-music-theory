# Quick Definition
Large pitch-class sets that serve as sources of pitch material in post-tonal music, from which smaller surface motives are drawn to unify sections and create large-scale harmonic structure.

# Formal Definition
Referential collections are large sets used by composers as sources of pitch material for entire sections of music. By drawing all or most smaller sets from a single large referential set, composers can unify entire sections. By changing the referential set, composers create a sense of large-scale movement from one harmonic area to another. Four collections have attracted extensive compositional and theoretical attention: the diatonic, octatonic, hexatonic, and whole-tone collections. Each has remarkable structural properties, harmonic resources, and a distinctive sound world.

Each collection has characteristic:

- Subset structure
- Symmetry properties
- Scalar orderings

# Musical Context/Application
Referential collections provide:
1. Source material for surface motives
2. Harmonic coherence within sections
3. Large-scale form through collection changes
4. Link between post-tonal and earlier/other musical traditions

# Examples
The four collections appear extensively in:
- Diatonic: Stravinsky's neoclassical works, minimalism (Reich)
- Octatonic: Bartók, Stravinsky, Messiaen
- Whole-tone: Debussy, impressionist works
- Hexatonic: Schoenberg, Bartók, Babbitt

Collections often interact with each other within single works (see Collectional Interaction).

# Common Confusions
- Collections are abstract pitch-class sets, not specific scales or modes
- The same collection can support different centers and orderings
- Collections can interact and overlap (share common subsets)
- Using a collection does not imply traditional functional relationships
