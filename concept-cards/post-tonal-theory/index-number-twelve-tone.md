# Quick Definition
The index number is the sum (mod 12) of corresponding pitch classes in two inversionally related series forms, determining which dyads, trichords, and larger collections are held invariant under inversion.

# Formal Definition
The *index number* is calculated by adding corresponding pitch classes from inversionally related series forms. If P_i and I_j are inversionally related, their index number n = i + j (mod 12). This index determines:
- Which dyads are formed when the series sound note-against-note
- Which subsets map onto each other or onto themselves under the inversion
- The axis of symmetry around which the inversion balances

# Mathematical Formulation/Recognition
Calculating index number:
- For series pair: n = (first note of P) + (first note of I) mod 12
- For invariant subsets: sum of corresponding pitch classes = n (consistent for all pairs)
- For P_m and I_j: index = m + j (mod 12)
- For P_m and RI_j: index = m + j (where RI_j = retrograde of I_j)

Applications:
- Finding combinatorial pairs: P_m combines with I_n to form aggregates when their hexachords are I-related at specific index
- Predicting invariants: subsets related at I_n remain intact
- Identifying series forms: if intervals match in reverse, calculate index from pitch sums

# Musical Context/Application
- Index number determines axis of symmetry (the pitch/pitch-class around which inversion balances)
- Constant index throughout a passage creates consistent vertical dyads
- Composers maintain index to preserve invariant relationships
- Used to identify RI-forms during twelve-counting
- Essential for understanding combinatorial and invariant relationships

# Examples
- Webern, "Wie bin ich froh!": P_7 and RI_7 have index 2 (7 + 7 = 14 → 2 mod 12)
- Axis of inversion: C#-G (sum to 2)
- Webern, Piano Variations, op. 27: Constant index 6 throughout second movement
- All P-I pairs are transposed to maintain index 6
- Seven dyads recur, all summing to 6

# Common Confusions
- Confusing index number with transposition level—they are different concepts
- Forgetting to reduce to mod 12 when sums exceed 11
- Not recognizing that index determines both axis and invariant relationships
- Applying wrong index when series forms have been transposed
