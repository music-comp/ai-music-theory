# Quick Definition
Quartal chords of six or more notes, where consonant perfect-fourth stacks become dissonant at six notes due to the emergent tritone, with progressively reduced linear potential as chord size increases.

# Formal Definition
Chords built of superimposed perfect fourths are consonant to and include the five-note chord. The six-note chord results in a categorical change in tension because of the sharp dissonant interval. These six- (or more) note chords belong to the dissonant group of three-, four-, and five-note chords containing an augmented fourth. As the number of chord members increases, the linear potential decreases. Twelve different notes may be placed a perfect fourth apart before one is repeated (twelve-note chords by fourths).

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
**Consonant/Dissonant Boundary:**
- 5-note perfect fourth stack: consonant
- 6-note perfect fourth stack: dissonant (tritone emerges)

**Tritone Emergence:**
In six perfect fourths:
- Notes 1-6 span 30 semitones
- Notes 1-6 reduced to one octave = 6 semitones = tritone

**Twelve-Note Maximum:**
12 different notes in perfect fourths before repetition:
C-F-Bb-Eb-Ab-Db-Gb-Cb-Fb-Bbb-Ebb-Abb

**Thirteenth Chord Equivalence:**
Thirteenth chords and multi-note quartal chords often contain the same notes. The difference is aural:
- If thirds overrun: sounds like thirteenth
- If fourths overrun: sounds like quartal
- Equal thirds and fourths: pivotal structure, usable as either

# Musical Context/Application
As multi-note quartal chords grow larger, they require special handling:
1. Parallel movement provides temporary suppleness
2. Interrupting unison provides contrast
3. Suddenly enriched dominant prevents harmonic suffocation

To avoid muddy sounds:
- Place larger intervals at the bottom
- Omit a member
- Use orchestral timbre separation

Multi-note quartal chords are effective for static harmonic situations where the linear reduction is acceptable.

# Common Confusions
Students may not recognize that six-note quartal chords are categorically different from five-note chords. The emergence of the tritone at six notes creates a qualitative change, not merely a quantitative addition. This boundary between consonant and dissonant groups is fundamental to working with multi-note quartal structures.
