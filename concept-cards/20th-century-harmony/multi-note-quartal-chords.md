# Quick Definition
Quartal chords of six or more notes, where the consonant boundary ends at five notes and the six-note chord introduces a categorical change in tension through the emergent tritone, with progressively reduced linear potential as size increases.

# Core Definition
Chords built of superimposed perfect fourths are consonant to and include the five-note chord. The six-note chord results in a categorical change in tension because of the sharp dissonant interval (tritone between first and sixth notes). These six-or-more-note chords belong to the dissonant group. Thirteenth chords and multi-note quartal chords often contain the same notes; the similarity is theoretical, not aural. If thirds overrun the chord, the ear hears a thirteenth; if fourths overrun, a quartal chord sounds. As chord members increase, linear potential decreases. Twelve different notes may be placed a perfect fourth apart before one repeats.

# Key Properties
1. Consonant boundary: five notes (no tritone)
2. Six notes: categorical tension change (tritone emerges)
3. Same notes as thirteenth chords but different sound (interval dominance determines identity)
4. Equal thirds and fourths = pivotal structure (either/both categories)
5. Linear potential decreases with size
6. Twelve notes possible before repetition
7. Prevent suffocation: parallel movement, interrupting unison, enriched dominant
8. Avoid muddiness: larger intervals at bottom, omit members, separate by timbre

# Construction / Recognition
- Count the size: 5 notes = consonant; 6+ = dissonant
- Check which intervals dominate: thirds = thirteenth; fourths = quartal; equal = pivotal
- Twelve perfect fourths span all twelve pitch classes before repetition

# Context & Application
Multi-note quartal chords require special handling due to their size. Parallel movement provides temporary suppleness; interrupting unisons or suddenly enriched dominants prevent harmonic suffocation. For clarity, place larger intervals at the bottom, omit members, and use orchestral timbre separation. Exploiting the consonant/dissonant group relationship creates effective quartal tension and release.
