# Quick Definition

The deliberate arrangement of intervals within compound chords according to their tension characteristics, placing consonant and dissonant intervals in specific vertical regions to control harmonic character.

# Core Definition

"Intervals may be arranged in any combination of tensions. They may be distributed so as to produce any desired shape and assembled to create various consonant or dissonant areas. The combination of consonant or dissonant intervals may create a consonant or dissonant bottom, middle, or top portion of the chord" (Persichetti, p. 163). "Compound harmony progresses well when the chordal materials function under a definite scheme of intervallic tension" (p. 165).

# Prerequisites

- **Compound chord** — Tension distribution is a construction principle for compound chords
- **Consonance-dissonance** — The classification system for intervals that governs distribution

# Key Properties

1. Intervals distributed to create consonant or dissonant bottom, middle, or top areas
2. Upper soft consonances + lower open consonances/sharp dissonances = one effective strategy
3. Upper sharp dissonances anchored a fifth or tenth below = tension + brilliance
4. Polyinterval texture enables harmonic areas to shift in any direction
5. The changing speeds of density and consonant-dissonant factor shifts create harmonic rhythm
6. Any compound structure may serve as central chord through relative tension of surrounding chords

# Construction / Recognition

## To Apply Tension Distribution
1. Classify available intervals by tension category
2. Decide on vertical distribution plan (e.g., mild top / sharp bottom, or reverse)
3. Place intervals according to plan
4. If sharp dissonance is placed high, anchor it a fifth or tenth below for brilliance
5. Vary distribution across successive chords to create harmonic rhythm

# Context & Application

Effective tension distribution creates chords with stable bottoms and brilliant tops, percussive attack when smaller intervals are low, balanced textures where tension can shift in any direction, and clear differentiation between arrival points and transitional moments.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Distributing intervals randomly without a plan
  **Correction**: Compound harmony progresses well under a definite tension scheme
- **Error**: Placing sharp dissonances high without anchoring
  **Correction**: Anchor the upper sharp dissonant interval a fifth or tenth below for tension and brilliance

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Tension distribution equals functional instability
  **Clarification**: A chord can be contextually stable regardless of internal tension distribution
- **Confusion**: Distribution is the same as chord inversion
  **Clarification**: Inversion changes bass note; distribution plans where different tension levels appear throughout the chord
