# Quick Definition
Chords containing all twelve chromatic tones arranged in thirds, representing the maximum extension of tertian harmony, with extremely limited harmonic functions but useful for punctuation, sustained tension, and contrast with thinner textures.

# Formal Definition
Mixed thirds may be superimposed until all twelve tones are present. The unwieldy terms "nineteenth," "twenty-first," or "twenty-third" chord are seldom used. Twelve-note chords containing twelve different member tones are so complex and thick that special attention must be given to register and instrumentation. This is an extremely limited species of harmony, which operates in a confined area; its harmonic functions are few.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
**Construction:**
All twelve chromatic pitch classes arranged by superimposed thirds

**Register Span:**
Twelve-note chords by thirds span a wide register, with members normally spaced in thirds.

**Voice Movement:**
- Parallel movement of all voices provides temporary "suppleness"
- Contrary motion in some voices changes distances between voices
- Contrary motion often transforms the twelve-note tertian chord into:
  - Chords by fourths
  - Chords by seconds
  - Polychords
  - Heterogeneous compound chords
  - Mirror chords

**Texture Management:**
The thick texture lightens when:
- The chord is placed in the upper register
- Consonant portions are played by separate orchestral choirs

# Musical Context/Application
Twelve-note chords have three primary functions:
1. **Punctuation** - dramatic accents in the harmonic flow
2. **Quiet sustained tension** - atmospheric static harmony
3. **Contrast** - answering unison or two-part writing with maximum density

Due to their complexity, twelve-note chords work best when isolated from ongoing harmonic progression. They serve as special effects rather than normative harmonic vocabulary.

# Common Confusions
Students may try to use twelve-note chords in normal harmonic progressions. Their density makes them unsuitable for this purpose; they are special-effect sonorities, not workable harmonic vocabulary. The limited functions (punctuation, sustained tension, contrast) should guide their use.
