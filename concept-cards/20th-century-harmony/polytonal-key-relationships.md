# Quick Definition
The intervallic relationships between simultaneous tonal centers that determine the resonance and tension level of polytonal combinations, ordered along the cycle of fifths from most consonant (P5) to most dissonant.

# Core Definition
The fundamental quality of polytonal texture is determined by the key relationship set up by the tonics. In major-key combinations, a polytonal order of tension from consonant to dissonant is secured by combining two keys that lie a perfect fifth, major ninth, major sixth, major third, major seventh apart -- and so on up the cycle of fifths. The high point of resonance in the order is at the center. Keys not closely related according to the circle of fifths more easily set apart the tonal key spheres. The tritone as a basis for key coalition forms a prime polytonal relationship because it is the most resonant of the dissonant combinations.

When combining more than two keys, the consonant-dissonant order of resonance is: all keys separated by a perfect fifth, a major ninth, a major sixth, a major third, etc. When intervals between keys are not the same, the larger interval is found between the bottom keys. When combining three or more mixed keys, the uppermost key is governed by the resonant relationship to the bottom, not inner key.

# Key Properties
1. Resonance order for two major keys: P5, M9, M6, M3, M7, etc. (cycle of fifths)
2. Tritone: most resonant of dissonant combinations (prime relationship)
3. Keys distant on cycle of fifths more easily differentiate tonal spheres
4. Three or more keys: same ordering principle applies
5. Unequal intervals: larger interval between bottom keys
6. Uppermost key relates resonantly to bottom key, not inner key
7. Minor keys at bottom produce fewer consonant combinations due to minor third
8. Passing secondary textures maneuver around most resonant polychordal pillars

# Construction / Recognition
- Determine the interval between combined tonics
- Locate that interval on the cycle-of-fifths resonance order
- For three or more keys: place largest interval between bottom keys
- Assess uppermost key's relationship to bottom key (not inner)
- For minor-key bottoms: note reduced consonant options
- Identify the most resonant polychords as structural pillars

# Context & Application
The resonance of polytonality depends upon the resonance of the over-all tonic formation as determined by its intervallic tension. Passing secondary textures are maneuvered around the most resonant polychords that form the structural pillars of the particular key combination. These secondary textures often become thick and troublesome and are made workable by omission of chord members, doubling of stronger intervals, ornamental coloring, unison and two-part interpolations, or by ostinatos. Similar inventory should be made of all key combinations including minor-key bases.

# Common Errors
- Placing smaller intervals between bottom keys (should be larger)
- Relating upper key to inner rather than bottom key
- Ignoring the reduced consonant options with minor-key bases

# Common Confusions
- **Consonant vs. resonant**: The cycle-of-fifths ordering rates consonance; the tritone is resonant despite being dissonant
- **Bottom key priority**: In three-or-more-key combinations, the bottom key determines the resonance of the upper key, not the inner key
- **Minor-key adjustment**: Minor thirds in the lower key reduce available consonant combinations
