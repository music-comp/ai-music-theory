# Quick Definition
The intervallic relationships between simultaneous key centers, which determine the resonance and tension level of polytonal combinations.

# Formal Definition
The fundamental quality of polytonal texture is determined by the key relationship set up by the tonics. In major-key combinations, a polytonal order of tension from consonant to dissonant is secured by combining two keys that lie a perfect fifth, major ninth, major sixth, major third, major seventh apart - and so on up the cycle of fifths. The high point of resonance in the order is at the center. Keys not closely related according to the circle of fifths more easily set apart the tonal key spheres.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Resonance order for major key combinations (most to least consonant):
1. Perfect fifth apart
2. Major ninth apart
3. Major sixth apart
4. Major third apart
5. Major seventh apart
6. (continues up cycle of fifths)

Tritone relationship:
- Most resonant of dissonant combinations
- Prime polytonal relationship
- Most clearly separates tonal spheres

For three or more keys:
- All keys separated by P5: Most consonant
- All by M9, M6, M3: Progressively more dissonant
- When intervals differ: Larger interval between bottom keys
- Uppermost key governed by resonance to bottom key, not inner key

Minor key considerations:
- Minor third in lower key reduces consonant combinations
- Different resonance qualities than major-only combinations

# Musical Context/Application
The resonance of polytonality depends upon the resonance of the over-all tonic formation as determined by its intervallic tension. Passing secondary textures are maneuvered around the most resonant polychords that form the structural pillars of the particular key combination. The choice of key relationship establishes the fundamental color and tension level of the polytonal texture.
