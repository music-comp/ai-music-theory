# Quick Definition
A form of repetition where a melodic, harmonic, or rhythmic pattern is restated at different pitch levels, with ascending sequence adding tension and descending sequence relaxing movement.

# Core Definition
**Sequences** may be melodic (tonal or real), harmonic, rhythmic, or in combination. Ascending sequence adds dynamic tension and descending sequential figures relax dynamic movement. Sequences have the notable effect of softening extremely dissonant passages through the predictability of their pattern (Ch. 11, pp. 234-236).

# Prerequisites
- **Extension and imitation** -- understanding developmental techniques

# Key Properties
1. Melodic sequence: pattern repeated at different pitch levels
2. Tonal sequence: adjusted to remain within key (intervals may vary)
3. Real sequence: exact interval preservation (may change key)
4. Harmonic sequence: chord progression repeated at new levels
5. Rhythmic sequence: durational pattern repeated
6. Combined sequence: multiple elements sequenced simultaneously
7. Ascending = increases dynamic tension; descending = relaxes
8. Predictability of pattern softens extreme dissonance

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify a repeated pattern at different pitch levels
- Determine whether sequence is tonal (scale-adjusted) or real (exact)
- Note the direction (ascending/descending) and its effect on tension
- Check whether the sequence domesticates dissonant material

# Context & Application
Sequences provide audible structure that can make even highly dissonant passages accessible. They may drive varied chords over harmonic barriers. Their modulatory potential makes them valuable for navigating between tonal areas.

# Common Errors
- Using sequences excessively (becomes mechanical)
- Not recognizing the tension-softening effect on dissonant material

# Common Confusions
- Tonal sequences adjust intervals to stay in key; real sequences preserve exact intervals
- Ascending sequences increase tension despite moving "up"
- Sequences can make dissonant passages more accessible through predictability
