# Quick Definition
A form of repetition in which a melodic, harmonic, or rhythmic pattern is restated at different pitch levels, creating directional motion.

# Formal Definition
Sequence is one of the most important devices in musical composition, emerging as a specific form of repetition. Sequences may be melodic (tonal or real), harmonic, rhythmic, or in combination. Ascending sequence adds dynamic tension while descending sequential figures relax dynamic movement. Sequences have the notable effect of softening extremely dissonant passages through the predictability of their pattern.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Sequence characteristics:
- **Melodic sequence**: Pattern repeated at different pitch levels
- **Tonal sequence**: Adjusted to remain within key (intervals may vary)
- **Real sequence**: Exact interval preservation (may change key)
- **Harmonic sequence**: Chord progression repeated at new levels
- **Rhythmic sequence**: Durational pattern repeated
- **Combined sequence**: Multiple elements sequenced simultaneously

Directional effects:
- Ascending: Increases dynamic tension
- Descending: Relaxes dynamic movement

# Musical Context/Application
In twentieth-century harmony, sequences operate across diverse harmonic vocabularies. The pattern-based nature of sequences can domesticate highly dissonant passages by providing audible structure. Sequences may drive varied chords over many harmonic barriers, as noted in Chapter 13's discussion of textural transitions. The modulatory potential of sequences makes them valuable for navigating between different harmonic areas.

# Common Confusions
- Tonal sequences adjust intervals to stay in key; real sequences preserve exact intervals
- Ascending sequences increase tension despite moving "up"
- Sequences can make dissonant passages more accessible
