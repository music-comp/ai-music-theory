# Quick Definition
The overall degree of harmonic tension in a chord, determined by analyzing its constituent intervals and their combined consonant-dissonant characteristics.

# Formal Definition
The quality of chordal tension is determined by the intervallic content of a chord and is affected by dynamics, medium, and spacing. All chords fall generally into one of two categories: those containing at least one sharp dissonance and those containing no sharp dissonance. Each category may be subdivided into those containing at least one tritone and those containing no tritone.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Chord classification system:
1. Contains sharp dissonance (minor 2nd or major 7th)
   - With tritone: restless + biting
   - Without tritone: stable despite bite
2. No sharp dissonance
   - With tritone: restless quality
   - Without tritone: stable quality

Additional factors:
- Tritone presence: adds restless quality
- Perfect fourth presence: adds ambiguity (context-dependent)
- Spacing and register: affect perceived tension
- Doubling: reinforces specific tension characteristics

# Musical Context/Application
Chords containing a tritone tend to have a restless quality, while those without tritones have stability even when extremely dissonant. The presence of a perfect fourth lends ambiguity, with its character determined by other intervals in the chord. Handling harmonic materials becomes simpler if general classification of intervallic characteristics is applied systematically.

# Common Confusions
Students may classify chord tension based solely on the "strongest" dissonance present. Persichetti's system considers the interaction of all intervals, particularly noting that chords without tritones can be stable even when highly dissonant.
