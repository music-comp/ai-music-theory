# Quick Definition
A four-category system for classifying chords by their interval content: sharp dissonance present or absent, crossed with tritone present or absent, with special rules for chords containing a perfect fourth.

# Core Definition
The quality of chordal tension affects and is affected by dynamics, medium, and spacing, but varies in different musical contexts. All chords fall generally into one of two categories: those containing at least one sharp dissonance (m2 or M7) and those containing no sharp dissonance. Each category is subdivided by the presence or absence of at least one tritone. Additionally, the presence of a perfect fourth lends ambiguity: its character is defined by the interval formed by the bass note and the note not involved with the fourth.

# Key Properties
1. **Category I**: contains sharp dissonance + tritone = restless + biting
2. **Category II**: contains sharp dissonance, no tritone = biting but stable
3. **Category III**: no sharp dissonance + tritone = restless quality
4. **Category IV**: no sharp dissonance, no tritone = stable quality
5. **Tritone effect**: adds restless quality regardless of dissonance level
6. **Without tritone**: stability persists even in extremely dissonant chords
7. **P4 ambiguity rule**: when bass-to-non-fourth-note interval is dissonant, P4 sounds consonant; when that interval is a soft consonance, P4 sounds mildly dissonant

# Construction / Recognition
1. List all intervals in the chord
2. Check for presence of any m2 or M7 (sharp dissonance)
3. Check for presence of any tritone
4. Place into one of four categories
5. If P4 is present: examine bass-to-non-fourth-note interval to resolve ambiguity
6. Consider how dynamics, medium, and spacing modify the classification in context

# Context & Application
This classification system provides a practical tool for controlling harmonic tension. Chords containing a tritone tend to have a restless quality, while those without tritones have stability even when extremely dissonant. This insight is particularly powerful: a chord full of seconds and sevenths but lacking a tritone can function as stable harmony. The system enables harmonic movement based on intervallic tension rather than traditional root relationships.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Classifying chord tension based solely on the "strongest" dissonance present. **Correction**: The system considers interaction of all intervals, particularly the tritone axis.
- **Error**: Assuming all dissonant chords are restless. **Correction**: Chords without tritones can be stable even when highly dissonant.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking category I is "worse" than category IV. **Clarification**: All categories are equally valid compositional resources; the system enables controlled movement between tension states, not a hierarchy of "good" vs. "bad" chords.
