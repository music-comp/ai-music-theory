# Quick Definition
The establishment of a tonal center in polychordal music, achieved not through traditional harmonic function but through tonal implications of a predominating melodic line or harmonic gravitation to a dominating chord.

# Formal Definition
Polyharmonic progressions touch so many key areas that tonality cannot be secured through the harmonic personality of a prevailing scale. Tonality is established by the tonal implications of a predominating melodic line or by harmonic gravitation to a dominating chord. Resonant polychords play the tonic role most convincingly; a wealth of invention must be saved for the cadence if a firm tonal center is desired.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Tonality establishment methods:

**Method 1: Melodic predominance**
- Melody implies tonal center
- Melodic line contains tonal gravity
- Polychordal accompaniment follows melodic tonality
- Traditional melodic cadence formulas help

**Method 2: Characteristic chord gravitation**
- One polychord functions as tonic
- Returns and emphases establish dominance
- Resonant polychords suit tonic role best
- Cadential invention crucial

Why traditional harmonic tonality fails:
- Polychordal progressions touch many key areas
- No single scale predominates
- Functional relationships (V-I, etc.) are obscured
- Harmonic personality of scale not available

Cadential considerations:
- Firm tonal center requires strong cadence
- Save inventive resources for cadential moments
- Resonant polychords for tonic
- Less resonant polychords for dominant-like function

# Musical Context/Application
Polychordal music requires alternative approaches to tonal organization. Composers must consciously establish tonal centers through melody or characteristic chord emphasis since traditional harmonic functions cannot serve this role.

Prolonged passages of pure polychords can result in tiring mass resonance. Solutions include:
- Ornamentation to freshen texture
- Unison and two-voice interruptions
- Omission of notes from chordal units
- Use of polychords as architectural pillars with other harmony between

# Common Confusions
- Polychordal tonality is not the same as traditional functional tonality
- Polytonality (multiple keys) is different from polychordal tonality (single center)
- Resonance correlates with tonic stability but is not absolute
- The methods described are compositional strategies, not natural occurrences
