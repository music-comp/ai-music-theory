# Quick Definition
Techniques for entering and exiting polychordal textures: approaching through coupled triads or chromatic chord conversion, and departing through the gradual evaporation of one component triad.

# Core Definition
Polychords are approached through coupled triads or chromatic conversion of chords. Motion from polychords to triads is created by the evaporation of one triad of the polychord. The harmonic units of a tonal polychordal succession may meet at a point where each progresses in a separate and consistent key zone; the polychordal progression then becomes polytonal. Atonality may be entered through florid writing when individual lines are given such freedom of chromatic movement that the implied harmony becomes indefinite and the feeling of key disappears.

# Prerequisites
- **Textural transition**: The broader transitional technique
- **Polychord**: The formation being approached or departed

# Key Properties
1. Approach via coupled triads: two triads move together toward polychordal combination
2. Approach via chromatic conversion: chromatic voice motion transforms simple chord into polychord
3. Exit via evaporation: one component triad gradually disappears
4. Polychordal succession may become polytonal when layers gain key independence
5. Atonality may be entered from polytonality through chromatic florid writing

# Construction / Recognition
- For approach: identify coupled triads converging or chromatic motion building a polychord
- For evaporation: observe one triad's voices dropping out while the other continues
- Note whether the polychordal succession develops independent key zones (becoming polytonal)
- Track whether florid chromatic writing dissolves polytonal texture into atonality

# Context & Application
These techniques allow polychords to function within larger harmonic progressions rather than as isolated sonorities. The coupled-triad approach creates a gradual buildup of polychordal complexity. The evaporation technique reverses this, dissolving complexity back to simplicity. The transformation from polychordal to polytonal occurs organically when layers gain horizontal independence. The further dissolution into atonality through chromatic freedom shows the continuum from simple triads through polychords through polytonality to atonality.

# Common Errors
- Approaching polychords abruptly without preparation
- Evaporating both triads simultaneously (loses polychordal identity)
- Confusing polychordal approach with polytonal development

# Common Confusions
- **Approach vs. evaporation**: Approach builds complexity; evaporation dissolves it
- **Evaporation of one triad**: Only one component disappears, leaving the other as a simple triadic texture
- **Continuum**: The sequence triad-polychord-polytonality-atonality represents increasing textural complexity, not discrete categories
