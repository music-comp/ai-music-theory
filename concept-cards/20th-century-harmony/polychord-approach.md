# Quick Definition
Techniques for entering and exiting polychordal textures through coupled triads, chromatic conversion, or gradual evaporation of chord components.

# Formal Definition
Polychords are approached through coupled triads or chromatic conversion of chords. Motion from polychords to triads is created by the evaporation of one triad of the polychord. These techniques provide smooth transitions into and out of polychordal textures, integrating complex harmonies with simpler ones within a continuous musical flow.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Approach methods:
- **Coupled triads**: Two triads move together toward polychordal combination
- **Chromatic conversion**: Chromatic voice motion transforms simple chord into polychord

Exit methods:
- **Evaporation**: One component triad gradually disappears
- **Voice elimination**: Systematic removal of voices from one layer
- **Convergence**: Layers merge into single chord

Transition principles:
- Gradual transformation maintains continuity
- Abrupt changes possible for dramatic effect
- Voice leading connects components smoothly

# Musical Context/Application
The approach and evaporation techniques allow polychords to function within larger harmonic progressions rather than as isolated sonorities. Coupled triads moving in parallel or contrary motion can converge on a polychordal formation. The evaporation technique reverses this process, dissolving the polychord back into a single triadic structure. These techniques integrate polychordal harmony into the broader harmonic vocabulary.

# Common Confusions
- Approach and exit techniques are gradual processes, not instantaneous
- Evaporation refers to one component disappearing, not the whole polychord
- Coupled triads can be diatonic or chromatic
