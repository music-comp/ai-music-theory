# Quick Definition
The practice of duplicating notes within quartal chords, where any note may be doubled to enrich harmonic color (outer parts) or strengthen moving voices (inner parts).

# Formal Definition
Any note in a perfect quartal chord may be doubled as well as any other note. Doubling of the outer parts enriches harmonic color and doubling of inner parts strengthens any moving voice. Three-note quartal chords with a doubling are useful in contexts where resolution to tertian chords is desired.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
**Doubling Effects:**
- Outer part doubling: enriches color
- Inner part doubling: strengthens moving voices
- Any note equally available for doubling

**Contrast with Tertian Doubling:**
In tertian harmony, certain doublings are preferred (root, fifth) while others are avoided (leading tone, seventh). In quartal harmony, the absence of inherent root identity means all doublings are equally viable.

**Functional Application:**
When resolving to tertian chords:
- Three-note quartal with doubling = four voices
- Two voices move conjunctly
- Two voices remain stationary
- Result: smooth connection to tertian harmony

# Musical Context/Application
Quartal doubling serves multiple purposes:
1. Enriching the color of sustained quartal chords
2. Providing voice-leading flexibility
3. Creating four-voice texture from three-note chord structure
4. Facilitating resolution to tertian harmony

The principle that any chord tone may be doubled reflects the rootless nature of quartal harmony, where no single pitch has hierarchical priority.

# Common Confusions
Students may apply tertian doubling rules to quartal chords. In quartal harmony, the absence of a clear root or tendency tones means all doublings are equally acceptable. The choice is based on color and voice-leading needs, not on chord-tone hierarchy.
