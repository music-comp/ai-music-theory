# Quick Definition
The practice of duplicating notes within quartal chords, where any note may be doubled equally -- outer parts for harmonic color enrichment, inner parts for strengthening moving voices -- reflecting the rootless nature of quartal harmony with no hierarchical doubling restrictions.

# Core Definition
Any note in a perfect quartal chord may be doubled as well as any other note. Doubling of the outer parts enriches harmonic color and doubling of inner parts strengthens any moving voice. Three-note quartal chords with a doubling are useful in contexts where resolution to tertian chords is desired, providing four voices for smooth conjunct voice movement. The principle that any chord tone may be doubled reflects the rootless nature of quartal harmony, where no single pitch has hierarchical priority.

# Key Properties
1. Any note equally available for doubling (no hierarchy)
2. Outer doubling = color enrichment
3. Inner doubling = moving voice strength
4. Three-note + doubling = four voices for tertian resolution
5. Resolution: two conjunct, two stationary
6. Reflects rootless quartal equality (unlike tertian doubling preferences)

# Construction / Recognition
- Any chord tone doubled freely
- Choose based on color (outer) or voice-strength (inner) needs
- Use doubled three-note for four-voice resolution to tertian chords

# Context & Application
Quartal doubling provides voice-leading flexibility and color options without the restrictions of tertian doubling rules. It creates four-voice texture from three-note chord structure, facilitating resolution to tertian harmony.
