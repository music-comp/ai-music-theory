# Quick Definition
A sustained harmonic formation of three or more tones that functions as an extended pedal while other voices move through changing harmonies.

# Formal Definition
When three or more tones are held as a pedal, a pedal chord evolves. This extends the pedal point concept from single or double tones to complete harmonic structures. The pedal chord maintains its stability while upper or surrounding voices progress through various harmonies, creating complex polychordal relationships between the static and moving elements.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Pedal chord characteristics:
- **Minimum size**: Three or more tones
- **Function**: Stable harmonic anchor against moving voices
- **Relationship to harmony**: Creates polychordal textures
- **Polytonality**: Triple pedal point often suggests polytonal organization

The pedal chord creates a more complex harmonic backdrop than single or double pedals, as the sustained formation itself carries harmonic implications that interact with the moving voices.

# Musical Context/Application
Pedal chords are particularly effective in polytonal contexts where the sustained chord establishes one tonal plane while the moving voices operate in another. The technique blurs the line between pedal function and polychordal writing. Pedal chords can provide harmonic continuity while allowing melodic voices considerable freedom.
