# Quick Definition
The use of symmetrical chords (augmented triads, diminished sevenths) as harmonically neutral pivot points between texture types, exploiting their rootless ambiguity to weave one harmonic fabric into another through chromatic motion.

# Core Definition
The equidistant chord has neither root nor power to resist homogeneous chordal formations and can, by chromatic motion, weave one harmonic fabric into another. Rough tritone relationship or prominent hidden fifths divert attention from harmonic transformations. Sequential patterns can drive varied chords over many harmonic barriers. The rootless quality of equidistant chords makes them ideal neutral territory between harmonic regions.

# Key Properties
1. Built from equal intervals (augmented triads from M3s, diminished sevenths from m3s)
2. No definable root -- harmonically neutral
3. No power to resist other chord types
4. Chromatic voice motion from equidistant chord can lead anywhere
5. Connects otherwise incompatible harmonies
6. Tritone relationships and hidden fifths divert attention from the transformation
7. Sequential patterns drive varied chords across barriers

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify equidistant chords (augmented triads, diminished sevenths, whole-tone formations)
- Note chromatic voice motion leading away from the equidistant chord
- Observe whether the equidistant chord connects two different texture types
- Look for sequential patterns that drive through diverse harmonic material
- Note tritone relationships and hidden fifths that mask the transformation

# Context & Application
Equidistant chords serve as neutral territory between harmonic regions. Their lack of root definition means they pull toward nothing specifically, allowing the composer to direct resolution toward any desired texture. The augmented triad (dividing the octave into three equal parts) and diminished seventh chord (dividing into four) are the most common equidistant formations used for transitional purposes. The technique is enhanced when combined with sequential patterns and attention-diverting devices.

# Common Errors
- Expecting equidistant chords to resolve in a specific direction
- Using equidistant chords without chromatic voice motion (they become static)
- Overusing the technique so that it becomes formulaic

# Common Confusions
- **Rootless, not merely ambiguous**: Equidistant chords truly lack roots, unlike ambiguous chords that have multiple possible roots
- **Neutrality as resource**: The inability to resist other chord types is an advantage, not a weakness
- **Attention diversion**: Tritone relationships and hidden fifths are supplementary masking devices, not substitutes for the equidistant pivot
