# Quick Definition
A modulation achieved by shifting an entire chord a half step through sudden parallel motion, creating immediate key change.

# Formal Definition
Chromatic modulation is attained by shifting an entire chord a half step in sudden parallel harmony. Abrupt modulations make the arriving tonic felt as a distant chord; to establish a firm feeling of the new key, an extended harmonic progression is necessary in order to secure the new center. All chromatic tones are in the domain of a key when the vertical aspect gives way to the horizontal movement of voices; therefore, all keys have twelve tones in common and may be reached quickly chromatically.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Chromatic modulation characteristics:
- **Mechanism**: Half-step parallel shift of entire chord
- **Effect**: Arriving tonic feels distant
- **Requirement**: Extended progression needed to establish new key
- **Principle**: All keys share twelve tones chromatically

All keys accessible because:
- Horizontal voice movement encompasses all chromatic tones
- Vertical harmony can shift chromatically to any pitch level
- Any chord can serve any of twelve tonal centers

# Musical Context/Application
Chromatic modulation provides immediate key shift but requires follow-through. The sudden parallel motion creates a dramatic harmonic jolt, but the new key is not secure until an extended progression establishes it. This technique is particularly effective for dramatic effect but should not substitute for organic harmonic development. The universality of chromatic resources means all keys are equidistant chromatically.

# Examples
- Chromatic modulation through half-step parallel chord motion
- Extended progression following chromatic shift to secure new center

# Common Confusions
- Chromatic shift alone does not establish new key; extended progression is needed
- The arriving key feels distant until confirmed by progression
- All keys are equally accessible chromatically
