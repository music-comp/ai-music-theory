# Quick Definition
Eight-note tertian chords extending beyond the diatonic seven-note collection, necessarily incorporating at least one chromatic tone, effective in parallel harmony and harmonic punctuations when thinned by omitting inner tones.

# Core Definition
As the tonal system expanded to accommodate more than seven different chord tones, thirds were added to the thirteenth chord as a fifteenth without duplicating chord members. The fifteenth chord contains eight different pitch classes, extending beyond the seven-note diatonic collection. For purposes of manipulation, the texture of these large chords should be thinned by omitting two or three inner tones. Separated triadic units must be avoided unless polychordal structure is desired. Complete fifteenth chords are effective in parallel harmony or in harmonic punctuations.

# Prerequisites
- **Thirteenth chords**: Seven-note maximum diatonic tertian formation

# Key Properties
1. Eight notes built in thirds: R-3-5-7-9-11-13-15
2. Necessarily includes at least one chromatic tone (exceeds seven-note diatonic collection)
3. Must be thinned by omitting two or three inner tones for manipulation
4. Avoid separated triadic units unless polychord is desired
5. Effective in parallel harmony and harmonic punctuations
6. Wide register span requires careful orchestration

# Construction / Recognition
- Build in thirds beyond the thirteenth to the fifteenth
- Verify that eight distinct pitch classes are present (not just doubled thirteenth)
- Thin by omitting inner tones for practical use
- Check for unintended polychordal separation

# Context & Application
Fifteenth chords represent the expansion of tertian harmony beyond the diatonic system. They are best used sparingly: in parallel harmony where all voices move together, or as harmonic punctuations providing maximum density at specific moments. The extreme register span demands careful orchestration.

# Common Errors
- Confusing fifteenth chords with doubled thirteenth chords (a true fifteenth has eight distinct pitches)

# Common Confusions
- **Chromatic necessity**: A fifteenth chord must include at least one chromatic tone to reach eight different pitches; it cannot be purely diatonic
