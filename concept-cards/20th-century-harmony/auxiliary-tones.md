# Quick Definition
Nonharmonic tones that ornament a chord tone by stepping to an adjacent pitch and returning, also called neighbor tones.

# Core Definition
**Auxiliary tones** (neighbor tones) belong to the first category of nonharmonic tones: approached and left without skip. Unlike passing tones which connect different chord tones, auxiliary tones ornament a single chord tone by moving to an adjacent pitch (upper or lower) and returning. They may be grouped chord-wise to form auxiliary chords. The auxiliary motion may be combined with other ornamental patterns before the chord tone returns (Ch. 11, pp. 229-233).

# Prerequisites
- **Nonharmonic tones** -- understanding the classification system

# Key Properties
1. Step away from chord tone, step back to same chord tone
2. Upper auxiliary (step above) or lower auxiliary (step below)
3. Returns to starting pitch (unlike passing tones)
4. May be grouped chord-wise to form auxiliary chords
5. May be combined with other ornamental tones before return
6. Step size depends on scale context

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify a tone that departs from and returns to the same chord tone by step
- Classify as upper or lower auxiliary
- Check for chord-wise groupings forming auxiliary chords

# Context & Application
In twentieth-century practice, auxiliary tones may derive from various scalar sources including synthetic scales. When grouped chord-wise, they form auxiliary chords that ornament principal harmony.

## Contrasts With
- Passing tones (auxiliary returns; passing continues)

# Common Errors
- Confusing auxiliary tones with passing tones (auxiliary returns, passing continues)

# Common Confusions
- Auxiliary tones return to their starting pitch; passing tones continue in one direction
- "Neighbor tone" is another term for auxiliary tone
- Step size depends on the scale context being employed
