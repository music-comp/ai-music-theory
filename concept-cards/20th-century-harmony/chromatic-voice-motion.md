# Quick Definition
Voice leading where all voices move by half step, allowing any combination of dissonances to have meaning and direction through the linear coherence of each chromatic line.

# Core Definition
Each voice may move chromatically to daringly complex chords because when all voices move by half step, any combination of dissonances may have meaning and direction. The chromatic lines provide linear coherence regardless of the vertical sonority. The voices containing the sharpest dissonant interval should be well separated to prevent excessive harshness (Ch. 9, p. 193).

# Prerequisites
- **Chord connection** -- understanding voice-leading principles between chords

# Key Properties
1. Each voice moves by minor second (half step)
2. Direction of each voice may be up, down, or alternating
3. Any dissonance combination is justified by linear coherence
4. Sharp dissonant intervals should be well separated in register
5. Linear logic supersedes vertical analysis
6. Enables departure from scale-based harmony into chromatic space

# Construction / Recognition
- Check whether each voice moves by half step between successive chords
- Note the resulting vertical sonorities (which may be extreme dissonances)
- Verify that voices containing sharp dissonances are well separated in register
- Observe the overall contour of converging or diverging chromatic lines

# Context & Application
Chromatic voice motion enables progressions of extreme dissonance with linear justification, smooth connection of otherwise unrelated sonorities, and contrapuntal organization of freely dissonant harmony. The technique is fundamental to expressionist and atonal voice leading, and serves as a transitional technique between tonal and post-tonal passages.

**Example** (Ch. 9, Ex. 9-24, p. 193): All voices moving chromatically, complex dissonant chords arising from half-step lines, sharp dissonant intervals well separated.

# Common Errors
- Placing voices with sharp dissonant intervals too close together in register
- Treating resulting vertical sonorities as traditionally functional chords
- Losing linear coherence by mixing chromatic and non-chromatic motion inconsistently

# Common Confusions
- **Chromatic vs. diatonic voice leading**: Chromatic is by half step only; diatonic follows scale with half and whole steps
- **Vertical vs. linear justification**: The resulting sonorities are justified by their linear origin, not by vertical analysis
- **Spacing requirement**: Well-separated voices are essential for the technique to work; close spacing creates excessive harshness
