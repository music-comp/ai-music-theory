# Quick Definition

Voice leading where all voices move by half step, allowing any combination of dissonances to have meaning and direction through the small-interval coherence of each line.

# Formal Definition

**Chromatic voice motion** is a technique where each voice moves chromatically (by half step) to create harmonic progressions. When all voices move by half step, any combination of dissonances may have meaning and direction because the chromatic lines provide linear coherence regardless of the vertical sonority. This technique enables the use of daringly complex chords that would otherwise lack harmonic justification.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Principle:
- Each voice moves by minor second (half step)
- Direction of each voice may be up, down, or alternating
- Vertical sonorities result from chromatic voice convergence/divergence

Voice spacing consideration:
- Voices containing the sharpest dissonant interval should be well separated
- Separation mitigates the harshness of clustered sharp dissonances

The technique permits:
- Any dissonance combination
- Complex cluster-like sonorities
- Complete freedom from traditional chord construction
- Linear logic superseding vertical analysis

# Musical Context/Application

Chromatic voice motion enables:
- Progressions of extreme dissonance with linear justification
- Departure from scale-based harmony into chromatic space
- Smooth connection of otherwise unrelated sonorities
- Contrapuntal organization of freely dissonant harmony

The technique is particularly effective for:
- Highly chromatic, expressionist passages
- Building or releasing tension through converging/diverging lines
- Creating seamless transitions between harmonic areas
- Maintaining coherence in atonal contexts

# Common Confusions

- **Chromatic vs. diatonic voice leading**: Chromatic motion is by half step only; diatonic motion follows scale patterns with half and whole steps.
- **Vertical vs. linear justification**: Chromatic voice motion justifies sonorities linearly; vertical analysis may show no conventional chord structure.
- **Spacing requirement**: The technique works best with well-separated voices; close spacing of chromatic voices creates excessive harshness.
