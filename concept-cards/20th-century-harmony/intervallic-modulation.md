# Quick Definition
Modulation realized through a specific interval other than the semitone, where maximum freshness is achieved when the modulatory interval contrasts with the characteristic interval of the prevailing root progression.

# Core Definition
Modulation may be realized through the semitone or other intervals. A fresh change is felt when the modulatory interval is one other than the characteristic interval of the prevailing root progression. Modulatory changes of the third relationship are effective in passages of second-relationship harmony, and changes of the second are effective in third-relationship harmony. Key changes have special lift when modulation is accompanied by an octave leap in register.

# Prerequisites
- **Modulation**: The broader process of key change

# Key Properties
1. Any interval may serve as the modulatory interval
2. Contrast with prevailing root progression interval produces freshness
3. Third-relationship modulation effective in second-relationship passages
4. Second-relationship modulation effective in third-relationship passages
5. Octave leap in register gives key changes special lift
6. A distant tonality may be established by reinforcement with auxiliary chords

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the characteristic root-progression interval of the passage (seconds, thirds, fifths, etc.)
- Choose a modulatory interval that contrasts with the prevailing root movement
- In second-based root progressions, modulate by thirds (and vice versa)
- Consider octave leaps at the point of modulation for added energy
- Reinforce distant arrivals with auxiliary chords in the new key

# Context & Application
The contrast between modulatory interval and prevailing root movement creates perceptual freshness -- the ear registers the change because the modulatory motion differs from the pattern. This principle extends the traditional concept of modulation by interval (modulation to the dominant, subdominant, etc.) into a more generalized framework where any interval can serve and where contrast is the primary aesthetic goal.

# Common Errors
- Using the same interval for modulation as for prevailing root progression (no freshness)
- Neglecting registration changes that enhance modulatory effect
- Failing to reinforce distant arrivals

# Common Confusions
- **Interval contrast, not interval size**: Freshness comes from contrast with the prevailing pattern, not from large intervals per se
- **Octave leap**: This is a registral, not harmonic, device that enhances the modulatory moment
- **Distant modulation**: Requires reinforcement to avoid sounding arbitrary
