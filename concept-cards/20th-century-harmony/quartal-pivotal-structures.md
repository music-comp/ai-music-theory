# Quick Definition
Chords containing equal numbers of thirds and fourths that can function as either tertian or quartal sonorities, serving as pivot points between the two harmonic systems.

# Formal Definition
Thirteenth chords and multi-note quartal chords often contain the same notes. The similarity is theoretical, not aural. If thirds overrun a six- or seven-note chord, the ear hears a thirteenth formation. If a six- or seven-note chord is overrun by fourths, a chord by fourths sounds. If the number of thirds and fourths is equal, the chord may be used as a pivotal structure and regarded as belonging to either the tertian or quartal category, or to both.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
**Determining Factor:**
The preponderance of one interval type determines perceived identity:
- More thirds = tertian (thirteenth)
- More fourths = quartal
- Equal thirds and fourths = pivotal

**Example Analysis:**
A 6-note chord might contain:
- 3 thirds + 2 fourths = tertian dominant
- 2 thirds + 3 fourths = quartal dominant
- 2 thirds + 2 fourths + 1 other = potentially pivotal

**Voicing Influence:**
The arrangement and emphasis of intervals affects perception:
- Thirds grouped: tertian sound
- Fourths grouped: quartal sound
- Mixed arrangement: more ambiguous, more pivotal

# Musical Context/Application
Pivotal structures enable smooth modulation between harmonic systems:
1. Approach a pivotal chord from tertian context
2. The chord functions as both tertian and quartal
3. Depart from the chord into quartal context (or vice versa)

This technique is valuable for composers working with mixed harmonic vocabularies, allowing transitions that maintain logical connection between contrasting systems.

# Common Confusions
Students may analyze chords strictly by pitch content, missing the perceptual difference created by voicing. Two chords with identical pitch classes can function as tertian or quartal depending on which intervals are emphasized through spacing and arrangement. The pivotal structure explicitly exploits this ambiguity.
