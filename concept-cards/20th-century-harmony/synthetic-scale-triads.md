# Quick Definition
Triads derived from synthetic (non-traditional) scales, which often contain augmented or diminished thirds that sound like fourths or seconds and may require alteration to one of the four basic triad types.

# Formal Definition
Cyclic relationships may be constructed in any scale, synthetic or otherwise. Synthetic scales often contain triads that have augmented or diminished thirds. These are awkward because they sound like fourths or seconds and usually result in misspelling a chord. Such chords are often altered to one of the four basic triads (major, minor, diminished, or augmented) in which only major and minor thirds are present.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
**Augmented Third Issue:**
- Augmented third = enharmonically equal to perfect fourth
- Creates quartal rather than tertian sound
- Solution: respell or alter to standard triad

**Diminished Third Issue:**
- Diminished third = enharmonically equal to major second
- Creates secundal rather than tertian sound
- Solution: respell or alter to standard triad

**Four Basic Triads (all use only major and minor thirds):**
1. Major: M3 + m3
2. Minor: m3 + M3
3. Diminished: m3 + m3
4. Augmented: M3 + M3

**Primary Chord Colors in Synthetic Scales:**
The colors of primary chords are determined by the intervallic make-up of the scale in force and will vary considerably across different synthetic constructions.

# Musical Context/Application
When working with synthetic scales, composers face a choice: maintain the precise intervallic content of the scale (producing unusual triads with augmented or diminished thirds) or alter these triads to standard forms. The decision depends on whether the quartal or secundal quality of the unusual triads serves the musical purpose.

Most often, composers alter to standard triads to maintain clear tertian sonority while preserving the melodic character of the synthetic scale.

# Common Confusions
Students may try to build triads strictly according to synthetic scale degrees without considering the sonic result. A triad with an augmented third doesn't sound like a triad - it sounds like a quartal formation. Recognizing when to alter for clarity is part of the composer's craft.
