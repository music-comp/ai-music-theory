# Quick Definition
A well-defined melodic segment insistently repeated, providing tonal clarity and rhythmic drive while other voices move freely.

# Formal Definition
An ostinato is a well-defined melodic segment insistently repeated. Unlike the pedal point which sustains or repeats single tones, the ostinato maintains a complete melodic pattern. The tonal simplicity of ostinato helps clarify the texture of polytonal writing. When ostinatos occur simultaneously, harmonic tension rises. The ostinato becomes melodically obstinate in an upper voice and obtrusive in a middle voice.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Ostinato characteristics:
- **Content**: Well-defined melodic segment (not single tones)
- **Repetition**: Insistent, persistent recurrence
- **Function**: Provides tonal anchor and rhythmic drive
- **Register effects**:
  - Upper voice: Melodically obstinate (prominent)
  - Middle voice: Obtrusive (interfering)
  - Lower voice: Foundational (clarifying)

Multiple ostinatos:
- Simultaneous ostinatos increase harmonic tension
- Different patterns may suggest different tonal areas

# Musical Context/Application
The tonal simplicity of ostinato helps clarify polytonal textures by providing a stable reference against which more complex harmonies can be heard. Ostinatos contribute to formal coherence through their repetitive nature. In twentieth-century practice, ostinatos may be derived from various scale formations and may interact with harmonies from different tonal areas. The rhythmic persistence of ostinato creates forward momentum.

# Common Confusions
- Ostinato is a melodic pattern, not a single sustained tone like pedal
- Register placement affects character (obstinate, obtrusive, or foundational)
- Multiple simultaneous ostinatos increase rather than clarify complexity
