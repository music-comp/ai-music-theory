# Quick Definition
A polychord with a major triad as the bottom unit and a minor triad as the upper unit (named from bottom upward), which is richer than the reverse combination because the upper minor triad receives additional support from tones of the lower chord.

# Formal Definition
A polychord may be made up of any kind of triads. When it is constructed of major triads it is most consonant and resonant, becoming progressively less so as minor, then augmented, and finally diminished triads are included. The qualities of the twelve major-minor (named from the bottom upward) possibilities may be observed when the upper units are added to the bottom chord in a rising cycle of fifths.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Naming convention: Bottom quality - Upper quality
- "Major-minor" = major bottom, minor upper

Comparison with minor-major:
- Major-minor: Upper minor triad receives support from lower chord overtones
- Minor-major: Upper major triad has less support from minor chord overtones
- Result: Minor over major is richer than major over minor

Resonant major-minor combinations (numbers 4, 5, 6, 9, 12 are most useful):
- Arranged by cycle of fifths from bottom unit bass
- Creates order of decreasing consonance
- Most resonant have overtone-related upper units

Construction:
- Bottom unit: Major triad (six-four for maximum resonance)
- Upper unit: Minor triad at various transposition levels
- 12 possible combinations following cycle of fifths

# Musical Context/Application
Major-minor polychords offer a darker color than major-major while maintaining considerable resonance. The minor upper triad creates a more somber or complex character while the major lower triad provides harmonic stability.

Numbers 4, 5, 6, 9, and 12 are more useful because of their resonant character. The selection among these depends on:
- Desired color
- Voice-leading considerations
- Relationship to surrounding harmony

# Common Confusions
- Naming is from bottom up: "major-minor" means major bottom, minor upper
- The resonance difference between major-minor and minor-major is real and significant
- Not all 12 combinations are equally useful
- Position and inversion of units also affects character
