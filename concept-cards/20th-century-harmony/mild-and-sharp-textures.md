# Quick Definition
Two general texture categories for added-note chords: mild type (containing no sharp dissonance) and sharp type (containing at least one sharp dissonance such as minor second or major seventh), each with distinct effects on harmonic flow.

# Formal Definition
In any kind of added-note chord, two general textures are possible: a mild type having no sharp dissonance and a sharp type having at least one sharp dissonance. Sharp dissonances include the minor second, major seventh, and their compounds. Added-note chords of the mild type often stop harmonic flow by their tendency to form a cadence; when used extensively, they cause harmonic progressions to become paralyzed.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Texture classification:
- **Mild texture**: No intervals of m2, M7, or their compounds
  - Intervals present: M2, m3, M3, P4, A4/d5, P5, m6, M6
  - Tendency: cadential, flow-stopping

- **Sharp texture**: At least one m2, M7, or compound
  - Creates tension and forward motion
  - Prevents harmonic stagnation

Flow principle:
- Mild chords alone -> harmonic paralysis
- Sharp chords alone -> sustained tension
- Mixed mild and sharp -> maintained harmonic flow

Solution: Mix freely the mild and sharp types to maintain flow

# Musical Context/Application
The distinction between mild and sharp textures is fundamental to controlling harmonic rhythm and direction in added-note harmony. Composers must balance these textures to avoid either:
1. Cadential stagnation (too many mild chords)
2. Excessive tension (too many sharp chords)

A flow can be maintained by mixing freely the mild and sharp types. Progressive motion of added-note chords is governed by the basic harmony to which added notes are attached. Traditional devices (cadences, sequences, harmonic formulas) help protect basic chords from the debilitating effect of crowding seconds.

# Common Confusions
- "Mild" does not mean "consonant" - mild textures still contain added dissonances
- "Sharp" refers to the presence of specific intervals (m2, M7), not overall dissonance level
- The paralysis from mild chords comes from their cadential tendency, not from lack of interest
- Mixing textures is about maintaining motion, not about variety for its own sake
