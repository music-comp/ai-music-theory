# Quick Definition
Nonharmonic tones approached by skip (leap) and resolved by step, creating a "leaning" effect into the resolution.

# Core Definition
**Appoggiaturas** are approached by a skip and left conjunctly (by step). The term derives from the Italian "appoggiare" (to lean), reflecting how these tones lean into their resolution. They typically occur on accented positions, creating emphasis on the dissonance before stepwise resolution. This is the inverse of the changing tone pattern (Ch. 11, pp. 229-230).

# Prerequisites
- **Nonharmonic tones** -- understanding the classification system

# Key Properties
1. Approach: by leap from previous tone
2. Departure: by step to chord tone (resolution)
3. Typically accented (coinciding with harmonic change)
4. Creates dramatic arrival on dissonance followed by smooth resolution
5. Inverse of changing tone pattern
6. Resolution may be ascending or descending

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify a tone approached by leap and left by step
- Note whether it is accented (coincides with harmonic change)
- Determine resolution direction

# Context & Application
In twentieth-century harmony, appoggiaturas retain their expressive function but operate within expanded harmonic contexts. They may resolve to dissonant chord tones, creating chains of dissonance-to-dissonance motion. The leaning quality gives melodic lines expressive intensity.

# Common Errors
- Confusing appoggiaturas with changing tones

# Common Confusions
- Appoggiaturas and changing tones are opposites: appoggiaturas leap then step; changing tones step then leap
- "Accented" refers to timing with harmonic change, not metric stress
- Resolution step may go up or down
