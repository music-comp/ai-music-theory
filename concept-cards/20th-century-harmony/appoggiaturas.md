# Quick Definition
Nonharmonic tones approached by skip (leap) and resolved by step, creating a "leaning" effect into the resolution.

# Formal Definition
Appoggiaturas constitute the third category of nonharmonic tones in Persichetti's classification. They are approached by a skip and left conjunctly (by step). The term derives from the Italian "appoggiare" (to lean), reflecting how these tones lean into their resolution. Appoggiaturas typically occur on accented positions, creating emphasis on the dissonance before its stepwise resolution.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Appoggiatura characteristics:
- **Approach**: By leap from previous tone
- **Departure**: By step to chord tone (resolution)
- **Metric position**: Typically accented (occurring with harmony change)
- **Direction**: Resolution may be ascending or descending

The pattern creates a dramatic arrival on a dissonance followed by smooth resolution. The leap approach gives the appoggiatura expressive weight.

# Musical Context/Application
In twentieth-century harmony, appoggiaturas retain their expressive function but operate within expanded harmonic contexts. They may resolve to tones of complex chords, creating chains of dissonance-to-dissonance motion. The appoggiatura is particularly effective in preparing intervallic textures, as noted in Chapter 13's discussion of textural transitions. The leaning quality of appoggiaturas gives melodic lines expressive intensity.

# Common Confusions
- Appoggiaturas and changing tones are opposites: appoggiaturas leap then step; changing tones step then leap
- Appoggiaturas are typically accented; other nonharmonic tones may or may not be
- The resolution step may go up or down depending on context
