# Quick Definition
The principles governing melodic movement in quartal harmony, where chord members move freely if one voice maintains strong melodic purpose, and tritones resolve to the nearest scale tone.

# Formal Definition
Any chord tone in any type of three- or four-note quartal chord may skip a fourth or seventh if the other tones remain stationary. The result is a larger version of the same chord. Any type of three-note chord by fourths can progress diatonically, chromatically, or by a skip to any other chord by fourths if one voice moves with strong melodic purpose. When a florid voice is added to three-note chords by fourths, greater harmonic freedom is possible.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
**Skip Principle:**
- Any chord tone may skip a fourth or seventh
- Other tones remain stationary
- Result: expanded version of original chord

**Tritone Resolution:**
In chords containing the augmented fourth:
- Upper note of tritone resolves to nearest scale tone
- If neighboring tones are equidistant: either direction acceptable

**Progression Types:**
Three-note quartal chords may progress:
1. Diatonically (within a scale)
2. Chromatically (by semitone)
3. By skip (larger intervals)

**Melodic Purpose:**
Strong melodic direction in one voice validates any progression between quartal chords.

**Florid Voice Addition:**
A decorative melodic voice over sustained quartal chords enables greater harmonic freedom.

# Musical Context/Application
Quartal voice leading is more flexible than tertian voice leading because of the rootless, ambiguous nature of quartal chords. Without the gravitational pull of traditional root movement, voices can move more freely as long as melodic logic is maintained.

A pedal point lessens any dissonant tone's need for resolution, further increasing voice-leading freedom in quartal contexts.

# Common Confusions
Students may seek strict voice-leading rules for quartal harmony analogous to tertian rules. Quartal voice leading is fundamentally more flexible; the guiding principle is melodic purpose rather than specific interval restrictions.
