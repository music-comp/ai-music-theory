# Quick Definition
Principles governing melodic movement in quartal harmony: any chord tone may skip a fourth or seventh if others remain stationary; any three-note quartal chord may progress diatonically, chromatically, or by skip if one voice moves with strong melodic purpose; and a florid voice enables greater harmonic freedom.

# Core Definition
Any chord tone in any type of three- or four-note quartal chord may skip a fourth or seventh if the other tones remain stationary, resulting in a larger version of the same chord. Any type of three-note chord by fourths can progress diatonically, chromatically, or by skip to any other chord by fourths if one voice moves with strong melodic purpose. When a florid voice is added to three-note chords by fourths, greater harmonic freedom is possible. A pedal point lessens any dissonant tone's need for resolution. Clarity is difficult in low registers; quartal chords flow more easily in upper voices.

# Key Properties
1. Skip principle: any tone skips a fourth or seventh, others stationary = larger version of same chord
2. Progression: diatonic, chromatic, or skip -- all valid if one voice has melodic purpose
3. Florid voice addition: enables greater harmonic freedom
4. Pedal point: lessens resolution requirements
5. Strong melodic direction in one voice validates any quartal progression
6. More flexible than tertian voice leading due to rootless ambiguity
7. Guided by melodic purpose, not by specific interval restrictions

# Construction / Recognition
- Any tone skipping a fourth or seventh expands the chord
- One voice with strong melodic direction validates any progression
- Florid decoration over sustained quartal chords = maximum freedom
- Pedal reduces tritone resolution pressure

# Context & Application
Quartal voice leading is fundamentally more flexible than tertian voice leading because of the rootless, ambiguous nature of quartal chords. Without traditional root-movement gravity, voices move more freely as long as melodic logic is maintained. The guiding principle is melodic purpose rather than specific interval restrictions.
