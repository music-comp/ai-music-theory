# Quick Definition

A succession of chords that establishes a definite direction with formal function, where the goal may be reached or abandoned, and tonality fixed or forsaken.

# Formal Definition

**Harmonic progression** is a succession of chords that establishes a definite direction and has formal function. Unlike a mere succession of chords, a progression implies goal-directed motion. The goal of a progression may be reached or abandoned, a tonality fixed or forsaken. Progressions may be established by root movement, contrapuntal lines, or beds of sound, and the composer is able to guide harmony in any direction using these means.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Progression governed by:
1. **Root movement**: Action of the root (may differ from bass location)
2. **Bass location**: The sounding bass note (may differ from root)
3. **Contrapuntal lines**: Melodic motion creating harmonic succession
4. **Beds of sound**: Mass sonority movement without clear root function

Directional factors:
- Root may fall as bass rises, or rise as bass falls
- Melodic and harmonic tension lines may vary independently from root movement
- Register placement affects direction of sound but strong harmonic pull can resist register climb

# Musical Context/Application

Twentieth-century harmonic progression may be based on:
- Fifth, third, or second relationships between chords
- Tension created by moving from one relationship type to another
- Horizontal movement of voices without clear root feeling
- Chromatic freedom not governed by scale

The composer considers:
- Textural influence of characteristic intervals
- Effect of melodic motifs on harmonic phrase shapes
- Tritone behavior in horizontal and vertical situations
- Recovery of equilibrium after swift compositional changes

# Common Confusions

- **Progression vs. succession**: A succession is any sequence of chords; a progression implies direction and formal function.
- **Root vs. bass**: These may differ; root determines harmonic quality while bass affects voicing and inversional character.
- **Goal-directed vs. static**: Progressions imply motion toward a goal; static harmony lacks this directional quality.
