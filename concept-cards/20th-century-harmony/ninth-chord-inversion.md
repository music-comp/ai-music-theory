# Quick Definition
The various positions of ninth chords created by placing different chord members in the bass, with distinctive characteristics including the rough texture of fourth inversion (ninth in bass) and the reduced agility when the ninth is below the root.

# Formal Definition
When the ninth of the chord is below the root, the chord is less agile; fluent part-writing helps the progression. The fourth inversion (ninth in the bass) creates a hard texture and is effective in rough-hewn passages. Omitting the fifth in fourth inversion produces more suppleness. The minor dominant ninth in fourth inversion is often found with its seventh omitted in parallel harmony. In fourth inversion, the interval of the minor ninth is sometimes spelled as a diminished octave to facilitate reading.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
**Inversions of Ninth Chords:**
- Root position: Root in bass
- First inversion: Third in bass
- Second inversion: Fifth in bass
- Third inversion: Seventh in bass
- Fourth inversion: Ninth in bass

**Fourth Inversion Characteristics:**
- Creates sharp dissonance (minor second or major seventh against bass)
- Hard texture, suitable for aggressive passages
- Omitting the fifth adds suppleness
- Minor ninth often respelled as diminished octave for easier reading

**Voicing Considerations:**
- When ninth is below root: reduced mobility, requires fluent voice leading
- Fourth inversion with seventh omitted: common in parallel harmony

# Musical Context/Application
The choice of inversion significantly affects the character and mobility of ninth chords. Root position and early inversions maintain the chord's suppleness, while fourth inversion creates a distinctive, somewhat harsh sonority. Composers select inversions based on the desired textural effect and the requirements of voice leading.

Fourth inversion ninth chords are particularly effective in loud, rough passages where their hard texture contributes to the musical expression. In gentler contexts, other inversions are preferred.

# Common Confusions
Students may assume ninth chords can be freely inverted like simpler chords. While ninth chords do have all possible inversions, each has distinctly different properties. The fourth inversion in particular is not just another voicing but a fundamentally different sonority requiring different handling.
