# Quick Definition
Cadential formulas in quartal harmony where the final chord is more powerful in inverted form, the penultimate chord may have any bass tone, and quartal chords can serve as "dominants" in cadences of any harmonic idiom.

# Core Definition
In quartal cadences the final chord is more powerful in its inverted form. Quartal chord members move so freely that in cadences made solely of chords by fourths the next to the last fourth chord may have any bass tone. Chords by fourths are used as "dominants" in cadences of any harmonic idiom. Any mixture of chords may be used in quartal cadences if the interval of the fourth is predominant in the harmony. The inverted final chord gives a sense of arrival through the fifth in the bass, without the definite root establishment of tertian cadences.

# Key Properties
1. Final chord more powerful in inverted form (fifth in bass)
2. Penultimate chord: any bass tone acceptable
3. No traditional dominant-tonic bass relationship required
4. Quartal chords function as "dominants" in any harmonic idiom
5. Mixed chord types acceptable if fourths predominate
6. Sense of arrival without definite root establishment

# Construction / Recognition
- Look for inverted quartal final chord (fifth in bass)
- Penultimate chord has free bass movement
- Fourths must predominate in the overall cadential texture
- Quartal "dominant" function in mixed contexts

# Context & Application
Quartal cadences provide an alternative to traditional dominant-tonic progressions, useful for maintaining tonal ambiguity while providing closure, creating modal or non-functional endings, and concluding mixed tertian-quartal passages. The freedom of penultimate chord bass placement reflects the rootless nature of quartal harmony.
