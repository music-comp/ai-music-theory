# Quick Definition
Cadential formulas using quartal harmony, where the final chord is more powerful in inverted form and the penultimate chord may have any bass tone, with quartal chords also functioning as "dominants" in cadences of any harmonic idiom.

# Formal Definition
In quartal cadences the final chord is more powerful in its inverted form. Quartal chord members move so freely that in cadences made solely of chords by fourths the next to the last fourth chord may have any bass tone. Chords by fourths are used as "dominants" in cadences of any harmonic idiom. Any mixture of chords may be used in quartal cadences if the interval of the fourth is predominant in the harmony.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
**Final Chord Principles:**
- Inverted form = more powerful than root position
- The fifth in bass provides stronger sense of arrival

**Penultimate Chord Freedom:**
- Any bass tone acceptable
- No traditional dominant-tonic bass relationship required
- Movement is guided by voice leading, not root function

**Quartal "Dominants":**
Chords by fourths function as dominants in:
- Pure quartal progressions
- Mixed tertian-quartal progressions
- Any harmonic idiom where fourth predominates

**Mixed Cadence Principle:**
Any chord mixture is acceptable if fourths predominate in the overall texture.

# Musical Context/Application
Quartal cadences provide an alternative to traditional dominant-tonic progressions. The inverted final chord gives a sense of arrival without the definite root establishment of tertian cadences. This makes quartal cadences useful for:
1. Maintaining tonal ambiguity while providing closure
2. Creating modal or non-functional endings
3. Concluding passages of mixed tertian-quartal harmony

The freedom of penultimate chord bass placement reflects the rootless nature of quartal harmony.

# Common Confusions
Students may expect quartal cadences to follow tertian models with specific bass relationships. Quartal cadences work differently: the sense of arrival comes from the inverted final chord and the predominance of fourths, not from root movement by fifth.
