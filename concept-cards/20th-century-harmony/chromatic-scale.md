# Quick Definition
The twelve-note scale dividing the octave into equal half steps, which serves as both an ornamentation of diatonic scales and an independent (dodecuple) scale with twelve equally important steps.

# Core Definition
The chromatic scale is made of the octave divided into twelve half steps. It may be used as an ornamentation of a diatonic scale, or as an independent scale (dodecuple) with twelve equally important steps. This chromatic scale may impose tonic feeling through fixed or shifting centers or may have no definite tonality. Modal versions are no more possible than in the whole-tone scale (equidistant intervals). Chords of equidistant intervals or any combination of mixed intervals may be built from the scale, constructed upon any or all chromatic tones, and brought within the gravitation of a tonic center through any plan of chordal relationships.

# Prerequisites
- **interval** -- the chromatic scale uses all twelve semitones

# Key Properties
1. **Twelve equal semitones**: H-H-H-H-H-H-H-H-H-H-H-H
2. **Dual function**: ornamental (diatonic embellishment) or independent (dodecuple)
3. **No modes possible**: all rotations are transpositions
4. **Tonal flexibility**: tonal (with center), pan-tonal, or atonal
5. **Complete chord freedom**: any equidistant or mixed interval combination
6. **Foundation**: the twelve-tone chromatic scale underlies all 20th-century harmonic thinking
7. **Key signatures seldom used**: in 20th-century music, due to rapid shifts in tonal centers and modality

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the twelve-semitone collection
2. Determine whether the chromatic writing is ornamental or independent
3. If independent, check for tonal center (present, shifting, or absent)
4. Classify the type of chromatic writing (figuration, chromatic harmony, chromatic melody, etc.)

# Context & Application
In twentieth-century music, key signatures are seldom used because tonal centers and modality shift rapidly and because atonality is often present. The enharmonic choice of spelling is determined by the ease of readability. Chromatic harmony often contains miscellaneous intervallic building materials, forming a compound type. When characteristic melodic groups are used vertically, chords are formed by intervals from the horizontal motif -- composition with twelve notes generates this kind of harmony.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming chromatic writing necessarily means atonality. **Correction**: Chromatic materials can function within a tonal framework through chordal relationships and tonal centers.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Equating "chromatic" with "twelve-tone technique." **Clarification**: Chromatic writing encompasses many approaches; twelve-tone technique is one specific method of organizing chromatic materials.
