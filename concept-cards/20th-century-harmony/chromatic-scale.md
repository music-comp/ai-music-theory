# Quick Definition
The twelve-note scale dividing the octave into equal half steps, which serves as both an ornamentation of diatonic scales and an independent (dodecuple) scale with twelve equally important steps.

# Formal Definition
The chromatic scale is made of the octave divided into twelve half steps. It may be used as an ornamentation of a diatonic scale or as an independent scale (dodecuple) with twelve equally important steps. This chromatic scale may impose tonic feeling through fixed or shifting centers or may have no definite tonality. Modal versions are no more possible than in the whole-tone scale.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Structure and properties:
- 12 equal semitones: H-H-H-H-H-H-H-H-H-H-H-H
- All intervals equidistant
- No modes possible (all rotations are transpositions)
- Foundation for 20th-century harmonic thinking
- May be tonal, pan-tonal, or atonal

Chord possibilities:
- Any equidistant interval combination
- Any mixed interval combination
- Built on any chromatic tone
- May or may not gravitate to tonic center

# Musical Context/Application
Chords may be constructed upon any or all chromatic tones and brought within the gravitation of a tonic center through any plan of chordal relationships. Chromatic harmony often contains miscellaneous intervallic building materials, forming compound types. The equidistant structure allows complete freedom in chord construction and voice leading.

# Common Confusions
Students may think chromatic writing necessarily means atonality. Chromatic materials can function within a tonal framework through establishment of chord relationships and tonal centers, or may operate independently of key.
