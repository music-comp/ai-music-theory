# Quick Definition

A compositional approach where a melody is constructed so that part of it inverts another part, and subsequent simultaneous use of original and inversion generates mirror chord structures derived from thematic material.

# Formal Definition

**Thematic mirror writing** is a compositional technique in which mirror harmony stems from thematic sources. An initial melody is constructed so that part of the melody is the intervallic inversion of another part. When the original melody and its inversion are subsequently used simultaneously, the combination breeds mirrored vertical structures that are generated by the thematic elements of the work, creating organic unity between melodic and harmonic content.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Process:
1. **Theme construction**: Create melody where segment A inverts to produce segment B
2. **Simultaneous presentation**: Sound original and inverted forms together
3. **Harmonic generation**: Vertical coincidences produce mirror chords
4. **Organic derivation**: All harmony derives from thematic intervals

The technique ensures:
- Melodic-harmonic unity: Chords grow from melodic material
- Structural coherence: Mirror relationships pervade both dimensions
- Motivic saturation: Thematic intervals appear vertically and horizontally

# Musical Context/Application

Thematic mirror writing provides:
- Organic connection between melody and harmony
- Built-in developmental potential (the theme generates its own harmonization)
- Structural unity across a composition
- Fresh sound arising from consistent intervallic relationships

This approach integrates mirror technique into the compositional process from the outset, rather than applying mirror principles to pre-existing material.

# Common Confusions

- **Thematic vs. applied mirror**: Thematic mirror writing generates harmony from melodic material; applied mirror writing mirrors pre-existing chords or progressions.
- **Self-inverting melody**: The melody must be specifically constructed for mirror potential; not all melodies produce useful mirror harmony.
- **Simultaneity requirement**: The mirror harmony emerges only when original and inversion sound together; separate presentation does not produce mirror chords.
