# Quick Definition
The process of generating musical material from initial melodic, rhythmic, or harmonic ideas, with awareness of tempo, dynamics, and medium as integral parts of thematic identity.

# Formal Definition
A melodic kernel of two or more tones may form the nucleus from which the subject matter of an entire work is shaped and harmony derived. The compositional process is meaningless unless thematic statements are identifiable, for continuity and coherence are effected through aural retention of motifs. It is imperative that a sense of tempo, dynamics, and medium be part of thematic conception. Even a single tone can serve as a point of departure through dynamic and timbre implications.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Thematic generation sources:
- Melodic kernel of two or more tones
- Scale formation
- Series of non-scale tones
- Chordal structure
- Single repeating tone finding new tone
- Complex chord shedding dissonant tones
- Instrumental interaction (brasses answering timpani)

Performance context elements:
- Tempo (essential to identity)
- Dynamics (essential to identity)
- Medium/timbre (essential to identity)
- Articulation and stress patterns

Pattern shapes generating material:
- Fast-moving soprano voices
- Brass figures accompanying strings
- Strings under solo piano unison melody

# Musical Context/Application
Creative momentum might stem from diverse sources: a scale formation, a series of non-scale tones, a chordal structure, or even the excitement of specific instrumental combinations. Material might be born of creative tension caused by pattern shapes. Thematic ideas may be purposefully enigmatic and vague, positive and complete, or musically neutral, but they should be positively vague, not vaguely positive.

# Examples

# Common Confusions
- Tempo, dynamics, and medium are integral to thematic identity, not separate
- Thematic statements must be identifiable for coherence
- Even vague themes should be "positively vague"
