# Quick Definition
The tonic and characteristic chords of a synthetic scale, identified through systematic analysis of the scale's color tones and intervallic content.

# Core Definition
The harmonic usefulness of new scales is determined by surveying their indigenous chordal materials. The primary chords are the tonic plus the two triads that include the most determinable characteristic color tones. Three identification methods exist: (1) if a major-scale tetrachord is present, color tones are those outside it; (2) if no major-key tetrachord, primaries are triads that are enharmonic spellings of major or minor triads; (3) if no such triads exist, characteristic tones form augmented or diminished intervals with the tonic. Most synthetic scales produce triads with diminished or augmented thirds that are usually chromatically converted to basic triads. The burden of maintaining the scale's flavor is placed upon the melody.

# Key Properties
1. **Three identification methods** (in priority order):
   - Major tetrachord present: color tones are outside it
   - No major tetrachord: primaries are enharmonic major/minor triads
   - No enharmonic triads: tones forming aug/dim intervals with tonic
2. **Unusual triads**: augmented/diminished thirds often chromatically converted to basic triads
3. **Melody carries the burden**: harmonic alteration must not disturb melodic scale tones
4. **Quick scale projection**: the sooner melody includes all scale tones, the better

# Construction / Recognition
1. Survey all triads built from the scale's tones
2. Apply the three-method hierarchy to identify primary chords
3. Chromatically convert unusual triads to major, minor, augmented, or diminished
4. Ensure melodic lines maintain strict scale tones regardless of harmonic alterations
5. Map secondary chords that function within the gravitational field of primaries

# Context & Application
A harmonic problem is created by the fact that most synthetic scales produce one or more triads with diminished or augmented thirds, and often these triads are primaries. They are usually chromatically converted into one of the four basic triads. Altering the harmonic texture should not disturb strict scale tones in melodic writing, because the burden of maintaining scale flavor is placed upon the melody.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Using all triads from a synthetic scale without identifying primaries. **Correction**: Primary chords carry the characteristic color; secondary chords support but don't define scale identity.
- **Error**: Altering the melody along with the harmony. **Correction**: Melody must maintain strict scale tones; only harmony may be chromatically adapted.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking triads with augmented/diminished thirds are usable as-is. **Clarification**: These sound like quartal or secundal formations, not triads, and are usually converted to standard triad types for clarity.
