# Quick Definition
The tonic and characteristic chords of a synthetic scale, identified through systematic analysis of the scale's intervallic content and color tones.

# Formal Definition
The harmonic usefulness of new scales is determined by surveying their indigenous chordal materials. The primary chords are the tonic plus the two triads that include the scale step or steps containing the most determinable characteristic colors. The remaining triads are secondary formations functioning within the gravitation of primary chords.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Identification methods:
1. If major-scale tetrachord present: color tones are those outside the tetrachord
2. If no major-key tetrachord: primaries are triads spelled as enharmonic major/minor triads
3. If no enharmonic major/minor triads exist: characteristic tones form augmented/diminished intervals with tonic

Harmonic adaptation:
- Triads with diminished/augmented thirds often chromatically converted
- Convert to major, minor, augmented, or diminished triads
- Maintain strict scale tones in melody

# Musical Context/Application
A harmonic problem is created by the fact that most synthetic scales produce one or more triads with diminished or augmented thirds, and often these triads are primaries. They are usually chromatically converted into basic triads. The burden of maintaining the flavor of the prevailing scale is placed upon the melody, which should include all scale tones quickly.

# Common Confusions
Students may try to use all triads from a synthetic scale without identifying primaries. The primary chords carry the characteristic color; secondary chords support but don't define the scale's identity.
