# Quick Definition
Harmonic writing that employs all twelve chromatic tones, ranging from chromatic embellishment of diatonic progressions to fully chromatic textures with or without tonal centers.

# Formal Definition
Chromatic harmony encompasses various approaches: chromatic figuration of non-chromatic harmony, chromatic harmony with diatonic melodic writing, chromatic melody generated by chromatic harmony, mixed chordal structures formed by chromatic motion of parts, and chromatic harmony generated by chromatic melodic writing. When characteristic melodic groups are used vertically, chords are formed by intervals from the horizontal motif.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Types of chromatic writing:
1. Chromatic figuration of non-chromatic harmony (decorative)
2. Chromatic harmony with diatonic melody (harmonic chromaticism)
3. Chromatic melody over chromatic harmony (full chromaticism)
4. Mixed structures from chromatic voice motion
5. Harmony derived from chromatic melodic motifs

Twelve-tone derivation:
- Characteristic melodic groups used vertically
- Chords formed by intervals from horizontal motifs
- Composition with twelve notes generates this harmony

# Musical Context/Application
Chromatic harmony often contains miscellaneous intervallic building materials, forming compound types. Chords may be constructed on any chromatic tone and brought within tonic gravitation through chordal relationships. The relationship between chromatic melody and harmony can work in either direction, with one generating or supporting the other.

# Examples
- Stravinsky, Symphony of Psalms, p. 2: Chromatic figuration of non-chromatic harmony
- Harris, Little Suite, p. 5: Chromatic harmony with diatonic melody
- Berg, Funf Orchester-Lieder, Op. 4, p. 5: Chromatic harmony with chromatic melody
- Ex. 2-53: Mixed chordal structures from chromatic part motion
- Sessions, Piano Concerto, p. 34: Full chromatic harmony

# Common Confusions
Students may treat all chromatic harmony as equivalent. Persichetti distinguishes multiple types based on the relationship between chromatic and diatonic elements in melody versus harmony, each with different compositional implications.
