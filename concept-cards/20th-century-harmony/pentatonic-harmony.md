# Quick Definition
The use of pentatonic scales for harmonic materials, which requires special techniques to prevent monotony due to the scale's limited intervallic resources.

# Formal Definition
Diatonic scales of five tones are harmonically limited in scope because of the lack of semitones. When melody and harmony are pentatonic, changing modal versions or moving from one pentatonic to another will help prevent monotony. Pentatonic materials function well melodically or harmonically, but seldom both simultaneously.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Devices to prevent pentatonic monotony:
- Change modal versions of pentatonic
- Move from one pentatonic scale to another
- Use ornamental tones
- Employ pedal points
- Apply frequent modal interchanges or modulations
- Combine with non-pentatonic harmony

Harmonic combinations:
- Pentatonic melody + non-pentatonic harmony (effective)
- Pure pentatonic (melody + harmony): limited to short spans
- One pentatonic type + another type (same or different centers)

# Musical Context/Application
Pentatonic melodies are often harmonized with foreign (non-pentatonic) chords. One type of pentatonic scale combines well with another type on the same or different key centers. Lavish use of ornamental tones, pedal points, and frequent modal changes help prevent harmonic monotony in pure pentatonic music.

# Common Confusions
Students may try to harmonize pentatonic melodies exclusively with pentatonic chords, leading to static harmony. The combination of pentatonic melody with non-pentatonic harmony or multiple pentatonic types is often more effective.
