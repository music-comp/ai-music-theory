# Quick Definition
Techniques for using pentatonic scales harmonically, overcoming their inherent limitations through modal change, ornamental tones, pedal points, and combination with non-pentatonic materials.

# Core Definition
Diatonic scales of five tones are harmonically limited in scope because of the lack of semitones. When melody and harmony are pentatonic, changing the modal versions or moving from one pentatonic to another will help prevent monotony. Pentatonic materials function well melodically or harmonically, but seldom both simultaneously. Pentatonic melodies are often harmonized with foreign chords. Lavish use of ornamental tones, pedal points, and frequent modal interchanges or modulations to other pentatonics also help prevent harmonic monotony.

# Key Properties
1. **Devices to prevent monotony**:
   - Change modal versions of pentatonic
   - Move from one pentatonic type to another
   - Use ornamental tones
   - Employ pedal points
   - Apply frequent modal interchanges/modulations
   - Combine with non-pentatonic harmony
2. **Foreign chord harmonization**: pentatonic melodies often need non-pentatonic chords
3. **Cross-type combination**: one pentatonic type combines well with another on same or different centers
4. **Short spans**: pure pentatonic harmony most effective briefly

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the pentatonic type and mode in melody
2. Check whether harmony is also pentatonic (pure) or foreign
3. Look for devices preventing monotony (modal shifts, ornamental tones, pedal points)
4. Note combinations of different pentatonic types or with non-pentatonic materials

# Context & Application
Pure pentatonic music (non-polymodal, etc.) is most effective when used for short spans of time. The combination of pentatonic melody with non-pentatonic harmony creates a tension between the two independent forces -- melody and harmony -- that can be creatively exploited. One type of pentatonic scale combines well with another type on the same or different key centers.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Harmonizing pentatonic melodies exclusively with pentatonic chords, leading to static harmony. **Correction**: Foreign (non-pentatonic) harmony is often more effective.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking pure pentatonic passages are inherently boring. **Clarification**: With proper use of ornamental tones, modal shifts, and pedal points, pentatonic passages can be vivid -- but brevity helps.
