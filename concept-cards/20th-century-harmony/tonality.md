# Quick Definition
The organization of musical elements around a central tone or chord, implied through harmonic articulation and the tension-relaxation relationship of chords around a tonal base.

# Formal Definition
Tonality does not exist as an absolute. It is implied through harmonic articulation and through the tension and relaxation of chords around a tone or chord base. The tonal meaning of an isolated chord is indefinite; when surrounded by other chords, its meaning may be restricted to a single tonality, to two or more wavering tonalities, or made obviously atonal. A particular style or period is not always limited to a predilection for a single kind of tonality. Twentieth-century music makes use of many degrees of tonality and employs many means for establishing them.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Traditional tonality requirements:
- Scale and chord relationships for organization
- Usually three basic chords: one above tonic, one below, tonic itself
- Harmony with tritone gravitation helps establish center

Tonal pillars in balanced support:
- Subdominant and dominant to tonic
- Submediant and mediant
- Leading tone and supertonic
- Scale steps embracing the tritone

Alternative tonal forces:
- Extremely dissonant chord refusing to become consonant
- Simple chords resisting complex harmonic forces
- Two tonalities merging as dominating polychord
- Important tones recurring at decisive points
- Atonal music searching for key at cadential points

# Musical Context/Application
In strong tonal context, all elements of progression subordinate to pull of tonal center and drive toward cadential realization. However, key feeling may be created by forces other than tonal pull of chordal pillars. Music may hold tonality loosely with inherent atonality, or tonality may be established through contradictory elements or streamlined drive to tonic. There are many degrees of key-center gravity or keylessness in the general area of tonality or atonality.

# Common Confusions
- Tonality is not absolute; it exists in degrees
- Traditional chord pillars are not the only means of establishing tonality
- The boundary between tonality and atonality is indefinite
