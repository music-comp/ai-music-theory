# Quick Definition
Techniques for shifting chord tones, melodic lines, or entire harmonic masses to different octave registers to add freshness, variety, and directional impetus.

# Core Definition
**Register transfer** encompasses several techniques: inverting or transposing intervals containing common tones to expand register range; moving a voice to a note higher than the one just left by the voice above (or lower than the one left by the voice below) for harmonic lift or drop; deflecting melodic lines by shifting notes up or down an octave; shifting the entire harmonic mass suddenly to another register; and exploiting instrumental range suppleness for linear flexibility. Melodic rises and dips may be reinforced by successive octaves, fifths, and parallel harmony (Ch. 9, pp. 189-191).

# Prerequisites
- **Chord connection** -- understanding voice-leading techniques between chords

# Key Properties
1. Common-tone interval inversion expands register range
2. Voice crossing past adjacent voice creates harmonic lift or drop
3. Melodic deflection (octave displacement of individual notes) animates a line
4. Mass register shift relocates entire texture suddenly
5. Instrumental range contributes to linear flexibility
6. Parallel harmony and octave coupling reinforce melodic contour

# Construction / Recognition
- Look for common tones that appear in different octaves between successive chords
- Identify voice crossings that create lift or drop effects
- Note individual melody notes displaced by an octave within an otherwise stepwise line
- Observe sudden shifts of the entire harmonic texture to a new register

# Context & Application
Register transfer provides freshness without changing pitch content, exploits the full range of instruments and voices, and creates dramatic contrast through textural relocation. The slightest shift in register can change the meaning of a musical idea. The technique is particularly valuable when smooth progressions need refreshing, when melodic lines require animation, or when dramatic contrast is desired.

# Common Errors
- Overusing register transfer so that the texture becomes disjointed
- Neglecting the effect of register shift on harmonic character
- Failing to exploit instrumental range capabilities

# Common Confusions
- **Transfer vs. transposition**: Register transfer moves pitches by octave(s); transposition moves by any interval
- **Single voice vs. mass transfer**: Individual voice transfer differs in effect from full texture shift
- **Structural vs. ornamental**: Context determines whether register transfer is structurally significant or decorative
