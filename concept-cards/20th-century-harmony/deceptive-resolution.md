# Quick Definition

Unexpected chords appearing at resolution points that add freshness to harmonic flow, often moving to a chord whose root relation forms an interval not characteristic of the prevailing cyclical relationship.

# Formal Definition

**Deceptive resolution** (or deceptive cadence) occurs when a chord at a resolution point is replaced by an unexpected chord, adding freshness to harmonic flow. In twentieth-century harmonic practice, deceptive cadences typically imply motion from one chord to another whose root relation to the first forms an interval not characteristic of the prevailing chordal cycle. Deceptive progressions may also move to a chord outside the established modal or key realm, or employ chromatic root movement.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Deceptive resolution by cyclical relationship:

**In fifth relationship**: The chord of weaker impulse moves to a chord whose root lies a second above its own root (instead of expected fifth motion)

**In third relationship**: Root movement is up a fifth (instead of expected third motion)

**In second relationship**: Root movement is up a third (instead of expected second motion)

Other deceptive techniques:
- Movement to chord outside established modal/key realm
- Chromatic root movement
- Obliteration of all voices but one
- Obliteration of all voices except indefinite percussion pitch

# Musical Context/Application

Deceptive resolutions create:
- Temporary rest on a harmonic slant
- Need for harmonic, rhythmic, and melodic continuance
- Freshness within established harmonic contexts
- Phrase extension through avoided cadence
- Surprise and interest through thwarted expectations

The technique is particularly effective when:
- A strong expectation has been established
- Continuation is desired rather than closure
- Variety is needed within predictable harmonic motion
- Dramatic or expressive effect is sought

# Common Confusions

- **Deceptive vs. merely unexpected**: True deception requires established expectation based on context or convention; random unexpected chords are not deceptive.
- **Deceptive vs. half cadence**: A deceptive cadence substitutes for an expected resolution; a half cadence intentionally stops on dominant.
- **Cycle-specific deception**: What constitutes deception depends on the prevailing cyclical relationship; the "wrong" interval differs by context.
