# Quick Definition
The simultaneous use of two or more different modes on the same or different tonal centers, creating layers of contrasting modal colors.

# Formal Definition
Polymodality involves two or more different modes on the same or different tonal centers. The modal strands may be melodic or harmonic. When the same mode occurs simultaneously on different tonal centers, the passage is polytonal and modal, but not polymodal. When different modes occur on different tonal centers at the same time, the passage is both polymodal and polytonal.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Polymodal combinations:
- Different modes, same tonic (polymodal only)
- Same mode, different tonics (polytonal, not polymodal)
- Different modes, different tonics (polymodal and polytonal)

Layer structure:
- Melodic strand in one mode
- Harmonic strand in another mode
- Independence of layers creates polymodal texture

# Musical Context/Application
Polymodality creates rich harmonic textures by layering different modal collections. The melody may be in one mode while the harmony uses a different mode, or multiple melodic/harmonic strands may each employ their own mode. This technique is particularly effective in twentieth-century music for creating complex yet coherent harmonic colors.

# Common Confusions
Students often confuse polymodality with polytonality. Polymodality requires different modes (which may or may not be on different tonics), while polytonality requires different tonal centers (which may or may not use different modes). A passage can be one, both, or neither.
