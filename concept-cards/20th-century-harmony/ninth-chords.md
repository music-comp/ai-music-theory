# Quick Definition
Five-note tertian chords built by adding another third above the seventh, analyzable as two overlapping triads with polychordal implications, with twelve distinct types possible due to enharmonic duplication reducing the theoretical sixteen.

# Core Definition
The ninth member, like the seventh, has been freed from traditional dissonant treatment. Mathematically, it should be possible to build sixteen different ninth chords with major and minor thirds on one note, but because of enharmonic duplication only twelve are possible. For scanning purposes the ninth chord is analyzed as two triads, the top triad anchored to the uppermost note of the bottom triad. This dual chording has polychordal implications. Any note may be considered the root, third, fifth, seventh, or ninth of twelve different ninth chords, so a single tone may be harmonized with sixty different ninths. The increased harmonic weight of ninths often presents the problem of immobility.

# Prerequisites
- **Seventh chords in twentieth-century harmony**: Four-note tertian chords as stable entities

# Key Properties
1. Twelve distinct types (not sixteen, due to enharmonic duplication)
2. Analyzed as two overlapping triads (polychordal implications)
3. A single tone can be harmonized with sixty different ninth chords
4. Doubling: root or fifth for solidity; third or seventh for density of color; ninth for increased tension
5. Omission: fifth for richness; third or seventh for less color
6. Increased harmonic weight creates immobility problem
7. When ninth is below root, the chord is less agile
8. All cyclic and chromatic progressions available (as with triads and sevenths)

# Construction / Recognition
- Five notes built in thirds: Root + 3rd + 5th + 7th + 9th
- Analyze as two triads: bottom triad (R-3-5) + top triad anchored at uppermost note of bottom
- Immobility can be solved by merely touching the ninth with a moving voice from a smaller chord
- Successions of varying ninth types are effective in sequential patterns and combined with sevenths
- When combining with quartal or non-tertian harmony, space members to include the building interval of the other system

# Context & Application
All progressions possible with triads and sevenths work with ninth chords. The augmented fourth root relationship provides fresh chord-to-chord activity. Ninths used with technical facility add freshness to harmonic progression. The problem of immobility can be mitigated by using the ninth as a passing sonority touched by a moving voice rather than fully sustained. When ninth chords precede quartal chords, arrange members to emphasize a fourth interval, preparing the ear for the texture change.

# Common Errors
- Mistaking ninth chords for polychords (ninths are unified five-note sonorities; polychords emphasize triadic independence)
- Sustaining full ninth chords without managing the immobility problem

# Common Confusions
- **Ninth vs. polychord**: While the polychordal analysis is useful for understanding structure, a true ninth chord functions as a unified sonority
- **Sixteen vs. twelve**: Enharmonic duplication reduces the theoretical sixteen possibilities to twelve distinct types
- **Sixty harmonizations**: Each melody note, considered as any of five chord positions in twelve types, yields sixty ninth-chord possibilities
