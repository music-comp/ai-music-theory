# Quick Definition
Five-note tertian chords built by adding another third above the seventh, analyzed as consisting of two overlapping triads, with twelve distinct types possible through combinations of major and minor thirds.

# Formal Definition
The ninth member of chords, like the seventh, has been freed from traditional dissonant treatment in twentieth-century practice. Ninth chords may progress within or outside any scale formation. Mathematically, it should be possible to build sixteen different ninth chords with major and minor thirds on one note; but because of enharmonic duplication of certain notes, only twelve are possible. For scanning purposes, the ninth chord is analyzed as consisting of two triads, the top triad anchored to the uppermost note of the bottom triad. This dual chording has polychordal implications.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
**Construction:**
Five notes built in thirds: Root + 3rd + 5th + 7th + 9th

**Polychordal Analysis:**
A ninth chord consists of two triads:
- Bottom triad: Root, 3rd, 5th
- Top triad: 3rd, 5th, 7th anchored to 9th above

**Twelve Ninth Chord Types:**
Due to enharmonic equivalence, only 12 (not 16) distinct ninth chords are possible on any root.

**Doubling Principles:**
- Root or fifth - for solidity
- Third or seventh - for density of color
- Ninth - for increased tension

**Omission Principles:**
- Fifth - for richness
- Third or seventh - for less color

# Musical Context/Application
All types of harmonic progression possible with triads and sevenths are possible with ninth chords. The augmented fourth root relationship provides fresh activity from chord to chord. Successions of ninths of varying intervallic textures are effective in sequential patterns and in combination with seventh chords.

The increased harmonic weight of ninths often presents the problem of immobility. This may be solved by merely touching the ninth with a voice moving from a triad or seventh chord, rather than sustaining the full ninth chord.

# Common Confusions
Students often mistake ninth chords for polychords or vice versa. While the polychordal analysis is useful for understanding ninth chord structure, a true ninth chord functions as a unified five-note sonority, whereas a polychord emphasizes the independence of its constituent triads.
