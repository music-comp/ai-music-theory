# Quick Definition

The intervallic relationship between successive chord roots, which provides directional strength independent of bass voice location or chord inversion.

# Formal Definition

**Root movement** describes the intervallic distance and direction traveled by the conceptual root from one chord to the next in a harmonic progression. In root progression, inverted or otherwise, there are two directional factors: the action of the root (which is not necessarily in the bass) and the location of the bass. These may agree or contradict in direction. Root movement provides characteristic strength, softness, blandness, or ambiguity to progressions based on the interval traversed.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Root movement character by interval:
- **Perfect fifth**: Strength (encompasses all twelve tones in the cycle of fifths)
- **Third (major/minor)**: Softness
- **Second (major/minor)**: Blandness
- **Tritone**: Ambiguity (uninvertible, indefinite direction)

Note: These four interval types together subsume all twelve tones.

Root direction:
- I-V, I-III, I-II: Root direction is UP regardless of voicing
- I-IV, I-VI, I-VII: Root direction is DOWN regardless of voicing
- Tritone: Direction indefinite due to its symmetrical nature

# Musical Context/Application

In twentieth-century tonal music, root distance between important chords is usually determined by the cyclical relationship upon which the music is constructed:
- **Fifth relationship**: Governed by perfect fifth series (12 tones)
- **Third relationship**: Chromatic compass via alternating major/minor thirds
- **Second relationship**: Series of major/minor seconds encircling 12 tones

Understanding root movement helps the composer:
- Control harmonic strength and directional clarity
- Create variety through different interval relationships
- Manage tonal ambiguity through tritone relationships
- Distinguish between functional and non-functional progressions

# Common Confusions

- **Root vs. bass**: The root determines harmonic identity; the bass determines voicing. They may be the same note or different notes.
- **Root direction vs. registral direction**: Root direction is conceptual (based on interval class); registral direction is physical (actual pitch movement).
- **Tritone ambiguity**: The tritone divides the octave equally, making its directional implication genuinely ambiguous.
