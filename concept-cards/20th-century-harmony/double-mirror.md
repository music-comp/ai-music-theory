# Quick Definition

A mirror chord that has been mirrored a second time, producing complex structures typically used alongside large unmirrored compound chords.

# Formal Definition

A **double mirror** is a mirror chord that undergoes a second application of the mirroring process, creating doubly reflected structures of considerable complexity. Any mirror chord may be doubly mirrored, resulting in formations that extend the symmetrical principles to a higher degree of elaboration. These complex structures are used primarily with large unmirrored compound chords to maintain textural consistency and complexity. Enharmonic changes are made when spelling becomes unwieldy.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Construction process:
1. Begin with original chord (any type)
2. First mirror: Reflect intervals around building tone(s)
3. Second mirror: Reflect the mirrored structure again around new or same axis

The double mirror principle can generate:
- Extremely large sonorities with internal symmetrical relationships
- Multiple axes of symmetry within single formations
- Complex but internally logical structures

Practical consideration: Enharmonic spelling simplifies notation when double mirroring creates complex chromatic relationships.

# Musical Context/Application

Double mirror structures serve:
- Creating massive sonorities for climactic passages
- Matching complexity of large compound chords in multi-textural writing
- Providing logical internal organization for extremely complex harmonies
- Generating systematic material for extended reflective passages

The primary use is alongside large unmirrored compound chords, where the double mirror provides comparable complexity with built-in symmetrical logic.

# Common Confusions

- **Double mirror vs. two mirrors**: A double mirror applies the process twice to one structure; two separate mirror chords are different formations.
- **Complexity management**: Double mirrors create very complex sonorities; they work best alongside equally complex compound chords to maintain textural balance.
- **Enharmonic necessity**: Strict intervallic spelling may create unwieldy notation; practical spelling takes precedence over theoretical purity.
