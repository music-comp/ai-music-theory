# Quick Definition
Points of arrival in serial music formed by primary chords (center, cadential above, mirror below) or ornamental voice movement toward the center chord, with set completion also functioning as a cadential event.

# Core Definition
Strong cadences in serial music may be formed by the primary chords (center chord, transposed cadential chord above, mirrored chord below) or by ornamental movement of the parts directed toward the center chord. A strong center or harmonic area may result from the completion or fulfillment of the twelve-tone set. In serial harmonic progression, the order of chords is often changed to increase the gravitational tendency of the chordal formations to move to the center chord.

Chords may be ornamented approaching cadences by nonharmonic tones, by harmonic tones (from related chords), or by serial tones that result from the simultaneous use of two or more forms of the set.

# Prerequisites
- **Serial harmony**: The framework in which serial cadences operate
- **Serial center chord**: The focal sonority toward which cadences move
- **Melodic set**: The tone succession generating cadential material

# Key Properties
1. Formed by primary chords (center, cadential above, mirror below)
2. Ornamental voice movement toward center chord creates cadential effect
3. Set completion/fulfillment functions as cadential event
4. Chord order manipulation increases gravitational tendency
5. Three ornamentation types: nonharmonic tones, harmonic tones, serial tones
6. Serial tones from simultaneous set forms provide unique ornamentation
7. Analogous to tonal cadences in function

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify movement toward the serial center chord
- Note whether primary chords (cadential above, mirror below) precede the center
- Check for set completion as a structural marker
- Observe chord order manipulation for gravitational effect
- Identify ornamental tones: nonharmonic, harmonic, or serial (from multiple set forms)

# Context & Application
Serial cadences function analogously to tonal cadences: they create arrival, structural articulation, and formal punctuation. The manipulation of chord order to increase gravitational tendency toward the center chord parallels the dominant-tonic pull of tonal cadences. Set completion provides an additional cadential resource unique to serial music -- the fulfillment of the chromatic aggregate marks structural boundaries.

# Common Errors
- Assuming serial music cannot have cadential function
- Ignoring set completion as a structural marker
- Failing to manipulate chord order for gravitational effect

# Common Confusions
- **Analogy with tonal cadences**: Serial cadences function analogously but not identically to tonal cadences
- **Set completion**: The fulfillment of the twelve-tone aggregate is itself a cadential event
- **Ornamentation**: The same ornamental principles (nonharmonic tones, etc.) apply to serial as to tonal contexts
