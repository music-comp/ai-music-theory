# Quick Definition
Points of arrival in serial music formed by primary chords derived from the melodic set or by ornamental voice movement directed toward the center chord.

# Formal Definition
Strong cadences in serial music may be formed by the primary chords (center chord, transposed chord above, mirrored chord below) or by ornamental movement of the parts directed toward the center chord. A strong center or harmonic area may result from the completion or fulfillment of the twelve-tone set. The gravitational tendency of serial chords to move to the center chord creates cadential effects analogous to tonal cadences.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Serial cadence construction:
- **Primary chords**: Center chord, transposition above, mirror below
- **Ornamental approach**: Voice movement directed to center chord
- **Set completion**: Fulfillment of twelve-tone set creates arrival
- **Chord order manipulation**: Reordering to increase gravitational tendency

Chord ornamentation in cadences:
- Nonharmonic tones
- Harmonic tones (from related chords)
- Serial tones from simultaneous use of two or more set forms

# Musical Context/Application
In serial harmonic progression, the order of chords is often changed to increase the gravitational tendency of chordal formations to move to the center chord. This manipulation creates cadential drive analogous to dominant-tonic motion in tonal music. The completion of a twelve-tone set can itself function as a cadential event, marking structural boundaries. Chords may be ornamented approaching cadences just as in tonal music.
