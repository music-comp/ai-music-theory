# Quick Definition
The use of ornamental tones -- especially appoggiaturas and suspensions -- to prepare intervallic textures of incoming harmony, and the accenting of melodic tones that outline the characteristic intervallic formation of the new area.

# Core Definition
The versatile ornamental tone provides textural means for entering any harmonic region. The appoggiatura and suspension are particularly effective in preparing the intervallic texture. The melodic freedom of broken chords permits the fluctuation of chords of varying construction. Accenting melodic tones that outline the characteristic intervallic formation of the new area creates a tonal bond between the contrasting harmonic materials.

The type of chordal formation may be changed by subtracting from a melodic motif tones that leave only the tones of the new texture.

# Prerequisites
- **Textural transition**: The broader transitional technique

# Key Properties
1. Appoggiaturas and suspensions are particularly effective preparation
2. Broken chords permit fluctuation between chord types
3. Accented melodic tones outlining new intervallic formation create bonds
4. Motif subtraction can leave only tones of the target texture
5. Ornamental tones provide entry to any harmonic region
6. The process can be gradual and organic

# Construction / Recognition
- Use appoggiaturas or suspensions that resolve to tones characteristic of the target texture
- Accent melodic tones that outline the target intervallic formation
- Exploit broken-chord freedom to fluctuate between chord types
- Subtract tones from a motif, leaving only the tones of the new texture
- Note whether ornamental devices are preparing a textural change

# Context & Application
Ornamental transitions are among the most organic and least disruptive textural change methods. Because ornamental tones are expected within any texture, they can introduce the intervals of a new texture without signaling a conscious change. The process of motif subtraction -- removing tones until only the target texture's intervals remain -- is particularly elegant because it transforms existing material rather than replacing it.

# Common Errors
- Using ornamental tones that do not prepare the target texture
- Making the subtraction process too abrupt
- Neglecting to accent the melodic tones that outline the new formation

# Common Confusions
- **Preparation, not decoration**: In this context, ornamental tones serve a transitional function, not merely a decorative one
- **Motif subtraction**: Removing tones from a motif to reveal the new texture is a compositional technique, not a reductive analysis
- **Accented outlines**: The melodic accent on target-formation tones creates an audible bond between the old and new textures
