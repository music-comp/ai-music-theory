# Quick Definition
The treatment of the augmented fourth (tritone) within quartal chords, where the upper note of the tritone resolves to the nearest note of the prevailing melodic or scale formation.

# Formal Definition
In chords containing an augmented fourth (perfect-augmented or augmented-perfect) the upper note of the tritone resolves best to the nearest note of the prevailing melodic or scale formation. If the neighboring tones of the scale lie equal distances away, either direction is taken. When the augmented fourth is present in a four-note chord by fourths, the tritone moves most easily when placed at the top of the chord.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
**Resolution Direction:**
- Upper note of tritone moves to nearest scale tone
- If equidistant neighbors: either direction acceptable

**Optimal Placement:**
In four-note quartal chords:
- Tritone at top: easiest resolution
- Tritone in middle: more constrained
- Tritone at bottom: most difficult to manage

**Pedal Point Effect:**
A pedal point lessens any dissonant tone's need for resolution, including the tritone in quartal structures.

**Contrast with Tertian Tritone:**
In tertian dominant function, both tritone notes have specific resolution directions (leading tone up, seventh down). In quartal context, only the upper note has clear directional tendency.

# Musical Context/Application
Tritone resolution in quartal harmony is more flexible than in traditional dominant function:
1. Only the upper note has strong directional pull
2. Scale context determines resolution target
3. Pedal points can suspend resolution requirements
4. Placement affects ease of resolution

This flexibility allows quartal harmony to incorporate the tritone without the strong functional implications it carries in tertian contexts.

# Common Confusions
Students may apply tertian tritone resolution rules (both notes resolving in contrary motion) to quartal contexts. In quartal harmony, only the upper note of the tritone has clear directional tendency; the lower note moves according to voice-leading needs rather than functional obligation.
