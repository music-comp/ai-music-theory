# Quick Definition
The treatment of the augmented fourth within quartal chords, where the upper note resolves to the nearest scale tone, equidistant neighbors allow either direction, and the tritone moves most easily when placed at the top of four-note chords; pedal points lessen resolution needs.

# Core Definition
In chords containing an augmented fourth (perfect-augmented or augmented-perfect) the upper note of the tritone resolves best to the nearest note of the prevailing melodic or scale formation. If the neighboring tones of the scale lie equal distances away, either direction is taken. When the augmented fourth is present in a four-note chord by fourths, the tritone moves most easily when placed at the top of the chord. A pedal point lessens any dissonant tone's need for resolution.

# Key Properties
1. Upper note of tritone resolves to nearest scale tone
2. Equidistant neighbors: either direction acceptable
3. Tritone at top of four-note chord = easiest resolution
4. Tritone in middle or bottom = more constrained
5. Pedal point lessens resolution requirement
6. Only the upper note has strong directional tendency (unlike tertian where both notes resolve)

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify augmented fourth within quartal chord
- Upper note moves to nearest scale tone
- Place tritone at top of chord for flexibility
- Pedal beneath suspends resolution pressure

# Context & Application
Tritone resolution in quartal harmony is more flexible than in tertian dominant function. Only the upper note has strong directional pull; the lower note moves according to voice-leading needs. This flexibility allows quartal harmony to incorporate the tritone without the strong functional implications it carries in tertian contexts.
