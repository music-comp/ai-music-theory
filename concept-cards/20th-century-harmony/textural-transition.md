# Quick Definition
The techniques for smoothly moving from one type of harmonic texture to another through intervallic preparation, unison passages, or ornamental devices.

# Formal Definition
A textural problem arises from the manipulation of the transitional materials that lead from one type of harmony to another. The characteristic interval of one kind of harmony is contained in any other kind of harmony and may be brought to the surface by intervallic inversion. In all textural changes, an interval may be featured that, when inverted, produces the characteristic interval of the new harmony. When moving from one type of harmony to another, the characteristic interval of the new harmony is best introduced well in advance.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Intervallic preparation methods:
- **Thirds to fourths**: Feature inverted fifth in tertian chords
- **Seventh chords to quartal**: Inverted fifth from root or third
- **To secundal**: Emphasize second intervals in arrangement
- **Ninth chords to clusters**: Invert seventh from root or third

Transitional devices:
- **Doubling colorful tones**: Major third, major seventh, prominent nonharmonic tones suggest octave/unison
- **Unison passages**: Harmonic ambiguity allows entrance of any texture
- **Ornamental tones**: Appoggiatura and suspension prepare intervallic texture
- **Broken chords**: Melodic freedom permits fluctuation of chord types

Additional transition techniques:
- Accenting melodic tones outlining new intervallic formation
- Subtracting tones from motif to leave only new texture tones
- Equidistant chords weaving one fabric into another
- Sequential patterns driving varied chords over harmonic barriers

# Musical Context/Application
The versatile ornamental tone provides textural means for entering any harmonic region. The appoggiatura and suspension are particularly effective in preparing intervallic texture. Rough tritone relationships or prominent hidden fifths can divert attention from harmonic transformations. Cluster members may be gradually subtracted, leaving chords suggesting the harmony to follow; the reverse is equally effective.

# Common Confusions
- The characteristic interval of one texture exists within other textures
- Intervallic inversion reveals hidden connections between textures
- Preparation should begin well in advance of the actual change
