# Quick Definition
The techniques for smoothly moving between different harmonic texture types, exploiting the fact that the characteristic interval of any texture is contained within any other texture and can be brought to the surface through intervallic inversion.

# Core Definition
A textural problem arises from the manipulation of the transitional materials that lead from one type of harmony to another. The characteristic interval of one kind of harmony is contained in any other kind of harmony and may be brought to the surface by intervallic inversion. In all textural changes, an interval may be featured that, when inverted, produces the characteristic interval of the new harmony. When moving from one type of harmony to another, the characteristic interval of the new harmony is best introduced well in advance.

# Prerequisites
- **Combining textures**: The broader principle of texture integration

# Key Properties
1. Characteristic interval of one texture is contained in all others
2. Intervallic inversion reveals hidden connections between textures
3. New texture's characteristic interval should be introduced in advance
4. Multiple transitional devices: intervallic preparation, unison, ornamental tones, equidistant chords, cluster subtraction, sequential patterns
5. Broken chords permit fluctuation of varying chord types
6. Rough tritone relationships and hidden fifths divert attention from transformations

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the characteristic interval of the current texture and the target texture
- Find that interval (or its inversion) within the current texture
- Bring it to the surface before making the transition
- Use transitional devices appropriate to the specific texture change
- Begin preparation well in advance of the actual change

# Context & Application
Specific intervallic preparation methods include: featuring inverted fifths in tertian chords to prepare for quartal (the fifth from the root inverts to a fourth); emphasizing seconds in seventh-chord arrangements to prepare for secundal harmony; inverting the seventh in ninth chords to suggest clusters. The versatile ornamental tone provides means for entering any harmonic region. The appoggiatura and suspension are particularly effective. Accenting melodic tones that outline the new intervallic formation creates a bond between contrasting materials. Sequential patterns can drive varied chords over many harmonic barriers.

# Common Errors
- Making abrupt texture changes without preparation
- Failing to introduce the new characteristic interval in advance
- Ignoring intervallic inversion as a transitional resource

# Common Confusions
- **Contained intervals**: The characteristic interval of every texture type already exists within every other -- it just needs to be surfaced
- **Advance preparation**: The best transitions start well before the actual texture change
- **Attention diversion**: Tritone relationships and hidden fifths can mask the transition, making it smoother
