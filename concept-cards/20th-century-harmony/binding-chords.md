# Quick Definition

Versatile chords with indefinite roots (augmented triads, diminished seventh chords, quartal chords, whole-tone chords) that link different textural types while synthesizing various harmonic contexts.

# Formal Definition

**Binding chords** are harmonies whose roots are indefinite and thus vanish quickly, enabling them to link one kind of texture to another while acting as connectors in a synthesis of harmonic contexts. These include augmented triads, diminished seventh chords, chords by perfect fourths, and chords by major seconds (whole-tone chords). Their ambiguous root function allows them to move between different harmonic vocabularies without strong directional implications.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Characteristics of binding chords:
- **Symmetrical structure**: Equal divisions of the octave
- **Indefinite root**: No single tone clearly functions as root
- **Quick root dissolution**: Root feeling vanishes rapidly
- **Versatile function**: Can connect disparate harmonic types

Types:
- Augmented triad (M3+M3): Divides octave in three
- Diminished seventh (m3+m3+m3+m3): Divides octave in four
- Perfect fourth chords (P4+P4...): Equal fourth stacking
- Major second chords (M2+M2...): Whole-tone collection

# Musical Context/Application

Binding chords serve:
- Transitional passages between harmonic types
- Connecting tertian, quartal, and secundal textures
- Creating harmonic ambiguity at formal junctures
- Synthesizing multiple harmonic vocabularies

Their versatility enables:
- Smooth modulation between key areas
- Transition from tonal to atonal passages
- Connection of contrasting harmonic styles
- Formal articulation through harmonic neutrality

# Common Confusions

- **Binding vs. pivot**: Pivot chords function in two keys simultaneously; binding chords connect textures through ambiguity rather than dual function.
- **Indefinite vs. absent root**: Binding chords may have theoretical roots that are not perceptually stable, rather than no root at all.
- **Transitional vs. structural**: Binding chords typically serve transitional function; they may not provide structural harmonic foundation.
