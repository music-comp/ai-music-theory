# Quick Definition
A passage where a modal melody is harmonized with chords from the same mode and on the same tonal center, creating unified modal color.

# Formal Definition
A pure modal passage is one in which a modal melody is harmonized with chords from the same mode and on the same tonal center. This contrasts with polymodal writing where different modes or tonal centers create layered textures. Pure modal passages maintain a single, consistent modal identity.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Requirements for pure modal passage:
1. Melody uses only tones from one mode
2. Harmony uses only chords from the same mode
3. Both share the same tonal center
4. Characteristic scale step emphasized in both

Maintenance techniques:
- Melodic reference to tonic (circling, repetition)
- Chant-like tonic repetition
- Frequent cadences
- Emphasis on primary chords with characteristic step

# Musical Context/Application
Although shifting modes are effective on a stationary key center, much melodic reference must be made to the tonic to stay within the mode. Otherwise, the tonality will switch to major under the ionic power of the tritone. Melodic circling or chant-like repetition of the tonic tone and frequent cadences help maintain the center.

# Common Confusions
Students may assume all modal music is "pure modal." Many modal passages involve polymodality, modal interchange, or foreign harmony. Pure modal passages require deliberate consistency of mode and tonal center in both melody and harmony.
