# Quick Definition

A compound chord composed of intervals progressively diminishing in size from the bottom upward, fashioned in the image of the overtone series.

# Core Definition

"Many other graphic interval arrangements are possible. Some chords are fashioned in the image of the overtone series. A pyramidal type of compound chord is composed of a series of intervals diminishing from the bottom upward" (Persichetti, p. 164). "This pyramidal type may contain all twelve intervals but not necessarily twelve different notes" (p. 165).

# Prerequisites

- **Compound chord** — The pyramidal chord is a specific type of compound chord
- **Overtone series** — The diminishing-interval structure mirrors overtone proportions

# Key Properties

1. Intervals diminish progressively from bottom (largest) to top (smallest)
2. Mirrors the overtone series where intervals become closer as they ascend
3. May contain all twelve interval types
4. Does not necessarily use twelve different pitch classes (enharmonic/octave duplication possible)
5. Creates natural, acoustically-grounded sonority
6. Maximum clarity at bottom with increasing complexity at top

# Construction / Recognition

## To Construct
1. Begin with largest interval at bottom (e.g., P8 or P5)
2. Add progressively smaller intervals ascending (P5, P4, M3, m3, M2, m2)
3. May include all twelve intervals but pitch classes may repeat

# Context & Application

Pyramidal chords produce rich but intelligible harmonic masses due to their acoustic rationale. They work effectively in orchestral writing where bass instruments provide stable foundation, for massive but coherent climactic sonorities, and as opening or closing statements.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Requiring twelve different pitch classes for the twelve intervals
  **Correction**: All twelve intervals may be present without twelve distinct pitch classes

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Pyramidal chords function like tonal roots
  **Clarification**: The overtone relationship is structural/acoustic, not tonal-functional
- **Confusion**: Pyramidal construction is the same as inversion
  **Clarification**: Pyramidal is built by interval-size arrangement, not by inverting an existing chord
