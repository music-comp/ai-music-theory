# Quick Definition

A compound chord composed of a series of intervals that progressively diminish in size from the bottom upward, creating a pyramid-like structure.

# Formal Definition

A **pyramidal chord** is a type of compound chord fashioned in the image of the overtone series, where intervals become progressively smaller ascending from the lowest tones. This construction mirrors the natural acoustic phenomenon where overtones become closer together as they ascend. A pyramidal chord may contain all twelve intervals but not necessarily twelve different pitch classes.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Construction principle:
- Largest intervals at the bottom (octaves, fifths)
- Progressively smaller intervals moving upward
- Smallest intervals (seconds) at the top

Example structure (ascending from bass):
- P8 (12 semitones)
- P5 (7 semitones)
- P4 (5 semitones)
- M3 (4 semitones)
- m3 (3 semitones)
- M2 (2 semitones)
- m2 (1 semitone)

This mirrors the overtone series proportions where the fundamental-to-octave ratio (2:1) is larger than subsequent intervals.

# Musical Context/Application

Pyramidal chords:
- Create a natural, acoustically-grounded sonority
- Provide maximum clarity at the bottom with increasing complexity/tension at the top
- Work effectively in orchestral writing where bass instruments can provide stable foundation
- Produce a rich but intelligible harmonic mass
- Allow twelve different intervals while potentially using fewer than twelve pitch classes through enharmonic or octave duplication

The acoustic rationale makes pyramidal chords particularly suitable for passages requiring massive but coherent sonorities.

# Common Confusions

- **Pyramidal vs. inverted structure**: A pyramidal chord is built according to interval size from the bottom, not by inverting an existing chord type.
- **Twelve intervals vs. twelve notes**: A pyramidal chord may use all twelve interval classes without necessarily having twelve distinct pitch classes.
- **Acoustic basis vs. functional harmony**: The overtone series relationship is structural/acoustic, not tonal-functional; pyramidal chords do not imply root movement or key relationships.
