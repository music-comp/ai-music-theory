# Quick Definition
The four distinct intervallic varieties of three-note secundal chords, classified by the combination of major and minor seconds from bottom to top, arranged in order from most consonant (major-major) to most dissonant (minor-minor).

# Formal Definition
The four intervallic types of three-note chords by seconds are: major-major (M2-M2), major-minor (M2-m2), minor-major (m2-M2), and minor-minor (m2-m2). Arranged in an order of consonance progressing to dissonance, the major-major is most consonant and the minor-minor is most dissonant.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Type classification (bottom interval first):
1. **Major-Major (M-M)**:
   - Example: C-D-E
   - Outer interval: M3
   - Character: Most consonant, whole-tone flavor
   - Diatonic availability: Common in major/minor/modal scales

2. **Major-Minor (M-m)**:
   - Example: C-D-Eb
   - Outer interval: m3
   - Character: Moderately dissonant
   - Diatonic availability: Common

3. **Minor-Major (m-M)**:
   - Example: C-Db-Eb
   - Outer interval: m3
   - Character: Sharper than M-m due to lower m2
   - Diatonic availability: Common

4. **Minor-Minor (m-m)**:
   - Example: C-Db-D (or C-C#-D)
   - Outer interval: M2
   - Character: Most dissonant, chromatic
   - Diatonic availability: Chromatic/synthetic scales only

# Musical Context/Application
The consonance-dissonance spectrum of these four types allows composers to control tension within secundal passages. The first three types occur naturally in diatonic contexts, while the minor-minor requires chromatic writing. This creates a natural division between diatonic and chromatic secundal harmony.

The choice of type affects:
- Overall texture (mild to sharp)
- Compatibility with diatonic or chromatic context
- Voice-leading complexity
- Blend with other harmonic materials

# Common Confusions
- The order (M-M, M-m, etc.) is from bottom to top, not top to bottom
- "Consonant" in secundal context is relative; even M-M contains dissonant seconds
- The outer interval is not the defining feature; the internal seconds are
- Minor-minor is specifically chromatic, not just "more dissonant diatonic"
