# Quick Definition
The technique of changing modes while keeping the tonal center constant, allowing shifts between modal colors on a single tonic.

# Formal Definition
When the modes change while the tonal center remains, modal interchanges result. This technique allows a composer to explore different modal colors while maintaining a stable sense of pitch center. The shifting modes create variety in harmonic color without the sense of tonal movement associated with modulation.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Modal interchange process:
1. Establish tonal center in initial mode
2. Shift to different mode on same tonic
3. May continue shifting through multiple modes

Example on D:
- D Dorian (minor 3rd, major 6th)
- shifts to D Phrygian (minor 2nd, 3rd, 6th, 7th)
- shifts to D Lydian (raised 4th, major 3rd)
- All share D as tonic

# Musical Context/Application
Modal interchange provides a way to create harmonic variety while maintaining a stable tonal center. Shifting modes are effective on a stationary key center, though much melodic reference must be made to the tonic to stay within the mode. Melodic circling or chant-like repetition of the tonic and frequent cadences help maintain the center.

# Common Confusions
Students may confuse modal interchange with modal modulation or chromatic alteration. Modal interchange specifically involves complete shifts between modes on a fixed tonic, not just borrowing individual altered tones or moving the tonic.
