# Quick Definition
Composition with "twelve notes related to one another," a primarily contrapuntal practice using a basic order of all twelve pitch classes as a unifying basis, essentially polyphonic in conception with connections to pre-tonal Medieval music.

# Core Definition
Twelve-tone technique or composition with "twelve notes related to one another" is primarily a contrapuntal practice. It is essentially a polyphonic conception with some points in common with the pre-"tonal" music of the Middle Ages. Twelve-tone writing is, therefore, most naturally approached in a treatise on counterpoint. When the controlling principle of scalar tonality is abandoned, chordal root organization of the twelve tones ceases to exist, and form and unity are created by melodic and rhythmic development. A basic order of tones, all twelve or fewer, may be used as a unifying basis for a work, and formal devices evolve from the basic shape.

# Key Properties
1. Primarily contrapuntal, not harmonic
2. Essentially polyphonic conception
3. Connections to pre-tonal Medieval music
4. Basic order (row) of all twelve pitch classes
5. Four forms: Original, Retrograde, Inversion, Retrograde Inversion
6. Each form transposable to twelve pitch levels (48 total forms)
7. Set completion creates structural articulation
8. Form and unity through melodic and rhythmic development

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the basic tone row (all twelve pitch classes in a specific order)
- Track the four basic forms and their transpositions
- Note the contrapuntal/polyphonic texture
- Look for set completions as structural markers
- Observe how formal devices derive from the basic row shape
- Distinguish from free serial technique (which may use fewer than twelve tones)

# Context & Application
Persichetti explicitly positions twelve-tone technique as a contrapuntal practice best addressed in counterpoint study, not as a harmonic system. Serial music of any type must be aurally created by melodic, harmonic, contrapuntal, rhythmic, and formal elements -- not alone by manipulative procedures. Serial technique does not in itself ensure communication or effective sound organization. When automatic writing is avoided, the unifying power of serial practice allows great harmonic variety.

# Common Errors
- Treating twelve-tone technique as a harmonic system rather than a contrapuntal one
- Assuming the technique alone guarantees musical quality
- Relying on manipulative procedures without aural creation

# Common Confusions
- **Contrapuntal, not harmonic**: Persichetti explicitly states this is primarily a contrapuntal practice
- **Technique vs. music**: Serial technique does not in itself ensure communication; it must be aurally created
- **Scope**: Persichetti treats twelve-tone technique briefly because he considers it properly a counterpoint topic
