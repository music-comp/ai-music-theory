# Quick Definition
Composition with "twelve notes related to one another," a primarily contrapuntal practice using a basic order of all twelve pitch classes as a unifying basis.

# Formal Definition
Twelve-tone technique or composition with "twelve notes related to one another" is primarily a contrapuntal practice. It is essentially a polyphonic conception with some points in common with the pre-"tonal" music of the Middle Ages. When the controlling principle of scalar tonality is abandoned, chordal root organization of the twelve tones ceases to exist, and form and unity are created by melodic and rhythmic development. A basic order of tones, all twelve or fewer, may be used as a unifying basis for a work.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Twelve-tone principles:
- Basic order (row/series) of all twelve pitch classes
- Four forms: Original (O), Retrograde (R), Inversion (I), Retrograde Inversion (RI)
- Each form transposable to twelve pitch levels (48 total forms)
- Successive chords encompass entire twelve-tone field

Set completion significance:
- Strong center may result from fulfillment of twelve-tone set
- Structural articulation through set completion
- Harmonic areas defined by set boundaries

Relation to earlier practices:
- Polyphonic conception
- Connections to pre-tonal Medieval practices
- Primarily contrapuntal in nature

# Musical Context/Application
Twelve-tone writing is most naturally approached in a treatise on counterpoint, according to Persichetti. Serial music of any type must be aurally created by melodic, harmonic, contrapuntal, rhythmic, and formal elements - not alone by manipulative procedures. Serial technique does not in itself ensure communication or effective sound organization. When automatic writing is avoided, the unifying power of serial practice allows great harmonic variety.

# Examples
Works in twelve-tone or free serial technique:
- Aaron Copland, Piano Fantasy (Boosey)
- Luigi Dallapiccola, Il Prigioniero (Zerboni)
- Irving Fine, String Quartet No. 1 (C. Fischer)
- Wallingford Riegger, Symphony No. 3 (Associated)
- George Rochberg, String Quartet No. 1 (C. Fischer)
- Roger Sessions, Symphony No. 2 (G. Schirmer)
- Igor Stravinsky, Agon (Boosey)
- Fartein Valen, Sonetto di Michelangelo (Norsk)

# Common Confusions
- Twelve-tone technique is primarily contrapuntal, not harmonic
- Technique alone does not guarantee musical success
- Must be aurally created, not just procedurally generated
