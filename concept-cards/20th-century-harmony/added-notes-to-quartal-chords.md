# Quick Definition
Seconds attached to perfect-fourth chords following a specific order for mild or sharp texture, with the resulting chord heard as quartal or tertian depending on whether the surrounding passage features predominantly quartal or tertian harmony.

# Core Definition
Seconds are added to chords by perfect fourths in a specific order for sonority. For mild texture (M2): below the seventh, above the fourth, below the fourth, above the "root." For sharp texture (m2): above the seventh, below the seventh, above the fourth, below the fourth, above the "root." The lower the second is placed, the less resonant. These chords may be regarded either as triads with added notes or as incomplete seventh/ninth chords with added notes -- context determines identity. In quartal-dominated passages, they sound quartal; in tertian-dominated passages, they sound tertian.

# Key Properties
1. Specific order for mild texture (M2 additions)
2. Specific order for sharp texture (m2 additions)
3. Lower placement = less resonant
4. Contextual identity: quartal or tertian depending on surrounding harmony
5. Same structure functions differently based on harmonic environment
6. Bridges quartal and tertian harmony through ambiguity

# Construction / Recognition
- Follow the specified order for desired texture (mild or sharp)
- Check context to determine quartal vs. tertian identity
- The seventh mentioned is the inverted fourth, not a tertian seventh

# Context & Application
This technique bridges quartal and tertian harmony through deliberate ambiguity. Composers exploit the dual identity for smooth transitions between harmonic styles or for creating intentional harmonic ambiguity.
