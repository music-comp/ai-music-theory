# Quick Definition
Seconds attached to chords built in perfect fourths, following a specific order for achieving either mild or sharp textures, with the resulting sonority determined by whether the context is predominantly quartal or tertian.

# Formal Definition
Seconds are added to chords by perfect fourths in a specific order for sonority. The resulting chords may be regarded either as triads with added notes or as incomplete seventh and ninth chords with added notes, depending on context. The lower the second is placed in the chord, the less resonant the formation.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Order for mild texture (M2 additions):
1. Below the seventh (inverted fourth)
2. Above the fourth
3. Below the fourth
4. Above the "root"

Order for sharp texture (m2 additions):
1. Above the seventh
2. Below the seventh
3. Above the fourth
4. Below the fourth
5. Above the "root"

Contextual interpretation:
- In quartal-dominated passage: heard as added-note chords by fourths
- In tertian-dominated passage: heard as added-note chords by thirds
- The same structure functions differently based on harmonic environment

# Musical Context/Application
This technique bridges quartal and tertian harmony. A chord that is ambiguous in isolation takes on definite character within its musical context. Composers may exploit this ambiguity for smooth transitions between harmonic styles or for creating harmonic ambiguity.

The resulting sonorities blend characteristics of both harmonic systems, making them useful for:
- Transitional passages between quartal and tertian sections
- Creating harmonic ambiguity
- Enriching quartal textures with additional color
- Softening or sharpening the quartal sound as needed

# Common Confusions
- The same pitch collection may function as quartal or tertian depending on context
- "Root" in quartal chords is not equivalent to root in tertian harmony
- The seventh mentioned is the inverted fourth, not a tertian seventh
- Resonance order differs for major and minor second additions
