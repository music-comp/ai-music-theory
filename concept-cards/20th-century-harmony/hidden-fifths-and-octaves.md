# Quick Definition
Perfect fifths or octaves approached by similar motion, which give coupled two-part harmony textural strength and thirdless harmony freedom of movement.

# Core Definition
**Hidden fifths and octaves** (also called direct fifths and octaves) give coupled two-part harmony textural strength, and thirdless harmony a freedom of movement. While traditional counterpoint restricted these progressions to preserve voice independence, twentieth-century practice recognizes their value for creating unified textures and enabling motion in quartal or quintal harmonic contexts (Ch. 9, p. 191).

# Prerequisites
- **Chord connection** -- understanding voice-leading between chords

# Key Properties
1. Two voices approach a perfect fifth or octave by similar motion
2. Give coupled two-part harmony textural strength
3. Give thirdless harmony freedom of movement
4. Voice fusion is desirable in coupled textures (unlike traditional concerns)
5. Melodic rises and dips may be reinforced by successive octaves, fifths, and parallel harmony

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify two voices moving in the same direction and arriving at a perfect fifth or octave
- Note whether the context is coupled two-part writing or thirdless harmony
- Observe whether the hidden intervals contribute to textural strength or linear freedom

# Context & Application
Hidden fifths and octaves are valuable for coupled two-part writing requiring unified texture, quartal and quintal harmony where thirds are absent, reinforcing melodic contours with harmonic support, and creating bold, open sonorities.

# Common Errors
- Avoiding hidden fifths and octaves reflexively based on common-practice training
- Using hidden intervals in contexts where voice independence is desired

# Common Confusions
- **Hidden vs. parallel**: Hidden fifths/octaves approach by similar motion; parallel fifths/octaves maintain the interval through parallel motion
- **Prohibited vs. resource**: Traditional prohibition focused on voice independence; modern practice uses the fusion effect as a resource
- **Context-dependent value**: Hidden intervals are valuable in coupled and thirdless textures, not universally applicable
