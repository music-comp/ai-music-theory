# Quick Definition
The series of upper partials generated by any sounding tone, providing the acoustic foundation for interval relationships, chord construction, and resonance, though with practical limits beyond the sixth partial.

# Core Definition
Any tone generates a series of overtones or partials that reach upward indefinitely, though not all are audible. A tone has both vertical and horizontal implications; its overtones may be used simultaneously in chordal structures or consecutively in melodic lines. The fifth (partial 3) is a lower partial than the third (partial 5) and consequently is more powerful -- an important factor in understanding relationships of tones, chords, and tonalities. Resonant harmony is not formed by seeking higher and higher overtones but by using overtones of overtones.

# Key Properties
1. **Partial series**: 1 (root), 2 (8ve), 3 (5th), 4 (8ve), 5 (M3), 6 (5th)
2. **Beyond partial 6**: tempered scale diverges from overtone pitches (partials 7, 11, 13, 14)
3. **Fifth > third**: partial 3 is lower and more powerful than partial 5
4. **Chord derivations**: triad (1-3-5), 7th chord (1-3-5-7), 9th chord (1-3-5-7-9), quartal (6-8-9)
5. **Overtones of overtones**: the key to resonant harmony (e.g., B is the 5th of E, which is the 5th of C's overtone chain)
6. **Resonance model**: wide spacing low, close spacing high = maximum brilliance
7. **Supporting resonance**: fifth or ninth below bass casts "reflected sheen"

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the fundamental tone
2. Map partials: 8ve, 5th, 8ve, M3, 5th, (m7*), 8ve, M2, M3, (#4*), 5th ...
3. Note that partials 7+ diverge from tempered tuning (marked with *)
4. For resonant voicing: follow the overtone model (wide low, close high)
5. For added resonance: add the fifth or ninth below the bass

# Context & Application
The acoustician's observations are useful to the composer only if blended with artistic intuition. Deduction concerning harmonic implications of partials beyond the 6th is not wholly practicable because the tempered scale does not coincide with partials 7, 11, 13, and 14. The overtone series sets a norm for brilliance: for maximum brilliance, let the lower tones of the chord be accompanied by their own overtones. Supporting resonance is occasionally applied through basses plucking the fifth or ninth below the bass line.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Trying to derive all chord structures directly from the overtone series. **Correction**: Beyond the 6th partial, the tempered scale diverges; the acoustician's observations must be blended with artistic intuition.
- **Error**: Seeking resonance by piling higher overtones. **Correction**: Resonant harmony uses overtones of overtones, not progressively higher partials.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the overtone series prescribes correct harmony. **Clarification**: It provides general facts about structure and resonance, but composition requires artistic intuition beyond acoustic deduction.
