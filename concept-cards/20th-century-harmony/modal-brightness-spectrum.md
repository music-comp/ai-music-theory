# Quick Definition
An arrangement of the seven diatonic modes from "darkest" to "brightest" based on the number of flats or sharps applied to a scale on a particular tone.

# Formal Definition
As working materials for composition, the modes may be arranged according to their tension relationships. The greatest number of flats that can be applied to a modal scale on a particular tone produces the "darkest" mode (locrian). Subtracting flats and then adding sharps in diatonic signature order produces an arrangement from darkest to brightest, with dorian as the middle point setting the norm.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Modal brightness order on any tonic (darkest to brightest):
1. Locrian (most flats): diminished 5th, minor 2nd, 3rd, 6th, 7th
2. Phrygian: minor 2nd, 3rd, 6th, 7th
3. Aeolian: minor 3rd, 6th, 7th
4. Dorian (center/norm): minor 3rd, 7th; major 6th
5. Mixolydian: minor 7th; major 3rd, 6th
6. Ionian: all major (no alterations from major scale)
7. Lydian (brightest): raised 4th

# Musical Context/Application
Within this related order, a flexible set of modes is at the composer's disposal, and definite control of these scale formations with their inherent qualities is possible. A melody with a crescendo in register, dynamics, rhythm, and modal materials can move systematically through the brightness spectrum for expressive effect.

# Common Confusions
Students may think of modes as having fixed "moods" without understanding the systematic relationship between them. The brightness spectrum provides a compositional tool for controlled movement through modal colors, independent of traditional major/minor associations.
