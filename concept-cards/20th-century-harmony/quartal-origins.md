# Quick Definition
The historical sources of quartal harmony in both triadic ornamentation (suspensions, appoggiaturas) and medieval polyphonic techniques (organum, early counterpoint).

# Formal Definition
Quartal materials stem from ornamentation of the triad and from the techniques of medieval polyphony. These two sources represent the historical pathways by which the fourth, traditionally treated as a dissonance against the bass in common-practice harmony, emerged as a constructive interval for chord building in twentieth-century practice.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
**From Triadic Ornamentation:**
- Suspension: 4-3 resolution creates temporary fourth
- Appoggiatura: approach note a fourth above chord tone
- Six-four chord: prominent fourth in second inversion
- When these fourths fail to resolve, quartal structures emerge

**From Medieval Polyphony:**
- Organum: parallel fourths and fifths
- Early counterpoint: fourth treated as consonance
- Pre-tonal practice: no prohibition against fourth with bass

**Historical Trajectory:**
Medieval (fourth = consonance) -> Common Practice (fourth = dissonance with bass) -> 20th Century (fourth = constructive interval)

# Musical Context/Application
Understanding quartal origins helps composers:
1. Create quartal textures through unresolved suspensions
2. Connect to medieval modal practices through quartal harmony
3. Recognize the historical rehabilitation of the fourth

The six-four chord with its characteristic interval of a fourth is often heard in the company of simple chords by fourths, showing the connection between triadic ornamentation and quartal harmony.

# Common Confusions
Students may think quartal harmony is entirely a twentieth-century invention. While systematic quartal chord construction is modern, the intervallic materials have deep historical roots in both ornamental practice and early polyphony.
