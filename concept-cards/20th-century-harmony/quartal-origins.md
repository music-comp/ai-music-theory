# Quick Definition
The two historical sources of quartal harmony: triadic ornamentation (suspensions, appoggiaturas, six-four chords) where fourths fail to resolve, and medieval polyphonic techniques (organum, early counterpoint) where the fourth was treated as consonance.

# Core Definition
Quartal materials stem from ornamentation of the triad and from the techniques of medieval polyphony. From triadic ornamentation: suspensions create temporary fourths (4-3 resolution), appoggiaturas approach from a fourth above, and the six-four chord features a prominent fourth. When these fourths fail to resolve, quartal structures emerge. From medieval polyphony: organum used parallel fourths and fifths, and early counterpoint treated the fourth as consonance. The historical trajectory runs from medieval consonance through common-practice dissonance back to twentieth-century constructive interval.

# Prerequisites
- **Quartal harmony**: The system whose origins this describes

# Key Properties
1. Two sources: triadic ornamentation + medieval polyphony
2. Suspension 4-3: temporary fourth that can become permanent
3. Six-four chord: prominent fourth connecting triadic and quartal textures
4. Organum: parallel fourths treated as consonance
5. Historical arc: medieval consonance -> common-practice dissonance -> 20th-century construction
6. The six-four chord is heard naturally alongside quartal chords

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify quartal textures that emerge from unresolved suspensions
- Recognize the six-four chord as a bridge between triadic and quartal harmony
- Medieval-style parallel fourths as a quartal texture source

# Context & Application
Understanding quartal origins helps composers create quartal textures through unresolved suspensions, connect to medieval modal practices, and recognize the historical rehabilitation of the fourth. The six-four chord's prominent fourth places it naturally in the company of quartal chords.
