# Quick Definition

A specific kind of static harmony where an entire scale forms the members of an implied secundal, static chord, with tones manipulated as basic chordal material without creating harmonic motion outside the underlying scale.

# Formal Definition

**Pandiatonic writing** is a specific kind of static harmony in which an entire scale is used to form the members of an implied secundal, static chord. The vertical structures are combinations of any number of tones from the prevailing scale, placed in variable spacings. The horizontal chord succession has no tonal direction; scale tones are manipulated as basic chordal material without creating harmonic motion outside the underlying static and unaltered scale.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Pandiatonic characteristics:
- **No characteristic harmonic functions**: Traditional chord functions suspended
- **Active counterpoint**: Rhythmically active voice leading
- **Erratic chord spacing**: Variable vertical distribution
- **No tonal direction**: Horizontal succession without goal-directedness

Voice-leading considerations:
- Fifths and tenths from bass more fluent than thick thirds or stubborn fourths
- Fourths tend to predominate in upper chord portion
- Seconds and sevenths harness texture for cadential arrivals
- Doubling seldom employed, even in five- or six-part harmony

Scale considerations:
- One mode seldom used for entire section
- Modes without flats/sharps risk "white-key fever"
- Any scale may serve as pandiatonic basis
- All intervallic characteristics of scale projected at once

# Musical Context/Application

In three-part pandiatonic harmony:
- Each succeeding chord often contains three fresh notes
- All seven scale tones brought to aural consciousness
- Ornamental tones in one chord become members of next chord

In seven-part harmony:
- All seven scale steps constantly sounded
- Maximum scale saturation

Pandiatonic writing serves:
- Creating harmonic stasis with active counterpoint
- Projecting scale characteristics without tonal function
- Twentieth-century alternative to functional harmony
- Neoclassical and American nationalist styles

# Common Confusions

- **Pandiatonic vs. tonal**: Pandiatonic writing uses diatonic scales without tonal function; tonal music uses scales with functional hierarchy.
- **White-key fever**: Overuse of C major/A minor (no accidentals) creates recognizable but potentially tiresome sound.
- **Static vs. inactive**: Pandiatonic writing lacks harmonic motion but has active counterpoint and rhythm.
