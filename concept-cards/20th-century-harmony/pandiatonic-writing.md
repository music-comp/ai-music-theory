# Quick Definition
A specific kind of static harmony in which an entire scale is used to form the members of an implied secundal, static chord, with tones manipulated as basic chordal material without creating harmonic motion outside the underlying unaltered scale.

# Core Definition
**Pandiatonic writing** is a specific kind of static harmony in which an entire scale is used to form the members of an implied secundal, static chord. The vertical structures are combinations of any number of tones from the prevailing scale, placed in variable spacings. The horizontal chord succession has no tonal direction; scale tones are manipulated as basic chordal material without creating harmonic motion outside the underlying static and unaltered scale. The harmony has no characteristic functions; the counterpoint is rhythmically active, and the chord spacing erratic. Melodic, contrapuntal, and vertical combinations may be underpinned by persistent intervals in the lower voices (Ch. 10, pp. 222-225).

# Prerequisites
- **Static harmony** -- understanding single-chord stasis (pandiatonicism expands this)

# Key Properties
1. Entire scale = implied secundal, static chord
2. No characteristic harmonic functions
3. Rhythmically active counterpoint
4. Erratic chord spacing
5. No tonal direction in horizontal succession
6. Fifths and tenths from bass more fluent than thick thirds or stubborn fourths
7. Fourths tend to predominate in upper chord portion
8. Seconds and sevenths harness texture for cadential arrivals
9. Doubling seldom employed, even in five- or six-part harmony
10. One mode seldom used for entire section; "white-key fever" contracted easily
11. Any scale (including synthetic) may serve as pandiatonic basis
12. In three-part harmony, each chord often contains three fresh notes, bringing all scale tones to aural consciousness

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify passages using only tones from one scale without traditional harmonic function
- Check for erratic spacing and absence of tonal direction
- Note active counterpoint over static harmonic content
- Verify absence of doubling in five+ part writing
- Observe whether all seven (or other) scale tones are brought to consciousness

# Context & Application
In three-part pandiatonic harmony, each succeeding chord often contains three fresh notes, bringing all seven scale tones to aural consciousness. In seven-part harmony, all seven scale steps are constantly sounded. Any scale may be used as a basis for pandiatonicism -- all intervallic characteristics of any scale, synthetic or otherwise, can be projected pandiatonically at once. One mode seldom suffices for an entire pandiatonic section, especially modes without flats or sharps, because "white-key fever" is contracted easily.

# Common Errors
- Using a single mode (especially white-key modes) for an entire pandiatonic section
- Employing traditional harmonic functions within pandiatonic texture
- Doubling tones in five- or six-part pandiatonic writing
- Using thick thirds or stubborn fourths in the bass region

# Common Confusions
- **Pandiatonic vs. tonal**: Pandiatonic writing uses diatonic scales without tonal function; tonal music uses scales with functional hierarchy
- **White-key fever**: Overuse of C major/A minor (no accidentals) creates a recognizable but tiresome effect
- **Static vs. inactive**: Pandiatonic writing lacks harmonic motion but has active counterpoint and rhythm
- **Any scale**: Not limited to diatonic modes; synthetic scales can be treated pandiatonically
