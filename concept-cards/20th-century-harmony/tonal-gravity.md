# Quick Definition
The attractive force pulling musical elements toward a tonal center, existing on a spectrum from strong gravitational pull to complete keylessness, and manipulable through various compositional techniques.

# Core Definition
There are many degrees of key-center gravity or of keylessness in the general area of tonality or atonality. At one extreme of the concept of key is tonality, the other extreme is atonality, and the point at which one ends and the other begins is indefinite. The gravity of a tonal center is decreased by moving a new voice through notes that give an important chord new roots. Key feeling may be gradually loosened or tightened. Music may hold its tonality loosely, elements of atonality being inherent from the onset.

# Prerequisites
- **Tonality**: The organizational principle whose strength is measured
- **Tonal center**: The focal point toward which gravity pulls

# Key Properties
1. Exists on a continuous spectrum, not as binary present/absent
2. Can be gradually loosened or tightened within a passage
3. Decreased by voice movement that gives chords new root interpretations
4. Increased by tritone-embracing scale steps, pillar reinforcement, pedal points, cadential formulas
5. Partial atonality is useful in introductions and transitions
6. The tonality/atonality boundary is indefinite

# Construction / Recognition
- Assess the strength of key pull in a passage on a continuum
- Note techniques that decrease gravity: voice movement providing new roots, chromatic saturation, equidistant harmony
- Note techniques that increase gravity: pillar reinforcement, pedal points, cadential motion, tritone emphasis
- Track gradual loosening or tightening of key feeling across a passage
- Identify partial atonality in introductions and transitions

# Context & Application
Melodic lines in a tonal framework can become so free that the implied harmony becomes evasively dissonant and a point reached where key feeling is lost. Partial atonality is useful in vague introductions and transition passages, and when preparing for a returning tonal thematic idea. The composer can manipulate tonal gravity throughout a work, creating passages of firm tonality, ambiguous wandering, or complete keylessness. This manipulation of gravity is itself a compositional resource.

# Relationships

## Enables
- Shifting tonality (when gravity is loosened)

## Related
- Tonal pillars (increase gravitational pull)
- Key center establishment (creates gravity)

## Contrasts With
- Atonality (absence of gravitational pull)

# Common Errors
- Treating tonal gravity as binary (present vs. absent)
- Assuming loosened gravity means compositional failure
- Ignoring the structural role of gravity fluctuations

# Common Confusions
- **Spectrum, not binary**: Gravity ranges from firm to absent with infinite gradations
- **Loosened gravity vs. modulation**: Loosened gravity weakens center without establishing a new one; modulation shifts to a new center
- **Partial atonality**: Useful compositional tool, not an error -- serves introductions, transitions, and preparations for tonal return
