# Quick Definition
The complete range of harmonic, melodic, rhythmic, and timbral materials available to the twentieth-century composer, encompassing tertian, quartal, secundal, polychordal, compound, serial, tonal, and atonal resources organized through root progressions, intervallic tensions, or serial devices.

# Core Definition
A synthesis of harmonic potentialities includes chord materials governed by root progressions, intervallic texture tensions, and serial devices. At any point in the formal shape a melodic group of notes may control harmonic texture. The techniques and materials of atonal music are often amalgamated with those of many types of tonal idioms; the harmonic stock includes endless possible harmonic combinations. Any tone can succeed any other tone, any tone can sound simultaneously with any other tone or tones, and any group of tones can be followed by any other group of tones, just as any degree of tension or nuance can occur in any medium under any kind of stress or duration.

# Key Properties
1. Three organizational principles: root progressions, intervallic tension, serial devices
2. Melodic groups may control harmonic texture at any point
3. Tonal and atonal resources may be amalgamated
4. Endless possible harmonic combinations
5. Any succession, simultaneity, or juxtaposition is possible
6. Any tension level, medium, and duration applicable
7. Success depends on context, form, and compositional skill

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify which organizational principle governs a passage (root progression, intervallic tension, serial)
- Note where melodic groups take control of harmonic texture
- Observe amalgamation of tonal and atonal resources
- Assess whether the full resource palette is being exploited
- Evaluate success by contextual and formal conditions

# Context & Application
The twentieth-century composer has access to the complete range of harmonic resources developed throughout music history, plus contemporary innovations. The choice of resources is a creative decision, not a technical limitation. Technical freedom requires greater compositional skill, not less. All historical resources remain available alongside contemporary innovations. The challenge is coherent integration, not resource limitation.

# Common Errors
- Self-limiting to a single resource category
- Assuming technical freedom eliminates the need for skill
- Treating all resources as equivalent without contextual judgment

# Common Confusions
- **Resources vs. rules**: Resources are available tools; their use is governed by context and skill, not prescriptive rules
- **Freedom vs. arbitrariness**: The availability of all resources demands more judgment, not less
- **Amalgamation**: Tonal and atonal resources are not mutually exclusive; they can coexist within a single work
