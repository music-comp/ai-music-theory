# Quick Definition
The integration of diverse harmonic techniques, textures, and organizational principles into a unified compositional approach encompassing both tonal and atonal resources.

# Formal Definition
A synthesis of harmonic potentialities includes chord materials governed by root progressions, intervallic texture tensions, and serial devices. At any point in the formal shape a melodic group of notes may control harmonic texture. The techniques and materials of atonal music are often amalgamated with those of many types of tonal idioms; the harmonic stock includes endless possible harmonic combinations. Contemporary harmonic writing is often a composite process involving the amalgamation of divergent conceptions of tonal formations.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Synthesis components:
- **Root progression harmony**: Traditional chord function
- **Intervallic tension harmony**: Texture-based organization
- **Serial devices**: Row-based organization
- **Melodic control**: Melodic groups governing texture
- **Tonal/atonal amalgamation**: Combined resources

Synthesis principle:
- Any tone can succeed any other tone
- Any tone can sound simultaneously with any other tone or tones
- Any group of tones can be followed by any other group
- Any degree of tension or nuance can occur in any medium
- Successful projection depends on contextual and formal conditions

# Musical Context/Application
The synthesis of harmonic potentialities represents the culmination of Persichetti's exploration of twentieth-century resources. Acceptance of one procedure does not necessitate exclusion of others. A fugue may be written over a cantus firmus, a hymn placed under a tone row, and quartal harmony blended with tertian. The success of any harmonic choice depends upon the skill and soul of the composer.

# Examples

- Synthesis is not eclecticism; it requires coherent integration
- All resources are available; none are mandatory
- Success depends on skill and sensitivity, not technique alone
