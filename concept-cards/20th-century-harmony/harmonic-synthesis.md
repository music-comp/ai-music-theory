# Quick Definition
The integration of all harmonic resources -- tertian, quartal, secundal, polychordal, compound, serial, tonal, and atonal -- into a unified compositional approach where acceptance of one procedure does not mean exclusion of others.

# Core Definition
Contemporary harmonic writing is often a composite process which may involve varying placement of the norm of dissonance, choice of a single harmonic idiom or the coalition of one with another, fusion of tonalities, singleness of sound organization or the juxtaposition of tonal and atonal aspects. The amalgamation of divergent conceptions of tonal formations is part of our harmonic language. Acceptance of one procedure does not necessarily mean the exclusion of others. A fugue may be written over a cantus firmus, a hymn placed under a tone row, and quartal harmony blended with tertian.

A synthesis of harmonic potentialities includes chord materials governed by root progressions, intervallic texture tensions, and serial devices. At any point in the formal shape a melodic group of notes may control harmonic texture. Any tone can succeed any other tone, any tone can sound simultaneously with any other tone or tones, and any group of tones can be followed by any other group of tones. Successful projection depends upon the contextual and formal conditions that prevail, and upon the skill and soul of the composer.

# Key Properties
1. All harmonic resources available simultaneously
2. Acceptance of one procedure does not exclude others
3. Three organizational principles: root progressions, intervallic tension, serial devices
4. Melodic groups may control harmonic texture at any point
5. Any tone can succeed, sound with, or precede any other
6. Success depends on context, form, skill, and creative sensitivity
7. Vertical combination produces polychordal/compound formations
8. Successive combination requires transitional technique

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify which harmonic resources are being combined in a passage
- Trace transitions between different harmonic textures
- Note whether combinations are vertical (simultaneous) or successive (sequential)
- Assess the coherence of the total harmonic result
- Look for the characteristic interval of one texture appearing within another

# Context & Application
This is the capstone concept of Persichetti's harmonic system. The entire book builds toward the understanding that all harmonic resources are available to the twentieth-century composer. The challenge is not which resources to use but how to integrate them coherently. The vertical combination of textures produces polychordal and compound formations; successive combinations require skillful transitional handling. The amalgamation of divergent harmonic conceptions is part of the contemporary harmonic language.

# Common Errors
- Treating synthesis as eclecticism (random mixing of styles)
- Assuming one harmonic system must be chosen exclusively
- Ignoring transitional requirements between textures

# Common Confusions
- **Synthesis vs. eclecticism**: Synthesis requires coherent integration; eclecticism is random combination
- **Freedom vs. arbitrariness**: Complete resource availability requires greater skill, not less
- **Capstone concept**: This chapter synthesizes all twelve preceding chapters, not just adjacent harmonic types
