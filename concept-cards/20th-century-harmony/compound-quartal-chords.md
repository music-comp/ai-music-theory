# Quick Definition
Quartal chords combined with thirds, where a major or minor third is added above or below a three-note quartal structure, creating fresh colors that function well in both quartal and tertian contexts.

# Formal Definition
Compound construction (thirds and fourths) brings fresh color to quartal harmony. A third may be added above or below a three-note chord by fourths. If the added third is major the chord sounds consonant; if the third is minor the chord sounds less consonant. A third above and below the quartal structure may appear with it simultaneously as a five-note chord; this formation is lush in sound and functions particularly well in progressions containing tertian or polychordal structures.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
**Construction Types:**
1. **Third below quartal chord:**
   - Example: A-C-F-Bb (m3 below + P4 + P4)
   - Or: B-C-F-Bb (M3 below + P4 + P4)

2. **Third above quartal chord:**
   - Example: C-F-Bb-D (P4 + P4 + M3)
   - Or: C-F-Bb-Db (P4 + P4 + m3)

3. **Thirds both above and below (5-note):**
   - Example: A-C-F-Bb-D (m3 + P4 + P4 + M3)
   - Lush sound, bridging tertian and quartal

**Consonance Gradient:**
- Major third added: more consonant
- Minor third added: less consonant

**Cadential Function:**
The compound three-note quartal chord with major third added is effective as a cadential tonic.

# Musical Context/Application
Compound quartal chords serve as bridges between pure quartal and tertian harmony. Their mixed intervallic content allows them to function in either context:
1. As passing chords between quartal structures
2. As cadential tonics combining quartal and tertian character
3. In progressions containing polychordal structures

The five-note form (thirds above and below) is particularly useful for lush, colorful sonorities that don't commit fully to either harmonic system.

# Common Confusions
Students may confuse compound quartal chords with added-note tertian chords. The distinction lies in the structural emphasis: compound quartal chords begin with the quartal structure as primary, with thirds added as extensions. Added-note chords begin with tertian structures.
