# Quick Definition
Quartal chords combined with thirds (compound construction), where a major or minor third added above or below a three-note quartal structure creates fresh colors: major third = consonant, minor third = less consonant; with a five-note form (thirds above and below) that is lush in sound.

# Core Definition
Compound construction (thirds and fourths) brings fresh color to quartal harmony. A third may be added above or below a three-note chord by fourths. If the added third is major, the chord sounds consonant; if minor, less consonant. The compound chord with major third is effective as a cadential tonic. As passing chords, both types are equally useful. A third above and below simultaneously creates a five-note chord that is lush in sound and functions well in progressions containing tertian or polychordal structures.

# Prerequisites
- **Three-note quartal chords**: The base formations receiving the added third

# Key Properties
1. Third added above or below three-note quartal chord
2. Major third addition = consonant
3. Minor third addition = less consonant
4. Major third compound = effective cadential tonic
5. Both types useful as passing chords
6. Five-note form (third above + below) = lush, bridging tertian and quartal
7. Five-note form functions in progressions with tertian or polychordal structures

# Construction / Recognition
- Base: three-note quartal chord (e.g., C-F-Bb)
- Add major third below: A-C-F-Bb or above: C-F-Bb-D
- Add minor third below: Ab-C-F-Bb or above: C-F-Bb-Db
- Five-note: A-C-F-Bb-D (m3 + P4 + P4 + M3)

# Context & Application
Compound quartal chords serve as bridges between quartal and tertian harmony. The five-note form with thirds both above and below is particularly useful for lush, colorful sonorities in mixed-idiom passages. The compound chord with major third added functions effectively as cadential tonic.
