# Quick Definition
Seconds attached to augmented-fourth chords, which always produce sharp textures regardless of whether added seconds are major or minor, because the inherent major seventh interval in the basic formation guarantees sharpness; these blend well with augmented fourth chords without added notes.

# Core Definition
The chromatic-second addition to chords by perfect fourths creates a sharp texture which blends well with the texture of augmented fourth chords without added notes. Whether the seconds fastened to augmented-fourth chord members are major or minor, the chord will always be of sharp texture because of the major seventh interval in the basic formation. Augmented fourth chords with and without added notes sound well side by side because they both belong to the category of sharp textures.

# Prerequisites
- **Added notes to quartal chords**: The broader technique of adding seconds to quartal formations

# Key Properties
1. M2 additions = sharp texture (due to inherent M7)
2. m2 additions = sharp texture (due to inherent M7)
3. No mild texture variant possible
4. The major seventh in the base formation guarantees sharpness
5. With and without added notes: both sharp, blend well in progression
6. Textural consistency within sharpness allows varying density

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify augmented fourth in the quartal chord (e.g., C-F#-B: M7 outer interval)
- Any second addition maintains sharp quality
- Combine freely with plain augmented fourth chords

# Context & Application
This technique is useful for passages requiring sustained tension, building climaxes, creating angular modernist textures, and maintaining textural consistency across varying density. The inability to produce mild textures is a feature, not a limitation.
