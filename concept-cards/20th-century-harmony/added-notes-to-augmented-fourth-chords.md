# Quick Definition
Seconds attached to chords containing augmented fourths (tritones), which always produce sharp textures regardless of whether the added seconds are major or minor, due to the inherent major seventh interval in the basic formation.

# Formal Definition
The chromatic-second addition to chords by perfect fourths creates a sharp texture which blends well with the texture of augmented fourth chords without added notes. Whether the seconds fastened to augmented-fourth chord members are major or minor, the chord will always be of sharp texture because of the major seventh interval in the basic formation.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Structure of augmented fourth chords:
- Built from stacked fourths including at least one tritone (A4/d5)
- Contains inherent major seventh interval
- Example: C-F#-B (two fourths, one augmented, creating M7 outer interval)

Added note behavior:
- M2 additions: sharp texture (due to M7 in basic structure)
- m2 additions: sharp texture (due to M7 in basic structure)
- No mild texture variant possible with augmented fourth chords

Blending principle:
- Augmented fourth chords (with or without added notes) share sharp texture
- They combine well in progression
- Mixed use creates textural consistency within sharpness

# Musical Context/Application
Augmented fourth chords with and without added notes sound well side by side because they both belong to the category of sharp textures. This consistency allows composers to vary harmonic density and color while maintaining a unified sharp textural quality.

The technique is particularly useful in:
- Passages requiring sustained tension
- Building climactic sections
- Creating angular, modernist textures
- Maintaining textural consistency across varying density

# Common Confusions
- Unlike perfect fourth chords, augmented fourth chords cannot produce mild textures with added notes
- The sharp quality comes from the inherent M7, not from the added note
- The tritone (A4) is distinct from the augmented sixth in function and context
- These chords are part of quartal harmony despite containing the traditionally "dissonant" tritone
