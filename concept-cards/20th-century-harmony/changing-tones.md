# Quick Definition
Nonharmonic tones approached by step and left by skip in the opposite direction, also known as escape tones or echappees.

# Formal Definition
Changing tones constitute the second category of nonharmonic tones in Persichetti's classification. They are approached conjunctly (by step) and left by a skip in the opposite direction. This creates a characteristic "escape" from the expected stepwise resolution, hence the alternate name "escape tone." The changing tone moves stepwise away from a chord tone, then leaps in the opposite direction.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Changing tone characteristics:
- **Approach**: By step from chord tone
- **Departure**: By leap in opposite direction
- **Direction change**: Always reverses direction between approach and departure
- **Leap size**: Variable, but typically a third or more

The pattern creates an angular melodic contour: step in one direction, leap in the other. This produces a more dramatic ornamental effect than passing or auxiliary tones.

# Musical Context/Application
Changing tones add angular interest to melodic lines and create momentary dissonance that resolves unexpectedly. In twentieth-century practice, the leap following the step may land on any pitch, not necessarily a chord tone of the prevailing harmony. This allows changing tones to serve as pivot points for unexpected melodic directions or to emphasize particular registral areas.

# Common Confusions
- Changing tones and appoggiaturas are opposites: changing tones step then leap; appoggiaturas leap then step
- The leap must be in the opposite direction from the step
- Not to be confused with "changing notes" or "cambiata" patterns which involve multiple ornamental tones
