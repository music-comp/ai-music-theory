# Quick Definition
Nonharmonic tones approached by step and left by skip in the opposite direction, creating an angular "escape" from expected stepwise resolution.

# Core Definition
**Changing tones** are approached conjunctly (by step) and left by a skip in the opposite direction. This creates a characteristic angular melodic contour: step in one direction, leap in the other. The technique is the inverse of the appoggiatura (which leaps to then steps from) (Ch. 11, pp. 229-230).

# Prerequisites
- **Nonharmonic tones** -- understanding the classification system

# Key Properties
1. Approach: by step from chord tone
2. Departure: by leap in opposite direction
3. Direction always reverses between approach and departure
4. Creates angular melodic contour
5. Inverse of appoggiatura pattern

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify a tone approached by step that is left by a leap in the opposite direction
- Verify the direction reversal between approach and departure

# Context & Application
Changing tones add angular interest to melodic lines and create momentary dissonance that resolves unexpectedly. In twentieth-century practice, the leap may land on any pitch, not necessarily a chord tone.

# Common Errors
- Confusing changing tones with appoggiaturas (they are inverses)

# Common Confusions
- Changing tones and appoggiaturas are opposites: changing tones step then leap; appoggiaturas leap then step
- Not to be confused with "changing notes" or "cambiata" patterns involving multiple ornamental tones
