# Quick Definition
Major seconds and minor sevenths, which produce a moderately tense sound quality positioned between consonance and sharp dissonance.

# Formal Definition
Mild dissonances are the major second and minor seventh intervals, characterized by their moderate tension derived from intermediate partials in the overtone series. These intervals provide harmonic color without the intensity of sharp dissonances, making them versatile tools in twentieth-century composition for creating graduated tension levels.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
- Major second: 9:8 frequency ratio (partial 9)
- Minor seventh: 16:9 frequency ratio
- Position between soft consonances and sharp dissonances on the tension spectrum
- When spaced more than an octave apart, mild dissonances become less biting yet more brilliant

# Musical Context/Application
Mild dissonances occupy a middle ground in the consonance-dissonance spectrum, allowing composers to create subtle tension gradations. They are frequently used in twentieth-century chord construction (ninths, elevenths) without overwhelming other harmonic colors. Like sharp dissonances, their quality changes when expanded beyond the octave, becoming more brilliant while losing some of their bite.

# Common Confusions
Students may not distinguish between mild and sharp dissonances when analyzing twentieth-century music. The distinction is important for understanding graduated tension patterns and harmonic direction in contemporary composition.
