# Quick Definition

A compound chord formed by fusing two different triads in open position so that two notes are shared in middle voices, resulting in a four-note formation with distinct polychordal flavor. Only six primary fused chords exist.

# Core Definition

"It is possible to fuse two triads so that a single chord of mixed intervals results. This species of compound harmony has a distinct polychordal flavor. Chords are formed by fusing two different kinds of triads so that two of the six notes are duplications, leaving a four-note formation. The duplicated notes are in the middle voices and are common to both triads. The chord is labeled from the bottom triad upwards" (Persichetti, p. 170). "There are only six compound chords of this variety; other fused combinations of triads form seventh or triadic harmony. These six fused chords are completely interchangeable and are free to move to and from each other in any order" (p. 170).

# Key Properties

1. Two different triads fused in open position
2. Two of six notes are duplications (common tones in middle voices)
3. Result: four-note chord with mixed intervals
4. Distinct polychordal flavor while functioning as single compound sonority
5. Only six primary fused chord varieties exist
6. All six completely interchangeable; free to move in any order
7. Labeled from bottom triad upward
8. Secondary chords: seventh, ninth, eleventh chords with sharp dissonant interval as blending agent
9. Flows freely in four-part writing

# Construction / Recognition

## To Construct
1. Select two triads of different quality
2. Arrange in open position
3. Identify the two common tones (middle voice duplications)
4. The four remaining unique pitches form the fused chord
5. Label from bottom triad upward

## To Recognize
1. Identify a four-note compound chord with polychordal flavor
2. Check whether it can be derived from two fused triads with shared middle-voice notes
3. Verify the duplications occur in middle voices

# Context & Application

Fused harmony flows freely in four-part writing. The six primary chords are completely interchangeable, forming a self-contained harmonic vocabulary. Their secondary chords (seventh, ninth, eleventh formations with blending sharp dissonant intervals) are numerous and provide expanded harmonic resources. This hybrid between added-note chords and polychords creates a distinctive harmonic color.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming more than six primary fused chords exist
  **Correction**: Only six produce compound harmony; other triad combinations form seventh or triadic structures
- **Error**: Labeling from top triad downward
  **Correction**: Convention labels from bottom triad upward

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Fused chords are polychords
  **Clarification**: They have polychordal flavor but function as single compound sonorities; the triads merge rather than maintaining separation
- **Confusion**: All triad combinations produce fused chords
  **Clarification**: Only six combinations produce compound formations; others reduce to seventh chords or standard triads
