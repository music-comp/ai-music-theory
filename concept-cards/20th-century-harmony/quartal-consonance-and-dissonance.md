# Quick Definition
In quartal harmony, consonance and dissonance are determined by the presence or absence of the tritone (augmented fourth), with perfect-fourth-only structures being consonant and tritone-containing structures being dissonant.

# Formal Definition
The perfect-perfect chord is seldom used as a dissonant structure. The absence of a sharp interval, the equality of the perfect fourths, and the mildness of the minor seventh create a texture that is consonant in a quartal context. Chords built of superimposed perfect fourths are consonant to and include the five-note chord. The six-note chord results in a categorical change in tension because of the sharp dissonant interval (tritone that appears when six perfect fourths are stacked).

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
**Consonant Group (perfect fourths only):**
- 3-note: P4 + P4 (no tritone)
- 4-note: P4 + P4 + P4 (no tritone)
- 5-note: P4 + P4 + P4 + P4 (no tritone)

**Dissonant Group (contains tritone):**
- 3-note: P4 + A4 or A4 + P4
- 4-note: contains at least one A4
- 6+ note perfect fourth stacks (tritone emerges between notes)

**Tritone Emergence:**
Six perfect fourths stacked = 30 semitones
The interval between the first and sixth notes = 30 - 24 = 6 semitones = tritone

**Resolution Behavior:**
In chords containing the augmented fourth, the upper note of the tritone resolves best to the nearest note of the prevailing melodic or scale formation. If neighboring scale tones are equidistant, either direction may be taken.

# Musical Context/Application
Understanding quartal consonance/dissonance guides compositional choices:
1. Consonant quartal chords can sustain without resolution pressure
2. Dissonant quartal chords benefit from tritone resolution
3. Exploiting the relationship between consonant and dissonant groups creates harmonic tension and release

A pedal point lessens any dissonant tone's need for resolution, allowing dissonant quartal chords to function more freely.

# Common Confusions
Students trained in tertian harmony may bring inappropriate consonance/dissonance expectations to quartal contexts. The minor seventh, dissonant in tertian harmony, is consonant in quartal harmony. The augmented fourth remains the primary dissonance in both systems.
