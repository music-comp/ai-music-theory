# Quick Definition
In quartal harmony, consonance and dissonance are determined by the presence or absence of the tritone: perfect-fourth-only structures are consonant through five notes; the six-note chord introduces a categorical change through the emergent tritone.

# Core Definition
The perfect-perfect chord is seldom used as a dissonant structure. The absence of a sharp interval, the equality of the perfect fourths, and the mildness of the minor seventh create a texture that is consonant in a quartal context. Chords built of superimposed perfect fourths are consonant to and include the five-note chord. The six-note chord results in a categorical change in tension because of the tritone between the first and sixth notes (30 semitones reduced to 6 = tritone). In chords containing the augmented fourth, the upper note resolves to the nearest scale tone. A pedal point lessens resolution needs.

# Prerequisites
- **Quartal harmony**: The system within which consonance/dissonance operates

# Key Properties
1. Consonant group: 3-, 4-, 5-note perfect-fourth stacks (no tritone)
2. Dissonant group: any chord containing augmented fourth
3. Six perfect fourths stacked = tritone emergence (categorical change)
4. Minor seventh = consonant in quartal context (dissonant in tertian)
5. Tritone upper note resolves to nearest scale tone
6. If equidistant neighbors: either direction acceptable
7. Pedal point lessens resolution requirements
8. Exploiting consonant/dissonant group relationships creates tension-release

# Construction / Recognition
- No tritone = consonant quartal chord
- Tritone present = dissonant quartal chord
- Six-note perfect-fourth stack: notes 1 and 6 form tritone
- Resolution: upper tritone note to nearest scale degree

# Context & Application
Understanding quartal consonance/dissonance guides compositional choices: consonant quartal chords sustain without resolution pressure; dissonant ones benefit from tritone resolution. The relationship between consonant and dissonant groups can be exploited for tension and release, analogous to consonance/dissonance management in tertian harmony but with different defining intervals.
