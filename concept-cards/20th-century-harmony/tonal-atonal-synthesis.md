# Quick Definition
The amalgamation of tonal and atonal resources within a single compositional framework, where techniques and materials from atonal music are integrated with those of tonal idioms, and serial devices coexist with root progressions and intervallic tensions.

# Core Definition
Some directions in serial composition point away from the specialized craft of strict "atonal" writing, where all elements are generated from a single germ cell, toward a flexible creative process that includes the vast musical resources of composition, both tonal and atonal. The techniques and materials of atonal music are often amalgamated with those of many types of tonal idioms; the harmonic stock includes endless possible harmonic combinations. A synthesis of harmonic potentialities includes chord materials governed by root progressions, intervallic texture tensions, and serial devices.

Contemporary harmonic writing may involve the juxtaposition of tonal and atonal aspects. The amalgamation of divergent conceptions of tonal formations is part of our harmonic language.

# Prerequisites
- **Harmonic synthesis**: The overarching integrative principle
- **Tonality**: One pole of the synthesis
- **Atonality**: The other pole
- **Serial harmony**: One organizational approach spanning both poles

# Key Properties
1. Tonal and atonal resources amalgamated within single works
2. Serial devices coexist with root progressions and intervallic tensions
3. Flexible creative process rather than strict adherence to one system
4. Not exclusive: tonal and atonal are not mutually exclusive categories
5. The synthesis points away from strict atonal writing toward inclusive practice
6. Any harmonic combination is possible within the synthesis

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify passages governed by root progression (tonal)
- Identify passages governed by serial devices (potentially atonal)
- Note passages governed by intervallic tension (independent of both)
- Track transitions between tonal and atonal sections
- Assess the overall coherence of the tonal-atonal combination

# Context & Application
Persichetti explicitly advocates for synthesis over exclusion. The mature twentieth-century composer does not choose between tonal and atonal but integrates both. A hymn may be placed under a tone row; quartal harmony may blend with tertian. At any point in the formal shape, a melodic group of notes may control harmonic texture -- this could be tonal, atonal, or serial in character. The success of such synthesis depends on the contextual and formal conditions and the composer's skill.

# Examples
Works containing contrasting tonal and atonal techniques:
- Bela Bartok, Piano Concerto No. 2 (Boosey)
- Karl Amadeus Hartmann, Symphony No. 6 (Schott)
- Charles Ives, Piano Sonata No. 2 (Concord) (Arrow)
- Vincent Persichetti, Quintet for Piano and Strings (Elkan-Vogel)
- Roman Vlad, Divertimento for 11 Instruments (Boosey)

# Common Errors
- Treating tonal and atonal as mutually exclusive
- Assuming serial devices require atonal context
- Failing to manage transitions between tonal and atonal sections

# Common Confusions
- **Not mutually exclusive**: Tonal and atonal resources can coexist -- they are not opposing systems
- **Serial is not necessarily atonal**: Serial techniques can be applied within tonal frameworks
- **Synthesis, not eclecticism**: The combination must be integrated, not merely juxtaposed
