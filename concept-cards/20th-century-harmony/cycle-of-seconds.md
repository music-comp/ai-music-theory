# Quick Definition
A harmonic relationship system where root movements of seconds govern chord progressions, with the supertonic (II) and leading-tone chord (VII) serving as primary chords supporting the tonic from positions a second in either direction.

# Core Definition
In second relationship (cycle of seconds), the supertonic and leading-tone chords help establish the tonic center from their positions a second in either direction. Primary chords are I, II, and VII, with the remaining chords secondary. The relationships II-I and VII-I dominate all others, functioning analogously to V-I and IV-I in traditional fifth-relationship harmony. The most natural root progressions parallel those in music based upon the cycle of fifths: the corresponding progression is I-V-VII-IV-II-I.

# Key Properties
1. Primary chords: I, II, VII
2. II-I and VII-I dominate all other relationships
3. Corresponding progression: I-V-VII-IV-II-I
4. Particularly well suited to phrygian mode
5. In phrygian, second-cycle primaries naturally include the characteristic modal flavor (lowered second)
6. Both II and VII in phrygian contain the distinctive phrygian second scale step
7. Cyclic relationships can be constructed in any scale, synthetic or otherwise

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify passages where root movements by second predominate
- Look for structural emphasis on I, II, and VII as arrival chords
- The ear accepts these relationships when consistently confirmed through passing chords and cadential movements
- In phrygian mode, second relationship happens to coincide with the natural primary chords of the mode
- Determine corresponding chord functions by paralleling fifth-cycle conventions

# Context & Application
Second-relationship harmony is particularly effective in phrygian mode, where the second-cycle primary chords naturally emphasize the modal character. Both II and VII contain the distinctive lowered second degree, making this the most idiomatic cyclic framework for phrygian. In other modes and scales, the colors of primary chords depend on the intervallic make-up of the scale in force.

# Common Errors
- Confusing cycle of seconds with stepwise voice leading or neighboring-chord motion
- Treating II and VII as passing or neighboring chords rather than structural primary pillars

# Common Confusions
- **Cycle vs. step motion**: The cycle of seconds is a structural relationship where II and VII function as primary harmonic pillars, not merely neighboring or passing chords moving by step
- **Modal alignment**: Not all modes align their natural primary chords with the second cycle; phrygian is the special case where alignment occurs
