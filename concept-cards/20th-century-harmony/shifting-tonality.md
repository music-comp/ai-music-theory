# Quick Definition
A sense of displaced or wandering tonality created by continuous, quickly shifting keys that do not establish any single tonal center.

# Formal Definition
Continuous, quickly shifting keys produce a sense of displaced tonality rather than modulation. Unlike modulation which moves definitively from one established key to another, shifting tonality creates a state of perpetual harmonic motion without landing. The gravity of a tonal center can be decreased by moving a new voice through notes that give an important chord new roots, creating constant harmonic reinterpretation.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Shifting tonality characteristics:
- **Speed**: Keys change continuously and quickly
- **Establishment**: No key is firmly established
- **Effect**: Displaced tonality rather than modulation
- **Mechanism**: Voice movement gives chords new root interpretations

Loosening key feeling:
- Moving voice provides new roots for existing chord
- Quick key changes prevent establishment of center
- Transient modulation contributes to shifting effect

# Musical Context/Application
Shifting tonality operates in the space between firm tonality and atonality. Key feeling may be gradually loosened or tightened throughout a passage. This technique provides harmonic variety and forward motion without the commitment to a new key that modulation implies. Music may hold its tonality loosely, with elements of atonality inherent from the onset.

# Common Confusions
- Shifting tonality is not the same as modulation; no key is established
- The effect is displacement, not arrival
- Lies in the continuum between tonality and atonality
