# Quick Definition
A sense of displaced or wandering tonality created by continuous, quickly shifting keys that do not establish any single tonal center, producing perpetual harmonic motion without landing.

# Core Definition
Continuous, quickly shifting keys produce a sense of displaced tonality rather than modulation. Unlike modulation, which moves definitively from one established key to another, shifting tonality creates a state of perpetual harmonic motion without arrival. The gravity of a tonal center is decreased by moving a new voice through notes that give an important chord new roots, creating constant harmonic reinterpretation. This lies on the continuum between firm tonality and atonality.

# Prerequisites
- **Modulation**: The process that shifting tonality subverts
- **Tonal gravity**: The force being weakened

# Key Properties
1. Keys change continuously and quickly
2. No key is firmly established
3. Effect is displacement, not arrival
4. Voice movement gives chords new root interpretations
5. Lies between firm tonality and atonality on the continuum
6. Key feeling may be gradually loosened or tightened

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify passages where keys change too rapidly for any to establish
- Note whether chord roots are constantly being reinterpreted by moving voices
- Distinguish from modulation (which establishes a new key) and from atonality (which has no key at all)
- Track the rate of tonal change: very fast changes indicate shifting tonality

# Context & Application
Shifting tonality operates in the space between firm tonality and atonality. Key feeling may be gradually loosened or tightened throughout a passage. This technique provides harmonic variety and forward motion without the commitment to a new key that modulation implies. Music may hold its tonality loosely, with elements of atonality inherent from the onset. Distant tonal centers are reached in countless ways, but unless this facile technique is held in careful check, harmonic growth will be replaced by superficial variety.

# Common Errors
- Treating shifting tonality as a type of modulation
- Assuming displacement indicates compositional failure
- Overusing the technique without structural purpose

# Common Confusions
- **Shifting vs. modulation**: Modulation establishes a new key with at least three confirming chords; shifting tonality produces displacement without establishment
- **Continuum position**: Shifting tonality lies between firm tonality and atonality -- it is neither
- **Controlled use**: The technique must be held in check to avoid replacing harmonic growth with superficial variety
