# Quick Definition
Triadic progressions that move freely outside any scale formation, with roots progressing most often by seconds, thirds, and the tritone rather than by fifths, allowing any triad to follow any other triad.

# Formal Definition
In music that lacks a definite scale or modality, any triad may be followed by any other triad and any sequence of key centers may be used. Root movements of major and minor seconds and major and minor thirds occur more often than fifths or the tritone because they are less likely to define any one scale. The root of one triad moves up or down to the root of another triad more often in intervals of seconds and thirds than in fifths. Both triads involved may be of any type, but are usually major or minor.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
**Preferred Root Movements:**
- Major second (M2)
- Minor second (m2)
- Major third (M3)
- Minor third (m3)

**Less Common (due to tonal implications):**
- Perfect fifth (P5) - strongly establishes modality/tonality
- Perfect fourth (P4)
- Tritone (A4/d5) - provides variety but less directional

**Augmented Fourth Relationship:**
When triads progress chromatically, root movement of the augmented fourth adds variety to the basic second-third relationship, particularly between major or minor triads.

**Triadic Choices for a Single Melody Note:**
Any note has three major and three minor triadic possibilities, allowing extensive harmonic variety even for simple scale-line melodies.

# Musical Context/Application
Chromatic triadic progression often creates harmonic and tonal restlessness. This restlessness can be focused by ensuring each voice has purpose and design, allowing any triad to progress freely with harmonic meaning. When chromatic triads wander harmonically and make vague phrase-shapes, a diatonic melody may bring the chromaticism into clear focus.

The technique is particularly effective when maintaining an underlying tonal center that emerges not from a prevailing mode but from melodic emphasis and phrase structure. The passage may be indisputably centered on a particular pitch without being governed by a preconceived tonal framework.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes assume chromatic triadic progression is random or atonal. While it operates outside traditional scale constraints, effective chromatic triadic writing maintains coherence through melodic purpose in each voice. The absence of scale-based logic does not mean absence of musical logic.
