# Quick Definition
Polychords whose units consist of seventh chords, quartal chords, or secundal structures rather than triads, used typically in brief chordal groups rather than extended passages, with some containing common tones for homogeneous sound.

# Formal Definition
Polychords consisting of seventh chord units are seldom used in extended passages but more often in brief chordal groups that intensify a single line or a two-part statement, or as a sforzando chord. When chords other than chords by thirds are used as building units in polychords, textural clarity becomes more of a problem.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Non-triadic unit types:

**Seventh chord units**:
- Seldom extended passages
- Brief chordal groups
- Intensify lines or statements
- Sforzando use
- May have common tones (homogeneous) or no common tones

**Quartal units**:
- Polychordal formations become enlarged three-note chords by fourths
- Textural clarity challenging
- Functions better mixed with triadic units

**Secundal units**:
- If all units secundal, creates polycluster
- Better as uppermost unit in mixed construction
- Functions sonorously at top of multi-unit polychord

Common tone considerations:
- Some seventh-chord polychords share notes = homogeneous sound
- Some have no common notes = more dissonant
- Common tones affect blend and clarity

# Musical Context/Application
Non-triadic polychords expand the polychordal palette beyond triadic sonorities. Seventh chord units are particularly useful for:
- Dramatic punctuation (sforzando)
- Intensification of melodic statements
- Brief harmonic color

Quartal and secundal units present clarity challenges but offer distinctive colors when properly handled. The most effective use places non-triadic elements in polychords of mixed construction.

# Common Confusions
- Non-triadic polychords are not automatically "more modern"
- All-secundal polychords are polyclusters (separate category)
- Brief use is aesthetic choice, not technical limitation
- Common tones significantly affect character
