# Quick Definition
The simultaneous combination of two or more chords from different harmonic areas, with the component parts referred to as chordal units, whose beginnings may be traced to double and triple pedal point where passing harmony creates bitonal hints.

# Formal Definition
A polychord is the simultaneous combination of two or more chords from different harmonic areas. The segments of the polychord are referred to as chordal units. The beginnings of polychords may be traced to double and triple pedal point, where hints of bitonality, caused by the relationship of the passing chords to the pedal chord, lie in the passing harmony.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Basic structure:
- Two or more distinct chords
- From different harmonic areas (not forming a single tertian structure)
- Sounded simultaneously
- Each component = chordal unit

Clear grouping requirement:
- Chordal units must maintain distinct identity
- Rearranging tones can destroy polychordal organization
- Units must be perceived as separate entities

Polychord vs. single chord:
- If upper unit forms chord by thirds with bottom unit, it may sound non-polychordal out of context
- Context determines whether structure is heard as polychord
- Surrounding polyharmony establishes polychordal perception

# Musical Context/Application
Polychords became a defining technique of 20th-century composition, particularly in the works of Stravinsky, Milhaud, and Bartok. They allow composers to create complex, rich sonorities while maintaining structural clarity through the separation of chordal units.

Polyharmony is seldom polytonal. Polytonality is present only when the chordal units that make up the structure adhere to separate keys. Polychords that are not polytonal are considerably more flexible and versatile; the harmonic areas of both chordal units shift often.

# Common Confusions
- Polychords are not always polytonal; most are not
- Clear grouping of units is essential; scrambled voicing destroys polychordal effect
- The same pitches can be polychordal or non-polychordal depending on voicing
- Context determines perception of ambiguous structures
