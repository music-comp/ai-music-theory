# Quick Definition
The technique of modulating to remote tonal centers, requiring careful control to prevent harmonic growth from being replaced by superficial variety, with distant keys established through auxiliary chord reinforcement or violent tonic repetition with color changes.

# Core Definition
Distant tonal centers are reached in countless ways, but unless this facile technique is held in careful check, harmonic growth will be replaced by superficial variety. A distant tonality may be established by the reinforcement of the new center by auxiliary chords, or by the pneumatic action of violent repetitions of the new tonic with color and spacing changes. When modulating to a permanent key, chords other than the tonic may be aimed for so that time can be made for settling into the feeling of the new tonality. A premature cadence is avoided by jumping to a distant key, then working gradually toward the desired key.

# Key Properties
1. Distant keys reached in countless ways
2. Must be controlled to avoid superficial variety
3. Reinforcement by auxiliary chords establishes distant key
4. Violent repetition of new tonic with color/spacing changes
5. Aim for non-tonic chords first to allow settling time
6. Jump to distant key, then work gradually toward desired key
7. Abrupt modulation requires extended progression to confirm

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the distance of the target key from the current key
- Use auxiliary chords to reinforce the new center
- Alternatively, repeat the new tonic with varied color and spacing
- Aim for non-tonic chords of the new key before the tonic
- Provide extended confirming progression after arrival
- Assess whether the modulation serves harmonic growth or mere variety

# Context & Application
The warning against superficial variety is one of Persichetti's key aesthetic judgments: the ease of reaching distant keys in twentieth-century harmony does not justify constant distant modulation. The composer must ensure that each distant modulation serves a structural purpose. The technique of jumping to a distant key and then working gradually toward the desired key is particularly useful for avoiding premature cadences while still achieving large tonal leaps.

# Common Errors
- Modulating to distant keys merely for variety rather than structural purpose
- Failing to reinforce the arrival with extended progression
- Jumping to distant keys without settling time

# Common Confusions
- **Easy does not mean advisable**: The technical ease of distant modulation in twentieth-century harmony does not justify its overuse
- **Superficial variety vs. harmonic growth**: Distant modulation serves the work only when it contributes to harmonic development
- **Pneumatic repetition**: Violent, colorful repetition of the new tonic is an effective establishment technique, not a crude device
