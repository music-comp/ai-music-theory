# Quick Definition
The tritone interval between combined keys, forming the prime polytonal relationship because it is the most resonant of dissonant key combinations, providing maximum differentiation of tonal spheres with balanced, neither-key-dominant tension.

# Core Definition
The tritone as a basis for key coalition forms a prime polytonal relationship because it is the most resonant of the dissonant combinations of keys. Those keys that are not closely related according to the circle of fifths will more easily set apart the tonal key spheres. The tritone-separated keys achieve maximum differentiation while maintaining a particularly resonant combined sonority. Neither key dominates the other because they are equidistant on the circle of fifths.

# Key Properties
1. Most resonant of dissonant key combinations
2. Maximum separation on circle of fifths
3. Clear differentiation of tonal spheres
4. Neither key dominates the other
5. The tritone-spanning scale steps (4th and 7th degrees) are the tones that define each key
6. Balanced tension between the two keys

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify keys separated by tritone (6 semitones, e.g., C and F#)
- Note the balanced quality -- neither key dominates
- Use the tritone relationship for maximum polytonal clarity
- Compare with other key distances on the cycle of fifths

# Context & Application
The tritone polytonal relationship provides maximum key separation with maximum resonance. This makes it particularly effective when clear polytonal texture is desired. The equal distance from both sides of the circle of fifths means neither key has harmonic priority over the other, creating balanced tension. This is why the scale steps spanning the tritone are so essential for establishing each key's identity in polytonal writing.

# Common Errors
- Confusing "most resonant of dissonant" with "most consonant"
- Overlooking the tritone's balanced quality (neither key dominates)
- Failing to use tritone-spanning tones to define each key

# Common Confusions
- **Resonant vs. consonant**: The tritone relationship is dissonant but resonant -- it rings clearly despite tension
- **Prime relationship**: "Prime" means most effective for polytonal purposes, not most consonant
- **Balance**: The tritone creates equality between keys because both are equidistant on the circle of fifths
