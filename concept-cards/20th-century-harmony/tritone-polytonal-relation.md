# Quick Definition
The tritone interval between combined keys, which forms the prime polytonal relationship due to being the most resonant of dissonant key combinations.

# Formal Definition
The tritone as a basis for key coalition forms a prime polytonal relationship because it is the most resonant of the dissonant combinations of keys. Those keys that are not closely related according to the circle of fifths will more easily set apart the tonal key spheres. The tritone-separated keys achieve maximum differentiation while maintaining a particularly resonant combined sonority.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Tritone relationship properties:
- Keys separated by tritone (6 semitones)
- Most resonant of dissonant combinations
- Maximum separation on circle of fifths
- Prime polytonal relationship

Why tritone is "prime":
- Keys are maximally distant (opposite on circle of fifths)
- Clear differentiation of tonal spheres
- Resonant despite dissonant combination
- Neither key dominates the other

Comparison to other relationships:
- P5: Most consonant but keys overlap significantly
- M2: Dissonant and close
- Tritone: Dissonant but balanced and resonant

# Musical Context/Application
The tritone polytonal relationship provides maximum key separation with maximum resonance. This makes it particularly effective when clear polytonal texture is desired. The equal distance from both sides of the circle of fifths means neither key has harmonic priority over the other, creating balanced tension. The tritone-spanning scale steps are crucial for establishing each key's identity.

- Tritone relationship creates equality between keys
