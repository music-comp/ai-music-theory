# Quick Definition
The ornamentation of serial chords using three types of tones: nonharmonic tones (functioning as in tonal music), harmonic tones from related chords, or serial tones arising from the simultaneous use of two or more forms of the melodic set.

# Core Definition
Chords may be ornamented by nonharmonic tones, by harmonic tones, or by serial tones that result from the simultaneous use of two or more forms of the set. Nonharmonic tones function as in tonal music (passing tones, neighbors, suspensions, etc.). Harmonic tones come from related chords within the set's harmonic vocabulary. Serial tones -- unique to serial composition -- arise when multiple set forms are used simultaneously, providing ornamentation that is organically derived from the row.

# Prerequisites
- **Serial harmony**: The compositional framework
- **Serial center chord**: The chord being ornamented
- **Melodic set**: The source of serial ornamental tones

# Key Properties
1. Three ornamental tone types: nonharmonic, harmonic, serial
2. Nonharmonic tones: function as in tonal music (passing, neighbor, suspension, etc.)
3. Harmonic tones: from related chords within the set's vocabulary
4. Serial tones: from simultaneous use of two or more set forms
5. Serial tones are unique to serial composition
6. Parallel harmony possible at doubling points in the set
7. Ornamentation approaches cadences in serial music just as in tonal

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify which ornamental tones are nonharmonic (not from the current chord)
- Note which are harmonic (from other chords in the set's vocabulary)
- Identify serial tones (from a different form of the set operating simultaneously)
- Observe how ornamental activity increases approaching serial cadences

# Context & Application
Serial ornamentation demonstrates that traditional decorative principles extend into serial contexts. Nonharmonic tones function identically to their tonal counterparts. Harmonic tones provide variety from the set's own resources. Serial tones offer a uniquely serial type of ornamentation where the interaction of multiple set forms generates chromatic embellishment organically related to the row. When the melodic set includes repeated notes, parallel harmony may be used momentarily at doubling points, providing another ornamental resource.

# Common Errors
- Restricting ornamentation to only one of the three types
- Ignoring the organic connection of serial tones to the row
- Neglecting to intensify ornamental activity approaching cadences

# Common Confusions
- **Three types, not one**: Serial music uses nonharmonic, harmonic, and serial tones for ornamentation -- not just serial
- **Traditional principles apply**: Nonharmonic tones function the same way in serial as in tonal music
- **Serial tones are unique**: The third type -- from simultaneous set forms -- is a distinctly serial ornamental resource
