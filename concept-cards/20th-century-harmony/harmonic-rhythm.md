# Quick Definition

The underlying rhythm created by the rate of harmonic change, which plays a large part in controlling and stabilizing musical flow.

# Formal Definition

**Harmonic rhythm** is the underlying rhythm that results from chord changes in a musical passage. It plays a large part in controlling and stabilizing musical flow. Harmony is always felt in relation to rhythmic structure, and not until chords evolve in a rhythmic form does harmony become wholly articulate. Quick harmonic changes create an undercurrent of restlessness, while widely spaced changes create breadth. The combination of fluctuating melodic and harmonic rhythms provides the composer with significant creative rhythmic potential.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Harmonic rhythm factors:
- **Rate of change**: How frequently chords change
- **Regularity**: Whether changes occur at predictable or irregular intervals
- **Relationship to meter**: Changes on strong beats vs. weak beats
- **Relationship to melodic rhythm**: Coinciding or contrasting

Key principles:
- Quick tempo may cause chord changes to sound like ornamental chords
- Simple chords moving at high speed may create complex sounds
- Harmonic rhythm does not move when chords are repeated
- Syncopated harmonic rhythm creates tension against melodic pulse

# Musical Context/Application

Harmonic rhythm interacts with:
- Linear motion and outline of outer voices
- Pull of harmonic or tonal centers
- Time duration of stressed and unstressed chords
- Graded tensions of texture and pitch
- Rhythm created by phrasing, bowing, and tonguing

Applications:
- Creating breadth or restlessness through pacing
- Building tension through acceleration
- Creating relaxation through deceleration
- Articulating formal sections through rhythmic contrast

# Common Confusions

- **Harmonic rhythm vs. melodic rhythm**: Harmonic rhythm concerns chord change rate; melodic rhythm concerns note durations and patterns in melody.
- **Chord change vs. movement**: Repeating a chord does not create harmonic rhythm movement, even if rhythmically articulated.
- **Speed vs. complexity**: Fast harmonic rhythm can make simple chords sound complex; slow harmonic rhythm clarifies complex chords.
