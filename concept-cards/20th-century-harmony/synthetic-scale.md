# Quick Definition
Original or artificially constructed scales formed by arranging major, minor, and augmented seconds in various orders, ideally derived from melodic or harmonic patterns rather than mathematical permutation.

# Core Definition
Although a single tone through its overtone series suggests the major scale most obviously, the major is only one of many scales contained in the basic twelve-tone chromatic scale. Free placement of scale steps results in original scale formations beyond major and minor modes. Most synthetic scales are constructed by placing any number of major, minor, and augmented seconds in any order. The permutation possibilities are staggering and the mathematical process has little creative connection with composition. It is advisable that scales be allowed to form as a result of the impetus of melodic or harmonic patterns; the material generated by thematic ideas may then be gathered up and placed into scale formation.

# Key Properties
1. **Building blocks**: major seconds (2 semitones), minor seconds (1), augmented seconds (3)
2. **Construction principle**: any number in any order
3. **Musical derivation preferred**: scales should emerge from melodic/harmonic ideas, not mathematical permutation
4. **Common named synthetic scales**: Hungarian Minor, Double Harmonic, Enigmatic, Neapolitan, Oriental, and others
5. **Seven-tone scales**: built from paired tetrachords (similar or different)
6. **Modal versions applicable**: the modal construction principle extends to all synthetic scales

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the interval pattern (sequence of major, minor, and augmented seconds)
2. Check if it matches a known synthetic scale type
3. If unknown, derive the scale from the melodic/harmonic context
4. Analyze tetrachord structure (similar or different upper/lower tetrachords)
5. Survey indigenous chordal materials

# Context & Application
The mathematical permutation process has little creative connection with composition. Scales should form from musical impetus. Once a scale is identified, its modal versions, mirrored form, and indigenous chord vocabulary can be explored systematically. The sooner the melodic voice includes all tones of the scale, the greater the chance of projecting the synthetic scale as a unit.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Constructing scales mathematically and then trying to compose with them. **Correction**: Scales should emerge from musical ideas; the scale is codified after the music suggests it.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking synthetic scales are arbitrary. **Clarification**: While construction is free, the most useful synthetic scales have historical precedent (folk scales) or emerge naturally from compositional practice.
