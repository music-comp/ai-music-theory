# Quick Definition
Original or artificially constructed scales formed by arranging major, minor, and augmented seconds in various orders, often derived from melodic or harmonic patterns rather than mathematical permutation.

# Formal Definition
Although a single tone through its overtone series suggests the major scale most obviously, the major is only one of many scales contained in the basic twelve-tone chromatic scale found in the upper region of the overtone series. Free placement of scale steps results in original scale formations beyond major and minor modes. Most synthetic scales are constructed by placing any number of major, minor, and augmented seconds in any order.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Construction principles:
- Use combinations of major seconds (2 semitones), minor seconds (1 semitone), and augmented seconds (3 semitones)
- Permutation possibilities are extensive
- Scales should form from melodic/harmonic patterns, not mathematical process

Common synthetic scales:
- Hungarian Minor: augmented second between 3-4 and 6-7
- Double Harmonic: augmented second between 1-2 and 6-7
- Enigmatic: unusual semitone distribution
- Various named folk scales (Neapolitan, Oriental, etc.)

# Musical Context/Application
It is advisable that scales be allowed to form as a result of the impetus of melodic or harmonic patterns; the material generated by thematic ideas may then be gathered up and placed into scale formation. The mathematical permutation process has little creative connection with composition.

# Common Confusions
Students may try to use synthetic scales by first constructing them mathematically. Persichetti emphasizes that scales should emerge from musical ideas rather than abstract permutation, with the scale codified after the music suggests it.
