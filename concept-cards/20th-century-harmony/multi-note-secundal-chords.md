# Quick Definition
Four- and five-note chords built from seconds, which move contrapuntally with difficulty and are best combined with three-note secundal chords or quartal chords to allow linear motion.

# Formal Definition
Four- and five-note chords by seconds move contrapuntally with difficulty. Linear motion can be obtained by including in the progression three-note chords by seconds or chords by fourths. The sevenths (inverted seconds) that are present in chords by fourths blend with the seconds in chords by seconds and leave space for movement of the parts.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Construction:
- 4-note secundal: Three stacked seconds (e.g., C-D-E-F)
- 5-note secundal: Four stacked seconds (e.g., C-D-E-F-G)
- Can extend to encompass entire scales (diatonic, chromatic, or synthetic)

Contrapuntal limitations:
- Dense texture restricts voice movement
- Close intervals leave little room for melodic motion
- Independent voice activity is difficult to achieve

Solutions for linear motion:
1. Include three-note secundal chords in progression
2. Include chords by fourths (contain compatible sevenths)
3. Mix secundal with polychordal or compound harmony

Blending principle:
- Sevenths in quartal chords (P4 inverted = P5, but stacked 4ths create 7ths)
- Blend with seconds in secundal chords
- Create space for voice movement

# Musical Context/Application
Multi-note secundal chords represent the transition point between true chords by seconds and clusters. When these thickened harmonies are allowed to "harden" without relief, they become static and lose their harmonic vitality.

Effective use requires:
- Mixing with lighter textures
- Combination with polychordal or compound harmony
- Strategic introduction and departure
- Attention to phrase breath points

A single chord by seconds can be enlarged to encompass an entire scale, but it may or may not be a cluster depending on the surrounding harmony.

# Common Confusions
- Multi-note secundal chords are not automatically clusters
- The difficulty of counterpoint is structural, not just technical
- Quartal chords blend well because of their inverted-second content (sevenths)
- Scale-encompassing secundal chords may or may not function as clusters
