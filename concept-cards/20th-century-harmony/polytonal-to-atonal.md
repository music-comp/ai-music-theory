# Quick Definition
The process by which polytonal textures dissolve into atonality when individual lines are given such chromatic freedom that the implied harmony becomes indefinite and the feeling of key disappears.

# Core Definition
Atonality may be entered through florid writing when the individual lines are given such freedom of chromatic movement that the implied harmony becomes indefinite and the feeling of key disappears. This represents one pathway from organized multi-key texture (polytonality) to keyless organization (atonality) within the continuum of tonal organization.

# Prerequisites
- **Polytonality**: The starting multi-key texture
- **Atonality**: The resulting keyless organization

# Key Properties
1. Caused by increasing chromatic freedom in individual lines
2. Implied harmony becomes indefinite
3. Key feeling gradually disappears
4. Transition can be gradual or reach a critical point
5. Demonstrates the continuum between polytonality and atonality
6. Lines lose their individual key identities through chromaticism

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify a polytonal passage where chromatic freedom is increasing
- Note whether individual lines are losing their scale-formation identities
- Observe the implied harmony becoming less definite
- Track the point where key feeling dissolves
- Distinguish from planned atonality (which never had keys)

# Context & Application
This transition demonstrates that polytonality and atonality exist on a continuum. When polytonal lines become so chromatically free that their individual key identities are lost, the overall texture loses its polytonal character and becomes atonal. The transition sits at the end of the textural continuum: triads to polychords to polytonality to atonality. The process can be gradual, passing through stages of increasing ambiguity, or can be sudden when chromatic saturation reaches a critical point.

# Common Errors
- Treating the transition as binary rather than gradual
- Assuming polytonal and atonal are opposites rather than adjacent on a continuum
- Failing to control the pace of chromatic dissolution

# Common Confusions
- **Gradual, not binary**: The transition passes through degrees of increasing ambiguity
- **Continuum position**: Polytonality and atonality are adjacent, not opposite
- **Chromatic freedom in lines**: It is the linear chromaticism, not just harmonic complexity, that causes the transition
