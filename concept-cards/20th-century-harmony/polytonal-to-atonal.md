# Quick Definition
The process by which polytonal textures can dissolve into atonality through florid chromatic writing that obscures key definitions.

# Formal Definition
Atonality may be entered through florid writing when the individual lines are given such freedom of chromatic movement that the implied harmony becomes indefinite and the feeling of key disappears. This represents one pathway from organized multi-key texture (polytonality) to keyless organization (atonality).

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Transition process:
1. **Polytonal starting point**: Clear multiple key areas
2. **Increasing chromaticism**: Lines gain chromatic freedom
3. **Harmonic indefiniteness**: Implied harmony becomes unclear
4. **Key feeling dissolution**: Sense of key disappears
5. **Atonal result**: No key gravitation

Mechanisms:
- Florid melodic writing
- Chromatic freedom in individual lines
- Accumulating harmonic indefiniteness
- Loss of scale reference

# Musical Context/Application
This transition demonstrates the continuum between polytonality and atonality. When polytonal lines become so chromatically free that their individual key identities are lost, the overall texture loses its polytonal character and becomes atonal. The transition can be gradual, passing through stages of increasing ambiguity, or can be sudden when chromatic saturation reaches a critical point.

# Common Confusions
- The transition is gradual, not binary
- Polytonal and atonal are not opposite but adjacent on a spectrum
- Chromatic freedom in lines, not just harmony, causes the transition
