# Quick Definition

Polychords with three or more triadic units, built on overtones of the bottom triad's third and fifth, used for brief climactic or fast quiet passages due to their massive, complex character.

# Core Definition

"There are four kinds of polychords with three or more units: those whose upper units are built on the third and fifth of the bottom triad (rarely the root); those whose upper units are built on overtones (at any octave level) of the third and fifth of the bottom triad; those whose upper units are built on overtones of the overtones of the third and fifth of the bottom triad; and those whose upper notes are built on overtones of the root, third, or fifth of triads other than the bottom triad" (Persichetti, p. 149). "Multi-unit polychords (usually not polytonal) are used for brief periods of time. Their natural habitat is a climactic section, or a fast but quiet section" (pp. 151-152).

# Prerequisites

- **Polychord** — Three-unit polychords extend the two-unit principle

# Key Properties

1. **Category 1**: Upper units on 3rd/5th of bottom triad (most related)
2. **Category 2**: Upper units on overtones of bottom's 3rd/5th (at any octave)
3. **Category 3**: Upper units on overtones of overtones (more remote)
4. **Category 4**: Upper units on overtones of non-bottom triads (most independent)
5. Bottom triad voices often spread apart for clarity
6. Doubling/coupling enlarges without adding complexity
7. Overlapping units may reduce three-unit to two-unit perception
8. Usually not polytonal; used for brief periods
9. Natural habitats: climactic sections, fast quiet passages

# Construction / Recognition

## To Construct
1. Select bottom triad and spread its voices
2. Build upper units on overtone-related pitches of bottom triad's 3rd and 5th
3. Space some units wider, overlap others as needed
4. Use doubling/coupling to enlarge without adding complexity
5. Plan for brief use in climactic or fast passages

# Context & Application

Multi-unit polychords are so massive and complex that extended use is impractical. Their natural habitats are climaxes (powerful) and fast quiet passages (fleeting color). Multi-unit polychords are usually not polytonal since maintaining multiple key adherences with three or more units is rarely practical.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Using multi-unit polychords in sustained passages
  **Correction**: Brief use is compositional wisdom; extended use causes tiring mass resonance
- **Error**: Overlapping all units tightly
  **Correction**: Overlapping may reduce three-unit to two-unit perception; maintain some spacing

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Three-unit polychords are simply bigger two-unit chords
  **Clarification**: They have distinct construction categories based on overtone derivation
- **Confusion**: Brief use is a limitation
  **Clarification**: It is compositional wisdom; the massive quality serves specific dramatic purposes
