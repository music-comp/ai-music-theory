# Quick Definition
Polychords with three or more triadic units, whose upper units are typically built on overtones of the bottom triad's third and fifth, used for brief climactic or fast quiet passages due to their massive, complex character.

# Formal Definition
Chordal units in polyharmony are often built upon different overtones of the same series. There are four kinds of polychords with three or more units: those whose upper units are built on the third and fifth of the bottom triad; those whose upper units are built on overtones of the third and fifth; those whose upper units are built on overtones of overtones; and those whose upper units are built on overtones of triads other than the bottom triad.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Four categories of multi-unit polychords:

1. **Direct building**: Upper units on 3rd and 5th of bottom triad
   - Most related to bottom unit
   - Strongest overtone reinforcement

2. **First-level overtones**: Upper units on overtones of bottom's 3rd and 5th
   - At any octave level
   - Still directly related to bottom

3. **Second-level overtones**: Upper units on overtones of overtones
   - More remote relationship
   - More complex sonority

4. **Independent triads**: Upper units on overtones of non-bottom triads
   - Most independent
   - Least integrated sonority

Voicing considerations:
- Bottom triad voices often spread apart
- Some units overlapped, others spaced wider
- Doubling and coupling enlarge without adding complexity
- Overlapping may reduce three-unit chord to two-unit perception

# Musical Context/Application
Multi-unit polychords are so massive and complex that they are used for brief periods. Their natural habitats are:
- Climactic sections
- Fast but quiet passages

Multi-unit polychords are usually not polytonal. The complexity of maintaining multiple key adherences is rarely practical with three or more units.

# Common Confusions
- Three-unit polychords are not simply "bigger" two-unit chords
- Overtone relationship creates different categories of complexity
- Overlapping can perceptually reduce unit count
- Brief use is compositional wisdom, not limitation
