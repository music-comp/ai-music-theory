# Quick Definition
The limited chordal resources derived from the whole-tone scale, which require special techniques to sustain harmonic interest.

# Formal Definition
The chords furnished by the whole-tone hexatonic scale are meager harmonic material. The six triads of the scale are all augmented, and four of them are effectively inversions of the first two. There is only one type of seventh, one type of ninth chord, and one category of chords by seconds. Nevertheless, whole-tone materials have possibilities when certain devices are employed.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Whole-tone chord inventory:
- 6 augmented triads (2 distinct, 4 inversions)
- 1 type of seventh chord (augmented with major 7th)
- 1 type of ninth chord
- 1 category of second-based chords

Devices to prolong interest:
1. Contrary motion
2. Alternating the two whole-tone scales
3. Sounding all six tones together
4. Changing density and spacing
5. Using both whole-tone scales simultaneously

# Musical Context/Application
Whole-tone harmony is creatively stimulating when amalgamated with other materials: whole-tone melody with non-whole-tone chords, diatonic melody over whole-tone harmony, whole-tone chords changed to added-note chords with minor seconds attached, alternating whole-tone and non-whole-tone passages, or combining whole-tone with another scale type.

# Common Confusions
Students may use whole-tone harmony in isolation without recognizing its limitations. The material is most effective when contrasted with or embedded in other harmonic contexts, not as a self-sufficient harmonic system.
