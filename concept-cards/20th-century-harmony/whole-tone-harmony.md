# Quick Definition
The limited chordal resources derived from the whole-tone scale, which require special devices to sustain harmonic interest and are most effective when combined with non-whole-tone materials.

# Core Definition
The chords furnished by the whole-tone hexatonic scale are meager harmonic material. The six triads are all augmented, and four of them are effectively inversions of the first two. There is only one type of seventh, one type of ninth chord, and one category of chords by seconds. Nevertheless, whole-tone materials have possibilities when certain devices are employed. The scale is most effective when amalgamated with other materials.

# Prerequisites
- **whole-tone-scale** -- the scale from which these chords derive

# Key Properties
1. **Chord inventory**:
   - 6 augmented triads (only 2 distinct; 4 are inversions)
   - 1 type of seventh chord
   - 1 type of ninth chord
   - 1 category of chords by seconds
2. **Five devices to prolong interest**:
   - Contrary motion
   - Alternating the two whole-tone scales
   - Sounding all six tones together harmonically
   - Changing density and spacing
   - Using both whole-tone scales simultaneously
3. **Six combination techniques** with non-whole-tone materials:
   - Whole-tone melody + non-whole-tone chords
   - Diatonic melody over whole-tone harmony
   - Whole-tone chords changed to added-note chords (minor seconds attached)
   - Alternating whole-tone and non-whole-tone passages
   - Whole-tone combined with another scale type
   - Both whole-tone scales simultaneously

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the chord types available from the whole-tone collection
2. Check which devices are being used to maintain interest
3. Look for combination with non-whole-tone materials
4. Note whether both whole-tone collections are employed

# Context & Application
When amalgamated with other materials, whole-tone harmony can be creatively stimulating. The various combination techniques provide practical ways to use whole-tone materials in extended passages without the monotony of pure whole-tone writing. Adding minor seconds to whole-tone chords transforms them into added-note chords with greater variety.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Using whole-tone harmony in isolation without recognizing its limitations. **Correction**: The material is most effective when contrasted with or embedded in other harmonic contexts.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking "more augmented triads = more variety." **Clarification**: The six augmented triads from a whole-tone scale reduce to only two distinct chords; apparent variety is illusory.
