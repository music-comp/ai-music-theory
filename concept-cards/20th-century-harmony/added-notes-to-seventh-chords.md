# Quick Definition
Seconds attached to seventh and ninth chords, where the sharply dissonant added tones do not compete with the seventh or ninth for resolution attention, functioning instead as color modifications that cling to the basic structure.

# Formal Definition
In an added-note seventh or ninth chord, even the sharply dissonant added tones do not compete with the seventh or ninth for dissonant-tone movement attention. The added tones are not strong contenders for resolution. They cling to inverted as well as to fundamental chords and may be doubled at will.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Practical considerations:
- Minor second additions more frequently used than major seconds
- Major second additions sometimes duplicate chord members (creating redundancy)
- Added notes may be doubled in outer voices
- Successive octaves in melodic coupling are effective

Construction:
- Base: Any seventh chord (Maj7, min7, dom7, dim7, half-dim7)
- Addition: M2 or m2 above or below any chord member
- Preference: m2 to avoid member duplication

Voice-leading behavior:
- Seventh still seeks resolution (traditional dissonance behavior)
- Added note has no independent resolution tendency
- Added note may be doubled freely

# Musical Context/Application
This technique extends the coloristic possibilities of tertian harmony without increasing the complexity of voice-leading demands. The traditional dissonance (seventh) maintains its expected behavior while the added tones provide textural enrichment.

Doubling the added note in outer voices is particularly effective when it occurs as successive octaves in melodic coupling. Over-all coupling (parallel octaves throughout) sounds well when used in an entire body of added-note harmony, a technique that would be avoided in traditional counterpoint.

# Common Confusions
- The added note does not create a larger chord type (e.g., adding to a seventh does not create a ninth chord)
- Distinction between added notes and extensions depends on context and function
- The seventh retains its traditional resolution tendency; the added note does not
- Parallel coupling of added notes is acceptable in this style
