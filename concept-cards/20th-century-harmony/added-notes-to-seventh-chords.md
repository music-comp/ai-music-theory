# Quick Definition
Seconds attached to seventh and ninth chords where the added tones do not compete with the seventh or ninth for dissonant-tone resolution attention, functioning as non-resolving color members that may be doubled at will, with minor seconds preferred to avoid chord-member duplication.

# Core Definition
In an added-note seventh or ninth chord, even the sharply dissonant added tones do not compete with the seventh or ninth for dissonant-tone movement attention. The added tones are not strong contenders for resolution. They cling to inverted as well as fundamental chords and may be doubled at will. Additions to seventh and ninth chords are more frequently minor seconds because a major-second addition sometimes results in duplication of a chord member. Doubling the added note in outer voices is effective when it occurs as successive octaves in melodic coupling. Over-all coupling sounds well when used in an entire body of added-note harmony.

# Key Properties
1. Added tones do not compete with seventh/ninth for resolution
2. Added tones are not strong contenders for resolution (they cling)
3. Minor seconds preferred (major seconds may duplicate chord members)
4. May be doubled at will
5. Effective in melodic coupling (successive octaves in outer voices)
6. Over-all coupling works well throughout added-note sections
7. Seventh retains traditional resolution behavior; added note does not

# Construction / Recognition
- Add M2 or m2 above/below any seventh/ninth chord member
- Prefer m2 to avoid member duplication
- Verify added note does not create a larger basic formation

# Context & Application
This technique extends coloristic possibilities without increasing voice-leading complexity. The traditional dissonance (seventh) maintains expected behavior while added tones provide textural enrichment. Parallel coupling of added notes is acceptable in this style -- a technique avoided in traditional counterpoint.
