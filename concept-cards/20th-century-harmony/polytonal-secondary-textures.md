# Quick Definition
The management of passing harmonic textures between structural polychordal pillars in polytonal writing, using chord member omission, doubling of stronger intervals, ornamental coloring, unison interpolations, or ostinatos to control thickness.

# Core Definition
The resonance of polytonality depends upon the resonance of the over-all tonic formation as determined by its intervallic tension. The passing secondary textures are maneuvered around the most resonant polychords that form the structural pillars of the particular key combination. These secondary textures often become thick and troublesome and are made workable by omission of chord members, doubling of stronger intervals, ornamental coloring, unison and two-part interpolations, or by ostinatos.

# Prerequisites
- **Polytonality**: The multi-key framework
- **Polytonal key relationships**: The resonance ordering system

# Key Properties
1. Secondary textures maneuver around most resonant polychordal pillars
2. Textures often become thick and troublesome
3. Techniques for managing thickness:
   - Omission of chord members
   - Doubling of stronger intervals
   - Ornamental coloring
   - Unison and two-part interpolations
   - Ostinatos
4. Structural pillars are the most resonant polychords of the key combination
5. Passing textures serve these pillars, not the reverse

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the most resonant polychords as structural pillars
- Note which passages are secondary (passing between pillars)
- Apply thinning techniques when texture becomes too thick
- Use ostinatos to clarify polytonal texture
- Insert unison or two-part passages to provide textural relief

# Context & Application
Effective polytonal composition requires not just choosing key relationships but managing the moment-to-moment texture that results. The structural pillars (most resonant polychords) anchor the harmonic design; the secondary passages between them need active management to remain clear and workable. The five management techniques provide a practical toolkit for composers dealing with polytonal density.

# Common Errors
- Allowing secondary textures to become unworkably thick
- Neglecting to identify structural pillars
- Treating all polytonal sonorities as equal rather than hierarchical

# Common Confusions
- **Pillars vs. passing**: Not all polytonal sonorities are structural; some are passing textures that serve the pillars
- **Thinning is not simplifying**: Omitting chord members or inserting unison passages maintains polytonal integrity while reducing density
- **Ostinato function**: In polytonal contexts, ostinatos serve primarily as clarifying agents, not merely as rhythmic devices
