# Quick Definition

The employment of chords as rhythmic, percussive events through reiteration, rhythmic accentuation, or timbral treatment that emphasizes attack over sustained sonority.

# Formal Definition

**Percussive use of harmony** refers to the treatment of chords primarily as rhythmic events rather than sustained harmonic structures. Accents may be produced by any material that calls attention to itself through stress, duration, pitch level, tone quality, relative harmonic values, or repetition. The tonal equivalent of percussion rhythms (instruments of indeterminate pitch) is the melodic repeated note, and reiteration of melodic tones is a rhythmic force that often stimulates repeated-chord activity.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Percussive harmonic techniques:
- **Chordal repetition**: Reiterated chords as rhythmic stimulant in slow harmonic rhythm
- **Drum-figure response**: Chord functioning percussively in answer to drum pattern
- **Harmonic sforzando**: Sudden large compound chord or low cluster for accent
- **Harmonic accent**: Chord outside key realm or sudden increase in voice number
- **Intervallic percussiveness**: Smaller intervals at chord bottom creating attack quality

Factors creating harmonic accent:
- Pitch (register extremes)
- Intensity (dynamics)
- Color (timbre, instrumentation)
- Duration (short attacks)
- Relative harmonic values (contrast with context)
- Repetition (insistent reiteration)

# Musical Context/Application

Percussive harmony serves:
- Creating rhythmic drive in slow harmonic rhythm passages
- Answering actual percussion with pitched percussion
- Providing harmonic accent and punctuation
- Generating excitement through rhythmic chord treatment

Instrumental considerations:
- Low notes on miscellaneous instruments accentuate percussiveness as pitch projection is dampened
- Specific instrumental colors enhance or reduce percussive quality
- Articulation markings affect percussive character

# Common Confusions

- **Percussive vs. sustained harmony**: The same chord can be treated percussively or sustained; treatment determines function.
- **Harmony vs. rhythm distinction**: Percussive harmony blurs the line between harmonic and rhythmic function.
- **Pitched vs. unpitched**: Percussive harmony uses pitched chords but treats them with rhythmic emphasis like unpitched percussion.
