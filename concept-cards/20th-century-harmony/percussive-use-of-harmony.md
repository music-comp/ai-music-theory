# Quick Definition
The employment of chords as rhythmic, percussive events through reiteration, rhythmic accentuation, or timbral treatment that emphasizes attack over sustained sonority.

# Core Definition
Accents may be produced by any material that calls attention to itself through stress, duration, pitch level, tone quality, relative harmonic values, or repetition. Rhythm, both accentual and durational, can serve as a chief compositional element. In answer to a drum figure, a chord may function percussively. An isolated chord may command attention when substituting for a bass drum stroke. If the chord is a sudden large compound chord or a low cluster, a **harmonic sforzando** may be produced. The introduction of a chord outside the key realm or a sudden increase in the number of parts may also produce harmonic accent. Smaller intervals placed at the bottom of chords produce harmonic percussiveness. Miscellaneous instruments sounding their lower notes accentuate percussiveness as pitch projection is dampened (Ch. 10, pp. 219-222).

# Key Properties
1. Six accent-producing factors: stress, duration, pitch level, tone quality, relative harmonic values, repetition
2. Chord may answer drum figures percussively
3. Harmonic sforzando: sudden large compound chord or low cluster
4. Chord outside key realm creates harmonic accent
5. Sudden voice-count increase creates harmonic accent
6. Smaller intervals at chord bottom create percussive quality
7. Low-register instruments accentuate percussiveness (dampened pitch projection)

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify chords treated as rhythmic events rather than sustained harmonies
- Note accent-producing factors at work
- Look for harmonic sforzandos (sudden compound chords or clusters)
- Check for low-register intervallic compression creating percussive attack

# Context & Application
Percussive harmony creates rhythmic drive in slow harmonic rhythm, answers actual percussion with pitched percussion, provides harmonic accent and punctuation, and generates excitement through rhythmic chord treatment.

# Common Errors
- Treating all chords as sustained harmonic events when percussive treatment would serve better
- Neglecting low-register placement as a percussive resource

# Common Confusions
- **Percussive vs. sustained harmony**: Same chord can be treated either way; treatment determines function
- **Harmony vs. rhythm**: Percussive harmony blurs the line between harmonic and rhythmic function
