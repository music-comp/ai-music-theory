# Quick Definition
The metamorphosis of thematic material through various compositional procedures, revealing hidden meanings and creating unified musical expression from initial ideas.

# Formal Definition
Careful inventory of melodic, rhythmic, and harmonic aspects of thematic ideas must be taken, because as a work progresses these elements are often used independently of each other. Not until full thematic intelligibility is realized do hidden meanings of the theme come to the surface. The composer must be fully aware of the potentialities of his material so that full advantage can be taken of the phenomenon of thematic transformation. Thematic meaningfulness can arise only from thematic unity and purpose.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Transformation resources:
- Melodic aspects used independently
- Rhythmic aspects used independently
- Harmonic aspects used independently
- Combination of selected elements

Requirements for effective transformation:
- Full awareness of material's potentialities
- Thematic unity and purpose
- Nourishment by motivic constituents
- Aural retention of original identity

Transformation outcomes:
- Hidden meanings surface through development
- Unified expression unfolds from varied treatments
- Formal shape influenced by transformative discoveries

# Musical Context/Application
If a theme is not nourished by its motivic constituents it will lack significance. The impulse to deviate from the predictable must simultaneously create a feeling of inevitability. Conflicting formal elements often result in free and imaginative forms, altering the whole course of a major work. Association of musical ideas is created by the melodic motif, harmonic succession, and rhythmic pattern; none becomes functional until transformed by the creative musical process.

# Examples
Works demonstrating thematic transformation:
- Bela Bartok, String Quartet No. 4 (Boosey)
- Alban Berg, Violin Concerto (Universal)
- Aaron Copland, Vitebsk (Cos Cob)
- Roy Harris, Symphony No. 3 (G. Schirmer)
