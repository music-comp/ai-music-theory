# Quick Definition
The instrumental or vocal resources for which music is conceived, which directly affects how harmonic materials function and are perceived.

# Formal Definition
The medium to which a musical idea is given has a direct bearing upon harmonic writing, as do intervallic texture, spacing, and dynamics. Quality of tone as defined by medium plays a functional role in harmonic movement. Harmonic writing should be conceived for the medium employed, not abstracted and then assigned to instruments.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Medium affects harmony through:
- Timbre (tone color): softens or intensifies intervals
- Register: determines available spacing
- Attack characteristics: affects harmonic clarity
- Sustain capability: affects voice leading perception
- Blend vs. contrast: affects harmonic unity

Timbral transformations:
- Orchestral color may soften extreme dissonance
- Orchestral color may rob triads of soft consonance
- High register/harmonics add brilliance to parallel harmony
- Clusters may be loosened by distributing to separate choirs

# Musical Context/Application
Awareness of timbre is essential to good harmonic craft. Orchestral color may soften the extreme dissonance of thirteenth chords, rob the triad of its soft consonance, or alter chordal direction. Polychords are made transparent when each triad is sounded by a separate orchestral section. Pizzicato strings define uncertain passing tones; a harp may underline obscure rhythms.

# Common Confusions
Students often write harmony abstractly (e.g., at the piano) and then orchestrate. Persichetti emphasizes that harmonic writing must be conceived for the specific medium, as the same chord progression functions entirely differently across different instruments.
