# Quick Definition
The instrumental or vocal resources for which music is conceived, which directly affect how harmonic materials function and are perceived, making timbre a functional element of harmonic writing.

# Core Definition
The medium to which a musical idea is given has a direct bearing upon harmonic writing, as do intervallic texture, spacing, and dynamics. The same chordal progression conceived in completely different media results in completely different kinds of harmonic settings, with chordal tension, spacing, and dynamics changing drastically. Awareness of timbre is essential to good harmonic craft. Harmonic writing should be conceived for the medium employed, not abstracted and then assigned to instruments.

# Key Properties
1. **Orchestral color may**: soften extreme dissonance, rob triads of soft consonance, alter chordal direction
2. **High register/harmonics**: add brilliance to parallel harmony
3. **Clusters**: may be loosened by distributing to separate orchestral choirs (seconds sound as thirds)
4. **Polychords**: made transparent when each unit sounded by separate orchestral section
5. **Pizzicato strings**: define uncertain passing tones
6. **Harp**: underlines obscure rhythms
7. **Overtone content of instruments**: affects perceived consonance (high overtones = resonant but dissonant; low overtones = consonant but less resonant)

# Construction / Recognition
1. Assess the intended medium before writing harmony
2. Consider how the instrument's timbre will modify interval tensions
3. Use orchestral distribution to clarify or enrich harmonic textures
4. Match or oppose timbral character with interval tension for expressive purposes

# Context & Application
Music written for the piano is effective on the piano, but that written for orchestra gives an improper impression when played on the piano. Sharp dissonances on double reeds playing loudly create a raucous effect (tension agreement); the same intervals on muted strings sound introspective (tension disagreement). Both agreement and disagreement between timbre and tension are valid expressive resources.

- Ex. 1-24: same chord progression in different media producing entirely different harmonic settings

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Writing harmony at the piano and then orchestrating. **Correction**: Harmonic writing must be conceived for the specific medium, as the same progression functions differently across instruments.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking orchestration is "dressing up" pre-composed harmony. **Clarification**: Medium is a functional part of harmonic writing, not a surface decoration applied after the fact.
