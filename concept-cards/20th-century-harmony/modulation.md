# Quick Definition
The process of changing from one tonal center to another, using pivot chords, chromatic motion, or other techniques to establish the new key.

# Formal Definition
Modulation is the process of changing the tonal center. The ambiguity of any chord is such that it can be related to any of the twelve tonal centers, making modulation inherently flexible. If a definite key change is desired, both keys should be firmly established by at least three center-affirming chords. Continuous, quickly shifting keys produce a sense of displaced tonality rather than modulation.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Modulation types:
- **Pivot chord modulation**: Common chord serves both keys
  - Diatonic pivot: Belongs to both scale formations
  - Chromatic pivot: Belongs to neither or only one
- **Chromatic modulation**: Entire chord shifts half step in parallel motion
- **Intervallic modulation**: Key change by specific interval (second, third, etc.)
- **Transient modulation**: Brief temporary key changes for variety

Modulation principles:
- Three center-affirming chords establish new key
- Aim for chords other than tonic to allow settling time
- Jump to distant key, work gradually to desired key to avoid premature cadence
- All keys have twelve tones in common chromatically

# Musical Context/Application
When modulating to a permanent key, chords other than the tonic may be aimed for so that time can be made for settling into the new tonality. A premature cadence is avoided by jumping to a distant key, then working gradually toward the desired key. Transient modulation provides variety in consonant passages but is less effective in very dissonant passages. Distant tonal centers are reached in countless ways, but unless this facile technique is held in check, harmonic growth will be replaced by superficial variety.

# Common Confusions
- Quick key shifts create displaced tonality, not clear modulation
- Chromatic modulation needs extended progression to secure new center
- Modulatory interval can contrast with prevailing root progression interval
