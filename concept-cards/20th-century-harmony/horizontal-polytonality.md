# Quick Definition
Polytonality established through imitative or contrapuntal writing where independent melodic lines operate in different keys.

# Formal Definition
Polytonality may be established through imitative writing (horizontal polytonality) as contrasted with chordal polytonality. Real canons at intervals other than the octave can imply polytonality when each voice operates in its own tonal area. Two or three melodic lines may produce transparent polytonality through their linear independence. The horizontal approach creates polytonal texture through melodic rather than chordal means.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Horizontal polytonality mechanisms:
- **Real canon at non-octave intervals**: Each voice in different key
- **Independent melodic lines**: Two or three lines in different tonalities
- **Imitative entries**: Each voice entering in its own key area

Characteristics:
- More transparent than chordal polytonality
- Melodic lines maintain individual key identities
- Harmonic implications arise from linear intersection
- Two or three lines can create clear polytonal effect

# Musical Context/Application
Horizontal polytonality produces a more transparent and contrapuntal texture than chordal polytonality. The polytonal relationship emerges from the interaction of independent melodic lines rather than from the superposition of harmonic layers. Real canons at intervals other than the octave naturally produce polytonal implications, making traditional contrapuntal techniques a resource for modern polytonal writing.
