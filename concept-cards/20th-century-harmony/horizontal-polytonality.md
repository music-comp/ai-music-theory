# Quick Definition
Polytonality established through imitative or contrapuntal writing where independent melodic lines operate in different keys, producing a more transparent texture than chordal polytonality, with real canons at non-octave intervals as a natural source.

# Core Definition
Polytonality may be established through imitative writing (horizontal polytonality). Real canons at intervals other than the octave can imply polytonality when each voice operates in its own tonal area. Two or three melodic lines may produce transparent polytonality through their linear independence. The horizontal approach creates polytonal texture through melodic rather than chordal means.

# Prerequisites
- **Polytonality**: The broader multi-key technique

# Key Properties
1. Melodic lines establish key identities rather than chordal progressions
2. Real canons at non-octave intervals naturally imply polytonality
3. Two or three lines can create transparent polytonal effect
4. More transparent than chordal polytonality
5. Harmonic implications arise from linear intersection
6. Each voice maintains individual key identity

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify independent melodic lines operating in different tonalities
- Look for real canonic imitation at intervals other than the octave
- Note the transparency of texture compared to chordal polytonality
- Verify that each line maintains a clear scale formation
- Distinguish from non-polytonal counterpoint (where lines share a single key)

# Context & Application
Horizontal polytonality produces a more transparent and contrapuntal texture than chordal polytonality. Real canons at intervals other than the octave naturally produce polytonal implications, making traditional contrapuntal techniques a resource for modern polytonal writing. The polytonal relationship emerges from the interaction of independent melodic lines rather than from the superposition of harmonic layers.

# Common Errors
- Assuming all canons create polytonality (only non-octave real canons do)
- Expecting the same density as chordal polytonality
- Failing to maintain each line's key identity

# Common Confusions
- **Real vs. tonal canons**: Only real canons (exact transposition) at non-octave intervals produce polytonality; tonal canons adjust intervals to stay in one key
- **Transparency vs. weakness**: Horizontal polytonality is more transparent, not weaker, than chordal
- **Harmonic implications**: The vertical combinations are secondary to the linear key identities
