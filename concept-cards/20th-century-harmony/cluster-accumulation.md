# Quick Definition
The building of large clusters through the additive use of chords by thirds, fourths, polychords, or smaller clusters, creating massive sonorities through gradual accumulation rather than simultaneous attack.

# Formal Definition
Large clusters may accumulate through the additive use of chords by thirds or fourths, polychords, or smaller clusters. This technique allows clusters to grow organically from smaller, more familiar harmonic materials.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Accumulation sources:
1. **Chords by thirds**: Tertian additions that gradually fill in
2. **Chords by fourths**: Quartal additions creating cluster texture
3. **Polychords**: Multi-unit structures that combine to form cluster
4. **Smaller clusters**: Cluster units that merge into larger cluster

Process:
1. Begin with smaller, identifiable harmonic unit
2. Add successive units (same or different type)
3. Allow sustained tones to accumulate
4. Result: large cluster built from discrete additions

Relationship to ornamental tones:
- Ornamental tones increase harmonic circulation in cluster passages
- Passing tones, neighbor tones, etc. add movement within static clusters
- Ornamental activity freshens otherwise static texture

# Musical Context/Application
Cluster accumulation provides a compositional pathway from familiar harmonic materials to complex cluster sonorities. Rather than presenting clusters as abrupt, foreign sounds, the technique integrates them into broader harmonic development.

This approach is particularly valuable for:
- Creating climactic passages through gradual density increase
- Integrating cluster harmony with tertian/quartal contexts
- Providing formal articulation through accumulative processes
- Making dense harmony accessible through audible construction

The technique also allows combination of cluster harmony with functional root relationships when clusters or chords by seconds appear with chords by thirds or fourths.

# Common Confusions
- Accumulation is a process, not a chord type
- The component harmonies may remain audible or merge completely
- Ornamental tones are not accumulation; they add movement, not mass
- Functional root relationships are possible even with cluster presence
