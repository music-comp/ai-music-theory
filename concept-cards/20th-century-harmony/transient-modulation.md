# Quick Definition
Brief, temporary key changes that provide harmonic variety within a passage without establishing a permanent new tonal center.

# Formal Definition
Transient modulation is an important means of securing variety in relatively consonant passages. It is less effective in very dissonant passages because chromatically complex harmony does not need the color of transitory modulation. In chromatically complex contexts, modulation is used to move from one section to another rather than within a progression itself. Modulation from a single tonality to polytonality may be attained by moving double strands of transient modulation in contrary motion of keys.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Transient modulation characteristics:
- **Duration**: Brief, temporary
- **Purpose**: Provides variety and color
- **Context effectiveness**:
  - Most effective in consonant passages
  - Less effective in dissonant passages
  - Chromatically complex harmony needs modulation for sectional moves only

Polytonal application:
- Double strands of transient modulation in contrary key motion
- Coupled tonic as excellent starting point
- Each key zone moves to new zone independently

# Musical Context/Application
Transient modulation adds harmonic color and variety to passages that might otherwise feel static. The brief visits to other keys enliven the texture without disrupting the overall tonal framework. In already complex chromatic harmony, transient modulation becomes redundant because the chromaticism itself provides variety. The technique can be expanded to create polytonal textures through contrary key motion.
