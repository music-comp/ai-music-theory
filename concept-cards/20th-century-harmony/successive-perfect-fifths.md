# Quick Definition
Consecutive perfect fifth intervals between voices, an important harmonic ingredient in contemporary composition that requires creative handling to prevent intervallic uniformity from becoming tiresome.

# Core Definition
Unless used imaginatively, successive perfect fifths become tiresome; the intervallic uniformity of any combination of notes moving in parallel motion challenges a composer's inventiveness. Fifths are emotionally immense, vague, and distant, or bare and dominating -- they form an important harmonic ingredient in contemporary composition. They are found in two-part writing, coupled two-part writing, parallel harmony, at sequential pattern joints, and at phrase joints. Although perfect fifths make independence of parts difficult to achieve, their function in the vast tonal reserve is important. As part of a moving chordal body of sound they can be used without dominating the general texture (Ch. 9, pp. 201-205).

# Prerequisites
- **Parallel harmony** -- understanding parallel voice movement

# Key Properties
1. Emotionally immense, vague, distant, or bare and dominating
2. Found in: two-part writing, coupled two-part writing, parallel harmony, sequential joints, phrase joints
3. Make voice independence difficult to achieve
4. Important harmonic ingredient despite traditional prohibition
5. Seven specific techniques push fifths to background
6. Chromatic fifths may be used more freely with contrary voice motion
7. Skipping fifths subdued when they leap to same chord members

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify parallel fifth motion between any two voices
- Note the context: two-part, coupled, parallel harmony, joints
- Check whether background techniques are employed to manage prominence
- Observe the emotional quality: immensity, vagueness, distance, or bare domination

# Context & Application
Background techniques: (1) Place conjunct fifths in lower voices with contrary/oblique upper parts; (2) Use rhythmically agile accompanying parts to divert attention; (3) Insert other intervals between some fifths; (4) Use contrary outer parts with fifths in inner parts; (5) Soften upper fifths by placing the third of the fifth in a lower voice; (6) Subdue skipping fifths by leaping to same chord members; (7) Use florid inner voices against outer-voice fifths.

# Common Errors
- Avoiding parallel fifths reflexively based on common-practice training
- Using extended parallel fifths without background techniques, causing monotony
- Placing fifths prominently without considering their dominating quality

# Common Confusions
- **Forbidden vs. resource**: Traditional prohibition stemmed from voice independence concerns; modern practice uses fifths as distinctive resource
- **Prominent vs. background**: The same interval can dominate or recede depending on treatment
- **Consecutive vs. simultaneous**: Successive fifths are parallel motion; simultaneous fifth is simply a chord interval
