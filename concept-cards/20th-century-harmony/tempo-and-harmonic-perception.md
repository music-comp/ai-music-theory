# Quick Definition

The relationship between tempo and how harmony is perceived, where quick tempo can make chord changes sound ornamental and simple chords moving rapidly can create complex sounds.

# Formal Definition

**Tempo** can be a determining factor in harmonic rhythm and perception. Quick tempo may cause chord changes to sound like ornamental chords rather than structural harmonic events. Simple chords moving at a high rate of speed may create relatively complex sounds as the rapid succession blurs individual chord identities. This principle reveals that harmonic complexity is partly a function of temporal presentation, not solely of chord construction.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Tempo effects on harmony:
- **Fast tempo + chord changes**: Changes perceived as ornamentation
- **Fast tempo + simple chords**: Create perception of complexity
- **Slow tempo + complex chords**: Individual chord character clarified
- **Repeated chords**: Do not create harmonic rhythm movement regardless of tempo

Perceptual thresholds:
- Below certain speeds, individual chords are clearly distinguished
- Above certain speeds, chords blend into composite sonorities
- The threshold varies by listener and context

# Musical Context/Application

Tempo considerations enable:
- Creating perceived complexity through simple means at fast tempos
- Clarifying complex harmony through slow presentation
- Using tempo as a compositional parameter affecting harmonic character
- Distinguishing structural from ornamental chord changes

Compositional applications:
- Fast passages with simple chords achieving rich texture
- Slow passages revealing intricate harmonic detail
- Tempo changes affecting harmonic perception without changing notes
- Strategic tempo choices for desired harmonic effect

# Common Confusions

- **Complexity source**: Perceived harmonic complexity can arise from tempo as much as from actual chord construction.
- **Ornamental vs. structural**: Tempo affects whether chord changes are perceived as ornamental details or structural events.
- **Score vs. sound**: What looks complex in score may sound simple at slow tempo; what looks simple may sound complex at fast tempo.
