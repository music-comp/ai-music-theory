# Quick Definition
The division of a scale into two four-note groups that may be similar or different, forming the structural basis of seven-tone scale construction.

# Formal Definition
The synthetic seven-tone, one-octave scale, like a major or minor, is made of a pair of four-note groups (tetrachords) that repeat the tonic at the eighth step. These tetrachords may be similar, as in major and Double Harmonic scales, or different, as in harmonic minor and Hungarian Major scales.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Tetrachord types by interval content:
- Major tetrachord: W-W-H (C-D-E-F)
- Minor tetrachord: W-H-W (C-D-Eb-F)
- Phrygian tetrachord: H-W-W (C-Db-Eb-F)
- Lydian tetrachord: W-W-W (C-D-E-F#)
- Harmonic tetrachord: H-A-H (contains augmented 2nd)

Scale construction:
- Lower tetrachord + upper tetrachord = 7-note scale
- Disjunct tetrachords (separated by whole step)
- Similar or dissimilar pairing

# Musical Context/Application
Understanding tetrachord construction allows composers to analyze and create synthetic scales systematically. The choice of similar vs. different tetrachords affects the scale's symmetry and harmonic characteristics. This principle extends the modal construction concept to all synthetic scales.

# Examples
- Major scale: major tetrachord + major tetrachord (similar)
- Harmonic minor: minor tetrachord + harmonic tetrachord (different)
- Double Harmonic: harmonic tetrachord + harmonic tetrachord (similar)
- Hungarian Major: lydian tetrachord + harmonic tetrachord (different)

# Common Confusions
Students may not recognize the tetrachordal structure underlying scales. Understanding this structure helps in analyzing unfamiliar scales and in creating coherent synthetic scales with specific characteristics.
