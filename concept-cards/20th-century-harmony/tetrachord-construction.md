# Quick Definition
The division of a scale into two four-note groups that may be similar or different, forming the structural basis of seven-tone scale construction.

# Core Definition
The synthetic seven-tone, one-octave scale, like a major or minor, is made of a pair of four-note groups (tetrachords) that repeat the tonic at the eighth step. These tetrachords may be similar, as in major and Double Harmonic scales, or different, as in harmonic minor and Hungarian Major scales. Understanding tetrachord construction allows systematic analysis and creation of synthetic scales.

# Key Properties
1. **Major tetrachord**: W-W-H (e.g., C-D-E-F)
2. **Minor tetrachord**: W-H-W (e.g., C-D-Eb-F)
3. **Phrygian tetrachord**: H-W-W (e.g., C-Db-Eb-F)
4. **Lydian tetrachord**: W-W-W (e.g., C-D-E-F#)
5. **Harmonic tetrachord**: H-A2-H (contains augmented 2nd)
6. **Scale = lower tetrachord + upper tetrachord** (separated by whole step in disjunct scales)
7. **Similar vs. dissimilar pairing** affects symmetry and harmonic character

# Construction / Recognition
1. Divide any seven-tone scale at the midpoint (after the 4th degree)
2. Identify the interval pattern of each four-note group
3. Classify each tetrachord by type
4. Note whether the pairing is similar (same type) or dissimilar (different types)
5. The junction between tetrachords is typically a whole step

# Context & Application
Tetrachord analysis reveals structural relationships between different scales. Similar tetrachords create symmetrical scales (like major); dissimilar tetrachords create asymmetrical scales with distinct upper and lower halves. This framework enables systematic creation of new scales by combining any two tetrachord types.

# Examples
- Major scale: major tetrachord + major tetrachord (similar)
- Harmonic minor: minor tetrachord + harmonic tetrachord (dissimilar)
- Double Harmonic: harmonic tetrachord + harmonic tetrachord (similar)
- Hungarian Major: lydian tetrachord + harmonic tetrachord (dissimilar)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Not recognizing the tetrachordal structure underlying familiar scales. **Correction**: Even major and minor scales are tetrachord pairs; this structure is universal.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking tetrachords must be "standard" types. **Clarification**: Any four-note group spanning a fourth (or other interval) can serve as a tetrachord for scale construction.
