# Quick Definition
The rearrangement of quartal chord members to place different notes in the bass, with inversions providing color variety through the prominence of the perfect fifth and preventing harmonic monotony from uniform fourth intervals.

# Formal Definition
In all types of three-note chords by fourths, two inversions are possible. Inverting chords having two perfect fourths helps prevent the harmonic monotony of uniform intervals. Either inversion can be used as a fundamental structure because of the presence of the strong perfect fifth. If the resonant interval of the perfect fifth is allowed to dominate the texture, the second (created by the inverted seventh) often sounds like a note added to a simple chordal formation. Positions featuring the perfect fifth give quartal harmony variety of color.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
**Inversion Effects:**
- Root position: fourths dominate
- First inversion: fifth appears between two upper voices
- Second inversion: fifth appears between two lower voices

**Four-Note Chord Inversions:**
Four-note quartal chords are extremely useful in their three inverted forms because of the variety of intervals they contain:
- Root position: three fourths
- Inversions: mixture of fourths, fifths, and seconds

**Movement Through Inversions:**
Moving through inversions of the same chord produces real harmonic movement without a root change.

**Open Spacing:**
The variety presented by open spacing increases harmonic expressiveness. Inversions combined with open voicing multiply the available colors.

# Musical Context/Application
Quartal chord inversions serve multiple purposes:
1. Breaking the monotony of stacked fourths
2. Introducing the resonant perfect fifth into the texture
3. Creating harmonic motion without changing roots
4. Providing color variety in sustained quartal passages

Four-note chords resolve easily to chords by thirds when two voices move conjunctly while the others remain stationary. Three-note quartal chords with a doubling are useful in this context.

# Common Confusions
Students may think quartal chord inversions function like tertian inversions with predictable bass-note effects. In quartal harmony, inversions primarily provide color variety and introduce fifths into the texture, rather than creating the hierarchical bass relationships of tertian inversions.
