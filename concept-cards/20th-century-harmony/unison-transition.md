# Quick Definition
The use of unison or octave passages as harmonically neutral bridges between different texture types, where the harmonic ambiguity of a single pitch line allows any new texture to emerge naturally.

# Core Definition
The doubling of a colorful tone (major third, major seventh, or prominent nonharmonic tone) strongly suggests octave or unison writing that in turn makes way for foreign harmonic resources. The unison can become a supple device for moving from one kind of texture to another. The harmonic ambiguity of sudden unison allows for the entrance of any texture. The unison effectively neutralizes the previous harmonic context, creating an open space from which any new texture can emerge.

# Prerequisites
- **Textural transition**: The broader transitional technique

# Key Properties
1. Doubling a colorful tone (M3, M7, prominent nonharmonic) suggests unison
2. Unison creates harmonic ambiguity -- neutralizes previous context
3. Any texture can emerge from a unison passage
4. Functions as a harmonically neutral bridge
5. Particularly effective when the doubled tone has been coloristically prominent

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify a colorful tone (major third, major seventh, prominent nonharmonic) in the current texture
- Double it at the octave, moving toward unison
- Use the unison as a pivot point for introducing the new texture
- The new texture can be any type -- the unison imposes no constraints

# Context & Application
The unison transition technique is one of the most versatile textural transition devices because it imposes no intervallic constraints on the new texture. Any texture can follow a unison. The technique works by collapsing harmonic complexity into a single pitch, creating a moment of harmonic suspension from which the composer is free to move in any direction. It is particularly effective at dramatic or structural turning points.

# Common Errors
- Using unison too frequently (becomes a crutch)
- Choosing a non-colorful tone for the doubling (less effective)
- Making the unison too brief to register as a harmonic reset

# Common Confusions
- **Harmonic reset, not gap**: The unison is an active transitional device, not merely a pause or gap
- **Colorful tone**: The doubling is most effective when the chosen tone has been prominent in the preceding texture
- **Universal pivot**: The unison can pivot to any texture because it has no harmonic content to contradict
