# Quick Definition
Seven scale patterns distinguished by their unique whole-step/half-step arrangements, each with a special character that twentieth-century composers exploit as independent scalar resources.

# Formal Definition
A central tone to which other tones are related can establish tonality, and the manner in which these other tones are placed around the central tone produces modality. Seven scale patterns stand apart from others because of their whole-half-step order: Ionian, Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Aeolian, and Locrian. While they bear medieval names, their twentieth-century usage differs fundamentally from medieval practice.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
The seven modes and their structures (W=whole, H=half):
- Ionian (major): W-W-H-W-W-W-H
- Dorian: W-H-W-W-W-H-W (natural minor with raised 6th)
- Phrygian: H-W-W-W-H-W-W (natural minor with lowered 2nd)
- Lydian: W-W-W-H-W-W-H (major with raised 4th)
- Mixolydian: W-W-H-W-W-H-W (major with lowered 7th)
- Aeolian (natural minor): W-H-W-W-H-W-W
- Locrian: H-W-W-H-W-W-W (diminished tonic triad)

# Musical Context/Application
The distinctive flavor of modes is exploited by employing harmonic progressions in which the characteristic scale step occurs often. This tone keeps the mode from becoming major or natural minor. For example, a lydian passage should contain many chords with the raised fourth step. Modes may be arranged from "darkest" (locrian) to "brightest" (lydian) based on signature order.

# Examples
- Sibelius, Symphony No. 6, pp. 3-4: Dorian writing
- Debussy, String Quartet, p. 3: Phrygian writing
- Britten, Seven Sonnets of Michelangelo, p. 14: Lydian writing
- Bartok, Piano Concerto No. 3, p. 3: Mixolydian writing
- Orff, Carmina Burana, p. 1: Aeolian writing

# Common Confusions
Students often think of modes as "scales starting on different notes of C major." In twentieth-century practice, modes are independent collections with their own tonic, harmonic vocabulary, and expressive character, not merely rotations of the major scale.
