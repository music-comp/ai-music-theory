# Quick Definition
Nonharmonic tones created when a tone from one chord is held over into the next, delaying the resolution and creating rhythmic tension through the three-part pattern of preparation-suspension-resolution.

# Core Definition
**Suspensions** belong to the hybrid group of nonharmonic tones formed by rhythmic misplacement of chord tones. In a suspension, movement is delayed: a tone belonging to the first chord is held while the harmony changes, creating dissonance against the new chord before resolving (typically by step downward). Suspensions represent chord tones displaced in time rather than foreign pitches (Ch. 11, pp. 229-231).

# Prerequisites
- **Nonharmonic tones** -- understanding the classification system

# Key Properties
1. Preparation: tone sounds as consonance in preceding chord
2. Suspension: same tone held while harmony changes, creating dissonance
3. Resolution: delayed tone moves (typically down by step)
4. Three-part pattern distinguishes suspensions from other nonharmonic types
5. Rhythmic displacement (delay) rather than foreign pitch
6. In twentieth-century practice, resolution may be to another dissonance

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify a tone that was consonant, is held across a harmonic change, then resolves
- Verify the three-part preparation-suspension-resolution pattern

# Context & Application
In twentieth-century harmony, suspensions may resolve to dissonant chord tones, creating chains of suspension. The held-tone quality relates suspensions to pedal point, though suspensions resolve while pedals persist.

# Common Errors
- Applying suspension without proper preparation
- Assuming resolution must always be downward by step in twentieth-century contexts

# Common Confusions
- Suspensions involve rhythmic delay; appoggiaturas involve melodic leap approach
- Suspensions must be prepared as consonances in the preceding chord
- In twentieth-century practice, resolution may be to another dissonance
