# Quick Definition
Nonharmonic tones created when a tone from one chord is held over into the next chord, delaying the resolution and creating rhythmic tension.

# Formal Definition
Suspensions belong to the hybrid group of nonharmonic tones formed by rhythmic misplacement of chord tones. In a suspension, movement is delayed: a tone belonging to the first chord is held while the harmony changes, creating dissonance against the new chord before resolving (typically by step downward). Suspensions represent chord tones displaced in time rather than foreign pitches.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Suspension characteristics:
- **Preparation**: Tone sounds as consonance in preceding chord
- **Suspension**: Same tone held while harmony changes, creating dissonance
- **Resolution**: Delayed tone moves (typically down by step) to chord tone
- **Rhythmic pattern**: Strong-beat dissonance resolving on weaker beat

The three-part pattern (preparation-suspension-resolution) distinguishes suspensions from other nonharmonic tones. The delay creates both melodic continuity and harmonic tension.

# Musical Context/Application
In twentieth-century harmony, suspensions operate within expanded harmonic vocabularies. The resolution may be to a dissonant chord tone, creating chains of suspended motion. Suspensions are particularly effective in preparing intervallic textures when moving between different types of harmony. The suspension's held-tone quality relates it to pedal point techniques, though suspensions resolve while pedals persist.

# Common Confusions
- Suspensions involve rhythmic delay; appoggiaturas involve melodic leap approach
- Suspensions must be prepared as consonances in the preceding chord
- In 20th-century practice, resolution may be to another dissonance
