# Quick Definition
Minor seconds and major sevenths, which produce an intense, cutting sound quality representing the most tension-filled intervals in the chromatic scale.

# Core Definition
Sharp dissonances are the minor second and major seventh intervals, characterized by their intense, biting quality derived from high partials in the overtone series. These intervals create maximum harmonic tension. When the cutting minor second is inverted, it opens into a wide-spreading major seventh and thereby loses some of its sting. When spaced beyond an octave, sharp dissonances become less biting yet more brilliant. A doubled dissonant tone increases the bite.

# Key Properties
1. **Minor second**: ~16:15 ratio, the most biting interval
2. **Major seventh**: ~15:8 ratio, wide-spreading intensity
3. **Inversion relationship**: m2 opens to M7, losing sting but gaining spread
4. **Compound spacing**: less biting, more brilliant
5. **Doubling effect**: doubled dissonant tones increase the bite
6. **Timbral interaction**: raucous on loud double reeds; introspective on muted strings

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify intervals of 1 semitone (m2) or 11 semitones (M7)
2. Note the intense, cutting quality
3. Observe how inversion transforms sting into spread
4. Assess how timbre (instrument choice) modifies the perceived intensity

# Context & Application
Sharp dissonances seem consistent in double reeds playing loudly, creating a raucous effect. The same intervals assigned to muted strings create an introspective quality -- one impression of tension agreement with other forces, the other of disagreement. In consistently dissonant harmony, sharp dissonances may become the "consonant" norm. They are essential colors in twentieth-century vocabulary, not problems to be resolved.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Avoiding sharp dissonances as unusable. **Correction**: They are essential 20th-century colors that may be sustained, emphasized, or used as stable sonorities.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Treating m2 and M7 as equivalent. **Clarification**: While inversionally related, the m2 has concentrated sting while the M7 has wide-spreading intensity -- different characters despite shared tension level.
