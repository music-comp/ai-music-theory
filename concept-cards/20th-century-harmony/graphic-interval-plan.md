# Quick Definition

A chord construction approach where inner logic of interval arrangement takes precedence over intervallic tension, organizing intervals according to visual or numerical patterns.

# Core Definition

"Some compound chords are characterized by an inner graphic plan rather than an arrangement of interval tensions. The important aspect of a chord of this kind is the logic of its inner construction rather than the motivating force of intervallic tension. Some such chords contain all twelve chromatic tones and eleven symmetrically invertible intervals" (Persichetti, p. 163). "Other chords of this kind are arranged in alternately odd- or even-numbered intervals counted in semitones" (p. 164).

# Prerequisites

- **Compound chord** — Graphic plans are an organizational approach for compound chords

# Key Properties

1. Inner construction logic takes precedence over tension arrangement
2. May contain all twelve chromatic tones with eleven invertible intervals
3. Alternating odd/even semitone patterns possible
4. Organizing principle is structural/conceptual, not perceptual
5. The graphic plan may not be audible but provides compositional coherence

# Construction / Recognition

## To Apply a Graphic Plan
1. Choose an organizational principle (numerical sequence, alternating pattern, symmetry)
2. Stack intervals according to the chosen scheme
3. Verify the internal logic is consistent
4. The resulting sonority need not sound like its organizational principle

# Context & Application

Graphic interval plans provide systematic basis for complex chord construction, internal logic for compositional coherence, freedom from tension-based thinking, and material for serial and systematic approaches. These work well when intellectual structure supports musical intent.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Expecting the graphic plan to be audible
  **Correction**: The plan serves compositional logic; it may or may not be perceptible to listeners

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Graphic plans and tension-based plans are mutually exclusive
  **Clarification**: Both are valid approaches; a chord may exhibit both qualities simultaneously
- **Confusion**: Graphic plans are arbitrary
  **Clarification**: They follow strict internal logic; the organizing principle is systematic even if non-tonal
