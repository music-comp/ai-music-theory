# Quick Definition

A chord construction approach where the inner logic of interval arrangement takes precedence over intervallic tension, organizing intervals according to visual or numerical patterns.

# Formal Definition

A **graphic interval plan** refers to compound chords characterized by an inner organizational scheme rather than an arrangement based on intervallic tension. In these chords, the important aspect is the logic of inner construction rather than the motivating force of consonance or dissonance. Such chords may be organized according to visual patterns, numerical sequences, or symmetrical schemes, including arrangements containing all twelve chromatic tones and eleven symmetrically invertible intervals.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Examples of graphic interval plans:
- **Chromatic aggregate**: All twelve tones with eleven different interval classes
- **Alternating patterns**: Intervals alternating between odd and even semitone counts
- **Numerical sequences**: Intervals following arithmetic progressions
- **Overtone modeling**: Intervals arranged to mirror the harmonic series
- **Symmetrical schemes**: Intervals arranged for visual or mathematical symmetry

The organizing principle is structural/conceptual rather than based on perceived consonance or dissonance.

# Musical Context/Application

Graphic interval plans provide:
- Systematic basis for complex chord construction
- Internal logic that may or may not be audible but provides compositional coherence
- Freedom from traditional tension-based thinking
- Material for serial and systematic compositional approaches

These chords work well when:
- Intellectual structure supports the musical intent
- The composer seeks alternatives to tension-based organization
- Complex sonorities require internal logic for practical management

# Common Confusions

- **Graphic vs. tension-based**: Graphic plans organize by pattern; tension-based plans organize by consonance/dissonance. Both are valid approaches.
- **Audibility**: The graphic plan may not be audible as such; it serves compositional logic rather than perceptual organization.
- **Strict vs. flexible**: A graphic plan provides a framework that may be applied strictly or used as a point of departure.
