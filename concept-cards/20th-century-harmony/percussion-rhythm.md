# Quick Definition

Rhythmic forces generated by percussion instruments or vocal sounds of indeterminate pitch, which may coincide with or oppose melodic and harmonic rhythms.

# Formal Definition

**Percussion rhythm** refers to rhythmic forces that may sprout from the percussion line of instruments or vocal sounds of indeterminate pitches. These forces interact with melodic and harmonic rhythms, either coinciding with them or opposing them in various ways. The relationship between percussion rhythm and pitched rhythm creates a dimension of rhythmic counterpoint in twentieth-century composition.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Percussion rhythm relationships:
1. **Coinciding**: Percussion aligns with melodic and harmonic rhythm
2. **Opposing melodic rhythm**: Percussion contradicts melodic accents
3. **Opposing harmonic rhythm**: Percussion contradicts harmonic changes
4. **Independent**: Percussion operates as separate rhythmic layer

Combination possibilities:
- Melodic and percussive rhythm opposing harmonic rhythm
- Harmonic and percussive rhythm opposing melodic rhythm
- All three layers in independent relationship
- Various partial alignments

# Musical Context/Application

Percussion rhythm serves:
- Adding rhythmic dimension independent of pitch
- Creating rhythmic counterpoint with pitched material
- Establishing pulse or groove
- Providing accentual punctuation

The interaction enables:
- Complex layered rhythmic textures
- Tension through rhythmic disagreement
- Resolution through rhythmic alignment
- Varied textural density through rhythmic activity levels

# Common Confusions

- **Percussion vs. percussive harmony**: Percussion rhythm uses unpitched instruments; percussive harmony treats pitched chords percussively.
- **Independence vs. chaos**: Independent percussion rhythm maintains its own coherent pattern; it does not randomly interrupt.
- **Rhythmic hierarchy**: Percussion rhythm may lead, follow, or exist in equal partnership with pitched rhythms.
