# Quick Definition
The perfect fifth and octave, which produce a spacious, stable sound due to their position as the lowest partials in the overtone series.

# Formal Definition
Open consonances are the perfect fifth and octave intervals, characterized by their strong relationship to the fundamental tone through the overtone series. The fifth (partial 3) is a lower partial than the third (partial 5) and consequently is more powerful; this is an important factor in understanding relationships of tones, chords, and tonalities.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
- Perfect octave: 2:1 frequency ratio (partial 2)
- Perfect fifth: 3:2 frequency ratio (partial 3)
- When spaced more than an octave apart, open consonances become stronger
- The fifth's power derives from being a lower, stronger overtone than the third

# Musical Context/Application
Open consonances provide stability and clarity in harmonic structures. When building resonant harmony, the fifth serves as the primary supporting interval. Basses plucking the fifth or ninth below the actual bass line cast a reflected sheen over the harmony. Wide spacing in lower registers using open consonances creates the most resonant chord voicings.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes confuse open consonances with "empty" harmony. While perfect fifths and octaves lack the color of thirds, they provide structural clarity and resonance that is essential in many twentieth-century textures.
