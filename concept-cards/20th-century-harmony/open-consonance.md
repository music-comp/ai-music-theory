# Quick Definition
The perfect fifth and octave, which produce a spacious, stable sound due to their position as the lowest and strongest partials in the overtone series.

# Core Definition
Open consonances are the perfect fifth and octave intervals. The fifth (partial 3) is a lower partial than the third (partial 5) and consequently is more powerful; this is an important factor in understanding relationships of tones, chords, and tonalities. The fifth's strength makes it the primary supporting interval for resonant harmony. When spaced more than an octave apart, open consonances become stronger rather than weaker.

# Key Properties
1. **Perfect octave**: 2:1 frequency ratio (partial 2) -- strongest consonance
2. **Perfect fifth**: 3:2 frequency ratio (partial 3) -- most powerful after the octave
3. **Wide spacing effect**: become stronger when spaced beyond an octave
4. **Structural role**: provide clarity and stability in harmonic structures
5. **The fifth's power**: being a lower partial than the third, the fifth is more powerful -- critical for understanding tonal and chordal relationships

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify intervals of 7 semitones (P5) or 12 semitones (P8)
2. Note their spacious, "open" quality lacking the warmth of thirds
3. In chords, identify how fifths and octaves provide structural foundation
4. Recognize that wide-spaced fifths in lower registers create maximum resonance

# Context & Application
Open consonances provide stability and clarity. Wide spacing in lower registers using open consonances creates the most resonant chord voicings, following the overtone series model. Basses plucking the fifth or ninth below the actual bass line cast a reflected sheen over the harmony. In twentieth-century writing, open consonances may sound "empty" or "archaic" depending on context.

- Hindemith, *Symphonie Mathis der Maler*, p. 1 (Schott): resonant spacing with open consonances

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating open consonances as "empty" or lacking function. **Correction**: They provide structural clarity and resonance essential in many 20th-century textures.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Equating open consonance with "hollow fifths" of medieval music. **Clarification**: In 20th-century usage, open consonances serve specific textural and resonance functions quite different from medieval practice.
