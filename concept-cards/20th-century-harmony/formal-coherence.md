# Quick Definition
The unified organization of musical material through motivic relationships, harmonic logic, and rhythmic patterns that creates comprehensible musical form.

# Formal Definition
The compositional process is meaningless unless thematic statements are identifiable, for continuity and coherence are effected through aural retention of motifs. If chromatic chordal mixtures accumulate, formal coherence is achieved by repetition, variation, or mutation of the chromatic sound groups. Thematic meaningfulness can arise only from thematic unity and purpose; if a theme is not nourished by its motivic constituents it will lack significance.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Coherence mechanisms:
- **Aural retention**: Listener remembers and recognizes motifs
- **Repetition**: Direct recurrence of material
- **Variation**: Modified recurrence maintaining identity
- **Mutation**: Gradual transformation of material

In chromatic/atonal contexts:
- Repetition of chromatic sound groups
- Variation of chromatic formations
- Mutation of chromatic mixtures
- Extreme motivic concentration
- Constant reference to previous material

Requirements:
- Identifiable thematic statements
- Thematic unity and purpose
- Motivic nourishment
- Aural intelligibility

# Musical Context/Application
Formal coherence is essential regardless of harmonic language. In highly chromatic or atonal contexts, traditional tonal relationships cannot provide coherence, so motivic relationships become even more critical. The various elements in atonal music are tightly knit by extreme motivic concentration, with constant reference to previous material. Even in the most diverse textural combinations, coherence depends on perceptible relationships.

# Examples
Works demonstrating formal coherence through diverse means:
- Bela Bartok, Music for Strings, Percussion and Celesta (Boosey)
- Alban Berg, Lyric Suite (Universal)
- Elliott Carter, String Quartet No. 2 (Associated)
- Roy Harris, Symphony No. 3 (G. Schirmer)
- Anton Webern, Five Pieces for String Quartet Op. 5 (Universal)

# Common Confusions
- Coherence in atonal music relies on motivic, not tonal, relationships
- Repetition, variation, and mutation all contribute to coherence
- Thematic identity requires nourishment by motivic constituents
