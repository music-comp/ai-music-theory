# Quick Definition
Chords built exclusively from perfect fourths, which have a pentatonic flavor, are tonally ambiguous since any member can function as root (like diminished sevenths and augmented triads), and are chromatically more supple than mixed-fourth chords.

# Core Definition
Three-, four-, and five-note chords by perfect fourths have a pentatonic flavor. The five-note form contains all the steps of the diatonic pentatonic scale. Chords by perfect fourths are ambiguous in that, like all chords built by equidistant intervals, any member can function as the root. The indifference of this rootless harmony to tonality places the burden of key verification upon the voice with the most active melodic line. A succession of chords by perfect fourths does not fall within the intervallic make-up of any one scale; if to be fit into a scale, miscellaneous fourths (including augmented) must be used. Therefore perfect-fourth chords are chromatically more supple than mixed-fourth chords. Scale tones used as roots for perfect-fourth chords duplicate at different tonal levels, indicating polytonal potential.

# Prerequisites
- **Quartal harmony**: The overall system of fourth-built chords

# Key Properties
1. Pentatonic flavor (five-note form = complete pentatonic collection)
2. Rootless: any member can serve as root (equidistant intervals)
3. Key verification falls to the most active melodic line
4. Do not fit within any single scale (chromatically supple)
5. Polytonal potential (scale tones duplicated at different levels)
6. No semitones present (neither pentatonic nor perfect-fourth structures contain them)
7. Consonant through five notes; six notes introduces tritone (dissonant)

# Construction / Recognition
- 3-note: P4+P4 (spans m7) = pentatonic subset
- 4-note: P4+P4+P4 (spans m10) = pentatonic subset
- 5-note: P4+P4+P4+P4 (spans M13) = complete pentatonic collection
- Rearranged as scale: C-F-Bb-Eb-Ab = Ab-Bb-C-Eb-F (pentatonic)
- Successive perfect-fourth chords move chromatically, not diatonically

# Context & Application
The tonal ambiguity of perfect fourth stacks makes them valuable for creating harmonic suspense, supporting melodic writing that defines tonality, and achieving chromatic flexibility not possible with diatonic chords. Clarity is difficult in low registers; these chords flow most easily in upper voices (woodwinds, women's voices). A pedal point lessens dissonant tones' need for resolution.

# Common Errors
- Failing to recognize the pentatonic collection within quartal stacks (notes in fourth order vs. scalar order)
- Expecting root-based analysis to work with rootless formations

# Common Confusions
- **Rootlessness**: The "root" emerges from melodic context, not from the chord itself
- **Chromatic suppleness**: Perfect-fourth successions are more chromatically flexible precisely because they do not fit any single scale
