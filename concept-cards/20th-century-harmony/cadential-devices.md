# Quick Definition

The various harmonic, melodic, and rhythmic techniques used to create cadences in twentieth-century music, where the connotation of rest is achieved through organized patterns verified rhythmically.

# Formal Definition

A **cadence** is an organization of melody and harmony in time, having a connotation of rest. Cadential devices are the techniques by which this sense of rest or arrival is created. The cadence is formed harmonically through a pattern of chords and melodically through the direction of each voice, but both are positive forces only when verified rhythmically. Cadential breathing places may occur at phrase ends, section ends, and finally at the end of the piece.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Cadential root relationships:
- **Perfect cadences**: First chord progresses to tonic whose root lies the interval of the existing cyclical relationship below its own
  - Fifth cycle: Root down a fifth (or up a fourth)
  - Third cycle: Root down a third
  - Second cycle: Root down a second
- **Plagal equivalents**: Reverse of perfect relationships (root up instead of down)

Cadential factors:
- Two final roots set cadential quality regardless of chord complexity or inversion
- Dissonant tone remaining stationary creates passive quality
- Rhythmic placement of final chord affects strength
- Common tones avoided in chromatic cadences to strengthen tonic footing

# Musical Context/Application

Cadences may include any harmony type:
- Tertian, quartal, added-note, secundal
- Polychordal, compound, mirror
- Pandiatonic, twelve-note

Special considerations:
- Weak-beat final chords require balance through repeated notes, ties, or ornamentation
- Final tonic with positive tonality allows free note addition
- Undertones may be added without disturbing tonic meaning
- Final chord may match opening chord regardless of tonal relationships
- Multiple simultaneous keys may create multi-tonal cadence

# Common Confusions

- **Rhythm as verifier**: Harmonic and melodic cadential patterns are incomplete without rhythmic confirmation.
- **Complexity and cadence**: Even extremely complex chords can function cadentially if root relationship and rhythm are clear.
- **Multiple simultaneous cadences**: In bitonal or polytonal music, different keys may cadence simultaneously.
