# Quick Definition
The various harmonic, melodic, and rhythmic techniques used to create cadences in twentieth-century music, where the connotation of rest requires rhythmic verification of harmonic and melodic patterns.

# Core Definition
A **cadence** is an organization of melody and harmony in time, having a connotation of rest. The cadence is created harmonically through a pattern of chords, and melodically through the direction of each voice. Both are positive forces only when verified rhythmically. Regardless of the complexity or inversions of the cadential chords, the two final roots set the quality of the cadential feeling. The first chord in a two-chord cadence progresses "perfectly" to a tonic whose root lies the interval of the existing cyclical relationship below its own. Cadences may include any type of harmony: tertian, quartal, added-note, secundal, polychordal, compound, mirror, pandiatonic, or twelve-note (Ch. 9, pp. 205-208).

# Prerequisites
- **Cyclical relationship** -- understanding root-distance systems that determine cadential quality

# Key Properties
1. Cadence = organization of melody + harmony + time, with connotation of rest
2. Harmonic and melodic patterns require rhythmic verification to function as cadence
3. Two final roots set cadential quality regardless of chord complexity or inversion
4. Perfect cadence: root lies the interval of existing cycle below its own
5. Plagal equivalents: reverse direction
6. Stationary dissonant tone creates passive quality
7. Weak-beat final chord requires balance through repeated notes, ties, or ornamentation
8. Common tones avoided in chromatic cadences to strengthen tonic footing
9. Final tonic with positive tonality allows free note addition (including undertones)
10. Cadences may involve any harmony type

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the two final roots of a cadential passage
- Determine which cyclical relationship they express
- Verify rhythmic confirmation of the cadence
- Note any passive quality from stationary dissonant tones
- Check weak-beat placement and compensating techniques

# Context & Application
Under certain formal conditions, the final cadence may find that a prominent subject in a different key has made a bid for a new tonality, causing a cadence of two or more simultaneous keys. The final chord may be identical with the opening chord regardless of tonal relationships. Temporary (deceptive) cadences rest briefly on a harmonic slant, creating need for continuance.

# Common Errors
- Neglecting rhythmic verification of harmonic cadential patterns
- Assuming traditional cadence types are the only options
- Using common tones in chromatic cadences (weakens tonic footing)

# Common Confusions
- **Rhythm as verifier**: Harmonic and melodic cadential patterns are incomplete without rhythmic confirmation
- **Complexity and cadence**: Even extremely complex chords can function cadentially if root relationship and rhythm are clear
- **Multiple simultaneous cadences**: In polytonal music, different keys may cadence simultaneously
