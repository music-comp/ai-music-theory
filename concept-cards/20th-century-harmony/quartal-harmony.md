# Quick Definition
A harmonic system in which chords are built by superimposing intervals of the fourth, creating a distinctly twentieth-century sound that stems from both triadic ornamentation and medieval polyphony, with most chord members placed a fourth apart to preserve the distinctive quartal character.

# Core Definition
Twentieth-century composers use quartal harmony (chords by fourths) as well as the tertian harmony (chords by thirds) of Classic and Romantic practice. Quartal materials stem from ornamentation of the triad (suspensions, appoggiaturas) and from the techniques of medieval polyphony (organum). Chords by fourths are built by superimposing intervals of the fourth. In other spacings, most of the chord members must be placed a fourth apart in order to preserve the distinctive quartal sound; otherwise the quartal structures may sound like eleventh, thirteenth, or added-note chords. This rootless harmony places the burden of key verification upon the most active melodic line.

# Prerequisites
- **Extended tertian harmony**: Understanding the tertian system that quartal harmony supplements

# Key Properties
1. Built by superimposing fourths (perfect and/or augmented)
2. Historical origins: triadic ornamentation + medieval polyphony
3. Spacing must preserve fourths to maintain quartal identity
4. Rootless: any member can function as root (like diminished seventh, augmented triad)
5. Tonality verified by the most active melodic line
6. Pentatonic flavor in perfect-fourth stacks
7. Flows more easily in upper registers (woodwinds, women's voices)
8. Can combine with tertian harmony through transitional devices

# Construction / Recognition
- Stack fourths upward from the bass
- Verify that most members remain a fourth apart in any respacing
- If thirds predominate in a rearranged chord: sounds tertian, not quartal
- If the chord loses its quartal sound in open spacing: restructure
- Perfect-fourth stacks = pentatonic flavor; augmented-fourth inclusion = tritone tension

# Context & Application
Quartal harmony offers a distinctly non-tertian vocabulary. Unlike tertian chords with clear root functions, quartal chords are ambiguous in root identity and more freely mobile. The open, "hollow" quality of stacked fourths contrasts with the fuller sound of thirds. Quartal and tertian harmonies can be combined through devices that emphasize the perfect fourth in tertian structures. Quartal chords can function as "dominants" in cadences of any harmonic idiom. A pedal point lessens dissonant tones' need for resolution.

# Common Errors
- Confusing quartal chords with inverted tertian chords or added-note formations
- Respacing quartal chords so that thirds predominate (destroying quartal identity)

# Common Confusions
- **Quartal vs. rearranged tertian**: A true quartal chord maintains fourth-based structure as its defining characteristic
- **Rootlessness**: Unlike tertian chords where the root is acoustically reinforced, quartal stacks have no inherent root preference
