# Quick Definition
A chord containing the interval of an augmented sixth, which has strong directional pull and serves as an ideal foundation for added-note techniques in twentieth-century harmony.

# Formal Definition
The augmented sixth is a chord whose motivating power derives from the augmented sixth interval, which has a natural tendency to expand, descend chromatically, or move obliquely (one voice held). Twentieth-century practice employs five basic augmented sixth chord types to which seconds may be attached.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Five basic augmented sixth types (from bass upward):
1. **Italian with Major Third**: Aug6 + M3 (traditional Italian)
2. **Minor Third variant**: Aug6 + m3 (color variant of Italian)
3. **Augmented Octave with Major Third**: Aug6 + A8 + M3 (ninth texture)
4. **Doubly Augmented Octave with Major Third**: Aug6 + AA8 + M3 (ninth texture)
5. **Augmented Third**: Aug6 + A3 (quartal context, often spelled enharmonically)

Resolution tendencies of Aug6 interval:
- Voices spanning the interval tend to expand outward
- Descend chromatically
- Move obliquely (one voice held)

The French augmented sixth (traditional) = Italian + M2 added above middle tone

# Musical Context/Application
The augmented sixth chord is an ideal candidate for added-note treatment because its directional pull is sufficiently strong to be unimpaired by the addition of color tones. The interval of the augmented sixth gives the chord its motivating power, while the interval a third from the bottom gives it its fundamental flavor.

Chords with augmented and doubly augmented octave forms are useful in passages featuring ninth, eleventh, and thirteenth chord textures. The augmented third variant is useful in quartal contexts.

# Common Confusions
- The French augmented sixth is actually an added-note chord (Italian + added second)
- The minor third variant is a color change, not a functional change from the major third version
- Enharmonic spelling of the augmented third variant may obscure its quartal function
