# Quick Definition

A chord created by adding below an original formation strictly inverted intervals in symmetrical reflection, where one half is an exact simultaneous inversion of the other half.

# Formal Definition

A **mirror chord** is any chord (tertian, quartal, secundal, polychordal, or compound) that has been expanded by adding symmetrically inverted intervals below the original formation. One half of a mirror chord is an exact and simultaneous inversion of the other half, creating reflective symmetry around a central axis. Mirror harmony has a textural complexion unlike any other chordal formation because reflective inversion opposes the natural acoustical properties of sound, where overtones are naturally generated from the bottom rather than from the middle as in a mirror.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Construction principle:
- Original chord structure above
- Same intervals inverted below the building tone(s)
- Symmetry around central axis (building tone)

When mirrored:
- **Fundamental chords** (triads, quartal, secundal): Generate larger formations of same category
- **Inverted chords**: Produce polychords
- **Polychords and compound chords**: Produce more complex versions of same type

The building tone never functions aurally as a root; the entire chordal bulk emerges as a chord in its own right, usually assuming the character of the bottom chord.

# Musical Context/Application

Mirror chords provide:
- Unique textural complexion not found in other chord types
- Systematic expansion of any harmonic vocabulary
- Complex sonorities with internal logic
- Material for reflective keyboard writing
- Structural basis for mirror-derived passages

The artificial quality of mirror harmony (opposing natural acoustic generation) creates distinctive color useful for special expressive purposes.

# Common Confusions

- **Building tone vs. root**: The building tone is the axis of symmetry, not a functional root; the chord takes its character from the bottom formation.
- **Mirror vs. inversion**: Chord inversion changes bass note; mirroring creates symmetrical reflection around an axis.
- **Natural vs. artificial**: Mirror harmony opposes natural acoustic generation (overtones from below), creating a distinctive "unnatural" but compositionally useful quality.
