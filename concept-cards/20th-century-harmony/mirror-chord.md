# Quick Definition

A chord created by adding below an original formation strictly inverted intervals in symmetrical reflection, where one half is an exact simultaneous inversion of the other half.

# Core Definition

"Any chord (tertian, quartal, secundal, polychordal, or compound) may be mirrored by adding below the original formation strictly inverted intervals in symmetrical reflection. One half of a mirror chord is an exact and simultaneous inversion of the other half. Mirror harmony has a textural complexion unlike any other chordal formation because reflective inversion opposes the natural acoustical properties of sound, in that the overtones that form the character of a single tone are naturally generated from the bottom, not from the middle, as in a mirror" (Persichetti, p. 171).

# Key Properties

1. Any chord type can be mirrored (tertian, quartal, secundal, polychordal, compound)
2. One half is exact simultaneous inversion of the other
3. Mirror axis: the building tone(s) at the center
4. Opposes natural acoustic properties (overtones from bottom, not middle)
5. Unique textural complexion unlike any other chordal formation
6. Mirroring fundamentals generates larger formations of same category
7. Mirroring inverted chords produces polychords
8. Mirroring polychords/compound chords produces more complex versions
9. Building tone never functions aurally as a root
10. Mirror chords usually assume character of bottom chord

# Construction / Recognition

## To Construct
1. Choose an original chord
2. Select a building tone (axis of symmetry)
3. Reflect each interval of the original chord below the building tone
4. The result is symmetrical around the building tone

## What Mirroring Produces
- Fundamental tertian/quartal/secundal chords -> larger formations of same type
- Inverted chords -> polychords
- Polychords and compound chords -> more complex versions of same

# Context & Application

Mirror harmony provides unique textural complexion not found in other chord types, systematic expansion of any harmonic vocabulary, complex sonorities with internal logic, and material for reflective keyboard writing. The artificial quality (opposing natural acoustic generation) creates distinctive color useful for special expressive purposes.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating the building tone as a functional root
  **Correction**: The building tone is an axis of symmetry, not a root; the chord takes character from the bottom formation

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Mirror chord = chord inversion
  **Clarification**: Chord inversion changes the bass note; mirroring creates symmetrical reflection around a central axis
- **Confusion**: Mirror harmony sounds natural
  **Clarification**: It opposes natural acoustic properties and has a distinctive "unnatural" but compositionally useful quality
