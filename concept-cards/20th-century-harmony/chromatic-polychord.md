# Quick Definition

Any polychord containing at least one diminished or augmented triad, requiring careful spacing when the altered triad is in the bottom position to avoid lower-register crowding.

# Core Definition

"All polychords containing at least one diminished or augmented triad are identified as chromatic polychords. When the bottom chordal unit is augmented or diminished it is advisable to spread the voices of the lower triad to avoid lower-register crowding" (Persichetti, p. 143). The most supple chromatic polychords combine one diatonic triad with one chromatic triad.

# Key Properties

1. Contains at least one diminished or augmented triad
2. Most supple: one diatonic + one chromatic triad
3. Augmented or diminished bottom unit: spread voices to avoid crowding
4. Types include major-dim, major-aug, minor-dim, minor-aug, dim-major, dim-minor, aug-major, aug-minor, dim-dim, dim-aug, aug-dim, aug-aug
5. Mixed diatonic/chromatic combinations balance stability with color

# Construction / Recognition

## To Construct
1. Select at least one augmented or diminished triad
2. If placing aug/dim as bottom unit, spread voices for clarity
3. For most supple results, pair one diatonic with one chromatic triad

# Context & Application

Chromatic polychords add distinctive color beyond the diatonic palette. They are valuable for increased harmonic tension, transitional passages, climactic moments, and color contrast with diatonic polychords.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Placing aug/dim triad in close position as bottom unit
  **Correction**: Spread voices of the lower triad to avoid lower-register crowding

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: "Chromatic" refers to chromatic scale usage
  **Clarification**: It refers to the triad type (augmented or diminished), not the scale source
- **Confusion**: Augmented triads bring the same color as diminished
  **Clarification**: Augmented triads bring whole-tone character; diminished triads bring octatonic associations
