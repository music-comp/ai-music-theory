# Quick Definition
The vertical distribution of intervals within a chord or between voices, which significantly affects consonant-dissonant quality, clarity, and character.

# Core Definition
Spacing is an inseparable part of the character of a tonal structure. When intervals are spaced more than an octave apart, their qualities transform systematically: soft consonances become richer; open consonances and the perfect fourth become stronger; dissonances become less biting yet more brilliant; the tritone becomes more ambiguous and veiled. Chordal structures are most resonant when the distances between members are similar to those in the overtone series (wide spacing in lower register, close spacing in upper register).

- **interval** -- the elements being spaced
- **consonance-dissonance** -- spacing modifies the tension classification

# Key Properties
1. **Soft consonances** (3rds, 6ths): become richer with wide spacing
2. **Open consonances** (5ths, 8ves) and P4: become stronger
3. **Dissonances** (2nds, 7ths): less biting, more brilliant
4. **Tritone**: more ambiguous/veiled, less resolution-driven
5. **Balance principle**: wide intervals at bottom = ordinary balance
6. **Tautness principle**: wide intervals at top = taut sound
7. **Even distribution**: consistent tension across all registers
8. **Overtone model**: wide low, close high = maximum resonance and brilliance
9. **Clearing**: closely spaced harmony clears when broken into two-part writing

# Construction / Recognition
1. Examine the vertical distribution of intervals in a chord
2. Note whether wide intervals are at the bottom (balanced) or top (taut)
3. Compare to the overtone series model for resonance assessment
4. Identify compound intervals and assess their transformed qualities
5. For crowded harmony: consider breaking into two-part writing to clear the texture

# Context & Application
For ordinary balance, wide intervals are placed at the bottom of the chord; for tautness, wide intervals at the top. For evenly distributed tension, all instruments are employed in their grateful registers. The overtone series sets a norm for brilliance: let the lower tones be accompanied by their own overtones. Closely spaced harmony crowded with small intervals clears when each chord is broken into a succession of intervals in two-part writing -- but clouded harmony may remain so and be effective.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Focusing only on which notes are in a chord without considering spacing. **Correction**: The same pitch content can produce dramatically different effects depending on vertical distribution.
- **Error**: Always following the "wide low, close high" rule. **Correction**: Taut voicings with wide intervals at the top and clouded voicings are also valid compositional choices.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking compound intervals are simply "bigger versions" of simple intervals. **Clarification**: Compound spacing qualitatively transforms interval character -- dissonances become brilliant rather than just louder.
