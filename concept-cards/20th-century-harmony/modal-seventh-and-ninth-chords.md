# Quick Definition
Extended tertian chords built within modal scales, where tritone-free constructions are preferred to avoid suggesting major-key dominant function.

# Core Definition
Modal chords by thirds, other than triads, need special attention because the tritone present in many seventh and ninth chords implies the dominant seventh of a major scale; the chord may then easily lose its tonic feeling and slip into a major scale. Useful seventh and ninth chords in the modes (excepting ionian) are those involving no tritone. Triads, sevenths, and ninths progress easily from one to another while remaining in the same mode.

# Key Properties
1. **Tritone-free sevenths** are the most useful in modal writing (major 7th, minor 7th types)
2. **Tritone-containing sevenths** (major-minor 7th, half-diminished) risk suggesting dominant function
3. **Ninth chords**: same principle -- avoid tritone content
4. **Free progression**: triads, 7ths, and 9ths move easily within the same mode
5. **Exception**: Ionian mode naturally contains the dominant seventh (it IS major)

# Construction / Recognition
1. Build seventh and ninth chords from the scale tones of the mode
2. Check each for tritone content
3. Prefer those without tritones for maintaining modal identity
4. Use tritone-containing chords cautiously, ensuring context prevents major-key hearing
5. Mix triads, sevenths, and ninths freely within the mode

# Context & Application
When a tritone is present in a modal seventh chord, it may pull the harmony toward major tonality, undermining the modal character. By selecting tritone-free extended chords, composers maintain modal identity while enriching the harmonic vocabulary beyond triads. The freedom to mix chord sizes (triads, 7ths, 9ths) within a mode provides harmonic variety without leaving the modal framework.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Building seventh chords on every scale degree without checking tritone content. **Correction**: In modal writing, tritone-containing chords risk suggesting major-key dominant function and should be avoided or used cautiously.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking all seventh chords sound "jazzy" or non-modal. **Clarification**: Tritone-free sevenths (major 7th, minor 7th) naturally belong to modal vocabulary and enrich modal harmony without disrupting modal identity.
