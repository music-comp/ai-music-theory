# Quick Definition
A transitional device involving the gradual addition or subtraction of cluster members to move between cluster textures and other chord types.

# Formal Definition
A common transitional harmonic device involving clusters is the gradual subtraction of cluster members, leaving chords that suggest the kind of harmony to follow. The reverse is no less effective: gradually adding tones to a simpler chord until a cluster is formed. This technique provides smooth transition between the dense secundal texture of clusters and other harmonic formations.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Subtraction process:
- Begin with full cluster
- Remove members one at a time
- Remaining tones suggest target harmony
- Final chord establishes new texture

Addition process (reverse):
- Begin with simple chord
- Add adjacent tones gradually
- Build toward full cluster
- Complete cluster establishes secundal texture

Principles:
- Gradual transformation maintains continuity
- Remaining/core tones determine harmonic direction
- Process can accelerate or decelerate

# Musical Context/Application
Cluster transition techniques solve the problem of integrating the dense secundal mass of clusters with other harmonic textures. By gradually adding or removing tones, the composer can move smoothly between highly dissonant cluster textures and more open chord formations. The technique emphasizes the continuity between different harmonic types and demonstrates that clusters exist on a continuum with other chord constructions.
