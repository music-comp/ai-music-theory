# Quick Definition
The acoustic quality of polychords determined by the intervallic structure of the bottom unit, the overtone-generating power of its tones, and the proximity of the upper unit to overtones of the lower unit's third and fifth.

# Formal Definition
The resonance of a polychord is determined by the intervallic structure of the bottom chordal unit and the power of its separate tones to generate overtones. Overtones resulting from a fundamental tone and from those overtones themselves may produce polyharmony. The upper triad of a polychord depends upon its proximity to the overtones of the third and fifth of the lower triad for resonance.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Bottom unit considerations:
- Second inversion (six-four) of major triad = most resonant bottom unit
- Internal spacing closest to harmonic series
- Major third of close-position triad not as close to overtone spacing as P4 of six-four
- When bottom triad spread apart, fundamental position most sonorous

Upper unit placement:
- Depends on proximity to overtones of bottom unit's 3rd and 5th
- Cycle of fifths from bass note orders resonance
- Rising order = decreasing consonance, increasing dissonance

Complete inventory (major-major, from bass of bottom six-four):
1. Upper triad on same bass note (most resonant)
2. Upper triad P5 above
3. Upper triad M2 above (etc., following cycle of fifths)
...
11. Upper triad m6 above (almost "dull thud")
12. Upper triad m2 above (least resonant)

# Musical Context/Application
Understanding resonance allows composers to control the clarity and blend of polychords. More resonant polychords suit stable, tonic-like functions while less resonant polychords create tension or dramatic effect.

Factors affecting resonance:
- Bottom unit voicing and quality
- Upper unit's relationship to overtone series
- Overall spacing between units
- Register of placement
- Common tones between units

The most resonant polychords place upper units on overtone-related pitches of the lower unit; the least resonant create "dull thuds" useful for specific dramatic purposes.

# Common Confusions
- Resonance is not the same as consonance; it refers to acoustic reinforcement
- The six-four is more resonant as bottom unit than root position in close spacing
- The cycle of fifths order is based on overtone relationships, not tonal function
- Less resonant polychords have valid musical uses
