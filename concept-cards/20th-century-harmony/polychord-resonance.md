# Quick Definition

The acoustic quality of polychords determined by the bottom unit's intervallic structure, its overtone-generating power, and the upper unit's proximity to overtones of the lower unit's third and fifth.

# Core Definition

"The resonance of a polychord is determined by the intervallic structure of the bottom chordal unit and the power of its separate tones to generate overtones. The second inversion of the major triad as a bottom unit is the most resonant chordal unit upon which polyharmony can be erected, because its internal spacing is closest to that of the harmonic series" (Persichetti, p. 137). "The upper triad of a polychord depends upon its proximity to the overtones of the third and fifth of the lower triad for resonance" (p. 137).

# Prerequisites

- **Polychord** — Resonance is a property of polychordal structures
- **Chordal unit** — Bottom unit determines resonance
- **Overtone series** — Resonance ranking is based on overtone relationships

# Key Properties

1. Bottom unit determines overall resonance through overtone generation
2. Six-four (second inversion) major triad is most resonant bottom unit
3. When bottom triad spread apart, fundamental position becomes most sonorous
4. Upper unit resonance depends on proximity to overtones of bottom unit's 3rd and 5th
5. Cycle of fifths from bass note orders resonance (decreasing consonance, increasing dissonance)
6. Last six polychords in the cycle are noticeably less resonant; number 11 is "almost a dull thud"
7. Common tones between units help blend components
8. Consonant outer-voice resonance spreads effectiveness throughout the formation
9. A polychord gains resonance when smaller intervals are upper and wider intervals are lower

# Construction / Recognition

## To Maximize Resonance
1. Use major triad in second inversion (six-four) as bottom unit
2. Place upper unit on overtone-related pitch of bottom unit's 3rd or 5th
3. Use wider intervals in lower register, smaller in upper
4. Include common tones between units for blend
5. Double consonant intervals for strength

# Context & Application

Understanding resonance allows composers to control clarity and blend of polychords. More resonant polychords suit stable, tonic-like functions. Less resonant polychords create tension or dramatic effect. The "dull thud" quality of the least resonant combinations has valid dramatic uses.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Using close-position root-position triad as bottom unit for maximum resonance
  **Correction**: The six-four position is more resonant because its internal spacing is closest to the harmonic series
- **Error**: Assuming less resonant polychords are unusable
  **Correction**: Even the "dull thud" of number 11 has valid musical applications under appropriate conditions

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Resonance and consonance are the same thing
  **Clarification**: Resonance refers to acoustic reinforcement through overtone relationships; consonance is a perceptual quality
- **Confusion**: The cycle-of-fifths ordering represents tonal function
  **Clarification**: It is based on overtone relationships, not tonal-functional significance
