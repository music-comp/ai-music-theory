# Quick Definition
The pitch or chord that functions as the point of gravitational focus in a musical passage, providing orientation even without traditional functional harmony.

# Formal Definition
A tonal center is the focal point around which harmonic activity is organized. In twentieth-century music, the search for tonality or key center feeling may become a creative force. The tonality of a structure may be generated from a unifying harmonic idea from which musical growth extends. Key consciousness may vanish temporarily only to make stronger an emerging key's return. In equidistant rootless harmony, any note may be made the tonal center through melodic insistence, spacing, or instrumentation.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Tonal center establishment methods:
- **Traditional**: Chord pillars (I, IV, V relationships)
- **Tritone emphasis**: Scale steps embracing the tritone
- **Persistence**: Insistent chord resisting tonal pressures
- **Recurrence**: Important tones recurring at decisive points
- **Registration**: Prominent pitch through spacing
- **Instrumentation**: Emphasis through timbre
- **Melodic insistence**: Repeated melodic focus on pitch

The center may be:
- Single tone (pitch center)
- Chord (harmonic center)
- Polychord (polytonal center)

# Musical Context/Application
A work may be built tonally upon an initial harmony that falls cadentially, with the final cadence finding refuge far from initial tonal implications. A succession of opening chords may suggest the tonal shape of the entire work in microcosm. An initial major-minor compound chord may promote polychordal writing that resolves when an obstinate melody note anchors the triads. Key feeling may be gradually loosened or tightened as the music progresses.

# Common Confusions
- Tonal center can be established without traditional V-I motion
- Center may be chord or polychord, not just single pitch
- Temporary disappearance of center can strengthen its eventual return
