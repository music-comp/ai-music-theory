# Quick Definition
The compositional process of integrating different harmonic textures -- tertian, quartal, secundal, polychordal, compound, serial, tonal, atonal -- through vertical combination (producing polychordal formations) or successive juxtaposition (requiring transitional technique).

# Core Definition
Contemporary harmonic writing is often a composite process involving varying placement of the norm of dissonance, choice of a single harmonic idiom or the coalition of one with another, fusion of tonalities, singleness of sound organization or the juxtaposition of tonal and atonal aspects. The amalgamation of divergent conceptions of tonal formations is part of our harmonic language. Acceptance of one procedure does not necessarily mean the exclusion of others.

The vertical combination of harmonic textures produces polychordal and compound formations, but successive combinations entail movement from one kind of texture to another and the functioning of heterogeneous harmonic materials side by side. A textural problem arises from the manipulation of the transitional materials that lead from one type of harmony to another.

# Prerequisites
- **Harmonic synthesis**: The overarching principle

# Key Properties
1. Vertical combination produces polychordal and compound formations
2. Successive combination requires transitional technique
3. Heterogeneous materials must function side by side
4. Transitional materials between texture types need careful handling
5. The characteristic interval of one harmony is contained in any other
6. Examples: fugue over cantus firmus, hymn under tone row, quartal blended with tertian

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify which texture types are being combined (tertian, quartal, secundal, etc.)
- Determine whether combination is vertical (simultaneous) or successive (sequential)
- For successive combination, locate the transitional technique
- Note the characteristic interval of each texture type
- Assess overall coherence of the combined result

# Context & Application
The characteristic interval of one kind of harmony is contained in any other kind of harmony and may be brought to the surface by intervallic inversion. This means transitions between any texture types are possible by highlighting the shared intervallic content. The practical challenge is managing the transitional materials so that texture changes sound organic rather than arbitrary. Multiple transitional devices are available: intervallic preparation, unison passages, ornamental tones, equidistant chords, cluster subtraction/addition, and polychord approach/evaporation.

# Common Errors
- Combining textures without transitional preparation
- Treating successive combination as simple juxtaposition
- Ignoring the shared intervallic content between texture types

# Common Confusions
- **Vertical vs. successive**: Simultaneous combination is polychordal/compound; sequential combination requires transition
- **Transitional problem**: The main challenge is not choosing textures but handling the materials that connect them
- **Shared intervals**: Every texture type contains the characteristic intervals of every other type -- the connection is always available
