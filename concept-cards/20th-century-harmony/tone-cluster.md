# Quick Definition
A passage dominated by chords by seconds arranged in predominantly uninverted forms so that most voices are a second apart, handled through outer-voice counterpoint while seconds fill in the frame, expanding and contracting the cluster.

# Formal Definition
When a passage is dominated by chords by seconds and arranged in predominantly uninverted forms so that most of the voices are a second apart, the chords are called clusters. These are not true chords by seconds, in part because of their generally consistent spacing but mainly because of the lack of defined inner voice movement. When large clusters are used, handling the voices is accomplished solely by considering the two-part contrapuntal frame formed by the outer voices.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Cluster characteristics:
- Dominated by seconds
- Predominantly uninverted (close position)
- Most voices a second apart
- No defined inner voice movement
- Governed by outer-voice counterpoint

Voice-leading approach:
1. Compose two-part frame (outer voices)
2. Fill in frame with seconds
3. Expand and contract clusters through outer-voice movement
4. Inner voices follow outer-voice contour

Cluster sources:
- Most scale formations can be clustered
- Diatonic scales: 7 modal clusterings per scale, 14 per two-octave scale
- Chromatic scale: least useful (becomes tiresome quickly)
- Synthetic scales: unique cluster possibilities

# Musical Context/Application
Clusters represent a distinct approach to harmony where vertical sonority is controlled primarily through linear considerations. The traditional notion of individual voice-leading is replaced by mass movement of the cluster, with texture expanding and contracting as outer voices move.

Clusters function in:
- Dramatic punctuations (large clusters)
- Coloristic passages
- Textural contrast with other harmony types
- Accumulative climaxes

Subtleties of cluster part-writing are difficult to compose or notate for piano; chamber combinations and orchestra better accommodate clusters.

# Common Confusions
- Clusters are not true "chords by seconds" because they lack defined inner voice movement
- The outer voices are the contrapuntal substance; inner voices merely fill
- Chromatic clusters become tiresome more quickly than diatonic clusters
- Cluster handling differs from traditional voice-leading
