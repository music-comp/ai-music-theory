# Quick Definition

A passage dominated by chords by seconds in predominantly uninverted close position, where most voices are a second apart, handled through outer-voice counterpoint while inner seconds fill the frame.

# Core Definition

"When a passage is dominated by chords by seconds and arranged in predominantly uninverted forms so that most of the voices are a second apart, the chords are called clusters. These are not true chords by seconds, in part because of their generally consistent spacing but mainly because of the lack of defined inner voice movement. When large clusters are used, handling the voices is accomplished solely by considering the two-part contrapuntal frame formed by the outer voices" (Persichetti, p. 125).

# Prerequisites

- **Secundal harmony** — Clusters emerge from the secundal harmonic system
- **Multi-note secundal chords** — Clusters extend multi-note secundal chords beyond the point of independent voice movement

# Key Properties

1. Dominated by seconds in predominantly uninverted (close) position
2. Most voices a second apart
3. No defined inner voice movement (the key distinction from true chords by seconds)
4. Voice-handling through two-part outer-voice contrapuntal frame
5. Inner voices fill in the outer-voice frame, expanding and contracting
6. Most scale formations can be clustered
7. Diatonic seven-note scale yields 7 modal clusterings per octave, 14 per two-octave scale
8. Chromatic scale is least useful for clusters (becomes tiresome quickly)
9. Large clusters powerful for dramatic punctuation; smaller clusters more agile
10. Subtleties of cluster part-writing are difficult to compose/notate for piano; orchestra and chamber groups accommodate clusters better

# Construction / Recognition

## To Write Cluster Harmony
1. Compose a two-part contrapuntal frame (outer voices)
2. Fill in the frame with seconds
3. Expand and contract clusters through outer-voice movement
4. Vary intervallic makeup and member count for textural interest and momentum

## To Recognize Clusters
1. Check for predominantly close-position seconds
2. Verify lack of defined inner voice movement
3. Confirm outer-voice frame governs the texture
4. Note that the context must establish cluster identity (otherwise seconds may sound like scales)

# Context & Application

Clusters represent a distinct approach where vertical sonority is controlled primarily through linear considerations of the outer voices. Traditional individual voice-leading is replaced by mass movement. Clusters function in dramatic punctuations (large clusters), coloristic passages, textural contrast with other harmony types, and accumulative climaxes.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Translating traditional harmony into clusters by arbitrarily filling in space
  **Correction**: "Translating traditional harmony into cluster harmony by arbitrarily filling in the space is calculative and usually without musical impetus" (p. 131)
- **Error**: Writing extended chromatic clusters
  **Correction**: Chromatic clusters become tiresome quickly; use diatonic, pentatonic, or synthetic scale clusters for variety

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Clusters are true chords by seconds
  **Clarification**: Clusters lack defined inner voice movement, which is the primary distinction from true secundal chords
- **Confusion**: The outer voices are decorative in cluster writing
  **Clarification**: The outer voices are the contrapuntal substance; inner voices merely fill the expanding/contracting frame
