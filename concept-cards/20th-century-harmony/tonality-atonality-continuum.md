# Quick Definition
The principle that tonality and atonality are not binary opposites but endpoints of a continuous spectrum, with many intermediate degrees of key-center gravity, where the point at which tonality ends and atonality begins is indefinite.

# Core Definition
At one extreme of the concept of key is tonality, the other extreme is atonality, and the point at which one ends and the other begins is indefinite. There are many degrees of key-center gravity or of keylessness in the general area of tonality or atonality. Music may hold its tonality loosely, elements of atonality being inherent from the onset. Melodic lines in a tonal framework can become so free that the implied harmony becomes evasively dissonant and a point reached where key feeling is lost. Partial atonality is useful in vague introductions and transition passages.

# Prerequisites
- **Tonality**: One endpoint of the continuum
- **Atonality**: The other endpoint

# Key Properties
1. Tonality and atonality are endpoints, not discrete categories
2. The boundary between them is indefinite
3. Many degrees of key-center gravity exist
4. Music can hold tonality loosely with inherent atonality
5. Key feeling can be gradually lost or gained
6. Partial atonality is a valid compositional state
7. The continuum applies to analysis, composition, and perception

# Construction / Recognition
- Assess the degree of key-center gravity in a passage (not binary tonal/atonal)
- Place the passage on the continuum from firm tonality to complete atonality
- Note gradual loosening or tightening of key feeling
- Recognize intermediate states: loose tonality, partial atonality, shifting tonality
- Avoid binary classification when the music occupies an intermediate position

# Context & Application
The continuum concept is fundamental to Persichetti's approach. It rejects the binary classification common in earlier theory (music is either tonal or atonal) in favor of a nuanced spectrum. This framework accommodates the wide range of tonal practices in twentieth-century music, from firmly tonal passages with tritone-embracing pillar chords through various degrees of loosened tonality, shifting tonality, partial atonality, and complete keylessness. The same principle extends through polytonality: triads to polychords to polytonality to atonality forms another continuum of increasing tonal complexity.

# Common Errors
- Classifying music as simply "tonal" or "atonal"
- Ignoring intermediate states
- Treating the boundary as definite

# Common Confusions
- **Not binary**: The most important insight is that tonality/atonality is not a binary distinction
- **Indefinite boundary**: There is no precise point where tonality becomes atonality
- **Multiple continua**: The basic tonal-atonal continuum is paralleled by the polychord-polytonality-atonality continuum
