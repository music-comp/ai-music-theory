# Quick Definition
The modification of modal scale tones, particularly the diminished triad, to achieve stability, resonance, or expressive color while maintaining modal identity.

# Formal Definition
Chromatic alterations, both melodic and harmonic, are devices natural to modal writing. The diminished triad is a frequent subject for chromatic alteration because of its restless tendency caused by its tritone. This chord is altered to give the tritone perfect-fifth stability and resonance by lowering the root or raising the diminished fifth.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Diminished triad alterations:
- Lower the root: converts diminished 5th to perfect 5th (creates minor triad)
- Raise the diminished 5th: converts to perfect 5th (creates major triad)
- Either alteration: eliminates tritone instability

Locrian tonic alternatives:
- Omit the fifth (power chord sound)
- Use first inversion (subdues tritone)
- Add notes (added-note chord)
- Total unison (obliterates tritone)

# Musical Context/Application
The diminished triad found on a different scale step in each mode is restless because of its tritone. When altered to achieve perfect-fifth stability, the chord gains resonance. In phrygian, altering the diminished V creates an additional dominant equivalent. In locrian, various strategies avoid the unstable tonic triad.

# Common Confusions
Students may apply chromatic alterations randomly or avoid them entirely in modal writing. The key is to alter strategically, particularly the diminished triad, while maintaining the characteristic modal tone in melodic lines.
