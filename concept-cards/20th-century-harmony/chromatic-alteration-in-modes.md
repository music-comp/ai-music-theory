# Quick Definition
The modification of modal scale tones, particularly the diminished triad, to achieve stability and resonance while maintaining modal identity.

# Core Definition
Chromatic alterations, both melodic and harmonic, are devices natural to modal writing. The diminished triad is a frequent subject for chromatic alteration because of its restless tendency caused by its tritone. This chord is altered to give the tritone perfect-fifth stability and resonance; the root is lowered or the diminished fifth raised. In phrygian, altering the diminished V creates an additional (third) dominant equivalent. Altering the harmonic texture should not disturb strict scale tones in the melodic writing, because the burden of maintaining the flavor of the prevailing mode is placed upon the melody.

# Key Properties
1. **Diminished triad alteration**: lower the root OR raise the diminished fifth
2. **Result**: converts the tritone to a perfect fifth, gaining stability and resonance
3. **Creates minor or major triad** from the diminished chord
4. **Phrygian special case**: altered diminished V creates a third dominant equivalent
5. **Melody carries the burden**: harmonic alterations must not disturb melodic scale integrity
6. **Natural to modal writing**: not a violation of modal purity

# Construction / Recognition
1. Locate the diminished triad in the mode (different position in each mode)
2. Lower its root a half step to create a minor triad, OR raise its fifth a half step to create a major triad
3. Ensure melodic lines maintain strict modal scale tones
4. Check whether the alteration creates an additional dominant equivalent

# Context & Application
The diminished triad found on a different scale step in each mode is restless because of its tritone. When altered for perfect-fifth stability, the chord gains resonance and can serve additional harmonic functions. The key principle is that the melody bears the primary responsibility for projecting modal identity, freeing the harmony to use strategic chromatic alterations.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Applying chromatic alterations randomly. **Correction**: Alter strategically, particularly the diminished triad, for specific harmonic purposes.
- **Error**: Altering the melody along with the harmony. **Correction**: The melody must maintain strict modal scale tones; only the harmony receives chromatic alteration.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking chromatic alteration destroys modal purity. **Clarification**: Persichetti considers chromatic alterations "natural to modal writing" -- they are standard tools, not violations.
