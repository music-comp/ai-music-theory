# Quick Definition
Nonharmonic tones that provide stepwise linear connection between chord tones, approached and left by step in the same direction.

# Core Definition
**Passing tones** belong to the first category of nonharmonic tones: those approached and left without skip. They create linear connection between chord tones, moving stepwise in a consistent direction. In twentieth-century practice, passing tones may be diatonic, chromatic, or drawn from synthetic scale sources. Ornamental tones may be derived from a scale built upon the root of the chord, with each root embellished as though it were a tonic. When multiple passing tones occur simultaneously, they create passing chords (Ch. 11, pp. 229-233).

# Prerequisites
- **Nonharmonic tones** -- understanding the classification system

# Key Properties
1. Approached by step from chord tone
2. Left by step to chord tone (same direction)
3. May be diatonic, chromatic, or from synthetic scales
4. May derive from scale built on chord root
5. Multiple simultaneous passing tones form passing chords

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify tones moving stepwise between two chord tones in the same direction
- Determine the scalar source (diatonic, chromatic, synthetic, root-derived)
- Check for simultaneous passing tones forming passing chords

# Context & Application
In twentieth-century harmony, passing tones operate with expanded scalar resources, including synthetic scales and scales derived from contrasting key centers (creating temporary polytonality within ornamental figuration).

## Related
- Auxiliary tones (also step-step, but returns to starting pitch)

## Contrasts With
- Auxiliary tones (passing tones continue in one direction; auxiliary tones return)

# Common Errors
- Assuming passing tones must come from the prevailing diatonic scale
- Not recognizing chord-wise groupings as passing chords

# Common Confusions
- Passing tones move through the space between chord tones; auxiliary tones move around a single tone
- In twentieth-century practice, any scale source may provide the passing tone
