# Quick Definition
A six-note symmetrical scale dividing the octave into six equal whole steps, which has severely limited harmonic resources but provides valuable contrast when combined with other materials.

# Core Definition
The whole-tone hexatonic scale offers a limited basis for extended musical expression. When mirrored there is no change except in register. A second whole-tone scale lies a half step above the first, but any attempt to produce more results in transposition and duplication of one of the two. Modal versions result in exact transposition of original forms (so no distinct modes exist). Its equidistant intervallic make-up deprives the scale of the fundamental intervals -- the perfect fourth and fifth -- and of the leading tone. A real feeling of tonality must be established by harmony outside the whole-tone category.

# Key Properties
1. **Structure**: W-W-W-W-W-W (all intervals equal)
2. **Only two distinct collections** exist (e.g., C and C# whole-tone scales)
3. **No modes possible**: all rotations are transpositions
4. **Reflectively identical**: mirror = retrograde
5. **Missing intervals**: no perfect 4th, no perfect 5th, no leading tone
6. **Chord resources**: meager -- only augmented triads, one seventh type, one ninth type
7. **Value lies in contrast**: most effective when combined with other materials

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify six tones each a whole step apart
2. Determine which of the two collections (C or C#) is in use
3. Note the absence of perfect fourths, fifths, and semitones
4. Check whether the passage is pure whole-tone or combined with other materials

# Context & Application
The true value of the whole-tone scale lies in the contrast it provides when combined with other scales and techniques. When amalgamated with other materials, it can be creatively stimulating. Pure whole-tone writing requires special devices to maintain interest: contrary motion, alternating the two scales, sounding all six tones together, changing density and spacing, and using both whole-tone scales simultaneously.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Overusing whole-tone scales without recognizing their severe limitations. **Correction**: Pure whole-tone writing quickly becomes monotonous; its value is primarily in contrast and combination.
- **Error**: Trying to create multiple whole-tone scales. **Correction**: Only two distinct whole-tone collections exist; all others are transpositions of these.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking whole-tone music is inherently "impressionistic." **Clarification**: While associated with Debussy, whole-tone materials appear across many 20th-century styles and serve diverse compositional purposes.
