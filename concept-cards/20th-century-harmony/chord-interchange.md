# Quick Definition

A harmonic technique using the alternation of two or three chords to create atmospheric passages where neither definite tonality nor forward harmonic motion is desired.

# Formal Definition

**Chord interchange** is a succession consisting of the alternation between two or three chords, used for "atmospheric" passages where neither definite tonality nor forward harmonic motion is desired, or for music with folk roots. This oscillating harmonic motion creates stasis or circularity rather than goal-directed progression, establishing a harmonic field rather than a harmonic journey.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Types of interchange:
- **Two-chord alternation**: A-B-A-B-A...
- **Three-chord alternation**: A-B-C-A-B-C... or A-B-A-C-A-B...
- **Return patterns**: Any chord may return to immediately preceding chord; succeeding chords may return to original formation

Characteristics:
- No forward progression implied
- Harmonic field established rather than traveled
- Rhythmic organization often takes precedence
- Tonal center may be ambiguous or absent

# Musical Context/Application

Chord interchange serves:
- Creating atmospheric, static harmonic fields
- Establishing folk-music character through simple oscillation
- Providing harmonic foundation for rhythmically active passages
- Suspending forward motion for dramatic or expressive purposes

The technique is effective for:
- Introductions and codas requiring harmonic stasis
- Ostinato-based passages
- Trance or meditative musical effects
- Folk-influenced compositions

# Common Confusions

- **Interchange vs. progression**: Interchange creates stasis through oscillation; progression implies forward motion toward a goal.
- **Simple vs. primitive**: Chord interchange is a sophisticated technique for specific effects, not evidence of limited harmonic vocabulary.
- **Static vs. boring**: When used appropriately, static harmony creates compelling atmosphere rather than tedium.
