# Quick Definition
A succession consisting of an interchange of two or three chords, used for atmospheric passages where neither definite tonality nor forward harmonic motion is desired, or for music with folk roots.

# Core Definition
**Chord interchange** is a succession consisting of the alternation between two or three chords. It may be used for "atmospheric" passages where neither definite tonality nor forward harmonic motion is desired, or for music with folk roots. Any chord can return to the chord that immediately precedes it, and succeeding chords may return to an original formation. This oscillating harmonic motion creates stasis or circularity rather than goal-directed progression (Ch. 9, pp. 185-186).

# Prerequisites
- **Harmonic progression** -- understanding what constitutes directional motion (to recognize its absence)

# Key Properties
1. Two-chord alternation: A-B-A-B pattern
2. Three-chord alternation: A-B-C-A-B-C or variants
3. Any chord may return to its immediate predecessor
4. Succeeding chords may return to the original formation
5. No forward progression implied
6. Harmonic field established rather than traveled
7. Effective for atmospheric or folk-rooted passages

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify passages where the same two or three chords alternate repeatedly
- Note the absence of goal-directed harmonic motion
- Observe whether rhythmic or melodic elements take precedence over harmonic direction
- Check for folk-music associations or atmospheric intent

# Context & Application
Chord interchange is effective for introductions, codas, ostinato-based passages, or any context requiring harmonic suspension without stasis on a single chord. It provides more harmonic variety than static harmony while avoiding the directional implications of true progression. The technique appears frequently in folk-influenced and minimalist-adjacent twentieth-century music.

## Contrasts With
- Harmonic progression (interchange lacks direction)
- Cadential devices (cadences imply arrival; interchange implies suspension)

# Common Errors
- Treating chord interchange as a sign of limited harmonic vocabulary rather than a deliberate technique
- Using interchange without clear atmospheric or folk-rooted intent
- Extending interchange beyond the point of effectiveness

# Common Confusions
- **Interchange vs. progression**: Interchange creates stasis through oscillation; progression implies forward motion toward a goal
- **Simple vs. primitive**: Chord interchange is a sophisticated technique for specific effects, not evidence of limited skill
- **Static vs. boring**: When used with appropriate timing, interchange creates compelling atmosphere
