# Quick Definition

The practice of doubling chord tones in twentieth-century harmony, where doubling considerations shift from securing maximum sound to achieving textural variety and managing consonance-dissonance relationships.

# Formal Definition

**Doubling in complex harmony** refers to the principles governing which chord tones are doubled (played by multiple voices at the same or different octaves) in twentieth-century harmonic practice. While three- and four-part harmony often focuses on securing maximum sound, harmony of more than four parts directs doubling considerations toward securing variety of texture. Various colors and weights are obtainable through omission and doubling of both dissonant and consonant chord members, with density and concentration varying under different dramatic conditions.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Doubling principles:
- **Consonant member doubling**: Generally safe, adds weight
- **Dissonant member doubling**: Raises harmonic tension
- **Doubled dissonance softening**: Contrary motion, or move one doubled tone before other resolves

Special considerations:
- Doubling dissonant tone enables simple chord to function with complex formations
- Coupling dissonant tone with consonant intervals softens quality
- In orchestral arrivals of dissonant material upon consonant climax, extensive doubling of consonant chord may be needed to avoid sound loss

# Musical Context/Application

Doubling serves:
- Managing tension levels through selective member doubling
- Creating textural variety in multi-voice harmony
- Balancing consonant and dissonant elements
- Enabling simple chords to participate in complex contexts

The technique requires attention to:
- Dramatic context and intended effect
- Balance between voices and registers
- Resolution treatment of doubled dissonances
- Orchestral and instrumental color possibilities

# Common Confusions

- **Traditional vs. contemporary doubling**: Traditional rules about doubling leading tones, etc., may not apply in twentieth-century contexts.
- **Doubling vs. spacing**: Doubling concerns which tones are duplicated; spacing concerns where all tones are placed.
- **Tension management**: Doubling dissonance increases tension; this may be desired or require softening techniques.
