# Quick Definition

Harmonic writing where a single chord or sonority is sustained without change, creating a feeling of breadth or relaxation through the absence of harmonic rhythm.

# Formal Definition

**Static harmony** is created by a lack of harmonic rhythm (one chord sustained), producing a feeling of breadth or relaxation. Static harmony is useful when attention is to be focused upon a rhythmic motif, or when repeated chords announce the rhythm of an unaccompanied melody to follow. This technique suspends forward harmonic motion while other musical parameters (rhythm, melody, texture) may remain active.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Static harmony characteristics:
- Single chord sustained or repeated
- No harmonic progression or change
- Harmonic rhythm = zero (no chord changes)
- Other musical elements may move freely

Uses:
- Focusing attention on rhythmic motif
- Preparatory announcement of melodic rhythm
- Creating breadth or spaciousness
- Providing contrast to active harmonic passages
- Establishing tonal/harmonic field

# Musical Context/Application

Static harmony serves:
- Providing rest from harmonic activity
- Highlighting rhythmic or melodic content
- Creating atmospheric or meditative passages
- Establishing harmonic areas before progression begins
- Generating tension through harmonic suspension

The technique works effectively:
- At beginnings (establishing harmonic field)
- At ends (final chord prolongation)
- In contrasting sections (against active harmony elsewhere)
- Under rhythmically active material (stable foundation)

# Common Confusions

- **Static vs. inactive**: Static harmony suspends harmonic motion; other musical elements may be highly active.
- **Static vs. simple**: Static harmony may use complex sonorities; "static" refers to lack of change, not lack of complexity.
- **Duration requirement**: Brief chord sustainings are not static harmony; the technique implies extended duration.
