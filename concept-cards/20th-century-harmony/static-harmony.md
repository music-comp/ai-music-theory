# Quick Definition
Harmonic writing where a single chord or sonority is sustained without change, creating breadth or relaxation through the absence of harmonic rhythm.

# Core Definition
A lack of harmonic rhythm (one chord) creates **static harmony** and a feeling of breadth or relaxation. Static harmony is useful when attention is to be focused upon a rhythmic motif, or when it is used in repeated chords announcing the rhythm of an unaccompanied melody to follow. Pandiatonic writing is a specific kind of static harmony (Ch. 10, pp. 222-223).

# Prerequisites
- **Harmonic rhythm** -- understanding chord-change rate (to understand its absence)

# Key Properties
1. Single chord sustained or repeated
2. No harmonic progression or change
3. Harmonic rhythm = zero
4. Creates breadth or relaxation
5. Other musical elements (rhythm, melody, texture) may remain active
6. Useful for focusing attention on rhythmic motifs
7. Pandiatonic writing is a specific form of static harmony

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify passages where a single chord persists
- Note the absence of harmonic change
- Observe which other parameters are active (rhythm, melody, counterpoint)
- Check whether the passage leads to pandiatonic writing

# Context & Application
Static harmony serves as harmonic foundation for rhythmic activity, as preparation for unaccompanied melodic passages, as contrast to harmonically active sections, and as the basis for pandiatonic expansion.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Harmonic rhythm (static harmony = zero harmonic rhythm)
## Enables
- Pandiatonic writing (specific expansion of static harmony)
## Related
- Pedal point (sustained tone, but other harmonies may move against it)
- Chord interchange (minimal harmonic motion rather than zero)
- Repeated notes and chords (articulation without harmonic change)
## Contrasts With
- Harmonic progression (directed motion vs. stasis)

# Common Errors
- Assuming static harmony means all musical activity stops
- Confusing static harmony with musical inactivity

# Common Confusions
- **Static vs. inactive**: Static harmony suspends harmonic motion; other elements may be highly active
- **Static vs. simple**: Static harmony may use complex sonorities; "static" refers to lack of change
- **Duration requirement**: Brief chord sustainings are not static harmony; the technique implies extended duration
