# Quick Definition
The individual chord segments that make up a polychord, which may be triads, seventh chords, quartal chords, or secundal structures, and whose clear grouping is requisite for polyharmony to be perceived.

# Formal Definition
The segments of the polychord are referred to as chordal units. Clear grouping of the chordal units is a requisite of polyharmony, and rearranging the tones of these units can destroy the polychordal organization. Chordal units may consist of triads of any quality, seventh chords, or non-tertian structures including chords by fourths and seconds.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Unit types:
- **Triadic**: Major, minor, diminished, augmented
- **Seventh chord**: Various seventh chord qualities
- **Quartal**: Chords by perfect or augmented fourths
- **Secundal**: Chords by seconds (creates polycluster if all units secundal)

Positioning terminology:
- **Bottom unit**: Lower chordal component
- **Upper unit(s)**: Higher chordal component(s)
- Position determines resonance contribution

Grouping requirements:
- Units must maintain registral separation
- Voicing must preserve unit identity
- Tonal scrambling destroys polychordal perception

# Musical Context/Application
The chordal unit concept allows analysis and construction of polychords as combinations of simpler structures. Each unit retains its harmonic identity while combining with others to create the complex polychordal sonority.

Understanding chordal units enables:
- Systematic construction of polychords
- Analysis of polychordal passages
- Control of resonance through unit choice
- Maintenance of clarity in complex textures

# Common Confusions
- Chordal units are not abstract pitch-class sets; they are voiced chords
- The term applies to any chord type, not just triads
- Rearranging unit tones changes the perception, potentially destroying polyharmony
- "Bottom" and "upper" refer to registral position, not bass note
