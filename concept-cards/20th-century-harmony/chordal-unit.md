# Quick Definition

The individual chord segments that make up a polychord, which may be triads, seventh chords, quartal chords, or secundal structures, and whose clear registral grouping is essential for polyharmonic perception.

# Core Definition

"The segments of the polychord are referred to as chordal units" (Persichetti, p. 134). "Clear grouping of the chordal units is a requisite of polyharmony, and rearranging the tones of these units can destroy the polychordal organization" (p. 136).

# Prerequisites

- **Polychord** — Chordal units are the building blocks of polychords

# Key Properties

1. Individual chord segments within a polychord
2. Unit types: triads (major, minor, diminished, augmented), seventh chords, quartal chords, secundal chords
3. Bottom unit determines resonance; its overtone-generating power is critical
4. Upper unit(s) depend on proximity to overtones of the bottom unit for resonance
5. Clear registral grouping is mandatory; tonal scrambling destroys polychordal perception
6. Units must maintain distinct identity through voicing and/or instrumentation

# Construction / Recognition

## To Use Chordal Units Effectively
1. Choose unit type based on desired resonance and color
2. Maintain each unit as a clearly grouped chord
3. Place units in distinct registral zones
4. Use contrasting instrumentation to define separate harmonic areas
5. Check that rearranging tones would destroy the polychordal effect

# Context & Application

The chordal unit concept enables systematic construction and analysis of polychords. Each unit retains harmonic identity while contributing to the complex polychordal sonority. Unit choice controls resonance (major triads most resonant), color, and textural clarity.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Rearranging unit tones across registral zones
  **Correction**: Each unit must maintain its registral grouping; scrambled voicing destroys polyharmony
- **Error**: Using only triadic units
  **Correction**: Seventh chords, quartal chords, and secundal chords also serve as valid units

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Chordal units are abstract pitch-class sets
  **Clarification**: They are voiced chords with specific registral placement; voicing is essential to their identity
- **Confusion**: "Bottom" and "upper" refer to bass notes
  **Clarification**: They refer to the registral position of the entire chordal unit, not just the bass
