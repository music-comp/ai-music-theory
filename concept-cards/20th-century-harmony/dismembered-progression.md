# Quick Definition
A technique where chords from a thematically significant, aurally retainable passage are omitted or shuffled from their original order, fragmenting the expected progression.

# Core Definition
If a passage is thematically significant and is easily retained aurally, chords may be omitted or shuffled in a **dismembered progression**. The technique relies on the listener's memory of the original material to perceive the dismembered version as a variant rather than as random chord succession. This extends the concept of harmonic elision into more radical reorganization of established material (Ch. 9, pp. 187-188).

# Prerequisites
- **Harmonic progression** -- understanding established chord patterns
- **Harmonic elision** -- understanding single-chord omission as foundation for more extensive manipulation

# Key Properties
1. Original passage must be thematically significant and aurally retainable
2. Operations include omission, shuffling (reordering), or both
3. Enough of the original must remain for recognition
4. Listener memory provides coherence across the fragmentation
5. Melodic or rhythmic continuity may help bridge gaps

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify a passage that has been clearly established and is memorable
- Look for a later passage where the same chords appear in altered order or with gaps
- Verify that the dismembered version is recognizable as a variant of the original
- Note whether melodic or rhythmic elements provide continuity

# Context & Application
Dismembered progressions serve developmental sections where established material is fragmented for variation, passages where unpredictability is desired within familiar contexts, and any situation where the dramatic intent calls for disrupted expectations. The technique requires that the original material be firmly established in the listener's ear before dismemberment occurs.

# Common Errors
- Dismembering a passage that has not been sufficiently established in the listener's memory
- Removing so many chords that the connection to the original is lost
- Confusing random chord succession with intentional dismemberment

# Common Confusions
- **Dismemberment vs. elision**: Elision omits expected chords from conventional patterns; dismemberment fragments previously established thematic material more extensively
- **Recognition requirement**: Unlike random chord succession, dismembered progression assumes the listener recognizes the source material
- **Coherence maintenance**: Dismemberment should not destroy musical sense; other parameters provide continuity
