# Quick Definition

A technique where chords from a thematically significant, aurally retainable passage are omitted or shuffled from their original order, fragmenting the expected progression.

# Formal Definition

**Dismembered progression** is a compositional technique applicable when a passage is thematically significant and easily retained aurally. Chords may be omitted or shuffled from their established order, creating a fragmented version of the original progression. The technique relies on the listener's memory of the original material to perceive the dismembered version as a variant rather than as random chord succession.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Dismemberment operations:
- **Omission**: Remove one or more chords from sequence
- **Shuffling**: Reorder chords from original sequence
- **Combination**: Both omit and reorder

Requirements for effectiveness:
- Original passage must be memorable
- Harmonic content must be aurally retainable
- Enough of original must remain for recognition
- Melodic or rhythmic continuity may help bridge gaps

Example:
- Original: A-B-C-D-E-F
- Dismembered (omission): A-C-D-F
- Dismembered (shuffle): A-D-B-F-C-E
- Dismembered (combined): A-D-F-B

# Musical Context/Application

Dismembered progressions serve:
- Creating variation on established harmonic material
- Adding unpredictability to familiar progressions
- Developing material through fragmentation
- Building tension through disrupted expectations

The technique is particularly effective:
- After clear establishment of the original pattern
- In developmental sections
- Where fragmentation suits dramatic intent
- When melodic continuity provides coherence

# Common Confusions

- **Dismemberment vs. elision**: Elision omits expected chords from conventional patterns; dismemberment specifically fragments previously established thematic material.
- **Recognition requirement**: Unlike random chord succession, dismembered progression assumes the listener recognizes the source material.
- **Coherence maintenance**: Dismemberment should not destroy musical sense; melodic, rhythmic, or formal elements provide continuity.
