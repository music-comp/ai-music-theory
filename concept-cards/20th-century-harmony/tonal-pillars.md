# Quick Definition
The primary chordal supports that establish and maintain a tonal center, typically built on scale degrees that provide balanced harmonic support around the tonic.

# Formal Definition
Tonal pillars are the basic chords needed to produce a feeling of tonality. Usually three basic chords are required: one built upon a scale step above the tonic, one below the tonic, and the tonic itself. The remaining secondary scale roots decorate these three tonal pillars. Harmony with tritone gravitation is helpful in establishing the center. The pillars provide a framework that secondary harmonies ornament.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Traditional tonal pillar configurations:
1. **Primary (V-IV-I)**: Subdominant below, dominant above, tonic
2. **Mediant-based (III-VI-I)**: Submediant below, mediant above
3. **Semitone-based (II-VII-I)**: Leading tone and supertonic framing tonic
4. **Tritone-embracing**: Scale steps spanning the tritone

Pillar principle:
- One chord above tonic
- One chord below tonic
- Tonic itself
- Secondary chords decorate these pillars

# Musical Context/Application
In twentieth-century practice, the concept of tonal pillars extends beyond traditional I-IV-V relationships. Any three scale-based chords that frame the tonic can function as pillars. The tritone-embracing pillars are particularly effective because the tritone's gravitational pull reinforces the center. Polytonal resonance also depends on the pillar relationship between combined tonics.

# Common Confusions
- Tonal pillars are not limited to I-IV-V
- Any above-below-tonic configuration can serve as pillars
- Secondary chords decorate pillars rather than replacing them
