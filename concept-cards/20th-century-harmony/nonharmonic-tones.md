# Quick Definition
Ornamental tones that are foreign to the prevailing chord, creating intervallic tension and restlessness that seeks resolution.

# Formal Definition
Nonharmonic tones are ornamental tones that are inherently restless because of their intervallic counteraction to the chord. They may be either accented (occurring with the change of harmony, regardless of metric position) or unaccented. Persichetti identifies four general categories: (1) passing and auxiliary tones approached and left without skip; (2) changing tones approached conjunctly and left by skip; (3) appoggiaturas approached by skip and left conjunctly; and (4) a hybrid group including suspensions and anticipations formed by rhythmic misplacement of chord tones.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Classification of nonharmonic tones by approach and departure:
| Type | Approach | Departure |
|------|----------|-----------|
| Passing/Auxiliary | Step | Step |
| Changing tone | Step | Leap (opposite direction) |
| Appoggiatura | Leap | Step |
| Suspension | Held over | Step |
| Anticipation | Step | Held |

Additional behaviors in 20th-century practice:
- May skip to and from a nonharmonic tone if it becomes a member of the following chord
- May imply a larger formation over the same root
- May resist any chordal affiliation

# Musical Context/Application
In twentieth-century harmony, nonharmonic tones function with greater freedom than in common-practice tonality. Ornamental tones may be derived from scales built upon the chord root (treating each root as a temporary tonic), or from contrasting key centers creating temporary polytonality. Nonharmonic tones may remain unresolved, causing permanent texture change. Before resolving, nonharmonic tones may be ornamented by and combined with other ornamental tones.

# Common Confusions
- "Accented" refers to timing with harmonic change, not metric stress
- In 20th-century practice, nonharmonic tones may remain permanently unresolved
- Nonharmonic tones can be grouped chord-wise to create ornamental chords
