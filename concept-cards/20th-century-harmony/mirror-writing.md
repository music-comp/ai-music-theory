# Quick Definition

A compositional technique employing simultaneous presentation of material and its exact intervallic inversion, creating symmetrical reflection around a central axis.

# Core Definition

"Four possible types of mirror chord writing are: a stationary building tone used to generate reflection (a); a moving building tone generating reflection (b); the building tones themselves becoming reflective by moving in contrary motion (c); building tones used freely (d)" (Persichetti, p. 173). "When strict mirror writing is desired, ornamental tones are kept in reflective relationship" (p. 177). "Greater freedom in harmony can be obtained through only partial reflection, the outer voices mirrored while the inner voices move freely" (p. 177).

# Prerequisites

- **Mirror chord** — Mirror writing uses mirror chords as its vertical vocabulary
- **Building tone** — The axis of symmetry must be understood

# Key Properties

1. **Type 1**: Stationary building tone generates reflection (simplest)
2. **Type 2**: Moving building tone (axis shifts while maintaining reflection)
3. **Type 3**: Building tones in contrary motion (axes themselves reflect)
4. **Type 4**: Free building tones (loosest application)
5. Strict mirror: all ornamental tones kept in reflective relationship
6. Partial reflection: outer voices mirrored, inner voices free
7. Mirror writing may stem from thematic sources

# Construction / Recognition

## To Apply Mirror Writing
1. Choose building tone behavior (stationary, moving, contrary, free)
2. For strict: maintain reflective relationship in all voices including ornaments
3. For partial: mirror outer voices only; free inner voices for harmonic flexibility
4. For thematic: construct melody with self-inverting segments for simultaneous use

# Context & Application

Mirror writing creates unique harmonic textures with inherent symmetry, generates complex sonorities from simpler sources, produces fresh sounds through the "unnatural" quality of reflective inversion, and enables thematic development where melody and inversion breed mirrored vertical structures.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Maintaining strict reflection in all voices when partial would serve better
  **Correction**: Partial reflection (free inner voices) provides greater harmonic flexibility while preserving mirror character

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Mirror writing equals canonic imitation
  **Clarification**: Mirror writing is simultaneous and inverted; canonic imitation is successive and may or may not be inverted
- **Confusion**: Strict mirror is always preferable
  **Clarification**: Strict and partial exist on a spectrum; the appropriate degree depends on musical context
