# Quick Definition

A compositional technique in which melodic or harmonic material is presented with its exact intervallic inversion sounding simultaneously, creating symmetrical reflection around a central axis.

# Formal Definition

**Mirror writing** is a compositional technique employing simultaneous presentation of musical material and its strict intervallic inversion. The inversion creates symmetrical reflection around one or more building tones (the axis of symmetry). This technique can be applied to chords, melodies, and complete textures, producing vertical structures and horizontal progressions with inherent reflective properties.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Four types of mirror chord writing:
1. **Stationary building tone**: A single fixed tone generates reflection above and below
2. **Moving building tone**: The axis tone moves while maintaining reflective relationship
3. **Reflective building tones**: Building tones themselves move in contrary motion
4. **Free building tones**: Building tones used without strict reflective constraints

Degrees of strictness:
- **Strict mirror**: All ornamental tones kept in reflective relationship
- **Partial reflection**: Outer voices mirrored while inner voices move freely

# Musical Context/Application

Mirror writing applications:
- Creating unique harmonic textures with inherent symmetry
- Generating complex sonorities from simpler source material
- Producing fresh sounds through the "unnatural" quality of reflective inversion
- Thematic development where melody and its inversion breed mirrored vertical structures
- Keyboard writing with equalized hand movement and uniform technique

Mirror writing may stem from thematic sources: an initial melody constructed so that part is the inversion of another part; subsequent simultaneous use of original and inversion breeds mirrored vertical structures.

# Common Confusions

- **Mirror vs. canon/imitation**: Mirror writing is simultaneous and inverted; canonic imitation is successive and may or may not be inverted.
- **Strict vs. partial**: Mirror writing exists on a spectrum; greater freedom in inner voices allows more harmonic flexibility while maintaining reflective character.
- **Horizontal vs. vertical**: Mirror writing affects both melodic lines and vertical chord structures through the same principle.
