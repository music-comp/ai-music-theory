# Quick Definition
The inversion of a scale's interval pattern, which reveals hidden harmonic potentialities and provides fresh compositional material.

# Formal Definition
Further harmonic possibilities may be tested by considering the mirrored version of a scale. Scanning intervallic points between the original and mirrored versions reveals hidden potentialities of the scale. Fresh material such as cadential patterns, passing textures, and outer-voice movement is suggested and brought into focus by reflection.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Mirror construction:
- Original intervals read ascending become descending
- W-W-H pattern becomes H-W-W (reversed)
- Some scales are reflectively identical (mirror = retrograde of original)

Reflectively identical scales:
- Dorian mode
- Chromatic scale
- Whole-tone scale
- Any scale with palindromic interval structure

# Musical Context/Application
Comparing a scale with its mirror reveals new compositional resources including cadential patterns, passing textures, and contrapuntal possibilities. Some scales, when inverted, produce an exact duplication of the original in retrograde, indicating their symmetrical structure.

# Common Confusions
Students may confuse mirroring with transposition. Mirroring inverts the interval pattern (turning ascending intervals into descending), creating a related but distinct scale that may share harmonic resources with the original.
