# Quick Definition
A rarely used ninth chord variant where one of the component thirds is smaller than a minor third, creating a harmonic squeeze (diminished third or its inversion, the augmented sixth) that demands chromatic resolution.

# Core Definition
One of the thirds that makes up a ninth chord may be (but rarely is) smaller than a minor third. The harmonic squeeze caused by the diminished third, or its inversion the augmented sixth, creates a need for chromatic resolution. This construction is unusual because it introduces a strong directional tendency into what might otherwise be a stable ninth chord formation. Unlike standard ninth chords that can function as consonances in twentieth-century harmony, this variant retains a quasi-dissonant character requiring resolution.

# Key Properties
1. Diminished third = enharmonically equivalent to major second
2. Its inversion (augmented sixth) = enharmonically equivalent to minor seventh
3. Creates "harmonic squeeze" demanding chromatic resolution
4. Rarely used -- most ninth chords exploit stability and independence from resolution
5. Introduces directional tendency into otherwise stable formation
6. Resolution is chromatic (upper note descends, lower ascends, or both converge)

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify a third in the ninth chord construction smaller than a minor third
- The harmonic squeeze is audible as chromatic tension
- Resolution follows the contraction principle of the diminished third
- Distinguish from standard ninth chords that are stable and non-resolving

# Context & Application
The diminished third within a ninth chord is a specialized effect introducing the harmonic tension associated with the augmented sixth chord into the context of extended tertian harmony. Composers use this when they want a ninth chord with built-in resolution tendency. The technique is rare precisely because most twentieth-century uses of ninth chords exploit their stability and independence from resolution.

# Common Confusions
- **Rare vs. standard**: This is an exceptional variant, not a normal ninth chord behavior
- **Enharmonic identity**: The diminished third sounds like a major second but functions differently due to harmonic context
