# Quick Definition
A rarely-used variant of ninth chord construction where one of the thirds is smaller than a minor third, creating a harmonic squeeze that produces a need for chromatic resolution.

# Formal Definition
One of the thirds that makes up a ninth chord may be (but rarely is) smaller than a minor third. The harmonic squeeze caused by the diminished third, or its inversion the augmented sixth, creates a need for chromatic resolution. This construction is unusual because it introduces a strong directional tendency into what might otherwise be a stable ninth chord formation.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
**Interval Relationships:**
- Diminished third = enharmonically equivalent to major second
- Augmented sixth (inversion) = enharmonically equivalent to minor seventh

**Resolution Tendency:**
The diminished third contracts chromatically, pulling toward resolution:
- Upper note descends by half step, OR
- Lower note ascends by half step, OR
- Both notes move toward each other

**Context:**
Unlike standard ninth chords which can function as consonances in twentieth-century harmony, ninth chords containing a diminished third retain a directional, quasi-dissonant character requiring resolution.

# Musical Context/Application
The diminished third within a ninth chord is a specialized effect. It introduces the harmonic tension associated with the German augmented sixth chord into the context of extended tertian harmony. Composers use this construction when they want a ninth chord with built-in resolution tendency, rather than a stable ninth formation.

The technique is rare precisely because most twentieth-century uses of ninth chords exploit their stability and independence from resolution requirements.

# Common Confusions
Students may confuse the diminished third in ninth chord construction with the diminished third found in augmented sixth chords. While enharmonically related, the contexts differ: the augmented sixth chord is a specialized dominant preparation, while the ninth chord with diminished third is a colored extension of standard ninth chord practice.
