# Quick Definition
A systematic ordering of the twelve ninth chord types from darkest to brightest, enabling composers to control harmonic color with precision and to create progressions based on gradual shifts in chord brightness.

# Formal Definition
A workable order of the twelve ninth chords from darkest to brightest is material for a composer's craft. When color gradations of the ninth formations are made part of the composer's aural apparatus, textural control of the various ninth chords becomes possible. Ninths used with technical facility add freshness to harmonic progression. The ordering allows interchange between certain adjacent chords of similar color.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
**Color Gradation Spectrum:**
The twelve ninth chords can be arranged from darkest to brightest based on their intervallic content:

- Darkest: Chords with more minor intervals, particularly minor ninths
- Brightest: Chords with more major intervals, particularly major ninths

**Interchange Principle:**
Certain chords in the gradation (indicated by arrows in the source) may be interchanged because they occupy similar positions on the color spectrum.

**Harmonization Potential:**
Since any note may be considered the root, third, fifth, seventh, or ninth of twelve different ninth chords, a single tone may be harmonized with sixty different ninths. For example, with the note E in the top voice, five different minor-minor ninth chords are possible.

# Musical Context/Application
Understanding ninth chord color gradation enables composers to:
1. Create smooth progressions by moving to adjacent color positions
2. Create dramatic contrasts by jumping between distant color positions
3. Harmonize melodies with consistent or deliberately varied harmonic color
4. Control the emotional temperature of passages through chord selection

The technique is particularly useful in sequential passages where color shifts can be systematically organized.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes confuse chord quality (major, minor, etc.) with chord color (bright, dark). While related, color gradation considers the total intervallic content of the ninth chord, not just the quality of its constituent thirds. A chord with a major quality can still occupy a relatively dark position if it contains other darkening intervals.
