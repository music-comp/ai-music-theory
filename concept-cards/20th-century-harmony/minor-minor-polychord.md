# Quick Definition

A polychord of two minor triads, the least resonant diatonic triad combination, with numbers 1, 5, and 9 most frequently used due to their relatively greater resonance.

# Core Definition

"In minor-minor polychords, the resonant numbers 1, 5, and 9 are most frequently used" (Persichetti, p. 142). Both units generate weaker overtone series due to minor quality, resulting in the least resonant of the diatonic polychord types.

# Prerequisites

- **Polychord** — The minor-minor is a subtype of polychord
- **Polychord resonance** — Resonance ranking places this type last among diatonic combinations

# Key Properties

1. Both units minor triads: least resonant of diatonic polychord types
2. Numbers 1, 5, and 9 most frequently used
3. Resonance hierarchy: Major-Major > Major-Minor > Minor-Major > Minor-Minor
4. Both units generate weaker overtone series; less natural reinforcement
5. Darker overall color; somber quality
6. Requires careful spacing for clarity

# Construction / Recognition

## To Construct
1. Place minor triad as bottom unit
2. Place minor triad as upper unit at desired transposition
3. Select from resonant combinations (1, 5, 9)
4. Use wider spacing and contrasting timbres to compensate for reduced resonance

# Context & Application

Minor-minor polychords create the darkest diatonic polychordal sonority. Their reduced resonance makes them suitable for somber passages, textural contrast with brighter polychords, and specific coloristic effects. Limited resonance requires attention to spacing, orchestration, and duration.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Using minor-minor in sustained passages without compensating for reduced resonance
  **Correction**: Use wider spacing, contrasting instrumentation, and limit duration to prevent opacity

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Minor-minor is a chromatic polychord
  **Clarification**: It is diatonic (both triads occur in diatonic scales); chromatic polychords contain augmented or diminished triads
- **Confusion**: Limited usefulness means the type should be avoided
  **Clarification**: The somber, dark quality is a distinctive resource when properly handled
