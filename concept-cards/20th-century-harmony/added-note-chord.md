# Quick Definition
A basic harmonic formation (triad or chord by thirds/fourths) whose textural quality has been modified by the imposition of tones not found in the original chord, specifically tones forming major or minor seconds with existing chord members.

# Formal Definition
An added-note chord is a chord by thirds or fourths to which one or more tones have been attached that form major or minor seconds with any member of the basic chord. These added notes are color modifications that change the texture rather than the function of the basic structure. The added tones are usually placed a second above or below any chord member to avoid creating seventh, ninth, or compound chord structures.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Construction principle:
- Start with a basic chord (triad, seventh chord, or quartal chord)
- Add tones that form M2 or m2 with any chord member
- Placement: second above or below any member

Traditional examples:
- Cadential tonic six-five (triad with added sixth)
- French augmented sixth chord (Italian +6 with M2 added above middle tone)

Key distinction:
- Added notes = color modifications attached to clear functional harmony
- Extensions (9ths, 11ths, 13ths) = tertian additions that expand the harmonic structure

# Musical Context/Application
Added-note chords function as color variants of their parent structures, preserving harmonic direction while adding textural interest. The added tones do not compete with traditional dissonances (sevenths, ninths) for resolution; they cling to the chord without demanding independent voice-leading. This technique was common in early 20th-century French music and became widespread in jazz and popular styles.

Added notes may be doubled at will, especially effective when occurring as successive octaves in melodic coupling. Over-all coupling sounds well when used throughout a body of added-note harmony.

# Common Confusions
- Added-note chords vs. extended tertian chords: Added notes create color without changing function; extensions build larger tertian structures
- Added-note chords vs. chords by seconds: Out of context they may be identical; only the surrounding harmony determines the category
- A large compound chord and a complex added-note chord are often identical; unless a firm harmonic progression is felt under the structure, compound harmony will prevail
