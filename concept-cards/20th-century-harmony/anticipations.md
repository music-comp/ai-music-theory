# Quick Definition
Nonharmonic tones that arrive early, sounding a tone belonging to the following harmony before that harmony arrives.

# Formal Definition
Anticipations belong to the hybrid group of nonharmonic tones formed by rhythmic misplacement of chord tones. In anticipation, movement is hastened: a tone belonging to the following chord sounds early, before the harmonic change. The anticipation is the rhythmic opposite of the suspension: where suspensions delay, anticipations rush ahead of the harmonic progression.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Anticipation characteristics:
- **Approach**: Typically by step from current chord tone
- **Timing**: Sounds before the harmony to which it belongs
- **Resolution**: Becomes consonant when new harmony arrives
- **Rhythmic pattern**: Weak-beat entrance of upcoming chord tone

The anticipation creates a sense of forward motion and harmonic impatience, propelling the music toward the next harmonic event.

# Musical Context/Application
In twentieth-century harmony, anticipations create rhythmic drive and harmonic overlap. They may anticipate tones of complex chords, and multiple anticipations can suggest the new harmony before it fully arrives. Anticipations contribute to the fluidity of twentieth-century texture, where harmonic boundaries become permeable. The hastened quality of anticipations contrasts with the delay of suspensions.

# Common Confusions
- Anticipations and suspensions are rhythmic opposites: anticipations hasten, suspensions delay
- Anticipations become consonant when the new harmony arrives; they do not resolve by motion
- Multiple anticipations may create polychordal overlap between harmonies
