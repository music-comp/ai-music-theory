# Quick Definition
Nonharmonic tones that arrive early, sounding a tone belonging to the following harmony before that harmony arrives, the rhythmic opposite of the suspension.

# Core Definition
In **anticipation**, movement is hastened: a tone belonging to the following chord sounds early, before the harmonic change. The anticipation is the rhythmic opposite of the suspension: where suspensions delay, anticipations rush ahead. Multiple anticipations can suggest the new harmony before it fully arrives, creating harmonic overlap (Ch. 11, pp. 229-231).

# Prerequisites
- **Nonharmonic tones** -- understanding the classification system

# Key Properties
1. Tone belonging to following chord sounds early
2. Becomes consonant when new harmony arrives
3. Rhythmic opposite of suspension (hastened vs. delayed)
4. Creates forward motion and harmonic impatience
5. Multiple anticipations may suggest new harmony prematurely
6. Creates harmonic overlap between successive chords

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify a tone that belongs to the next chord but sounds before the harmonic change
- Verify it becomes consonant upon arrival of the new harmony
- Note whether multiple anticipations create harmonic overlap

# Context & Application
Anticipations create rhythmic drive and harmonic overlap, contributing to the fluidity of twentieth-century texture where harmonic boundaries become permeable.

# Common Errors
- Confusing anticipation with suspension
- Not exploiting the harmonic overlap potential of multiple anticipations

# Common Confusions
- Anticipations and suspensions are rhythmic opposites
- Anticipations become consonant when new harmony arrives; they do not resolve by motion
- Multiple anticipations may create polychordal overlap between harmonies
