# Quick Definition
The sounding of a melodic line by various media at the same pitch or at different octaves, providing significant textural contrast and coloristic variety in twentieth-century composition, where success depends on keen formal timing.

# Core Definition
**Unison writing** implies the sounding of a line by various media at the same pitch or at different octaves. Much use is made of unison texture in twentieth-century composition; it has significant formal and coloristic functions. Its chief value in a harmonic scheme is its textural contrast. The success of unison writing depends upon keen formal timing (Ch. 11, pp. 243-245).

# Prerequisites
- **Harmonic progression** -- understanding the harmonic context from which unison departs

# Key Properties
1. Line sounded at same pitch or different octaves by various media
2. Chief value: textural contrast within harmonic scheme
3. Success depends on keen formal timing
4. Uses include: opening/interrupting calls, fanfare flourishes, pastoral interludes, veiled lines (octaves apart), antiphonal answers, added string power, broken-chord outlines
5. Effective for: stating theme without harmony, contrasting single line with chordal mass, strengthening loud passages, subsiding sound, rushing octaves gathering power, introducing new tonal areas by outlining scale, projecting rhythmic variants in recitative style

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify passages where all parts converge on a single line (possibly at different octaves)
- Note the formal position of the unison (opening, interruption, transition, climax, subsiding)
- Assess whether the timing serves a clear formal purpose

# Context & Application
Unison writing provides essential textural contrast in harmonic music. It can serve as transitional device between harmonic textures, as the ambiguity of sudden unison allows for any texture to follow. Unison can outline harmonic activity through broken chords, making it versatile for connecting harmonic regions.

# Common Errors
- Using unison without clear formal justification (poor timing)
- Extending unison writing beyond its effective duration
- Neglecting the textural ambiguity that unison creates for transition purposes

# Common Confusions
- Unison includes octave doubling, not just same-pitch doubling
- Success depends on timing and formal placement, not just the technique itself
- Unison can serve harmonic functions by outlining chords through broken-chord patterns
