# Quick Definition
The sounding of a melodic line by various media at the same pitch or at different octaves, providing textural contrast and coloristic variety.

# Formal Definition
Unison writing implies the sounding of a line by various media at the same pitch or at different octaves. Much use is made of unison texture in twentieth-century composition for significant formal and coloristic functions. Its chief value in a harmonic scheme is textural contrast. The success of unison writing depends upon keen formal timing.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Unison writing functions:
- **Opening/interrupting calls**: Dramatic gestures
- **Fanfare flourishes**: Bold statements
- **Pastoral interludes**: Simple textures
- **Veiled lines**: Octave-doubled atmospheric passages
- **Antiphonal answers**: Call-and-response patterns
- **Added power**: String or instrumental reinforcement
- **Broken chord outlines**: Harmonic skeleton revealed

Applications:
- State theme without harmonic fabric
- Contrast single line with chordal mass
- Add strength to loud, full passages
- Create subsiding sound or gathering power
- Introduce new tonal area by outlining scale
- Project rhythmic variants in recitative style

# Musical Context/Application
Unison writing provides essential textural contrast in harmonic music. It can serve as a transitional device between different harmonic textures, as the ambiguity of sudden unison allows for the entrance of any texture. Unison can outline harmonic activity through broken chords, making it a versatile tool for connecting different harmonic regions or types.

# Common Confusions
- Unison includes octave doubling, not just same-pitch doubling
- Success depends on timing and formal placement, not just the technique
- Unison can serve harmonic functions by outlining chords
