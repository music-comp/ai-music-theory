# Quick Definition
Two or more clusters, derived from one scale or different scales, sounding simultaneously with sufficient space between units to allow each to sound distinctly, with roots forming chords by seconds, triads, or fourths.

# Formal Definition
Two or more clusters, derived from one scale or different scales, may be used simultaneously as a polycluster. The roots of the various cluster units of a polycluster may form a chord by seconds, a triad, or a chord by fourths. Space must be left between the cluster units to allow them to sound distinctly. The larger each cluster, the wider must be the space.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Construction elements:
1. **Cluster units**: Two or more independent clusters
2. **Spacing requirement**: Gaps between units for distinct perception
3. **Root relationships**:
   - By seconds (secundal root structure)
   - By thirds (triadic root structure)
   - By fourths (quartal root structure)

Size-space relationship:
- Larger clusters require wider spacing
- Insufficient space causes units to merge into single cluster
- Spacing is proportional to cluster density

Contrapuntal implications:
- If cluster sizes fluctuate, contrapuntal voices automatically double
- Each cluster outlined by two independent voices (top and bottom)
- Results in 4+ voice counterpoint (2 voices per cluster minimum)

Scale derivation:
- From single scale: homogeneous color
- From different scales: contrasting colors
- Mixed derivation creates rich textural variety

# Musical Context/Application
Polyclusters extend the polychordal principle to secundal harmony. They create massive, complex sonorities while maintaining some structural clarity through the separation of units. The technique is most effective when the ear can distinguish the component clusters.

Effective use requires:
- Clear spacing between units
- Distinct registral placement
- Possible timbral differentiation (in orchestral settings)
- Attention to root relationships for structural coherence

The number of contrapuntal voices is automatically doubled when cluster sizes fluctuate, as each cluster requires at least two boundary voices.

# Common Confusions
- Polyclusters require separation; merged clusters are single clusters, not polyclusters
- The root relationship is conceptual, not functional in the traditional sense
- Fluctuating sizes automatically create more voices to track
- Polyclusters are not simply "big clusters" but structured combinations
