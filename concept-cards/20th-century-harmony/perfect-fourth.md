# Quick Definition
An interval of five semitones that uniquely functions as either consonance or dissonance depending entirely on its harmonic context.

# Formal Definition
The perfect fourth is an interval that sounds consonant in dissonant surroundings and dissonant in consonant surroundings. It is difficult to classify out of musical context, as its quality is determined by the other intervals present in the harmonic structure. In a chord, the perfect fourth's consonant-dissonant quality is defined by the interval formed between the bass note and the note not involved with the fourth.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
- Five semitones
- Frequency ratio: 4:3
- Inversion of the perfect fifth
- In chords: when the interval between bass and the non-fourth note is a mild/sharp dissonance, the fourth sounds like an open consonance; when it is a soft consonance, the fourth sounds mildly dissonant
- When spaced more than an octave apart, the perfect fourth becomes stronger

# Musical Context/Application
The presence of a perfect fourth in a chord lends ambiguity because of this interval's ability to function either as consonance or dissonance. The fourth is classifiable only in its total interval context, with other intervals in the chord determining its character. Inverting the perfect fifth alters its basic function, transforming this solid interval into the unstable perfect fourth.

# Common Confusions
Students often try to classify the perfect fourth as either consonant or dissonant in absolute terms. Its unique characteristic is that its function depends entirely on context, making it a chameleon-like interval in twentieth-century harmony.
