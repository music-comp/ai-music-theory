# Quick Definition
An interval of five semitones that uniquely functions as either consonance or dissonance depending entirely on its harmonic context.

# Core Definition
The perfect fourth (4:3 ratio) sounds consonant in dissonant surroundings and dissonant in consonant surroundings. It is difficult to classify out of musical context. In a chord, the perfect fourth's consonant-dissonant quality is defined by the interval formed between the bass note and the note not involved with the fourth: when this interval is a mild or sharp dissonance, the fourth sounds like an open consonance; when it is a soft consonance, the fourth sounds mildly dissonant. The fourth is the inversion of the perfect fifth, but inverting the fifth alters its basic function, transforming this solid interval into the unstable fourth. When spaced beyond an octave, the fourth becomes stronger.

# Key Properties
1. **Five semitones**, 4:3 frequency ratio
2. **Context-dependent**: consonant in dissonant surroundings, dissonant in consonant surroundings
3. **Inversion of P5**: but with fundamentally different character
4. **In chords**: character determined by the bass-to-non-fourth-note interval
5. **Compound spacing**: becomes stronger beyond the octave
6. **Ambiguity**: lends ambiguity to any chord containing it

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify intervals of 5 semitones
2. Assess the surrounding harmonic context to determine function
3. In three-note chords: examine the interval between bass and the note not forming the fourth
4. If that interval is dissonant, the fourth sounds consonant; if consonant, the fourth sounds dissonant

# Context & Application
The presence of a perfect fourth in a chord lends ambiguity. The chord is classifiable only in its total interval context, with other intervals determining the fourth's character. This chameleon-like behavior makes the fourth a uniquely flexible compositional resource.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Classifying the P4 as always consonant or always dissonant. **Correction**: Its function depends entirely on the surrounding intervals.
- **Error**: Treating P4 and P5 as functionally equivalent. **Correction**: Inversion fundamentally changes function; the solid fifth becomes the unstable fourth.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Applying common-practice "dissonant fourth" rule universally. **Clarification**: In 20th-century practice, the fourth's function is determined by total interval context, not by a fixed rule.
