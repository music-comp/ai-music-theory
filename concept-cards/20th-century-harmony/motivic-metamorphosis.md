# Quick Definition
The transformation and development of motivic material through various compositional procedures, giving structural units their life and identity.

# Formal Definition
Development of thematic material involves motivic metamorphosis; the life of a structural unit depends to a great extent upon the transformative character of its elements. The many motivic cells that make up a theme are the concern of the composer when extending harmonic material. The motivic fragments chosen and the way they are transformed are elements that give a work its personal identity.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Metamorphosis techniques:
- **Intervallic expansion**: Widening intervals
- **Intervallic contraction**: Narrowing intervals
- **Order change**: Rearranging pitch sequence
- **Inversion**: Mirror image
- **Retrograde**: Reversed order
- **Retrograde inversion**: Both reversed and inverted
- **Augmentation**: Lengthened durations
- **Diminution**: Shortened durations
- **Dismemberment**: Parts held for later expansion
- **Filling out**: Ornaments producing new segments
- **Octave displacement**: Shifting register

Identity and transformation:
- Original identity often obscured
- Unified expression unfolds nonetheless
- Transformation gives life to structural units

# Musical Context/Application
Motivic metamorphosis is central to compositional craft in twentieth-century music. The transformation techniques allow a small amount of material to generate extensive musical development while maintaining underlying unity. Even when the original motif becomes unrecognizable, the transformation process creates coherent musical expression.
