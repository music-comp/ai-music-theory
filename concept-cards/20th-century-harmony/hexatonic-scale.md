# Quick Definition
Six-note scales that include the whole-tone scale and various other formations, providing more harmonic flexibility than pentatonic scales due to the additional interval.

# Formal Definition
There are various kinds of basic six-tone or hexatonic scales. The modal construction technique that produces the seven diatonic modes and five modes of pentatonics produces six modes of each hexatonic scale type, except the whole-tone scale which has no modes. Harmonic monotony is somewhat more easily avoided than in pentatonic scales because of the additional interval.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Hexatonic types:
- Whole-tone (W-W-W-W-W-W): symmetrical, no modes
- Augmented (m3-H-m3-H-m3-H): symmetrical
- Various asymmetrical hexatonics with 6 modes each

Modal generation:
- 6 modes for asymmetrical hexatonics
- 1 form for whole-tone (self-generating)
- Limited forms for other symmetrical hexatonics

# Musical Context/Application
The hexatonic scale (excepting whole-tone) has a primarily melodic function. When used for melodic writing, a complete and fully independent line evolves. The harmony is usually non-hexatonic and moves independently of melody implications. In hexatonic passages, melody and harmony are often in polytonal relationship.

# Common Confusions
Students often equate hexatonic with whole-tone. While the whole-tone is a hexatonic scale, there are many other six-note scales with different intervallic properties and more modal flexibility.
