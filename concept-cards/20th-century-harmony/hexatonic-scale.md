# Quick Definition
Six-note scales that include the whole-tone scale and various other formations, offering more harmonic flexibility than pentatonic scales but functioning primarily melodically.

# Core Definition
There are various kinds of basic six-tone or hexatonic scales. The modal construction technique produces six modes of each hexatonic scale type, except the whole-tone scale (which has no distinct modes). Harmonic monotony is somewhat more easily avoided than in pentatonic scales because of the additional interval. The hexatonic scale (excepting whole-tone) has a primarily melodic function -- when used for melodic writing, a complete and fully independent line evolves. The harmony is usually non-hexatonic and moves independently, with the two forces often in polytonal relationship.

# Key Properties
1. **Six modes** for asymmetrical hexatonics (not for whole-tone)
2. **More flexible** than pentatonic due to additional interval
3. **Primarily melodic function**: generates complete, independent melodic lines
4. **Harmony usually non-hexatonic**: moves independently of melody
5. **Polytonal relationship**: melody and harmony often in different tonal orbits
6. **Two strong forces**: melody (hexatonic) and harmony (non-hexatonic) create fluctuating tension design

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the six-note collection
2. Determine if it is whole-tone (equidistant) or asymmetrical
3. For asymmetrical: identify which of six modal versions is in use
4. Check whether the harmony is hexatonic or independent (non-hexatonic)

# Context & Application
The hexatonic scale provides a melodic foundation over which independent harmony moves freely. The consonant-dissonant tension between the two separate forces -- hexatonic melody and non-hexatonic harmony -- creates its own fluctuating design and shape. Hexatonic passages often have melody and harmony in polytonal relationship, creating complex but coherent textures.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Equating hexatonic with whole-tone. **Correction**: The whole-tone is only one hexatonic type; many others exist with different intervallic properties and modal flexibility.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Trying to harmonize hexatonic melodies with hexatonic chords. **Clarification**: The hexatonic scale functions primarily melodically; harmony typically comes from other sources.
