# Quick Definition

The technique of increasing or decreasing the number of voices in successive chords to create variety in texture and harmonic weight.

# Formal Definition

**Density variation** is the compositional technique of changing the number of voices in successive chords to achieve variety in textural density. This technique affects the perceived weight, fullness, and intensity of harmonic passages. In three- and four-part harmony, spacing concerns often focus on securing maximum sound, but in harmony of more than four parts, doubling and spacing considerations are directed toward securing variety of texture through controlled density changes.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Density factors:
- **Number of voices**: More voices = greater density
- **Pitch spacing**: Closer spacing = greater density perception
- **Register distribution**: Voices in same register = greater density
- **Doubling**: Octave doublings add weight without new pitch content

Applications:
- Add voices approaching climax
- Remove voices for transparency
- Sudden density change for dramatic effect
- Gradual density change for smooth transitions

# Musical Context/Application

Density variation serves:
- Creating textural variety within harmonic passages
- Building and releasing tension through voice count
- Articulating formal sections through textural contrast
- Providing breath and transparency in complex harmony

Considerations:
- Dramatic conditions affect optimal density concentration
- Arrival on consonant chord in dissonant context may need extensive doubling to avoid sound loss
- Various colors and weights available through selective omission and doubling

# Common Confusions

- **Density vs. dissonance**: Density concerns voice count and spacing; dissonance concerns intervallic relationships. A dense texture may be consonant.
- **More voices vs. louder**: Density adds weight and fullness but is not simply dynamic increase.
- **Consistent vs. varied density**: Both have valid uses; the choice depends on musical context and expressive intent.
