# Quick Definition
Increasing or decreasing the number of voices in successive chords to create variety in texture and harmonic weight.

# Core Definition
The number of voices in successive chords may be increased or decreased for variety in density. The spacing concern in three- and four-part harmony is often one of securing maximum sound, but in harmony of more than four parts, doubling and spacing considerations are directed to securing variety of texture. Various colors and weights are obtainable through omission and doubling of both dissonant and consonant chord members. The density and concentration of tonal sound varies under different dramatic conditions (Ch. 9, pp. 191-192, 197).

# Prerequisites
- **Chord connection** -- understanding voice-leading between chords

# Key Properties
1. Voice count changes affect perceived weight, fullness, and intensity
2. Three-/four-part: focus on maximum sound
3. More than four parts: focus on textural variety
4. Colors and weights come from selective omission and doubling
5. Density varies according to dramatic conditions
6. In orchestral arrival of dissonant material upon consonant climax, extensive doubling may be needed

# Construction / Recognition
- Count the number of independent voices at successive chord changes
- Note whether voice count increases or decreases over a passage
- Observe whether density changes correspond to dramatic or formal events
- Check spacing and doubling choices in multi-voice passages

# Context & Application
Density variation serves textural variety, tension building and release through voice count, formal articulation through textural contrast, and providing breath and transparency in complex harmony. A climactic harmonic point may be left vacant by rests (Ex. 9-22). The technique is essential for orchestral writing where balancing density across sections requires careful management.

# Common Errors
- Maintaining uniform density throughout a passage, losing textural variety
- Adding voices without considering the dramatic context
- Neglecting to provide extensive doubling at consonant climactic arrivals in dissonant contexts

# Common Confusions
- **Density vs. dissonance**: Density concerns voice count and spacing; dissonance concerns intervallic relationships
- **More voices vs. louder**: Density adds weight and fullness but is not simply dynamic increase
- **Consistent vs. varied density**: Both have valid uses depending on context
