# Quick Definition
The blending of chords by fourths with chords by seconds (including clusters), achieved by emphasizing the second intervals present in quartal voicings or inverting the seventh in ninth chords to reveal the secundal texture.

# Core Definition
The voices of an inverted seventh chord may be arranged so that the interval of the second is emphasized and the contextual way prepared for secundal harmony. In ninth chords, the interval of the seventh from the root or third is inverted to suggest the secundal texture of clusters or added-note chords. The characteristic interval of one kind of harmony is contained in any other kind and may be brought to the surface. When moving from one type to another, the characteristic interval of the new harmony is best introduced well in advance.

# Key Properties
1. Seventh chord voicings can emphasize seconds to prepare secundal entry
2. Ninth chord's seventh from root/third inverts to second, suggesting clusters
3. Secundal chords contain wider intervals that prepare quartal return
4. The second/seventh inversional pair is the bridge interval
5. Added-note chords provide intermediate texture between quartal and secundal
6. Preparation should begin well in advance

# Construction / Recognition
- In inverted seventh chords, arrange voices to feature second intervals
- In ninth chords, invert the seventh interval to create seconds
- Watch for clusters emerging from added-note accretions
- In secundal passages, note wider intervals suggesting quartal return
- The transition is bidirectional and can pass through intermediate textures

# Context & Application
Quartal-secundal blending represents a more distant textural transition than tertian-quartal integration, but the same principle applies: shared intervals provide the bridge. The seventh/second inversional relationship connects quartal and secundal textures through intermediate formations (seventh and ninth chords). Added-note chords serve as natural intermediate textures, containing both quartal and secundal characteristics. Cluster subtraction provides an additional pathway from secundal to quartal texture.

# Common Errors
- Attempting direct quartal-to-cluster transition without intermediate preparation
- Ignoring the seventh/second inversional bridge
- Treating quartal and secundal as completely unrelated textures

# Common Confusions
- **Intermediate formations**: Seventh and ninth chords naturally bridge quartal and secundal textures

- **Second = inverted seventh**: The inversional relationship is the foundation of this blending technique
