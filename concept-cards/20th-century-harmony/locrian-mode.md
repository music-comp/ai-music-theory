# Quick Definition
The darkest diatonic mode with a diminished tonic triad, requiring special techniques to establish tonic stability due to its tritone relationship with the fifth scale degree.

# Core Definition
Locrian is unmistakable because of its diminished tonic triad. The diminished triad occurs on the tonic itself, creating a fundamental instability problem. If the diminished tonic is altered chromatically to avoid tritone involvement with the key center chord, the locrian flavor is lessened. The mode is infrequently used but has distinct compositional applications. Since the tonic triad contains the unstable tritone, melodic use may be made of the chords on the first and fifth scale steps, and the tritone relationship considered a thematic characteristic.

# Key Properties
1. **Structure**: H-W-W-H-W-W-W
2. **Diminished tonic triad**: the only mode where the tonic itself is diminished
3. **Position**: "darkest" mode in the brightness spectrum (most flats)
4. **Five tonic alternatives**:
   - Omit the fifth (open fifth / power chord)
   - Use first inversion (subdues the tritone)
   - Add notes (added-note chord)
   - Total unison (obliterates the tritone)
   - Treat I-V tritone as thematic characteristic
5. **Chromatic alteration risk**: altering the tonic to avoid the tritone lessens the locrian flavor

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the mode by its H-W-W-H-W-W-W pattern
2. Note the diminished tonic triad
3. Assess which tonic strategy is being used (omission, inversion, addition, unison, or thematic use)
4. Check whether the tritone is treated as a problem or a feature

# Context & Application
The locrian mode presents a unique compositional challenge: its defining characteristic (the diminished tonic) is also its primary instability. Composers can embrace this instability as a thematic element or manage it through the various tonic alternatives. The mode is most effective in short passages or where its characteristic tension serves the musical narrative.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Avoiding locrian entirely due to its unstable tonic. **Correction**: The instability can be managed through specific techniques or embraced as a thematic feature.
- **Error**: Chromatically altering the tonic to make it "normal." **Correction**: This destroys the locrian flavor; use the tonic alternatives instead.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking locrian is "unusable." **Clarification**: While infrequently used, locrian has distinct compositional applications, especially for short passages or when the tritone relationship serves as a thematic element.
