# Quick Definition
The darkest diatonic mode with a diminished tonic triad, requiring special techniques to establish tonic stability due to its tritone relationship with the fifth scale degree.

# Formal Definition
Locrian is unmistakable because of its diminished tonic triad. In locrian, the diminished triad occurs on the tonic, but if this diminished tonic is altered chromatically to avoid tritone involvement with the key center chord, the locrian flavor is lessened. The mode is infrequently used but has distinct compositional applications.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Structure: H-W-W-H-W-W-W (natural minor with lowered 2nd and 5th)
Characteristic: diminished 5th from tonic (unstable tonic triad)
Position: "darkest" mode (most flats in brightness spectrum)

Tonic triad solutions:
- Omit the fifth (power chord/open fifth)
- Use first inversion (subdues tritone)
- Add notes (added-note chord)
- Total unison (obliterates tritone)
- Treat I-V tritone as thematic characteristic

# Musical Context/Application
The locrian tonic triad contains the unstable tritone, making melodic use of the relationship between the first and fifth scale steps possible as a thematic characteristic. Various techniques avoid or embrace the tritone: omitting the fifth, using inversions, adding notes, or employing total unison on the tonic.

# Common Confusions
Students may avoid locrian due to its unstable tonic or try to "fix" it with chromatic alterations that destroy its character. The tritone relationship can be treated as a compositional feature rather than a problem to be solved.
