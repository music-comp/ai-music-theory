# Quick Definition
A polychord constructed of two major triads, which is the most consonant and resonant polychord type, with the twelve possible combinations arranged in order of decreasing consonance following the cycle of fifths from the bass note of the bottom six-four chord.

# Formal Definition
When a polychord is constructed of major triads it is most consonant and resonant. A complete inventory of major-major polychords may be made by building the upper major triads on the notes of a cycle of perfect fifths that begins with the bass note of the bottom six-four chordal unit. The triads are added, in turn, to the bottom six-four chord, creating a rising order, based upon the cycle of fifths, which places the chords in a natural sequence of decreasing consonance and increasing dissonance.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Twelve major-major combinations (bass note C, bottom unit = C major six-four):

| Number | Upper Root | Relationship | Character |
|--------|-----------|--------------|-----------|
| 1 | C | Unison | Most resonant |
| 2 | G | P5 above | Very resonant |
| 3 | D | M2 above | Resonant |
| 4 | A | M6 above | Moderately resonant |
| 5 | E | M3 above | Moderate |
| 6 | B | M7 above | Less resonant |
| 7 | F# | A4 above | Nebulous |
| 8 | Db | m2 above | Sharp |
| 9 | Ab | m6 above | Less resonant |
| 10 | Eb | m3 above | Less resonant |
| 11 | Bb | m7 above | Almost dull thud |
| 12 | F | P4 above | Forms tertian chord |

Note: Numbers 1 and 12 may form chords by thirds out of context but function polychordally in polychordal environment.

# Musical Context/Application
Major-major polychords are the most consonant and are frequently used for stable, tonic-like polychordal sonorities. The cycle-of-fifths ordering provides a systematic approach to selecting upper triads for specific resonance qualities.

The last six polychords are noticeably less resonant; the sound of number 11 is almost a "dull thud." Even less resonant polychords have value for specific dramatic or coloristic purposes.

# Common Confusions
- The ordering is by resonance (overtone relationship), not by "brightness" or "darkness"
- Numbers 1 and 12 sound like single tertian chords out of context
- The six-four bottom unit provides maximum resonance as bottom chord
- Less resonant does not mean less useful
