# Quick Definition
Five-note scales with various intervallic arrangements, which are harmonically limited due to lack of semitones but melodically distinctive.

# Formal Definition
There are various kinds of basic five-tone or pentatonic scales. The diatonic pentatonic (the most common type) contains no semitones, creating harmonic limitations. The modal construction technique that produces the seven diatonic modes produces five modes of each type of pentatonic scale.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Common pentatonic types:
- Diatonic pentatonic: major scale without 4th and 7th (W-W-m3-W-m3)
- Other pentatonic varieties with different interval distributions

Five modes of diatonic pentatonic:
- Mode 1: C-D-E-G-A (major pentatonic)
- Mode 2: D-E-G-A-C
- Mode 3: E-G-A-C-D
- Mode 4: G-A-C-D-E
- Mode 5: A-C-D-E-G (minor pentatonic)

# Musical Context/Application
Diatonic pentatonic scales are harmonically limited because of the lack of semitones. When all five tones sound together they form a somewhat static chord. It is extremely difficult to achieve harmonic and melodic direction in pure pentatonic form. Pure pentatonic music is most effective for short spans.

# Common Confusions
Students often use only the "black-key pentatonic" without exploring other pentatonic types and modes. Each mode of the pentatonic has distinct melodic characteristics despite sharing the same pitch content.
