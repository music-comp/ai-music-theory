# Quick Definition
The correspondence between large tertian chord structures and scale formations, where eleventh and thirteenth chords often coincide with the intervallic content of various scales, functioning as rich tonic formations.

# Formal Definition
The intervallic make-up of eleventh and thirteenth chords often coincides with various scale formations. The chords become excellent six- and seven-note tonic formations around which polychords and compound structures may function. These eleventh and thirteenth formations are usually guided by a melodic line derived from the scale implied by the rich tonic chord.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
**Correspondence:**
- Eleventh chord (6 notes) = 6-note scale subset
- Thirteenth chord (7 notes) = complete 7-note scale

**Example Equivalences:**
- C major thirteenth (C-E-G-B-D-F-A) = C major scale arranged in thirds
- C-Eb-G-Bb-D-F-A = C dorian scale arranged in thirds
- Various synthetic scales produce corresponding colored thirteenth formations

**Functional Application:**
When a thirteenth chord serves as tonic:
- Melodic material derives from the implied scale
- Polychords and compound structures relate to this tonic
- The rich tonic guides all harmonic activity

# Musical Context/Application
This scale-chord relationship is particularly important when using eleventh and thirteenth chords as stable tonic formations rather than as dominant or transitional harmonies. The complete seventh-chord scale correspondence allows the composer to:

1. Derive melodic material directly from the tonic chord
2. Create polychordal and compound structures that relate to the tonic
3. Establish a harmonic environment where the large chord provides both vertical and horizontal coherence

These formations are seldom found in extensive part-writing due to their density, but they excel as anchor points for more elaborate harmonic schemes.

# Common Confusions
Students may think of large tertian chords only as extensions of simpler dominants, not realizing they can function as stable tonics. When a thirteenth chord equals a complete scale, it becomes the harmonic and melodic center of a passage, not a chord requiring resolution.
