# Quick Definition

The central tone or tones around which mirror chord intervals are symmetrically reflected, serving as the axis of inversion without functioning as a traditional root.

# Formal Definition

A **building tone** is the pitch or pitches located in the center of a mirror chord from which the reflective structure is generated. The building tone serves as the axis of symmetry around which intervals are inverted both above and below. Although the chord members are built from the building tone, it never functions aurally as a root; instead, the entire chordal bulk emerges as a chord in its own right, typically assuming the character of the bottom chord.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

Building tone behaviors:
1. **Stationary**: Single fixed tone generates reflection (simplest form)
2. **Moving**: Single tone moves while maintaining reflective relationships
3. **Reflective pair**: Two building tones move in contrary motion to each other
4. **Free**: Building tones used without strict reflective constraints

When reflective scales have tonics at different pitch levels, the space between building tones affects harmonic character: wider spacing produces more pronounced chord flavors.

Keyboard mirror pivotal points: D and G# serve as reflective pivot points where identical fingering produces strict mirroring.

# Musical Context/Application

Building tones determine:
- The axis around which mirrored structures form
- The intervallic content of the resulting chord (intervals from building tone reflected in both directions)
- The degree of reflective strictness possible in a passage
- The practical keyboard layout for reflective writing

The absence of root function means building tones operate structurally rather than harmonically; analysis focuses on the symmetry relationship rather than tonal function.

# Common Confusions

- **Building tone vs. root**: The building tone is a structural axis, not a functional harmonic root; it does not determine chord quality or progression in the tonal sense.
- **Single vs. multiple building tones**: Mirror chords may have one or more building tones; multiple tones create more complex reflective relationships.
- **Audibility**: The building tone may or may not be a sounded pitch; it is the conceptual center of symmetry.
