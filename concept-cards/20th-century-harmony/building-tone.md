# Quick Definition

The central tone or tones around which mirror chord intervals are symmetrically reflected, serving as the axis of inversion without functioning as a traditional root.

# Core Definition

"Even though the members of mirror chords are built from a tone or tones located in the center of the chord the building tone never functions aurally as a root. It is the entire chordal bulk that emerges as a chord in its own right. Mirror chords usually assume the character of the bottom chord" (Persichetti, p. 172). The keyboard mirror pivotal points are D and G# (p. 178).

# Prerequisites

- **Mirror chord** — Building tones are the axis of mirror chord construction

# Key Properties

1. Located at the center of the mirror chord
2. Serves as axis of symmetry for intervallic reflection
3. Never functions aurally as a root
4. The entire chord emerges as a unit; character comes from the bottom chord
5. Four behaviors: stationary, moving, reflective pair (contrary motion), free
6. Wider spacing between building tones = more pronounced chord flavors
7. Keyboard pivotal points: D and G# (identical fingering in both hands at any of twelve pivots)

# Construction / Recognition

## Building Tone Behaviors
1. **Stationary**: Single fixed tone; simplest mirror generation
2. **Moving**: Tone shifts while maintaining reflective relationship
3. **Reflective pair**: Two tones moving in contrary motion
4. **Free**: Tones used without strict reflective constraint

# Context & Application

Building tones determine the axis of symmetry, the intervallic content of the resulting chord, the degree of reflective strictness possible, and the practical keyboard layout for reflective writing. The absence of root function means analysis focuses on symmetry relationships rather than tonal function.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating the building tone as a functional root for analysis
  **Correction**: The building tone is a structural axis; the chord takes character from the bottom formation

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Building tone is always a sounded pitch
  **Clarification**: It may or may not be sounded; it is the conceptual center of symmetry
- **Confusion**: Single building tone is the only option
  **Clarification**: Multiple building tones create more complex reflective relationships
