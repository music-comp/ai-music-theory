# Quick Definition
The seven distinct seventh chord qualities that can be constructed using only major and minor thirds, ranging from the major-major seventh to the diminished seventh.

# Formal Definition
It is possible to build seven different seventh chords with major and minor third construction. Seventh chords with augmented and diminished thirds are misspellings of other chords. Each of the seven types has a distinct color and character, and twentieth-century composers exploit these differences for harmonic variety.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
**The Seven Seventh Chord Types:**

| Type | Intervallic Structure | Common Name | Example on C |
|------|----------------------|-------------|--------------|
| 1 | M3 + m3 + M3 | Major seventh | C-E-G-B |
| 2 | M3 + m3 + m3 | Dominant seventh | C-E-G-Bb |
| 3 | m3 + M3 + M3 | Minor-major seventh | C-Eb-G-B |
| 4 | m3 + M3 + m3 | Minor seventh | C-Eb-G-Bb |
| 5 | m3 + m3 + M3 | Half-diminished | C-Eb-Gb-Bb |
| 6 | m3 + m3 + m3 | Diminished seventh | C-Eb-Gb-Bbb |
| 7 | M3 + M3 + m3 | Augmented-major seventh | C-E-G#-B |

**First Inversion Spacing in Fifths:**
When spaced in fifths, the first inversion creates a unique sound that enables it to act as a key center chord.

# Musical Context/Application
Great variety in seventh chord texture can be had through doubling (any note may be doubled) and inversion. Sevenths may be inverted and spaced in any manner without destroying their harmonic suppleness. Composers create harmonic progressions by moving through various seventh chord types, using their color differences as an expressive resource.

The progression from one seventh chord type to another on the same root creates a sense of harmonic motion without root movement, a distinctly twentieth-century technique.

# Common Confusions
Students often think only of the dominant seventh as a standard seventh chord type. In twentieth-century harmony, all seven types are equally available as independent sonorities, each with its own expressive character. The minor-major seventh and augmented-major seventh, rarely used in common-practice music, become valuable resources.
