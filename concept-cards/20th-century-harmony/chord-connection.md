# Quick Definition
The manner in which successive chords are linked through voice leading, involving both what chord follows what chord and how the voices move between them.

# Core Definition
Two inseparable factors are involved with harmonic progression: what chord follows what chord, and how they are connected. When melodies sound together chords are formed, and when chords follow each other melodic motion is involved. There is seldom pure harmony or counterpoint, for they are deeply involved with each other. Separate chord tones of any progression have melodic tendencies; even the most isolated chord is full of melodic potential. **Outer voices** govern harmonic direction and **inner voices** secure the relationship of the chords. For unruffled harmonic motion, inner voices are moved as little as possible and common tones are held (Ch. 9, pp. 188-193).

# Prerequisites
- **Harmonic progression** -- understanding chord succession and direction

# Key Properties
1. Outer voices govern harmonic direction; inner voices secure chord relationship
2. Parallel intervals lessen voice individuality; contrary and oblique motion give independence
3. Smooth progressions risk becoming overly smooth -- freshness requires ingenuity
4. Leaping voices, straying dissonances, escaping common tones, modulatory twists, and chromatic daring are all part of harmonic technique
5. The inherent urge of independent voices to maintain identifiable lines can overpower harmonic impulse
6. Harmony and counterpoint are inseparable aspects of the same phenomenon

# Construction / Recognition
- Examine outer voice motion for harmonic direction
- Check inner voice motion for minimal movement and held common tones
- Identify freshness techniques: common-tone inversion, voice crossing, melodic deflection, register shift, density changes, rests at climactic points, shared melody
- Note when chromatic voice motion enables complex dissonances through half-step lines

# Context & Application
Techniques for freshness include: inverting or transposing common-tone intervals to expand register range; moving a voice past an adjacent voice for harmonic lift or drop; deflecting melodic lines by shifting notes an octave; shifting entire harmonic mass to a new register; reinforcing melodic rises with parallel harmony; varying the number of voices; leaving climactic points vacant by rests; having voices share melody. When all voices move by half step, any combination of dissonances may have meaning and direction -- voices containing the sharpest dissonant interval should be well separated.

# Common Errors
- Making all progressions uniformly smooth, losing harmonic freshness
- Neglecting outer voices when determining harmonic direction
- Moving inner voices unnecessarily when common tones are available
- Clustering sharp dissonances in close spacing during chromatic voice motion

# Common Confusions
- **Smooth vs. fresh**: Smooth connections are one facet of craft; maintaining freshness through varied techniques is equally important
- **Harmony vs. counterpoint**: These are not separate domains; chord connection inherently involves both vertical and horizontal thinking
- **Inner vs. outer voices**: Outer voices determine direction; inner voices determine relationship -- both are essential but serve different functions
