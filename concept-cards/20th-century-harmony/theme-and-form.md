# Quick Definition
The inseparable relationship between thematic material and formal structure, where form emerges as the outward equivalent of instinct, taste, and style.

# Formal Definition
Form and style are inseparably related. Form is the outward equivalent of instinct, taste, and style, and is the manner in which kinds of materials are presented. Divergent musical materials may be brought out of context into a piece, but the success of the decision to change the prevailing milieu depends upon creative sensitivity. Conflicting formal elements often result in free and imaginative forms, and the whole course of a major work is altered. The impulse that incites the composer to deviate from the predictable must at the same time create a feeling of inevitability of form.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Formal elements and their functions:
- **Melodic motif**: Creates association of ideas
- **Harmonic succession**: Creates association of ideas
- **Rhythmic pattern**: Creates association of ideas
- **None functional until transformed**: Creative process activates elements

Formal generation possibilities:
- Simple chords fighting dissonant forces (may succeed or fail)
- Dissonant chord refusing to become consonant (distinctive progression)
- Dramatically placed chord succession (indicates formal direction)
- Initial harmony falling cadentially (formal basis)
- Harmonic brightening as notes slow, darkening as notes speed
- Electronic and acoustic combination (modern resources)

# Musical Context/Application
Association of musical ideas through motif, harmony, and rhythm becomes functional only through creative transformation. Specific ideas for shaping formal structure stimulate creative writing. A succession of opening chords may suggest the formal shape of the entire work in microcosm. Any tone can succeed any other tone, any tone can sound with any other, and any group can follow any other - successful projection depends on contextual and formal conditions.
