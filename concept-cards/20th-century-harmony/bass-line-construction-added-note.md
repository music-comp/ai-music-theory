# Quick Definition
A melodic harmonization technique where each melody note is treated as a member of a triad, with bass notes chosen from seconds above or below any triadic member to create varied outer-voice relationships and phrase shapes.

# Formal Definition
Progression in triad-with-added-bass harmony is often governed by an upper melodic line. Each note of a melody, except ornamental tones, may be considered a member of a major, minor, diminished, or augmented triad. A bass line is then constructed from notes a second from any triadic member. Bass notes are chosen to form the kind of outer-voice relationship that best suits the desired texture and phrase shapes.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Process:
1. Take melody note
2. Determine which triad(s) it could belong to (as root, 3rd, or 5th)
3. Select appropriate triad quality (M, m, dim, aug)
4. Choose bass note: M2 or m2 above or below any triadic member
5. Select based on desired outer-voice relationship

For each melody note, options include:
- 4 possible triad qualities
- Note could be root, third, or fifth of each
- Bass could be any of 6 possible seconds (M2/m2 above/below each member)
- Creates extensive palette for each melodic moment

Selection criteria:
- Outer-voice contour (contrary, parallel, oblique, similar motion)
- Phrase shape requirements
- Textural consistency with surrounding material
- Linear quality of resulting bass line

# Musical Context/Application
This technique liberates harmony from traditional root progression while maintaining triadic sonority. The composer gains:
- Wide selection of bass notes for any melody note
- Freedom of linear movement in bass
- Textural consistency through chord type
- Independence from functional harmony

The technique works best in passages that:
- Consist solely of this chord type
- Avoid reference to primary/secondary relationships
- Let melody govern progression
- Maintain consistent spacing

# Common Confusions
- This is not traditional figured bass or functional bass line construction
- The bass note does not imply root function
- Ornamental tones in the melody are excluded from harmonization
- The wide choice of bass notes does not mean random selection; musical criteria guide choices
