# Quick Definition

A scale that is symmetrically invertible, producing either the same scale or a related scale when its intervals are reflected in contrary motion.

# Formal Definition

A **reflective scale** is a scale whose intervallic structure is symmetrically invertible. When two scales move identically, interval for interval, in contrary motion, they form a reflective pair. Some scales, such as Dorian mode and the Double Harmonic scale, are inversely identical (self-reflective). The entire diatonic scale system is symmetrically invertible, with mirrored diatonic modes appearing in reversed order of color gradation.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation

**Diatonic mode reflective pairs:**
- Lydian <-> Locrian
- Ionian (Major) <-> Phrygian
- Mixolydian <-> Aeolian (Natural Minor)
- Dorian <-> Dorian (self-reflective)

The color gradation of modes (bright to dark) reverses in mirror:
- Lydian (brightest) mirrors to Locrian (darkest)
- The central Dorian remains unchanged

**Synthetic scale mirroring:**
Any scale may be reflected. Synthetic scales produce their own mirror forms, which may or may not be named scales.

**Self-reflective scales:**
Scales like Dorian and Double Harmonic are inversely identical; mirroring produces the same intervallic pattern.

# Musical Context/Application

Reflective scales enable:
- Systematic mirror harmony derived from scalar material
- Harmonic progressions where chords reflect each other through scale relationships
- Simultaneous use of a scale and its reflection for textural richness
- Modal writing with built-in contrary motion possibilities

When reflective scales have tonics at different pitch levels, the resulting mirror chords gain more pronounced character as the space between building tones widens.

# Common Confusions

- **Mirror of mode vs. mode itself**: Mirroring a mode produces a different mode (except Dorian); this is not the same as transposition.
- **Self-reflective vs. reflective pairs**: Self-reflective scales (Dorian, Double Harmonic) mirror to themselves; most scales mirror to different scales.
- **Scale reflection vs. melodic inversion**: Scale reflection concerns the intervallic structure of the scale itself, not merely inverting a melody using that scale.
