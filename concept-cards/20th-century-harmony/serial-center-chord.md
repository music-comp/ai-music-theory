# Quick Definition
A chord of three or more notes drawn from a melodic set that serves as the focal sonority in free serial music, with transposition upward yielding a cadential chord and mirroring yielding a third primary chord, analogous to tonal pillar relationships.

# Core Definition
In an extended passage or section of free serial music, any combination of tones may be set up as a tonality. A succession of three or more notes from the selected melodic set or row of tones may be used as a center chord. A transposition of the center chord erected upon its uppermost note may form a cadential chord above the center; a mirror of the center chord may form another primary chord below. Changing the octave pitch of one or more tones produces inversions. Inversion of the center chord may yield a new set of primary chords. All other combinations of tones may form secondary harmony.

# Prerequisites
- **Serial harmony**: The compositional framework
- **Melodic set**: The tone succession from which the center chord is drawn

# Key Properties
1. Three or more notes from the melodic set
2. Functions as the focal sonority (analogous to tonic)
3. Transposition on uppermost note yields cadential chord above
4. Mirror of center chord yields primary chord below
5. Inversions of center chord yield new primary chord sets
6. All other tone combinations form secondary harmony
7. The three primary chords parallel the tonal pillar concept (above, below, center)

# Construction / Recognition
- Select three or more consecutive notes from the melodic set
- Transpose the center chord upward from its top note to create the cadential chord
- Mirror (invert) the center chord to create the third primary chord
- Invert the center chord to derive additional primary chord sets
- Classify all remaining tone combinations as secondary harmony
- Observe the gravitational tendency of serial chords toward the center

# Context & Application
The serial center chord system creates a hierarchy analogous to tonal pillar relationships: center chord (tonic), cadential chord above (dominant-like), mirror chord below (subdominant-like). This allows for cadential motion, harmonic direction, and formal articulation within serial music. In serial harmonic progression, the order of chords is often changed to increase the gravitational tendency of chordal formations to move to the center chord.

# Common Errors
- Confusing the serial center chord with a tonal root
- Failing to derive all three primary chords
- Ignoring the gravitational tendencies between serial chords

# Common Confusions
- **Analogy, not identity**: The center chord is analogous to a tonic but does not function through scale-based relationships
- **Three primary chords**: Center, cadential (transposed above), and mirror (below) form a complete serial tonal system
- **Inversions**: Inverting the center chord produces additional primary chord sets, expanding the harmonic resources
