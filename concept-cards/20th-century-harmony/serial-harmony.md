# Quick Definition
Harmony regulated by a horizontal, unifying melodic idea (twelve-tone or not), creating chords of extraordinary compactness through manifold variations of motif relationships, where harmonic obligations arise from chromaticism set up in harmonic areas from a characteristic series of tones.

# Core Definition
When harmony is regulated by a horizontal, unifying idea (twelve-tone or not), the texture may be serial; this kind of writing creates harmony of extraordinary compactness through the manifold variations of the motif relationships. Chordal formations that arise from serial linear writing have little or no function in a scalar tonal sense. Harmonic obligations arise from a chromaticism set up in harmonic areas from a characteristic series or portion of a series of tones. Logical melodic thinking gives the individual parts their direction and the ear of the composer gives them the quality of chordal movement.

Some directions in serial composition point away from strict "atonal" writing, toward a flexible creative process that includes the vast musical resources of composition, both tonal and atonal. A synthesis of harmonic potentialities includes chord materials governed by root progressions, intervallic texture tensions, and serial devices.

# Prerequisites
- **Atonality**: The tonal context (though serial harmony can include tonal resources)
- **Melodic set**: The horizontal basis for harmonic derivation

# Key Properties
1. Harmony derived from horizontal melodic set
2. May use all twelve tones or fewer
3. Chords formed through horizontal movement or vertical telescoping
4. Chordal formations have no scalar tonal function
5. May encompass both tonal and atonal resources
6. Harmonic obligations arise from the series, not from tonal function
7. Logical melodic thinking gives direction; composer's ear gives chordal quality
8. When set includes identical notes, colorful serial doublings occur

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the melodic set governing the passage
- Trace how chords derive from horizontal movement (voices using set simultaneously) or vertical telescoping (set divided between voices)
- Note the absence of traditional tonal function in resulting chords
- Look for synthesis of tonal and atonal resources
- Identify serial doublings where set contains repeated notes

# Context & Application
Serial harmony represents Persichetti's broadened view of serial technique, extending beyond strict twelve-tone practice to include any harmony governed by a melodic set. The synthesis of tonal and atonal resources within serial writing allows great harmonic variety. Any succession of any number of tones, not necessarily all different, may be used in two or more voices at once (forming harmony from horizontal movement) or divided between voices (forming harmony from vertical telescoping).

# Common Errors
- Assuming serial harmony must use all twelve tones
- Treating serial technique as automatically guaranteeing musical quality
- Ignoring the ear in favor of purely procedural derivation

# Common Confusions
- **Scope**: Serial harmony need not use all twelve tones -- any succession of any number of tones qualifies
- **Tonal integration**: Serial harmony can encompass tonal resources, not only atonal ones
- **Technique vs. music**: Serial technique alone does not ensure communication; the composer's ear must govern
