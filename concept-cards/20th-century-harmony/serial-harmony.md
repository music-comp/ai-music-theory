# Quick Definition
Harmony regulated by a horizontal, unifying melodic idea (twelve-tone or not), creating chords through manifold variations of motif relationships.

# Formal Definition
When harmony is regulated by a horizontal, unifying idea (twelve-tone or not), the texture may be serial. This kind of writing creates harmony of extraordinary compactness through the manifold variations of the motif relationships. Chordal formations that arise from serial linear writing have little or no function in a scalar tonal sense. Harmonic obligations arise from chromaticism set up in harmonic areas from a characteristic series or portion of a series of tones.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Serial harmony principles:
- Harmony derived from horizontal melodic set
- May use all twelve tones or fewer
- Chords formed through horizontal movement or vertical telescoping
- Any succession of any number of tones may be used

Chord derivation methods:
- **Horizontal movement**: Two or more voices using set simultaneously
- **Vertical telescoping**: Set divided between voices
- **Center chord**: Three or more notes from set
- **Cadential chord**: Transposition of center chord on uppermost note
- **Mirror chord**: Inversion of center chord

Modulation in serial harmony:
- Transposition of melodic set causes modulatory shifting
- Identical chords between set forms serve as pivot formations
- Closely related areas: Inversion/retrograde inversion starting fifth above/below

# Musical Context/Application
Some serial directions point away from strict "atonal" writing (all elements from single germ cell) toward flexible processes including tonal and atonal resources. Serial technique does not in itself ensure communication or effective sound organization. When automatic writing is avoided, the unifying power of serial practice allows great harmonic variety. Logical melodic thinking gives parts direction; the composer's ear gives chordal movement quality.

- Chords from serial writing have no scalar tonal function
- Serial technique alone does not guarantee musical success
