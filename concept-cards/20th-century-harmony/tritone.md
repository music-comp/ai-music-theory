# Quick Definition
The augmented fourth or diminished fifth, dividing the octave exactly in half, possessing an ambiguous quality that sounds neutral in chromatic passages and restless in diatonic passages.

# Core Definition
The tritone (augmented fourth or diminished fifth) divides the octave at its halfway point and is the least stable of the intervals. It sounds primarily neutral in chromatic passages and restless in diatonic passages. Its quality is determined solely by context. Inverting the tritone causes a pronounced shift in register even though no change in intervallic distance has taken place. When spaced beyond the octave, the tritone becomes more ambiguous and veiled in chromatic contexts, and less addicted to resolution in diatonic contexts.

# Prerequisites
- **interval** -- the tritone is an interval
- **consonance-dissonance** -- the tritone falls into the "ambiguous" category

# Key Properties
1. **Six semitones** (three whole tones) -- exact midpoint of the octave
2. **Self-inverting**: augmented fourth inverts to diminished fifth with same span
3. **Chromatic context**: sounds primarily neutral
4. **Diatonic context**: sounds restless, implies dominant function
5. **Compound spacing**: more ambiguous/veiled, less resolution-driven
6. **Chordal role**: presence adds restless quality; absence permits stability even in dissonant chords

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify intervals of exactly 6 semitones
2. Assess the surrounding context: chromatic (neutral) or diatonic (restless)
3. In chords, note that tritone presence is a primary classification criterion
4. Recognize that inversion shifts register dramatically without changing the interval's size

# Context & Application
Chords containing a tritone tend to have a restless quality, while those without tritones have stability even when extremely dissonant. This makes tritone presence the secondary axis (after sharp dissonance) in Persichetti's four-category chord classification system. The tritone's contextual behavior -- neutral vs. restless -- allows it to serve very different functions depending on the harmonic environment.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming the tritone is always dissonant and unstable. **Correction**: In chromatic contexts, it can function as a neutral interval requiring no resolution.
- **Error**: Treating augmented fourth and diminished fifth as identical. **Correction**: While enharmonically equivalent, their notational context implies different harmonic functions.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the tritone always needs resolution. **Clarification**: Resolution tendency depends on context; in chromatic passages the tritone is neutral, in diatonic passages restless.
