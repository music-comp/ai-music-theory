# Quick Definition
The principle that any tone can succeed any other tone, any tones can sound together, and any group can follow any other, with success depending on contextual conditions and compositional skill.

# Formal Definition
Any tone can succeed any other tone, any tone can sound simultaneously with any other tone or tones, and any group of tones can be followed by any other group of tones, just as any degree of tension or nuance can occur in any medium under any kind of stress or duration. Successful projection will depend upon the contextual and formal conditions that prevail, and upon the skill and soul of the composer.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Freedom parameters:
- **Melodic succession**: Any tone can follow any tone
- **Vertical combination**: Any tones can sound together
- **Harmonic progression**: Any group can follow any group
- **Dynamic range**: Any tension level possible
- **Timbral choice**: Any medium applicable
- **Temporal aspect**: Any duration or stress

Constraints on freedom:
- Contextual conditions
- Formal conditions
- Compositional skill
- Creative sensitivity ("soul")

# Musical Context/Application
This principle summarizes the expansion of harmonic resources throughout the twentieth century. The removal of stylistic prohibitions does not remove the need for compositional craft - rather, it increases the demand for sensitivity and skill. Freedom without craft produces chaos; craft without freedom produces sterility. The twentieth-century composer must balance complete resource availability with artistic judgment.

- More freedom requires more skill, not less
- Context and form provide necessary constraints
