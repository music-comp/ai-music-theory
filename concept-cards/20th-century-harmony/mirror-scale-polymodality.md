# Quick Definition
A balanced form of polytonality achieved through mirror (inversionally related) scales, creating transparent polytonal texture with inherent structural symmetry.

# Formal Definition
Well-balanced polytonality is secured through mirror scales (polymodality). This technique uses scales that are inversionally related (mirror images of each other) to create a particularly balanced and transparent polytonal texture. The symmetrical relationship between the scales produces a coherent whole while maintaining distinct modal identities. This represents a specific application of polymodality within polytonal contexts.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Mirror scale characteristics:
- Scales inversionally related (mirror images)
- Intervals of ascending scale mirror intervals of descending scale
- Creates symmetrical total pitch content
- Balances upward and downward motion tendencies

Application to polytonality:
- Uses modal distinction (polymodality)
- Achieves balanced polytonal texture
- Inversional symmetry provides structural coherence
- Each voice operates in complementary scalar territory

# Musical Context/Application
Mirror scale polymodality provides a more balanced and transparent form of polytonality than arbitrary key combinations. The mirror relationship between the scales creates inherent structural coherence while maintaining clear modal distinction. This technique bridges polytonality and mirror harmony concepts, using inversional symmetry to organize multiple modal planes.
