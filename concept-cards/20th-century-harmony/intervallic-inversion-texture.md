# Quick Definition
The technique of featuring an interval that, when inverted, reveals the characteristic interval of a new harmonic texture, facilitating smooth transitions between texture types.

# Formal Definition
The characteristic interval of one kind of harmony is contained in any other kind of harmony and may be brought to the surface by intervallic inversion. In all textural changes, an interval may be featured that, when inverted, produces the characteristic interval of the new harmony. This principle allows seamless movement between otherwise disparate harmonic vocabularies.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Interval inversion relationships (mod 12):
- Fifth (7) inverts to Fourth (5)
- Third (4) inverts to Sixth (8)
- Second (2) inverts to Seventh (10)
- Tritone (6) inverts to Tritone (6)

Texture transition applications:
- **Tertian to quartal**: Feature inverted fifth (fourth) in triads
- **Seventh chords to quartal**: Use inverted fifth from root or third
- **Any chord to secundal**: Emphasize seconds in voicing
- **Ninth chords to clusters**: Invert seventh from root or third

# Musical Context/Application
By featuring the interval that inverts to the characteristic interval of the target harmony, the composer prepares the listener's ear for the new texture. The transition feels organic rather than abrupt because the connecting interval already exists within the departing harmony. This technique demonstrates that all harmonic textures share intervallic content; their differences lie in which intervals are emphasized.

# Examples
- Ex. 13-1: Fourth given prominent position in chords by thirds to allow chords by fourths to enter
- Ex. 13-1: Fifth contained in triad inverted to allow for oncoming fourths
- Ex. 13-1: In seventh chords, inverted fifth from root or third introduces quartal harmony
- Ex. 13-1: Second emphasized in inverted seventh chord to prepare for secundal harmony
- Ex. 13-1: In ninth chords, seventh from root or third inverted to suggest secundal clusters

# Common Confusions
- Intervallic inversion reveals hidden connections, not arbitrary relationships
- The characteristic interval of one texture exists within all other textures
- The technique prepares the ear for what follows
