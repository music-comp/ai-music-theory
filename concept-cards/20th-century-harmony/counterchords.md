# Quick Definition
The counterpoint of chords derived from a two-part linear frame, with two techniques: using the two-part counterpoint as outer voices of polyharmony, or using it as the roots of triads of both chordal units.

# Formal Definition
The direction of polyharmony is determined by linear movement. The counterpoint of chords (counterchords) is derived from a two-part linear frame of single-tone lines. Two kinds of counterchordal techniques serve as a working basis for polyharmonic writing: two-part counterpoint used as the outer voices of polyharmony, and two-part counterpoint used as the roots of triads of both units making up the polyharmony.

# Musical/Mathematical Formulation
Two counterchordal techniques:

**Technique 1: Outer-voice frame**
- Compose two-part counterpoint
- Use as outer voices of polychordal texture
- Fill in chordal units between outer voices
- Linear movement governs harmonic direction

**Technique 2: Root-based frame**
- Compose two-part counterpoint
- Each note becomes root of a triadic unit
- Roots move freely between inner and outer voices
- Triads built on each note of the frame

Triadic possibilities from any melody note:
- Each note can be root, third, or fifth
- Of major, minor, diminished, or augmented triad
- Creates wide choice of textures
- Allows harmonic tension to fluctuate

# Musical Context/Application
Counterchordal technique provides a compositional method for polychordal writing that maintains linear logic. Rather than building polychords vertically, the composer derives them from horizontal voice-leading.

This approach ensures:
- Linear coherence underlying complex harmonies
- Control over harmonic rhythm
- Systematic generation of polychordal progressions
- Balance between horizontal and vertical dimensions

The technique also allows ornamentation of lines, which freshens texture and captures attention through individual voice activity.

# Common Confusions
- Counterchords are a compositional technique, not a chord type
- The two techniques produce different results from the same linear material
- Root-based technique allows roots to appear in any voice, not just bass
- The linear frame determines direction; chordal choices determine color
