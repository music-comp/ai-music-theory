# Quick Definition

The counterpoint of chords derived from a two-part linear frame, with two techniques: using the two-part counterpoint as outer voices of polyharmony, or as the roots of triads of both chordal units.

# Core Definition

"The direction of polyharmony is determined by linear movement. The counterpoint of chords (counterchords) is derived from a two-part linear frame of single-tone lines. Two kinds of counterchordal techniques serve as a working basis for polyharmonic writing: two-part counterpoint used as the outer voices of polyharmony, and two-part counterpoint used as the roots of triads of both units making up the polyharmony" (Persichetti, p. 143). "Any note of any melodic line can become the root, third, or fifth of a major, minor, diminished, or augmented triad" (p. 145).

# Key Properties

1. **Technique 1 (outer-voice frame)**: Two-part counterpoint serves as soprano and bass of polychordal texture; chordal units fill between
2. **Technique 2 (root-based frame)**: Two-part counterpoint serves as roots of both chordal units; roots move freely between inner and outer voices
3. Any melody note can be root, third, or fifth of major, minor, diminished, or augmented triad
4. Great number of triadic possibilities gives wide choice of textures
5. Harmonic tension can fluctuate within the counterchordal framework

# Construction / Recognition

## Technique 1: Outer-Voice Frame
1. Compose two-part counterpoint
2. Use as outer voices of polychordal texture
3. Fill in chordal units between the outer voices

## Technique 2: Root-Based Frame
1. Compose two-part counterpoint
2. Build triads on each note (choosing root, 3rd, or 5th function and triad quality)
3. Allow roots to move freely between inner and outer voices

# Context & Application

Counterchordal technique provides a compositional method that maintains linear logic. Rather than building polychords vertically, the composer derives them from horizontal voice-leading. Ornamentation of lines freshens the texture and captures attention through individual voice activity.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming roots must always appear in the bass
  **Correction**: In technique 2, roots move freely between inner and outer voices

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Counterchords are a chord type
  **Clarification**: They are a compositional technique, not a chord classification
- **Confusion**: The two techniques produce identical results
  **Clarification**: Technique 1 (outer voices) and technique 2 (roots) produce fundamentally different textures from the same linear material
