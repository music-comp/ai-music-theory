# Quick Definition
In twentieth-century practice, seventh chords have become stable entities whose traditionally dissonant seventh need not be prepared or resolved, functioning with the same harmonic freedom as triads.

# Core Definition
The seventh and ninth members of chords are traditionally dissonant tones but they have been freed of some of their former restrictions. Seventh chords have become stable entities in themselves with their dissonant tones not necessarily prepared or resolved. Like triads, seventh chords may progress within or outside any scale formation, original or traditional. Under certain formal conditions the seventh is treated as a dissonant tone needing resolution; but as independent seventh chords they have the facility of triads. All types of harmonic progression possible with triads are possible with seventh chords, including cyclic progressions and chromatic movement.

# Key Properties
1. Seventh chords function as stable, independent entities
2. Dissonant tones need not be prepared or resolved
3. All cyclic progressions (fifths, thirds, seconds) work with seventh chords
4. Augmented fourth root relationship is especially fresh with dominant sevenths
5. Any note may be doubled
6. First inversion spaced in fifths acts as a key center chord
7. Voice movement through different seventh formations on the same root creates "progression"
8. Seventh chords with augmented or diminished thirds are misspellings of other chords

# Construction / Recognition
- Seven types constructible from major and minor thirds only (see seven-types-of-seventh-chords)
- Sevenths may be inverted and spaced in any manner without destroying suppleness
- First inversion spaced in fifths has a unique sound suitable for establishing key center
- Moving voices through various seventh formations on one root = harmonic motion without root change
- Tritone root relationships are particularly effective with dominant seventh chords

# Context & Application
The emancipation of the seventh from its dissonant-resolution obligation is a defining shift of twentieth-century harmony. Seventh chords join triads as freely mobile harmonic units, deployable in all cyclic relationships. The variety of seventh chord types (seven distinct qualities) offers composers rich coloristic resources. A feeling of "progression" can be created by moving through different seventh formations on the same root, exploiting color differences between types.

# Common Errors
- Automatically resolving seventh chords in twentieth-century contexts
- Treating only the dominant seventh as a standard type; all seven types are equally available

# Common Confusions
- **Dissonance is contextual**: The "dissonance" of the seventh is a historical convention, not an absolute quality; in twentieth-century contexts, the seventh can function as consonance
- **Stability vs. resolution**: Seventh chords can be stable endpoints, not just transitional sonorities requiring resolution
