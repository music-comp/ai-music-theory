# Quick Definition
The formal space S of a GIS must be theoretically complete (satisfying Condition B), even though practical musical contexts use only a portion of this extended space.

# Formal Definition
Lewin emphasizes: "We must conceive the formal space of a GIS as a space of theoretical potentialities, rather than as a compendium of musical practicalities." Condition (B) requires that for any element s and interval i, some element t exists with int(s, t) = i. This may require conceptually extending practical spaces to include supersonic/subsonic pitches, infinitely remote time points, or infinitely distant harmonic relationships.

# Mathematical Formulation
- Condition (B): For all s in S and i in IVLS, there exists unique t with int(s, t) = i
- This implies: S must contain all theoretical targets of all intervals from all points
- Practical subset: The actually used elements in a composition or analysis
- Theoretical extension: The full space required for mathematical completeness

# Musical Context/Application
This distinction is fundamental to GIS theory. The twelve pitch classes form a complete practical AND theoretical space. But chromatic pitch space must extend to inaudible frequencies for Condition (B). Harmonic space (Figure 2.2) extends infinitely to accommodate all dominant/mediant chains. A piece may use only a small region, but the theoretical framework encompasses the entire space.

# Common Confusions
- Theoretical extension doesn't mean these elements are musically useful
- The space S is determined by Condition (B), not by practical composition
- Different GIS may have the same practical content but different theoretical extensions
- "Conceivability" is the criterion: we can conceive supersonic pitches even if we can't hear them
