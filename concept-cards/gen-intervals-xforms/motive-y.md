# Quick Definition
Motive Y (Ab-F-Eb) is derived from motive X through a "folding" transformation that converts the T relation into T^(-1), creating a new motive that retains the T' connection while reorienting the T-related pitches.

# Formal Definition
Motive Y = (Ab4, F4, Eb4) is derived from motive X = (Db4, Eb4, Ab4) through a transformation that preserves the T' relationship (Ab to Eb) while "folding" the T relation (Db to Eb) into its inverse T^(-1) (F to Eb). This folding occurs both temporally (serially) and in registral space.

# Mathematical Formulation
Derivation from X to Y (Figure 10.11):

X structure:
```
Db ----T----> Eb
              ^
Ab ----T'-----+
```

Y structure:
```
       Eb <----T^(-1)---- F
       ^
Ab ----T'
```

The transformation X -> Y:
- Retains: Ab and Eb connected by T'
- Folds: The T-relation (Db-Eb) becomes T^(-1)-relation (F-Eb)
- Implication: If X becomes T(X), the new T-relation specifically engages Eb-to-F, retrograding Y's last two pitches

# Musical Context/Application
Motive Y follows X in the music and demonstrates how Debussy derives new material through transformational processes rather than simple transposition or variation. The Y motive and its permuted/transposed forms recur throughout the piece, particularly in association with the structurally important F-Ab dyad.

# Examples
From the analysis (Figures 10.10-10.15):

**Y in the opening:**
Y = Ab4-F4-Eb4 appears immediately after X, connected by the shared Ab and Eb.

**F-Ab dyad:**
The interval from F to Ab within Y "began as a secondary phenomenon; its complement spanned Ab-to-F within Y, which appeared as the difference between the T'-related Ab-to-Eb and the T^(-1)-related F-to-Eb."

**Structural proliferation:**
The F-Ab interval (3 semitones) becomes increasingly prominent:
- "F-to-Ab then assumed greater prominence in the melody as the temporal boundary for Z1"
- Verticalized in harmony (Figure 10.16)
- Generates structural Cb in bass
- "Once the melodic F-Ab is verticalized in the harmony... the interplay of horizontal and vertical 3-intervals next generates a structural Cb in the bass"

**Inverted Y forms:**
- I(Y) = F-Ab-Bb (inversion about F-and-Ab)
- J(Y) = Ab-Cb-Db (appears in bass, Figure 10.18)
- T_(-3)(Y) = F-D-C (cadential form at measure 18)

**Y within networks:**
Figure 10.15(c): F4-Ab4-Bb4 (serial form of inverted Y) identified with the 5-semitone rise of extended chromatic accompaniment.

# Common Confusions
The "folding" transformation from X to Y is not a standard serial operation (not T, I, R, or RI). It belongs to "the same genre" as FLIPEND and FLIPSTART discussed in section 8.3.2, but is neither of those specifically. Students should understand this as a registral/temporal reorientation rather than a standard twelve-tone operation.
