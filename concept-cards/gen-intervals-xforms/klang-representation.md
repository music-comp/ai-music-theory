# Quick Definition
A formal representation of a chord as an ordered pair (p, sign), where p is a pitch class (the root) and sign is + for major or - for minor, yielding a space of 24 Klangs.

# Formal Definition
A Klang is an ordered pair (p, sign) where:
- p is a pitch class (the root or tonic of the chord)
- sign takes values + (major) or - (minor)
- The Klang models a harmonic object with root p and modality determined by sign

Transposition preserves sign: (C, +) transposed by i yields (C+i, +).

# Mathematical Formulation
The space of Klangs:
- 12 pitch classes x 2 modes = 24 Klangs
- Examples: (C, +) = C major, (C, -) = C minor
- Transposition: (p, sign)T_i = (p+i, sign)

The Klang space is acted upon by various transformations:
- Transpositions (preserve mode)
- Mode-changing operations (DOM, MED, PAR, REL, LT, SLIDE, etc.)

# Musical Context/Application
The Klang representation solves a problem with fundamental bass intervals: those intervals cannot distinguish major from minor chords with the same root. By pairing pitch class with mode, Klangs enable transformations that change mode (like parallel or relative operations) to be formalized alongside transpositions.

# Examples
Standard Klangs:
- (C, +) = C major
- (C, -) = C minor
- (G, +) = G major
- (A, -) = A minor

The 24 Klangs form the space for Klang transformations developed from Riemann's function theory.

# Common Confusions
- A Klang is an ordered pair, not a chord voicing or specific pitch collection
- The space of 24 Klangs assumes equal temperament (in other tunings, the space might differ)
- Sign refers to mode, not register or other qualities
- Klangs do not specify inversions, doublings, or voice leading
