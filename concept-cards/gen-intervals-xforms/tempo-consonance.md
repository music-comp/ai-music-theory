# Quick Definition
Tempo consonance refers to the quality of simplicity or complexity in the numerical ratio between two tempi. Tempi in simple ratios (2:3, 3:4) are "consonant"; complex ratios create "dissonance" analogous to pitch intervals.

# Formal Definition
When two local time units have durations in ratio p:q (or equivalently, tempi in ratio q:p), the simplicity of this ratio can be characterized as consonant or dissonant by analogy with frequency ratios in pitch.

# Mathematical Formulation
**Ratio representation:**
If tempo T1 = MM(n) and tempo T2 = MM(m), the ratio is n:m or equivalently m:n for durations.

**Pitch analogy (Figure 4.2b):**
Representing tempi as pitches with corresponding frequency ratios:
- MM180 : MM120 = 3:2 (perfect fifth)
- MM120 : MM96 = 5:4 (major third)
- MM96 : MM48 = 2:1 (octave)

The "harmony" of the resulting pitches reveals the "consonance" of the tempo relationships.

# Musical Context/Application
The tempo-consonance metaphor helps musicians and analysts:
- Grasp complex tempo relationships intuitively
- Plan ensemble coordination
- Understand why some polyrhythms are harder than others
- Appreciate the compositional logic of tempo structures

The metaphor follows Zarlino's approach (variety of consonances) rather than seeking a single referential tempo.

# Examples
**Carter mm.22-32 tempo network:**
- MM48:MM96 = 1:2 ("octave" - maximally consonant)
- MM36:MM48 = 3:4 ("fourth")
- MM120:MM160 = 3:4 ("fourth")
- MM60:MM80 = 3:4 ("fourth")
- MM90:MM180 = 1:2 ("octave")

The network is highly "consonant" - simple ratios throughout.

**Figure 4.2b chord:**
The tempi form a "Db major with added sixth and major seventh" - a striking consonance in the pitch representation.

**Performance insight:**
Understanding tempo-consonance helps players hear cross-rhythmic relationships as musical intervals rather than arbitrary complexities.

**Nancarrow's irrational ratios:**
Ratios like pi:1 or e:1 would be maximally "dissonant" - incommensurable, like irrational pitch intervals.

# Common Confusions
1. **Metaphor, not identity:** Tempo consonance is an analogy. We don't literally hear tempo ratios the same way we hear frequency ratios.

2. **Performer's tool:** The pitch representation (Figure 4.2b) is a practical aid for musicians, not a claim about perception.

3. **Zarlino vs. Rameau:** The approach values variety of consonances rather than hierarchy under a single "root" tempo.

4. **The "root" metaphor:** MM48 as "root" in Carter is suggestive, not a theoretical claim that tempo perception works like tonal root perception.
