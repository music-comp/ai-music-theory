# Quick Definition
The fundamental reconception that intervals are not merely measurements between elements but can be understood as the unique transposition operations that map one element to another.

# Formal Definition
Instead of thinking "i is the intervallic distance from s to t," we can think "T_i is the unique transposition operation on this space that maps s into t." This shift transforms intervals from passive measurements into active transformations.

The equivalence works through the INJ function: IFUNC(X, Y)(i) = INJ(X, Y)(T_i), where X and Y are one-element sets containing s and t respectively. The unique transformation T_i satisfying INJ(X, Y)(T_i) > 0 has label i = int(s, t).

# Mathematical Formulation
For any GIS with simply transitive transposition group:
- Given s, t in S, there exists unique T_i such that T_i(s) = t
- This T_i has index i = int(s, t)
- The arrow labeled "i" or "-5" on a network can represent either:
  - The interval i between elements, or
  - The transposition T_i between Gestalts

# Musical Context/Application
This reconception allows us to subsume interval theory into transformation theory. It also unifies two apparently different musical phenomena: intervallic relationships between individual elements and transpositional relationships between larger Gestalts. When a motif is transposed, the same transformation that relates the Gestalts also relates corresponding elements within them.

# Examples
In Schoenberg's op. 19, no. 6 (Figure 7.1):
- The opening chord "rh" contains pitch-intervals -5, -9, and -14 semitones
- The falling-ninth motif transposes through the piece by T_-5, T_-9, and T_-14
- The symbols "-5" on both the chord structure and the transposition scheme point at "the same phenomenon"

# Common Confusions
- This is not saying intervals and transpositions are identical, but that they can be understood as two manifestations of one underlying phenomenon
- The shift requires thinking of single elements as one-element Gestalts
- This reconception does not eliminate the usefulness of interval language; it provides an alternative perspective
