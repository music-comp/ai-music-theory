# Quick Definition
An analysis demonstrating how the Minuet opening and Adagio introduction of Beethoven's First Symphony are different realizations of one abstract harmonic gesture (Figure 7.9), offering an alternative to traditional parody-based critical interpretations.

# Core Definition
Figure 7.9 presents a normative fundamental-bass network common to both the Adagio introduction (Figure 7.8b) and the Minuet opening (Figure 7.8a). The Adagio projects a "progressive" reading (chain of V-I cadences moving along the circle of fifths: F, C, G). The Minuet projects a "balanced" reading (two CADENCEs elided, with the repeated G-C progression reflecting the elision). Traditional criticism describes the Minuet as "parodying" the Adagio; the transformational perspective enables hearing both as equally valid realizations of one underlying abstract gesture (Lewin, 7.4, pp. 169-174).

# Prerequisites
- **Normative network** — The abstract graph that both passages realize

# Key Properties
1. Normative network (Figure 7.9): arrows labeled with ratios 2/3, 3/4, 5/6
2. A-minor "tail" (5/6) is secondary and can be analyzed separately
3. Adagio reading: progressive chain of V-I cadences (tonics F, C, G)
4. Minuet reading: balanced structure of two elided CADENCEs
5. Figure 7.12: local tonics form a backwards CADENCE (dominants follow tonics)
6. The repeated G-C in the Minuet projects the elided structure of Figure 7.11

# Context & Application
The analysis engages philosophical and aesthetic issues about musical structure and temporality. Lewin uses the analogy of meeting Mr. X at a formal event and then at a park to illustrate why neither realization should be considered the "norm" and the other a "parody." He notes: "The mere possibility of this shift in critical stance is important" (p. 170). Figure 7.13 continues the analysis, asserting structural correspondence between the Minuet's continuation and the first movement's path to the dominant.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Figures 7.8-7.9, pp. 169-170): The Minuet harmonies (old-fashioned hearing: F-G-C repeated-D-G with C pedal) and the Adagio harmonies (with interpolated C between G7 and A minor) both map onto Figure 7.9.

**Example 2** (Figures 7.11-7.12, pp. 172-173): Two elided CADENCEs (C-F; G-C and G-C; D-G), with their tonics (F, C, G) forming a backwards CADENCE.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming the F root in the Minuet's first measure without contextual support
  **Correction**: Lewin acknowledges this is "old-fashioned" but argues it is supported by the preceding slow movement ending in F major

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Believing the Adagio must be structurally prior because it comes first
  **Clarification**: The transformational perspective allows both to be equal realizations; temporal priority does not confer structural priority
