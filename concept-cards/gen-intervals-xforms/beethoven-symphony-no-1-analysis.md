# Quick Definition
An analysis demonstrating how the opening of the Minuet and the symphony's introduction can be understood as different realizations of one underlying abstract harmonic gesture, showing that transformational theory can offer alternatives to parody-based critical interpretations.

# Formal Definition
Figure 7.9 presents a "normative fundamental-bass network" common to:
- Figure 7.8(a): Opening of the Minuet
- Figure 7.8(b): Opening of the symphony (Adagio introduction)

Both passages realize the same abstract gesture differently:
- Adagio: Progressive reading (chain of V-I cadences)
- Minuet: Balanced reading (two CADENCEs elided)

# Mathematical Formulation
The normative network (Figure 7.9):
- Arrows labeled with fundamental bass ratios: 2/3, 3/4, 5/6
- Expresses fractions between 1/2 and 1 to capture "falling" progressions
- The A-minor "tail" (5/6 ratio) is treated as secondary

CADENCE graph (Figure 7.10):
- Two-part structure for V-I progressions

The normative network without tail = two CADENCEs elided (Figure 7.11)

# Musical Context/Application
Traditional criticism describes the Minuet as "parodying" the Adagio. The transformational perspective offers an alternative: both realize one underlying abstract gesture, neither having structural priority over the other. This shift in critical stance does not claim superiority but opens new analytical possibilities.

# Common Confusions
- The "interpolated C" in the Adagio is a theoretical construct, not a sounded event
- The F root in the Minuet requires hearing through the C pedal (aided by memory of slow movement ending in F)
- The normative network is abstract; neither passage has inherent priority
- Fundamental bass ratios model root relations, not complete harmonic transformations
