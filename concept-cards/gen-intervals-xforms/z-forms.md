# Quick Definition
Z-forms (Z1, Z2, Z3) are four-note motivic cells in Debussy's "Reflets dans l'eau" that synthesize X, Y, and T(X) material, appearing at structurally significant moments accompanied by CHR networks and crescendi.

# Core Definition
Z1 appears at m. 9 synthesizing X, Y, T(X) relationships. Z2 arises in m. 10 as extension. Z3 appears retrograded at m. 30 around C5/Db5. Z-forms are articulated by rhythm and contour; Z1 analyzed as T^{-1}-related dyad pairs in inverse-T' relation (Figure 10.12), Z2 as T^{-1}-related pairs in T-relation (Figure 10.14). T(Z1) appears at the Eb-major breakthrough mm. 57-58 (Lewin, Section 10.4, pp. 269-278).

# Prerequisites
- **Motive X** and **Motive Y** — Z-forms synthesize their content

# Key Properties
1. Z1 = F-Eb-Bb-Ab at m. 9
2. Z2 extends Z1 in m. 10
3. Retrograde Z3 at m. 30: C5/Db5 registral ambiguity
4. Accompanied by CHR networks and crescendi
5. T(Z1) at breakthrough supports the tonal shift to Eb

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:

# Context & Application
Z-forms mark structural turning points: developmental launch (Z1, m. 9), registral question (ret Z3, m. 30), and climactic breakthrough (T(Z1), m. 57).

# Examples
**Example 1** (Figure 10.12, p. 269): Z1 recapitulates T^{-1} from F to Eb (Y) and T^{-1} from Bb to Ab (T(X)-to-X return).
