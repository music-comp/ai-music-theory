# Quick Definition
Arrows on transformation graphs labeled with inversion operations, enabling networks to represent inversional relationships alongside or instead of transpositional ones. I-arrows create non-intervallic networks even in commutative GIS contexts.

# Core Definition
"Inversional relations between elements in a GIS, even a well-behaved commutative GIS, may also give rise to non-transpositional arrows on graphs, that is I-arrows" (Lewin, 8.5, p. 190). I-arrows may be two-way when the inversion is an involution (I(I(x)) = x). Figure 8.12 contrasts an intervallic/transpositional network (a) with a mixed I-arrow and T-arrow network (b) for the same Webern passage, showing how I-arrows better reflect rhythmic motifs, registration symmetry, row structure, and canonic structure.

# Prerequisites
- **Inversion operation** — I-arrows are labeled with inversions
- **Non-intervallic transformations** — I-arrows create non-intervallic networks

# Key Properties
1. Labeled with inversion operations (I, I^u, etc.)
2. May be two-way (for involutory inversions)
3. Create non-intervallic networks even in commutative GIS
4. Can combine with T-arrows (transpositional) in mixed networks
5. Visual convention: straight arrows for I, curved for T (or vice versa)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Identify inversional relationships between elements
2. Label arrows with the specific inversion operation
3. Use two-way arrows for involutions
## To Recognize:
1. Arrows labeled with inversion operations (not transpositions)
2. Typically straight lines contrasting with curved T-arrows

# Context & Application
In Figure 8.12(b), "I" is inversion about pitch A4. The I-arrows reflect the rhythmic motif, mirror symmetry, row structure, and dux/comes canon structure more clearly than pure T-arrows.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Figure 8.12, p. 191): Webern op. 27, second movement opening. Network (b) uses I-arrows (straight) and T-arrows (curved). Four IPAIR subnetworks: Bb-G#, A-A, F-C#, D-E.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming I-arrows can only be one-way
  **Correction**: For involutory inversions, I-arrows are two-way

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking I-arrows and T-arrows are interchangeable
  **Clarification**: I-arrows represent fundamentally different (non-transpositional) relationships
