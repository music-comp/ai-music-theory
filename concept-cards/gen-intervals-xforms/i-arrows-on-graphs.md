# Quick Definition
Arrows on transformation graphs labeled with inversion operations rather than transpositions, allowing networks to represent inversional relationships alongside or instead of intervallic/transpositional ones.

# Formal Definition
I-arrows are arrows labeled with inversion operations:
- The label I (or I^u_v, I^u, etc.) indicates an inversion transformation
- When nodes N_1 and N_2 are connected by an I-arrow, CONTENTS(N_2) = I(CONTENTS(N_1))
- I-arrows may be two-way (since many inversions are involutions: I^(-1) = I)

# Mathematical Formulation
For inversion I about pitch (class) A:
- (Klang)I = the I-transform of Klang
- I(I(x)) = x for many inversions (self-inverse)
- Two-way I-arrows represent this involutory property

Network with I-arrows:
- May include both I-arrows and T-arrows
- I-arrows typically drawn straight; T-arrows curved (or vice versa)
- The combination creates non-intervallic networks

# Musical Context/Application
I-arrows capture inversional symmetry in music more directly than transposition arrows. They are particularly useful for analyzing music where inversion is structurally prominent, such as Webern's canonic and symmetric writing.

# Examples
From Webern's Piano Variations, second movement opening (Figure 8.12):
- Network (a): intervallic/transpositional arrows (curved)
- Network (b): combines T-arrows (curved) and I-arrows (straight)
- I = I^A_A (inversion about pitch A4)
- Vertical I-arrows reflect the mirror symmetry of pitch registration
- Four IPAIR subnetworks: Bb-and-G#, A-and-A, F-and-C#, D-and-E

Network (b) reflects:
1. The rhythmic motif (vertical I-arrow as visual motif)
2. Mirror symmetry of registration
3. Row structure and dux/comes relationship of canon

# Common Confusions
- I-arrows may be two-way (for involutions) or one-way (for non-involutory inversions)
- "I" in an I-arrow is a transformation, not an interval
- Combining I-arrows and T-arrows creates genuinely non-intervallic networks
- The visual distinction (straight vs. curved) is conventional, not mandatory
