# Quick Definition
CHR is a graph modeling a continuous chromatic rise of a musical object (a 3-note chord) over a total span of three semitones, appearing isographically at different transposition levels in the accompaniment beneath Z-forms in Debussy's "Reflets dans l'eau."

# Core Definition
The CHR graph depicts three successive semitone transpositions of a chord (Figure 10.13). CHR networks appear beneath Z1 at m. 9, transposed beneath Z2 at m. 10, and retrograded at m. 14. The 3-semitone total span of CHR identifies with the F-Ab interval within Y. Isographic CHR networks at different transposition levels are associated with thematic crescendi (Lewin, Section 10.4, pp. 269-271).

# Prerequisites
- **Transformation network** — CHR is a network graph
- **Isography** — CHR networks appear isographically

# Key Properties
1. Three semitone steps: total span = 3 semitones
2. Appears in accompaniment beneath Z-forms
3. The 3-semitone span connects to Y's F-Ab interval
4. Isographic CHR networks appear at different transpositions
5. Associated with crescendi (thematic element)
6. Retrograde CHR appears at m. 14

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Identify continuous chromatic rise in accompaniment
2. Verify three semitone steps totaling 3 semitones
## To Recognize:
1. Parallel chromatic chord motion over 3 semitones beneath Z-forms

# Context & Application
CHR networks link the accompaniment structure to the motivic structure (Z-forms) and to the proliferating 3-semitone interval. Their association with crescendi becomes a thematic marker.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Figures 10.13, 10.10, pp. 269-270): CHR beneath Z1 at m. 9 rises 3 semitones. At m. 10, a T-transposed isographic CHR rises 5 semitones from the same starting point, yielding the homomorphic relationship of Figure 10.15.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating CHR as a pitch pattern rather than a graph
  **Correction**: CHR is a graph (abstract structure) that can be filled with different chord contents

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking all CHR networks are identical
  **Clarification**: They are isographic (same graph) but contain different chords at different transpositions
