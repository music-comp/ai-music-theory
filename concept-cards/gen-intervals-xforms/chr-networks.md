# Quick Definition
CHR networks model continuous chromatic motion of a musical object (chord, dyad, or voice) through semitone transpositions, creating a graph structure that captures stepwise chromatic progressions.

# Formal Definition
A CHR (chromatic) network is a transformation graph where nodes represent successive chromatic transpositions of a musical object and arrows represent semitone motion. The graph depicts "a continuous chromatic rise of a certain object... over a total span of [n] semitones." CHR networks can appear in prime form (ascending) or retrograde form (descending).

# Mathematical Formulation
CHR graph structure (Figure 10.13):
```
node1 ---(+1)---> node2 ---(+1)---> node3 ---(+1)---> node4
```
- Each arrow represents T_1 (transposition by semitone)
- Total span = number of arrows
- Retrograde CHR: descending semitone motion

Network properties:
- Nodes: states of the chromatic object
- Arrows: semitone transpositions
- Span: total chromatic distance traversed

# Musical Context/Application
CHR networks capture the characteristic chromatic voice-leading of impressionist and late-romantic music. They reveal structural connections between chromatic passages and diatonic/motivic material, particularly when the total span of a CHR network coincides with structurally significant intervals.

# Examples
**First CHR in "Reflets" (measure 9):**
"Beneath Z1 in the accompaniment, a new idea makes its appearance. This idea involves a continuous chromatic rise of a certain object (here a certain 3-note chord), over a total span of three semitones."

The 3-semitone span coincides with:
- F-to-Ab within Z1
- The Ab-F dyad within Y
- Creating homophonic identity between melodic and accompanimental structures

**Isographic CHR networks:**
At measure 10, "a new network isographic to the network of measure 9 in the accompaniment" appears, transposed by T. The extended accompaniment rises 5 semitones (not just 3), captured by Figure 10.15(a).

**Retrograde CHR (measure 14):**
"The retrograde CHR gestures in the alto voice" descend from Gb-within-MAGIC to Eb (Figure 10.17b), retrograding the earlier ascending CHR patterns.

**Dynamic associations:**
"The CHR network in the accompaniment has risen 3 semitones overall, while Z1 in the melody has also risen 3 semitones overall... The homophony... is marked by a crescendo. The crescendo is to become a significant thematic element."

**Structural CHR (measures 22-24):**
Ascending and retrograde CHR lines create voice-leading between MAGIC harmony and dominant-ninth structure: "a retrograde CHR line connecting the Eb of Tristan to the Gb of MAGIC."

# Common Confusions
CHR networks are not mere catalogues of chromatic motion but structural elements whose spans correlate with motivic intervals. The 3-semitone span of the initial CHR derives from Y's F-Ab interval, connecting surface chromatic motion to deep motivic structure. Students should look for such correlations rather than treating chromatic passages as neutral filler.
