# Quick Definition
The "variable" motive V in Debussy's "Reflets dans l'eau" always proceeds by step up, third-leap up, step down, step down, step down (returning to its starting note), but with variable step sizes and extremely plastic rhythm, representing the "perpetual mutations of things seen through reflets dans l'eau."

# Core Definition
V stands for "variable." The motive proceeds by step up, third-leap up, step down, step down, step down, ending on the same note it started. "The sizes of the 'steps' and the 'third' vary considerably, sometimes even within one V-statement, and the rhythm of the motive is also extremely plastic." V appears over mm. 24-29 prolonging MAGIC harmony and dominant-ninth harmony. The "breakthrough" form at mm. 57-58 represents the climactic moment (Lewin, Section 10.4, pp. 282-288).

# Key Properties
1. Fixed contour: step up, third up, step down, step down, step down
2. Returns to starting pitch
3. Variable interval sizes ("steps" and "third" vary)
4. Extremely plastic rhythm
5. Represents perpetual mutations through water reflections

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Follow the contour pattern with variable interval sizes
## To Recognize:
1. Identify the characteristic up-up-down-down-down contour returning to start

# Context & Application
V appears during the rippling figuration (mm. 24-29) and at the climactic breakthrough (mm. 57-58). The second V-statement's descending part connects to the hexachordal line Bb-Ab-Gb; F-Eb-Db, recalling m. 14.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Figure 10.10, pp. 282-283): Two V-statements over mm. 24-29. The descending second V hooks up with the hexachord Bb-Ab-Gb; F-Eb-Db (same as melody of m. 14, launched by MAGIC harmony).

**Example 2** (pp. 287-288): The "breakthrough" V at mm. 57-58, where "Bb4 and C5... finally make their breakthrough... to Eb5" in Eb-major tonality.
