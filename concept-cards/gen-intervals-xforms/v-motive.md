# Quick Definition
The V motive (for "variable") is a stepwise figure in "Reflets dans l'eau" characterized by: step up, third-leap up, step down, step down, step down, returning to its starting note, with highly plastic interval sizes and rhythm.

# Formal Definition
V is a motivic contour pattern consisting of six directional moves that return to the starting pitch: ascending step, ascending third-leap, three descending steps. The "steps" and "third" vary considerably in size (sometimes within a single statement), and the rhythm is "extremely plastic." This variability represents "the perpetual mutations of things seen through reflets dans l'eau."

# Mathematical Formulation
V motive contour: (+step, +third, -step, -step, -step)
Net motion: returns to starting pitch (sum of intervals = 0)

Properties:
- Variable step sizes (diatonic, chromatic, or mixed)
- Variable third size (major, minor, or augmented)
- Rhythmic plasticity (no fixed duration pattern)

# Musical Context/Application
The V motive appears during the rippling figuration of measures 24-29, where its variability captures the visual effect of seeing objects through water's surface. The motive's return to its starting pitch creates closed, circular structures that prolong the underlying harmonies.

# Examples
**Appearance and function (measures 24-29):**
"The rippling figuration over measures 24-29 ornaments a basic succession of two harmonies: The MAGIC harmony just under discussion moves to a dominant-ninth structure at measure 27, as shown on figure 10.10. The figure also shows... how the progression involves precisely the two chromatic voice-leading gestures already exposed during measures 18-19, that is D-to-Eb and Cb-to-C."

"Within the overall progression, each of the two harmonies is prolonged by (or accompanies) a form of a new motive I call V, marked in parentheses on figure 10.10."

**Variability:**
"V stands for 'variable'; the motive always proceeds by step up, third-leap up, step down, step down, and step down, ending on the same note with which it started, but the sizes of the 'steps' and the 'third' vary considerably, sometimes even within one V-statement, and the rhythm of the motive is also extremely plastic."

**Programmatic significance:**
"These features of V presumably represent the perpetual mutations of things seen through reflets dans l'eau. (One thinks of Monet's pond at Giverny.)"

**Hexachordal connection:**
"The descending part of the second V-statement here hooks up with the rippling figuration just before measure 30, to project the descending hexachordal line Bb-Ab-Gb; F-Eb-Db. We have heard this descending hexachord before, namely in the melody of measure 14."

**Breakthrough V (measures 57-58):**
"The 'breakthrough' form of V at measures 57-58 directly follows two V-essays of a very different character." This climactic V-statement supports the melodic breakthrough to Eb5 in Eb major.

# Common Confusions
The V motive is defined by contour pattern, not interval sizes. Students should not expect consistent intervallic content across V-statements. The "variable" designation indicates that the motive's identity resides in its directional profile and circular return, not in specific pitch intervals.
