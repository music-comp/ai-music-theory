# Quick Definition
A Klang transformation network analyzing Wagner's Tarnhelm motive, taking (B, +) as tonic and revealing functional relationships with the Valhalla network through shared graph structure.

# Formal Definition
The Tarnhelm network (Figure 8.2a) analyzes the harmonic content of the Tarnhelm motive:
- Takes (B, +) as tonic for the motive in its own context
- Asserts structural relaxation on that Klang
- (E, +) is bracketed to indicate it functions by implication only; (E, -) substitutes in the music

# Mathematical Formulation
Network structure uses transformations:
- SUBM (submediant transformation)
- PAR (parallel transformation)

The combination of SUBM and PAR makes this genuinely non-intervallic:
- No simply transitive group contains both
- The network cannot be reduced to GIS structure

# Musical Context/Application
The Tarnhelm network is significant because:
1. It establishes B major as a contextual tonic for the motive
2. Wagner interprets the motive in B minor or B major contexts elsewhere (end of Gotterdammerung I, end of Tristan)
3. Its graph structure relates to the Valhalla network, revealing a deep functional connection

# Examples
From the Ring:
- The Tarnhelm (magic helmet) motive appears throughout the cycle
- The network shows its harmonic structure centered on (B, +)
- The (E, +) Klang functions implicitly though (E, -) sounds
- Visual comparison with Valhalla network (Figure 8.2b) shows structural relationship

# Common Confusions
- The (B, +) tonic is contextual, not the key of the surrounding music
- The bracketed (E, +) represents functional implication, not a sounded Klang
- The network relationship with Valhalla is structural, not audibly obvious
- "Non-intervallic" does not mean the analysis is less rigorous
