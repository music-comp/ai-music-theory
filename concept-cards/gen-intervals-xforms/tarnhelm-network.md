# Quick Definition
A Klang transformation network (Figure 8.2a) analyzing Wagner's Tarnhelm motive with (B, +) as tonic, using SUBM and PAR -- demonstrating genuinely non-intervallic transformational structure and revealing a visual/functional relationship with the Valhalla network.

# Core Definition
The Tarnhelm network "takes (B, +) as a tonic for the motive in its own context; it asserts structural relaxation on that Klang" (Lewin, 8.1.1, p. 178). The network uses both SUBM and PAR, making it genuinely non-intervallic. "(E, +) on network (a) is bracketed to indicate that this Klang functions by implication only; (E, -) substitutes for it in the music" (p. 178). The graphs "make visually clear a strong functional relationship between the Tarnhelm progression and the modulating portion of the Valhalla theme, a relationship which it is difficult to express in words" (p. 178).

# Key Properties
1. Tonic: (B, +)
2. Uses SUBM and PAR transformations
3. (E, +) is implied; (E, -) substitutes in the music
4. Genuinely non-intervallic (no simply transitive group contains both SUBM and PAR)
5. Visually related to Valhalla network, revealing deep functional connection

1. A Klang network centered on B major with SUBM and PAR arrows

# Context & Application
Wagner "not infrequently interprets the motive in a larger context of B minor or B major, e.g. at the end of Gotterdammerung I, or at the end of Tristan" (p. 178).
