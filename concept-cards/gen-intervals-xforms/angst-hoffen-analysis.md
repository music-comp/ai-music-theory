# Quick Definition
Lewin's analysis of Schoenberg's "Angst und Hoffen" (op.15 no.7) demonstrates INJ's power to engage wedge transformations, inversional symmetry, and the interplay of progressive and internal transformations.

# Formal Definition
Key structures:
- X = Angst chord = {Gb, Bb, D}
- Y = Hoffen chord = {Fb, Bb, Eb}
- w^E = wedge-to-E transformation
- I = I_E^Bb = inversion about E and Bb axis

INJ values reveal:
- INJ(X, Y)(w^E) = 2 (progressive but imperfect)
- INJ(X, X)(I) = 3 (fully internal)
- INJ(Y, Y)(I) = 2 (partially internal - "missing partner")

# Mathematical Formulation
Wedge analysis:
- w^E(D) = Eb (in Y)
- w^E(Bb) = Bb (in Y)
- w^E(Gb) = G (NOT in Y - "if only" Fb were F)

Inversion analysis:
- I(Gb) = D, I(D) = Gb, I(Bb) = Bb (all in X)
- I(Bb) = Bb, I(Eb) = F (NOT in Y), I(Fb) = E (NOT in Y)

The Fb of Y breaks both the wedge-to-E convergence and the I-symmetry.

Modulation: The F#-centered analysis (Figure 6.3c) relates to E-centered by:
- w^F# = T_2 w^E T_2^-1
- J = T_2 I T_2^-1

# Musical Context/Application
The analysis reveals multiple structural functions for Fb:
1. "Wrong note" - substitute for F that would complete the wedge
2. Enharmonic E - the focal point of w^E, giving tonic quality
3. Missing I-partner - Eb lacks its inversional partner F
4. Symbol of the absent lover (text: "Angst und Hoffen")

The piece develops toward resolution: the note F appears on "begehre" (require), allowing the wedge to converge to E in the Seufzer chord.

# Common Confusions
The analysis uses INJ for both chords and melody, showing the function's versatility. The melodic space involves (ordinal, pitch-class) pairs, not just pitch classes. Different spaces can be analyzed with the same INJ machinery.
