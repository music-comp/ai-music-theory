# Quick Definition
Transformations that cannot be expressed within the framework of a simply transitive group (GIS structure), requiring networks that go beyond intervallic/transpositional analysis.

# Formal Definition
A transformation is non-intervallic when:
1. No simply transitive group on the relevant family of objects contains the transformation, OR
2. The transformation group containing it is not simply transitive

For Klang transformations: Any group containing both SUBM (or MED) and PAR cannot be simply transitive, because both MED^7 and PAR map (C, +) to (C, -).

# Mathematical Formulation
The non-intervallic nature of certain Klang networks:
- The group containing MED is simply transitive (could be intervallic)
- Adding PAR creates a non-simply transitive group
- Proof: Given (C, +) and (C, -), both MED^7 and PAR map (C, +) to (C, -)
- This violates the uniqueness requirement of simple transitivity

Similarly, using SUBM and PAR, or REL and MED, leads to non-simply transitive groups.

# Musical Context/Application
Non-intervallic transformations are essential for capturing mode-changing relationships that pervade tonal music. The PAR, REL, LT, and SLIDE transformations all represent musically fundamental relationships that resist reduction to intervals. This demonstrates that transformational theory genuinely extends beyond GIS structure.

# Common Confusions
- "Non-intervallic" does not mean arbitrary or unstructured
- Non-intervallic transformations still form well-defined groups (just not simply transitive ones)
- The term applies to the transformation's status, not its musical effect
- Some Klang networks ARE intervallic (those using only MED powers)
