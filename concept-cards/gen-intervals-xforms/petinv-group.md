# Quick Definition
PETINV is the group generated by PETEY (interval-preserving and transposition operations) together with all inversion operations. It is the complete group of canonical GIS transformations.

# Core Definition
Theorem 3.5.11(B) defines PETINV = PETEY union INVS (all inversions) and proves it is a group. Closure requires four cases: (1) PETEY * PETEY is in PETEY; (2) PETEY * INVS yields inversions (via Theorems 3.5.6A, 3.5.7A); (3) INVS * PETEY yields inversions; (4) INVS * INVS yields PETEY elements (Theorem 3.5.8). Inverses exist since (I_u^v)^{-1} = I_v^u (Lewin, Theorem 3.5.11(B), pp. 89-90).

# Prerequisites
- **PETEY Group** — PETINV extends PETEY by adding inversions
- **Inversion Operation** — The inversions that are adjoined to PETEY

# Key Properties
1. PETINV = PETEY union INVS
2. PETEY is a subgroup (index 2 if inversions exist)
3. Inversions form a coset of PETEY
4. Composition of two inversions is in PETEY (Theorem 3.5.8)
5. Composition of PETEY element with inversion is an inversion
6. In commutative 12-tone GIS: PETINV = {T_0,...,T_{11}, I_0,...,I_{11}}, the familiar 24-element group

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Start with PETEY
2. Adjoin all inversion operations I_u^v
3. The resulting family is automatically a group

## To Recognize:
1. Identify a group of operations containing all T_i, P_i, and I_u^v

# Context & Application
PETINV is the natural "canonical group" for a GIS — it contains all standard operations one considers in GIS-based analysis. In the 12-tone system, it is the familiar group of 24 operations (12 transpositions + 12 inversions). In non-commutative GIS, it is much larger.

# Examples
**Example 1**: In 12-tone pitch-class GIS: PETINV = {T_0,...,T_{11}, I_0,...,I_{11}} with 24 elements.

**Example 2**: In non-commutative time-span GIS: PETINV contains all T_{(i,p)}, all P_{(h,u)}, and all I_s^t — a much larger group.

## Enables

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Forgetting to check all four cases for closure
  **Correction**: Must verify PETEY*PETEY, PETEY*INVS, INVS*PETEY, and INVS*INVS

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking PETINV is just PETEY plus one inversion
  **Clarification**: PETINV includes ALL inversion operations, which in non-commutative GIS are far more numerous (distinct I_u^v for each pair u, v).
