# Quick Definition
PETINV is the group generated by PETEY (interval-preserving and transposition operations) together with all inversion operations. It is the complete group of canonical GIS transformations.

# Formal Definition
**Theorem 3.5.11(B):** Let PETINV be the family PETEY plus the family INVS of all inversion operations. Then PETINV is also a group of operations.

# Mathematical Formulation
**Proof that PETINV is a group:**

PETINV = PETEY union INVS

**Inverses exist:**
- PETEY is a group, so inverses of PETEY elements are in PETEY subset PETINV
- By Corollary 3.5.9, (I_u^v)^(-1) = I_v^u, so inverses of inversions are in INVS subset PETINV

**Closure (four cases):**
1. X, Y in PETEY: XY in PETEY subset PETINV
2. X in PETEY, Y in INVS: TI = J (some inversion) by 3.5.6(A); PJ = K (some inversion) by 3.5.7(A). So XY = PTI = PJ = K in INVS.
3. X in INVS, Y in PETEY: Similarly, XY in INVS
4. X, Y in INVS: XY in PETEY by Theorem 3.5.8

Thus PETINV is closed. Combined with existence of inverses, PETINV is a group.

# Musical Context/Application
PETINV contains all the standard pitch-class operations: transpositions and inversions. In non-commutative GIS, it also includes the distinct interval-preserving operations. This is the natural "canonical group" of transformations for a GIS.

The structure of PETINV reflects the algebraic relationships between T, P, and I operations developed throughout Chapter 3.

# Examples
**12-tone pitch-class group:**
PETINV = {T0, T1, ..., T11, I0, I1, ..., I11}
This is the familiar group of 24 operations.
(Since T = P in commutative case, PETEY = TNSPS.)

**Non-commutative time-span GIS:**
PETINV contains:
- All transpositions T(i,p)
- All interval-preserving operations P(h,u)
- All inversions I_s^t

This is a much larger group than in the commutative case.

**Group structure:**
PETINV has PETEY as a subgroup of index 2 (if inversions exist).
Inversions form a coset of PETEY.

# Common Confusions
1. **PETINV is not just PETEY + one inversion:** It includes ALL inversion operations, which may be numerous in non-commutative GIS.

2. **Four cases for closure:** Students must verify all four combinations: PETEY*PETEY, PETEY*INVS, INVS*PETEY, INVS*INVS.

3. **Inverses of inversions:** These are in INVS (by Corollary 3.5.9), not necessarily the same inversion in non-commutative case.

4. **In commutative GIS:** PETINV is the familiar Tn/TnI group with 2n elements (n transpositions + n inversions).
