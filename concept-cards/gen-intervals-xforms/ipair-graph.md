# Quick Definition
A graph consisting of two nodes connected by a two-way I-arrow, representing an inversional pair. IPAIR functions as a "thematic graph" in Webern's Piano Variations, recurring isographically at multiple structural levels.

# Core Definition
"Let us denote by IPAIR the graph consisting of two nodes connected by a two-way I-arrow" (Lewin, 8.5, p. 192). In Webern's Piano Variations, IPAIR manifests isographically at three levels: (1) four pitch-class pair subnetworks in the second movement opening, (2) the row-form network (two I-related rows), and (3) the antecedent/consequent structure of the third movement. "IPAIR, in short, is a thematic graph" (p. 192).

# Prerequisites
- **I-arrows on graphs** — IPAIR consists of a two-way I-arrow

# Key Properties
1. Two nodes connected by a two-way I-arrow
2. The two-way arrow reflects the involutory nature of the inversion
3. Different IPAIRs (with different inversions) are isographic
4. IPAIR recurs as a thematic element across structural levels
5. Functions at pitch-class, row-form, and large-scale formal levels

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Identify two elements related by inversion
2. Connect their nodes with a two-way I-arrow
## To Recognize:
1. A pair of elements where each is the I-inversion of the other
2. The pattern recurring at multiple structural levels

# Context & Application
IPAIR as a "thematic graph" demonstrates how a simple graph structure can unify analysis across multiple domains and levels within a composition.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Figure 8.12b, p. 191): Four IPAIRs in Webern op. 27 second movement: Bb-G#, A-A, F-C#, D-E, all using I = I^A (inversion about A).

**Example 2** (p. 192): Row-form network: two row forms, each the I-inversion of the other, manifest IPAIR. Third movement antecedent/consequent forms also manifest IPAIR isorhythmically.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating any pitch-class pair as an IPAIR
  **Correction**: IPAIR requires a specific inversional relationship, not just any pairing

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking IPAIR requires the same inversion in every instance
  **Clarification**: Different instances may use different inversions and still be isographic as IPAIRs
