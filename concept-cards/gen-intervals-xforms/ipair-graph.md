# Quick Definition
A graph consisting of two nodes connected by a two-way I-arrow, representing an inversional pair relationship that can serve as a thematic structural element across a composition.

# Formal Definition
IPAIR is defined as:
- Two nodes connected by a two-way I-arrow
- I represents a specific inversion (e.g., I^A_A = inversion about pitch class A)
- The two-way arrow signifies that I is its own inverse (I(I(x)) = x)

# Mathematical Formulation
IPAIR graph structure:
- NODES: {N_1, N_2}
- ARROW: all pairs in the relation
- SGP: {E, I} where E = identity and I is the inversion
- TRANSIT: (N_1, N_2) -> I, (N_2, N_1) -> I, (N_i, N_i) -> E

For isographic applications:
- Different IPAIRs (with different inversions J) are isographic if their groups {E, J} are isomorphic to {E, I}

# Musical Context/Application
IPAIR functions as a "thematic graph" in Webern's Piano Variations op. 27:
1. Four IPAIR subnetworks appear in the second movement opening (Figure 8.12b)
2. The row-form network is isographic to IPAIR (two rows, each I-inversion of other)
3. The antecedent/consequent structure of movement 3 manifests IPAIR

The graph captures mirror/canon relationships fundamental to Webern's compositional technique.

# Examples
From Webern Piano Variations:
- Figure 8.12(b): Four IPAIRs relating Bb-G#, A-A, F-C#, D-E
- All use I = I^A_A (inversion about A)
- The row-form network: prime and its I-inversion form an IPAIR
- Movement 3 antecedent/consequent: isorhythmic I-related row forms

The isography across these different applications shows IPAIR as thematic:
- Pitch-class pairs
- Row-form pairs
- Large-scale formal pairs

# Common Confusions
- IPAIR requires a specific inversion, not just "some" inversional relationship
- Different inversions yield isographic (not identical) IPAIR graphs
- The "pair" is structural (related by I), not just any two elements
- IPAIR as thematic means it recurs isographically at different structural levels
