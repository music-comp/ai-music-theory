# Quick Definition
Harmonic intuition refers to our culturally conditioned perception of harmonic relationships between pitches, which Lewin argues underlies our understanding of intervals in just intonation.

# Formal Definition
In the discussion of Example 2.1.5, Lewin argues that our intuitions of harmonic intervals are "highly conditioned by cultural factors" and do not simply reflect acoustic physics. We intuit chains of basic harmonic relationships (octave, fifth, third) rather than directly perceiving complex frequency ratios like 45/32. The validity of measuring intervals by such ratios derives from our intuition of these basic moves.

# Mathematical Formulation
- We intuit basic harmonic relations with accepted measurements:
  - "t lies an octave above s": FQ(t) = 2 * FQ(s)
  - "t is the major third of s": FQ(t) = (5/4) * FQ(s)
  - "t is the dominant a fourth below s": FQ(t) = (3/4) * FQ(s)
- Complex intervals arise as chains of basic intuitions
- The ratio 45/32 = 2 * (5/4) * (3/4) * (3/4) reflects a chain: octave, mediant, dominant, dominant

# Musical Context/Application
Lewin's discussion challenges the view that harmonic intervals are directly perceived through physics. Instead, our sense of harmonic relationship arises from culturally learned chains of basic moves in harmonic space. This has implications for music theory: we should understand intervals as arising from intuited harmonic paths, not just acoustic measurements.

# Examples
From Example 2.1.5 and Figure 2.1:
- The succession C4-F#4 is heard harmonically as: "F#4 is the leading tone to the dominant of C"
- Constructing a path: C4 -> G3 (dominant) -> D3 (dominant of dominant) -> F#3 (mediant of D) -> F#4 (octave)
- The path gives: FQ(F#4) = 2 * (5/4) * (3/4) * (3/4) * FQ(C4)
- Hence int(C4, F#4) = 45/32

Lewin stresses: We do NOT intuit C4 and F#4 as sharing a common partial (32nd partial of one, 45th of the other), nor do we directly perceive the factorization 45/32 = 3^2 * 5 / 2^5.

# Common Confusions
- The acoustic ratio is valid as a measurement, but not as a direct intuition
- Complex ratios arise from chains of simple harmonic moves, not direct perception
- Different cultures may intuit different harmonic paths to the same acoustic interval
- The "natural" factorization of a ratio may differ from the "intuited" factorization
