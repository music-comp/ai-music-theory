# Quick Definition
The rotation operations RO, R3, R6, and R9 are the transposition-like operations in both STRANS1 and STRANS2 that move all pitch classes in the octatonic collection by a uniform distance (0, 3, 6, or 9 semitones respectively).

# Formal Definition
The rotation operations are induced from the standard twelve-tone transpositions T_0, T_3, T_6, and T_9 (the transpositions that preserve the octatonic collection). They are called "rotations" because they rotate all pitch classes uniformly through the octatonic set, preserving the structure of both constituent diminished-seventh chords.

# Mathematical Formulation
Rotation operations:
- RO = T_0 restricted to S (identity)
- R3 = T_3 restricted to S (minor third up)
- R6 = T_6 restricted to S (tritone)
- R9 = T_9 restricted to S (minor third down = major sixth up)

Properties:
- All rotations preserve each diminished-seventh chord
- {RO, R3, R6, R9} forms a subgroup isomorphic to Z_4
- All rotations appear in both STRANS1 and STRANS2
- Rotations commute with all other STRANS operations

Note: R6 is shared between STRANS1 and STRANS2; RO is the identity in both.

# Musical Context/Application
Rotation operations capture the familiar concept of transposition within octatonic space. They are the most intuitive STRANS operations because they correspond directly to standard twelve-tone transpositions. The rotation subgroup {RO, R3, R6, R9} appears in both STRANS1 and STRANS2.

# Examples
**Naming justification:**
"The operations RO, R3, R6, and R9 may be thought of as 'rotations,' to justify the use of the letter R in their names."

**Simple transitivity within dim7:**
"If t is in the same diminished-seventh chord as s, OP will be RO, R3, R6, or R9."

The rotations keep each diminished-seventh chord intact:
- From C, rotations produce: C (RO), D# (R3), F# (R6), A (R9) - all in same dim7
- From C#, rotations produce: C# (RO), E (R3), G (R6), A# (R9) - all in same dim7

**Shared between STRANS groups:**
RO and R6 appear in both STRANS1 and STRANS2:
- STRANS1 = {RO, R3, R6, R9, K, L, M, N}
- STRANS2 = {RO, Q3, R6, Q9, X1, X2, X4, X5}

R3 and R9 appear only in STRANS1; their "queer" counterparts Q3 and Q9 appear in STRANS2.

# Common Confusions
Students may expect R3 to appear in STRANS2, but it does not. STRANS2 contains Q3 instead, which rotates the two diminished-seventh chords in opposite directions. The shared rotations (RO and R6) are those that commute with both STRANS groups.
