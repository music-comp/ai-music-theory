# Quick Definition
The composition of RICH with itself: TCH = (RICH)(RICH). TCH always produces a transposed form of the input series, with the transposition interval depending on the series' internal structure.

# Formal Definition
TCH is defined as (RICH)(RICH):
- TCH(s) is always some transposed form of s
- Specifically: TCH(s) = T_i(s), where i = int(s_1, s_N) + int(s_2, s_{N-1})
- The interval i is called "the TCH-interval for s"

# Mathematical Formulation
For series s = s_1, s_2, ..., s_N:
- TCH = (RICH)(RICH)
- TCH(s) = T_i(s)
- i = int(s_1, s_N) + int(s_2, s_{N-1})

Properties:
- For retrograde or inverted forms of s: TCH-interval is -i (negative/inverse)
- For retrograde-inverted forms of s: TCH-interval is i (same as original)

# Musical Context/Application
TCH governs "structural sequencing" in RI-chaining contexts. When a motive is repeatedly RICH-chained, alternate forms are TCH-related (transposed). The TCH interval emerges from the motive's internal structure, creating a deep connection between motivic shape and large-scale transposition scheme.

# Examples
Wagner's Zauber motive (A-C-Eb-E):
- TCH-interval = int(A, E) + int(C, Eb) = 7 + 3 = 10
- Z_3 = TCH(Z_1) = T_10(Z_1)
- Structural sequence: mm. 1096-1140 at T_10 of mm. 1074-1100

Webern's Piano Variations row:
- TCH-interval = int(Eb, G#) + int(B, A) = 5 + 10 = 3
- TCH produces T_3 transposition

# Common Confusions
- TCH is not a fixed transposition; the interval depends on the series
- Writing "T_10" instead of "TCH" would obscure the connection between Wagner and Webern analyses
- TCH for an RI form of s has the same interval as TCH for s itself
- TCH emerges from RICH composition, not as an independent definition
