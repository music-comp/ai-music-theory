# Quick Definition
An ictus is a time-point at which a listener first becomes aware of predominating interval patterns, marking the emergence of perceivable mensural structure during the temporal unfolding of music.

# Formal Definition
In the theory of unfolding interval vectors, an ictus occurs at a time-point when:
1. Certain intervals begin to predominate over others
2. The listener can first sense regular mensural structuring
3. The beat becomes perceptible as a measuring unit

The ictus marks the transition from amorphous succession to structured mensuration.

# Mathematical Formulation
**Detection via unfolding interval vector:**
As elements accumulate, track which intervals recur.
When some interval(s) predominate (appear more frequently than others), that time-point is a candidate ictus.

**In GIS3 analysis:**
Both pitch-class intervals and temporal intervals may begin to predominate at the ictus, binding pitch structure to mensural structure.

# Musical Context/Application
The ictus concept addresses a psychological question: At what point during listening do we first hear a beat? The answer depends on accumulated intervallic evidence, not just the first attack.

Lewin cites his earlier work on "foreground rhythmic and metric patterning" as background for this concept.

# Examples
**Webern analysis (Figure 3.3-3.4):**
Elements: (Eb, 0), (B, 3), (Bb, 4), (D, 5)...

After (D, 5):
- Temporal interval 1 begins to predominate
- Pitch-class interval 11 also predominates
- Time-point 5 is the first ictus

**Why time-point 5?**
- First notated barline
- First "strong" quarter (intuitively)
- First predominance of any intervals

**Before the ictus:**
Time-points 0-4 provide no clear mensural structure. Listeners reconstruct this period retrospectively from the ictus.

**After the ictus:**
Subsequent patterns are heard in relation to the established mensuration. New icti may reinforce or challenge the structure.

# Common Confusions
1. **Ictus =/= downbeat:** An ictus is a perceptual moment, not a notational position. They may coincide but are conceptually different.

2. **First ictus =/= first note:** The ictus occurs when patterns emerge, not at the first attack.

3. **Retrospective hearing:** Before the ictus, events are "heard" only retrospectively. The theory models developing perception.

4. **Multiple icti:** A piece may have several icti, marking shifts in mensural structure (e.g., time-point 10 in the Webern analysis).
