# Quick Definition
An ictus is a time-point at which a listener first becomes aware of predominating interval patterns, marking the emergence of perceivable mensural structure during the temporal unfolding of music.

# Core Definition
In the theory of unfolding interval vectors, an ictus occurs at a time-point when certain intervals begin to predominate over others, allowing the listener to first sense regular mensural structuring. The ictus marks the transition from amorphous succession to structured mensuration. Lewin connects this to his earlier work on "foreground rhythmic and metric patterning" (p. 74).

# Prerequisites
- **Unfolding interval vector** — The progressive record of intervals that reveals when patterns emerge
- **GIS3-interval** — Compound pitch-time intervals whose recurrence signals structural emergence

# Key Properties
1. The ictus is a perceptual moment, not a notational position
2. It occurs when intervals begin to predominate (appear more frequently)
3. Both pitch-class intervals and temporal intervals may predominate simultaneously at the ictus
4. Events before the ictus are heard retrospectively in light of the emergent structure
5. Multiple icti may occur in a piece, marking shifts in mensural structure

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Build the unfolding interval vector as elements enter chronologically
2. Track which intervals recur and begin to predominate
3. The time-point at which predominance first emerges is the ictus
## To Recognize:
1. A time-point where the interval vector shows first recurrence or predominance
2. The moment when metric hearing becomes possible for a listener

# Context & Application
The ictus concept addresses a psychological question: at what point during listening do we first hear a beat? The answer depends on accumulated intervallic evidence, not just the first attack. The theory models developing perception -- events before the ictus are not yet situated in a mensural framework and are reconstructed retrospectively.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Figures 3.3-3.4, pp. 73-76): Webern Piano Variations analysis. Elements enter as (Eb, 0), (B, 3), (Bb, 4), (D, 5)... After (D, 5) arrives: temporal interval 1 begins to predominate and pitch-class interval 11 also predominates. Time-point 5 is proposed as the first ictus. This coincides with the first notated barline and the first "strong" quarter.

**Example 2** (Figure 3.5, p. 75): At time-point 10, the GIS3-interval (11, 5) recurs for the first time (Eb-to-D and D-to-C#), intensifying the "5/4 meter" sensation established at the ictus.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Equating the ictus with the first downbeat or first note
  **Correction**: The ictus occurs when patterns emerge, which may be several events after the first attack

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The ictus is a notational concept like a downbeat
  **Clarification**: It is a perceptual moment that may or may not coincide with notated bar positions
- **Confusion**: Events before the ictus have no analytical significance
  **Clarification**: Pre-ictus events contribute to the unfolding interval vector and are heard retrospectively once the ictus establishes a mensural framework
