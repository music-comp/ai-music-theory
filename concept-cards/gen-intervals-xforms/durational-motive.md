# Quick Definition
A durational motive (DM) is a specific rhythmic pattern of time intervals between attacks that can undergo serial transformations parallel to those applied to pitch motives.

# Formal Definition
A durational motive is a series of numerical durations representing the time intervals (in beat units) between successive note attacks. Like pitch motives, durational motives can be subjected to transformations including transposition (multiplication of all values), inversion, retrograde, and retrograde-inversion.

# Mathematical Formulation
For a durational motive DM = (d1, d2, d3):
- Multiplicative transposition T_n: T_n(DM) = (n*d1, n*d2, n*d3)
- Multiplicative inversion I about product p: I_p(DM) = (p/d1, p/d2, p/d3)
- Additive inversion I about sum s: I_s(DM) = (s-d1, s-d2, s-d3)
- Retrograde R(DM) = (d3, d2, d1)
- RICH(DM) = multiplicative or additive retrograde-inversion

# Musical Context/Application
Durational motives enable analysts to apply the same transformational apparatus used for pitch analysis to the rhythmic domain. This reveals how composers create rhythmic coherence and development through transformational processes that parallel pitch manipulation.

# Examples
In Mozart's K.550 development (Figure 10.1), Lewin identifies the durational motive DM = 1 + 2 + 2 (in quarter-note beats) as the rhythmic setting for the pitch motive PM.

The transformations proceed as follows:
- Bracket 1, 2: DM = 1 + 2 + 2
- Bracket 3: T_2(DM) = 2 + 4 + 4 (augmentation by factor of 2)
- Bracket 4a: R(bracket 3) = 4 + 4 + 2
- Bracket 4b: I(bracket 3) = 4 + 2 + 2 (also RICH of 4a)
- Bracket 5: RICH(DM) = 2 + 2 + 4

The multiplicative inversion can be computed as 8/2, 8/4, 8/4 = 4, 2, 2, or additively as 6-2, 6-4, 6-4 = 4, 2, 2.

# Common Confusions
Students often overlook that durational inversion can be computed either multiplicatively (division about a product) or additively (subtraction from a sum), and both yield the same result in certain cases. The dual nature of durational inversion differs from pitch-class inversion, which is purely additive in mod-12 arithmetic.
