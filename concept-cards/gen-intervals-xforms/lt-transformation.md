# Quick Definition
Riemann's "leading-tone exchange" as a Klang transformation: the operation that exchanges a major triad with the minor triad whose root is a major third above (and vice versa): (C, +)LT = (E, -) and (E, -)LT = (C, +).

# Formal Definition
LT (Leading-Tone exchange) is defined as:
- (p, +)LT = (p + 4, -): Major Klang to minor Klang with root a major third higher
- (p, -)LT = (p - 4, +): Minor Klang to major Klang with root a major third lower

LT is its own inverse: (LT)(LT) = identity

# Mathematical Formulation
LT transformation:
- (p, +)LT = (p + 4, -) mod 12
- (p, -)LT = (p + 8, +) mod 12
- LT^2 = identity (LT is an involution)

The name comes from the fact that C major and E minor share two common tones (E and G), with only the leading-tone B/C relationship differing.

# Musical Context/Application
LT captures a parsimonious voice-leading relationship where two triads share two common tones. This transformation is central to Neo-Riemannian theory (though Lewin predates that term). LT represents one of the "basic" triadic transformations alongside PAR and REL.

# Examples
Applications:
- (C, +)LT = (E, -): C major to E minor
- (E, -)LT = (C, +): E minor to C major
- (F, +)LT = (A, -): F major to A minor
- (G#, -)LT = (E, +): G# minor to E major

Common tones:
- C major {C, E, G} and E minor {E, G, B} share {E, G}
- The transformation moves C to B (leading tone motion)

# Common Confusions
- LT is not the same as MED or any power of MED
- The "leading tone" in the name refers to voice-leading, not the scale degree per se
- LT is an involution (self-inverse)
- LT is the "L" (Leittonwechsel) in the PLR group of Neo-Riemannian theory
