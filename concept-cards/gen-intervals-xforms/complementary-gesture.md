# Quick Definition
An intervallic/transpositional structure articulating pitch-class interval 8 into two subintervals of 10, serving as the complement to the nuclear gesture in the analysis of Brahms's Horn Trio op. 40, last movement.

# Core Definition
The complementary gesture "articulates 8 into 10 + 10" (Lewin, 7.3, p. 165), complementing the nuclear gesture's 4 = 2 + 2. In passage (g), rhythmic augmentation by factor 2 produces "the complementary gesture times 2": an overall interval of 4 subarticulated into 8 + 8, where "just as rhythmic values are multiplied by 2..., so intervallic values are also multiplied by 2. Instead of the complementary gesture 8 = 10 + 10, we now have an expanded gesture involving twice those numbers: 2 * 8 = 2 * 10 + 2 * 10, or modulo 12, 4 = 8 + 8" (p. 168).

# Prerequisites
- **Nuclear gesture** — The complementary gesture is defined in relation to the nuclear gesture

# Key Properties
1. Overall interval: 8
2. Subarticulation: 10 + 10
3. The "times 2" variant: 4 = 8 + 8 (intervallic values multiplied by 2)
4. The pickup interval 5 is preserved (not complemented to 7)
5. Each stage may be inflected by its own pickup

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Identify an overall pitch-class interval of 8
2. Verify it subarticulates into two steps of 10
## To Recognize:
1. Complement the nuclear gesture's intervals: 8 complements 4, 10 complements 2

# Context & Application
The complementary gesture governs several passages in Brahms's Horn Trio finale. In passage (g), the interaction with the "octave-Bb idea" creates the "times 2" variant. This variant was "already presented in the first movement" in passage (h), derived "in a musical manner that was almost the equivalent of an algebraic demonstration" (p. 169).

# Examples

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Expecting the pickup interval to be complemented (becoming 7 instead of 5)
  **Correction**: "The pickup interval of 5 does not get complemented; it always remains 5, never becoming 7" (p. 165)

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the "times 2" variant is a different gesture entirely
  **Clarification**: It is the same complementary gesture with both rhythmic and intervallic values multiplied by 2
