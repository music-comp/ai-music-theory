# Quick Definition
An intervallic configuration comprising pitch-class interval 8 subarticulated into two intervals of 10 - the complement to the nuclear gesture in Brahms's Horn Trio op. 40, last movement.

# Formal Definition
The complementary gesture (Figure 9.6a):
- Overall interval: 8
- Subarticulation: 10 + 10 (mod 12)
- Often inflected by pickup interval 5 (which does not complement to 7)

The gesture complements the nuclear gesture (4 = 2 + 2): interval 8 complements 4; interval 10 complements 2.

# Mathematical Formulation
As a network graph:
- Three nodes connected by arrows labeled 10
- Overall span: 8 = 10 + 10 (mod 12)
- Verification: 10 + 10 = 20 = 8 (mod 12)

The complementary gesture times 2 (Figure 9.6b):
- Overall: 4 = 2 x 8 (mod 12)
- Subarticulation: 8 + 8 = 2 x 10 + 2 x 10 (mod 12)

# Musical Context/Application
The complementary gesture governs the second half of the basic motive and appears throughout the movement in dialogue with the nuclear gesture. The "complementary gesture times 2" emerges in the horn solo through rhythmic augmentation, demonstrating how intervallic augmentation can accompany temporal augmentation.

# Examples
From Figure 7.7:
- (a) Second half of basic motive: 8 = 10 + 10
- (c) Melody descending from first climax: complementary gesture with pickups
- (g) Horn solo mm. 137-44: complementary gesture Bb-to-Gb
- (g) Horn solo mm. 145-49: complementary gesture times 2 (4 = 8 + 8)

The horn solo's rhythmic augmentation (values x 2) accompanies intervallic augmentation (intervals x 2).

# Common Confusions
- "Complementary" refers to the mod-12 complement relationship with the nuclear gesture
- The pickup interval 5 does NOT complement to 7; it always remains 5
- The "times 2" gesture emerges through both rhythmic and intervallic processes
- The graph homomorphism from (a) to (b) in Figure 9.6 formalizes the augmentation relationship
