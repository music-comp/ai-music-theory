# Quick Definition
An underlying structural pattern, symbolized by a normative graph, of which different musical passages are distinct realizations in different environments -- enabling an alternative to parody-based critical interpretations.

# Core Definition
When two musical passages share the same underlying transformational structure, Lewin proposes that both can be conceived as "different realizations, in different environments, of one underlying abstract gesture, a gesture symbolized by" a normative graph (7.4, p. 170). This shifts critical stance from viewing one passage as parodying the other (inferring structural priority from temporal priority) to viewing both as equally valid manifestations of an abstract structure.

# Prerequisites
- **Transformational attitude** — The conceptual framework for thinking in terms of gestures
- **Normative network** — The graph that symbolizes the abstract gesture

# Key Properties
1. An abstract gesture is not tied to any particular musical realization
2. Multiple passages may realize the same gesture differently
3. Neither realization has inherent structural priority over the other
4. The gesture is captured by a graph of transformational relationships
5. Different environments produce different surface characteristics while sharing deep structure

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Identify two or more passages sharing transformational structure
2. Abstract the common graph of nodes and labeled arrows
3. Propose this graph as the "normative" gesture
## To Recognize:
1. The same graph structure appears in multiple passages with different surface features
2. The passages can be understood without granting structural priority to either

# Context & Application
Lewin illustrates this with an analogy: encountering Mr. X at a formal event and then at a park picnic. Rather than saying one encounter is "normative" and the other a "parody," we recognize a common "X-ness" manifested differently. He notes that "the mere possibility of this shift in critical stance is important, no matter which stance one prefers" (p. 170).

# Examples

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming the temporally earlier passage must be the "norm" that later passages parody
  **Correction**: The abstract gesture framework allows either to be treated as equally primary

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking abstract gestures eliminate temporal/contextual considerations
  **Clarification**: Temporal and contextual factors remain important; the abstract gesture is one analytical tool among many
