# Quick Definition
A compositional phenomenon where large-scale passages are related by transposition through RI-chaining, even when their foreground musical events are entirely different.

# Formal Definition
Structural sequencing occurs when:
1. Serial forms are connected by repeated RI-chaining (RICH operations)
2. The TCH transformation relates alternate forms
3. Large passages built on these forms exhibit transposition relationships
4. The foreground content of these passages need not be sequentially related

# Mathematical Formulation
Given series s with TCH-interval i:
- If Z_1 is a form of s, and Z_2 = RICH(Z_1), then Z_3 = TCH(Z_1) = T_i(Z_1)
- Passages built on Z_1 and Z_3 are "structurally sequential" at transposition level i
- The foreground realizations of these passages may be completely different

# Musical Context/Application
Structural sequencing represents a deep-level organizing principle that may not be audible at the surface but creates coherence in the underlying transformational structure. It demonstrates how motivic relationships can govern large spans of music at a level divorced from immediate thematic content.

# Common Confusions
- Structural sequencing is not audible as traditional sequential repetition
- The foreground may show completely different material
- The sequence relationship exists at the level of organizing serial forms, not melodic content
- This is a formal/structural relationship, not a perceptual one at the surface level
