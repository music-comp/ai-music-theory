# Quick Definition
The formal inverse of DOM; SUBD transforms a Klang so that it becomes the subdominant of the result: (F, +)SUBD = (C, +) means "F major becomes the subdominant of C major."

# Formal Definition
SUBD = DOM^(-1):
- (p, sign)SUBD = (q, sign), where p is the subdominant of q
- Reading: (p, sign) becomes the subdominant of (q, sign)
- Preserves mode

# Mathematical Formulation
SUBD transformation:
- (p, sign)SUBD = (p + 7, sign) mod 12
- SUBD is equivalent to T_7 on the pitch-class component
- Mode is preserved

Relationship to DOM:
- SUBD = DOM^(-1)
- (DOM)(SUBD) = (SUBD)(DOM) = identity

# Musical Context/Application
SUBD arrows can represent plagal motion (IV to I) when pointing forward chronologically. The graphic format distinguishes a SUBD arrow read forwards from a DOM arrow read backwards, allowing analytical precision about functional direction even when both involve the same pitch-class pair.

# Examples
Applications:
- (F, +)SUBD = (C, +): F major becomes subdominant of C major
- (G, -)SUBD = (D, -): G minor becomes subdominant of D minor

On a network (plagal cadence in C major):
- Draw SUBD arrow pointing from (F, +) on left to (C, +) on right
- This shows F major leading to C major with subdominant function

Contrast with half cadence in F major:
- (F, +) on left, (C, +) on right
- DOM arrow points leftward from (C, +) to (F, +)
- Shows (C, +) referring back to (F, +) as dominant

# Common Confusions
- SUBD and DOM^(-1) are the same operation but convey different analytical meanings
- A SUBD arrow forward differs analytically from a DOM arrow backward
- SUBD does not change mode
- The distinction between forward SUBD and backward DOM is about functional interpretation, not mathematical content
