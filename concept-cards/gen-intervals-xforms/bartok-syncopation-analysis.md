# Quick Definition
Lewin's analysis of Bartok's "Syncopation" (Mikrokosmos No. 133) demonstrates RI-chaining in non-serial, non-twelve-tone music, revealing how transformational theory applies to modal and pentatonic contexts.

# Formal Definition
This analysis shows how RICH transformations and RI-chaining can organize harmonic progressions in music that is distinctly not serial or twelve-tone. The piece features two contrasting pitch collections (white notes vs. black notes) with RI-chains operating within each collection, governed by the "color barrier" that prevents chains from crossing between collections.

# Mathematical Formulation
The RI-chains in "Syncopation":
- Left hand (white notes): A-C-E -> C-E-G -> E-G-B -> G-B-D (G Mixolydian mode)
- Right hand (black notes): Two chains with trichord substitution at the "color barrier"

Triple proportion in Figure 10.6:
- C#-D#-F# : F#-G#-B :: G#-A#-C# : C#-D#-F#
- TCH interval = 5 (spanning the trichords)

# Musical Context/Application
"Syncopation" exemplifies how transformational methods developed for atonal and twelve-tone analysis extend to other idioms. The piece's structural contrasts between hands (white/black notes, down/up metrically, sotto/sopra) organize through RI-chains within each domain.

# Examples
From Figure 10.5, the piece's structural elements:

**Tonal contrasts:**
- Left hand: white notes, G Mixolydian, G-major chord of reference
- Right hand: black notes, F# pentatonic, F# added-sixth chord of reference
- Final sonority (G) + (A#-C#-D#-F#): the only non-syncopated attack

**RI-chaining in left hand (measures 11-25):**
Registrally ordered trichords: A-C-E -> C-E-G -> E-G-B -> G-B-D
The chain "cannot pass the color barrier" when it generates the white F natural at measure 25 (not black F#).

**RI-chaining in right hand:**
First chain: C#-D#-F# -> D#-F#-G# -> F#-G#-B (breaks at color barrier)
Substitution: G#-A#-C# replaces G#-B-C# (B replaced by A#)
Second chain continues from G#-A#-C#

**Triple proportion (Figure 10.6):**
"C#-D#-F# (input) is to F#-G#-B (output) as G#-A#-C# (input) is to C#-D#-F# (output)"
This creates "isography of the left side with the right side" and sounds "almost like I-IV-V-I."

# Common Confusions
Students may assume transformational analysis only applies to twelve-tone or atonal music. This analysis demonstrates that RI-chaining and related operations function meaningfully in modal and tonal contexts, though collections may impose constraints (like the "color barrier") that do not exist in chromatic space.
