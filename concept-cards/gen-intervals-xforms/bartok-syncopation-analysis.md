# Quick Definition
Lewin's analysis of Bartok's "Syncopation" (Mikrokosmos No. 133) demonstrates RI-chaining in modal and pentatonic contexts, revealing how the "color barrier" between white-note and black-note collections constrains transformational processes and how isographic triple proportions emerge.

# Core Definition
This analysis (Section 10.2) shows how RICH transformations and RI-chaining organize harmonic progressions in music that is distinctly non-serial. The piece features structural contrasts between hands: left hand plays white notes (G Mixolydian), right hand plays black notes (F# pentatonic). RI-chains operate within each collection but are constrained by the "color barrier" that prevents chains from crossing between collections (Lewin, pp. 258-262).

# Key Properties
1. Left hand: white notes, G Mixolydian, G-major chord of reference
2. Right hand: black notes, F# pentatonic, F# added-sixth chord of reference
3. Final sonority (G) + (A#-C#-D#-F#): the only simultaneously attacked, non-syncopated harmony
4. RI-chains operate within each collection but break at the color barrier
5. Right-hand chains require trichord substitution when the barrier is reached
6. Triple proportion with isographic left/right sides (Figure 10.6)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Identify white-note and black-note collections
2. Trace RI-chains within each collection
3. Identify color barrier breakpoints
4. Analyze substitutions at barrier crossings
## To Recognize:
1. Look for RI-chaining constrained by collection boundaries

# Context & Application
This analysis demonstrates that transformational methods developed for atonal and twelve-tone music extend to modal and tonal contexts. The color barrier is a compositionally imposed constraint that creates opportunities for substitution and structural articulation.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Figure 10.5, pp. 258-261): Left hand RI-chain: A-C-E -> C-E-G -> E-G-B -> G-B-D. Chain breaks when F natural (not F#) appears.

**Example 2** (Figure 10.5): Right hand chain breaks at F#-G#-B; the white B must be replaced by black A# = Bb, yielding G#-A#-C# instead of G#-B-C#. "The substitution preserves the outer voices of the trichord involved, and also preserves its set-class."

**Example 3** (Figure 10.6, p. 262): Triple proportion: C#-D#-F# (input) is to F#-G#-B (output) as G#-A#-C# (input) is to C#-D#-F# (output). TCH interval = 5. "The triple proportion here sound[s] almost like I-IV-V-I."

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming RI-chains always continue freely in any pitch-class context
  **Correction**: Collection-based constraints (like the color barrier) may force substitutions or chain breaks

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking transformational analysis applies only to twelve-tone music
  **Clarification**: This analysis specifically demonstrates applicability to modal and pentatonic contexts
