# Quick Definition
A conceptual stance that asks "what characteristic gesture should I perform to get from s to t?" rather than "what is the measured distance between s and t?" - positioning the analyst inside the music as an idealized dancer or singer rather than as an external observer.

# Formal Definition
The transformational attitude contrasts with the Cartesian observational stance:
- Cartesian: "The interval from s to t" models a relation of extension observed in external space; we "see" distances between points
- Transformational: Given locations s and t, we ask "If I am at s and wish to get to t, what characteristic gesture (member of STRANS) should I perform to arrive there?"

The attitude generalizes: "If I want to change Gestalt 1 into Gestalt 2, what sorts of admissible transformations will do the best job?"

# Mathematical Formulation
Rather than computing int(s, t) as a measurement, we seek the transformation f such that f(s) = t. The mathematical content may be identical, but the conceptual framing differs:
- Interval view: int(s, t) = i (passive measurement)
- Transformational view: T_i(s) = t (active operation)

# Musical Context/Application
The transformational attitude places the analyst "inside the music" rather than as a detached observer. It connects naturally to embodied musical experience - the kinetic sense of moving between pitches when singing, or the gestural sense of modulating between keys. This perspective is particularly valuable for analyzing music where transformation and process are foregrounded.

# Examples
When analyzing a sequence that moves through keys by descending fifths:
- Cartesian view: The keys are separated by intervals of a fifth
- Transformational view: We perform the gesture of "descending a fifth" repeatedly, experiencing the motion rather than observing the distances

# Common Confusions
- The transformational attitude does not reject interval measurement; it offers an alternative conceptualization
- "Inside the music" does not mean purely subjective; formal rigor is maintained
- The attitude is not limited to pitch transformations; it applies to any musical parameter with appropriate transformation structure
