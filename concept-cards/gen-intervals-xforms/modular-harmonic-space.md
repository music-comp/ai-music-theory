# Quick Definition

Modular harmonic space is a GIS of pitch classes under just intonation, visualized as a two-dimensional game board where intervals measure "b dominants and c mediants," with IVLS = Z x Z.

# Core Definition

"The musical space comprises the pitch classes generated by the space of example 2.1.5 above. Given pitch classes s and t, int(s, t) is the ordered pair of integers (b, c) such that t lies b dominants and c mediants from s" (Lewin, Example 2.1.6, p. 50). The interval group IVLS is Z x Z under componentwise addition, the direct product of the integers with themselves (Section 2.4). The space is visualized as Figure 2.2, an infinite game board.

# Prerequisites

- **Group** — IVLS = Z x Z is a group (direct product)
- **Direct Product** — the interval group is a direct product of Z with Z
- **Just Intonation Pitch Space** — this space modularizes Example 2.1.5

# Key Properties

1. S = pitch classes on the infinite game board (Figure 2.2)
2. IVLS = Z x Z under componentwise addition
3. int(s, t) = (b, c) where t is b squares east and c squares north of s
4. This reduces 2.1.5 by collapsing octaves (powers of 2)
5. Subscripted pitch classes (C_0, C_1, ...) distinguish different harmonic positions

# Construction / Recognition

## To Construct:
1. Start from just intonation pitch space (2.1.5)
2. Reduce modulo octaves (declare pitches equivalent if they differ by powers of 2)
3. The resulting pitch classes arrange on the infinite game board

## To Recognize:
1. Two-dimensional space of pitch classes
2. Horizontal axis = dominant relationships, vertical axis = mediant relationships
3. Intervals are integer pairs (b, c)

# Context & Application

Lewin visualizes this space as Figure 2.2, a game board where intervals are "moves." The interval (2, 1) is a "knight's move" (2 east, 1 north). The space extends infinitely because distinct subscripted pitch classes (C_0, C_1, ...) differ by syntonic commas. This system can be further reduced to mean-tone or equal-tempered systems by imposing additional equivalences.

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 50): int(C, G) = (1, 0), int(G, D) = (1, 0), int(D, F#) = (0, 1), int(C, F#) = (2, 1), int(C, F) = (-1, 0), int(C, Ab) = (0, -1), int(C, Db) = (-1, -1).

**Example 2** (p. 51): The "knight's move" (2, 1) takes C_0 to F#_1, or A_1 to D#_2, or Db_(-1) to G_0 on the game board.

**Example 3** (p. 51): Reducing further by declaring C_(-1) = C_0 = C_1 (same letter, ignore subscripts) yields a mean-tone system, and further enharmonic reduction recovers the 12-note equal-tempered system of Example 2.1.3.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming C_0 and C_1 are the same pitch class.
  **Correction**: In just intonation, C_0 and C_1 differ by a syntonic comma and are acoustically distinct.

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking this is the same as the chromatic pitch-class GIS.
  **Clarification**: This is a two-dimensional harmonic space with an infinite number of pitch classes, not the 12-element chromatic system.
