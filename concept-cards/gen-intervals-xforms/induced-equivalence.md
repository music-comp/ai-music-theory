# Quick Definition
The induced equivalence is an equivalence relation on the space S of a GIS generated by a congruence on IVLS: two elements s and s' are equivalent whenever int(s, s') is congruent to the identity element e.

# Core Definition
Given a GIS (S, IVLS, int) and a congruence CONG on IVLS, the induced equivalence EQUIV on S declares s and s' equivalent whenever int(s, s') is congruent to e in IVLS. Theorem/Definition 3.2.1 proves this is indeed an equivalence relation. Lemma 3.2.2 establishes the critical compatibility property: if s ~ s' and t ~ t', then int(s, t) is congruent to int(s', t') (Lewin, Theorem/Definition 3.2.1 and Lemma 3.2.2, pp. 65-66).

# Prerequisites
- **Congruence Relation in GIS** — The induced equivalence derives from a congruence on IVLS
- **Generalized Interval System** — Requires the int function and GIS conditions
- **Equivalence Relation** — The induced equivalence must satisfy reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity

# Key Properties
1. s EQUIV s' iff int(s, s') CONG e
2. Reflexive: int(s, s) = e, and e CONG e
3. Symmetric: if int(s, s') CONG e, then int(s', s) = int(s, s')^{-1} CONG e
4. Transitive: if int(s, s') CONG e and int(s', s'') CONG e, then int(s, s'') = int(s, s') int(s', s'') CONG e
5. Compatibility (Lemma 3.2.2): if s ~ s' and t ~ t', then int(s, t) CONG int(s', t')
6. The equivalence classes of S under EQUIV form the space of the quotient GIS

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Start with a GIS (S, IVLS, int) and a congruence CONG on IVLS
2. Declare s ~ s' iff int(s, s') CONG e
3. The equivalence classes partition S into the quotient space S/EQUIV

## To Recognize:
1. Identify an equivalence relation on S
2. Check whether elements are equivalent precisely when their interval is congruent to the identity
3. Verify the compatibility condition of Lemma 3.2.2

# Context & Application
The induced equivalence captures the musical idea that elements become "the same" when they differ only by an interval declared trivial by the congruence. In pitch space, octave equivalence is induced by the congruence mod 12 on semitone intervals. In time-point space, beat-class equivalence is induced by the congruence mod N on beat intervals.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 64): Octave equivalence in chromatic space:
- CONG: intervals congruent when they differ by multiples of 12
- EQUIV: pitches equivalent when separated by whole octaves (int(s, s') divisible by 12)
- Equivalence classes = 12 pitch classes

**Example 2** (p. 68): Beat-class equivalence:
- CONG: time intervals congruent when they differ by N beats
- EQUIV: time-points equivalent when N beats apart
- Equivalence classes = N beat-classes

**Example 3** (p. 67): Just-intonation octave classes:
- CONG: frequency ratios congruent when they differ by powers of 2
- EQUIV: pitches equivalent when octave-related (int(s, s') = 2^n)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Defining the equivalence independently of the congruence on IVLS
  **Correction**: The equivalence on S must be induced by a congruence on IVLS; arbitrary equivalences may not yield a well-defined quotient GIS

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking int(s, s') CONG e means int(s, s') = e
  **Clarification**: Elements in the same equivalence class need not have zero interval; they have an interval congruent to zero

- **Confusion**: Assuming the symmetry of EQUIV is obvious
  **Clarification**: Symmetry requires showing that if x CONG e then x^{-1} CONG e — a lemma requiring proof using group properties
