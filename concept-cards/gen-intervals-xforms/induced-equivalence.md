# Quick Definition
The induced equivalence is an equivalence relation on the space S of a GIS that is generated by a congruence relation on the interval group IVLS, declaring two elements equivalent when the interval between them is congruent to the identity.

# Formal Definition
Let (S, IVLS, int) be a GIS and CONG be a congruence on IVLS. The induced equivalence EQUIV on S is defined by:

s EQUIV s' if and only if int(s, s') is congruent to e (the identity element of IVLS)

This is indeed an equivalence relation (reflexive, symmetric, transitive).

# Mathematical Formulation
**Theorem/Definition 3.2.1:**

Reflexivity: int(s, s) = e, so s EQUIV s

Symmetry: If int(s, s') is congruent to e, then int(s', s) = int(s, s')^(-1) is also congruent to e

Transitivity: If int(s, s') and int(s', s'') are both congruent to e, then int(s, s'') = int(s, s') int(s', s'') is congruent to e * e = e

**Lemma 3.2.2:** If s EQUIV s' and t EQUIV t', then int(s, t) is congruent to int(s', t').

Proof: int(s, t) = int(s, s') int(s', t') int(t', t), and both int(s, s') and int(t', t) are congruent to e.

# Musical Context/Application
The induced equivalence captures the idea that elements become "the same" when they differ only by an interval that has been declared trivial (congruent to identity). In music:

- Pitches become equivalent (same pitch class) when they differ by an octave
- Time-points become equivalent (same beat class) when they differ by a measure
- Diatonic pitches become equivalent when they differ by a scale-octave

# Examples
**Octave equivalence in chromatic space:**
- CONG declares intervals congruent when they differ by multiples of 12 semitones
- EQUIV makes pitches equivalent when they differ by any number of octaves
- int(s, s') divisible by 12 => s EQUIV s'

**Beat-class equivalence:**
- CONG declares time intervals congruent when they differ by N beats (measure length)
- EQUIV makes time-points equivalent when they are N beats apart
- int(s, s') divisible by N => s EQUIV s'

**Just intonation octave classes:**
- CONG declares frequency ratios congruent when they differ by powers of 2
- EQUIV makes pitches equivalent when they are octave-related
- int(s, s') = 2^n for integer n => s EQUIV s'

# Common Confusions
1. The equivalence is *induced* by a congruence on intervals, not defined independently on elements. One cannot arbitrarily choose which elements are equivalent; the equivalence must arise from interval structure.

2. The condition int(s, s') congruent to e is not the same as int(s, s') = e. Elements in the same equivalence class need not have zero interval between them, only an interval that is congruent to zero.

3. Students may forget that symmetry of EQUIV requires showing that inverse of a congruent-to-e element is also congruent to e. This is a lemma requiring proof.
