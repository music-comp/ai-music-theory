# Quick Definition
A progression of inversion centers occurs when successive harmonic substitutions or transformations employ different inversional axes, creating a network where the centers themselves form a coherent pattern related to motivic material.

# Formal Definition
When multiple inversion operations organize musical structure, the centers (or axes) of these inversions can form their own systematic progression. This creates a meta-level network where the nodes are inversion operations and the arrows indicate transposition relationships between the inversional indices.

# Mathematical Formulation
For inversion operations I_n and I_m:
- T_k(I_n) = I_{n+k} (transposition of inversional index)
- A progression I_a -> I_b -> I_c has characteristic intervals int(a,b) and int(b,c)

Network structure (from Prokofieff analysis, Figure 10.9a):
- Nodes: I_G, I_{F#/G}, I_{F#}
- Arrows: T_11 (semitone descent)
- The family S of operands = inversion operations
- SGP = group of left-multiplications-by-transposition-operations

# Musical Context/Application
Progressions of inversion centers reveal how composers systematically deploy inversional relationships. The progression itself often relates to surface motivic content, creating connections between local melodic gestures and large-scale harmonic organization.

# Examples
In the Prokofieff Melodies op.35 analysis (Figures 10.8-10.9):

**Progression of centers:**
1. I_G (inversion about G): relates expected Eb major to actual E minor at first cadence
2. I_{F#/G} (inversion about F#-and-G): relates expected Eb major to actual Eb minor at second cadence
3. I_{F#} (inversion about F#): relates Eb minor to D major and C major to F minor in measure 6

**The descent G -> F#/G -> F#:**
"The structuring power of that F# is then developed as shown by the next vertical arrows... F# becomes a new center of inversion."

**Motivic connection:**
The T_11 arrows connecting the inversion centers (descent by semitone) are isographic to the melodic motive G-F#-F in the accompaniment of measure 1. Thus the incipit motive generates both:
- Surface melodic content
- Deep-level organization of inversional centers

Lewin notes: "The structural centrality of F# between F and G in this arrangement recalls the melodic position of F# in measure 1, where it mediated between a preceding melodic G and a subsequent melodic F."

# Common Confusions
The progression of inversion centers operates on a different hierarchical level than the inversion operations themselves. Students should distinguish between:
1. The pitch-class sets being inverted
2. The inversion operations applied
3. The transpositional relationships between the inversional indices
