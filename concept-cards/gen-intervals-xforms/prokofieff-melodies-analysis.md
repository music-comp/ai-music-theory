# Quick Definition
Lewin's analysis of Prokofieff's Melodies op.35, no.1 demonstrates how pitch-class inversion operations with progressing centers can organize triadic substitutions and harmonic progressions in early 20th-century diatonic music.

# Formal Definition
This analysis applies pitch-class set theory and inversion operations to explain unexpected harmonic substitutions. Where traditional Klang analysis proves inadequate due to parallel voice-leading and unclear functional relationships, pitch-class inversions with specific, structurally significant centers reveal deeper organizational principles.

# Mathematical Formulation
The inversional structure (Figure 10.8b):
- I_G: Inversion about G relates expected Eb major to heard E minor (measure 2.5)
- I_{F#/G}: Inversion about F#-and-G relates expected Eb major to heard Eb minor (measure 5.5)
- I_{F#}: Inversion about F# relates Eb minor to D major, and C major to F minor (measure 6)

Network of inversion centers (Figure 10.9a):
- I_G -> I_{F#/G} -> I_{F#} with T_11 arrows
- T_11(I_G) = I_{F#/G} and T_11(I_{F#/G}) = I_{F#}

# Musical Context/Application
The analysis shows how chromatic pitch relations (particularly G and F#) established melodically in the opening measure generate the inversional centers that organize subsequent harmonic substitutions. The progression of inversional centers (G -> F#/G -> F#) develops the chromatic motive while providing coherent explanation for "strange" harmonies.

# Examples
From Figure 10.7 and 10.8:

**The harmonic problem:** At cadences in measures 2.5 and 5.5, we expect Eb major but hear E minor and Eb minor respectively. These substitutions are "far from traditional" yet create coherent structure.

**Klang analysis limitations (Figure 10.8a):**
SLIDE transformation relates Eb minor to D major, paralleling Eb major to E minor. But the proportion among triads in measure 6 "seems hard to portray by Klang-relations that mesh with our aural intuitions."

**Pitch-class inversion analysis (Figure 10.8b):**
- First cadence: I_G(Eb major) = E minor. "The pitch class G is a plausible center of inversion; it is highly accented by its boundary functions within the opening melodic phrase."
- Second cadence: I_{F#/G}(Eb major) = Eb minor. "This inversion... develops the chromatic relation of the pitch classes G and F#."
- Measure 6: I_{F#} relates Eb minor/D major and C major/F minor simultaneously.

**Motivic connection (Figure 10.9):**
The network of inversion operations (I_G -> I_{F#/G} -> I_{F#}) with T_11 arrows is isographic to the accompaniment figure G-F#-F in measure 1, the melodic incipit motive.

# Common Confusions
Students may attempt to force all triadic analysis into Klang (functional) terms. This analysis shows that pitch-class set operations can provide clearer insight when parallel voice-leading and non-functional progressions obscure Klang relations. The inversional approach complements rather than replaces functional analysis.
