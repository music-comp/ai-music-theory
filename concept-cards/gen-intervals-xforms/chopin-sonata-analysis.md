# Quick Definition
Lewin's analysis of Chopin's Bb-minor Sonata, first movement, demonstrates how a formally valid intervallic relation (same interval class for two dyads) can become perceptually meaningful through musical development, illustrating GIS methodology.

# Formal Definition
The analysis examines four motives (a)-(d) and their rhythmic and intervallic relations. The key claim: dyad x from motive (a) and dyad y from motive (b) belong to the same interval class. This relation is initially inaudible but becomes perceptually significant after the repeat.

# Mathematical Formulation
**Motives (Figure 4.6):**
- (a): Grave opening motive (augmented rhythmically to fit Doppio Movimento)
- (b): First theme motive
- (c): Bridge motive (from end of bridge)
- (d): Second theme motive

**Dyads:**
- x: first dyad of (a)
- y: first dyad of (b)
- z: recurring dyad in second group

Claim: x and y share the same interval class.

**Rhythmic chain:**
(b) -> (c): augment by 2, then augment last two notes by 2
(c) -> (d): augment by 2, exchange first two note durations
(d) -> (a bis): augment by 2

Thus (b)-(c)-(d)-(a) forms a consistent rhythmic expansion chain.

# Musical Context/Application
The analysis supports the methodological claim that formal relations may become perceptually meaningful through musical development:

1. First hearing of (b): the x-y relation is obscure
2. Chain (b)-(c)-(d)-(a): rhythmic expansion makes (b) and (a) related
3. After repeat: (a bis) to (b bis) reveals (b) as transformation of (a)
4. x-y relation now audible as "ironically scurrying transformation"

The analysis also involves:
- The Db cadence at end of exposition
- Hearing Fb (as third of Db) vs. E natural at repeat
- The dyad z mediating between x and y

# Examples
**Rhythmic transformation:**
(a) in augmented notation matches (d)'s rhythm approximately
(b) is rhythmic generator; (a) is endpoint of expansion

**Tonal interpretation:**
- x = root + minor third of Db (if heard as Fb)
- y = minor third + root of Bb
- Both dyads begin on Db

**Contextual hearing:**
First time: E natural (Beethoven op. 111 association)
Second time: Fb (Db major cadence context)

This reinterpretation helps the x-y interval-class relation become audible.

# Common Confusions
1. **The analysis is methodological:** It illustrates how perception develops, not just what the formal relations are.

2. **Enharmonic interpretation matters:** Hearing Fb vs. E natural changes how x relates to Db harmony.

3. **Repeat is essential:** The analysis assumes the repeat goes to (a), making the chain (b)-(c)-(d)-(a bis) audible.

4. **Not immediate perception:** The x-y relation is NOT claimed to be obvious. It becomes meaningful through the piece's development.
