# Quick Definition
Lewin proposes as an exercise developing analogous dual simply transitive groups on the hexatonic collection S = {C, C#, E, F, G#, A}, extending the octatonic GIS methodology to a six-note symmetric collection.

# Formal Definition
The hexatonic collection (alternating minor thirds and semitones) supports analogous group-theoretic structures to the octatonic collection. The exercise invites developing two simply transitive groups on this six-element set that form a dual pair, similar to STRANS1 and STRANS2 on the octatonic.

# Mathematical Formulation
Hexatonic collection:
- S = {C, C#, E, F, G#, A}
- |S| = 6 pitch classes
- Structure: two augmented triads {C, E, G#} and {C#, F, A}

Expected structure:
- Standard operations preserving S: T_0, T_4, T_8, and three inversions
- Should yield groups of order 6
- Dual groups would form GIS structures analogous to GIS1 and GIS2

Properties to develop:
- Identify which T and I operations preserve S
- Determine which operations form a simply transitive group
- Find the dual group of commuting operations
- Construct the corresponding GIS structures

# Musical Context/Application
The hexatonic collection is important in late-Romantic and post-tonal music, particularly in the work of composers like Liszt and Richard Strauss. Developing GIS structures on the hexatonic would provide analytical tools for this repertoire analogous to the octatonic tools developed in Appendix B.

# Examples
**The exercise statement:**
"As an exercise, the reader may consider a new family of pitch classes, S = (C, C#, E, F, G#, A), and develop on the new S two analogous simply transitive groups of operations."

**Structural analogy:**
Like the octatonic (two interlocking diminished-seventh chords), the hexatonic comprises two interlocking augmented triads:
- {C, E, G#}
- {C#, F, A}

Operations that preserve hexatonic:
- T_0, T_4, T_8 (transpositions by major thirds)
- Three inversions that swap the augmented triads

**Expected features:**
The dual groups on hexatonic should exhibit:
- Simple transitivity (unique operation between any two elements)
- Commutation (each group's elements commute with all of the other's)
- GIS structures with one group as transpositions, other as interval-preserving

# Common Confusions
The hexatonic has order 6, not 8, so the group structures will be different from the octatonic case. Students should not simply copy the octatonic structure but work through the group theory for the specific collection. The groups will likely be of order 6 (not 8) and may have different properties.
