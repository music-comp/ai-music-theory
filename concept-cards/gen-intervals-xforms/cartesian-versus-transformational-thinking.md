# Quick Definition
Two contrasting conceptual frameworks for understanding musical space: Cartesian thinking conceives of music as points in external space measured by an observer, while transformational thinking conceives of music as gestures performed by someone inside the musical experience.

# Formal Definition
Cartesian thinking:
- Conceives primary musical objects as atomic individual "elements"
- Positions the theorist as an external observer
- Models "interval from s to t" as extension in geometric space
- Draws on the image of distances between holes in a flute or points on a stretched string

Transformational thinking:
- Conceives musical objects as contextually articulated phenomena (sets, series, etc.)
- Positions the theorist inside the music as idealized performer
- Models transformation from s to t as a characteristic gesture
- Does not require an external observer

# Mathematical Formulation
Both frameworks may use identical formal apparatus:
- int(s, t) = i (Cartesian: measured distance)
- T_i(s) = t (Transformational: performed gesture)

The mathematical equivalence established by the simply transitive group theorem shows these are formally interchangeable, though conceptually distinct.

# Musical Context/Application
Historical music theory has been predominantly Cartesian, projecting intuitions onto geometric space (the "line" of the stretched string). The transformational attitude offers an alternative that may better capture kinetic and processual aspects of musical experience - the sense of performing music rather than merely observing it.

# Examples
Consider a singer moving from C to G:
- Cartesian: The singer traverses a distance of a fifth in pitch space
- Transformational: The singer performs the gesture of "ascending a fifth"

Both describe the same event but from different experiential positions.

# Common Confusions
- These are not competing mathematical theories but different conceptual framings
- Cartesian thinking is not inferior; it captures aspects of musical experience that may be less accessible to transformational thinking
- The book does not abandon Cartesian structures but shows how they can be subsumed within a more general transformational framework
