# Quick Definition
The melodic component of the Fate motive from Wagner's Die Walkure, functioning as both a 3-note series and an intervallic network with structure 2 = (-1) + 3 semitones, originally spanning A to B.

# Core Definition
"FATE functions both as a 3-note series and as an intervallic network, 2 = (-1) + 3 semitones. The interval 2 of that network is originally spanned from A to B, and the thematic pitch-idea of 'A to B' thereby goes along with the prime form of the FATE motive/series/network" (Lewin, 8.2.6, p. 184). Two forms of FATE embed the A-to-B gesture: A-C-B (bass) and A-G#-B (melody, the prime form).

# Prerequisites
- **RICH transformation** — FATE forms are linked by RICH in the analysis

# Key Properties
1. Intervallic network: 2 = (-1) + 3
2. Prime form: A-G#-B (melody, mm. 1-2)
3. Alternative form: A-C-B (bass, embedding A-to-B via different route)
4. TCH interval = 2 = int(A, B)
5. Related to LOVE motive through shared A-to-B gesture and interval 3

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Identify the 3-note melodic cell with intervals (-1) and (+3)
2. Verify overall span of 2 semitones
## To Recognize:
1. Three-note motive with semitone descent followed by minor-third ascent (or equivalent)

# Context & Application
FATE is related to the LOVE motive fragment: LOVE projects A-to-B with pickup F#, defining ambitus F#-to-B. FATE projects A-to-B with G# pickup 3 below B. The interval 3 connects both motivic fragments (Figure 8.7).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating FATE as a fixed pitch sequence rather than a series/network
  **Correction**: FATE functions as both series and intervallic network; the network structure is analytically primary

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Conflating FATE with the DEATH motive
  **Clarification**: FATE is the melodic component; DEATH is the broader motivic complex
