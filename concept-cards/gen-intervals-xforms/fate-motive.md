# Quick Definition
The melodic component of the Fate motive from Wagner's Todesverkuendigung, functioning as a 3-note series with intervallic network 2 = (-1) + 3 semitones, spanning the essential A-to-B gesture.

# Formal Definition
FATE is defined (Figure 8.6, mm. 1-2) as:
- A 3-note series (e.g., A-G#-B in prime form)
- Intervallic structure: 2 = (-1) + 3 semitones
- The interval 2 spans from A to B, the "thematic pitch-idea"
- Functions both as serial motive and intervallic network

# Mathematical Formulation
FATE as intervallic network (Figure 8.7b):
- Three nodes connected by arrows
- Arrow labels: -1 (semitone down), 3 (minor third up)
- Overall span: 2 = (-1) + 3

Two FATE forms embed A-to-B:
- A-C-B (bass form): int(A, C) = 3, int(C, B) = -1
- A-G#-B (melody form): int(A, G#) = -1, int(G#, B) = 3

Both have TCH interval = int(A, B) = 2.

# Musical Context/Application
The FATE motive emerges from the preceding LOVE material through:
1. Shared A-B gesture with pickup elaboration
2. Harmonic matrix projecting pitch-class FATE (A-C-B) in LOVE harmonization
3. Intervallic relationship (Figure 8.7) showing common 3-arrow and 2-arrow

The interval 2 as TCH interval connects the motive's internal structure to the large-scale sequence scheme of the scene.

# Examples
From the Todesverkuendigung:
- Prime melodic form: A-G#-B (mm. 1-2)
- Bass form: A-C-B (derived from LOVE harmonization)
- Both forms launch independent RI-chains
- TCH sequences proceed at interval 2 in both voices

# Common Confusions
- FATE is an intervallic network AND a serial motive, functioning in both capacities
- The two FATE forms (A-C-B and A-G#-B) are different series but share the A-B span
- FATE differs from FATE' (which appears at mm. 56ff with different intervals)
- The -1 and 3 subintervals can be arranged in either order depending on the form
