# Quick Definition
A Klang transformation where the input Klang becomes the mediant of its output, changing mode in the process: (C, +)MED = (A, -) and (C, -)MED = (Ab, +).

# Core Definition
"MED: (p, sign) becomes the mediant of its MED-transform. For example, (C, +)MED = (A, -), and (C, -)MED = (Ab, +). If we are at (C, +) on a graph and follow an arrow labeled MED, we arrive at (C, +)MED = (A, -)" (Lewin, 8.1.1, p. 176). MED changes both pitch class and mode. Crucially, DOM = MED^2: "given any Klang, if it becomes the mediant Klang of a second Klang which in turn becomes the mediant Klang of a third Klang, then the first Klang is the dominant of the third Klang" (8.1.2, p. 178).

# Prerequisites
- **Klang representation** — MED operates on Klangs

# Key Properties
1. (p, +)MED = (p + 9, -) mod 12 [major to relative minor, down a minor third]
2. (p, -)MED = (p + 8, +) mod 12 [minor to relative major, down a major third]
3. MED changes mode
4. DOM = MED^2 (two applications of MED equal one DOM)
5. SUBM = MED^{-1}
6. The group generated by DOM and MED equals the group of powers of MED alone
7. STRANS generated by MED is simply transitive on Klangs derivable from a given Klang

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. For major Klang (p, +): move root down a minor third, change to minor
2. For minor Klang (p, -): move root down a major third, change to major
## To Recognize:
1. A Klang arrow that changes mode and moves the root
2. The input is the mediant of the output

# Context & Application
Since DOM = MED^2, the group STRANS generated by DOM and MED is generated by MED alone. This group is simply transitive on the relevant family of Klangs, so networks using only MED powers "can be considered 'formally intervallic'" (p. 179). However, MED^7 applied to a minor Klang does NOT yield the parallel major (it yields (Cb, +) not (C, +)), so MED^7 is not PAR as a general operation (p. 179).

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 176): (C, +)MED = (A, -): C major becomes mediant of A minor. (C, -)MED = (Ab, +): C minor becomes mediant of Ab major.

**Example 2** (p. 178): DOM = MED^2: ((C, +)MED)MED = (A, -)MED = (F, +) = (C, +)DOM. Also: ((C, -)MED)MED = (Ab, +)MED = (F, -) = (C, -)DOM.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Klang representation** — MED is defined on Klangs
## Enables
- **Non-intervallic transformations** — Networks mixing MED with PAR are non-intervallic
## Related
- **DOM transformation** — DOM = MED^2
## Contrasts With
- **REL transformation** — REL and MED agree on major Klangs but differ on minor: (C, -)REL = (Eb, +) but (C, -)MED = (Ab, +)
- **SUBM transformation** — SUBM = MED^{-1}

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming MED and REL are the same operation
  **Correction**: They agree on major Klangs ((C, +)MED = (C, +)REL = (A, -)) but differ on minor Klangs

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking MED^7 = PAR
  **Clarification**: MED^7 on (C, +) yields (C, -), same as PAR; but MED^7 on (C, -) yields (Cb, +), not (C, +). They are different operations.
