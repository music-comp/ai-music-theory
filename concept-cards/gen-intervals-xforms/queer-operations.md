# Quick Definition
The queer operations Q3 and Q9 are STRANS2 operations that rotate the two diminished-seventh chords within the octatonic collection in opposite directions, creating transformations with no analogue in standard twelve-tone theory.

# Formal Definition
Q3 and Q9 are operations on the octatonic set S that act differently on the two constituent diminished-seventh chords. Q3 rotates one diminished-seventh chord "forward" (by minor third) while simultaneously rotating the other "backward" (by major sixth, equivalent to minor third in the opposite direction). Q9 is the inverse of Q3.

# Mathematical Formulation
Q3 operation:
- On dim7 chord {C, D#, F#, A}: C -> D#, D# -> F#, F# -> A, A -> C (forward rotation)
- On dim7 chord {C#, E, G, A#}: C# -> A#, A# -> G, G -> E, E -> C# (backward rotation)

Q9 operation (inverse of Q3):
- On dim7 chord {C, D#, F#, A}: C -> A, A -> F#, F# -> D#, D# -> C (backward rotation)
- On dim7 chord {C#, E, G, A#}: C# -> E, E -> G, G -> A#, A# -> C# (forward rotation)

Properties:
- Q3 * Q9 = Q9 * Q3 = RO (identity)
- Q3 * Q3 = R6 (or Q9 * Q9 = R6)
- Q3 and Q9 commute with all members of STRANS1

# Musical Context/Application
The queer operations reveal symmetries in the octatonic collection that are invisible from the standard T/I perspective. They demonstrate that octatonic space supports transformations fundamentally different from those available in twelve-tone chromatic space.

# Examples
**Q3 definition:**
"Q3 rotates each of the diminished-seventh chords within S, but in opposite directions; it maps C to D#, D# to F#, F# to A, A to C, and also C# to A# (not to E), A# to G, G to E, and E to C#."

**Q9 as inverse:**
"Q9 is the inverse operation to Q3; it maps C to A,..., D# to C, and also C# to E,..., and A# to C#."

**Contrast with rotation:**
Unlike R3 (which would map both diminished-seventh chords in the same direction), Q3 maps them in opposite directions. This is why it is called "queer" - it has no analogue among standard operations.

**Role in STRANS2:**
Q3 and Q9 are two of the eight operations in STRANS2:
- STRANS2 = {RO, Q3, R6, Q9, X1, X2, X4, X5}
- They help make STRANS2 simply transitive while being different from STRANS1

# Common Confusions
Q3 is not the same as R3 (T_3 restricted to octatonic). R3 rotates both diminished-seventh chords in the same direction; Q3 rotates them in opposite directions. The "queer" designation indicates this unusual, non-standard behavior that has no twelve-tone analogue.
