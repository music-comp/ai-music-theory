# Quick Definition
The interval of 3 semitones (minor third) that begins as a secondary phenomenon within Motive Y's F-Ab span and progressively proliferates to become a dominant structural element controlling chromatic events, bass-line structure, and the Tristan-chord sequence in Debussy's "Reflets dans l'eau."

# Core Definition
The 3-semitone interval begins as the complement of Y's Ab-to-F span, which "appeared as the difference between the T'-related Ab-to-Eb and the T^{-1}-related F-to-Eb." It assumes greater prominence as the temporal boundary of Z1 (supported by CHR's 3-semitone rise), then is verticalized in harmony (Figure 10.16), generates structural Cb in the bass, and is "fully unleashed" in the Tristan-chord sequence at mm. 20-21 (Lewin, Section 10.4, pp. 270-281).

# Key Properties
1. Begins as secondary (within Y's F-Ab span)
2. Becomes temporal boundary for Z1
3. Verticalized in harmony (Figure 10.16)
4. Generates structural Cb in bass
5. "Fully unleashed" in Tristan-chord 3-semitone sequence
6. D natural and Cb are generated by the 3-interval expanding from F-Ab

# Context & Application
The proliferation of the 3-interval demonstrates how a secondary intervallic relationship can grow to dominate a composition's chromatic structure. The trajectory from F-Ab through Cb to D natural and the Tristan sequence is a masterful compositional development.

# Examples

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating the minor third as structurally important from the start
  **Correction**: It begins as secondary and only progressively achieves structural dominance

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Equating interval-3 with a triadic relationship
  **Clarification**: The 3-interval here functions as a generative chromatic element, not a triadic component
