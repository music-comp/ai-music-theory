# Quick Definition
The three-semitone interval (minor third) emerges as a central structural element in "Reflets dans l'eau," beginning as a secondary phenomenon within motive Y (F-Ab) and gradually proliferating to generate bass notes, chromatic sequences, and ultimately the climactic Tristan-chord cadenza.

# Formal Definition
The interval 3 (three semitones, minor third) develops from an initially subsidiary interval to a primary structural generator through progressive compositional deployment. The interval connects melodic, harmonic, and sequential domains, demonstrating how a local motivic element can achieve large-scale structural significance.

# Mathematical Formulation
Origin and proliferation:
- Initially: Ab-F within Y (complement of T^(-1)T' interval)
- Melodic: temporal boundary of Z1 (F to Ab)
- Vertical: soprano-bass coupling at pitch-class interval 3
- Generative: produces structural Cb in bass
- Sequential: Tristan-chord transposition by T_3

Formal relationships:
- int(F, Ab) = 3 (or -9 mod 12)
- The interval appears horizontally, vertically, and as sequential distance

# Musical Context/Application
The three-semitone interval's gradual emergence demonstrates compositional economy and organic development. What begins as a derived interval (the difference between T' and T^(-1) relationships) becomes a primary constructive element that generates new pitch material and governs large-scale sequential structure.

# Examples
**Origin in motive Y:**
"The interval began as a secondary phenomenon; its complement spanned Ab-to-F within Y, which appeared as the difference between the T'-related Ab-to-Eb and the T^(-1)-related F-to-Eb."

**Increasing prominence (measures 9-10):**
"F-to-Ab then assumed greater prominence in the melody as the temporal boundary for Z1, supported by the concomitant 3-semitone rise of CHR in the accompaniment."

**Verticalization (Figure 10.16):**
"Figure 10.16 shows how the melodic F-to-Ab is verticalized in the harmony, and how the interplay of horizontal and vertical 3-intervals next generates a structural Cb in the bass. That Cb is the first 'middleground' chromaticism of the piece; it will take on formidable proportions hereafter."

**Bass generation:**
The product network (Figure 10.16) shows how J(Y) = Ab-Cb-Db in the bass results from the 3-interval coupling with I(Y) = F-Ab-Bb in the soprano.

**Measure 18 expansion:**
"Just so, the same Ab-F now generates D natural, the minor third below F" through T_(-3)(Y) = F-D-C.

**Unleashing (measures 20-21):**
"Now that D natural, as well as Cb, is on the scene along with F and Ab, the minor third or 3-semitone interval can be fully unleashed; the sequence at hand unleashes it."

The Tristan-chord cadenza sequences by 3 semitones, "a sequence which Wagner artfully only suggests in the first-act Prelude."

# Common Confusions
The three-semitone interval is not simply any minor third but specifically the F-Ab interval and its derivatives in this piece. Its structural significance derives from its motivic origin and progressive deployment, not from abstract interval-class properties. Students should trace its specific compositional path.
