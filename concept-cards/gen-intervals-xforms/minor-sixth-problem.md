# Quick Definition
The minor sixth (ratio 8:5) poses recurring difficulties for theorists attempting to derive all intervals from purely harmonic space, as it falls outside the senario and requires eighth-partial relationships. Both Zarlino and Hindemith encounter this interval as problematic.

# Core Definition
The minor sixth consistently resists derivation from systems limited to six harmonic elements. Zarlino must adjoin 8 to the senario, falsely claiming 6 is a "harmonic mean" between 8 and 5. Hindemith must generate Ab1 outside his target octave and transfer it up. Lewin argues that a modal explanation (the minor sixth as fourth-plus-minor-third in a minor mode) would resolve both difficulties, suggesting the interval's "minor" character is fundamentally modal rather than harmonic (Lewin, Appendix A, pp. 247-250).

# Prerequisites
- **Senario** — The system whose limitation creates the problem
- **Multiple Musical Spaces** — The problem reveals the need to integrate harmonic and melodic spaces

# Key Properties
1. Ratio 8:5 requires the number 8, outside the senario
2. Zarlino's false claim: 6 is a "harmonic mean" between 8 and 5
3. Hindemith generates Ab outside target octave, transfers it in
4. Modal solution: fourth + minor third in a minor mode (e.g., A3-D4-F4 in Dorian)
5. The problem is structural, not accidental — it recurs across centuries of theory

# Context & Application
Lewin argues that the minor sixth's "minor" character is modal: F4-D5 in D-Dorian has "minor" modal character despite its large size, while E4-C5 in C-major has "major" modal character despite its small size. Without Stufen theory to make such discriminations, theorists are forced into problematic purely harmonic derivations.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 248): Zarlino's attempt: "He has to adjoin the number 8 to the senario... he even claims that 6 is a 'harmonic mean term' between 8 and 5; this is simply false."

**Example 2** (p. 250): Hindemith's parallel: "The one pitch with which Hindemith has trouble is Ab2... his troubles resemble Zarlino's troubles with the minor sixth."

**Example 3** (p. 248): Modal solution: G3-(C4)-E4 in Ionian yields major sixth; A3-(D4)-F4 in Dorian yields minor sixth — explaining both sixths through modal context.
