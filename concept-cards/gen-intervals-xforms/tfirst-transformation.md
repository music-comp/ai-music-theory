# Quick Definition
A serial transformation that transposes a series by its first interval; its inverse TFIRST^(-1) transposes by the complement of the first interval, making the first note of the original series become the second note of the transformed series.

# Formal Definition
TFIRST operates on a series s:
- TFIRST transposes s by its first interval (the interval from first to second element)
- TFIRST^(-1) transposes s by the complement/inverse of the first interval
- Effect of TFIRST^(-1): The first note of s becomes the second note of TFIRST^(-1)(s)

# Mathematical Formulation
For series s with first interval i = int(s_1, s_2):
- TFIRST(s) = T_i(s)
- TFIRST^(-1)(s) = T_{-i}(s)

TFIRST^(-1) and TLAST duality:
- TFIRST^(-1) makes first note become second
- TLAST makes last note become next-to-last
- These are dual operations on opposite ends of the series

# Musical Context/Application
TFIRST and its inverse, along with TLAST, reveal structural relationships in serial music that would be obscured by using specific transposition numbers. The transformations capture how series beginnings and endings relate across forms.

# Examples
From Webern op. 5, no. 4 (Figure 8.10):
- TFIRST^(-1) connects two forms of FLYAWAY
- Together with TLAST, creates a network where the Ab form is "central"
- The chronological order of forms in the music differs from the network's visual layout
- The Ab form ends the piece, matching its cadential "centrality" in the network

# Common Confusions
- TFIRST and TFIRST^(-1) are different transformations
- The transformations depend on series structure, not on fixed transposition values
- "Dual" to TLAST means complementary structural role, not mathematical duality
- Using specific interval numbers (T_5, T_2) would hide the structural relationships
