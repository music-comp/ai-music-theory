# Quick Definition
The octatonic pitch-class set S = {C, C#, D#, E, F#, G, A, A#} is an eight-note collection with special properties that allow construction of non-commutative GIS structures and dual simply transitive groups.

# Formal Definition
The octatonic collection is a set S of eight pitch classes comprising two interlocking diminished-seventh chords. This collection supports two distinct simply transitive groups of operations (STRANS1 and STRANS2), each of which can serve as the transposition operations for a GIS structure. The dual structure arises because STRANS2 consists exactly of the operations that commute with all members of STRANS1, and vice versa.

# Mathematical Formulation
Octatonic collection S:
- S = {C, C#, D#, E, F#, G, A, A#}
- Two diminished-seventh chords: {C, D#, F#, A} and {C#, E, G, A#}
- |S| = 8 pitch classes

Standard operations preserving S:
- Transpositions: T_0, T_3, T_6, T_9 (by minor thirds)
- Inversions: I_5, I_E (I_11), I_8, I_A# (I_10) where subscript indicates index

These eight operations form a group isomorphic to D_4 (dihedral group of order 8).

# Musical Context/Application
The octatonic collection is fundamental to the music of Bartok, Stravinsky, Messiaen, and many other twentieth-century composers. Its symmetric structure (alternating whole and half steps) creates unique transformational possibilities that differ from twelve-tone chromatic space.

# Examples
**Definition from text:**
"Let S be the octatonic family of pitch classes comprising C, C#, D#, E, F#, G, A, and A#."

**Operations preserving S:**
"Eight of the standard 'atonal' operations on the twelve pitch-classes transform S into itself; these operations are T_0, T_3, T_6, T_9, I_5, I_E, I_8, and I_A#."

**Diminished-seventh structure:**
The octatonic divides into two diminished-seventh chords:
- Dim7 #1: {C, D#, F#, A}
- Dim7 #2: {C#, E, G, A#}

Operations either preserve each diminished-seventh chord (rotations) or swap between them (K, L, M, N operations).

**Uniqueness property:**
The operations that preserve S form a simply transitive group: "Given members s and t of S, there is a unique OP, among the eight cited operations on S, satisfying OP(s) = t."

# Common Confusions
The octatonic collection is not the same as the diminished scale, though they contain the same pitch classes. The GIS structures on the octatonic use different transformation groups than the standard chromatic GIS. Students should not assume that familiar twelve-tone operations have the same meaning when restricted to octatonic space.
