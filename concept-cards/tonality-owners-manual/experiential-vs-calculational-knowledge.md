# Quick Definition
Two fundamentally different modes of musical understanding: experiential knowledge is embedded in direct perceptual experience (feeling tension "in the chord itself"), while calculational knowledge derives musical facts through conscious reasoning from other information.

# Formal Definition
Experiential knowledge involves direct sensitivity to musical properties where the perception precedes conceptual identification -- one may know a chord is a dominant before identifying its component pitches. Calculational knowledge involves deriving musical properties from other perceptual data through inference, as when an absolute-pitch listener counts semitones to identify an interval. The two modes may produce identical behavioral results (correct identification) while differing fundamentally in phenomenological character.

# Musical Context
This distinction bears on the entire enterprise of music theory. Style-dependent expectations and responses (e.g., a dominant seventh feeling tense in classical music but stable in blues) appear embedded in perceptual experience rather than generated by calculation. The challenge for theory is to "stretch our ears while remaining realistic about human limitation" (p. 406).

# Examples
- Perceiving a dominant seventh chord's "dominantness" as immediate and forceful, connected to style-dependent expectations, versus calculating chord identity from recognized pitches (p. 403)
- Feeling the sense of return in a sonata recapitulation versus knowing intellectually that the tonic key has returned (p. 404)
- Theory changing experience: understanding the 3 + 2 echo structure in Brothomstates' "In" genuinely alters the listening experience (pp. 404-406)

# Common Confusions
- It can be "genuinely difficult to know whether we are thinking something or experiencing it directly" (p. 406)
- Confusion between these modes may explain why composers "wasted so much energy manipulating inaudible pitch-relationships" (p. 406)
- Theory can sometimes enrich experience, moving knowledge from calculational to experiential -- but this does not always occur
