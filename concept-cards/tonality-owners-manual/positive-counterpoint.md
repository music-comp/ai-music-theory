# Quick Definition
An approach to counterpoint that gives musicians affirmative goals -- a set of plausible options for what to do next -- rather than a negative approach built on prohibitions against parallels and awkward leaps.

# Formal Definition
Positive counterpoint formulates rules of thumb based on the configurational perspective: if you are on a perfect consonance, move to a nearby imperfect consonance on the graph of diatonic consonances (Figure P3.4); if you are on an imperfect consonance, move about half the time to another imperfect consonance via parallel or contrary motion, and otherwise to a nearby perfect consonance. This captures the intuitive knowledge of a composer who knows what to do next: "if I am located at this point in musical space, then my most obvious destinations are..."

# Musical Context
In Palestrina's masses, about 90% of fifths move to sixths or thirds, while about 80% of unisons move to thirds. Positive counterpoint explains these patterns not as the result of prohibitions but as natural consequences of moving short distances in the space of diatonic consonances. The approach becomes increasingly important as additional voices bring more contrapuntal options. It aims to complement the "negative counterpoint" of traditional pedagogy with an understanding of musical affordances and possibilities.

# Common Confusions
- Positive counterpoint does not replace traditional rules; it supplements them with a goal-oriented perspective
- The approach works with the configurational (Tinctorian) coordinate system: "fifths often move to sixths" means voices configured as a fifth often rearrange to a sixth
