# Quick Definition

An approach to counterpoint that gives musicians affirmative goals -- a set of plausible options for what to do next ("if I am here, my destinations are...") -- rather than the negative approach built on prohibitions against parallels and awkward leaps.

# Core Definition

Positive counterpoint formulates rules of thumb based on the configurational perspective: if on a perfect consonance, move to a nearby imperfect consonance on the graph of diatonic consonances; if on an imperfect consonance, move about half the time to another imperfect consonance via parallel or contrary motion, and otherwise to a nearby perfect consonance (Prelude to Ch. 3, pp. 90-91). The approach captures "the intuitive knowledge of a composer who knows what to do next" by providing concrete destinations rather than prohibitions. Charles Rosen "was right that pretty much anyone can follow rules, avoiding forbidden parallels and awkward leaps... But at the same time genuine contrapuntal understanding can be quite difficult to obtain" (Prelude to Ch. 4, p. 154).

# Key Properties

1. Focuses on possibilities and affordances rather than rules and prohibitions
2. Uses the configurational perspective (graph of consonances) to identify destinations
3. Perfect consonances typically move to nearby imperfect consonances
4. Imperfect consonances move to other imperfect consonances or nearby perfect consonances
5. Statistical support: ~90% of Palestrina's fifths move to sixths or thirds
6. OUCH theory is a four-voice embodiment of this approach

# Construction / Recognition

## To Apply:
1. Identify current vertical interval/configuration
2. Consult the graph of diatonic consonances for nearby destinations
3. Choose from the available short-distance options
4. This naturally produces idiomatic counterpoint

# Context & Application

In Palestrina's masses, about 90% of fifths move to sixths or thirds, while about 80% of unisons move to thirds (Prelude to Ch. 3). These patterns arise not from prohibitions but from the natural tendency to move short distances in the space of consonances. OUCH theory (Section 6) embodies positive counterpoint for four-voice music, providing "a positive set of options to pursue" (p. 128). The Pathetique prelude demonstrates "embodied mathematics, drawing musical consequences from a small number of postulates" -- genuine contrapuntal understanding that goes beyond rule-following (p. 154).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating positive counterpoint as a replacement for traditional rules
  **Correction**: It supplements prohibitions with goal-oriented thinking, not replaces them

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking contrapuntal understanding = knowing the rules
  **Clarification**: Following rules is "not after all a very difficult craft" (Rosen); genuine understanding involves knowing what possibilities the space affords
