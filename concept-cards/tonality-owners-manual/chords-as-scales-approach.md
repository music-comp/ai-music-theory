# Quick Definition
An analytical framework that models accompanimental textures as melodies moving along small, shifting chord-based scales rather than as concrete chorale-like voices.

# Formal Definition
The chords-as-scales approach allows us to model a richer musical ontology in which arpeggiated accompaniments are treated as nonspecific "accompanimental stuff" that augments concrete soprano and bass voices. Rather than verticalizing an arpeggio into specific chorale voices, we postulate a concrete bass and soprano, with the latter articulated by the top of an arpeggio that moves along a shifting triadic alphabet. This background is not localized to any specific register and is substantially immune to standard contrapuntal strictures.

# Musical Context
Eighteenth-century musicians recognized a class of musical objects qualitatively different from chorale-like arrangements of pitches. Figured-bass notation reflects this thinking, combining concrete soprano and bass with abstract harmonies. The chords-as-scales approach formalizes this intuition, providing a way to analyze accompaniment patterns without forcing them into voice-leading paradigms that produce analytical contradictions.

# Common Confusions
- This approach does not abandon analytical rigor; it simply recognizes that not all musical texture functions as concrete polyphony. The extreme notes of an arpeggiation pattern tend to be treated as belonging to concrete voices when they form the outer voices of a texture.
- Weber (1817-1821) explicitly argued that arpeggiated lines are not actual parts but only "imagined" parts, and hence are less strictly bound by the rule against parallel fifths.
