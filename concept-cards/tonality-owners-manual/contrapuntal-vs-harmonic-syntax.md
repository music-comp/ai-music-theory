# Quick Definition

Western classical music combines two different syntaxes: a contrapuntal syntax regulating dissonance treatment and a harmonic syntax governing genuine chord progressions. The interaction of these two systems creates much of the analytical ambiguity in tonal music.

# Core Definition

Western classical music combines a contrapuntal syntax regulating dissonance and a harmonic syntax governing the progression of "genuine harmonies" (p. 304). The contrapuntal system does not require distinguishing genuine consonances from those produced by nonharmonic embellishment -- it governs all dissonances. The harmonic system, which developed after the contrapuntal system stabilized, governs only consonances qualifying as genuine, requiring the analyst to distinguish "law-abiding harmonies from the merely 'apparent' chords that are the byproduct of nonharmonic motion" (p. 304). Composers' tendency to make simultaneous nonharmonic tones mutually consonant inevitably produces "apparent chords" that do not participate in harmonic syntax (p. 304).

# Key Properties

1. Two separate syntaxes operating simultaneously
2. Contrapuntal syntax: regulates all dissonances, no harmony/nonharmony distinction needed
3. Harmonic syntax: governs only "genuine" harmonies, requires distinguishing real from apparent chords
4. Harmonic system developed after contrapuntal system stabilized
5. Composers making nonharmonic tones consonant creates "apparent chords"
6. The interaction creates the central analytical ambiguity of tonal music

# Construction / Recognition

## To Navigate the Dual Syntax:
1. Apply contrapuntal rules to identify nonharmonic tones
2. Remove nonharmonic tones to reveal harmonic progressions
3. Verify the resulting progression obeys harmonic syntax
4. Be aware that some consonances may be "apparent" rather than "genuine"

# Context & Application

This dual syntax explains why harmonic analysis is so difficult. The contrapuntal system predates the harmonic system historically. As harmonic syntax developed, it was layered on top of existing contrapuntal constraints. The tension between the two systems is a central theme of both Chapter 5 and the Prelude to Chapter 7.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating every consonance as a genuine harmony
  **Correction**: Some consonances are "apparent chords" produced by nonharmonic motion and do not participate in harmonic syntax

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking contrapuntal and harmonic syntax operate independently
  **Clarification**: They interact -- resolving their interaction is the central challenge of harmonic analysis
