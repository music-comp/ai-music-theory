# Quick Definition
A notation system for describing how scales transform into other scales, using **T** for chromatic transposition and t for scalar transposition, combined with subscripts indicating the size of each motion.

# Formal Definition
Scalar voice leading notation describes the motion of a scale (or chord) within a larger collection using two components: **T**_n (bold T with subscript) represents chromatic transposition by n semitones, while t_n represents scalar transposition by n scale steps. The combination **T**_n t_m describes the net effect: for diatonic scales in chromatic space, the basic voice leading is **T**_{-7}t_4 (or **T**_5t_{-3}), meaning a chromatic transposition of -7 semitones combined with 4 scale-degree rotations. This notation captures both the pitch-class displacement and the scale-degree reassignment in a single expression. Each step on the scalar spiral diagram corresponds to one application of the basic voice leading.

# Musical Context
This notation is essential for analyzing modulatory passages at the scalar level. It allows precise description of how abstract voices (melodic slots, modeled by fingers at the piano) change both their pitch-class assignments and their scale-degree roles during modulation. The notation applies identically to chords within scales and scales within the chromatic aggregate.

# Common Confusions
- **T** (bold) and t (non-bold) represent fundamentally different kinds of motion: **T** moves through the chromatic aggregate while t rotates within the collection. Both are needed to fully describe a scalar voice leading.
- The subscripts are not root intervals: **T**_{-7} does not mean "down a fifth" but "down seven semitones in chromatic space," which happens to correspond to a fifth.
