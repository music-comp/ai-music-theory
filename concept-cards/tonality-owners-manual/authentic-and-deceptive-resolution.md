# Quick Definition

Two ways the descending basic voice leading embeds in triadic harmony: landing on root-and-third of the destination triad (descending fifth, "authentic") or on third-and-fifth (ascending step or descending third, "deceptive"). A passing tone in the melodic third creates a resolving tritone linking contrapuntal and harmonic logic.

# Core Definition

In the descending basic voice leading, the initial dyad is almost always root and third of a triad. The destination dyad can either be root/third of the next triad (producing a descending-fifth root progression, the "authentic" resolution) or third/fifth (producing alternative root motions, the "deceptive" resolution) (Figure 3.1.7, p. 100). The ascending basic voice leading embeds into a wider range of progressions; in both cases, "a passing tone can create a resolving tritone" (p. 100). In the ascending form, this yields 6-7-1 against 4-3, "combining plagal root motion with an authentic resolution of the tritone" (p. 100). In baroque music this figure is often harmonized as ii-vii^6 or IV-vii^64 over a stable bass; later practice supports the voice leading with a changing bass, reflecting "a shift from a dyadic-contrapuntal conception of functionality... to a later and more triadic orientation" (p. 101).

# Key Properties

1. Initial dyad is almost always root-third of a triad
2. Authentic: destination = root-third of next triad (descending fifth root motion)
3. Deceptive: destination = third-fifth of next triad (alternative root motion)
4. Passing tone in melodic third creates resolving tritone
5. Ascending form: 6-7-1 against 4-3 (plagal root + authentic tritone resolution)
6. Historical shift from stable-bass to changing-bass harmonization

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify:
1. Find two voices moving by the basic voice leading (root down by third, third up by step)
2. Check whether the destination dyad is root-third (authentic) or third-fifth (deceptive) of the harmony
3. Look for passing tones creating tritone resolution

# Context & Application

This dual embedding explains why the same contrapuntal move produces different harmonic results. It links the contrapuntal and harmonic dimensions of functional tonality: the tritone resolution connects the dyadic system to harmonic function. The historical shift from stable-bass to changing-bass harmonization reflects the broader evolution from modal counterpoint to functional harmony.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Equating "authentic/deceptive" here with cadence types only
  **Correction**: These terms describe how dyadic voice leading embeds in triadic harmony at any point, not just at cadences

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the ascending basic voice leading has only one embedding
  **Clarification**: It can appear as root-third or third-fifth of the initial triad, giving four possible positions (p. 100)
