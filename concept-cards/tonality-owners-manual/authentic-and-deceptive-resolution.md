# Quick Definition
Two ways the descending basic voice leading of diatonic thirds embeds into triadic harmony: the authentic resolution lands on root-and-third of the destination triad (descending fifth), while the deceptive resolution lands on third-and-fifth (ascending step or descending third).

# Formal Definition
In the descending basic voice leading, the initial dyad is almost always root and third of a triad. The destination dyad can either be root/third of the next triad (producing a descending-fifth root progression, the "authentic" resolution) or third/fifth (producing alternative root motions, the "deceptive" resolution). The ascending basic voice leading embeds into a wider range of progressions. A passing tone inserted into the melodic third creates a resolving tritone: in the descending form, ^7-^1 against ^4-^3; in the ascending form, ^6-^7-^1 against ^4-^3, combining plagal root motion with authentic tritone resolution.

# Musical Context
This dual embedding explains why the same contrapuntal move can produce different harmonic results. The descending form with passing tritone becomes the standard V-I cadence; the ascending form with passing tritone (^6-^7-^1 against ^4-^3) is frequently harmonized in baroque music as ii-vii^6 or IV-vii^64 over a stable bass, or with a changing bass in later practice. The contrast between these two approaches reflects a historical shift from dyadic-contrapuntal to triadic-harmonic conceptions of function.

# Common Confusions
- "Authentic" and "deceptive" here refer to how dyadic voice leading embeds in triadic harmony, not just to cadence types
- The ascending basic voice leading can appear as either root-third or third-fifth of the initial triad, giving four possible positions
