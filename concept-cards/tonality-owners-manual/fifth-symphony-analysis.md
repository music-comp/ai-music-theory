# Quick Definition

Tymoczko's analysis of the Fifth Symphony first movement revealing covert sequential structures, the Ludwig schema in transition and coda, chordal-interval patterns derived from the opening motive, and a development section built as a grand contrary-motion "up-and-down-the-ladder" structure.

# Core Definition

The movement opens with "an obsessive focus on tonic and dominant" for thirty-eight measures, "offset by rhythmic variety, the piece exploring many different four-measure arrangements of tonic and dominant" (p. 483). The opening motive articulates a chordal-interval pattern (0, 0, -1, -1) that "becomes increasingly explicit over the course of the movement, as if Beethoven were gradually revealing the underlying structure" (p. 483). The transition is an "accelerating double sentence" whose Ludwig "recalls the Pathetique's allegro opening" (p. 484). The second theme is "a long ascending-step near sequence" using the disguised model technique (p. 486). The development is a sixty-five-measure "up-and-down-the-ladder," ascending by fifth (F-C-G) through grand contrary-motion gestures with a six-octave descent (C7 to C1) against ascending bass, then descending (G-C-F) (pp. 488-490). The coda "feels somewhat beyond analysis," replacing schematic cleverness with "raw compositional force" (p. 493).

# Prerequisites

- **The Ludwig** -- The primary schematic device in the transition and coda
- **Disguised model technique** -- Used in the second theme's chain of continuity
- **Contrary-motion counterpoint** -- The development's grand contrary-motion structure

# Key Properties

1. Covert rather than overt sequences distinguish the Fifth from the Tempest
2. Chordal-interval pattern (0, 0, -1, -1) permeates the first phrase and recurs throughout
3. The second theme's disguised model creates chainlike continuity across three sequential units
4. The development forms a symmetric up-and-down-the-ladder ascending and descending by fifth
5. Dyadic logic augments Roman-numeral analysis throughout
6. The coda substitutes gestural force for schematic cleverness

# Construction / Recognition

## To Follow Tymoczko's Analysis:
1. Trace the (0, 0, -1, -1) chordal-interval pattern from the opening through the movement
2. Identify the accelerating double sentence in the transition (mm. 26-44)
3. Map the disguised-model ascending-step sequence in the second theme (mm. 71-94)
4. Chart the development's ascending-fifth then descending-fifth structure
5. Note where the coda abandons schematic content for gestural force

# Context & Application

The Fifth Symphony is "in many ways cousin to the Tempest: largely sequential, with a middle-register minor tonic" (p. 482). But where the Tempest's sequences are obvious, the Fifth's are "covert and disguised." Tymoczko's aim is to show that the movement's power derives from "unusually long paragraphs formed from entirely schematic material" (p. 488), not from divine inspiration or compositional genius, but rather "musical thought" (p. 488).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Taking the "Knock of Fate" as primarily a motif rather than a chordal-interval pattern
  **Correction**: The (0, 0, -1, -1) pattern permeates the entire first phrase and recurs throughout

- **Error**: Accepting Schenker's development reductions at face value
  **Correction**: They are "arbitrary, motivated less by internal musical logic than by an external mandate to force the music to confirm to preconceived reductional templates" (p. 491)

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the development's grand structure is about motivic unity
  **Clarification**: It is an up-and-down-the-ladder structure using contrary-motion sequences, where dyadic logic augments Roman-numeral analysis

- **Confusion**: Thinking the coda's power requires analytical explanation
  **Clarification**: It "feels somewhat beyond analysis" -- raw force replaces schematic content, marking where Beethoven's energy exceeds classical idiom
