# Quick Definition
Tymoczko's analysis of the Fifth Symphony first movement revealing covert sequential structures, the Ludwig schema in transition and coda, chordal-interval patterns derived from the opening motive, and a development section built as a grand contrary-motion "up-and-down-the-ladder" structure.

# Formal Definition
The movement opens with obsessive tonic-dominant focus for thirty-eight measures, offset by rhythmic variety exploring many four-measure arrangements of I and V. The opening motive articulates a systematic pattern of chordal intervals (0, 0, -1, -1) traced throughout the first phrase and gradually made more explicit. The transition features an "accelerating double sentence" with Ludwig patterns recalling the Pathetique. The second theme is an ascending-step near sequence with disguised model. The development ascends by fifth (F-C-G) through grand contrary-motion gestures and descends (G-C-F), forming an "up-and-down-the-ladder" structure. The coda substitutes raw gestural force for schematic cleverness.

# Musical Context
The Fifth Symphony is cousin to the Tempest -- largely sequential, with middle-register minor tonic -- but with covert rather than overt sequences. Its unprecedented formal tautness has invited analysis of "organic unity," but Tymoczko finds the deeper interest in showing how schematic material generates the movement's power. The coda "feels somewhat beyond analysis," marking where Beethoven's energy exceeds classical idiom.

# Common Confusions
- The famous "Knock of Fate" is not just a motif but articulates a chordal-interval pattern that permeates the entire first phrase
- The development's grand contrary-motion structure has six-octave descent (C7 to C1) against ascending bass -- dyadic logic augments Roman-numeral analysis
- Schenker's reductions of the development are "arbitrary, motivated less by internal musical logic than by an external mandate to force the music to confirm to preconceived reductional templates" (p. 491)
