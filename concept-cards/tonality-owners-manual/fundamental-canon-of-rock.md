# Quick Definition
A continuously descending canonic line embedded in the ubiquitous four-chord rock sequence, described as a "deep schema" existing in the musical unconscious and manifesting in many seemingly unrelated ways.

# Formal Definition
The fundamental canon of rock is the hidden descending canon implied by the four-chord progression D-A-E-G (or any of its rotations). Because all four maximal loop families are rotations of this single progression, and because each complete loop transposes voices down by one chordal step, the same canonic line underlies "Helpless," "Natural Woman," "The Air Near My Fingers," and countless other songs. It is a *deep schema*, rarely grasped explicitly but manifesting in the characteristic voice-leading patterns of rock harmony.

# Musical Context
This canon unifies the seemingly disparate loop families of rock harmony into a single underlying structure. Three songs in three different keys can be played simultaneously without dissonance, creating "polytonality without dissonance" where the sense of key depends on where one feels the hypermetric accent. The canon is not articulated by specific instruments but constructed by listeners connecting common tones and stepwise motions across chord changes.

# Common Confusions
- The "fundamental canon" is described "only half-jokingly" -- it is a theoretical construct, not a claim that all rock songs literally derive from one canon
- The canon is implicit, not typically heard explicitly by listeners or intended by composers
- Different loop families emphasize different portions of the same underlying descending line
