# Quick Definition

Replacing a chord with one whose root is a major third away, exploiting the near-symmetry of the triad so that each voice moves by at most one semitone -- formalized as t_1 **T**_{-4} or t_{-1} **T**_4.

# Core Definition

Because the major triad "divides the octave into three almost-even parts" (p. 52), "any members of this collection can be related by applying major-third substitution (t_1 **T**_{-4} or t_{-1} **T**_4) to their constituent chords; because of the triad's near symmetry, this major-third substitution has only a small effect on the voices" (p. 53). On the spiral diagram, major-third-related triads occupy the same angular position at different radial distances, so substitution corresponds to radial motion.

# Prerequisites

- **Principle of Musical Approximation** -- The substitution exploits near-symmetry
- **LP and PL Voice Leading** -- LP/PL are the specific voice leadings for major-third substitution

# Key Properties

1. Replaces a triad with one a major third (4 semitones) away
2. Moves each voice by at most one semitone
3. Corresponds to radial motion on the spiral diagram
4. Major-third-related triads share the same angular position
5. Works for any nearly even chord, not just major triads
6. The closer to symmetry, the smaller the voice-leading disruption

# Construction / Recognition

## To Apply:
1. Take any major triad
2. Replace it with the major triad a major third above (LP) or below (PL)
3. Verify that voices move by small intervals (at most one semitone each)
4. The resulting progression preserves the overall voice-leading direction

# Context & Application

Major-third substitution generates families of related progressions in both classical chromaticism (Gesualdo, Schubert) and rock harmony. Any descending sequence of major triads can be rewritten by substituting major-third-related chords.

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 53, Figure 2.1.7): C-B-Bb becomes C-Eb-Gb or C-G-D through major-third substitution.

**Example 2**: Rock progressions using bIII (e.g., I-bIII-bVII-IV) exploit major-third substitution of V.

# Relationships

## Builds Upon
- **Principle of Musical Approximation** -- Near-symmetry makes the substitution smooth
- **LP and PL Voice Leading** -- The specific voice leadings involved

## Contrasts With
- **Tritone Substitution** -- Major-third substitution replaces by M3 (4 semitones); tritone by TT (6 semitones)

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Thinking major-third substitution only works for major triads
  **Correction**: It works for any nearly even chord; the closer to symmetry, the smoother

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Confusing with tritone substitution
  **Clarification**: Different intervals (M3 vs. TT) and different voice-leading mechanisms
