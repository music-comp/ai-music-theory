# Quick Definition

Two approaches to modulation: scale-first modulation exploits scalar proximity (moving between closely related key areas by efficient scalar voice leading), while chord-first modulation exploits the voice-leading properties of chords themselves (using chordal subsets that are chromatically close).

# Core Definition

Tymoczko distinguishes "scale-first" from "chord-first" modulations, first developed in *A Geometry of Music* for twentieth-century music but applicable to earlier styles as well. Scale-first modulations "exploit scalar proximity, moving by fifth between closely related key areas for a very smooth modulatory effect" (p. 375). Chord-first modulations "exploit the voice-leading properties of *chords themselves*" (p. 375) -- the modulation proceeds because specific chordal subsets are chromatically close (e.g., F-sharp--A--C-sharp is close to G--A--C-sharp in A^7^).

Chopin's D major prelude illustrates both: the initial key changes use scale-first modulation (ascending fifths through closely related keys), while the phrases end with chord-first modulations (exploiting chromatic proximity of specific chord subsets).

# Prerequisites

- **Key distance (pitch-class-changing)** -- The framework for measuring scalar proximity
- **Enharmonic equivalence** -- Chord-first modulations often involve enharmonic reinterpretation

# Key Properties

1. Scale-first: keys are close because their scales share many notes
2. Chord-first: keys are connected because specific chords contain chromatically proximate subsets
3. The two mechanisms are not mutually exclusive -- they often appear in the same piece
4. Scale-first modulations tend to be smoother; chord-first tend to be more dramatic
5. In the paired-spiral-diagram model, scale-first modulation moves the scalar spiral while chord-first modulation emphasizes the chordal spiral

# Construction / Recognition

## To Distinguish Scale-First from Chord-First:
1. Check whether the new key is related by efficient scalar voice leading to the old (scale-first)
2. Check whether a specific chord provides a pivot through chromatic proximity of subsets (chord-first)
3. Scale-first: the modulation exploits the relationship between *collections*
4. Chord-first: the modulation exploits the relationship between *specific chords*

# Context & Application

The distinction is musically significant because it captures the difference between smooth diatonic modulation (common in the classical era) and more dramatic chromatic modulation (increasingly common in the nineteenth century). In paired spiral diagrams, scale-first modulation keeps the chords moving while the keys change; chord-first modulation lets the chords lead and pulls scales along behind them.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating all modulations as one type
  **Correction**: Identifying the mechanism (scalar proximity vs. chordal proximity) is analytically productive

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking scale-first and chord-first are mutually exclusive
  **Clarification**: A single piece (like Chopin's D major prelude) routinely uses both mechanisms
