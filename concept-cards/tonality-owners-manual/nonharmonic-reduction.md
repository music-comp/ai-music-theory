# Quick Definition

The removal of nonharmonic tones to reveal an underlying "harmonic skeleton" of chord tones -- the simplest form of analytical reduction, analogous to parsing a sentence into component clauses. Most appropriate for strict styles with clear policies on harmonic content and melodic embellishment.

# Core Definition

Nonharmonic reduction is "a kind of syntactic analysis analogous to the parsing of a sentence into its component clauses" (p. 203). "By dividing music into 'structural' harmonies decorated by nonharmonic tones, we clarify its organizational logic, reducing a complex surface to a small number of stereotypical harmonies decorated in a small number of stereotypical ways" (p. 203). It reveals "the rules of the game that is musical composition: rules for constructing consonances, rules for decorating them with dissonances, and rules governing the progression of consonances themselves" (p. 204). It "makes more sense for Palestrina and Bach than for Debussy or Sonic Youth" (p. 204). Even in strict styles, nonharmonic reduction "sometimes fails, either because there is no way to eliminate the purportedly nonharmonic notes, or because those notes seem to have genuinely harmonic functions" (p. 204).

# Key Properties

1. Simplest form of analytical reduction
2. Analogous to syntactic parsing of a sentence
3. Removes nonharmonic tones to reveal harmonic skeleton
4. Primarily a pedagogical tool
5. Most successful in strict styles (Palestrina, Bach)
6. Even in strict styles, interpretive choices arise
7. Fundamentally interpretive, producing "partially true models" (p. 204)

# Construction / Recognition

## To Perform:
1. Identify prevailing harmony at each point
2. Classify dissonant notes by type (passing, neighbor, suspension, etc.)
3. Replace each nonharmonic tone with the chord tone it embellishes
4. Verify the resulting skeleton produces acceptable progressions

# Context & Application

Nonharmonic reduction is "in the first instance, a pedagogical tool for helping students come to grips with earlier music in all its intimidating complexity" (p. 204). It is "most appropriate when genres have strict policies regarding harmonic content and melodic embellishment" (p. 204). Even simple cases can be ambiguous: Figure P5.1 (p. 204) shows a Bach passage admitting both intervallic and chordal readings. Tymoczko prefers to "imagine the music as suspended between these two poles" (p. 204).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating nonharmonic reduction as always mechanical and objective
  **Correction**: Even in strict styles, ambiguities arise; the process is "fundamentally interpretive"

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Assuming one analysis must be right and another wrong
  **Clarification**: Music can be "suspended between two poles"; even simple reduction produces "partially true models of complex and ambiguous musical surfaces" (p. 204)
