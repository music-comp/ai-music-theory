# Quick Definition

A general model of musical repetition combining three elements: a "cell" or "unit" of melodic lines, transpositions applied at each repeat, and a permutation (or "twist") moving lines among voices. Sequences are fundamental transformational processes, not just harmonic patterns.

# Core Definition

"A general model of repetition should therefore combine three elements, a 'cell' or 'unit' consisting in a set of melodic lines to be repeated, a collection of transpositions applied at each repeat, and a permutation or 'twist' moving the lines among the available voices" (p. 155). The model uses Taneyev arrows forming a stable structure. "A central discovery of the Renaissance was that voice leadings could be repeated, chained together algorithmically to convert an isolated event into an ongoing process" (p. 155). The framework reveals "deep connections between the seemingly distinct domains of repetition, canon, and voice leading" (p. 162). "The theory of voice leading and the theory of sequence are one and the same" (p. 163).

# Key Properties

1. Three elements: cell, transpositions, permutation
2. Unifies sequences, rounds, and transposing rounds under one model
3. Taneyev arrows form a stable repeating structure
4. Theory of voice leading = theory of sequence
5. Every voice leading generates a sequence; every sequence determines a voice leading
6. Renaissance discovery: voice leadings can be chained algorithmically

# Construction / Recognition

## To Apply the Model:
1. Identify the cell (minimal repeating block of melodic lines)
2. Determine the transpositions applied at each repetition
3. Determine the permutation (which voice gets which material)
4. Draw Taneyev arrows labeled with transpositions

# Context & Application

The framework encompasses "Renaissance disguised repetition, evolving into the overtly repetitive sequences of the baroque, which embed repeating contrapuntal patterns into larger musical blocks. Nineteenth-century composers make increasing use of contrary-motion 'wedge' sequences... paving the way for the very flexible transformations of modern music" (p. 155). The model resolves longstanding difficulties in identifying sequential units by distinguishing simple from grand periods.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Thinking sequences are purely harmonic phenomena
  **Correction**: They are transformational processes combining transposition and permutation

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Treating sequences and canons as separate phenomena
  **Clarification**: Canon and sequence are deeply connected; canonic structure arises naturally from the permutation component
