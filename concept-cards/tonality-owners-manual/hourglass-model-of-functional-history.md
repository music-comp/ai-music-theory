# Quick Definition
A metaphor describing how the harmonic vocabulary of functional tonality first tightens (from baroque through classical) and then loosens (through the nineteenth century), with Haydn, Mozart, and Beethoven representing the point of maximal harmonic regimentation.

# Formal Definition
The functional tradition resembles an hourglass in its historical trajectory of permissible progressions. Early functional music (baroque) has a relatively wide harmonic vocabulary including vi--V, nonsequential iii, and other penumbral progressions. During the classical period, harmonic practice congeals to its most restricted state -- the narrowest point of the hourglass. In the nineteenth century, the vocabulary expands again, recovering iii and introducing ct^o7^, ii^ø7^, and other chromatic enrichments (Figure 7.1.7). This model captures the paradox that some progressions common in Bach are rare in Mozart but reappear in Brahms.

# Musical Context
The hourglass model explains why applying a single, rigid harmonic grammar to all periods of functional tonality produces inaccurate results. It also illuminates the relationship between functional tonality and the broader history of Western harmony, showing functionality not as a monolithic system but as a historically evolving practice.

# Examples
- Figure 7.1.7: Salient major-mode differences between earlier and later functional dialects, including the trajectories of vi--V, iii, ct^o7^, and ii^ø7^.
- In rock, harmonic vocabulary depends on mode in ways that do not apply to classical functionality: VI--VII--i is common in aeolian but rare in ionian.
- Functional tonality exhibits an unusual degree of modal symmetry compared to other styles, using virtually the same repertoire of progressions in major and minor.

# Common Confusions
- The hourglass does not imply that classical music is "better" or "purer" -- it simply describes a historical pattern of restriction and expansion.
- The minor mode retains a greater attraction to the mediant throughout all periods, which is a pre-existing modal tendency that was never fully abandoned, not a deviation from an established default.
