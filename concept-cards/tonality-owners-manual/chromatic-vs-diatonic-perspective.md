# Quick Definition

Two competing perspectives on harmonic analysis: a diatonic view that postulates chords moving within diatonic scales (which are themselves moving), versus a chromatic view that models voice leadings directly on chromatic triads without reference to diatonic collections.

# Core Definition

Tymoczko contrasts two analytical approaches using Beethoven's Ninth Symphony scherzo (mm. 143--176, Figure P8.1). The diatonic approach postulates "a nested pair of repeating contrapuntal patterns, triads moving inside diatonic scales that are themselves moving" (p. 357). This hierarchical analysis simplifies melodic description: diatonically, the fifth of every triad ascends by *scale step*; chromatically, one must say major and minor triads move differently. The neo-Riemannian alternative models the passage as two nonhierarchical chromatic transformations (R and L) operating directly on chromatic triads.

From this analysis, "scale degrees and pitch classes are fundamentally analogous" (p. 359) -- not ontologically different but occupying different levels of a potentially nested hierarchy where "any collection's output [is] available to serve as the input of the next" (p. 360).

# Key Properties

1. Diatonic analysis: hierarchically nested transformations at chord and scale levels
2. Chromatic analysis: flat, nonhierarchical transformations on chromatic triads
3. The diatonic approach uses standard harmonic procedures and offers unified explanations
4. The chromatic approach connects to nineteenth-century chromaticism
5. Scale degrees and pitch classes are fundamentally analogous -- both label positions in collections
6. Musical objects can participate in complex hierarchies, with any collection's output serving as input to the next

# Construction / Recognition

## To Compare the Two Perspectives:
1. Model the passage with chords moving within diatonic scales (diatonic perspective)
2. Model the same passage with chromatic voice-leading transformations (chromatic perspective)
3. Compare: which provides simpler description? Which generalizes more readily?
4. Consider whether the passage uses hierarchically nested patterns (suggesting diatonic analysis) or nonhierarchical alternation (suggesting chromatic analysis)

# Context & Application

The prelude highlights "Beethoven's position at the fulcrum between classical and Romantic eras" (p. 359). The diatonic approach connects his procedures to standard harmonic analysis; the chromatic approach connects them to later nineteenth-century chromaticism. Neither analysis is simply "correct" -- they highlight different aspects.

The deeper insight is that the flat model (scales mapping scale degrees directly to pitch classes) should be replaced by a hierarchical model (collections nested within larger collections), generalizing from modulation to subchordal voice leading.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming one must choose exclusively between diatonic and chromatic analysis
  **Correction**: Both perspectives illuminate different aspects; the best analysis may integrate both

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the diatonic perspective requires a Pythagorean (non-tempered) universe
  **Clarification**: In a fixed twelve-note chromatic universe, the diatonic scale is simply a subset; hierarchical nesting works perfectly well in equal temperament
