# Quick Definition
A galant schema that alternates rightward and leftward steps on the dyadic circle, tonicizing a pair of fifth-related harmonies with ascending and descending basic voice leadings.

# Formal Definition
On the 2-in-7 spiral diagram, the Quiescenza alternates one-step clockwise and one-step counterclockwise motions, oscillating between adjacent points. This juxtaposes ascending and descending basic voice leadings, creating a pattern that tonicizes two fifth-related harmonies. The schema is one of the common functional patterns that arise from short-distance motions in the space of imperfect consonances, alongside the converging cadence, Fonte, and Monte.

# Musical Context
The Quiescenza is one of the schemas catalogued by Robert Gjerdingen in *Music in the Galant Style*. It typically appears over a pedal bass, alternating between tonic and dominant harmonies. Tymoczko's analysis reveals that, like other galant schemas, it arises not from arbitrary convention but from the basic affordances of the diatonic dyad space -- the fact that oscillating between adjacent points on the circle naturally produces fifth-related tonicizations.

# Common Confusions
- The Quiescenza is not just a harmonic formula; its underlying logic involves the oscillation of two-voice counterpoint on the dyadic circle
- The alternating motion distinguishes it from the Fonte (consistently clockwise) and Monte (alternating clockwise and three-step counterclockwise)
