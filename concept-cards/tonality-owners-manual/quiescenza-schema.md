# Quick Definition

A galant schema that alternates rightward and leftward steps on the dyadic circle, oscillating between adjacent points to tonicize a pair of fifth-related harmonies with ascending and descending basic voice leadings.

# Core Definition

On the 2-in-7 spiral diagram, the Quiescenza alternates one-step clockwise and one-step counterclockwise motions, oscillating between adjacent points (Figure 3.1.10, p. 103). This juxtaposes ascending and descending basic voice leadings, creating a pattern that tonicizes two fifth-related harmonies. It is one of the short-distance paths on the dyadic circle, alongside the converging cadence (single CW step), Fonte (series of CW steps), and Monte (alternating CW with three-step CCW). Tymoczko also identifies a common I-IV-V-I pattern (not identified as schematic by Gjerdingen) that similarly juxtaposes rightward and leftward motions.

# Prerequisites

# Key Properties

1. Alternates clockwise and counterclockwise single steps
2. Oscillates between adjacent points on the dyadic circle
3. Tonicizes a pair of fifth-related harmonies
4. Typically appears over a pedal bass
5. Arises from geometric affordances, not arbitrary convention

# Construction / Recognition

## To Construct:
1. Start on any point of the dyadic circle
2. Move one step clockwise (descending basic voice leading)
3. Return one step counterclockwise (ascending basic voice leading)
4. Repeat the oscillation

## To Recognize:
1. Look for oscillating two-voice patterns alternating ascending and descending motion
2. The harmonies should alternate between two fifth-related chords
3. Often found over a pedal bass

# Context & Application

The Quiescenza is one of the schemas catalogued by Gjerdingen in *Music in the Galant Style*. Tymoczko's analysis reveals that it arises not from convention but from "the basic affordances of musical space" -- oscillating between adjacent points on the circle naturally produces fifth-related tonicizations (p. 103).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating the Quiescenza as purely harmonic (I-V-I)
  **Correction**: Its logic involves oscillation of two-voice counterpoint on the dyadic circle

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Confusing Quiescenza with Fonte or Monte
  **Clarification**: Quiescenza oscillates (CW then CCW); Fonte moves consistently CW; Monte alternates CW with three-step CCW
