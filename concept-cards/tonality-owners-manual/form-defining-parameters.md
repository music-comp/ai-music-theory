# Quick Definition
Beethoven's innovative use of basic musical parameters -- attack density, register, dynamics, and registral separation -- to demarcate and shape large-scale formal sections, creating unprecedented contrasts that define form through parametric trajectories rather than primarily through tonal or thematic convention.

# Formal Definition
Where earlier classical composers used moderate variation in basic parameters, Beethoven employed them in extreme and form-defining ways. Attack density differentiates formal zones (e.g., first theme moving from eighths to sixteenths, second theme accelerating from half notes to trills). Register creates form-defining arches linking formal sections. Registral separation expands to the edges of the piano at structurally significant moments. These parameters supplement and sometimes supplant the conventional markers of sonata form (medial caesura, cadential articulation).

# Musical Context
This concept explains Beethoven's formal innovations as operating on a different aesthetic plane than his predecessors. Hepokoski and Darcy's sonata theory centers on the "medial caesura" as a formal marker, but Beethoven frequently avoids or repurposes this gesture. The danger is interpreting Beethoven as more beholden to convention than he actually was -- he is "simultaneously eccentric and archetypal" (p. 453).

# Examples

# Common Confusions
- Form-defining parameters do not replace tonal structure but add an additional layer of formal articulation
- Beethoven's avoidance of the medial caesura does not mean he is "failing" at sonata form -- it reflects a different aesthetic orientation
- The concept applies to parameters often ignored in traditional analysis (rhythm, dynamics, register) alongside conventional harmonic analysis
