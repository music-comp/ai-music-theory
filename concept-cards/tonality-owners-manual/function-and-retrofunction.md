# Quick Definition

Functional harmony (descending fifths, T-S-D-T) and retrofunctional harmony (ascending fifths, descending melodic steps) are two complementary systems often coexisting within a single piece -- rock's retrofunctional norm is "an intuitive counterknowledge arising from the deep structure of musical space" (p. 57).

# Core Definition

The gameboard on the spiral diagram "embodies a retrofunctional norm" (p. 57) because starting with descending melodic steps and diatonic proximity produces expectations "both different from those appropriate to classical music, and consistent with what we find in the repertoire" (p. 57). "The rules of functional harmony are precisely not the rules you would come up with if you were to construct a musical syntax on your own" (p. 57). Rock musicians juxtapose "distinct harmonic subsystems, one functional and one modal, each with its own characteristic repertoire of harmonic moves" (p. 78). Rameau's four-chord functional archetype (d-G-C-F) is "the retrograde" of rock's ascending-fifth pattern (C-G-D-A) -- "three fifths and a minor third, ascending in rock and descending in functional harmony" (p. 68).

# Key Properties

1. Functional: descending fifths, T-S-D-T archetype, culturally sanctioned
2. Retrofunctional: ascending fifths, descending steps, intuitive counterknowledge
3. Both systems coexist in rock songs (code switching)
4. Retrofunctional norms arise from deep musical space, not deliberate opposition
5. Root-position triads in rock reveal retrofunctional logic; classical inversions obscure it
6. Rameau's d-G-C-F is the exact retrograde of rock's C-G-D-A

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify:
1. Check root-progression direction: ascending fifths/thirds = retrofunctional; descending fifths = functional
2. Note whether the melody descends stepwise (suggests retrofunction) or follows tonal patterns (suggests function)
3. Check for V-I cadences (functional) vs. IV-I returns (retrofunctional)
4. Look for code switching between sections (e.g., retrofunctional verses, functional choruses)

# Context & Application

Songs like "Sympathy for the Devil" and "With a Little Help from My Friends" juxtapose modal (retrofunctional) verses with functional dominants in choruses. In "With a Little Help," the ABAB alternation between function and retrofunction is "highlighted by the vocals, with Ringo Starr taking the modal unit and the background vocals singing the functional unit" (p. 79).

# Examples

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Analyzing rock retrofunctional progressions as "incorrect" classical function
  **Correction**: Rock's II chord is "a thing-in-itself, largely unknown in classical harmony and not reducible to its categories" (p. 59)

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking retrofunction is deliberate rebellion against classical norms
  **Clarification**: Rock harmony is "more intuitive than classical harmony"; the oppositional quality is "more byproduct than motivation" (footnote 13)
