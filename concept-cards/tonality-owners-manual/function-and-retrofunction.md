# Quick Definition
Functional harmony (descending fifths, T-S-D-T) and retrofunctional harmony (ascending fifths, descending melodic steps) are two complementary harmonic systems that often coexist within a single piece of music.

# Formal Definition
Functional harmony uses "strong" root progressions (descending fifths, ascending major thirds) organized around a T(onic)-S(ubdominant)-D(ominant)-T(onic) archetype. Retrofunctional harmony inverts these norms, favoring ascending fifths, ascending minor thirds, and descending steps -- progressions that arise naturally from the deep structure of musical space when melody takes the lead. Tymoczko describes retrofunction as "an intuitive counterknowledge arising from the deep structure of musical space, a subterranean musical practice distinct from, and to some extent opposed to, the culturally sanctioned routines of the notated tradition."

# Musical Context
Rock music and modal music of the Renaissance both exhibit retrofunctional tendencies, because in both styles functional-harmonic constraints are comparatively weak. The coexistence of function and retrofunction in rock has been called "code switching" (Cohn's term from linguistics). Songs like "Sympathy for the Devil" and "With a Little Help from My Friends" alternate between modal (retrofunctional) and functional sections. Rameau's four-chord functional archetype (d-G-C-F) is the exact retrograde of rock's ascending-fifth pattern (C-G-D-A).

# Examples
- Beatles' "With a Little Help from My Friends": verse alternates ABAB between function (ii-V^7-I) and retrofunction (I-V-ii), with Ringo singing the modal parts and background vocals the functional parts (Figure 2.9.1)
- Rolling Stones' "Sympathy for the Devil": mixolydian I-bVII-IV verses (retrofunctional) with V^7-I chorus (functional), Figure 2.4.1
- Thomas Morley's "April Is in My Mistress' Face" (1596): opens functionally, ends with retrofunctional descending 6-5 sequence (Figures 2.9.2-2.9.3)
- Rameau's *double emploi*: four descending fifths d-G-C-F, the retrograde of rock's ascending-fifth norm

# Common Confusions
- Retrofunctional does not mean "anti-functional" or deliberately oppositional -- rock musicians make affirmative use of deep musical relationships, not conscious rebellion
- "In rock music the harmonies tend to go backwards, except when they don't" -- this sounds untestable but reflects genuine polyvalence between distinct harmonic subsystems
- The classical rules of functional harmony are precisely *not* what an untrained musician would discover independently -- retrofunction is the more intuitive system
