# Quick Definition
Chopin's use of a single melodic strategy -- stepwise descending voice leading -- to generate a range of harmonic effects spanning diatonic modality, functional harmony, and chromaticism, demonstrating the independence of melody and harmony as heterogeneous forces.

# Formal Definition
Chopin deploys descending stepwise voice leading in three harmonic environments: diatonic triads (producing modal-sounding progressions), functional harmony (conforming to standard chord grammar), and chromatic seventh chords (producing nonfunctional chromaticism). The A minor prelude, for example, features a background of third-related triads connected by stepwise root descent (e-G-b-D-(F)-a), progressing to seventh chords linked by semitonal voice leading. The same contrapuntal logic -- short clockwise motions in spiral diagrams -- operates across different chordal and scalar environments, illustrating the Prime Directive's principle that similar voice-leading operations produce different results in different collectional contexts.

# Musical Context
These analyses demonstrate that melody and harmony are heterogeneous, independent forces combined in various ways. The A minor Mazurka Op. 7, no. 2 opens with strikingly modal diatonic triads before proceeding to nonfunctional chromatic sevenths. The Revolutionary Etude features ascending surface voices within a descending harmonic field, a technique shared with the Waldstein opening. Chopin shows "explicit awareness of the Prime Directive" (p. 610).

# Examples
- Chopin's first etude, Op. 10, no. 1: stepwise descending voice leading compatible with functional harmony (Figure 9.3.1, p. 420)
- A minor prelude: diatonic triads connected by stepwise descent progressing to chromatic sevenths (Figures 9.3.2-9.3.4, pp. 420-422)
- A minor Mazurka, Op. 7, no. 2: modal opening progressing to chromatic sevenths (Figure 9.3.5, p. 423)
- Revolutionary Etude: Schenker and Tymoczko both identify chromatic 6-5 sequence, but Tymoczko continues stepwise descent through following dominant sevenths (Figures 9.3.8-9.3.9, pp. 423-424)

# Common Confusions
- Chopin's chromaticism is not "beyond" functional explanation -- it manifests a contrapuntal logic that can generate functional effects alongside nonfunctional ones
- Schenker's claim that "all conventional concepts fail" for the Revolutionary Etude reflects his holistic conviction that prolongational analysis replaces traditional theory; Tymoczko places the two forms of explanation on different conceptual levels
- The same melodic strategy in different scalar/chordal environments produces the full range from diatonic modality to chromaticism
