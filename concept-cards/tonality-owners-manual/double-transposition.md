# Quick Definition
The simultaneous application of transposition along the scale (big-T) and transposition along the chord (little-t), notated T_x t_y, which transforms thematic material from one harmony to another while preserving its rhythmic and registral profile.

# Formal Definition
Double transposition (T_x t_y) combines scale-transposition (T) and chord-transposition (t) to transfer melodic-thematic material between harmonies. For example, a melody harmonized by I can be doubly transposed to produce a version harmonized by V, with the melody adjusting both its scalar position and its chordal position. This operation is intrinsic to melodic directedness, as it is "the glue binding harmony and melody together" (p. 409). The nonharmonic system ensures that out-of-chord notes resolve by step to in-chord notes, while registral limitations ensure within-chord notes outline close-position triads and seventh chords.

# Musical Context
Double transposition becomes increasingly important as musical culture shifts from polyphonic to homophonic defaults. It is relatively rare in The Well-Tempered Clavier, more common in C. P. E. Bach, and ubiquitous in Mozart and Haydn. Three of the eleven schemas in Gjerdingen's Music in the Galant Style employ double transposition between tonic and dominant.

# Common Confusions
- Double transposition is not simply transposition of a melody to a new key -- it simultaneously shifts position within the chord
- The starred transformation in Figure 9.1.3 maps tonic-triad notes into a nontriadic subset of the dominant seventh, illustrating that not all double transpositions produce equally clean results
- Double transposition is the mechanism behind the sentence form's fundamental default: I-V statement answered by V-I (Section 9.7)
