# Quick Definition

The simultaneous application of transposition along the scale (big-T) and transposition along the chord (little-t), notated T_x t_y, which transforms thematic material from one harmony to another while preserving its rhythmic and registral profile.

# Core Definition

Double transposition (T_x t_y) combines scale-transposition (T) and chord-transposition (t) to transfer melodic-thematic material between harmonies. It is "intrinsic to melodic directedness," serving as "the glue binding harmony and melody together" (p. 409). The technique applies double transposition to "specifically thematic objects with distinctive rhythmic and registral profiles: the melody of mm. 2-5 is transformed by double transposition into that of mm. 6-9" in Mozart K.550 (p. 409). Figure 9.1.3 catalogues the most common T_x t_y possibilities available to functional composers, including transformations between tonic and dominant.

# Key Properties

1. Combines two independent transposition operations simultaneously
2. Preserves rhythmic and registral profile of the theme
3. The starred transformation maps tonic-triad notes into a nontriadic subset of the dominant seventh
4. Becomes increasingly important as music shifts from polyphonic to homophonic defaults
5. Three of eleven Gjerdingen schemas employ it between tonic and dominant

# Construction / Recognition

## To Apply Double Transposition:
1. Identify the scalar transposition needed (T_x: how many scale steps?)
2. Identify the chordal transposition needed (t_y: how many chord steps?)
3. Apply both simultaneously to transform the thematic material
4. Check whether the result preserves the rhythmic and registral profile

# Context & Application

Double transposition is "relatively rare in The Well-Tempered Clavier, more common in C. P. E. Bach, and ubiquitous in Mozart and Haydn" (p. 409). Its historical trajectory reflects the shift from polyphony to homophony, where prominent thematic material requires systematic transformation. It underpins the sentence form's fundamental default: I-V statement answered by V-I (Section 9.7).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating double transposition as simple transposition to a new key
  **Correction**: It simultaneously shifts position within the chord, not just along the scale

- **Error**: Expecting all double transpositions to produce equally clean results
  **Correction**: The starred transformation in Figure 9.1.3 maps tonic-triad notes into a nontriadic subset of the dominant seventh

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking double transposition applies only to classical-period music
  **Clarification**: It appears across functional styles, becoming more prominent with homophonic textures

- **Confusion**: Confusing double transposition with sequence
  **Clarification**: A sequence repeats at the same T and t; double transposition may involve different combinations
