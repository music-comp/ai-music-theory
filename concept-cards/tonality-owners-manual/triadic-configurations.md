# Quick Definition
Five basic upper-voice configurations for three-voice triadic counterpoint: Close (C), Open (O), Half-open (H), Doubled Interval (DI), and Open Octave (OO), each representing a different pattern of vertical spacing measured along the chord's intrinsic scale.

# Formal Definition
When three voices sound at least two distinct pitch classes with adjacent voices separated by an octave or less, five configurations result: Close position (C) -- complete triad spanning less than an octave (53, 63, 64); Open position (O) -- complete triad with each voice two triadic steps above the next; Half-open (H) -- two voices an octave apart with the third between them; Doubled Interval (DI) -- two adjacent voices on the same pitch, third less than an octave away; Open Octave (OO) -- two voices an octave apart, third outside this interval. These divide into complete voicings (C and O) and incomplete voicings with doublings (DI, H, OO).

# Musical Context
These configurations originated in eighteenth-century figured-bass pedagogy describing keyboard right-hand positions. They can model voice-leading constraints: close-to-close and open-to-open progressions avoid parallel perfect intervals (except root-position-to-root-position). DI typically moves to C; OO to O; H to C or O. About 75% of three-voice transitions in Palestrina and Victoria follow these rules. The fundamental three-voice triadic harmony is the third, accompanied by fifth, doubling, or sixth (in that order of frequency) -- doubled thirds are more common than first-inversion triads in three-voice Renaissance music.

# Common Confusions
- "Close position" and "open position" are precise set-class designations (012 and 024 in chordal steps), not vague descriptions
- These categories can be applied to any trichord in any scale when conceived as patterns of spacing-along-a-chord, extending them beyond triads
