# Quick Definition

Five basic upper-voice configurations for three-voice triadic counterpoint: Close (C), Open (O), Half-open (H), Doubled Interval (DI), and Open Octave (OO). These are vertical configurations measured along the chord's intrinsic scale, originating in figured-bass pedagogy and mapping directly onto set-theoretic concepts.

# Core Definition

When three voices sound at least two distinct pitch classes with adjacent voices separated by an octave or less, five configurations result (Figure 3.5.1, p. 120): Close position (C) -- complete triad spanning less than an octave (53, 63, 64); Open position (O) -- complete triad with each voice two triadic steps above the next; Half-open (H) -- two voices an octave apart with the third between them; Doubled Interval (DI) -- two adjacent voices on the same pitch, third less than an octave away; Open Octave (OO) -- two voices an octave apart, third outside this interval. These divide into complete voicings (C and O) and incomplete voicings with doublings (DI, H, OO). Within categories, configurations are related by octave displacements: DI->H, C->O, and H->OO by transposing the middle voice by octave (p. 120).

# Key Properties

1. Five configurations when three voices sound at least two pitch classes, no more than an octave apart
2. Close and Open are complete voicings; DI, H, OO involve doublings
3. DI typically moves to C; OO to O; H to C or O
4. Close-to-close and open-to-open avoid parallels (except root-to-root)
5. About 75% of three-voice transitions in Palestrina/Victoria follow the connection rules
6. Doubled thirds are more common than first-inversion triads in three-voice Renaissance music
7. These categories can be applied to any trichord in any scale

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify Configuration:
1. Measure the intervals between three voices in chordal steps (along the triad)
2. C = all voices within one octave, sounding complete triad
3. O = complete triad, each voice two chordal steps above the next
4. H = octave between two voices, third between them
5. DI = two voices on same pitch
6. OO = octave between two voices, third outside

## To Navigate Between Configurations:
1. C goes to C, O, or H (not directly to OO)
2. O goes to O, C, or H (not directly to DI)
3. H goes to C or O
4. DI goes to C; OO goes to O

# Context & Application

These configurations originated in eighteenth-century figured-bass pedagogy describing keyboard right-hand positions (p. 120). They map directly onto set theory and geometry: "thinking in vertical configurations is geometrical thinking, with terms like 'close position' corresponding to compact regions in the space of musical possibility" (pp. 121-122). In three-voice composition, there is an unusually strong tension between harmony and melody: the desire for harmonic richness pushes toward complete triads while melodic elegance pulls toward incomplete sonorities (p. 123). # Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming close position is always preferable
  **Correction**: Different configurations are appropriate in different contexts; doubled thirds are actually very common in Renaissance three-voice music

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Treating "close" and "open" as vague descriptions
  **Clarification**: These are precise set-class designations (012 and 024 in chordal steps) with specific geometric meanings (p. 120)

- **Confusion**: Thinking configurations only apply to traditional triads
  **Clarification**: The five positions, conceived as spacing-along-a-chord patterns, can be applied to any trichord in any scale (p. 125)
