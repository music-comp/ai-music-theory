# Quick Definition
An anticipation that is itself decorated by a neighbor tone, creating one of the most analytically ambiguous idioms in the Palestrina style.

# Formal Definition
A decorated anticipation occurs when an anticipation is recursively embellished by a neighbor tone, so that the anticipation does not simply sound and hold but moves to a neighboring pitch before the harmony catches up. This figure is analytically ambiguous because the same melodic formula can function as either a decorated anticipation or a passing tone, depending on context -- and the two readings can lead to very different harmonic interpretations (including the presence or absence of parallel fifths).

# Musical Context
Decorated anticipations form one of the Palestrina style's most ambiguous idioms. They occur in the context of suspensions, where the anticipation of the next harmony is decorated by a lower neighbor. This figure sometimes appears in eighth notes in Palestrina's music.

# Common Confusions
- The same melodic formula can have different structural meanings depending on the surrounding voices. In one context it is a decorated anticipation; in another, reading it that way would produce parallel fifths, forcing a different interpretation.
- This ambiguity reveals that nonharmonic analysis is sometimes interpretive rather than mechanical, even in the strict Palestrina style.
