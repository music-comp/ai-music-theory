# Quick Definition
The sole surviving incomplete neighbor in the Palestrina style -- a dissonant note approached by step and left by leap (typically a descending third), which then resolves by step.

# Formal Definition
The cambiata (also called nota cambiata or "changing tone") is the only incomplete neighbor retained in the strict Palestrina style after the elimination of the various incomplete neighbors found in earlier polyphony. It typically involves a stepwise approach to a dissonance followed by a leap of a third in the opposite direction, with subsequent stepwise resolution. Jeppesen notes that accented third-species dissonances are acceptable for Palestrina only when they form this specific figure, not in the context of unidirectional melodic lines.

# Musical Context
The cambiata is characteristic of the strict Renaissance style and represents the one exception to the general SNAP framework's prohibition of incomplete neighbors. Earlier polyphony (e.g., Josquin, Clemens) features a wider array of incomplete neighbors, upward-resolving suspensions, and other nonstandard dissonance treatments that the Palestrina style eliminates.

# Common Confusions
- The cambiata is not the same as a generic incomplete neighbor. It is a specific figure with a characteristic stepwise-approach/leap-away/stepwise-resolution profile.
- In the strict Palestrina style, this is the only form of incomplete neighbor that is permitted, in contrast to the freer dissonance treatment of earlier and later periods.
