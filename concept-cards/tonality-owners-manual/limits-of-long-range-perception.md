# Quick Definition
The empirically supported observation that listeners' abilities to perceive large-scale musical structures -- such as long-range key relationships and tonal return in sonata form -- are far more limited than theorists have traditionally assumed.

# Formal Definition
A fundamental perceptual constraint on music theory: there is a biological difference between absolute-pitch listeners (who may directly perceive key return) and relative-pitch listeners (the vast majority) who access key information only through explicit calculation. Psychological experiments suggest that claims about hearing notes "connecting" across fifty measures, or sensing tonal resolution after ten or more minutes, may not be grounded in genuine perceptual experience. This challenges theories built on assumptions of long-range tonal hearing.

# Musical Context
This limitation is particularly relevant to Schenkerian claims about large-scale prolongation, sonata-form theories emphasizing tonal return, and any analytical approach positing perceptible connections across large temporal spans. The danger is that "some substantial proportion of these claims might not be grounded in anything real" (p. 404).

# Examples
- Experiments showing listeners are vulnerable to tricks such as rewriting a piece to end in a foreign key (p. 402-403)
- The claim that a sonata recapitulation creates "resolution" comparable to a dominant resolving -- questionable even for absolute-pitch listeners (p. 404, citing Cook 1987)
- Experts confidently claiming to hear a note in one measure connecting to another fifty measures away (p. 404)

# Common Confusions
- Relative-pitch listeners who can track modulations through repeated listening are not thereby demonstrating direct perceptual sensitivity to long-range key structure
- Being able to "think the thought" that a piece has returned to the tonic is not the same as experiencing a felt sense of tonal resolution
- Social and institutional forces may encourage musicians to exaggerate their perceptual powers
