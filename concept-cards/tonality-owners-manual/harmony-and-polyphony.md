# Quick Definition
The question of whether early functional harmony was confined to secular homophonic styles or also appears within the polyphonic tradition -- with Tymoczko arguing that protofunctionality can be found in both, emerging "from the bottom up" through local voice-leading patterns.

# Formal Definition
Tymoczko addresses the basic question of genre in the development of functionality: while the earliest and most obviously functional music is popular, secular, and homophonic, statistical evidence reveals increasing functionality throughout sixteenth-century polyphony as well. Computational analysis of raw triadic progressions in Ockeghem, Josquin, and Palestrina shows a dramatic increase in root-position major triads, descending-fifth root progressions, and bass leaps by fourth or fifth from Ockeghem to Palestrina. Protofunctionality in polyphonic music arises from the inseparability of harmony and counterpoint.

# Musical Context
The Pope Marcellus Mass Kyrie serves as Tymoczko's test case: despite being polyphonic with no homophonic textures, about 85% of its progressions conform to functional norms (versus ~75% for other Palestrina ionian pieces, and ~95% for fully functional music). The protofunctionality arises from local voice-leading patterns rather than top-down structures like regular phrase organization. Counterpoint and harmony are an "inseparable amalgam" in this music.

# Common Confusions
- Polyphonic music is not inherently non-functional. The Pope Marcellus Mass demonstrates that polyphony and functional harmony can coexist.
- Jeppesen's description of Palestrina as harmony-free, purely contrapuntal music better suits Ockeghem. In Palestrina, harmonies are becoming objects of compositional concern.
- The development of functionality was a bottom-up process beginning with local details, not a top-down process beginning with phrase structure or large-scale planning.
