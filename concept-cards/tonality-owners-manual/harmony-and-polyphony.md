# Quick Definition

The question of whether early functionality was confined to secular, homophonic music or also appears in the polyphonic tradition. Tymoczko finds statistical evidence for increasing functionality throughout sixteenth-century polyphony, demonstrating that harmony and counterpoint are inseparable.

# Core Definition

Tymoczko argues that the earliest and most obviously functional music is popular, secular, and homophonic, but statistical evidence shows increasing functionality throughout sixteenth-century polyphony (p. 275). From Ockeghem to Palestrina, there is a dramatic increase in root-position major triads, descending-fifth root progressions (tripling from 4% to 13%), and 6/4-5/3 suspension patterns (pp. 275-276). Jeppesen's description of Palestrina as "purely contrapuntal, harmony-free music" is better suited to Ockeghem, where the bass moves primarily by step (pp. 276-277). Palestrina has notable preferences for major triads, root-position chords, and bass leaps by fourth or fifth -- suggesting harmonies are becoming objects of compositional concern (p. 277).

# Key Properties

1. Earliest functional music is secular, homophonic, popular
2. But functional tendencies also appear in polyphonic traditions
3. Dramatic statistical changes from Ockeghem to Palestrina
4. Root-position major triads and descending-fifth progressions increase
5. Bass leaps by fourth/fifth nearly always support root-position triads in Palestrina
6. Ockeghem, Josquin, and Palestrina speak different musical dialects
7. The music is neither purely linear nor purely harmonic -- "an inseparable amalgam" (p. 287)

# Construction / Recognition

## To Detect Harmonic Thinking in Polyphony:
1. Check proportion of root-position vs. first-inversion triads
2. Check frequency of bass leaps by fourth/fifth
3. Compare major vs. minor triad frequencies
4. Calculate proportion of descending-fifth root progressions
5. Look for idiomatic harmonic-melodic patterns (Prinner, ascending subdominant, etc.)

# Context & Application

The Pope Marcellus Kyrie demonstrates protofunctionality within polyphony: ~85% functional progressions, with harmony and melody "inextricably linked" (p. 287). This loosens the association between functionality and homophony.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating Renaissance polyphony as purely linear and harmony-free
  **Correction**: Statistical evidence shows increasing harmonic thinking, especially in Palestrina

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking functionality requires homophony
  **Clarification**: The Pope Marcellus Kyrie is both polyphonic and strongly protofunctional
