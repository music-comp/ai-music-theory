# Quick Definition
Sequences generated by taking various numbers of clockwise steps on the triadic circle, producing a catalog of common schemas from subtle near-parallel canons to overt ascending-fifth imitative patterns.

# Formal Definition
For an n-note chord, n-step motion produces transpositional (parallel) sequences; any other step count produces canonic sequences. On the 3-in-7 triadic circle: one-step and two-step motions produce the basic voice leading and descending-fifth pattern with subtle canonic imitation (the triad divides the octave evenly enough that short motions create little melodic activity); four- and five-step motions produce three-note scale fragments or triadic arpeggios with clearer canonic implication; seven-step motion embeds arpeggios in a round moving by chordal step. The six-voice Bach sequence (Musical Offering) superimposes a triadic canon in the right hand with a dyadic canon in the left.

# Common Confusions
- Short motions on the triadic circle (-1, -2) produce very subtle canons due to the triad's near-evenness; the imitation may be imperceptible
- Each column of the schema table is related by the Tinctoris Transform (differing only in transpositional level)
- The examples can be read backwards to produce ascending sequences
