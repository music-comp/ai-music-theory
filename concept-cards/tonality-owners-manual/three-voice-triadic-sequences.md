# Quick Definition

Sequences generated by taking various numbers of clockwise steps on the triadic circle. For a three-note chord, three-step motion is transpositional (parallel); all other step counts are canonic. The triad's near-evenness makes short motions' canonic imitation very subtle.

# Core Definition

For an *n*-note chord, *n*-step motion produces transpositional sequences; any other produces canonic ones (p. 171). On the 3-in-7 triadic circle: one and two steps produce the basic voice leading and descending-fifth pattern with subtle canon (Figure 4.4.1, p. 171); four and five steps produce three-note scale fragments or triadic arpeggios; seven-step motion embeds arpeggios in a round moving by chordal step. Each column of the schema table relates via the Tinctoris Transform. The six-voice Bach *Musical Offering* Ricercare superimposes a triadic canon in the right hand with a dyadic canon in the left (Figure 4.4.9, p. 174).

# Key Properties

1. Three-step motion = transpositional (parallel); all others = canonic
2. Short motions (-1, -2) produce very subtle canons due to triad's near-evenness
3. Five-step: ascending-fifth canon with ascending triadic arpeggios
4. Seven-step: round moving by chordal step
5. Columns of the schema table related by Tinctoris Transform
6. Can read examples backwards for ascending sequences

# Construction / Recognition

## To Construct:
1. Choose a step count on the triadic circle
2. Apply that motion repeatedly
3. Three-step = parallel; other step counts produce canonic patterns

# Context & Application

These patterns catalog the common three-voice sequential schemas. Short motions (-1, -2) "produce little melodic activity and the canonic imitation is very subtle" (p. 171). Five-step patterns produce clear ascending-fifth canons (Strozzi, Bach). Seven-step patterns embed arpeggios in rounds (Strozzi's "L'amante modesto"). Complex multivoice textures can be built by superimposing smaller schemas (Bach *Musical Offering*).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Expecting short-motion triadic sequences to sound obviously canonic
  **Correction**: The triad's near-evenness makes the imitation very subtle for short motions

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking each step count produces a qualitatively different type of sequence
  **Clarification**: All columns are related by the Tinctoris Transform; they differ only in transpositional level
