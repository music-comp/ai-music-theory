# Quick Definition

A reconception of musical reduction not as revealing hidden prolongational structure, but as constructing speculative blueprints -- simplified versions of a passage that are simple enough to imagine inventing, yet rich enough to imagine expanding into the finished piece.

# Core Definition

Reduction as paraphrase treats analytical reduction as "a kind of paraphrase, a speculative hypothesis about the longer strategic goals that might animate a passage. We need not say less-important material 'represents' more-important material, any more than we say that omitted material 'represents' the events in a paraphrase. Instead, what is important is simply that our reductions serve as plausible blueprints, their notes connecting the way one scene in a movie might continue an earlier narrative. A good blueprint is an analytical way station: simple enough that we can imagine ourselves inventing it, and yet rich enough that we can imagine expanding it into the finished piece" (p. 411).

# Prerequisites

- **Melodic strategy** -- Paraphrase-based reduction depends on understanding melody as strategic rather than prolongational
- **Heterogeneous hierarchy** -- The framework that allows different analytical layers without requiring unified prolongation
- **Nonharmonic reduction** -- Understanding the basic process of removing nonharmonic tones

# Key Properties

1. Reductions are speculative hypotheses, not claims about hidden structure
2. A good reduction is both simple enough to invent and rich enough to expand
3. Sequences and schemas can be legitimate reductive targets (atomic units requiring no further reduction)
4. The ontology of reduction is scalar -- abstract voices available in every octave, not specific pitches
5. Voice exchanges need not be reduced away; they may be part of the target
6. The Schenkerian "background" is reconceived in scale-theoretic terms: surface voices moving along nested alphabets (subchord, chordal, scalar, chromatic)

# Construction / Recognition

## To Construct a Paraphrase-Based Reduction:
1. Identify nonharmonic tones and remove them to obtain a harmonic skeleton
2. Identify sequential and schematic patterns as potential reductive endpoints
3. Remove chordal skips to obtain melodic outlines
4. Do NOT continue removing harmonies according to a phrase model
5. Present the result as a scalar background whose voices exist in every octave
6. Verify that the reduction is both plausible as a blueprint and expandable to the surface

# Context & Application

This approach permits the benefits of Schenkerian analysis while "jettisoning its problematic metaphysics" (p. 411). It is particularly useful for Beethoven, whose personal vocabulary of contrary-motion patterns and sequential schemas falls outside the Schenkerian catalogue of acceptable reductive targets. Standard Schenkerianism, with its "single-minded focus on stepwise melody" and "concomitant disdain for sequential structure," cannot adequately analyze much of Beethoven's music (p. 428).

# Examples

**Example 1** (pp. 426-428): Beethoven's Op. 109 variations theme analyzed as a near sequence by contrary motion, with diatonic-third voice leadings as atomic reductive units rather than surface decoration to be eliminated.

**Example 2** (pp. 479-480): The Tempest exposition reduced to six schematic processes, each a recognizable pattern or sequence that could guide improvisation -- providing "a sense of ownership that I do not get from Schenkerian analysis" (p. 480).

**Example 3** (pp. 423-424): Chopin's Revolutionary Etude features ascending surface pattern within a descending harmonic field -- a scalar background rather than Schenker's specific pitches with register transfer.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming any simplified version of a passage is a valid paraphrase
  **Correction**: Valid paraphrases must be musically plausible blueprints that could actually be expanded into the finished piece

- **Error**: Insisting on reducing all voice exchanges away
  **Correction**: Voice exchanges may be integral to a reductive target, especially in contrary-motion schemas

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking paraphrase-based reduction is less rigorous than Schenkerian reduction
  **Clarification**: It is differently rigorous -- demanding plausibility as a blueprint rather than conformity to a fixed template

- **Confusion**: Assuming standard Schenkerianism is "insufficiently hierarchical"
  **Clarification**: Tymoczko argues it is insufficiently hierarchical in ignoring the ontological difference between chord and scale, flattening multileveled musical structures
