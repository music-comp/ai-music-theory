# Quick Definition

The aspiration to relatively objective harmonic labeling, tempered by the recognition that resolving ambiguity requires background knowledge. "Analytical objectivity" is strongly context-dependent, requiring stylistic knowledge external to the passage at hand.

# Core Definition

Tymoczko argues it is "still reasonable to aspire to a fairly objective labeling of harmonies" subject to the caveat that "the resolution of ambiguity needs to be carried out on the basis of background knowledge" (p. 308). This complicates theory evaluation: rather than separating theory-neutral analysis from syntax evaluation, "what counts as the best analysis may itself be theory-dependent" (p. 309). The challenge is not so much subjectivity as holism -- the need to resolve ambiguities by drawing upon stylistic knowledge external to the piece (p. 308).

# Key Properties

1. Objectivity is context-dependent, not absolute
2. Resolving ambiguity requires background knowledge
3. Theory and analysis must be evaluated together as a package (p. 309)
4. Computational methods can capture this holism (p. 309)
5. A two-stage process: raw first-pass analysis, then context-informed refinement (p. 308)

# Construction / Recognition

## To Pursue Analytical Objectivity:
1. Begin with a theory-neutral first pass treating every consonance as harmonic
2. Use the raw counts as input for a second pass adjudicating among readings
3. Balance local details with general stylistic expectations
4. Acknowledge that the "best" analysis may be theory-dependent

# Context & Application

The "Martians" thought experiment asks whether intelligent beings without our pedagogy could do harmonic analysis. The answer: not initially, but with enough experience they could learn (p. 309). Computational methods modeling presuppositions can reproduce expert analytical judgments (p. 308).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming harmonic analysis can be fully mechanical and theory-neutral
  **Correction**: Background knowledge is needed to resolve ambiguities; theory and analysis are a package

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Equating the need for background knowledge with pure subjectivity
  **Clarification**: The circularity can be benign ("hermeneutic") when enough unambiguous passages calibrate expectations (p. 308)
