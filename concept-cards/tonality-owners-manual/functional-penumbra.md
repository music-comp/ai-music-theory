# Quick Definition
The zone of second-order idioms and practices surrounding the first-order defaults of the harmonic grammar, some features of which remain fixed throughout the functional tradition while others evolve historically.

# Formal Definition
Tymoczko conceives the descending-thirds model not as universal laws but as first-order defaults surrounded by a penumbra of second-order idioms. Fixed features of the penumbra include: mediants acting as post-tonic chords moving to vi or IV, as alternative dominants, or as tonic substitutes (Figure 7.1.6); and V--vii^ø7^ supporting melodic ^7--^6--^5 against bass ^5--^4--^3. Evolving features include the historical decrease of vi--V and nonsequential iii during the eighteenth century, and the nineteenth-century recovery of iii alongside increased use of ct^o7^ and ii^ø7^.

# Musical Context
The penumbra explains why functional tonality is not a rigid system but a flexible practice admitting historical variation. Different periods and composers exhibit different penumbral features while sharing the same core grammar. The evolution of the penumbra is captured by the hourglass model.

# Common Confusions
- Penumbral progressions are not "wrong" or "rule-breaking" -- they are conventional second-order defaults that complement the first-order grammar.
- Some penumbral features (like the mediant's flexibility) persist across all periods, while others are period-specific.
