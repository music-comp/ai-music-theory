# Quick Definition
Voice leading that minimizes the total distance voices must travel between two chords, achieved by keeping the center of gravity approximately fixed and the change in vertical configuration small.

# Formal Definition
The most efficient voice leading between two vertical configurations minimizes the change in center of gravity. Additionally, efficient voice leading requires that configurations themselves change by only a small amount. Transpositionally related chords can be connected by efficient voice leading when their roots move by intervals related to the chord's evenness: dyads at the tritone, triads at the major third (or near-major-third intervals like minor third or perfect fourth), and seventh chords at the minor third.

# Musical Context
Efficient voice leading explains why similar motion is rare in counterpoint: contrary motion preserves center of gravity (change = 0), and as change increases, the voice leading becomes more unbalanced until we reach oblique motion, then similar motion where total voice movement increases. This reframes the traditional prohibition on "direct fifths and octaves" as a consequence of the preference for minimal melodic motion. The concept underwrites progressions from the Renaissance to the present, including Schubert's major-third triadic progressions, Wagner's minor-third seventh-chord progressions, and the ubiquitous fifth-progressions of functional tonality.

# Common Confusions
- Efficient voice leading is not just about avoiding parallel fifths; it is a broader principle about minimizing total voice displacement
- The scarcity of similar motion in Renaissance counterpoint reflects efficient voice-leading principles, not just the prohibition on parallel perfect intervals
