# Quick Definition
Dominant or dominant-seventh chords temporarily tonicizing a non-tonic chord, enabling ascending-fifth modulations and chromatic enrichment within functional tonality.

# Formal Definition
Applied (or secondary) dominants are chords that function as V or V^7^ of a diatonic chord other than the tonic. They are written as V/X or V^7^/X, where X is the target chord. In the context of ascending-fifth sequences, applied dominants tonicize each successive fifth, creating a chain of temporary key areas. Applied dominants are particularly characteristic of Bach's ascending-fifth sequences, where each sequential unit is tonicized. In later classical style, ascending-fifth sequences are rarer and usually lack Bach's omnipresent tonicizations.

# Musical Context
Applied dominants play a crucial role in modulatory sequences and in the "up-and-down-the-ladder" schema. They also appear in the descending-thirds model's penumbra: the mediant (iii) can function as an alternative dominant, while applied dominants on ^6 appear in the traditional rule of the octave.

# Common Confusions
- Applied dominants can create ambiguity about whether a passage has truly modulated or is simply tonicizing a local chord. Listeners cannot immediately distinguish a foreign dominant seventh as an applied chord versus the start of a new key.
- The presence of applied dominants does not necessarily indicate modulation -- they can function within a single key as chromatic embellishments.
