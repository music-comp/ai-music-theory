# Quick Definition
A descending melodic pattern from the third to the first scale degree (3^-2^-1^) that, together with the longer 5^-4^-3^-2^-1^ form, underlies many baroque and classical phrase structures.

# Formal Definition
The 3^-2^-1^ template (and its longer form 5^-4^-3^-2^-1^) is a descending melodic pattern that serves as a foundational structural framework for many phrases in functional tonal music. These templates represent one of several independent developments in the emergence of functional tonality, alongside harmonic developments. The templates link melodic motion to harmonic defaults through the fifths-and-steps arrangement: descending scale degrees are associated with characteristic harmonizations.

# Musical Context
The development of these melodic templates is one of the processes contributing to the gradual emergence of functional tonality. They appear in early protofunctional music (such as Michaelis's frottola with its 5^-6^-5^-4^-3^-2^-1^ melody) and become increasingly standardized through the baroque and classical periods. The Prinner schema is essentially a harmonized 6^-5^-4^-3^ descent, while the Fenaroli features 7^-1^-2^-3^ ascending against descending fifths.

# Common Confusions
- The 3^-2^-1^ template is not unique to Schenkerian theory. Tymoczko treats it as one of several important melodic routines rather than as the fundamental background structure of all tonal music.
- These templates exist as tendencies in protofunctional music long before they become standardized frameworks in later periods.
- There is no reason to assume that the melodic structure of an entire piece will necessarily descend from a tonic-triad note to the root.
