# Quick Definition

The descending melodic templates (3-2-1 and 5-4-3-2-1) that underlie many baroque and classical phrases, identified as one of the separate processes constituting the development of functional tonality.

# Core Definition

The development of the 3-2-1 and 5-4-3-2-1 melodic templates is one of the multiple separate processes constituting the evolution of functional tonality (p. 258). These templates underlie "so many baroque and classical phrases" (p. 258). Their development is part of the gradual regimentation of melodic routines that characterizes the transition from protofunctionality to mature functionality. In the fifths-and-steps arrangement, the upper-voice analogue to the rule of the octave associates melodic scale degrees with harmonic defaults (p. 261).

# Key Properties

1. One of multiple independent processes in tonality's development
2. 3-2-1 and 5-4-3-2-1 underlie many baroque/classical phrases
3. Connected to the fifths-and-steps arrangement's default harmonizations
4. Part of the gradual regimentation of melodic routines
5. Early instances appear in protofunctional music (e.g., Michaelis's 5-6-5-4-3-2-1)

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify:
1. Look for descending stepwise upper-voice motion toward the tonic (3-2-1 or 5-4-3-2-1)
2. Check whether the melody is harmonized with the fifths-and-steps defaults
3. Note whether the template structures the entire phrase

# Context & Application

The 5-6-5-4-3-2-1 melody in Michaelis's "Passando per una rezzola" (1504) already uses these templates in a protofunctional context (p. 262).
