# Quick Definition
A seventh chord built on a diminished triad with a minor seventh (e.g., B--D--F--A), which gains increased usage in the nineteenth century as part of the expanding functional harmonic vocabulary.

# Formal Definition
The half-diminished seventh chord (ii^ø7^) is a diatonic chord that becomes increasingly prominent in the later functional tradition. In the descending-thirds model, V--vii^ø7^ can support melodic ^7--^6--^5 against bass ^5--^4--^3 as parallel tenths often decorated by octave displacement. The half-diminished seventh is listed alongside the common-tone diminished seventh as a characteristic feature of the expanding harmonic penumbra in the nineteenth century (Figure 7.1.7).

# Musical Context
The ii^ø7^ typically functions as a pre-dominant chord in minor keys (and sometimes in major via modal borrowing). Its increased use in the nineteenth century reflects the general loosening of harmonic restrictions after the classical-era bottleneck described by the hourglass model.

# Common Confusions
- The half-diminished seventh should be distinguished from the fully diminished seventh chord, which has a diminished seventh interval rather than a minor seventh.
- In the functional tradition, ii^ø7^ and vii^ø7^ serve different roles despite sharing the same chord quality: ii^ø7^ is pre-dominant while vii^ø7^ is dominant-function.
