# Quick Definition
A nonharmonic tone that sounds a note of the upcoming chord before that chord arrives, typically on a metrically weak beat.

# Formal Definition
An anticipation is a nonharmonic tone that arrives at a chord tone before the rest of the harmony changes to that chord. In Palestrina's music, anticipations almost always occur in the context of suspensions, to the point where it might make more sense to speak of a "suspension/anticipation system" rather than of anticipations as a freestanding class. Freestanding anticipations (not decorating suspensions and not themselves decorated by other notes) comprise less than 1% of Palestrina's dissonances, making them less frequent than ascending neighbors.

# Musical Context
Anticipations are the rarest of the four SNAP categories in Palestrina. They are more common in Josquin as freestanding events. In the baroque period, "arpeggiated anticipations" appear in Bach's chorales, where an arpeggiating voice (usually moving down by third, from tonic to subdominant) reaches the next harmony an eighth note early.

# Common Confusions
- A single melodic formula involving an anticipation can play different structural roles depending on the surrounding voices. The same figure might be an anticipation in one context and a passing tone in another (to avoid parallel fifths).
- Decorated anticipations form one of the Palestrina style's most ambiguous idioms, where an anticipation is recursively decorated by a neighbor tone.
