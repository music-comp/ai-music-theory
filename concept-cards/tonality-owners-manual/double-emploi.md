# Quick Definition
Rameau's concept describing the minor-third juncture in a four-chord functional cycle (d-G-C-F), which is the exact retrograde of rock's ascending-fifth pattern.

# Formal Definition
Rameau interpreted functional harmony using a sequence of four descending diatonic fifths: d-G-C-F. The phrase structure was offset relative to harmonic structure: C-F-d-G-(C) rather than d-G-C-F-(d). His "double emploi" is the minor-third juncture between the ends of the fifth sequence (F back to d), sometimes elided into a ii6/5 composite chord. Rock's four-chord ascending-fifth loop (C-G-D-A) is the retrograde: three fifths and a minor third, ascending rather than descending. This resemblance underscores the sense in which rock is genuinely *retrofunctional*.

# Musical Context
The connection between Rameau's functional archetype and rock's loop structure reveals the deep relationship between the two systems. Both organize harmony around sequences of fifths with a minor-third "seam." But the direction is reversed: classical functionality descends, rock retrofunction ascends. This is not coincidence but a reflection of the underlying geometry of triadic voice leading, with the two systems representing complementary approaches to the same spatial constraints.

# Examples
- Rameau's functional cycle: d-G-C-F (four descending fifths), offset as C-F-d-G-(C)
- Rock's retrofunctional cycle: C-G-D-A (three ascending fifths + ascending minor third)
- The ii6/5 chord in classical harmony as the elision of the minor-third juncture
- IV-I cadence ending the three main rock loop families compared to the ii-V juncture in functional harmony

# Common Confusions
- The *double emploi* is specifically about the minor-third step that connects the end of a fifth chain back to the beginning -- not about any minor-third progression
- Rock's use of ascending fifths is not a conscious inversion of Rameau's model -- the resemblance arises from shared geometrical constraints
- The offset between phrase structure and harmonic structure is crucial to both systems
