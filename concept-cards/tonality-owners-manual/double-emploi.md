# Quick Definition

Rameau's concept describing the minor-third juncture in a four-chord functional cycle (d-G-C-F), whose exact retrograde (C-G-D-A) is rock's ascending-fifth loop -- underscoring rock's genuinely retrofunctional character.

# Core Definition

"Rameau interpreted functional harmony using a sequence of four descending diatonic fifths d-G-C-F whose phrase structure was offset relative to its harmonic structure: C-F-d-G-(C) rather than d-G-C-F-(d). His 'double emploi' is the minor-third juncture between the ends of the fifth sequence, sometimes elided into a ii65 composite" (p. 68). Rock's four-chord loop is the retrograde: "three fifths and a minor third, ascending in rock and descending in functional harmony. This resemblance underscores the sense in which rock music is genuinely retrofunctional" (p. 68).

# Key Properties

1. Rameau's functional cycle: d-G-C-F (four descending fifths)
2. Rock's retrofunctional cycle: C-G-D-A (three ascending fifths + minor third)
3. The two are exact retrogrades
4. The double emploi is the minor-third "seam" connecting the fifth chain
5. In classical harmony, sometimes elided into ii65
6. Both systems organize around sequences of fifths with a minor-third seam

# Construction / Recognition

## To See the Connection:
1. Write out Rameau's four-chord cycle: d-G-C-F
2. Retrograde it: F-C-G-d, or equivalently C-G-D-A
3. Note the shared structure: three fifths and one minor third
4. Observe that the direction reverses (descending vs. ascending)

# Context & Application

The connection reveals that rock and classical functional harmony are complementary explorations of the same underlying geometry, with reversed directionality.

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 68): Rameau's d-G-C-F vs. rock's C-G-D-A.

**Example 2**: The ii65 chord as the elision of the minor-third juncture in classical harmony.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Thinking rock's ascending fifths are a conscious inversion of Rameau
  **Correction**: The resemblance arises from shared geometrical constraints, not deliberate opposition

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking double emploi refers to any minor-third progression
  **Clarification**: It specifically refers to the minor-third juncture connecting the ends of a fifth chain
