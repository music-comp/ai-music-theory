# Quick Definition

The relative strength or finality of a cadential progression, determined by the number of rightward steps traversed on the descending-thirds harmonic map from the last non-tonic chord to the tonic resolution.

# Core Definition

Within the descending-thirds model of functional harmony (Fig. 7.1.3), cadential weight corresponds to the number of rightward steps on the harmonic map from the final non-tonic chord to the tonic. A V-I cadence traverses two positions (the maximum direct leap), giving it the greatest cadential weight. A IV-I cadence traverses one position. Progressions involving more intermediate chords (e.g., ii-V-I or IV-V-I) accumulate weight through combined motion. The cadential 6/4 (I6/4) embellishes the dominant, effectively delaying the arrival of the tonic to increase the sense of resolution. The model privileges "strong" progressions -- descending third, descending fifth, and ascending step -- which move rightward by one, two, or three steps respectively (p. 313).

# Prerequisites

- **Harmonic cycle theory** -- The descending-thirds harmonic map provides the framework for measuring cadential weight

# Key Properties

1. Weight is proportional to rightward distance traversed on the harmonic map
2. V-I traverses the maximum two-position leap, giving it the greatest weight
3. IV-I traverses one position, giving it less weight
4. Full cadences (ii-V-I) accumulate weight through combined motion
5. The cadential 6/4 extends the dominant's duration, increasing perceived resolution
6. This framework explains the traditional hierarchy of cadence types

# Construction / Recognition

## To Assess Cadential Weight:
1. Locate the final non-tonic chord on the descending-thirds harmonic map (Fig. 7.1.3)
2. Count the number of rightward steps to the tonic
3. For multi-chord cadential progressions, sum the accumulated rightward motion
4. Account for cadential 6/4 as a dominant embellishment that extends weight

# Context & Application

Cadential weight explains the hierarchy of cadence types in functional music: authentic cadences (V-I) feel more conclusive than plagal cadences (IV-I), and full cadences (ii-V-I) feel more conclusive than either. The fauxbourdon ROTO variants often delay the cadential dominant through "standing on the predominant," where predominant chords on scale-degrees 6 and 4 are connected by I6/4, creating greater accumulated cadential weight before resolution (p. 333, Fig. 7.5.6).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assessing cadential weight based only on the final two chords
  **Correction**: Cadential weight is a property of the progression as a whole, accumulating through the combined rightward motion of all chords

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Treating the cadential 6/4 as a tonic chord in second inversion
  **Clarification**: The cadential 6/4 is functionally an embellishment of the dominant, not a tonic chord -- it extends dominant duration rather than introducing a tonic arrival
