# Quick Definition

The most far-reaching form of reduction: removing notes to reveal long-range connections between nonadjacent events, providing conjectures about a composer's strategic ends. "Paradoxically, both the most speculative and least problematic form of reduction" (p. 208).

# Core Definition

Summarizing reduction "boils a complex passage down into something simpler, finding familiar musical patterns guiding longer stretches of musical time" (p. 208). "If nonharmonic reduction reveals the rules of the musical game, then summarizing reduction provides conjectures about a composer's strategic ends, analogous to the suggestion that an expert chess player intends to put pressure on a specific square" (p. 208). It produces "something like a paraphrase of a composer's musical ends" (p. 209). "We should no more aspire to come up with a singular correct summary of a piece than we should strive to produce the perfect paraphrase of a movie" (p. 209). Tymoczko rejects the Schenkerian claim that diminution involves notes "representing" more important notes, preferring the movie-summary analogy.

# Key Properties

1. Most far-reaching and most speculative form of reduction
2. Paradoxically least problematic -- paraphrases are inherently tentative
3. Analogous to chess strategy or movie summaries, not syntactic parsing
4. No reason to expect summaries to take specific forms (species counterpoint, tonic-dominant bass)
5. Schema theory reveals strategies: Prinner, Indugio, Morte
6. Diminution is not "representation" but a higher-level plan with subsidiary events
7. Inherently requires high-level understanding; skeptical of automation

# Construction / Recognition

## To Perform:
1. Identify familiar musical patterns guiding longer stretches of time
2. Remove subsidiary material to reveal the guiding pattern
3. Consider the result as a hypothetical blueprint, not the single correct answer
4. Align your reduction with knowledge of the composer's tendencies

# Context & Application

Schema theory's major achievement is revealing a wider range of strategies: "a host of common patterns such as the 'Prinner,' 'Indugio,' and 'Morte'" (p. 209). These schemas are "often occluded by diminution, with important schematic notes hidden among a wealth of decorative figuration" (p. 209). Tymoczko is skeptical of attempts to automate summarizing reduction, "which inherently requires high-level understanding" (p. 209). The music-theoretical community "could benefit from more informality: rather than conceiving of summarizing reduction as a syntactic project analogous to nonharmonic reduction, it would be better to imagine it as producing hypotheses about composers' intentions" (p. 209).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating summarizing reduction as producing the single correct analysis
  **Correction**: It produces tentative paraphrases; there is no singular correct summary

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking diminution means less important notes "represent" more important ones
  **Clarification**: The relationship is like a plot summary, not a syntactic representation; omitting episodes doesn't imply they "represent" the core narrative (p. 208)

- **Confusion**: Expecting summaries to conform to specific formal constraints
  **Clarification**: There is no reason bass lines must always move from tonic to dominant, or melodies must descend from a tonic-triad note (p. 209)
