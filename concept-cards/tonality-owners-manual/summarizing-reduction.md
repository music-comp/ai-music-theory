# Quick Definition
A form of analytical reduction that removes notes to reveal long-range connections between nonadjacent events, providing conjectures about a composer's strategic ends.

# Formal Definition
Summarizing reduction is the most far-reaching form of reductive analysis. It boils a complex passage down into something simpler, finding familiar musical patterns guiding longer stretches of musical time. If nonharmonic reduction reveals the rules of the musical game, summarizing reduction provides conjectures about a composer's strategic ends -- analogous to the suggestion that a chess player intends to put pressure on a specific square. It produces a hypothetical blueprint or outline.

# Musical Context
Summarizing reduction gives us something like a paraphrase of a composer's musical ends. Schemas like the "Prinner," "Indugio," and "Morte" are often revealed through this kind of reduction. Important schematic notes may be hidden among a wealth of decorative figuration (diminution). Unlike nonharmonic reduction, summarizing reduction is inherently speculative.

# Examples
- Schema theory reveals common patterns such as the "Prinner," "Indugio," and "Morte" through summarizing reduction
- Gjerdingen (2007) cites the commedia dell'arte as a model for musical schemas, where skeletal plots are embellished spontaneously

# Common Confusions
- Summarizing reduction should not be confused with nonharmonic reduction. It is a paraphrase, not a syntactic decomposition. We should no more aspire to produce a single correct summary of a piece than we should strive to produce the perfect paraphrase of a movie.
- Tymoczko is skeptical of Schenkerian theory's claim that diminution involves less important notes "representing" more important notes. Instead, he prefers the analogy of a movie summary: omitting episodes does not imply they "represent" the core narrative.
- There is no reason to expect summaries to take a specific form (e.g., conforming to species counterpoint or bass lines always moving from tonic to dominant).
