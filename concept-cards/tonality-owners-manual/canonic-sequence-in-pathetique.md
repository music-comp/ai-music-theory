# Quick Definition
The Ab major episode from Beethoven's *Pathetique* sonata (III, mm. 79-86), which combines a canonic sequence, a near sequence, and a contrary-motion sequence in close succession, illustrating the many varieties of transformed repetition.

# Formal Definition
The passage opens with a canonic sequence whose melodic intervals alternate between voices: one voice ascends by fourth, the other descends by fifth, switching hands after every dyad. Despite this alternation, the left hand always sounds the root and the right hand the third. On the 2-in-7 diagram, it moves clockwise combining descending basic voice leading with alternating two- and five-step voice exchanges. At the bar level, this produces a transpositional sequence moving each voice down by step. The odd number of diatonic notes causes interval exchange when the theme returns to tonic.

# Musical Context
The episode is a "tutorial in the many varieties of repetition." Its three sections present: (1) a canonic sequence with antiparallel voice leading forming a tonally functional period with half and full cadences; (2) a near sequence stating a melody on Eb, Db, and Ab that embeds the diatonic third's ascending basic voice leading; (3) a contrary-motion sequence moving hands an octave apart with each iteration. The passage is "simultaneously funny and profound," presenting embodied mathematics -- contrapuntal schemas embedded in works of art.

# Common Confusions
- The impression of "invertible counterpoint" arises because each hand plays the intervals of the other hand's antecedent but on the same notes -- contour and rhythm trump pitch-class content
- The near sequence in the middle section is not strictly sequential but embeds dyadic sequences in nonsequential triads (Eb-bb-Ab-Eb)
