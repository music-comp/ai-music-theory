# Quick Definition
The principle that multiple different theoretical frameworks can accurately describe the same set of musical practices, with no single theory being uniquely "correct."

# Formal Definition
Theoretical underdetermination is the ability to construct different theories accounting for one and the same set of observable musical facts. Just as physics has Lagrangian, Hamiltonian, and vector formulations of Newtonian mechanics, music theory has Rameau's descending-fifth theory, Tymoczko's descending-thirds theory, theories based on idioms, functional categories, linear relationships, and others. None is right or wrong; they offer different perspectives on a practice that was largely understood tacitly. The near-equivalence between Rameau's theory and Tymoczko's own (demonstrated by the fact that every rightward progression on the descending-thirds model articulates a segment of Rameau's descending-fifth paradigm) exemplifies this phenomenon.

# Musical Context
Theoretical underdetermination is important for the study of functional harmony because it explains why different analytical traditions (Schenkerian, Riemannian, Rameauian, schema-based) can all produce valid insights despite their apparent incompatibility. The choice among theories involves preferences that are "as much aesthetic as scientific."

# Common Confusions
- Underdetermination does not mean "anything goes" -- different theories make different predictions and have different strengths (e.g., Rameau's theory struggles with simple I--V--I exchanges).
- The existence of multiple valid theories does not undermine the reality of the patterns they describe.
