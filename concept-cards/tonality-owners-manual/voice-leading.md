# Quick Definition
The manner in which individual voices (melodic lines) move from one chord to the next, understood geometrically as paths through higher-dimensional chord space.

# Formal Definition
Voice leading describes the mapping between the notes of successive chords, specifying how each note moves to a note in the following chord. The theory of voice leading, pioneered by Richard Cohn, uses geometry and mathematics to elucidate the connection between harmony and melody. In the spiral diagram framework, voice leadings are decomposed into combinations of transposition along the chord (t_x) and transposition along the scale (T_y), where efficient voice leadings result from these two operations nearly counteracting each other.

# Musical Context
Voice leading is the fundamental link between harmony and counterpoint. It operates differently at different levels of the quadruple hierarchy: at the chord-in-scale level, it connects successive harmonies; at the scale-in-aggregate level, it connects keys (modulation). The book argues that counterpoint is not fundamentally about avoiding parallels or balancing independent lines, but about understanding the interdependence between harmonic and melodic forces.

# Common Confusions
- Voice leading in rock is partially *constructed by the listener* from strummed chords, unlike classical music where it is embodied in notation
- Efficient voice leading is not always stepwise in the chromatic sense -- it preserves chordal-step distance
- Surface voices (concrete melodies) must be distinguished from abstract voices (scale degrees or melodic slots)
