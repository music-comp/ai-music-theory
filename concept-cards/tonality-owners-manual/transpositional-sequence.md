# Quick Definition

A sequence where all transposition arrows have the same subscript and do not cross: each voice retains its own melodic material and is transposed by the same amount. On the dyadic spiral, transpositional sequences result from an even number of basic voice-leading steps.

# Core Definition

In a transpositional sequence, the Taneyev arrows are all parallel (uncrossed) with identical transposition labels (p. 156). The entire block of music shifts by the same interval at each repetition, preserving all internal relationships exactly. These sequences are always harmonically stable (producing transpositionally related blocks regardless of cell content) and "typically occur in explicitly parallel passages (e.g., fauxbourdon) or else at the beginning of longer sequential units" (p. 166). They are "public knowledge, found in virtually every composer's work and discussed in virtually every harmony textbook" (p. 188).

# Key Properties

1. All arrows parallel (uncrossed) with identical transposition labels
2. Each voice retains its melodic material
3. Always harmonically stable
4. Simple period = grand period (no permutation)
5. On the dyadic spiral, results from even number of steps
6. The most familiar type of sequence

# Construction / Recognition

## To Construct:
1. Write a cell (block of music)
2. Transpose all voices by the same interval
3. Repeat

## To Recognize:
1. Check that all voices transpose by the same amount
2. Verify no voice exchange or permutation
3. On the dyadic circle, verify even number of steps

# Context & Application

Transpositional sequences contrast with canonic sequences (crossed arrows) and contrary-motion sequences (different transpositional subscripts). At the bar level, the Pathetique episode's canonic sequence is transpositional (each bar transposes down by step). Fauxbourdon is the paradigmatic example. Marianna Martines's ascending-step sequence (Figure 4.1.3, top) is a standard three-voice instance.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming any sequence with transposition is transpositional
  **Correction**: Transpositional specifically requires all voices transposing by the same amount with no permutation

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Conflating transpositional with "real" sequence
  **Clarification**: A transpositional sequence preserves exact intervals; a "tonal" sequence adjusts for the diatonic scale but the concept overlaps significantly
