# Quick Definition
A sequence where all transposition arrows have the same subscript and do not cross: each voice retains its own melodic material and is transposed by the same amount. The most familiar type of sequence.

# Formal Definition
In a transpositional sequence, the Taneyev arrows are all parallel (uncrossed) with identical transposition labels. This means the entire block of music shifts by the same interval at each repetition, preserving all internal relationships exactly. On the dyadic spiral, transpositional sequences result from an even number of basic voice-leading steps (no chordal transposition). Transpositional sequences are always harmonically stable (producing transpositionally related blocks regardless of cell content) and typically occur in explicitly parallel passages or at the beginning of longer sequential units.

# Musical Context
Transpositional sequences are "public knowledge, found in virtually every composer's work and discussed in virtually every harmony textbook." They are the most straightforward type of repetition. At the bar level, the *Pathetique* episode's canonic sequence is a transpositional sequence moving each voice down by step. Transpositional sequences contrast with canonic sequences (crossed arrows) and contrary-motion sequences (different transpositional subscripts).

# Common Confusions
- A transpositional sequence is not just any sequence with transposition; it specifically requires that all voices transpose by the same amount with no voice exchange or permutation
- On the dyadic circle, transpositional sequences use an even number of steps, while canonic sequences use an odd number (without voice exchanges)
