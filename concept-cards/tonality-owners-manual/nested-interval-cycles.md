# Quick Definition

A circular nesting of diatonic interval cycles -- fifths containing thirds containing steps -- each contained within its predecessor, forming a musical "ouroboros" that generates the basic voice leadings for functional harmony's primary sonorities.

# Core Definition

A sequence of falling fifths can be perturbed to return to its starting point after seven notes, the final interval tempered to a diminished "near fifth." These falling fifths can be halved to form a cycle of descending thirds, which in turn split into steps, which can themselves be factored into descending fifths -- "a circular nesting of interval cycles each contained within its predecessor, a musical *ouroboros*" (p. 316, Figure 7.2.1). Because the diatonic scale has seven notes (a prime number), every "maximally even" chord is a near interval cycle. The layers can be rearranged to produce the basic voice leadings for functional harmony's primary sonorities: fifths, thirds, triads, and seventh chords (Figure 7.2.3). The tertian layer reformulates the descending-thirds grammar at the pitch-class level (Figure 7.2.4).

# Prerequisites

- **Descending-thirds arrangement** -- The harmonic grammar that the nested cycles mathematically underpin

# Key Properties

1. Falling fifths subdivide into descending thirds, which subdivide into steps, which factor back into fifths
2. The seven-note diatonic scale permits this infinite bifurcation because 7 is odd
3. Each layer generates "maximally even" diatonic chords linked by basic voice leadings (Figure 7.2.3)
4. The structure allows composers to titrate the degree of sequential repetition in their music
5. Some functionality is sequential only at the abstract pitch-class level; other passages are overtly sequential

# Construction / Recognition

## To Trace the Nested Cycles:
1. Start with a cycle of descending diatonic fifths (e.g., C--F--B--E--A--D--G--C)
2. Subdivide each fifth into two descending thirds (e.g., C--A--F--D--B--G--E--C)
3. Subdivide each third into two descending steps
4. Note that the steps can themselves be factored into descending fifths, completing the circle
5. In a piece, look for these levels manifesting at different structural levels simultaneously

# Context & Application

The nested structure explains how functional music can move seamlessly between monodic, dyadic, and triadic logics (p. 319). A single piece can present descending steps on the melodic surface, embellishing descending thirds in the abstract progression of pitch classes, mostly grouped into descending-fifth triadic progressions. "The beauty of this music is its multivalence: it is not based on fifths or thirds or steps but rather on a delicate balance of all three" (p. 319).

The structure also suggests a historical path from simpler protofunctionality to the sophisticated system of the eighteenth century, with functional tonality exploiting the close harmonic relationship of third-related triads to expand I--IV--V into I--vi--IV--ii--vii^o^--V (p. 317).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming the nested structure means functional harmony is derived from pure mathematical necessity
  **Correction**: "The chain of reasoning we have been following is not one of necessity, but rather possibility" (p. 320)

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the ouroboros means functional tonality is uniquely "natural"
  **Clarification**: Retrofunctional progressions based on ascending thirds are equally natural and equally suited to human music-making (p. 321)

- **Confusion**: Assuming listeners hear all levels simultaneously
  **Clarification**: The nesting is mathematical; listeners do not necessarily perceive all levels at once, though composers can bring different levels to the surface
