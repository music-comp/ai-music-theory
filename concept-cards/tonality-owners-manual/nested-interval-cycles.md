# Quick Definition
A mathematical structure in which diatonic interval cycles (fifths, thirds, steps) nest within each other like Russian dolls, each layer being half the previous one, forming a musical "ouroboros."

# Formal Definition
A sequence of falling fifths, perturbed so that it returns to its starting point after seven notes (the last interval tempered to a diminished fifth), can be successively bisected: fifths split into thirds, thirds into steps, and steps back into fifths -- a circular nesting of interval cycles each contained within its predecessor (Figure 7.2.1). Because the diatonic scale has seven notes (a prime number), every "maximally even" chord is a near interval cycle, and every interval cycle can be extended to form a maximally even chord. The layers can be rearranged to produce basic voice leadings for all primary functional sonorities (Figure 7.2.3).

# Musical Context
This structure allows composers to titrate the degree of sequential repetition: some functionality is not at all sequential on the surface (regularity evident only at the abstract level of pitch-class content), while other passages feature overt sequential structure with voices articulating the descending-third chain at various levels. The nested cycles connect monodic, dyadic, and triadic logics within functional music.

# Examples
- Figure 7.2.1: Nested interval cycles from fifths to thirds to steps.
- Figure 7.2.2: Four levels of the pattern in the first A major prelude from *The Well-Tempered Clavier* -- steps inside thirds inside fifths inside steps.
- Roy Orbison's "Oh, Pretty Woman" (Figure 7.2.6): All three levels present simultaneously -- descending steps (melodic surface), descending thirds (pitch-class content), and descending fifths (triadic progressions).

# Common Confusions
- The nested structure does not mean functional tonality is a "natural language" derived from acoustic properties: it is *one* manifestation of *one* set of affordances. Retrofunctional progressions (ascending thirds) are equally natural.
- The nesting is mathematical, not perceptual: listeners do not necessarily hear all levels simultaneously.
