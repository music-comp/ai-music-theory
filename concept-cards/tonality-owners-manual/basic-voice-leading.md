# Quick Definition
The minimal voice leading connecting each point on a spiral diagram to its nearest neighbor, produced by single-step motion along the circle. For diatonic thirds, the descending basic voice leading moves the root down by third and the third up by step (T_-4 t_1).

# Formal Definition
For any n-note chord in any m-note scale, the basic voice leading is the voice leading generated by single-step clockwise motion on the spiral diagram. It combines transposition along the scale (T_-(m-n)) with transposition along the chord (t_(n-1)). For 2-in-7 (diatonic dyads): T_-4 t_1, moving root down by third, third up by step. For 3-in-7 (diatonic triads): T_-5 t_2, moving root down by step, lowering center of gravity by 1/3. The ascending basic voice leading is the inverse (counterclockwise motion).

# Musical Context
The basic voice leading is the atomic move of diatonic counterpoint. Repeatedly applying it cycles through all inversions of the chord type before returning to the starting configuration transposed by octave. In two-voice counterpoint, the descending basic voice leading generates descending-fifth sequences, while the ascending form generates ascending-fifth patterns. The passing tone often inserted into the melodic third creates a resolving tritone (^6-^7-^1 against ^4-^3), linking contrapuntal and harmonic logic.

# Common Confusions
- "Basic" does not mean simple or trivial; it means the minimal generating motion on the spiral diagram
- The same abstract structure (T_-4 t_1) applies to all two-note intervals in all seven-note scales, though the concrete intervals differ
- The ascending and descending forms have different triadic embeddings and different functional implications
