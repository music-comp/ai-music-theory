# Quick Definition

The minimal voice leading connecting each point on a spiral diagram to its nearest neighbor, produced by single-step motion along the circle. For diatonic thirds, the descending basic voice leading moves the root down by third and the third up by step (T_{-4}t_1).

# Core Definition

For any *n*-note chord in any *m*-note scale, the basic voice leading is generated by single-step clockwise motion on the spiral diagram. It combines transposition along the scale (T_{-(m-n)}) with transposition along the chord (t_{(n-1)}). For 2-in-7 (diatonic dyads): T_{-4}t_1, moving root down by third, third up by step. For 3-in-7 (diatonic triads): T_{-5}t_2, with root descending by one step, lowering the center of gravity by 1/3 (p. 113). For 4-in-7 (diatonic seventh chords): one clockwise step produces descending-third progressions (T_{-2}t_1); two steps produce descending fifths (T_{-4}t_2, p. 138). The ascending basic voice leading is the inverse (counterclockwise motion). Repeatedly applying the basic voice leading cycles through all inversions before returning to the starting configuration transposed by octave (p. 98).

# Key Properties

1. Connects each point on the spiral to its nearest clockwise neighbor
2. Combines scale transposition with chord transposition: T_{-(m-n)}t_{(n-1)}
3. Generates all other voice leadings when iterated (odd iterations transpose along chord, even do not)
4. The same abstract structure applies to any chord in any scale with relabeled note names
5. Ascending and descending forms have different triadic embeddings and functional implications
6. The passing tone inserted into the melodic third creates a resolving tritone (6-7-1 against 4-3)

# Construction / Recognition

## To Construct:
1. Identify your chord type and scale (e.g., third in diatonic = 2-in-7)
2. On the spiral diagram, move one step clockwise from your starting chord
3. Calculate: T_{-(m-n)} combined with t_{(n-1)}
4. The result shows how each voice moves

## To Recognize:
1. Look for two-voice passages where one voice moves by step and the other by third (for dyads)
2. For triads, look for single-step descent in one voice with the other two held or moved minimally
3. Check if the voice leading connects transpositionally related chords

# Context & Application

The basic voice leading is the atomic move of diatonic counterpoint. In two-voice counterpoint, the descending form generates descending-fifth sequences while the ascending form generates ascending-fifth patterns. The passing tone often inserted into the melodic third creates a resolving tritone, linking contrapuntal and harmonic logic. In the Beethoven development of Op. 2 no. 1, superimposing basic voice leadings for thirds and fifths produces the ubiquitous four-voice descending-fifth sequence (Figure 3.3.2, p. 108). The basic voice leading for fifths can be embedded in fauxbourdon 7-6 sequences, incomplete seventh chord sequences, and full descending-fifth seventh chord sequences (Figure 3.3.3, p. 108).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming the basic voice leading for thirds produces the same intervals as for fifths
  **Correction**: The concrete intervals differ (third/step vs. fifth/step) even though the abstract structure (T_{-4}t_1) is the same

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking "basic" means simple or trivial
  **Clarification**: "Basic" means the minimal generating motion on the spiral diagram; it is the fundamental building block from which all other voice leadings derive

- **Confusion**: Conflating ascending and descending forms
  **Clarification**: They have different triadic embeddings and different functional implications; the descending form produces authentic resolutions while the ascending form can produce plagal root motion with authentic tritone resolution
