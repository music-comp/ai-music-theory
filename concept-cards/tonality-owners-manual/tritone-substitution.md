# Quick Definition

Replacing a chord with one a tritone away -- maximally distant on the diatonic scale (distance 4) and essentially absent from the rock repertoire, though used as a learned convention in jazz.

# Core Definition

Tritone-related triads are "four steps apart" on the diatonic distance scale, "the maximum possible distance" (p. 55). The Rolling Stone corpus contains "not a single tritone progression" between major triads (footnote 12, p. 56). This near-total absence "strongly supports the diatonic distance model" (inferred from p. 56). Some styles like heavy metal "emphasize these diatonically distant progressions precisely for their ominous and unusual quality" -- what Huron calls "contratonal" music (footnote 9). In jazz, tritone substitution overrides the diatonic preference using shared guide tones.

# Prerequisites

- **Diatonic Distance** -- Tritone relations represent maximum diatonic distance

# Key Properties

1. Maximum diatonic distance (4 steps on the circle of fifths)
2. Essentially absent from the rock corpus
3. Used in jazz as a learned convention (shared guide tones)
4. Heavy metal uses tritone relations for "contratonal" effect
5. Maximum diatonic distance does not mean maximum voice-leading distance

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify:
1. Check if two chord roots are a tritone (6 semitones) apart
2. In rock: absence is the norm; presence signals unusual harmonic language
3. In jazz: typically replaces a dominant seventh (e.g., Db7 for G7)

# Context & Application

The absence of tritone progressions in rock, combined with the prevalence of fifth and step progressions, provides strong empirical support for the diatonic distance model of rock harmony.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Thinking tritone substitution is natural for all styles
  **Correction**: In rock, tritone progressions are avoided; in jazz, they are a learned convention

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking maximum diatonic distance means maximum voice-leading distance
  **Clarification**: Tritone-related triads can still be connected by fairly efficient voice leading; the rarity reflects a diatonic norm
