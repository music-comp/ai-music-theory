# Quick Definition
Long-range melodic motion through the notes of a tonic triad, reconceived not as a generic process independent of functional harmony but as a careful arrangement of schematic building blocks that exploit the fundamental geometry of musical possibility.

# Formal Definition
The quadruple hierarchy underwrites a collectional analysis of arpeggiation more precise than Schenkerian antecedents. Rather than positing arpeggiation as an abstract process existing prior to functional conventions, Tymoczko shows that large-scale arpeggiation is achieved through specific arrangements of familiar voice-leading schemas -- ascending sequences of thirds and sixths, Quiescenza patterns, fauxbourdon ROTO variants -- that themselves exploit the geometry of chordal and scalar space. The strategic goal (e.g., ascent from root to fifth) is achieved using familiar dyadic and seventh-chord voice-leading schemas while remaining consistent with functional-harmonic norms.

# Musical Context
This reconception challenges the Schenkerian view that arpeggiation is a basic structural operation. Instead, it is an emergent property of well-chosen schematic material. This distinction matters because it grounds long-range melodic structure in the same voice-leading geometry that governs local harmonic practice, rather than requiring a separate metaphysical framework.

# Examples
- Mozart's K.284, I: two ascending arpeggiations along the tonic triad D-F-sharp-A, the first through ascending sequences of thirds and sixths, the second through contrary-motion Quiescenza and fauxbourdon ROTO (Figure 9.1.5, pp. 410-411)
- The introductory fanfare of K.284: near sequence of falling thirds that sounds iconic without implying specific harmonies (p. 411)

# Common Confusions
- Arpeggiation is not a primitive operation but an emergent product of schematic arrangement
- The distinction from Schenkerian arpeggiation is not merely terminological -- it changes how we understand the relationship between local and large-scale structure
- Arpeggiation through a triad can be achieved by many different schematic paths, each with its own harmonic implications
