# Quick Definition
The spiral diagram for two-note chords (dyads) in diatonic space, where imperfect consonances (thirds and sixths) are arranged around a circle with seven equally spaced points, and neighboring points are connected by the basic voice leading.

# Formal Definition
The 2-in-7 spiral diagram represents all diatonic dyads. Since the chord has two notes, the line of transposition winds twice around the circle; since the diatonic scale has seven notes, there are seven equally spaced points. Sliding along the spiral transposes along the scale; looping around transposes along the chord, turning thirds into sixths and vice versa. Single-step clockwise motion from C to F combines T_-4 and t_1, producing the descending basic voice leading. The spiral can be collapsed into a circle since there is exactly one chord at every angular position.

# Musical Context
The dyadic circle is the foundation for understanding two-voice counterpoint geometrically. Fifth-related dyads are literally adjacent on the circle, explaining why two-voice music is suffused with fifth-progressions even when roots are not explicit compositional objects. The circle also generates common galant schemas: the converging cadence, Fonte (descending fifths), Monte (ascending steps), and Quiescenza all correspond to short-distance paths on this graph.

# Common Confusions
- The dyadic "circle of fifths" arises from contrapuntal geometry, not from acoustics or the circle of fifths connecting scales -- these are three independent phenomena
- The diagram represents any two-note interval in any seven-note scale, not just thirds and sixths specifically
