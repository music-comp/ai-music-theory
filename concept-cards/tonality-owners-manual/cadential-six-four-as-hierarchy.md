# Quick Definition
The understanding that the cadential six-four chord (I64) is hierarchically dependent on the following dominant, so that progressions like ii6-I64-V are understood as ii6-(I64=>V), with the six-four embellishing rather than replacing the dominant.

# Formal Definition
Though Tymoczko argues that functional harmony is largely nonhierarchical, the cadential six-four is a clear exception: progressions like V65/V-I64-V or vi-I64-V move from the initial chord to the final dominant by way of a subsidiary or decorative six-four. This brings otherwise anomalous progressions under standard functional principles. The view is supported by the parallel idiom V-IV64-I, where a second-inversion triad decorates the final tonic, symbolized V-(IV64=>I). Secondary dominants provide another limited example of harmonic hierarchy, though their scope is more restricted than often claimed.

# Musical Context
This represents one of the few cases where Tymoczko endorses genuine harmonic hierarchy in functional music. It provides a bounded alternative to Schenkerian claims about wholesale prolongation, acknowledging that some chords genuinely embellish others while insisting this is limited rather than pervasive.

# Common Confusions
- This limited hierarchical embedding does not generalize to all of harmony -- it is specific to certain well-defined idioms
- Secondary dominants provide another example of limited hierarchy, but their scope is more restricted than the language analogy suggests: any secondary dominant could signal either a momentary digression or a genuine key change
- "Centricity is sticky" -- it changes more slowly than harmonies, but this does not mean chords "represent" or "prolong" other chords in general
