# Quick Definition

The complex interaction between grouping structure, harmonic grammar, and double transposition in classical sentence form, where the demands of thematic parallelism (I-V answered by V-I) produce surface melodies that ascend by step along a descending chordal background.

# Core Definition

In the sentence form's fundamental default, a melodic fragment harmonized by I-V is answered by V-I using double transposition T_x t_y. If the I-V statement uses descending voice leading (V just below I in chord space), the response will typically use descending voice leading from V to I (I just below V). Together, the sentence traverses a loop in chord space. The requirement of motivic similarity ensures that "motives ascend by step along a descending chordal background, producing scalar motion at the surface as a byproduct of thematic parallelism and voice-leading geometry" (synthesized from pp. 444-446). This represents "one of the most complex interactions among the musical systems" described in Chapter 9.

# Key Properties

1. I-V statement is answered by V-I through double transposition
2. The sentence traverses a complete loop in chord space
3. Surface voices move contrary to underlying voice leading, ascending by chordal step as chords descend
4. Scalar surface motion is a byproduct of thematic parallelism, not an independent melodic goal
5. The circular journeys through triadic space acquire rhetorical significance through sentence conventions

# Construction / Recognition

## To Analyze Sentence-Form Double Transposition:
1. Identify the I-V statement and its double-transposition response
2. Map the journey through chord space -- does it form a loop?
3. Check whether surface voices ascend while the background descends
4. Verify that the scalar motion results from parallelism rather than independent melodic planning

# Context & Application

The concept shows how functionality's "circular journeys through triadic space" (as opposed to more efficient modal voice leadings) acquire rhetorical significance through sentence-form conventions. A single phrase can "simultaneously exhibit properties of sentence, period, and sequence depending on which parameter is foregrounded" (synthesized from pp. 441-442).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating the scalar surface motion as an independent melodic goal
  **Correction**: It is a byproduct of thematic parallelism operating within voice-leading geometry

- **Error**: Assuming sentence, period, and sequence are mutually exclusive categories
  **Correction**: A single phrase can exhibit properties of all three depending on which parameter is foregrounded

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking this analysis requires Schenkerian concepts like prolongation
  **Clarification**: The analysis uses standard scalar discourse at an unusual hierarchical level -- no prolongation or register transfer is needed

- **Confusion**: Thinking circular chord-space journeys are unique to sentences
  **Clarification**: They are characteristic of functional tonality generally, but acquire rhetorical significance through sentence conventions
