# Quick Definition
The complex interaction between grouping structure, harmonic grammar, and double transposition in classical sentence form, where the demands of thematic parallelism (I-V answered by V-I) produce surface melodies that ascend by step along a descending chordal background.

# Formal Definition
In the sentence form's fundamental default, a melodic fragment harmonized by I-V is answered by V-I using double transposition T_x t_y. If the I-V statement uses descending voice leading (V just below I in chord space), the response will typically use descending voice leading from V to I (I just below V). Together, the sentence traverses a loop in chord space. This requirement of motivic similarity ensures that motives ascend by step along a descending chordal background, producing scalar motion at the surface as a byproduct of thematic parallelism and voice-leading geometry.

# Musical Context
This represents one of the most complex interactions among the musical systems described in Chapter 9. The circular journeys through triadic space that are characteristic of functionality (as opposed to more efficient modal voice leadings) acquire rhetorical significance through the conventions of sentence form, with the first half posing a musical question answered by the second.

# Examples
- Mozart's K.282, II: surface voices move contrary to underlying voice leading, ascending by chordal step as chords descend (Figure 9.7.3, pp. 445-446)
- The sentence form default: I-V statement answered by V-I, traversing a complete loop in chord space (Section 9.7, pp. 444-446)
- Beethoven's Op. 31, no. 3, I, second theme: simultaneously interpretable as period, cadential structure, or sequence, depending on which musical parameter is emphasized (Figure 9.6.7, pp. 441-442)

# Common Confusions
- The scalar surface motion in sentence forms is not an independent melodic goal but a byproduct of thematic parallelism operating within voice-leading geometry
- A single phrase can simultaneously exhibit properties of sentence, period, and sequence depending on which parameter is foregrounded
- The analysis makes no reference to Schenkerian concepts like prolongation or register transfer -- it uses standard scalar discourse at an unusual hierarchical level
