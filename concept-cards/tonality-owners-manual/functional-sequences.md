# Quick Definition
Repeating harmonic patterns transposed diatonically at regular intervals, forming one of the three independent subsystems of functional harmony alongside harmonic cycles and fauxbourdon.

# Formal Definition
Functional sequences are classified by their interval of transposition from one grand period to the next. For a given diatonic interval, there are four possible two-chord sequences. Sequences typically move by small intervals with descent preferred over ascent. From Corelli to Mozart, descending-third motion rises substantially (to almost a quarter of Mozart's sequences). The most common sequences feature descending-fifth progressions repeating at regular intervals. Almost all common functional sequences feature descending fifths; the main exceptions are pure descending thirds and the Pachelbel sequence (D4A2), where the engine of repetition is deceptive rather than authentic.

# Musical Context
Sequences and harmonic cycles form a continuum rather than separate categories. Nonsequential harmony is itself quasi-sequential -- an oblique reworking of the circle of descending thirds. Sequences provide composers with a way to make the latent sequential structure of functional harmony explicit on the musical surface.

# Common Confusions
- The asymmetry between ascending and descending sequences is harmonic, not merely linear: the ascending 5--6 (D3A4) is common while its retrograde (descending 6--5, A3D4) is virtually unknown, even though both are equally useful contrapuntally.
- Most "fifth sequences" are actually step sequences in which the bass alternates ascending fourths and descending fifths.
