# Quick Definition
Tymoczko's preference for "thin" uses of music-theoretical terms involving minimal presuppositions, as opposed to "thick" definitions loaded with methodological commitments.

# Formal Definition
Thin terminology uses words with minimal theoretical baggage: "D phrygian" means "music that generally uses the two-flat diatonic collection while articulating D as its tonal center." Roman-numeral labels like "I^6" mean "scale degrees ^1, ^3, and ^5 with ^3 in the bass," carrying no presumptions about roots or inversional equivalence. Thick terminology, by contrast, bakes theoretical commitments into definitions -- as when "authentic cadence" is defined to *require* a root-position dominant, or "modality" to presuppose reciting tones.

# Musical Context
The insistence on thick terminology has the effect of "balkanizing us into distinct linguistic communities," with historicists, Formenlehre theorists, Schenkerians, and empirical musicologists each cultivating their own terminological gardens. Tymoczko argues that simpler terms are better suited to a slowly evolving musical practice in which stylistic boundaries are inherently fuzzy. This connects to the philosophical discussion of Wittgenstein on the difficulty of defining "game" -- conceptual boundaries are often vague by nature.

# Examples
- "Modality" (thin): largely diatonic music with a wide range of possible tonal centers. "Modality" (thick): presupposes reciting tones, plagal/authentic distinction, specific modal finals
- "I^6" (thin): scale degrees 1, 3, 5 with 3 in bass. "I^6" (thick): presupposes a root, inversional equivalence, and specific harmonic function
- Caplin's insistence that "authentic cadence" *must* involve a root-position dominant lasting until the tonic arrival (thick)
- Hepokoski/Darcy's assertion that a "second theme" *must* be preceded by a "medial caesura" (thick)

# Common Confusions
- Thin terminology is not imprecise -- it is deliberately minimal to avoid presupposing contested claims
- The preference for thin terms is not anti-theoretical but anti-dogmatic: it keeps disputes about music from becoming disputes about language
- Acknowledging grey areas where concepts "fail to grip" is not a weakness but an honest response to the continuity of musical practice
