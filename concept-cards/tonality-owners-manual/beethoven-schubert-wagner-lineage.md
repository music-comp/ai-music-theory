# Quick Definition

The analytical thread connecting Beethoven's schematic language through Schubert's greater lyricism and formal flexibility to Wagner's more thoroughgoing rejection of schematic composition, tracing how Beethovenian techniques were transformed during the dissolution of the classical consensus.

# Core Definition

Beethoven's language is characterized by "limitation -- redeploying musical resources toward broader stretches of time" (synthesized from Chapter 10). Schubert continues Beethoven's "problematizing of sonata-form conventions" while enriching local vocabulary with greater lyricism: the Quartettsatz blurs transitions, medial caesuras, and essential expository cadences, suggesting "a genuine theoretical insight, that formal conventions and large-scale key-relations are less significant than one might think" (p. 505). Wagner goes further in the Lohengrin prelude, replacing Beethoven's schematic content with a focus on orchestration and large-scale formal processes, achieving a single formal trajectory using only two triads (A major and F-sharp minor) and contrary motion across five octaves.

# Prerequisites

- **The Ludwig** -- Beethoven's characteristic contrary-motion vocabulary that Schubert fuses with other schemas
- **Contrary-motion counterpoint** -- The technique that all three composers share

# Key Properties

1. Schubert fuses Ludwig with fauxbourdon ROTO in the Quartettsatz opening
2. Schubert's formal freedom is a theoretical insight, not compositional ineptitude
3. Wagner replaces schematic content with orchestration and registral processes
4. The Lohengrin prelude uses only two triads for a complete formal trajectory
5. The trajectory moves from schema-rich to schema-poor composition while preserving large-scale architecture

# Construction / Recognition

## To Trace the Lineage:
1. Identify Beethovenian schemas (Ludwig, contrary motion, sequences) in Schubert
2. Note how Schubert loosens or blurs classical formal conventions
3. In Wagner, look for Beethovenian techniques (contrary motion, registral expansion) operating without schematic content
4. Track the shift from harmonic complexity to orchestral/registral complexity as form-defining

# Context & Application

These analyses demonstrate that the concepts developed throughout the book -- the Ludwig, contrary-motion counterpoint, fauxbourdon, sequence-based reduction -- apply not just to Beethoven but to the broader trajectory of nineteenth-century music. The dissolution of the classical consensus involves expanding vocabulary while preserving command of large-scale architecture.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Interpreting Schubert's formal looseness as ineptitude
  **Correction**: It represents "a genuine theoretical insight, that formal conventions and large-scale key-relations are less significant than one might think" (p. 505)

- **Error**: Thinking Wagner simply abandons Beethoven's techniques
  **Correction**: He transforms them, using contrary motion and registral expansion with radically simplified harmonic content

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Assuming the lineage implies direct influence
  **Clarification**: The connection is analytical -- shared techniques and their transformation -- not necessarily biographical

- **Confusion**: Thinking the dissolution of classical consensus means loss of structural coherence
  **Clarification**: Large-scale architecture is preserved even as schematic vocabulary is simplified or replaced
