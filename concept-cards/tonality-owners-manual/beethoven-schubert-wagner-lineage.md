# Quick Definition
The analytical thread connecting Beethoven's schematic language through Schubert's greater lyricism and formal flexibility to Wagner's more thoroughgoing rejection of schematic composition, tracing how Beethovenian techniques were transformed during the dissolution of the classical consensus.

# Formal Definition
Beethoven's language is characterized by limitation -- redeploying musical resources toward broader stretches of time. Schubert continues Beethoven's problematizing of sonata-form conventions while enriching local vocabulary with greater lyricism: the Quartettsatz blurs transitions, medial caesuras, and essential expository cadences, suggesting "a genuine theoretical insight, that formal conventions and large-scale key-relations are less significant than one might think" (p. 505). Wagner goes further in the Lohengrin prelude, replacing Beethoven's schematic content with a focus on orchestration and large-scale formal processes, achieving a single formal trajectory using only two triads and contrary motion.

# Musical Context
These analyses demonstrate that the concepts developed throughout the book -- the Ludwig, contrary-motion counterpoint, fauxbourdon, sequence-based reduction -- apply not just to Beethoven but to the broader trajectory of nineteenth-century music. Schubert fuses Ludwig and fauxbourdon ROTO (Quartettsatz opening); Wagner uses whole-octave contrary-motion expansion with only A major and F-sharp minor triads.

# Examples
- Schubert's Quartettsatz opening: fuses Ludwig with fauxbourdon ROTO, canonic round expanding to full quartet range (Figures 10.6.1-10.6.2, pp. 505-507)
- Schubert's three-part exposition structure: blurring classical formal conventions while maintaining schematic detail (pp. 504-505)
- Wagner's Lohengrin prelude: "a five-octave crescendo and decrescendo" using only two triads, contrary motion serving the form-defining function of orchestration (Section 10.7)

# Common Confusions
- Schubert's formal freedom is not ineptitude but a genuine theoretical insight about the relative importance of different musical parameters
- Wagner does not simply abandon Beethoven's techniques -- he transforms them, using contrary motion and registral expansion with radically simplified harmonic content
- The dissolution of the classical consensus involves expanding vocabulary while preserving command of large-scale architecture
