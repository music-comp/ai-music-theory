# Quick Definition
The higher-level goals that guide melodic composition, such as stepwise descents between tonic-triad notes, understood as strategic aims pursued within the constraints of a local harmonic grammar -- analogous to strategic goals in sports pursued within the rules of the game.

# Formal Definition
Melodic strategy reconceives the Schenkerian notion of long-range melodic structure in terms of higher-order compositional goals pursued within a sequential harmonic framework. If the harmonic grammar provides the "rules of the game," then phrase-level melodic templates are the "higher-level goals" (p. 409). Directed melodies often articulate the doubly parallel motion represented by spiral diagrams -- combining big-T transposition along the scale with little-t transposition along the chord. The resulting "background" provides melodic destinations between which surface voices dance.

# Musical Context
This concept allows Tymoczko to incorporate Schenkerian insights about long-range melodic coherence without adopting Schenkerian metaphysics about prolongation and representation. It applies across functional music from the classical period through Chopin and beyond.

# Examples
- Mozart's Symphony No. 40, K.550, opening: linear descent from D to F-sharp (^5 to ^7) while reserving root-position dominant for the phrase end (Figure 9.1.1, p. 408)
- Mozart's K.284, I: ascending arpeggiation along the tonic triad D-F-sharp-A, achieved through schematic building blocks (Figure 9.1.5, pp. 410-411)
- Chopin using descending melodic steps to generate effects ranging from diatonic modality to functional harmony to chromaticism (Section 9.3, pp. 419-425)

# Common Confusions
- Melodic strategy is not the same as Schenkerian prolongation -- it describes goals pursued within harmonic constraints, not a unified recursive structure
- A single melodic strategy (e.g., stepwise descent) can produce very different harmonic effects depending on the chordal and scalar environment
- The "slower melody" beneath the surface is a genuine phenomenon, but its significance lies in strategic organization rather than hierarchical embedding
