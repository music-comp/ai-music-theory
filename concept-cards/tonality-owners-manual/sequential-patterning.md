# Core Definition

Interest in sequential patterning is one of the multiple separate processes in the evolution of functional tonality. The mid-seventeenth-century enthusiasm for sequences represents an inflection point and a reason for highlighting Corelli (p. 257). Sequential patterns are already present in Renaissance music (Monteverdi's "Ohime" features ascending-fifth sequences, and the Prinner and Fenaroli schemas have sequential character), but they become increasingly prominent and standardized in the seventeenth century. The fifths-and-steps arrangement itself has sequential structure -- the V-I-V-I (7-1-2-3) pattern repeating in contrary motion as I-IV-I-IV (3-4-5-6) at the interval of a fifth (p. 261).

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify Sequential Patterning:
1. Look for repeated harmonic-melodic units at regular transposition intervals
2. Check whether the sequential structure is combined with functional progressions
3. Note whether sequences are becoming more standardized across a repertoire

# Context & Application

Sequential patterning connects protofunctionality to the mature baroque style, with the standardization of sequences representing one path through which harmonic vocabulary expanded beyond simple oscillations.
