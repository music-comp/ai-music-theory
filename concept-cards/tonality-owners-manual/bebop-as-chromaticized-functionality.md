# Quick Definition

The analysis of Bebop and later jazz as extensions of functional tonality that chromaticize the descending voice leading of the diatonic fifth-sequence, allowing roots to descend by semitone, tritone, or fifth -- making Bebop "more Romantic than modernist."

# Core Definition

"In Bebop and later jazz styles, the descending voice leading of the diatonic fifth-sequence is chromaticized, allowing roots to descend by semitone, tritone, or fifth" (p. 528). The 2-in-7 diatonic spiral diagram is replaced by the 2-in-12 chromatic spiral, which "can model both root progressions and upper-voice pairs connected by efficient voice leading" (p. 529). Tritone substitution extends the circle of dyadic fifths. Upper-voice pairs (third-seventh, fifth-ninth, etc.) "always slide downward" semitonally (p. 529). This represents "an ancient musical logic, part of a dialogue beginning before Ockeghem's 'generalized fauxbourdon' and still audible in Neil Young's 'Helpless'" (p. 529). "From this point of view, Bebop is more Romantic than modernist; for rather than rejecting functional convention, Bebop musicians complexified it, adding new stories to the building rather than razing it to the ground" (p. 529).

# Key Properties

1. Chromaticizes the diatonic fifth-sequence rather than abandoning it
2. The 2-in-12 diagram models both root progressions and upper-voice pairs
3. Tritone substitution extends the circle of dyadic fifths
4. Upper-voice fifths always slide downward in efficient voice leading
5. Represents continuation of "ancient musical logic" from Ockeghem through Neil Young
6. "More Romantic than modernist" -- complexification rather than rejection

# Construction / Recognition

## To Analyze Bebop Harmony Using This Framework:
1. Replace the 2-in-7 diatonic spiral with the 2-in-12 chromatic spiral
2. Map chord roots on the diagram (moving radially or by clockwise step)
3. Factor upper voices into fifth-related pairs (third-seventh, fifth-ninth)
4. Track the downward semitonal motion of upper-voice pairs
5. Note where roots descend by fifth, semitone, or tritone

# Context & Application

This analysis demonstrates that the book's theoretical framework extends beyond common-practice tonality to twentieth-century styles. The chromaticized space provides a unified model where both traditional and tritone-substituted progressions are represented as motions in the same geometrical space.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating Bebop as a rejection of functional harmony
  **Correction**: It is an extension and complexification, "adding new stories to the building rather than razing it to the ground"

- **Error**: Analyzing tritone substitution as an arbitrary harmonic device
  **Correction**: It reflects the voice-leading geometry of the 2-in-12 space, preserving efficient voice leading

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the 2-in-12 diagram applies only to root progressions
  **Clarification**: It models both root progressions and upper-voice pairs connected by efficient voice leading

- **Confusion**: Assuming Bebop's chromaticism breaks the link to earlier music
  **Clarification**: It continues the "ancient musical logic" of descending voice leading from Renaissance to present
