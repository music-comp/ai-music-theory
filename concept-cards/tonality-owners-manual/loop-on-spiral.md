# Quick Definition
A full circuit around the center of the spiral diagram, corresponding to transposition along the smaller chord-like collection, moving each note by one chordal step.

# Formal Definition
A loop around the center of the spiral corresponds to transposition along the chord (t_{+-1}). A clockwise loop moves notes downward by one chordal step; a counterclockwise loop moves notes upward by one chordal step. A double loop moves each voice by two steps, a triple by three, and so on. An n-fold loop (where n is the chord size) transposes each voice by octave and is equivalent to sliding along the complete spiral. Loops require "jumping rings" (leaving the spiral at some point). Paths that return to their starting point *without enclosing the center* leave each voice exactly where it began.

# Musical Context
Loops are central to understanding efficient voice leading between chords at different radial positions on the spiral. The most efficient voice leadings between two chords typically combine a loop with a slide in the opposite direction, so the two operations partially cancel each other. In rock music, the repeating chord progressions analyzed in Chapter 2 trace loops through chord space, with each complete loop producing a "Shepard-tone passacaglia" effect.

# Common Confusions
- "Loop" always means loops *enclosing the center of the spiral* unless otherwise noted -- paths returning to their start without enclosing the center do nothing
- A loop is not the same as going "around" the spiral; you must jump between rings
- The sign of the chordal transposition is *opposite* to the sign of the angular motion
