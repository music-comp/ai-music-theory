# Quick Definition
The operation that shifts musical material along a chord, a scale, or any other set of notes, using the collection as a "musical ruler" to measure distance in steps rather than semitones.

# Formal Definition
Transposition along a collection treats the collection as a slightly uneven ladder, measuring distance with ladder rungs (steps) rather than a fixed unit like semitones. The notation uses lowercase t_x for x-step transposition along the chord and uppercase T_y for y-step transposition along the scale, with boldface **T** for chromatic transposition. The two operations commute (t_x T_y = T_y t_x), subscripts add (t_x T_y combined with t_a T_b yields t_{x+a} T_{y+b}), and for an n-note chord in an o-note scale, t_n = T_o.

# Musical Context
Tymoczko identifies this as "the single most important concept in music theory." It spans the border between implicit and explicit knowledge -- composers may use it unknowingly as a byproduct of trying to minimize contrapuntal motion. The concept unifies melodic development (transposing along a scale), motivic development (transposing along a chord), and the construction of efficient voice leadings (combining both).

# Examples
- "Doe a deer" shifted up one rung along the diatonic ladder (T_1, Figure P2.1)
- Louis Armstrong's "West End Blues," m. 7: motive transposed down by chordal step along the Eb major triad (t_{-1}, Figure P2.2)
- Scarlatti K.3, mm. 3-6: right hand moves up by triadic step (t_1) while left hand ascends by two scalar steps (T_2), the two producing very similar results (Figure P2.3)
- Transposing motives along their own "intrinsic scale" (Figure P2.4)

# Common Confusions
- Transposition along a collection is NOT standard chromatic transposition -- it preserves distances *as measured by the collection*, not in semitones
- t_1 (one chordal step) and T_2 (two scalar steps) can produce very similar but not identical results, so t_{-1} T_2 "does very little"
- Musical identity is "up to us," determined by transformational properties rather than fixed by intrinsic constitution
