# Quick Definition
The aspect of musical knowledge that consists of internalized idioms and tendencies conforming to no general laws, comparable to knowledge of fashion or slang.

# Formal Definition
Particular or idiomatic musical knowledge refers to style-specific practices that are "a matter of knowing to do things *this* way and not another, for no particularly good reason." This is the domain of schema theory, deriving from Leonard Meyer by way of Robert Gjerdingen. It encompasses specific gestures, often tied to particular formal contexts, that cannot be derived from more general principles.

# Musical Context
This type of knowledge is revealed in the specific schemas and idioms that define musical styles. It explains why galant composers preferred I^6 over iii in certain contexts, why Bach used a specific cadential formula with the seventh resolving down by fourth, and why particular voice-leading patterns became associated with particular formal locations. It is the knowledge preserved by hands-on pedagogical traditions that resist systematic generalization.

# Examples
- Bach's cadential idiom where V^2 steps down to tonic without the seventh resolving by step (Figure 1.5.1)
- Gjerdingen's "Prinner" schema functioning as a precadential "riposte" in galant music but as a cadence in the Renaissance
- The galant preference for I^6 over iii in the nonsequential Romanesca (Figure 1.5.3)

# Common Confusions
- Particular knowledge is not opposed to general principles -- Tymoczko argues both coexist, with schemas originating in constraints inherent to musical materials
- The "deep Prinner" (a general voice-leading schema) should be distinguished from the "shallow Prinner" (a specific galant mannerism)
- Idiomatic knowledge is not arbitrary -- many schemas reflect optimal paths through geometrical space
