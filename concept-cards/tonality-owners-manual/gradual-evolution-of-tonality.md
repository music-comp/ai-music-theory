# Quick Definition

Tymoczko's central argument that functional harmony developed gradually over centuries through multiple independent processes, with no single point of origin. Like the evolution of dinosaurs into birds, there is no sharp boundary between modality and tonality.

# Core Definition

Functional harmony developed gradually over centuries, with no single point at which the process began or ended (p. 257). The evolution involved numerous separate processes at different times: restriction of harmonic vocabulary, modal homogenization, development of inversional equivalence, acceptance of seventh chords and second-inversion triads, standardization of modulatory pathways, interest in sequential patterning, melodic routines like the leading tone's resolution, and the 3-2-1 template (pp. 257-258). These processes are "largely independent, separable, and linked by no single logic" (p. 258). The proportion of functional progressions rises gradually from about 60% in Ockeghem to about 95% in Haydn/Mozart/Beethoven (Fig. 6.6.8).

# Key Properties

1. Gradual process over centuries, not sudden emergence
2. Multiple independent processes, not a single logic
3. Data shows gentle, consistent rise from early polyphony to classicism (Fig. 6.6.8)
4. Particularly dramatic in the century from 1550 to 1650
5. "Foolish to try to draw a sharp line between modality and functionality" (p. 295)
6. Development was largely implicit -- embodied routines, not explicit theories (p. 257)
7. Converging evidence from individual pieces, computational surveys, and human analyses

# Context & Application

Scholars have situated tonality's origin from the early 1400s to after Bach (p. 257). Tymoczko rejects all single-point-of-origin theories in favor of the gradualist view. The development was bottom-up, beginning with local details and gradually encompassing phrase structure, modulatory patterns, and formal templates (p. 291).

# Examples

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Identifying a single point of origin for functional tonality
  **Correction**: It developed gradually through multiple independent processes over centuries

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking functionality requires all processes to be in place simultaneously
  **Clarification**: "We find some varieties of functional tonality that are focused on primary triads, and others that make heavy use of seventh chords, secondary diatonic triads, or sequences" (p. 258)
