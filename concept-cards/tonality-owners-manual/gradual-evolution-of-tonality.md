# Quick Definition
Tymoczko's central thesis that functional tonality developed gradually over centuries through multiple independent processes, analogous to the evolution of dinosaurs into birds, rather than emerging suddenly at any single point.

# Formal Definition
The gradual evolution of tonality is the overarching framework for understanding the origin of functional harmony. Tymoczko rejects essentialist or saltationist accounts, arguing instead that the transition involved numerous separate processes occurring at different times: restriction of harmonic vocabulary, modal homogenization, development of inversional equivalence, acceptance of seventh chords, standardization of modulatory pathways, interest in sequential patterning, leading tone resolution, and development of melodic templates. These processes are largely independent, separable, and linked by no single logic. The transition was primarily implicit, involving composers learning patterns and idioms without necessarily recognizing them explicitly.

The paratonal century (1550-1650) shows particularly dramatic development.

# Common Confusions
- There is no single point at which the process began or ended. The gradual nature of the evolution means that any attempt to identify a single origin point is misguided.
- "Functional" does not mean a single thing. There are multiple functionalities: some feature IV, others ii; some emphasize sequences, others melodic templates.
- The development was largely implicit -- a matter of internalized routines rather than explicit theoretical thinking. Written theory typically trailed behind practice.
- The history of practice is part of the history of theory; studying verbal discourse alone cannot capture the development of musical grammar.
