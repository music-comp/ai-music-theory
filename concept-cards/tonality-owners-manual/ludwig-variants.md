# Quick Definition
The diverse forms of the Ludwig schema: ascending-fourth, ascending-fifth, descending-fifth, hybrid, octave-focused, and third-focused variants, each producing different harmonic progressions (V-I-IV, IV-I-V, V-I-vii-degrees/V) while sharing the fundamental contrary-motion dyadic logic.

# Formal Definition
Ludwig variants are classified along several dimensions. By bass motion: descending-fifth (V-I-IV or V-I-vii-degrees/V) and ascending-fifth (V/IV-IV-I or IV-I-V). By emphasis: octave-focused units (octave or unison as primary harmony) and third-focused units (imperfect consonances emphasized). By combination: hybrid Ludwigs combine ascending-fourth and ascending-fifth units. Nonsequential Ludwigs combine intervals that do not form exact sequences. The Ludwig flowchart (Figure 10.2.1) represents all consonances invertible at the octave, providing choices about whether to move by descending fourth or fifth at each step.

# Musical Context
These variants constitute Beethoven's personal vocabulary, appearing across his entire output from early sonatas to late quartets. The descending-fifth variant is more common in development sections (overt sequences), while ascending variants appear more often in thematic contexts. Hybrid forms allow Beethoven to create extended passages of continuous "Ludwiging" that drive registral expansion.

- Op. 10, no. 3, I: hybrid ascending-fifth Ludwig, V/IV-IV-I, first unit octave-focused (Figure 10.1.10, p. 462)
- Op. 31, no. 1, III: ascending-fifth Ludwig with lower-neighbor version (Figure 10.1.11, p. 463)
- Quiescenza variant: the Ludwig replaces Gjerdingen's oscillatory Quiescenza with more energetic expansion (Figure 10.1.5, p. 461)

# Common Confusions
- "Octave-focused" and "third-focused" refer to which dyad is emphasized within the Ludwig unit, not to the overall registral range
- The Ludwig as flowchart (Figure 10.2.1) contains all consonances invertible at the octave, meaning any passage of two-voice Ludwiging can have either voice doubled at the third
- The distinction between variants is analytical -- in practice, Beethoven moves fluidly between them within a single passage
