# Quick Definition

The diverse forms of the Ludwig schema: ascending-fourth, ascending-fifth, descending-fifth, hybrid, octave-focused, and third-focused variants, each producing different harmonic progressions while sharing the fundamental contrary-motion dyadic logic.

# Core Definition

Ludwig variants are classified along several dimensions. By bass motion: descending-fifth (producing V-I-IV or V-I-vii/V progressions) and ascending-fifth (producing V/IV-IV-I or IV-I-V). By emphasis: "octave-focused Ludwig units where the octave or unison is the primary harmony" versus "third-focused units that emphasize imperfect consonances" (p. 462). By combination: hybrid Ludwigs combine ascending-fourth and ascending-fifth units. The Ludwig flowchart (Figure 10.2.1) represents the Ludwig "not as a fixed idiom, a precomposed chunk of music waiting to be plugged into a composition, but as something malleable: not so much 'the Ludwig schema' as 'Ludwig-ing,' a particular kind of musical flow" (p. 465). This flowchart "contains all those consonances that are invertible at the octave" (p. 466), meaning any passage of two-voice Ludwiging can have either voice doubled at the third.

# Key Properties

1. Descending-fifth variants are common in development sections (overt sequences)
2. Ascending variants appear more often in thematic contexts
3. Octave-focused units emphasize perfect consonances; third-focused units emphasize imperfect ones
4. The Ludwig flowchart contains all consonances invertible at the octave
5. Any two-voice Ludwig passage can have either voice doubled at the third
6. Beethoven moves fluidly between variants within a single passage

# Construction / Recognition

## To Classify a Ludwig Variant:
1. Determine bass motion: descending-fifth, ascending-fifth, or hybrid
2. Identify whether primary harmonies are octaves/unisons (octave-focused) or thirds/sixths (third-focused)
3. Check if the pattern is sequential or nonsequential (combining ascending fourth with ascending fifth)
4. Note whether the variant replaces an existing schema (e.g., Quiescenza replaced by more energetic expansion)

# Context & Application

These variants constitute Beethoven's personal vocabulary, appearing across his entire output from early sonatas to late quartets. The Ludwig can replace Gjerdingen's oscillatory "Quiescenza" with "a more energetic expansion" (p. 461). When combined with parallel motion options, "contrary and parallel motion together provide 'a simple but comprehensive set of outer-voice defaults for functionally tonal music-making'" (p. 468).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating "octave-focused" and "third-focused" as referring to registral range
  **Correction**: They refer to which dyad is emphasized within the Ludwig unit, not overall range

- **Error**: Expecting a single fixed Ludwig schema
  **Correction**: The Ludwig is a flowchart of malleable options, not a fixed idiom

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the distinction between variants is rigid
  **Clarification**: In practice, Beethoven moves fluidly between variants within a single passage

- **Confusion**: Assuming Ludwig variants are unique to Beethoven
  **Clarification**: The patterns reflect basic contrapuntal geometry; Beethoven's uniqueness lies in systematic deployment
