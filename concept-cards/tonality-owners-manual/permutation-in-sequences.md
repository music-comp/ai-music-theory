# Quick Definition

The operation of reassigning melodic lines to different voices as a sequence repeats, analogous to a round moving material through the ensemble. Combined with transposition, permutation produces transposing rounds. Previous theorists' neglect of permutation caused misidentification of sequential units.

# Core Definition

Permutation moves melodic content through the ensemble without transposing; transposition moves through pitch without reassigning voices; combining both produces a transposing round (Figure 4.1.1, p. 155). In the Taneyev model, permutation is represented by crossed arrows. A sequence's permutation structure determines whether it is transpositional (no crossings), partially canonic (some), or fully canonic (all). "Previous theorists have often focused on the grand period to the exclusion of the simple period; in large part this is because they have neglected permutation as a sequence-generating operation" (p. 157). Geometry justifies including permutation: "any harmonically stable voice leading generates a sequence, and some of these permute their voices" (p. 165).

# Key Properties

1. Moves melodic content among voices without transposing
2. Represented by crossed arrows in Taneyev notation
3. Determines canonic structure (no, partial, or full canon)
4. Causes grand period to contain multiple simple periods
5. Neglect by previous theorists caused analytical difficulties
6. Geometrically justified: some harmonically stable voice leadings necessarily permute

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify:
1. Track where each voice's melodic content goes in the next unit
2. If content stays in same voice: no permutation (transpositional)
3. If content moves to a different voice: permutation (canonic)

# Context & Application

"Three Blinde Mice" uses pure permutation without transposition (Figure 4.1.3). Rounds used for standing-on-the-dominant are zero-transposition permutation patterns (p. 179). Including permutation "deepens our appreciation for the many functions of transformed repetition" (p. 179), connecting rounds with sequences under one framework.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Confusing permutation with voice exchange
  **Correction**: Permutation moves entire melodic lines between voices across units; voice exchange rearranges notes within a single chord

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking rounds and sequences are fundamentally different
  **Clarification**: Rounds are sequences with zero transposition and only permutation operating
