# Quick Definition
The operation of reassigning melodic lines to different voices as a sequence repeats, analogous to how a round moves material through the ensemble. Combined with transposition, permutation produces transposing rounds.

# Formal Definition
Permutation moves melodic content through the instrumental ensemble without transposing it; transposition moves it through pitch without reassigning voices; combining both produces a transposing round. In the Taneyev arrow model, permutation is represented by crossed arrows. A sequence's permutation structure determines whether it is transpositional (no crossings), partially canonic (some crossings), or fully canonic (all crossings). Previous theorists neglected permutation, causing them to misidentify sequential units by focusing on grand periods rather than simple periods.

# Musical Context
"Three Blinde Mice" (1609) uses permutation without transposition -- a pure round. The Bach G# minor WTC fugue combines transposition and permutation, with melodic material shifting voices while transposing down by step. Geometry justifies including permutation alongside transposition: any harmonically stable voice leading generates a sequence, and some of these necessarily permute their voices. The inclusion of permutation deepens appreciation for the many functions of transformed repetition, connecting rounds with sequences under one framework.

# Common Confusions
- Permutation is distinct from voice exchange; permutation moves entire melodic lines between voices across sequential units, while voice exchange rearranges notes within a single chord
- Rounds are not separate from sequences; they are sequences where transposition is zero and only permutation operates
