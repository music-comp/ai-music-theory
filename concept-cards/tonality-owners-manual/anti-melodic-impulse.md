# Quick Definition
Beethoven's distinctive tendency to build music from deliberately generic, almost proto-musical material rather than clear singable tunes -- subordinating local melodic interest to long-term formal processes and emphasizing larger shapes over local ornament and detail.

# Formal Definition
Where Mozart and Haydn frequently oriented music around clear and singable tunes, Beethoven had a fondness for material that was "almost deliberately generic" -- "sub- or proto-music, unrefined nuggets of musical ore" (p. 453). His harmony often consists of simple tonic-dominant alternations with fewer secondary diatonic triads than Bach or Brahms. This banality of themes is "inseparable from his focus on long-term formal processes, as if he deliberately subordinated local interest to long-term narrative" (p. 453). This places Beethoven as a kindred spirit to Xenakis and Penderecki, who emphasized larger shapes over local pitch detail.

# Musical Context
This concept is crucial for understanding Beethoven's aesthetic distance from his predecessors and why his music poses challenges for theories designed equally for Haydn, Mozart, and Beethoven. His self-conscious iconoclasm -- starting pieces in medias res, writing periods without root-position cadences, omitting the medial caesura, expanding the nonharmonic domain -- reflects an attitude that "music is fundamentally under our control, that we can change the rules when we want to" (p. 456).

# Examples
- The Waldstein's second theme: "deliberately featureless, singable but not worth singing," harmonized by a stock Pachelbel progression (Figure P10.3, p. 453)
- The Fifth Symphony's opening: obsessive focus on tonic and dominant for the first thirty-eight measures, with rhythmic variety compensating for harmonic limitation (Figure 10.4.3, p. 483)
- Beethoven's attitude summed up: "music with genuine philosophical content, implicitly humanist where so much theory inclines toward scholasticism" (p. 456)

# Common Confusions
- Anti-melodic does not mean anti-musical -- the reduction of local interest enables unprecedented large-scale coherence
- Motivic unity (recycling ideas throughout a piece) is different from what Tymoczko finds interesting about Beethoven -- the recurring cross-piece structures matter more than within-piece motive recycling
- Beethoven's simplicity is not a limitation but a deliberate aesthetic choice enabling new forms of musical continuity
