# Quick Definition

Beethoven's tendency to use deliberately generic, almost featureless thematic material -- "sub- or proto-music, unrefined nuggets of musical ore" -- subordinating local melodic interest to long-term formal processes and large-scale narrative.

# Core Definition

Where "Mozart and Haydn frequently oriented their music around clear and singable tunes, Beethoven had a fondness for material that was almost deliberately generic" (p. 453). Tymoczko describes this as "something like sub- or proto-music, unrefined nuggets of musical ore." The frequent banality of Beethoven's themes is "inseparable from his focus on long-term formal processes, as if he deliberately subordinated local interest to long-term narrative" (p. 453). This extends to harmony: "Beethoven's harmony often consists of simple tonic and dominant alternations, with many fewer secondary diatonic triads than in J. S. Bach or Brahms" (p. 453). Tymoczko hears Beethoven as "a kindred spirit" to Xenakis and Penderecki, "a composer emphasizing larger shapes over local ornament and detail" (p. 453).

# Key Properties

1. Themes are often deliberately generic or featureless
2. Local melodic interest is subordinated to long-term formal processes
3. Harmony tends toward primary-triad simplicity
4. The approach evokes gestural music that emphasizes trajectories over pitch detail
5. An atonal recomposition of the Waldstein exposition preserves "a remarkable amount of its formal clarity and expressive intensity" (p. 453)
6. "Atonal Mozart would just sound awful" (p. 453)
7. Beethoven's self-conscious iconoclasm includes starting pieces in medias res, omitting medial caesuras, expanding the nonharmonic domain

# Construction / Recognition

## To Recognize the Anti-Melodic Impulse:
1. Note themes that are singable but "not worth singing" (like the Waldstein second theme)
2. Check for stock harmonic progressions (e.g., Pachelbel progression)
3. Observe whether the formal interest lies in parameters other than pitch content
4. Listen for rhythm, register, and dynamics serving form-defining roles
5. Check whether motivic unity matters less than cross-piece structural patterns

# Context & Application

The anti-melodic impulse connects to the aesthetic distinction between beauty and sublimity. Beethoven's attitude is that "music is fundamentally under our control, that we can change the rules when we want to -- that we can, for want of a better phrase, take ownership of style itself. The result is music with genuine philosophical content, implicitly humanist where so much theory inclines toward scholasticism" (p. 456). This challenges Formenlehre theorists who apply schemas designed for Haydn and Mozart equally to Beethoven.

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 453): The Waldstein second theme -- "deliberately featureless, singable but not worth singing, and harmonized by a stock Pachelbel progression."

**Example 2** (p. 453): An atonal recomposition of the Waldstein exposition that preserves its formal clarity demonstrates that pitch content is not the primary carrier of formal meaning.

**Example 3** (pp. 451-452): Comparison of attack density in Waldstein exposition versus Mozart's first piano sonata showing Beethoven's wider rhythmic range and slower formal cycles.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Interpreting Beethoven's simple themes as evidence of limited invention
  **Correction**: The simplicity is deliberate, subordinating local interest to large-scale formal narrative

- **Error**: Analyzing Beethoven's themes primarily for pitch content
  **Correction**: The formal interest often lies in rhythm, register, dynamics, and large-scale trajectory

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking motivic unity is what makes Beethoven's music cohere
  **Clarification**: Tymoczko finds cross-piece structural patterns (the Ludwig, contrary motion) more interesting than within-piece motive recycling

- **Confusion**: Assuming anti-melodic means anti-musical
  **Clarification**: The reduction of local interest enables unprecedented large-scale coherence and expressive intensity
