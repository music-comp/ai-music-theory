# Quick Definition
Beethoven's characteristic technique of using contrary-motion sequences (especially the Ludwig) to progressively expand the registral distance between the hands, creating cumulative dramatic effect that serves as a pitch-domain analogue of the crescendo.

# Formal Definition
Registral expansion occurs when harmonic and thematic repetition continues an ongoing process of widening the gap between outer voices, rather than changing direction as other composers typically do. The Ludwig naturally produces this effect because its contrary-motion units push voices outward. In Beethoven, this unidirectionality is distinctive: "what makes these passages so characteristically Beethovenian is their unidirectionality, harmonic and thematic repetition continuing an ongoing process of registral expansion" (p. 467). Other parameters (dynamics, rhythm, orchestration) often reinforce this expansion.

# Musical Context
Registral expansion connects Beethoven's contrapuntal technique to his sublime aesthetic. It creates the dramatic gestural waves that define his style, functioning alongside crescendo, rhythmic acceleration, and orchestral thickening. Beethoven's predecessors like Clementi used similar patterns but with less directional consistency.

# Examples
- Eroica first movement, mm. 57-60: register expands by two octaves alongside full-orchestra crescendo and rhythmic acceleration (Figure 10.2.11, p. 471)
- Op. 2, no. 1, I, second theme: hybrid Ludwig expanding by three octaves, culminating in contrary-motion return to close position (Figure 10.1.6, p. 461)
- Op. 2, no. 2, I, mm. 25-31: five successive Ludwig units with left hand octave-displaced (Figure 10.2.3, p. 467)
- Tempest second theme: registral expansion by transposition along the dyad, t1 then t2 (Figure 10.3.8, p. 478)

# Common Confusions
- Registral expansion is not unique to Beethoven but his unidirectional use of it is distinctive -- other composers like Jacquet de La Guerre alternate directions
- The technique works through accumulation: individual Ludwig units are simple, but their sustained application creates unprecedented dramatic force
- The hands expand to the edges of Beethoven's piano at structurally significant moments, making the physical instrument part of the formal design
