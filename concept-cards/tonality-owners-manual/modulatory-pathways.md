# Quick Definition

The standardization of orderly progressions through secondary keys, identified as one of the separate processes constituting the development of functional tonality. In protofunctional music, modulation is rare, undirected, and sometimes haphazard.

# Core Definition

The standardization of modulatory pathways is one of the multiple separate processes in the development of functional tonality (p. 258). In protofunctional music, there is no notion of modulation as an orderly progression through secondary keys; the music rarely modulates, and sometimes does so in ways that seem undirected or haphazard, with pieces occasionally beginning in one key and ending in another (p. 269). Tymoczko focuses on local harmonic structure because pieces can sound functional even while eschewing conventional modulatory trajectories -- citing Coltrane's Giant Steps as combining local ii-V-I organization with a key scheme moving by major third (p. 290).

# Prerequisites

- **Protofunctionality** — The context in which modulation is initially absent

# Key Properties

1. One of multiple independent processes in tonality's development
2. Absent in protofunctional music (rare, undirected modulation)
3. Protofunctional pieces can begin in one key and end in another
4. Local functionality can exist without conventional modulation
5. Modulation standardizes later in tonality's development

# Construction / Recognition

## To Assess Modulatory Pathways:
1. Check whether modulations follow orderly trajectories (e.g., to closely related keys)
2. Note whether pieces maintain a consistent tonal center
3. Assess whether modulations seem directed or haphazard
4. Consider whether local functionality exists independently of modulatory structure

# Context & Application

The decision to focus on local harmonic structure is "motivated by my belief that pieces can sound functional even while eschewing conventional modulatory trajectories" (p. 290). Functionality began as a bottom-up process at the local level, only later encompassing modulatory patterns and formal templates (p. 291).

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 269): Protofunctional music rarely modulates and sometimes begins in one key and ends in another.

**Example 2** (p. 290): Coltrane's Giant Steps combines local ii-V-I with a major-third key scheme.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming functional music must have conventional modulatory trajectories
  **Correction**: Local functionality can exist independently of modulatory structure

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the absence of standard modulation disqualifies music as "functional"
