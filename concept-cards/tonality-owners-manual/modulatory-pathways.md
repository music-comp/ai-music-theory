# Quick Definition
The standardization of orderly progressions through secondary keys, which is one of several independent processes in the development of functional tonality and one of the later developments to emerge.

# Formal Definition
The standardization of modulatory pathways refers to the establishment of conventional routes through secondary keys (e.g., moving to the dominant key, the relative major/minor, the subdominant key) as expected features of tonal composition. In protofunctional music, there is no notion of modulation as an orderly progression through secondary keys; the music rarely modulates and sometimes does so in ways that seem undirected or haphazard, with pieces occasionally beginning in one key and ending in another.

# Musical Context
Tymoczko argues that the development of functional harmony was a bottom-up process beginning with local details and gradually encompassing larger timespans. Local harmonic structure precedes large-scale tonal planning. Even John Coltrane's Giant Steps combines local ii-V-I organization with a non-standard key scheme moving by major third. This view is consistent with psychological research questioning listeners' ability to track modulatory relations over moderate timespans.

# Examples
- In protofunctional music (Dalza, frottola), pieces sometimes begin in one key and end in another, with no sense of orderly modulation
- The paratonal century (1550-1650) sees the gradual standardization of modulatory conventions
- John Coltrane's Giant Steps as a modern example of functional local harmony with non-standard large-scale tonal planning

# Common Confusions
- Modulation is not a prerequisite for functional harmony. Music can sound functional while eschewing conventional modulatory trajectories.
- The standardization of modulatory pathways is one of the later developments in the emergence of functional tonality, not one of the earliest.
- Homophony and regular phrase structure are also somewhat secondary to the development of functionality; polyphonic and irregularly phrased music can be strongly functional.
