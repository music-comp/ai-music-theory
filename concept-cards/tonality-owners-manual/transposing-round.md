# Quick Definition
A sequence that combines both transposition and permutation, so that melodic lines simultaneously move through pitch space and through the instrumental ensemble.

# Formal Definition
A transposing round applies both transposition (moving material through pitch) and permutation (moving material among voices) at each repetition. In the Taneyev arrow model, this means crossed arrows with nonzero transposition labels. The bottom passage of Figure 4.1.1 transposes each line down while passing it to another voice. Ligeti's *Passacaglia Ungherese* makes a complete circle in the space of chromatic major thirds, its round structure producing registral stability within the canonic group while potentially creating contrary motion with non-canonic voices.

# Musical Context
Transposing rounds appear throughout Western music, from Renaissance canonic sequences to modern compositional techniques. They represent the full combination of the two basic structure-preserving transformations: transposition and permutation. Rounds without transposition (pure permutation) include "Three Blinde Mice" and Prinner/Fenaroli/standing-on-the-dominant patterns used to animate static harmony. The framework unifies seemingly disparate phenomena under a single model.

# Common Confusions
- A round without transposition is a special case (zero transposition); a sequence without permutation is another special case (identity permutation); the transposing round is the general case
- The term "round" here is more general than the familiar singing-round; it encompasses any situation where melodic material permutes among voices
