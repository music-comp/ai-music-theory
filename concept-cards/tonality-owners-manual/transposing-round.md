# Quick Definition

A sequence combining both transposition (moving material through pitch) and permutation (moving material among voices), so that lines simultaneously move through pitch space and the instrumental ensemble. The general case of which sequences and rounds are special instances.

# Core Definition

A transposing round applies both transposition and permutation at each repetition (Figure 4.1.1, p. 155). In the Taneyev arrow model, this means crossed arrows with nonzero transposition labels. A round without transposition (zero transposition) is a special case; a sequence without permutation (identity permutation) is another; the transposing round is the general case. Ligeti's *Passacaglia Ungherese* makes a complete circle in the space of chromatic major thirds (Figure 4.2.7, p. 163), its round structure producing registral stability within the canonic group. Rounds without transposition are commonly used to animate static harmony, as in Prinner, Fenaroli, and standing-on-the-dominant schemas (p. 178).

1. Combines transposition (pitch movement) with permutation (voice movement)

# Construction / Recognition

## To Construct:
1. Write a cell with multiple melodic lines
2. Assign each line a destination voice and a transposition level
3. Apply both operations simultaneously at each repetition

## To Recognize:
1. Check for both transposition and voice exchange at each repetition
2. Look for canonic imitation combined with sequential transposition

# Context & Application

Transposing rounds appear throughout Western music. Three-voice rounds dividing voices 2 + 1 are used to enliven static harmony, dating to the early sixteenth century (Figure 4.5.6, p. 178). Beethoven's Op. 10 no. 3, I, mm. 333ff presents a round where upper voices embed a two-voice canon in contrary motion with bass (Figure 4.5.7, p. 179). "Such rounds differ from standard sequences only in that their harmonies do not transpose" (p. 179).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating rounds and sequences as fundamentally different things
  **Correction**: They are special cases of the same general model; the transposing round unifies them

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Equating "round" with the familiar singing-round
  **Clarification**: The term encompasses any situation where melodic material permutes among voices
