# Quick Definition

Two efficient voice leadings between major triads related by major third: LP (**T**_4 t_{-1}) moves root down and fifth up by semitone while holding the third; PL (**T**_{-4} t_1) is the reverse.

# Core Definition

LP corresponds to outward radial motion on the 3-in-12 spiral: **T**_4 t_{-1}. For C major to E major: (C,E,G) -> (B,E,G#), "the LP voice leading of neo-Riemannian theory" (p. 50). PL is inward radial motion: **T**_{-4} t_1. For E major to C major: (G#,E,B) -> (G,E,C), "the PL voice leading" (p. 51). "All motions on the diagram" can be expressed as combinations of LP/PL (radial) and chromatic transposition (sliding) (p. 51). Tymoczko calls this "the major-third system" (footnote 4).

# Prerequisites

- **Radial Motion on Spiral** -- LP/PL are specific instances of radial motion

# Key Properties

1. LP = **T**_4 t_{-1}: root down by semitone, third holds, fifth up by semitone
2. PL = **T**_{-4} t_1: root holds, third up by semitone, fifth down by semitone (reverse of LP)
3. All radial motion on the major-triad spiral = LP or PL
4. Major-third-related triads share the same angular position on the spiral
5. Total voice motion sums to zero (one voice up, one down, one fixed)
6. Named from neo-Riemannian theory (L=Leading-tone exchange, P=Parallel)

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify:
1. Find two major triads related by major third
2. LP: the root of chord 1 moves down by semitone; the third holds; the fifth moves up by semitone
3. PL: the root holds; the third moves down by semitone (or up in reverse); the fifth moves down (or up in reverse)

# Context & Application

These voice leadings appear throughout Western music from Gesualdo (chromatic third progressions) to Schubert (major-third triadic shifts) to rock harmony. They arise from the near-symmetry of the major triad being only one semitone from the augmented triad.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Thinking LP and PL move the same notes
  **Correction**: LP moves root and fifth; PL moves third and fifth (different notes move in each)

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking major-third-related triads must be harmonically close
  **Clarification**: They are diatonically distant (distance 2) but contrapuntally close, which is why they sound both familiar and exotic
