# Quick Definition
Two efficient voice leadings between major triads related by major third: LP moves root down by semitone and fifth up by semitone (holding the third), while PL moves third down by semitone and fifth up by semitone (holding the root).

# Formal Definition
LP (combining "Leading-tone exchange" and "Parallel" operations from neo-Riemannian theory) corresponds to **T**_4 t_{-1}: transposing up four chromatic semitones and down one chordal step. For C major to E major: (C, E, G) -> (B, E, G#). PL is the reverse, **T**_{-4} t_1: transposing down four semitones and up one chordal step. For E major to C major: (G#, E, B) -> (G, E, C). On the spiral diagram, LP corresponds to outward radial motion and PL to inward radial motion.

# Musical Context
These voice leadings are fundamental to both neo-Riemannian theory and voice-leading geometry. They describe the connection between major-third-related triads (C-E, E-Ab, Ab-C), which are two units apart in diatonic distance but connected by very efficient voice leading due to the near-symmetry of the major triad. The LP/PL distinction corresponds to the direction of radial motion on the spiral. These progressions appear throughout Western music from Gesualdo to Schubert to rock.

# Common Confusions
- LP and PL are different voice leadings, not the same operation applied in different directions -- they move different notes
- The names derive from neo-Riemannian theory (L = Leading-tone exchange, P = Parallel) but Tymoczko reinterprets them geometrically
- Major-third-related triads are diatonically distant (distance 2) but contrapuntally close -- this is why they sound both familiar and exotic
