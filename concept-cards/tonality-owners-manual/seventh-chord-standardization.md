# Quick Definition

One of the several independent processes in the development of functional tonality: the broad acceptance of seventh chords as standard harmonic vocabulary, evolving from the Renaissance seventh (an irreducible suspension idiom) into fully acknowledged harmonic entities.

# Core Definition

The standardization of seventh chords is one of the multiple separate processes constituting the development of functional tonality (p. 258). Seventh chords evolved from the Renaissance seventh -- an irreducible suspension idiom appearing as early as Dufay (p. 215). They are "the inevitable result of attempting to reconcile the earlier practice with the philosophical view that nonharmonic tones must necessarily be decorative" (p. 220). The vii-dim-6 chord gradually gives way to V43 as the preferred dominant over bass degree 2, with the later chord inheriting the voice leading of its ancestor -- the "seventh" often ascends rather than resolving downward (pp. 280-281, Figs. 6.4.8-6.4.9).

# Key Properties

1. One of several independent processes in tonality's development
2. Evolved from Renaissance suspension idioms
3. The shift from vii-dim-6 to V43 occurs during the functional era
4. V43 inherits vii-dim-6's voice leading (seventh often ascends)
5. This continuity between modal and functional practice was missed by Dahlhaus and Bent

3. Note that V43 is described by thoroughbass pedagogues as optional embellishment of vii-dim-6

# Context & Application

Dahlhaus and Bent pointed to vii-dim-6--I (with ascending fourth scale degree) as proving functional concepts inapplicable to Renaissance music. Tymoczko argues this reveals continuity, not discontinuity: the voice-leading of the earlier chord persists into later practice (pp. 279-281).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating vii-dim as necessarily an incomplete V7
  **Correction**: Functional composers often treat vii-dim as an independent chord (p. 280)

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the ascending fourth scale degree in vii-dim-6--I proves the inapplicability of functional concepts to Renaissance music
  **Clarification**: This voice leading persists even in V43-I progressions in the nineteenth century, showing continuity not discontinuity (p. 281)
