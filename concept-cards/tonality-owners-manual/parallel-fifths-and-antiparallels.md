# Quick Definition
Parallel fifths are simultaneous fifth intervals moving in the same direction; antiparallels (or "hidden" fifths) occur when voices move in similar motion to a perfect fifth. Both are traditionally prohibited, but the rules are applied inconsistently across musical levels.

# Formal Definition
Parallel fifths occur when two voices maintain a perfect fifth interval while both move in the same direction. Antiparallel fifths (also called hidden, direct, or covered fifths) occur when two voices approach a perfect fifth by similar motion without both voices moving by step. These prohibitions are central to counterpoint pedagogy, but Tymoczko identifies deep inconsistencies in their application: suspensions regularly license parallel fifths at the harmonic-skeleton level, and some parallels are said to be permissible because they occur "only on the surface," while others are allowed because they occur "only in the harmonic skeleton."

# Musical Context
Pedagogues sometimes forbid practices that were merely somewhat uncommon, including consecutive strong-beat parallels, parallels masked by suspension, and antiparallel fifths in four or fewer voices. Haydn reportedly criticized Kirnberger as "too cautious, too confining." Weber (1817-1821) protested against "the furious persecution against every thing that has the name of fifths." In arpeggiated textures, parallels are often unavoidable without radically inconsistent analysis.

# Common Confusions
- The treatment of parallel fifths is inconsistent across different musical levels. It is incoherent to say that some parallels are allowed "on the surface" while others are allowed "in the harmonic skeleton."
- Pedagogical prohibition of antiparallels can be associated with the abdication of aesthetic responsibility, substituting rules for judgment about whether a particular passage is pleasing or stylistic.
- In pitch-class space, there is no distinction between parallel fifths and parallel fourths, and parallel octaves occur only in extremely rare situations.
