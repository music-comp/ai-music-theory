# Quick Definition

Parallel fifths (simultaneous fifths moving in the same direction) and antiparallels (hidden/direct fifths approaching a fifth by similar motion) are traditionally prohibited, but their treatment is inconsistent across musical levels -- suspensions license parallels at the skeleton level while surface parallels are sometimes permitted.

# Core Definition

Parallel fifths occur when two voices maintain a perfect fifth while moving in the same direction. Antiparallel fifths occur when voices approach a fifth by similar motion. These prohibitions are central to counterpoint pedagogy, but Tymoczko identifies deep inconsistencies: suspensions regularly license parallel fifths at the harmonic-skeleton level (p. 217), and some parallels are permissible "on the surface" while others are allowed "only in the harmonic skeleton" (p. 218). It is incoherent to say a grammatical progression with a suspension "stands for" a nongrammatical one without it (p. 217). Haydn criticized Kirnberger as "too cautious, too confining" (p. 211).

# Key Properties

1. Traditional prohibition central to counterpoint pedagogy
2. Suspensions regularly license parallels at the skeleton level (Fig. 5.1.10)
3. Some parallels are permitted "on the surface," others "in the skeleton" -- an inconsistency
4. Pedagogues sometimes forbid merely uncommon practices (p. 211)
5. Forbidding antiparallels allows avoiding aesthetic judgment (p. 211)

# Construction / Recognition

## To Check for Parallel Fifths:
1. Identify all perfect fifths between voice pairs
2. Check whether consecutive fifths move in the same direction
3. Consider whether suspensions or other nonharmonic tones are present
4. Assess whether parallels occur at the surface, skeleton, or both levels

# Context & Application

The treatment of parallels exemplifies the tension between rules and aesthetic judgment. Pedagogues sometimes forbid practices that were merely "somewhat uncommon" (p. 211). Weber protested "the furious persecution against every thing that has the name of fifths." The inconsistency between surface and skeleton parallels reveals a fundamental problem with the view that suspensions are merely decorative.

# Examples

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Forbidding all parallels regardless of context
  **Correction**: The treatment of parallels should involve aesthetic judgment, not mere rule-following (p. 211)

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Believing parallels are consistently treated across musical levels
  **Clarification**: Surface parallels are sometimes allowed, skeleton parallels sometimes allowed -- both cannot be consistently prohibited (p. 218)
