# Quick Definition

Beethoven's technique of altering the initial unit of a sequence so that the sequential structure clarifies only as the music progresses -- creating chainlike continuity where each unit has a direct but different link to its predecessor.

# Core Definition

In a disguised model sequence, "the initial unit is altered so that the sequential structure clarifies as the music progresses" (p. 476). This is an instance of the broader formal principle of "becoming clear only in retrospect" (pp. 475-476): "the opening music initially sounds like it could be an improvised introduction, but quickly turns into a thematic allegro, while the transition initially sounds like it could be a thematic statement, but quickly starts modulating" (p. 475). The technique creates chainlike continuity where successive units share different relationships: in the Fifth Symphony second theme, "the sequence's first statement reworks the pitch classes of the horn call; the second statement uses the same harmonic progression as the first; and the third unit uses the same melody as the second" (p. 486).

# Key Properties

1. The first sequential unit differs from subsequent units
2. The sequential pattern becomes increasingly evident as the music progresses
3. Successive units are linked by different relationships (harmonic, melodic, intervallic)
4. Creates an impression of improvised discovery -- the music finding its pattern in real time
5. Exemplifies Beethoven's formal playfulness and humor

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify a Disguised Model:
1. Look for a passage that becomes recognizably sequential only after its first unit
2. Compare the first unit to subsequent units -- note specific alterations
3. Trace the chain of relationships: what does each unit share with its predecessor?
4. Verify that the sequential plan is audible in retrospect but not at the outset

# Context & Application

This technique appears in both the Tempest and Fifth Symphony transitions and second themes, contributing to Beethoven's distinctive approach to formal continuity. It is part of his broader strategy of misaligning rhetoric and formal function, where what initially sounds like one formal type turns out to be another. "If we are alive to Beethoven's playful humor, then we will not be shocked by these misalignments between rhetoric and formal function" (p. 475).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Confusing a disguised model with a free or varied sequence
  **Correction**: A disguised model specifically involves an altered first unit that conceals the sequential plan -- not merely variation within a sequence

- **Error**: Analyzing the first unit on its own terms without recognizing its sequential function
  **Correction**: The first unit's identity is revealed only by what follows

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the disguised model is merely structural complexity
  **Clarification**: It is also characteristic of Beethoven's formal playfulness and humor

- **Confusion**: Assuming retrospective clarification means the listener cannot follow in real time
  **Clarification**: The chainlike continuity ensures each moment connects to the next; the sequential plan simply becomes explicit gradually
