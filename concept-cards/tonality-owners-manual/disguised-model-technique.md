# Quick Definition
Beethoven's technique of altering the initial unit of a sequence so that the sequential structure clarifies only as the music progresses -- creating chainlike continuity where each unit has a direct but different link to its predecessor, with the pattern "becoming clear only in retrospect."

# Formal Definition
In a disguised model sequence, the first unit differs from subsequent units in ways that obscure the sequential pattern at its outset. As the sequence progresses, the pattern becomes increasingly evident. This creates a chain of continuity where successive units are linked by different relationships: unit 2 might share the harmonic progression of unit 1, unit 3 might share the melody of unit 2, and so on. The technique exemplifies Beethoven's formal principle of retrospective clarification, where the identity and function of formal sections becomes clear only after they have passed.

# Musical Context
This technique appears in both the Tempest and Fifth Symphony transitions and second themes. It contributes to Beethoven's distinctive approach to formal continuity, where rhetoric and formal function are deliberately misaligned -- what initially sounds like a thematic statement turns out to be a transition, what sounds like a cadence becomes the start of something new.

# Examples
- Tempest transition: the initial unit is altered so sequential structure clarifies as the music progresses, fauxbourdon triads ascending by fifth with phrasing accelerating from four bars to two to one (Figure 10.3.5, p. 476)
- Fifth Symphony second theme: first unit reworks horn-call pitch classes, second shares first's harmonic progression, third shares second's melody (Figure 10.4.7, p. 487)
- This is an instance of the broader principle: "becoming clear only in retrospect" (pp. 475-476)

# Common Confusions
- A disguised model is not the same as a free or varied sequence -- it specifically involves an altered first unit that conceals the sequential plan
- The technique is characteristic of Beethoven's formal playfulness and humor, not just structural complexity
- It creates the impression of improvised discovery, as if the music is finding its pattern in real time
