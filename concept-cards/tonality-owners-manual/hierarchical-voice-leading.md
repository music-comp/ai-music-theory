# Quick Definition

The principle that voice leadings can operate simultaneously at multiple structural levels -- chords moving within scales, and scales moving within the chromatic aggregate -- producing hierarchically nested contrapuntal patterns that are self-similar across levels.

# Core Definition

Tymoczko identifies "hierarchically self-similar transformations occurring on two different structural levels" (p. 358). In Beethoven's Ninth Symphony scherzo, the *chordal* voice leading takes a clockwise step in the triadic spiral diagram (connecting third-related triads by ascending diatonic step), while the *scalar* voice leading moves counterclockwise in the scalar spiral diagram (connecting fifth-related scales by descending chromatic step). These are "two hierarchically nested basic voice leadings" operating simultaneously (p. 358, Figure P8.3).

The deeper insight is that "any collection's output [is] available to serve as the input of the next" (p. 360) -- not just the two-level hierarchy of chord-in-scale, but potentially arbitrary nesting of collections. This replaces a "flat" model (where scales map scale degrees directly to pitch classes) with a hierarchical one (where collections nest inside larger collections, Figure P8.4).

# Key Properties

1. Voice leadings can operate at the chordal level and the scalar level simultaneously
2. The operations can be self-similar: the same type of basic voice leading at different levels
3. The hierarchy can extend beyond two levels (e.g., Tymoczko's *Wheels within Wheels* uses three)
4. Scale degrees and pitch classes are "fundamentally analogous" -- both label positions in collections (p. 359)
5. The hierarchical model replaces the "flat" model of chord-and-scale interaction

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify Hierarchical Voice Leading:
1. Analyze the chordal voice leading: how do chords move within their scale?
2. Analyze the scalar voice leading: how does the scale change over the passage?
3. Check whether both levels use basic voice leadings (near-step motion on spiral diagrams)
4. Note whether the operations are self-similar (same type of transformation at different levels)
5. Consider whether additional levels of nesting are present

# Context & Application

The concept is "extremely difficult to implement at the speed of music, whether in improvisation or at the slower pace of notation" (p. 360), especially with unfamiliar collections. Music theory can help musicians internalize this abstract perspective, "explaining how to replace specific idioms with more general techniques applying to all levels of the collectional hierarchy" (p. 360).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming only two levels of hierarchy are possible (chord and scale)
  **Correction**: Arbitrary nesting is possible; *Wheels within Wheels* uses three levels simultaneously

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking hierarchical voice leading is the same as Schenkerian "levels"
  **Clarification**: Tymoczko's levels are *collections* (chords in scales in the aggregate), not *reductions* (foreground, middleground, background); the mechanisms are geometric, not prolongational
