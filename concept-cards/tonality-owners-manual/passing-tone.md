# Quick Definition
A nonharmonic tone that connects two chord tones by stepwise motion, typically occurring on a metrically weak beat.

# Formal Definition
A passing tone is a nonharmonic tone approached and left by step in the same direction, connecting two chord tones. In the SNAP system, passing tones are one of the four fundamental categories of nonharmonic tone. They can be diatonic or chromatic, and occur in both ascending and descending forms. Schenker took passing tones as the model for all dissonances, asserting that no "composite sound" exists between the dissonant passing note and the sustained note.

# Musical Context
Passing tones are among the most common nonharmonic tones across all tonal styles. In the first practice, they are typically unaccented and occur on metrically weak beats. Some passing tones cut across the binary division of the beat ("121" passing tones). In later music, passing tones can have genuinely harmonic effects -- as in the ascending subdominant, where a passing 7^ between 6^ and 8^ can create dominant functionality.

# Common Confusions
- Not all passing tones are purely decorative. The ascending passing 7^ in IV-to-I progressions can create a sense of dominant functionality, particularly when reinforced by 4^-3^ to create a resolving tritone.
- Schenker's claim that passing tones form no "composite sound" is too absolute. Tymoczko argues it is vital to hear the composite sounds formed by passing tones and harmonic tones.
