# Quick Definition
The distinction between harmonic cycles that appear early in a phrase (avoiding melodic ^2-^1 and root-position V-I) and those that appear at cadences (featuring bass ^5-^1 and melodic ^2-^1), understood as alternative paths through a single harmonic map rather than fundamentally different syntactic categories.

# Formal Definition
Different harmonic cycles have varying degrees of "cadential weight," with phrases typically moving from less-conclusive to more-conclusive progressions. Cadential weight can be quantified by considering whether a cycle contains a predominant, ends with a root-position tonic-dominant progression, and similar features (Figure 9.2.2, p. 413). The traditional harmonic theorist's central claim is that early-phrase and cadential progressions are similar, differing in degree rather than kind -- both are journeys through the same map of harmonic possibilities. This contrasts with the Schenkerian "phrase model" in which early-phrase progressions are hierarchically nested inside a structural progression.

# Musical Context
This distinction is "perhaps the single most pressing problem of contemporary music theory" (p. 417), as it directly affects elementary pedagogy. It separates sequential (chord-to-chord) models of harmony from recursive (prolongational) models. The difference lies in whether initial tonics "attach" syntactically to final dominants or whether phrases simply move through increasingly conclusive harmonic cycles.

# Examples
- Bach chorales: cadential weight generally increases throughout a phrase, with the final cycle longer and stronger (Figure 9.2.2, p. 413)
- Haydn's G minor sonata, Hob. XVI:44, II: Laitz interprets four successive harmonic cycles as three embedded within a fourth; Tymoczko sees four sequential cycles (Figure 9.2.5, p. 416)
- Computer-generated chorale harmonization using phrase-position-dependent chord probabilities (Figure 9.2.3, p. 413)

# Common Confusions
- When inversions are ignored, early-phrase and cadential progressions show remarkably even distribution across functional categories (Figure 9.2.4, p. 414)
- The debate is not about whether phrases exhibit structural patterns, but whether those patterns require recursive embedding or can be explained by strategic arrangement of sequential cycles
- Specific inversions correlate strongly with phrasal position, but this reflects strategy rather than syntax
