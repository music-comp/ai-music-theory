# Quick Definition
A triadic "default grammar" that emerges whenever functional-harmonic constraints are weak, reflecting the deep geometry of three-note chords rather than any human convention.

# Formal Definition
The modal state of nature is a proto-grammar arising not from cultural convention but from the underlying geometry of three-note chords -- a geometry that is intrinsically neutral with respect to harmonic progression, acquiring directionality from external factors like the preference for descending melody. It produces resonances across very different musical styles because it offers "straightforward solutions to basic musical problems -- stepwise descending melodies and closely related triads." It is most directly accessible when harmonic function is weak and harder to see in harmonically regimented styles.

# Musical Context
This concept explains the deep resemblances between Renaissance modal music and rock -- not through historical continuity (which Tymoczko finds little evidence for) but through shared access to the fundamental geometry of triadic voice leading. Both rock musicians and early seventeenth-century composers operated where functional-harmonic constraints were comparatively weak, free to explore the connection between descending voice leading and retrofunctional harmony. The modality of the 1960s is best understood as "the delayed echo of modernism" rather than a continuation of folk tradition.

# Examples
- Heinrich Schutz's Psalm 84, mm. 136-146: eleven consecutive root-position triads of uncertain tonal provenance that resonate with rock harmony (Figure 2.8.1)
- Grieg's "Watchman's Song" Intermezzo: i-VII-bII-IV-V with 8-7-6-5 descent, harmonies more related to Schutz and rock than to Corelli or Haydn (Figure 2.8.2)
- "Helpless," "Eight Days a Week," and related songs: straightforward solutions to the problem of harmonizing descending steps with closely related triads
- Evidence against folk-music continuity: pre-1950 folk recordings tend to be harmonically spare, with clear modal triadic harmony being largely a 1960s phenomenon

# Common Confusions
- The resemblance between Renaissance and rock modality is likely due to shared triadic geometry, NOT historical transmission through folk tradition
- The modal state of nature is not "anti-functional" -- it is the default that exists prior to or alongside functionality
- Retrofunctional techniques persist even within functional music as a "hidden cantus firmus," visible in ascending-fifth sequences and other undertheorized corners
