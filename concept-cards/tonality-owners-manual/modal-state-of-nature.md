# Quick Definition

A triadic "default grammar" that emerges whenever functional-harmonic constraints are weak, reflecting the deep geometry of three-note chords rather than any human convention -- producing resonances across Schutz, Grieg, Shostakovich, and rock.

# Core Definition

"There is a triadic 'state of nature' that is most directly accessible when harmonic function is weak, and which is correspondingly harder to see in more harmonically regimented styles. This proto-grammar arises not from any human convention but from the underlying geometry of three-note chords -- a geometry which is intrinsically neutral with respect to harmonic progression, acquiring directionality from external factors such as the preference for descending melody" (p. 76). It offers "straightforward solutions to basic musical problems -- stepwise descending melodies and closely related triads" (p. 86). It is "a 'syntax' supported not by convention but by constraints inherent in our musical materials" (p. 86).

# Prerequisites

- **Function and Retrofunction** -- The state of nature is the default that exists when function is weak
- **Melodic Principle of Harmony** -- The state arises from melodic-harmonic interdependence

# Key Properties

1. Emerges when functional-harmonic constraints are weak
2. Arises from geometry, not convention
3. Produces resonances across very different styles and eras
4. Offers straightforward solutions to basic melodic-harmonic problems
5. Retrofunctional techniques persist within functional music as a "hidden cantus firmus"
6. Represents a species of "heavily diatonic chromaticism"

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify:
1. Look for music where functional-harmonic conventions are absent or weak
2. Check for descending stepwise melodies harmonized with closely related triads
3. Note root-position triads and retrofunctional root progressions
4. Compare with the loop families of rock harmony

# Context & Application

Retrofunctional techniques thread "like a hidden cantus firmus throughout functionality, an unruly strip of wilderness surrounded by the well-charted territory of rule-governed harmony" (p. 76). This explains cross-stylistic resonances between Schutz's early-seventeenth-century music and rock, between Grieg and the Beatles. Tymoczko argues these resonances reflect shared triadic geometry, not historical transmission.

# Examples

**Example 1** (pp. 74-75, Figure 2.8.1): Schutz's Psalm 84, mm. 136-146 -- eleven consecutive root-position triads resonating with rock harmony.

**Example 2** (pp. 75-76, Figure 2.8.2): Grieg's "Watchman's Song" Intermezzo -- i-VII-bII-IV-V with 8-7-6-5 descent.

**Example 3** (p. 77, Figure 2.8.3): Mozart K.333 -- retrofunctional motion (IV-I in subdominant) within an otherwise functional context.

**Example 4** (p. 77, Figure 2.8.4): Bach BWV 311 -- retrofunctional ascending-fifth sequence at the end of a phrygian-melody chorale.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming Renaissance-rock resemblances indicate folk-music continuity
  **Correction**: The resemblances reflect shared triadic geometry, not historical transmission

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the modal state of nature is "anti-functional"
  **Clarification**: It is the default that exists prior to or alongside functionality; retrofunctional techniques persist within functional music
