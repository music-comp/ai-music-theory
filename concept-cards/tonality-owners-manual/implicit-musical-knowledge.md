# Quick Definition
Musical knowledge that is embedded in compositional, improvisational, and perceptual habits rather than being available to conscious reflection.

# Formal Definition
Implicit musical knowledge is the understanding of musical structure that composers and listeners possess without being able to articulate it verbally. It encompasses the intuitive grasp of voice leading, harmonic grammar, contrapuntal conventions, and stylistic norms that guide music-making but may not be codified in treatises or textbooks. It stands in contrast to "explicit" or "theorist theory" knowledge.

# Musical Context
Implicit knowledge pervades all musical traditions. Composers write without being able to explain what they are doing; listeners understand without being able to describe what they hear. This knowledge was historically transmitted through hands-on apprenticeship rather than textbooks. Modernism's distrust of inherited convention led to the "withering of a vast tradition of implicit knowledge," with composers proposing artificial systems to replace natural grammars.

# Examples
- Rock musicians intuitively favor retrofunctional progressions (ascending fifths, descending steps) without formal training in voice-leading geometry (Ch. 2)
- Bach's cadential idiom where the bass seventh resolves down by fourth rather than by step -- a recurring pattern not described in textbooks (Figure 1.5.1)
- The preference for I^6 over iii in the nonsequential Romanesca, a practice predating the eighteenth century and reflecting implicit chord grammar (Figure 1.5.4)

# Common Confusions
- Not the same as "unconscious" knowledge -- implicit knowledge can sometimes be made explicit through theory, but it need not be conscious during composition or listening
- Should not be confused with the "what-you-see-is-what-you-hear" principle, which wrongly assumes that score patterns are directly available to listener awareness
- Implicit knowledge is not inferior to explicit knowledge -- it may be richer and more nuanced than any theoretical formalization
