# Quick Definition

The relationship between linear (contrapuntal) and vertical (harmonic) forces is fundamentally context-dependent: descending voice leading is retrofunctional with triads but functional with seventh chords. Different geometrical environments provide different harmonic affordances, refuting both compatibilist and incompatibilist views.

# Core Definition

With diatonic thirds, descending melodic motion produces descending-fifth progressions and functionality. With diatonic triads, descending melodies produce retrofunctional progressions (ascending thirds, fifths). With diatonic seventh chords, functionality is restored. "There is no simple relationship between linear procedure and harmonic outcome, but rather a shifting and context-dependent set of implications" (p. 147). This refutes the Schenkerian "compatibilist" view (counterpoint inevitably generates functional harmony) and Cohn's "incompatibilist" view (chromaticism and function are opposed forces) (p. 148). Instead, "the relationship between horizontal and vertical is fundamentally context-dependent, with different geometrical environments providing different harmonic affordances" (p. 148).

# Key Properties

1. Dyads: descending voice leading -> descending fifths (functional)
2. Triads: descending voice leading -> ascending thirds/fifths (retrofunctional)
3. Seventh chords: descending voice leading -> descending fifths (functional again)
4. The relationship is context-dependent, not fixed
5. A continuum exists from smooth cooperation to total effacement of functionality
6. Random clockwise steps on dyadic circle sound functional; on triadic circle they sound like rock

# Context & Application

Randy Newman's "When She Loved Me" embeds nonfunctional triadic patterns from Gesualdo's "Moro Lasso" within a functional seventh-chord progression (Figure 3.9.2, p. 148). The Beatles' I-II-IV-I in "Eight Days a Week" draws nonfunctional triads out of functional seventh-chord origins (Figure 3.9.3, p. 148). This demonstrates bidirectional movement: nonfunctional idioms embedded in functional contexts, and functional idioms stripped to nonfunctional surfaces. The spiral diagrams visualize a spectrum from smooth harmony-counterpoint cooperation to passages that "totally efface the sense of functionality and sometimes even centricity" (p. 149).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming counterpoint always produces functional harmony
  **Correction**: This is only true in certain geometrical environments (dyads, seventh chords); triads create retrofunctionality

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Treating functional/nonfunctional as a binary opposition
  **Clarification**: There is a continuum from smooth cooperation to total effacement of functionality (p. 149)
