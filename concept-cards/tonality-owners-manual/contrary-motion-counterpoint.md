# Quick Definition

Beethoven's characteristic fondness for outer-voice contrary motion as a comprehensive approach to functional counterpoint, encompassing the Ludwig, wedge sequences, registral expansion, and patterns that exploit the fifth-based geometry of diatonic dyads.

# Core Definition

Contrary-motion patterns in Beethoven range from simple Ludwig units to elaborate passages "combining free 5-6 ascent in the left hand with a right-hand contrary-motion melody that descends through thirds and sixths" (Figure 10.2.10 caption, p. 469). When augmented by parallel-motion options (Figure 10.2.6), contrary and parallel motion together provide "a simple but comprehensive set of outer-voice defaults for functionally tonal music-making" (p. 468). This adopts the Tinctorian perspective, "analyzing functional counterpoint not in terms of independent voices, but rather parallel and contrary patterns, isolating those contrapuntal possibilities consistent with the harmonic grammar" (p. 468). The resulting idioms are "more general than the independent atoms that usually go by that name, being closer a complete list than an arbitrary set of building blocks" (p. 468).

# Prerequisites

- **The Ludwig** -- The most common manifestation of Beethoven's contrary-motion practice
- **Basic voice leading** -- Understanding diatonic dyadic voice leading

# Key Properties

1. Contrary motion is Beethoven's default; Mozart favored outer-voice parallelism
2. The Ludwig is the most common manifestation, but many other patterns exist
3. Contrary and parallel options together constitute a nearly complete set of outer-voice defaults
4. Serves as a pitch-domain analogue of the crescendo
5. The approach is closer to "a complete list than an arbitrary set of building blocks"

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify Contrary-Motion Counterpoint:
1. Check whether outer voices move in opposite directions
2. Identify whether the pattern is a Ludwig, a wedge, a triadic contrary motion, or a free combination
3. Note whether the contrary motion drives registral expansion
4. Check whether the pattern is consistent with functional harmonic grammar

# Context & Application

Contrary motion distinguishes Beethoven from Mozart, who favored outer-voice parallelism. The Ludwig functions as "a pitch-domain analogue of the crescendo" (p. 471), enabling the dramatic gestural waves that define Beethoven's style. In some sense, "Ludwiging is just contrary motion itself, exploiting the fifth-based geometry of diatonic dyads" (p. 466).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating contrary motion as an occasional device in Beethoven
  **Correction**: It is a comprehensive approach to tonal counterpoint, his primary default

- **Error**: Analyzing contrary-motion patterns as individual isolated schemas
  **Correction**: They form a nearly complete set of outer-voice defaults, not an arbitrary collection

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the distinction between parallel and contrary patterns is secondary
  **Clarification**: It is more fundamental than the distinction between individual schemas

- **Confusion**: Assuming Beethoven's contrary-motion patterns are unique to him
  **Clarification**: The patterns reflect the basic geometry of contrapuntal possibility; Beethoven's uniqueness lies in his systematic and unidirectional deployment
