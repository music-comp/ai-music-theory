# Quick Definition

The use of invertible counterpoint within sequential structures, where harmonically unstable (wedge) sequences exploit voice exchangeability at specific intervals (octave, tenth, twelfth) to produce consonant results from contrary-motion patterns.

# Core Definition

"Any sequence with differing transpositional subscripts technically involves some sort of invertible counterpoint" (p. 159, fn. 5). In harmonically unstable sequences, different transpositions create wedge patterns passing through unrelated vertical states unless the unit features invertible counterpoint. The 5-6 pattern (Figure 4.3.6, p. 168) is invertible counterpoint at the tenth: one voice moves down by fifth, the other up by sixth, "equivalent to transposing by tenth and transposing the whole pattern up by sixth." This can be embedded in triadic 5-6 sequences as root-fifth, fifth-third, or both simultaneously producing three canonic voices (Figures 4.3.6-7, p. 168). Barbara Strozzi's "Il Contrasto" uses antiparallel fifths with contrary-motion contraction and third substitution (Figure 4.3.8, p. 169).

# Key Properties

1. Any differing transpositional subscripts involve invertible counterpoint
2. 5-6 pattern = invertible counterpoint at the tenth
3. Can embed in triadic sequences as root-fifth, fifth-third, or both
4. Three canonic voices arise from simultaneous root-fifth and fifth-third embedding
5. 5-10 expansion transforms 5-6 into outer-voice pattern
6. Two-voice canonic sequences can be generated by contrary-motion distortion of parallel motion

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify:
1. Look for harmonically unstable sequences (different transpositional subscripts)
2. Check if consonance is maintained through invertible counterpoint
3. For 5-6 pattern: check for alternating fifths and sixths with canonic exchange

# Context & Application

Bach's WTC second E minor prelude features invertible counterpoint at the twelfth (Figure 4.1.5). The ascending 5-6 sequence generates one of the most common ascending-step sequences in WTC fugues (Figures 4.3.7). The "interesting conceptual point is that canon and contrary motion arise as byproducts of the basic mechanics of sequential voice leading, rather than requiring deliberate compositional artifice" (p. 169).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating invertible counterpoint as separate from sequences
  **Correction**: It is a structural property inherent in any sequence with differing transpositions

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking 5-6 pattern and canonic sequence are different things
  **Clarification**: The 5-6 pattern is simultaneously invertible counterpoint at the tenth and a canonic sequence -- two descriptions of the same phenomenon
