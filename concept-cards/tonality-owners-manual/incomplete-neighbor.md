# Quick Definition

A nonharmonic tone connected by step to only one of its adjacent chord tones, classified by metrical weight (accented/unaccented) and direction of stepwise connection (to predecessor/successor). Three of the four combinations are common.

# Core Definition

Incomplete neighbors are nonharmonic tones connected by step to only one adjacent chord tone -- they are either approached or left by leap (p. 236). They are classified by metrical weight and direction of stepwise connection: appoggiatura (accented, step to successor), escape tone/echappee (unaccented, step to predecessor), unaccented incomplete neighbor step to successor (common), and accented connected to predecessor (extremely rare, p. 236). The notes they "represent" for reduction are not necessarily the notes to which they are connected by step (p. 236).

# Key Properties

1. Connected by step to only one adjacent chord tone
2. Four species based on accent and stepwise direction; three are common
3. The fourth type (accented, connected to predecessor) is extremely rare (Fig. 5.4.2)
4. Can decorate each other recursively (Fig. 5.4.4)
5. The note represented for reduction may differ from the note connected by step
6. Largely eliminated in the Palestrina style (only cambiata survives)
7. Return as standard in the standardized second practice

# Construction / Recognition

## To Classify an Incomplete Neighbor:
1. Identify a nonharmonic tone approached or left by leap
2. Determine if it is accented or unaccented
3. Determine if the stepwise connection is to the preceding or following tone
4. Appoggiatura: accented, step to successor
5. Escape tone: unaccented, step to predecessor
6. Standard incomplete neighbor: unaccented, step to successor
7. Rare type: accented, step to predecessor (possibly an added sixth)

# Context & Application

Incomplete neighbors are one of the new contrapuntal devices embraced in the eighteenth century as part of the standardized second practice. They are interesting because the tone they represent for reduction is not always the tone connected by step: a melodic decoration of the following note may stand for the preceding note in the harmonic skeleton (p. 236, Fig. 5.4.3).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming the reduction-target of an incomplete neighbor is always the note connected by step
  **Correction**: The bass B-flat in Fig. 5.4.3 decorates the following C melodically but would be replaced by the preceding F upon reduction

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Treating the rare fourth type (accented, connected to predecessor) as a standard nonharmonic tone
  **Clarification**: This type is extremely rare and often interpretable as an added sixth (Fig. 5.4.2)
