# Quick Definition

A Renaissance cadential formula in which two voices converge by step onto a unison or octave. Aron (1532) described such cadences as being "on" the converged-upon note, but by that time musical practice already showed the bass note was becoming the primary cadential focus.

# Core Definition

The clausula vera (true cadence) is a converging-voice cadential idiom from the Renaissance. Dahlhaus and Bent observe that as late as 1532, Petro Aron described a cadence where voices converge on E as "a cadence on E, not A" (p. 271). However, by that time abundant evidence shows the meaning was already in flux: pieces ending without converging voices, cadences where the converged-upon note is not used as a tonal center, a strong preference for the bass to sound the converged-upon note, and theoretical lists of cadences lacking converging voices (pp. 271-272). Cadences converging on the sixth above the bass are very rare, suggesting bass-centered thinking was already established (Fig. 6.3.6).

# Key Properties

1. Two voices converging by step onto a unison or octave
2. By 1532, the theoretical description already lagged behind practice
3. Pieces could end without converging voices
4. Bass increasingly important as the cadential note
5. Cadences converging on the sixth above the bass are very rare
6. Key example of "origin and meaning" divergence

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify:
1. Look for two voices approaching a unison/octave by stepwise contrary motion
2. Note which voice the bass sounds
3. Check whether the converged-upon note functions as the cadential goal
4. Assess whether the bass note or converged-upon note is the perceived tonal center

# Context & Application

The clausula vera exemplifies how theory can lag behind practice. The auditory system lends salience to the bass of a root-position triad through "tonal fusion" -- a psychological effect that was as real in the Renaissance as today (p. 272).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming Aron's theoretical description was accurate about how cadences were heard
  **Correction**: Musical practice shows bass-centered hearing was already established by Aron's time

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the clausula vera proves Renaissance musicians lacked harmonic awareness
  **Clarification**: The theoretical lag shows that practice was ahead of theory, not that musicians were unaware of harmonic implications
