# Quick Definition

Tymoczko's analysis of Beethoven's Op. 28 "Pastorale" sonata first movement as dominated by fauxbourdon (parallel motion), with repetition and parallelism creating a relaxed aesthetic, and a dialogue between parallel and contrary motion structuring the entire movement.

# Core Definition

The Pastorale is "the rare piece that is largely devoted to the third system of functional tonality" (p. 494) -- fauxbourdon/parallel motion rather than sequences or harmonic cycles. "Almost every phrase is played twice, with the repeat sometimes extended" (p. 494). The first theme uses "descending-third fauxbourdon, IV-ii-vii-degrees-V over a tonic pedal, replacing the suspensions of the standard 7-6 sequence with accented passing tones" (p. 495). The transition "crosses fauxbourdon with the Prinner" (p. 496). The second theme features a "contrary-motion antecedent answered by parallel-motion consequent" (p. 498) -- reversing the opening's pattern. The movement reveals Beethoven's conception of musical identity as "not a specific melody but something more general -- a texture, a shape, a sequence of schemas" (p. 500).

# Prerequisites

- **The Ludwig** -- Contrary-motion patterns that appear in dialogue with fauxbourdon

# Key Properties

1. Fauxbourdon dominates rather than sequences or harmonic cycles
2. Almost every phrase is repeated, creating the relaxed aesthetic
3. Systematic transformation between parallel and contrary motion
4. The transition crosses fauxbourdon with Prinner
5. Tonal ambiguity between A major and F-sharp minor for nearly thirty measures
6. Beethoven declares himself "a composer of patterns rather than melodies"

# Construction / Recognition

## To Follow Tymoczko's Analysis:
1. Parse the exposition as six large phrases
2. Identify fauxbourdon as the dominant voice-leading technique in each
3. Track the dialogue between parallel and contrary motion
4. Note where repetition extends or transforms the original statement
5. Observe the restatement of the second theme a third higher as evidence of pattern-based composition

# Context & Application

The six expositional phrases are each sequential or schematic, "with no extended passage of nonsequential, nonschematic, melodic music in the entire movement" (synthesized from analysis). This demonstrates that Beethoven's schematic vocabulary is "compatible with a wide range of expressive moods" (p. 500) -- from the violence of the Tempest to the placidity of the Pastorale.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Thinking the Pastorale is less schematic than the Tempest or Fifth
  **Correction**: It simply emphasizes different schemas (fauxbourdon rather than sequences)

- **Error**: Treating the parallel/contrary alternation as surface variety
  **Correction**: The systematic transformation between parallel and contrary motion is a structural principle

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking fauxbourdon is merely a surface technique in Beethoven
  **Clarification**: In the Pastorale, it is the primary structural principle, dominating the entire exposition

- **Confusion**: Assuming tonal indecision is a compositional flaw
  **Clarification**: The thirty-measure vacillation between A major and F-sharp minor is a deliberate formal ambiguity
