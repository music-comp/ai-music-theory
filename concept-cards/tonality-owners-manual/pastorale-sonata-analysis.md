# Quick Definition
Tymoczko's analysis of Beethoven's Op. 28 "Pastorale" sonata first movement as dominated by fauxbourdon (parallel motion), with repetition and parallelism creating a relaxed aesthetic, and a dialogue between parallel and contrary motion that structures the entire movement.

# Formal Definition
The Pastorale is unusual among Beethoven's movements in being largely devoted to the "third system of functional tonality" (fauxbourdon/parallel motion), rather than sequences or harmonic cycles. Almost every phrase is played twice, with the repeat sometimes extended. The first theme uses descending-third fauxbourdon; the transition crosses fauxbourdon with Prinner; the second theme features contrary-motion antecedent answered by parallel-motion consequent (reversing the opening's pattern). The movement systematically juxtaposes and transforms parallel into contrary motion and vice versa.

# Musical Context
This analysis demonstrates that Beethoven could apply his pattern-making approach to fauxbourdon-dominated textures as well as sequential ones. The movement reveals Beethoven's conception of musical identity: "not a specific melody but something more general -- a texture, a shape, a sequence of schemas" (p. 500). When the second theme restates itself a third higher, Beethoven "seems to declare that he is a composer of patterns rather than melodies."

# Examples
- Opening: descending-third fauxbourdon IV-ii-vii-degrees-V over tonic pedal with accented passing tones replacing suspensions (Figures 10.5.1-10.5.2, pp. 495-496)
- Repeat transforms parallel into contrary motion through "thinking within the chord" (Figure 10.5.3, p. 496)
- Transition: fauxbourdon crosses with Prinner, two transpositions connecting to form a descending scale (Figures 10.5.6-10.5.7, pp. 497-498)
- Second theme: ascending steps against descending fifths stating the functional defaults, answered by parallel-motion consequent (Figure 10.5.8, p. 498)

# Common Confusions
- The Pastorale is not less schematic than the Tempest or Fifth Symphony -- it simply emphasizes different schemas (fauxbourdon rather than sequences)
- The systematic alternation between parallel and contrary motion is a structural principle, not just surface variety
- Tonal indecision between A major and F-sharp minor for nearly thirty measures is a deliberate formal ambiguity (pp. 498-499)
