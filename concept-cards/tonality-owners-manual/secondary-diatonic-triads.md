# Quick Definition

The diatonic triads beyond I, IV, and V (principally ii, vi, and iii), whose functional roles developed gradually. IV emerges as a privileged third sonority around Gombert; ii eventually supplants IV as principal predominant in the seventeenth century; vi becomes a pre-predominant; iii nearly disappears.

# Core Definition

The evolution of secondary diatonic triads' roles is a key aspect of the development of functional tonality. Up through about Gombert, IV is just another chord, no different from ii, vi, or iii (p. 293). After this, IV emerges as special, only to be supplanted by ii in the seventeenth century. vi gradually adopts a new role as a pre-predominant, usually moving to ii or IV before the dominant (p. 294). The mediant becomes increasingly rare (p. 259). The harmonic cycles gradually lengthen from I-V-I to I-ii-V-I to I-vi-ii-V-I, each adding secondary triads to the functional sequence (p. 294).

# Key Properties

1. IV emerges as privileged third sonority ~Gombert era
2. ii supplants IV as principal predominant in seventeenth century
3. vi becomes pre-predominant (moving to ii or IV before V)
4. iii nearly disappears in mature functional music
5. Multiple "functionalities" exist: some feature IV, others ii (p. 293)
6. vi-to-V progression largely disappears; vi moves through predominants

# Construction / Recognition

## To Track Secondary Triad Development:
1. Measure frequency of ii, vi, iii relative to IV across periods
2. Calculate tendency of each to move to V (first-order properties)
3. Note the gradual lengthening of harmonic cycles

# Context & Application

The changing roles of secondary triads show that "we need to be careful not to generalize about functional harmony as such" -- instead recognizing "the existence of multiple functionalities" (pp. 293-294).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating the predominant function of ii as universal throughout music history
  **Correction**: IV was the primary predominant in much sixteenth-century music; ii took over in the seventeenth century

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking there is one "correct" version of functional harmony
  **Clarification**: Multiple functionalities exist, some emphasizing IV and others ii
