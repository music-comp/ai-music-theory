# Quick Definition

A hierarchical approach to voice leading where an abstract chord provides scale-like collections within which surface voices move, the chord itself shifting by efficient voice leading in the background -- "like a melodic sequence inside a modulating scale" (p. 135).

# Core Definition

Surface voices move along the pitches of an abstract background chord that "sounds a chord's notes in every octave, providing a field of contrapuntal play" (p. 134). Background slots shift as the chord changes, typically by efficient voice leading; the surface voices' motion-within-the-chord is unperturbed by these shifts. This can be modeled with nested spiral diagrams: one for voices moving within the chord (using little-t and tau transposition), another for the chord moving within the scale (using big-T). "For the first time we need two diagrams rather than a single spiral on its own. This hierarchical depth is characteristic of four-voice music" (p. 134). The approach gives "nonmetaphysical meaning to the claim that the right-hand B3 and D4 in mm. 3-4 of the Waldstein are 'the same' as the left-hand B2 and D3 -- that is, the same abstract scale degrees articulated by different concrete voices" (p. 136).

# Key Properties

1. Background chord provides scale-like collections in every octave
2. Surface voices move within this shifting background
3. Requires two nested spiral diagrams (hierarchical depth)
4. Doublings and voice exchanges become surface artifacts of motion within a simpler background
5. Implements Schenkerian insight in a non-metaphysical way
6. Three levels of transposition: tau (along dyad), t (along triad), T (along scale)

# Construction / Recognition

## To Analyze:
1. Identify the background voice leading (chord-to-chord progression)
2. Number the abstract scale degrees from bottom to top
3. Track which abstract degree each surface voice sounds
4. Note how surface voices ascend or descend within the background

## To Compose:
1. Write a background chorale (chord progression)
2. Let surface voices move along the background's abstract scale degrees
3. Allow voices to switch between degrees independently
4. The background handles the harmonic motion; voices handle the counterpoint

# Context & Application

The Waldstein opening exemplifies this: a triadic background descends chromatically (C-B-Bb-A) while right-hand voices ascend, producing ascending thirds E-G-B-D-F-A-C (Figure 3.7.2, p. 133). The Tristan prelude's opening "Desire" motif ascends through four background scale degrees (Figure 3.7.5, p. 136). Tallis's forty-voice Spem in alium is the extreme case: forty voices move within a shifting three-note field (Figure 3.7.7, p. 137). Functional tonality adds yet another hierarchical level: voices inside chords inside scales inside chromatic space -- "wheels within wheels, spiral diagrams nested like the epicycles of Ptolemaic astronomy" (p. 137).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating the background as metaphorical
  **Correction**: The background is a literal collection of pitches available in every octave; surface voices genuinely move along it

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Equating this with Schenkerian prolongation
  **Clarification**: While it resonates with Schenkerian insight, this approach gives the claim "nonmetaphysical meaning" through geometric modeling; doublings and voice exchanges are surface artifacts, not prolongational operations (p. 136)
