# Quick Definition
The perspective in which chords with different doublings (e.g., CGG and CCG) are treated as "the same" entity, implemented through the hierarchical picture where surface voices move within abstract scale-like chords whose notes may not all be present or may appear in multiple octaves.

# Formal Definition
Cardinality equivalence treats chords like CGG and CCG as different configurations of surface voices within a single underlying two-note "scale" CG, analogous to how the Waldstein analysis represents different harmonic configurations as surface-level motion within a three-voice background. This perspective bridges the gap between mathematics and musical intuition by allowing: (1) chords to function as abstract scale-like objects whose notes exist in every octave; (2) background voice leadings containing notes not found on the surface; (3) "splitting" where a single voice divides into multiple voices and "merging" where multiple voices collapse into one. Quantization -- snapping out-of-grid notes into a scale -- provides an alternative to postulating specific voice leadings at the background level.

# Musical Context
This concept addresses a fundamental challenge in voice-leading geometry: mathematically constructing circular spaces for chords with fixed doublings inevitably spreads sonically similar objects throughout the spiral. The hierarchical approach resolves this by assigning doublings and voice exchanges to the surface level, keeping the background clean. It also enables analysis of passages where the number of voices changes.

# Common Confusions
- CGG and CCG are genuinely different contrapuntally (the former is two diatonic steps from F major, the latter is not) even though they are harmonically similar
- The hierarchical perspective makes Schenkerian analysis "insufficiently hierarchical" in one sense -- it ignores the ontological difference between chord and scale
- Quantization offers an alternative to postulating specific background voice leadings, allowing different voice groups to move in different ways
