# Quick Definition
An accompanimental overlap occurs when a new accompanimental pattern begins at the moment of a theme's cadential arrival, but the next thematic unit's first true downbeat occurs in the following measure. Only the accompaniment overlaps; the structural beginning of the next section is in the next bar.

# Core Definition
An accompanimental overlap occurs when the moment of a theme's cadential arrival brings the new accompanimental figuration of the next thematic unit, but the first true "downbeat" of that unit occurs in the **following measure**. Only the accompaniment itself overlaps with the end of the prior theme, not the structural beginning of the next section. This creates greater rhythmic continuity than no overlap at all, but less powerful than an elision (which overlaps structure, not just texture) (Caplin, Ch. 11, pp. 337-338).

# Key Properties
1. New accompaniment begins at cadential arrival
2. Next theme's structural first downbeat is in the FOLLOWING measure
3. Only texture (accompaniment) overlaps, not structure
4. Creates moderate rhythmic continuity
5. Common between main theme and transition, and within subordinate-theme groups

# Relationships
## Contrasts With
- **Elision** -- In elision, the new theme begins AT the cadential arrival (structural overlap)
## Related
- **Elision** -- Both are boundary processes creating continuity

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Treating an accompanimental overlap as an elision
  **Clarification**: In overlap, the theme begins in the bar AFTER the cadence; in elision, AT the cadence
