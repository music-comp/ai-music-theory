## Accompanimental Overlap

An **accompanimental overlap** occurs when the moment of a theme's cadential arrival brings with it the new accompanimental figuration of the next thematic unit.

### How It Works

1. Theme reaches cadential arrival on a downbeat
2. At this same moment, a new accompanimental pattern begins
3. The first true "downbeat" of the next thematic unit occurs in the **following measure**
4. Only the accompaniment itself overlaps with the end of the prior theme

### Key Distinction from Elision

The structural beginning of the next section does **not** occur at the cadential arrival:
- In accompanimental overlap: theme begins in bar **following** the cadence
- In elision: theme begins **at** the moment of cadence

### Effect

Creates greater rhythmic continuity than if no overlap occurred, but less powerful than elision since only texture (not structure) overlaps.

### Common Context

Frequently occurs between:
- Main theme and transition
- Within subordinate-theme group
