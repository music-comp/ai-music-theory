# Theme (Definition)

The term **theme** has a specific meaning in the study of classical form that differs from popular usage.

## Popular Usage

In common usage, a "theme" usually refers to:
- A highly recognizable melody or "tune"
- The part of a piece we tend to whistle or hum
- Traditional theories may number themes as "first theme," "second theme," etc.

## Technical Definition

In this theoretical framework, **theme** refers to a **complete formal unit** that includes:
- Its particular **melodic-motivic content**
- Its **accompanimental texture**
- Its supporting **harmonic progressions**

## Key Characteristics

A theme:
- Is normally brought to a close by a **genuine cadence** of some kind
- Typically fills **eight measures** when beginning a movement
- May be much longer when occurring later in a movement
- Represents a unified whole, not just a melody

## Theme Types

The major theme types include:
- **Sentence**
- **Period**
- **Hybrid themes**
- **Compound themes** (16 measures)
- **Small ternary**
- **Small binary**

## Important Distinction

The theme is much more than just its melody. It encompasses all musical components that together form a coherent formal unit.
