## Definition

Main theme function in minuet form arises through initial tonic-stabilizing progressions of the home key. The strength of this expression depends on harmonic progressions, cadences, and degree of tight-knit organization.

## Levels of Expression

### Minimal Main-Theme Expression
- Music modulates before any home-key cadence
- Form likely sentential with first (and usually final) cadence in subordinate key

### Moderate Main-Theme Expression
- Opening music leads to home-key HC
- Form likely periodic, with matching authentic cadence in subordinate key

### Strongest Main-Theme Expression
- Music in home key closes with **PAC before the end of the section**
- This **early authentic cadence** creates strongest sense of main-theme function
- Unit following the early PAC begins again in home key and modulates, or begins directly in subordinate key

## When A Section is Nonmodulating

If the A section remains entirely in the home key:
- Its thematic functional expression is exclusively main theme
- The section is relatively short and compact (tight-knit)
- Ends with PAC

## Analytical Identification

To identify main-theme function:
1. Look for tonic-stabilizing progressions
2. Note any home-key cadential articulation
3. Assess whether home-key PAC occurs before the section's end
4. Compare relative "weight" of home-key vs. subordinate-key passages
