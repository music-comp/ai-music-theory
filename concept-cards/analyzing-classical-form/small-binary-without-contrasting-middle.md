# Quick Definition
Part 2 of a small binary is sometimes built without a clearly discernible contrasting middle. Instead, it may take the form of a loose sentence, bring exclusively continuation or cadential material, or suggest B-section characteristics (dominant emphasis, model-sequence) without constituting a complete contrasting middle.

# Core Definition
Even without a complete contrasting middle, Part 2 may strongly suggest B-section function at its opening by emphasizing dominant harmony or immediately introducing model-sequence technique. The overall Part 2 may function as a large 8-m. continuation (like the second half of a compound sentence). When organized as a sentence, the initial 4 measures often use model-sequence technique and lack tonic prolongation, preventing a true presentation phrase but giving an overall sentential shape.

# Key Properties
1. Part 2 may lack complete contrasting middle
2. May take loose sentential form
3. May bring exclusively continuation/cadential material
4. Opening may still suggest B-section characteristics
5. May function as large 8-m. continuation
6. Always ends with PAC in home key

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 267): Haydn, Sonata H. 31, iii -- Part 2 as model-sequence with continuation throughout.
**Example 2** (p. 275): Beethoven, Op. 131, iv -- Part 2 as loose sentence with response-statement arrangement.
