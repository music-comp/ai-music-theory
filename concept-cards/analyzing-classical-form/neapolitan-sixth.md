# Quick Definition
A major triad built on the lowered second scale degree, typically in first inversion (bII6), functioning as a pre-dominant harmony, especially common in minor mode.

# Formal Definition
The "Neapolitan" or "phrygian" harmony in first inversion (bII6) is an important pre-dominant, especially in minor. Though most typically built over scale degree 4 in the bass voice (first inversion), the Neapolitan can also be found in root position (bII) and other positions.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Structure:
- Major triad on lowered scale degree 2 (bII)
- Typically in first inversion (bII6) with scale degree 4 in bass
- Contains: lowered 2, 4, lowered 6 (in minor) or borrowed lowered 6 (in major)

Function:
- Pre-dominant, preparing the dominant
- Shares scale degree 4 with other pre-dominants (II, IV)
- The lowered 2 creates a half-step approach to the leading tone

The bass note (scale degree 4 in first inversion) connects this chord to the family of pre-dominants built over that bass note.

# Musical Context/Application
The Neapolitan sixth:
- Functions as pre-dominant within cadential progressions
- Particularly associated with minor mode (though usable in major via mixture)
- Creates a distinctive chromatic color through the lowered second degree
- The descending half-step from lowered 2 to leading tone creates strong melodic pull

The term "phrygian" refers to the characteristic half-step descent in the bass (from 4 to the leading tone, then to 5) reminiscent of the phrygian mode.

# Common Confusions
- Treating the Neapolitan as a modulation to the key of bII (it functions as pre-dominant in the home key)
- Expecting it only in first inversion (root position and other positions occur)
- Assuming it only appears in minor (it can appear in major through mixture)
- Confusing with augmented-sixth chords (different structure, similar chromatic function)
