# Quick Definition
A major triad on the lowered second scale degree, typically in first inversion (bII6), functioning as a pre-dominant harmony. Especially common in minor mode. Also called "phrygian" harmony due to the characteristic half-step bass descent.

# Core Definition
The "Neapolitan" or "phrygian" harmony in first inversion (bII6) is another important pre-dominant, especially in minor (p. 32). Though most typically built over scale degree 4 in the bass voice (first inversion), the Neapolitan can also be found in other positions such as root position (bII) (p. 33). The term "phrygian" refers to the characteristic half-step descent in the bass reminiscent of the phrygian mode.

# Key Properties
1. Major triad on lowered scale degree 2 (bII)
2. Typically in first inversion (bII6) with scale degree 4 in bass
3. Pre-dominant function, preparing the dominant
4. Especially common in minor mode
5. Also called "phrygian" harmony
6. Can appear in root position or other positions
7. Half-step descent from b2 to leading tone creates strong melodic pull

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for a major triad on b2
2. Check for first inversion with scale degree 4 in bass (most typical)
3. Verify it functions as pre-dominant, preparing motion to V
4. Note its association with minor mode

# Context & Application
The Neapolitan sixth functions as a pre-dominant within cadential progressions, providing distinctive chromatic color through the lowered second degree. Particularly effective in minor-mode contexts, though usable in major through modal borrowing.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating the Neapolitan as a modulation to the key of bII
  **Correction**: It functions as a pre-dominant in the home key

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Expecting it only in first inversion
  **Clarification**: Root position and other positions also occur, though first inversion is most typical
- **Confusion**: Assuming it appears only in minor
  **Clarification**: It can appear in major through modal borrowing
