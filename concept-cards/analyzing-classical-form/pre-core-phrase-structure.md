# Quick Definition
Pre-cores can be structured as complete or incomplete thematic units, ranging from tight-knit forms (sentence, period, hybrid) to transition-like, incomplete, or multiple-unit constructions.

# Core Definition
Caplin differentiates pre-cores first as complete or incomplete thematic units (p. 465). Complete pre-cores contain initiating, medial, and concluding phrase functions; they may be tight-knit (resembling a main theme) or transition-like (modulatory, sentential). Incomplete pre-cores lack a full complement of functions, usually missing the concluding function. More complex pre-cores consist of multiple thematic units, which themselves may be complete or incomplete.

# Prerequisites
- **Pre-core** — The formal unit being classified
- **Sentence** — Common form for tight-knit pre-cores
- **Period** — Alternative form for tight-knit pre-cores

# Key Properties
1. Complete tight-knit: sentence, period, or hybrid; analogous to main theme
2. Complete transition-like: sentential, modulatory, harmonic goal is dominant of development key
3. Incomplete: usually lacks concluding function; tends to be short
4. Multiple units: two thematic units, one or both typically incomplete
5. Tight-knit pre-cores especially common in Mozart
6. Essential tonal difference from main theme: pre-core is in subordinate key or development key

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Determine if the pre-core has initiating, medial, and concluding functions (complete vs. incomplete)
2. If complete, assess whether it is tight-knit (sentence/period/hybrid) or transition-like (modulatory)
3. Check for multiple units, especially in larger pre-cores

# Context & Application
The variety of pre-core structures reflects the development section's generally less conventional organization. Unlike the core (which has a fairly predictable model-sequence-fragmentation process), the pre-core can take almost any phrase-structural form.
