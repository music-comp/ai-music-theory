# Quick Definition
How music is organized into units of various sizes at multiple hierarchical levels: motives (sub-measure), basic ideas (2 m.), phrases (4 m.), themes (8 m.). Grouping structure is independent of melodic content (cereal box analogy: box size vs. contents). Symmetrical groupings (powers of 2) are tight-knit.

# Core Definition
The opening unit of the sentence is a phrase insofar as these four measures group together to form a coherent whole, which distinguishes itself from the following group of measures. This phrase, a constituent part of the grouping structure of the sentence, serves a distinct formal function (pp. 68-69). The labels "idea" and "phrase" refer to grouping structure; "basic idea" and "presentation" refer to formal function. This distinction is developed extensively throughout the text (p. 69). Fragmentation exclusively concerns grouping structure -- the length of units regardless of their melodic content (p. 74).

# Prerequisites
- **Theme definition** — Grouping structure applies to themes and their sub-units

# Key Properties
1. Hierarchical: motive -> b.i. (2 m.) -> phrase (4 m.) -> theme (8 m.)
2. Independent of melodic content (cereal box analogy)
3. Symmetrical groupings (2+2, 4+4) are tight-knit
4. Fragmentation is a grouping-structure phenomenon
5. Distinguished from formal function: grouping = size/organization; function = temporal role

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Determine the size of each unit (how many measures it spans)
2. Note how units group into larger units
3. Identify changes in unit size (fragmentation, extension)

# Context & Application
Grouping structure and formal function are the two complementary perspectives for analyzing classical form. They often align (the 4-m. presentation phrase expresses presentation function) but may diverge (continuation function may begin in a 2-m. unit maintained from the presentation).

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 56, Ex. 2.1): Beethoven, Op. 2, No. 1 -- 2+2 (presentation) + 1+1+2 (continuation) = 8 m. theme.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing grouping structure with melodic content
  **Correction**: Grouping concerns unit size, not what the units contain

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Equating grouping labels with function labels
  **Clarification**: "Phrase" (grouping) and "presentation" (function) refer to different things even when applied to the same music
