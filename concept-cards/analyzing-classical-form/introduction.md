# Quick Definition
A thematic introduction is a short before-the-beginning framing function (rarely more than 2 measures) that precedes a theme, featuring minimal motivic content, typically accompanimental figurations, and tonic prolongation, with a progressive dynamic building anticipation.

# Core Definition
A thematic introduction precedes the theme's beginning with minimal motivic content so as not to project a sense of being a basic idea. It often consists of accompanimental figurations that continue after the theme begins. Most introductions prolong tonic harmony in root position. They typically feature a progressive dynamic (increasing energy and anticipation). Not to be confused with a "slow introduction" (Chapter 16), which precedes an entire sonata-form exposition.

# Key Properties
1. Short: rarely more than 2 measures
2. Minimal motivic content
3. Often accompanimental figurations
4. Tonic prolongation
5. Progressive dynamic
6. External to the theme's measure structure

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 156): Beethoven, Op. 22, ii, m. 1 -- left-hand "vamp" as 1-m. introduction.
**Example 2** (p. 200): Beethoven, Violin Concerto Op. 61, i, m. 1 -- timpani introduction (rhythmic motive).
