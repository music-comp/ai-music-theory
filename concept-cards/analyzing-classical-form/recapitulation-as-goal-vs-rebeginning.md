# Quick Definition
The recapitulation functions as the "end" of sonata form from an overall perspective, but its opening normally gives the impression of "rebeginning," especially in Haydn and Mozart; Beethoven's forceful openings can create an apotheosis effect.

# Core Definition
"From the perspective of the overall form, the recapitulation, as a whole, functions as the 'end' of the sonata form. But this quality of ending emerges with full force only toward the conclusion of the section" (p. 499). At its beginning, it "normally gives the impression of 'rebeginning' the form." With Beethoven, "the start of the recapitulation is often given an entirely different dynamic, usually one that is louder, more forceful, and more dramatically charged," creating an "effect of apotheosis" that may reinforce the "misguided interpretation that the recapitulation begins with an authentic cadence."

# Key Properties
1. Overall function: "end" of sonata form
2. Ending quality emerges fully only toward the section's conclusion (final PAC)
3. Opening character: "rebeginning" (especially Haydn and Mozart)
4. Beethoven: often louder, more forceful opening (apotheosis effect)
5. Apotheosis effect can misleadingly suggest cadential V-I at the start

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Interpreting the forte V-I at the recapitulation's start as an authentic cadence
  **Correction**: This resolution initiates a new section (rebeginning), not a cadential close; the true "ending" quality emerges later with the subordinate theme's final PAC
