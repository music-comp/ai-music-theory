# Quick Definition
A contrasting middle may begin and end on dominant without constituting a true standing on the dominant: in dominant emphasis, tonic harmonies are primary and dominant is subordinate, whereas in a genuine standing on the dominant, dominant harmony is prolonged throughout with tonic subordinate.

# Core Definition
In alternating V-I patterns within a contrasting middle, it can be difficult to determine which harmony is being prolonged. In a standing on the dominant, the dominant is prolonged: V - (I) - V - (I) - V, with tonic harmonies functioning as subordinate neighbor chords. In dominant emphasis, tonic harmonies are primary: (V) - I - (V) - I, with dominant receiving emphasis but not being prolonged. The distinction requires musical judgment and careful listening -- considering which harmony falls on strong metric positions, whether V resolves to I or I moves to V, and what the melodic behavior emphasizes. There is no mechanical procedure that always works. Even with dominant emphasis (not prolongation), the B section may end with a dominant arrival rather than a true HC if proper cadential conditions are not met.

# Key Properties
1. Standing on the dominant: V prolonged, I subordinate (V - (I) - V - (I) - V)
2. Dominant emphasis: I primary, V emphasized but not prolonged ((V) - I - (V) - I)
3. Metric position of each harmony is a key determining factor
4. Resolution direction matters: does V resolve to I, or does I move to V?
5. Melodic behavior may favor one interpretation
6. No mechanical procedure exists; musical judgment required
7. Noncadential endings possible with dominant emphasis

# Construction / Recognition
## Guidelines for Distinguishing:
1. Check which harmony falls on strong metric positions
2. Determine resolution direction (V to I, or I to V)
3. Consider melodic emphasis (does melody emphasize scale degree 5 or 1?)
4. Assess the particular musical context
5. Accept that genuine ambiguity may exist

# Context & Application
This distinction matters because it affects how the B section is labeled and understood functionally. A true standing on the dominant creates maximal expectation for tonic resolution at the recapitulation. Dominant emphasis creates a looser contrasting function but with less single-minded dominant prolongation. In genuinely ambiguous cases, both interpretations may be valid, and the passage may deliberately blur the distinction.
