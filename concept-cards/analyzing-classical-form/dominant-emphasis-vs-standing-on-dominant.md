# Dominant Emphasis vs. Standing on Dominant

A contrasting middle may begin and end with dominant without necessarily being a true standing on the dominant.

## The Distinction

**Standing on the dominant**:
- Dominant harmony is **prolonged** throughout
- Tonic harmonies (if present) are subordinate to dominant
- Pattern: V - (I) - V - (I) - V

**Dominant emphasis**:
- Dominant receives emphasis but is NOT prolonged
- Tonic harmonies function as primary
- Pattern: (V) - I - (V) - I - (V) - I

## Analysis Challenge

In alternating V-I patterns, it can be difficult to determine:
- Which harmony is being prolonged?
- Which harmony is subordinate?

## Guidelines

Consider:
- Which harmony falls on strong metric positions?
- Does V resolve to I, or does I move to V?
- What is the melodic behavior (does it emphasize 5 or 1)?

## No Mechanical Procedure

Determining the correct analysis:
- Relies on musical judgment
- Requires careful listening
- Must consider the particular context

## Noncadential Ending

Even with dominant emphasis (not prolongation), the B section may end with a **dominant arrival** rather than a true HC if proper cadential conditions aren't met.
