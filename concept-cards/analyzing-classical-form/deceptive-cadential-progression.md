# Quick Definition
A variant of the authentic cadential progression in which the expected final root-position tonic is replaced by a related harmony, most commonly VI (with bass ascending from 5 to 6). Also includes V resolving to I6, V converting to V7/IV, and other substitutions.

# Core Definition
The deceptive cadential progression arises when the final tonic of the authentic cadential progression is replaced by a related harmony. The most common form sees the bass ascend stepwise from scale degree 5 to scale degree 6, which supports a submediant (VI) substituting for the implied final tonic (p. 40). Less frequently, the dominant leads to I6 rather than root-position I, often with a passing V4/2 inserted; this V4/2 is an exception to the rule that cadential dominant must never be inverted, since the bass motion from 5 to 4 represents passing motion rather than genuine change of position (p. 41).

# Key Properties
1. Final tonic of authentic cadential progression is replaced by another harmony
2. Most common: bass ascends 5 to 6, supporting VI
3. May be embellished by passing V7/VI
4. Different harmonies may be built over scale degree 6 in bass
5. V to I6 (first-inversion tonic) is another deceptive form
6. V-V4/2-I6: exception to root-position rule (bass 5-4 is passing motion)
7. Tonic converted to V7/IV creates a dramatic deception
8. The deceptive progression completes (with substitute) rather than abandoning cadential motion

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for a cadential progression that sets up authentic arrival
2. Check if the expected root-position I is replaced by another harmony
3. Most common substitute: VI with bass on scale degree 6
4. Also check for I6, V7/IV, or other substitutes
5. Distinguished from evaded cadence: deceptive completes, evaded abandons

# Context & Application
Deceptive cadential progressions deny expected authentic cadential arrival but complete the cadential motion with a substitute. They often prompt continuation or repetition of cadential material. The deceptive cadential progression is distinct from the basic cadence types (PAC, IAC, HC): deceptive progressions do not produce a basic cadence type but rather a cadential deviation.

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 40-41, Ex. 1.16): (a) V-VI with stepwise bass ascent; (b) embellished with passing V7/VI; (c) different harmonies over scale degree 6; (d-e) V to I6 with passing V4/2; (f) tonic converted to V7/IV.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking only V-VI counts as deceptive
  **Correction**: V-I6, V-V7/IV, and other substitutes also qualify

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing deceptive cadential progression with evaded cadence
  **Clarification**: Deceptive completes with a substitute harmony; evaded abandons the cadential motion entirely
- **Confusion**: Missing the V4/2 exception in V-V4/2-I6
  **Clarification**: Bass motion 5-4 is contrapuntal passing, not genuine inversion of the dominant
