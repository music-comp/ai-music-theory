# Quick Definition
A variant of the authentic cadential progression in which the expected final tonic is replaced by a related harmony (usually VI).

# Formal Definition
The deceptive cadential progression arises when the final tonic of the authentic cadential progression is replaced by a related harmony. The most common form sees the bass ascend stepwise from scale degree 5 to scale degree 6, which supports a submediant (VI) substituting for the implied final tonic.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Common forms of deceptive cadential progression:

**Standard form:**
- I6 - II6 - V(7) - VI
- Bass motion: stepwise ascent from 5 to 6
- VI functions as tonic substitute

**Variants:**
- V - VI embellished by passing V7/VI
- Different harmonies built over scale degree 6 in bass
- V - I6 (tonic in first inversion rather than expected root position)
- V(6-4 to 5-3) - V4/2 - I6 (with passing dominant)
- V - V7/IV (tonic converted to secondary dominant)

**Exception to the root-position rule:**
The appearance of V4/2 in a deceptive cadential progression leading to I6 is an exception to the rule that the cadential dominant must never be inverted. Here, the bass motion from 5 to 4 represents contrapuntal "passing" motion more than genuine change of position.

# Musical Context/Application
Deceptive cadential progressions:
- Deny expected authentic cadential arrival
- Often prompt continuation or repetition of cadential material
- The VI ending still represents completion of the cadential progression
- Create formal ambiguity requiring resolution

The deceptive progression completes (with the substitute harmony) rather than abandoning the cadential motion - distinguishing it from the evaded cadence.

# Common Confusions
- Confusing deceptive cadential progression with evaded cadence (deceptive completes with substitute; evaded abandons)
- Thinking only V-VI counts as deceptive (V-I6 and other substitutes also qualify)
- Not recognizing the V4/2 exception in the V-V4/2-I6 progression
- Assuming deceptive always involves VI (other harmonies can replace tonic)
