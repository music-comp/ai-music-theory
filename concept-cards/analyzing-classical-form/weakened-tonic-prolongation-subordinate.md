# Quick Definition
A subordinate theme's presentation can be loosened by weakening its tonic prolongation -- typically through a dominant pedal that undermines but does not obliterate the surface-level tonic prolongation implied by the melodic material. This creates two harmonic levels: a surface tonic prolongation satisfying presentation function, and a deeper dominant prolongation creating loosening.

# Core Definition
The tonic prolongation supporting a presentation phrase can be weakened in subordinate themes. A typical technique involves a **dominant pedal** over which the melodic material implies tonic prolongation. Two levels of harmonic activity result: (1) a surface level containing the tonic prolongation, satisfying the harmonic requirement of presentation function; (2) a deeper level containing the dominant prolongation (created by the bass pedal), undermining but not obliterating the tonic. This complication contributes from the very start to looser expression. The distinction from a genuine standing on the dominant lies in what the melodic-motivic material implies: if it implies tonic prolongation, it is a weakened presentation; if dominant prolongation, it is a standing on the dominant (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 382-383; see also Ch. 9, p. 291).

# Key Properties
1. Dominant pedal undermines tonic prolongation without obliterating it
2. Two harmonic levels: surface tonic (presentation) and deeper dominant (loosening)
3. Melodic material's harmonic implications determine the interpretation
4. If melody implies tonic = weakened presentation
5. If melody implies dominant = standing on the dominant
6. Contributes to loose expression from the theme's very beginning
