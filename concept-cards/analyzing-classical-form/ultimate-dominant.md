# Quick Definition
The dominant harmony serving as the goal (final harmony) of a half-cadential progression, occupying the ultimate (last) position and requiring consonant triad form.

# Core Definition
"In a half-cadential progression, the dominant itself becomes the goal harmony and thus occupies the ultimate position" (p. 39). "Whether a dominant is characterized as ultimate or penultimate has nothing to do with harmony per se; rather, it is a matter of how the dominant groups with its surrounding harmonies" (p. 39). The ultimate dominant does NOT group with the following tonic, which "normally initiates a new harmonic progression." It must be a root-position consonant triad -- "adding a dissonant seventh ... would overly destabilize the ultimate dominant" (pp. 39-40).

# Prerequisites
- **Cadential progression** — The ultimate dominant exists within a half-cadential progression
- **Dominant function** — Must understand dominant harmony

# Key Properties
1. Serves as the harmonic GOAL of the half-cadential progression
2. Must be a root-position consonant TRIAD (not seventh chord)
3. Does NOT group with the following tonic (which begins a new progression)
4. V7 is inappropriate -- the seventh would "overly destabilize"
5. The distinction from penultimate is about grouping, not chord content
6. Possesses sufficient stability to represent a harmonic ending point

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate the final harmony of a half-cadential progression
2. Verify that it is V in root position as a consonant triad
3. Confirm that the following tonic initiates a new progression (does not belong to the HC)
4. If the dominant includes a seventh, it is likely penultimate (authentic), not ultimate

# Context & Application
The ultimate dominant ends contrasting middles, transitions, and similar formal units. Although the ultimate dominant "usually resolves to tonic," that tonic "normally initiates a new harmonic progression" rather than belonging to the half-cadential progression. The stability of the consonant triad is essential for the dominant to function as a credible ending point.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Adding a seventh to the ultimate dominant
  **Correction**: The seventh "would overly destabilize the ultimate dominant"; use a consonant V triad

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the tonic following a half cadence belongs to the half-cadential progression
  **Clarification**: The following tonic "normally initiates a new harmonic progression" and does not belong to the half-cadential progression
