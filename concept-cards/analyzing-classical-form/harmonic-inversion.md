# Quick Definition
The positioning of a chord with a note other than the root in the bass voice, indicated by figured-bass symbols, which significantly affects the chord's functional weight and cadential potential.

# Core Definition
Harmonic inversion refers to the position of a chord based on which chord tone is in the bass voice, indicated by standard figured-bass symbols (I6, V4/3, etc.). Caplin emphasizes that inversion significantly affects functional status: "The tonic triad in root position (I) is considerably stabler than that triad in first inversion (I6). Indeed, we will see that the first-inversion tonic plays a special role in articulating harmonic directionality, especially by signaling the onset of a cadential progression" (p. 31). Root-position V is essential for cadential function.

# Prerequisites
- **Figured bass** — Provides the notation system for indicating inversions
- **Scale degree** — Inversion is identified relative to the chord root

# Key Properties
1. Root position: root in bass (maximum stability)
2. First inversion: third in bass (I6, V6, V6/5)
3. Second inversion: fifth in bass (I6/4, V4/3)
4. Third inversion (seventh chords): seventh in bass (V4/2)
5. Root-position I is considerably stabler than I6
6. I6 signals the onset of cadential motion
7. Root-position V is required for cadential function; inverted V cannot be cadential

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Determine which chord tone is in the bass voice
2. Apply the appropriate figured-bass symbol
3. Assess the functional implications of the inversion
4. Note: a change of inversion within the same harmony is shown as I——6 (without repeating the Roman numeral)

# Context & Application
Inversion affects harmonic weight, cadential potential, voice-leading possibilities, and formal function. Within pedal points, subordinate harmonies cannot have their inversion determined since their bass is replaced by the pedal. The contrast between I6 (initial tonic) and I (final tonic) is fundamental to the directionality of cadential progressions.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming inverted V can function cadentially
  **Correction**: Only root-position V can serve as a cadential dominant

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Treating all inversions as having equal functional weight
  **Clarification**: Root position carries the greatest stability and functional weight; inversions progressively reduce this
