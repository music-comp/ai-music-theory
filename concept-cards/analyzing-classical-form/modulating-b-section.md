# Quick Definition
A contrasting middle may modulate to the dominant region and confirm it as a subordinate key through a PAC, after which a retransition passage returns the music to the home key, preparing for the recapitulation.

# Core Definition
A modulating B section typically follows a nonmodulating A section (one that stays in the home key). The B section modulates to the dominant region and confirms the new key with a PAC. After this cadential confirmation, a retransition passage brings the music back to the home key, ending with a home-key HC or dominant arrival to prepare for the recapitulation. The retransition may be as brief as a single V7 chord or may be extended with model-sequence technique. The modulating B section still serves the fundamental purpose of dominant emphasis -- by literally going to the dominant key, the PAC in V represents the ultimate form of dominant emphasis.

# Key Properties
1. Follows a nonmodulating A section
2. Modulates to dominant region
3. Confirms new key with PAC
4. Retransition follows to restore home key
5. Retransition ranges from single V7 chord to extended passage
6. Represents ultimate dominant emphasis (literal modulation to V)
7. Overall A-B-A' tonal plan preserved: tonic-dominant-tonic

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Check whether A section is nonmodulating (stays in home key)
2. Look for modulation to dominant region in B section
3. Locate PAC in the new key
4. Find the retransition passage returning to home key
5. Check for HC or dominant arrival at retransition's end

# Context & Application
The modulating B section creates the largest tonal contrast within the small ternary, anticipating the tonal drama of sonata form on a smaller scale. The overall form still exhibits the fundamental tonic-dominant-tonic plan: A section in tonic, B section emphasizing dominant (by going to the dominant key), A' section in tonic. If the A section modulated (to V or III), the B section usually returns quickly to the home key rather than continuing in the subordinate key.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing dominant prolongation with modulation to the dominant key
  **Correction**: A standing on the dominant prolongs V harmony in the home key; a modulating B section genuinely moves to V as a key, confirmed by PAC
