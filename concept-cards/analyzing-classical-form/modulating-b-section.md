# Modulating B Section

A contrasting middle may modulate to the dominant region and confirm it as a subordinate key through a PAC.

## Context

A modulating B section typically follows:
- A **nonmodulating** A section (stays in home key)
- If A modulated, B usually returns quickly to home key

## Structure

1. B section modulates to dominant region
2. Confirms new key with PAC
3. **Retransition** follows to return home

## Retransition Function

After the modulating B section confirms the subordinate key:
- A retransition passage brings back the home key
- May be as short as single V7 chord
- May be extended with model-sequence technique
- Ends with home-key HC or dominant arrival

## Harmonic Process

The modulating B section still emphasizes dominant:
- By literally going to the dominant KEY
- The PAC in V is the ultimate dominant emphasis

## Result

The overall form still exhibits:
- A section: Tonic emphasis
- B section: Dominant emphasis (through modulation)
- A' section: Tonic emphasis (after retransition)
