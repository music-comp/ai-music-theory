## Definition

A truncated small ternary is an incomplete thematic structure consisting of an exposition (A section) and a contrasting middle (B section) only. The expected recapitulation (A' section) is eliminated.

## Structure

| Section | Function | Present? |
|---------|----------|----------|
| A | Exposition | Yes |
| B | Contrasting Middle | Yes |
| A' | Recapitulation | **Eliminated** |

## Where It Occurs

Most commonly found in:
- Interior themes of large ternary form
- Certain couplets in rondo forms
- Any context where the return to the main theme makes the A' section redundant

## Why Truncation Occurs

1. The B section ends on home-key dominant, which motivates the return of the main theme
2. The contrasting middle's dominant emphasis substitutes for the A' section's function
3. The return of the main theme (in large ternary) fulfills the recapitulatory role

## Recognition

- The A section typically modulates (often to relative major in minore contexts)
- The B section develops material and returns to home-key dominant
- Instead of A' section, the main theme of the larger form returns directly

## Example Context

In Haydn's String Quartet in B-flat, Op. 64, No. 3, ii:
- The interior theme's A section closes with PAC in relative major
- The B section brings a standing on the dominant
- The main theme returns directly, truncating the expected A' section
