# Recapitulation Harmonic-Tonal Organization

The tonal plan of the recapitulation contrasts markedly with the exposition.

## Fundamental Difference

Unlike the exposition (featuring dramatic opposition between home and subordinate keys), the recapitulation normally remains throughout in the home key. Very rarely does another key receive cadential confirmation.

## Flat-side Tonal Regions

To avoid monotony, the recapitulation often explores flat-side regions:
- Subdominant (IV)
- Supertonic (ii)
- Regions from modal mixture (flat-VI, flat-III, flat-II)

Tonicizations especially likely in main theme and transition units.

## Subordinate Theme

By contrast, remains entirely in the home key and is usually harmonized the same basic way as in the exposition.

## Opening Harmony

With some exceptions, the recapitulation begins with home-key tonic, resolving the dominant at the end of the development. This is NOT an authentic cadence - the dominant is an "ultimate" dominant that resolves to begin a new section.
