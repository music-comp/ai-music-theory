# Quick Definition
Three cadential extension techniques produce distinctly different aesthetic effects: IAC/deceptive cadence provides a temporary but insufficient closure; evaded cadence is the most dramatic, thwarting imminent closure at the last second; abandoned cadence is less dramatic, causing the progression to lose direction and "wander off." The evaded cadence is particularly well suited to subordinate themes.

# Core Definition
Three types of cadential extension produce different aesthetic effects: (1) **IAC or deceptive cadence**: provides a temporary goal, though insufficiently conclusive; some closure occurs (more with IAC than deceptive). (2) **Evaded cadence**: considerably more dramatic; imminent closure is thwarted at the last second and attempted again; the lack of any closure, combined with breaking off a goal-directed process just before completion, arouses powerful expectation. This is particularly well suited to subordinate themes because establishing the subordinate key as a foil to the home key is more effective when confirmation is hard-won. (3) **Abandoned cadence**: much less dramatic; the cadential progression loses direction and the music wanders off, but the resulting formal loosening is pronounced and appropriate for subordinate themes (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 395-396).

# Prerequisites
- **Evaded cadence** -- The most dramatic type
- **Abandoned cadence** -- The least dramatic type

# Key Properties
1. IAC/deceptive: temporary, insufficient closure; moderate effect
2. Evaded: most dramatic; thwarted at last second; powerful expectation
3. Abandoned: least dramatic; progression loses direction; pronounced loosening
4. Evaded cadence is ideal for subordinate themes (key confirmation = hard-won)
5. All three contribute to formal loosening appropriate for subordinate themes
