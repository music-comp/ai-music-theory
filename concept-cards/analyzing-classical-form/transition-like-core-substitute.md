# Quick Definition
A thematic unit occupying the central section of the development whose tonal and phrase-structural organization resembles a transition: beginning in one key, modulating, and closing with an HC or dominant arrival.

# Core Definition
"In place of a core, the central section of the development may be occupied by a thematic unit whose tonal and phrase-structural organization resembles a transition" (p. 474). It follows upon a distinct pre-core, "begins in one key with a standard initiating function (presentation, compound basic idea), modulates to some other key (development or home), and closes with an HC or dominant arrival." Model-sequence technique of modest scope may appear but "not in a way that gives rise to a genuine core."

# Prerequisites
- **Development without core** — The context for core substitutes
- **Transition** — The formal function it resembles

# Key Properties
1. Follows upon a distinct pre-core
2. Begins in one key with standard initiating function
3. Modulates to development key or home key
4. Closes with HC or dominant arrival
5. Model-sequence technique may appear in continuation function
6. Models and sequences too brief/small to constitute a genuine core

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for modulatory activity in the central section of the development
2. Check for an initiating function (presentation, compound basic idea) at its start
3. Confirm that any sequential passages have small models (not 4-8 measures)

# Examples
**Example 13.15** (p. 475): Haydn, Piano Trio in F-sharp minor, H. 26, ii — development begins with pre-core identical to main theme in A major; transition-like unit (m. 20) has 1-m. basic idea, presentation, descending-fifth sequential continuation; premature dominant arrival at m. 24.
