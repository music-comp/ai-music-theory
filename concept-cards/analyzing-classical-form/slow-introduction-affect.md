# Slow Introduction Style and Affect

The characteristic expressive qualities of slow introductions.

## Two Seemingly Incompatible Affective Worlds

Classical slow introductions unite:

### 1. The Stately, Heraldic, Solemn
- Slow tempo
- Fanfare gestures
- Tutti textures
- Dotted rhythms
- Initial forte dynamic

### 2. The Anticipatory, Uncertain, Unstable
- Marked discontinuities (melody, rhythm, texture, dynamics)
- Instabilities of harmonic progression
- Minor modality
- Chromaticism (at times extreme)

## Balance of Qualities

Some slow introductions play down or lack stately character, but almost always contain something uncertain and hesitant.

## Baroque Antecedents

The stately qualities derive from high-Baroque "French overture" style:
- Fanfare gestures
- Double-dotted rhythms
- Courtly pomp and circumstance

The anticipatory, hesitant gestures are lacking in French overture - the later classical style dramatizes the sense of introduction by making it more premonitory.
