## Essential Expositional Closure (EEC)

The concept of multiple PACs within a subordinate-theme group raises the question: is one PAC more important than others?

### Hepokoski-Darcy View

Some theorists (Hepokoski and Darcy) suggest the **first SK: PAC** is of greatest structural import:
- It achieves the principal tonal goal of the exposition
- They term this the "essential expositional closure" (EEC)

### Alternative View

Other theorists focus on:
- The highly dramatic cadential arrival in the **final** subordinate theme
- Often after massive ECP
- Seen as moment of greatest cadential articulation

### Caplin's Position

From a purely **syntactical** point of view:
- All perfect authentic cadences are **equally strong**
- They all fulfill necessary requirements for **thematic closure**

From a **rhetorical** point of view:
- Cadences display a range of expression
- Some seem weak and tame, others highly dramatic
- No way to generalize about relative rhetorical strength
- In some cases first cadence is strongest; in others, final cadence

### Recommendation

Take each subordinate-theme group on its own terms:
- Analyze the various rhetorical differences among cadences ad hoc
- Remember: from a formal perspective, all PACs are structurally the same
