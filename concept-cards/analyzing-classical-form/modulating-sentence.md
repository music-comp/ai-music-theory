# Quick Definition
An 8-m. sentence whose continuation phrase modulates to a closely related key (usually dominant or relative major) and closes with a PAC in the new key. The presentation phrase never modulates; modulation is exclusively a continuation-phrase event.

# Core Definition
A modulating sentence is an 8-m. sentence whose continuation phrase modulates to a new, closely related key (p. 88). Most often, the new key is the dominant region (e.g., C major to G major), or if the opening key is minor, the mediant/relative major (e.g., C minor to E-flat major). The presentation phrase remains in the home key with tonic prolongation and does not modulate. Even when a presentation ends with a clear V7-I progression, this does NOT create a cadence because the progression supports an obvious repetition of the basic idea, thus not creating appropriate conditions for formal ending (p. 88). The continuation closes with a PAC in the new key.

# Prerequisites
- **Sentence structure overview** — The modulating sentence is a variant of the basic sentence
- **Continuation phrase** — Modulation occurs within the continuation
- **Presentation phrase** — Must understand that presentation never modulates or cadences

# Key Properties
1. 8 measures: presentation (4 m.) + continuation (4 m.)
2. Presentation remains in home key with tonic prolongation
3. Continuation modulates to closely related key
4. Closes with PAC in the new key
5. Common destinations: dominant (major mode) or relative major (minor mode)
6. V7-I at end of presentation is NOT a cadence (supports repetition, not ending)
7. Fragmentation within continuation may also bring V7-I without cadential force

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Identify an 8-m. sentence with standard presentation + continuation
2. Check whether the continuation modulates away from the home key
3. Verify the final cadence is a PAC in the new key
4. Confirm that the presentation stays in the home key

# Context & Application
The modulating sentence provides harmonic contrast: after emphatic emphasis on home-key tonic in the presentation, the sudden modulation in the continuation brings welcome harmonic variety (p. 88). The key distinction is that presentation never modulates; modulation is exclusively a feature of the continuation phrase. This form is useful when a theme needs to move to a new tonal area efficiently, such as in transitions or subordinate theme contexts.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Hearing a V7-I progression at the end of the presentation as a cadence
  **Correction**: A V7-I that supports repetition of the basic idea does not create cadential conditions; the context of repetition prohibits cadence formation

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the presentation phrase can modulate
  **Clarification**: The presentation phrase is always tonic-prolongational and never modulates; modulation occurs only in the continuation
