# Modulating Sentence

A **modulating sentence** is an 8-measure sentence whose continuation phrase modulates to a new, closely related key.

## Tonal Destinations

Most often, the new key is:
- **Dominant region** of the opening key (e.g., C major to G major)
- **Mediant/relative major** if the opening key is minor (e.g., C minor to E-flat major)

## Structure

- **Presentation phrase**: Remains in the home key with tonic prolongation
- **Continuation phrase**: Modulates and closes with a PAC in the new key

## No Cadence at End of Presentation

Even when a presentation ends with a clear V7-I progression, this does NOT create a cadence. The progression supports an obvious repetition of the basic idea and thus does not create appropriate conditions for formal ending.

The fragmentation that follows in the continuation may also bring V7-I progressions, but again the sense of repeating the fragment prevents cadential closure until the final cadential idea.

## Harmonic Contrast

After emphatic emphasis on the home-key tonic in the presentation, the sudden modulation in the continuation brings welcome harmonic contrast.

## Key Distinction

The presentation phrase never modulates; modulation is exclusively a feature of the continuation phrase in a modulating sentence.
