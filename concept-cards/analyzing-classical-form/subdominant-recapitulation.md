# Subdominant Recapitulation

When the recapitulation's main theme begins in the subdominant rather than tonic.

## The Deviation

The "double return" of main theme and home key is often cited as hallmark of classical sonata form. But some examples see main theme return in context of subdominant harmony.

## Historical Background

Like deletion of opening main-theme ideas:
- Has antecedents in Baroque and pre-classical practice
- Raises question of whether true recapitulation is at hand

## Famous Example

Mozart's Piano Sonata in C, K. 545, presents well-known example of recapitulation beginning in subdominant region.

## Later Compensation

When recapitulation begins in subdominant:
- A tonic setting of main-theme ideas may appear later
- Most likely in the coda

## Subordinate Theme Variant

A related deviation occurs when subordinate theme (not main theme) begins in subdominant. Transition is adjusted to end on dominant of subdominant, then theme is further adjusted to return to home-key tonic.
