# Quick Definition
A comparative overview of the four phrase deviation types: extension (adds material after completion), expansion (inserts material before completion), compression (shortens), and interpolation (inserts foreign material that disrupts the function).

# Core Definition
The four phrase deviations differ in position, effect, and frequency: Extension adds material at the end after the function is expressed (most common). Internal expansion inserts material within, before the function is complete (especially associated with presentation). Compression shortens the phrase below normative length. Interpolation inserts material foreign to the ongoing function (rarest; when in doubt, prefer extension).

# Key Properties
1. Extension: end of phrase, adds material, 4m -> 5-6m (most common)
2. Internal expansion: middle of phrase, inserts material, 4m -> 5-6m
3. Compression: within phrase, removes material, 4m -> 3m
4. Interpolation: middle of phrase, disrupts process (rarest)
5. Extension and compression may balance each other within a theme
