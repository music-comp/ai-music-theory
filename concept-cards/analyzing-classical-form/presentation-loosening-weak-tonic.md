# Quick Definition
Presentation function acquires a loose expression when its supporting tonic prolongation is weakened or destabilized through three methods: (1) inverting the prolonged tonic (I6 instead of I), (2) placing subordinate harmonies (especially dominant) on metrically strong positions, or (3) undermining the prolongation with a dominant pedal in the bass.

# Core Definition
The strongest and stablest tonic prolongation features root-position tonic with subordinate harmonies on weak metrical positions -- typical of tight-knit sentences. The tonic prolongation can be weakened by three methods: (1) inverting the prolonged harmony (opening on I6 instead of I, creating a less stable foundation); (2) metrically accenting subordinate harmonies (placing the dominant on strong positions, weakening tonic stability); (3) undermining with a dominant pedal, where all constituent harmonies are placed over a dominant pedal continuing from the end of the transition. The dominant pedal creates two levels of prolongation: at the lower level, tonic is still prolonged (satisfying the presentation's requirement), but at a higher level, the dominant pedal undermines this prolongation without obliterating it. This distinction is important for separating a genuine weakened presentation from a subordinate theme that begins with a standing on the dominant (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 384-385).

# Key Properties
1. Three methods: inverted tonic, metrically accented dominant, dominant pedal
2. Root-position tonic with weak-beat subordinate harmonies = strongest (tight-knit)
3. Inverted tonic (I6) creates less stable foundation
4. Metrically strong dominant weakens tonic stability
5. Dominant pedal creates two-level prolongation (tonic within dominant)
6. Distinguished from standing on the dominant by nature of implied prolongation

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Check the tonic's position: root position or first inversion?
2. Check metrical placement of subordinate harmonies: strong or weak beats?
3. Look for dominant pedal continuing from end of transition
4. If melodic material implies tonic prolongation over dominant pedal: weakened presentation
5. If material implies dominant prolongation: standing on the dominant (different concept)

# Context & Application
Weakened tonic prolongation is one of three methods of loosening the presentation function in subordinate themes (alongside additional repetition and full phrase repetition). The dominant pedal variant is especially important because it creates a direct formal link between the transition's final dominant and the subordinate theme's opening, contributing to the formal continuity characteristic of sonata-form expositions.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing a weakened tonic prolongation (dominant pedal over presentation material) with a standing on the dominant
  **Correction**: If the material implies tonic prolongation, it is a weakened presentation; if it implies dominant prolongation, it is a standing on the dominant
