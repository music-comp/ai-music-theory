# Quick Definition
The most important structural change required of the transition in the recapitulation is that it no longer modulate to a new key; it must be adjusted to remain in the home key, typically emphasizing the subdominant region so that the return to tonic for the subordinate theme sounds fresh.

# Core Definition
The transition must be adjusted to remain in the home key rather than modulating to the subordinate key as in the exposition. If the exposition's transition was nonmodulating, it need not necessarily be altered, but for variety's sake it often is. Typically, the recapitulation transition emphasizes the subdominant region so that the return to the tonic for the beginning of the subordinate theme sounds fresh. The tonicized subdominant may then easily function as the pre-dominant leading to the home-key dominant that marks the end of the transition.

# Key Properties
1. Must no longer modulate to subordinate key
2. Adjusted to remain in home key
3. Typically emphasizes subdominant region
4. Subdominant emphasis makes tonic return for subordinate theme sound fresh
5. Tonicized subdominant serves as pre-dominant to home-key dominant
6. Nonmodulating exposition transitions may or may not be altered

# Construction / Recognition
## To Analyze:
1. Compare transition in recapitulation with exposition version
2. Identify where the tonal adjustment occurs (often at the beginning)
3. Look for subdominant emphasis (IV region prominently tonicized)
4. Check how the subdominant connects to the dominant ending the transition
5. Note the resulting key at the standing on the dominant (home key, not subordinate key)

# Examples
**Example** (pp. 302-303): Beethoven, Op. 2, No. 1, i, mm. 109-117 -- transition begins basic idea in F minor (rather than C minor as in exposition), immediately signaling it will stay in home key. Sequential progression tonicizes B-flat minor (subdominant region). IV6 at m. 114 serves as pre-dominant for the HC at m. 117. Standing on the dominant is downplayed relative to exposition.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming the transition must be completely rewritten
  **Correction**: Often only the tonal goal is adjusted; much material may be retained with the modulation redirected to remain in the home key
