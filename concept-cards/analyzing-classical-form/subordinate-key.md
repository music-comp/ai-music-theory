# Quick Definition
The subordinate key (SK) is the secondary tonal center in a sonata-form exposition, creating a tonal conflict with the home key; it is V (dominant) in major-mode movements, III (relative major) or v (minor dominant) in minor-mode movements, and must be confirmed by a PAC.

# Core Definition
The subordinate key is the rival tonality to the home key, established in the exposition and resolved in the recapitulation. In major-mode movements, the subordinate key is typically V (the dominant). In minor-mode movements, it is typically III (the relative major) or sometimes v (the minor dominant). The subordinate key must be confirmed by a PAC at the end of the subordinate theme. In the exposition, the subordinate key temporarily gains a sense of "structural superiority" over the home key. In the recapitulation, the subordinate theme returns transposed into the home key, resolving the tonal conflict.

# Key Properties
1. Secondary tonal center creating conflict with home key
2. Major mode: SK = V (dominant)
3. Minor mode: SK = III (relative major) or v (minor dominant)
4. Must be confirmed by PAC (at end of subordinate theme)
5. Temporarily gains "structural superiority" in exposition
6. Resolved when subordinate theme returns in home key in recapitulation

# Context & Application
The tonal contrast between home key and subordinate key is the fundamental dramatic engine of sonata form. The exposition establishes this contrast, the development intensifies it by moving to even more remote regions, and the recapitulation resolves it. The subordinate key's confirmation by PAC in the exposition is a crucial structural landmark -- it represents the "essential expositional closure." Even in monothematic expositions (where the subordinate theme uses main-theme material), the different key creates genuine formal differentiation.
