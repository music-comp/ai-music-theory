# Quick Definition
An optional section preceding the exposition of a fast sonata movement, functioning as "before-the-beginning" and uniting the expression of stately solemnity with anticipatory uncertainty.

# Core Definition
"The exposition of a fast sonata movement is optionally preceded by a slow introduction, which functions as a 'before-the-beginning' of the entire movement" (p. 573). Only a small minority of sonata forms begin with one, occurring most often in "public" genres (symphony, overture) and occasionally in chamber music. "Of all the large-scale units of classical form, slow introductions are the least predictable in their organization." They typically invoke "a solemn, serious tone" while arousing "a strong sense of anticipation for the livelier character expressed by the rest of the movement."

# Key Properties
1. Optional before-the-beginning section
2. Most common in symphonies and overtures; rare in solo sonatas
3. Least predictable in internal organization of all formal units
4. Typically ends on dominant of home key (sometimes PAC eliding with exposition)
5. Contains prominent melodic-motivic material with variety of harmonic progressions
6. Unites stately/solemn with anticipatory/uncertain affect
7. Can range from brief to very extensive (Beethoven's 2nd and 7th Symphonies)
8. May itself open with a thematic introduction

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for a contrasting slow tempo before the exposition's fast tempo
2. Note fanfare gestures, dotted rhythms, solemn character
3. Identify the harmonic goal (usually dominant of home key)
4. Locate the transition to the exposition (tempo change, basic idea return)

# Examples
**Example 16.1** (p. 574): Mozart, Violin Sonata in B-flat, K. 454, i — compound basic idea (fanfare + lyrical idea); compound sentence with IAC (m. 7) and PAC (m. 9); standing on dominant resembles contrasting middle; truncated small ternary when tonic resolution initiates exposition.

**Example 16.3** (p. 579): Haydn, Symphony No. 104 "London," i — fortissimo fanfare opening; continuation clarifies modality; bipartite organization (mm. 1-7, 7-14) + closing section (14-17); final cadence elides with exposition.

## Contrasts With
- **Thematic introduction** — Much smaller, different hierarchical level
- **Coda section** — The other framing section (after-the-end vs. before-the-beginning)

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Conflating a slow introduction with a thematic introduction
  **Clarification**: A slow introduction resides on the same hierarchical level as exposition/development/recapitulation and contains thematic units; a thematic introduction is a small unit (like a basic idea) preceding a theme
