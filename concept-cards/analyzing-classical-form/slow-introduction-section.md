# Slow Introduction Section

An optional section preceding the exposition, functioning as a "before-the-beginning" of the entire movement.

## Definition

A slow introduction optionally precedes a fast sonata movement, functioning as "before-the-beginning" of the entire movement. Only a small minority of sonata forms begin with slow introduction.

## Typical Genres

Most often occurs in "public" genres:
- Symphony
- Overture
- Sometimes chamber music (especially string quartet)
- Only rarely in solo sonata

## Key Characteristics

- Contains prominent melodic-motivic material
- Supported by variety of harmonic progressions
- Usually ends on dominant of home key
- Invokes solemn, serious tone
- Arouses strong sense of anticipation for livelier character
- Least predictable in internal organization

## Scope

Some are relatively short, others extensive (Beethoven's Second and Seventh Symphonies occupy significant proportion of movement). A slow introduction can even open with a thematic introduction.
