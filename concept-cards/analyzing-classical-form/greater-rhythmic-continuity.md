# Quick Definition
The recapitulation often reduces or eliminates the rhythmic interruptions (rests, fermatas, caesuras) found in the exposition, creating greater forward momentum following the emotionally charged development section.

# Core Definition
The exposition often contains prominent interruptions in rhythmic activity -- rests, fermatas, and pauses, particularly in the main theme and at the transition's end (medial caesura). The recapitulation often removes these discontinuities to foster greater rhythmic momentum. Following an emotionally charged development section, the flow of events may seem too inhibited by too many stops and starts, and the progress needs to feel more continuous toward the final cadential goal. Methods include filling in rests with newly added material, eliminating pauses between phrases, reducing fermata emphasis, and compressing two-part transitions to avoid redundant double caesuras.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of recapitulation harmonic-tonal organization
- Knowledge of ornamental versus structural changes

# Key Properties
- Reduces or eliminates rests, fermatas, and pauses found in the exposition
- Fills in rhythmic gaps with newly added material
- Eliminates pauses between phrases
- Reduces fermata emphasis
- Compresses two-part transitions to avoid double caesura in home key
- Creates sense of inevitability and forward drive
- Motivated by emotional momentum from the development section

# Construction / Recognition
Compare the rhythmic profile of the recapitulation with the exposition, noting any rests, fermatas, or pauses in the exposition that have been filled in, shortened, or eliminated in the recapitulation. Look especially at the main theme (where rests may be filled with newly composed material) and the medial caesura area (where pauses may be reduced or eliminated).

# Context & Application
Greater rhythmic continuity is both an ornamental and structural change that reflects the recapitulation's different formal function. After the development section has generated maximum instability and dramatic momentum, the recapitulation needs to channel that energy toward the essential sonata closure. Interrupting this momentum with the same pauses that characterized the exposition's more leisurely presentation would undermine the formal drive toward cadential resolution.

# Examples
- General pattern: exposition's main theme contains rests between phrases; recapitulation fills these with newly added material to maintain momentum
- Two-part transitions compressed to avoid two successive HCs and standing on dominant in the home key

# Common Errors
- Attributing every rhythmic change in the recapitulation to "greater continuity" when some changes may have other motivations
- Overlooking subtle rhythmic changes (such as shortened fermatas) that contribute to the overall increase in continuity

# Common Confusions
- Greater rhythmic continuity is NOT simply faster tempo -- it refers to the elimination of pauses and interruptions while the underlying tempo remains the same
