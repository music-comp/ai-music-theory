# Quick Definition
A significant minority of transitions remain in the home key, ending with dominant harmony there (HK: HC). The subordinate theme must then begin with a direct modulation to the subordinate key. A nonmodulating transition often motivates an internal HC within the subordinate theme.

# Core Definition
A nonmodulating transition remains in the home key, destabilizing it by ending with dominant harmony (HK: HC) in contrast to the main theme's tonic ending (PAC). The subordinate theme must then begin with a **direct modulation** to the subordinate key. Since the subordinate-key dominant is left unemphasized at the end of the transition, an **internal HC** within the subordinate theme often rectifies this (Caplin, Ch. 11, pp. 331, 398).

# Key Properties
1. Significant minority of transitions
2. Ends with HK: HC or dominant arrival
3. Subordinate theme must directly modulate to SK
4. Often motivates an internal HC in the subordinate theme
5. Main theme typically ends with PAC (to create tonal contrast with HC ending)
