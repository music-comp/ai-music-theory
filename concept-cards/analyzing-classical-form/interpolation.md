# Interpolation

**Interpolation** is the insertion of material that is perceived as "outside" the formal process, disrupting the continuity of the phrase structure.

## Definition

An interpolation involves inserting material that:
- Does NOT belong to the ongoing formal function
- Creates a "parenthetical" effect
- Disrupts (rather than continues) the phrase structure

## Distinction from Internal Expansion

- **Internal expansion**: Inserted material continues the ongoing function
- **Interpolation**: Inserted material disrupts or suspends the ongoing function

## Characteristics

Interpolated material:
- Sounds foreign to its context
- Creates a sense of formal disruption
- May introduce new themes or textures
- Often clearly separable from surrounding material

## Contexts

Interpolations may occur:
- Within phrases (disrupting their flow)
- Between phrases (delaying expected continuations)
- At moments of formal transition

## Effect

The interpolated passage creates a feeling that the formal process has been momentarily suspended, only to resume when the interpolation concludes.

## Notation

In analytical diagrams, interpolations are often indicated with brackets or parentheses to show their "outside" status.
