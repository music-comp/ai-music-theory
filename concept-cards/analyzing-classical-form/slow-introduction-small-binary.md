# Quick Definition
A slow introduction may emerge as a highly deviated small binary form, with Part 1 modulating to a related key and Part 2 returning to the home key, though the form exhibits irregularities (such as elided parts or a closing section stronger than Part 2's ending) typical of the introduction's nonconventional nature.

# Core Definition
A slow introduction may emerge as a highly deviated small binary form. Part 1 opens with a fanfare and modulates to a related key (PAC). Part 2 returns to the home key (PAC or elided cadence into the exposition), and may be followed by a closing section. Several unusual features arise: the two parts of a small binary are normally never elided, as may occur here; the closing section may bring stronger closure than the end of Part 2; and the parts may be asymmetrical. Such irregularities are not surprising given the nonconventional form normally taken by a slow introduction. The binary framework provides organization without strict adherence to conventional patterns.

# Key Properties
- Part 1: opens with fanfare, modulates to related key (PAC)
- Part 2: returns to home key (PAC or elided into exposition)
- May include a closing section following Part 2
- Parts may be elided (unusual for standard small binary)
- Closing section may bring stronger closure than Part 2
- Parts may be asymmetrical
- Highly deviated from standard small binary norms
- Irregularities are typical of introduction's nonconventional nature

# Construction / Recognition
Identify a bipartite structure in the slow introduction, marked by a cadence roughly in the middle. If Part 1 modulates away from the home key and Part 2 returns, a small binary framework is present. Note deviations from standard binary practice, such as elided parts, asymmetry, or closing sections that are more cadentially decisive than the main parts.

# Context & Application
The small binary form provides a useful analytical framework for introductions that exhibit a clear bipartite tonal plan (away and back), even when the specific details deviate from standard binary practice. The binary organization creates a sense of formal completeness and tonal journey (modulation and return) that is appropriate for a large-scale introductory section. The deviations from standard binary form are characteristic of the inherent looseness of slow introductions.

# Examples
- Haydn, Symphony No. 104 ("London"), i: overall form emerges as highly deviated small binary (Part 1 = mm. 1-7, modulating to relative major; Part 2 = mm. 7-14, returning to home key), followed by closing section (mm. 14-17); two parts are elided, and closing section brings stronger cadential closure than Part 2

# Common Errors
- Expecting a slow introduction in binary form to conform to standard small binary conventions
- Treating the closing section as Part 2 because of its stronger cadential weight

# Common Confusions
- A "highly deviated" small binary is NOT a failed small binary -- the deviations are inherent to the introduction's nonconventional nature and do not invalidate the binary analytical framework
