# Quick Definition
Sonata form creates and resolves a dramatic conflict between the home key and a subordinate key: the exposition establishes the conflict, the development intensifies it, and the recapitulation resolves it by keeping everything in the home key.

# Core Definition
The exposition begins in the **home key** (HK), confirmed by a cadence, then modulates to the **subordinate key** (SK), confirmed by a PAC. The development explores additional **development keys** (DK). The recapitulation begins in and remains in the home key throughout. As a general rule, the exposition emphasizes major modality (even minor-mode movements spend most time in a major-mode SK), while the development tends to emphasize minor modalities (Caplin, Ch. 9, p. 285).

- **Sonata form overview** -- Must understand the three-section structure

# Key Properties
1. Major-mode movements: SK = dominant (V); DK = vi, iii, ii
2. Minor-mode movements: SK = relative major (III) or minor dominant (v); DK = iv, v
3. Exposition emphasizes major modality
4. Development emphasizes minor modality
5. Recapitulation remains entirely in home key, with typical subdominant emphasis early on

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Determine the home key from the main theme
2. Identify the subordinate key from the PAC closing the subordinate theme
3. In the development, listen for minor-mode regions
4. Confirm the recapitulation returns to and stays in the home key

# Context & Application
The tonal plan is described as creating a "dramatic conflict" of tonalities. The subordinate key challenges the home key's supremacy in the exposition; the development intensifies this by exploring more remote regions; the recapitulation resolves the conflict by presenting all material in the home key, allowing the home key to emerge "fully victorious."

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming the subordinate key in minor movements is always the relative major
  **Correction**: While III is the most common SK in minor, the minor dominant (v) is also used

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the development must confirm development keys with PACs
  **Clarification**: Development keys are normally confirmed by an HC, less often by a PAC
