# Quick Definition
A contrapuntal embellishment where a seventh above the bass suspends and resolves to a sixth, commonly used to embellish descending-second sequential progressions.

# Formal Definition
The 7-6 suspension is a contrapuntal ornament frequently used to embellish the descending-second (stepwise) sequential progression. A series of 7-6 suspensions creates alternation between seventh chords and triads, adding melodic interest while maintaining the descending stepwise motion.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
How the suspension works:
- Upper voice sustains the seventh above bass
- Seventh resolves down by step to sixth
- Creates preparation-suspension-resolution pattern
- Applied in series within sequences

In descending-second sequences:
- All chords typically in first inversion (to avoid parallel fifths)
- 7-6 suspensions add melodic interest
- The seventh is dissonant, the sixth consonant
- Creates alternating tension and release

# Musical Context/Application
The 7-6 suspension:
- Embellishes descending-second sequences
- Adds melodic and rhythmic variety
- Maintains proper voice leading
- Creates chain of dissonance-resolution motions

The technique transforms a simple stepwise descent into a more elaborate melodic-contrapuntal texture.

# Common Confusions
- Confusing 7-6 suspension with 5-6 pattern (different techniques, different contexts)
- Not recognizing the dissonance-resolution structure
- Treating suspended notes as chord tones
- Forgetting that the underlying progression uses first-inversion chords
