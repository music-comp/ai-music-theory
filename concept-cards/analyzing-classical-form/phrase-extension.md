# Phrase Extension

**Phrase extension** involves adding material AFTER a phrase is perceived to be functionally complete, thus lengthening the phrase beyond its normal 4-measure length.

## Definition

An extension prolongs the time span of the phrase by continuing beyond what would be its normative ending point. Material is added at the END of a phrase.

## Distinguishing Extensions

Extensions are distinguishable from other phrase deviations by:
- Their position (AFTER functional completion)
- Their content (additional material rather than inserted material)
- Their effect (prolonging rather than disrupting)

## Common Contexts

Extensions frequently occur:
- Within **continuation phrases** of sentences
- Within **consequent phrases** of periods
- Following any phrase that needs additional emphasis

## Extension vs. Internal Expansion

- **Extension**: Material added AFTER the phrase is complete
- **Internal expansion**: Material inserted WITHIN the phrase (before completion)

## Result

A phrase with extension is longer than normative. For example:
- A 4-measure continuation becomes 6 measures with extension
- A 4-measure consequent becomes 5 measures with extension

## Notation

In analytical diagrams, use the term "ext." to indicate extended passages.
