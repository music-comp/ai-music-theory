# Quick Definition
Extension adds material to a phrase, typically at its end, bringing more content than the minimum necessary to express the ongoing formal function, and resulting in a phrase longer than its normative 4-measure length.

# Core Definition
According to Caplin, extension is the most frequently occurring phrase deviation in the classical repertory. An extended phrase brings more material than is necessary to express the relevant formal function. Common situations include: repeating material (literally or varied, sometimes as an "echo"), extending continuation with additional fragments over sequential progressions, repeating material after a cadential deviation (deceptive, evaded, or abandoned cadence), and repeating material after an IAC to achieve a stronger PAC.

# Prerequisites
- **Phrase deviations** — The overarching concept of altered phrase lengths

# Key Properties
1. Most frequently occurring deviation technique
2. Adds material that continues to express the ongoing function
3. Common triggers: cadential deviations (deceptive, evaded, abandoned), IAC, echo repetition
4. Additional fragments often supported by sequential progressions
5. May result in a third statement of the basic idea in continuation
6. Differs from interpolation: extended material belongs to the ongoing function

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Determine where the phrase would normatively end
2. Identify additional material beyond that point
3. Verify the extra material continues to express the same function
4. Look for triggers (cadential deviation, IAC, repetition)

# Context & Application
Extension is far more common than interpolation. When in doubt about whether added material is extension or interpolation, Caplin advises opting for the extension interpretation unless the material clearly does not belong to the ongoing function.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 160): Mozart, Rondo K. 494, mm. 1-12 -- 2-m. basic idea extended by one bar when second half is repeated (echo effect).
**Example 2** (p. 161): Beethoven, Sonata Op. 2/3, i, mm. 1-13 -- continuation concludes first with IAC; repetition of continuation extends the theme and pushes PAC to m. 13.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Phrase deviations** — Extension is one of the four main types
## Enables
- **Theme expansion** — Extensions at the phrase level create expanded themes
## Related
- **Internal expansion** — Both add material, but expansion inserts within the phrase
- **Cadential extension** — A specific type triggered by cadential deviations
## Contrasts With
- **Phrase compression** — Opposite process (shortening)
- **Interpolation** — Interpolation inserts foreign material; extension continues the function

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing extension with interpolation
  **Correction**: Extension continues the ongoing function; interpolation disrupts it with foreign material

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking extension must use identical material
  **Clarification**: Extension can use different, contrasting material, as long as it continues to express the ongoing function
