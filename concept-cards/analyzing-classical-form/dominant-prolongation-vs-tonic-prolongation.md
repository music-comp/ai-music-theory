# Dominant Prolongation vs. Tonic Prolongation

In passages with alternating V and I chords, determining which harmony is primary requires careful analysis.

## The Analytical Challenge

Alternating V-I patterns can be heard as either:
- **Tonic prolongation**: (V) I - (V) I - (V) I
- **Dominant prolongation**: V - (I) V - (I) V - (I) V

## Determining Factors

Consider:
- **Metric position**: Which harmony falls on strong beats?
- **Resolution direction**: Does V resolve to I, or does I move to V?
- **Melodic emphasis**: Does the melody emphasize 1 or 5?
- **Contextual function**: What does the passage need to accomplish?

## No Mechanical Procedure

Distinguishing these requires:
- Musical judgment
- Careful listening
- Consideration of context

There is no formula that always works.

## Practical Implications

- **B section**: Usually dominant prolongation or emphasis
- **Presentation**: Must be tonic prolongation
- **Closing section**: Usually tonic prolongation

## When Ambiguous

In genuinely ambiguous cases:
- Both interpretations may be valid
- The passage may deliberately blur the distinction
- Acknowledge the ambiguity in analysis
