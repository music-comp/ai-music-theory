## Definition

The large ternary is a full-movement form consisting of three parts: a main theme, an interior theme, and a return of the main theme. It is used almost exclusively in slow movements and was especially cultivated by Haydn.

## Structure

| Part | Function | Tonal Region |
|------|----------|--------------|
| First Part | Main Theme | Home key (begins and ends with PAC) |
| Second Part | Interior Theme | Minore or contrasting region |
| Third Part | Return of Main Theme | Home key |

## Key Characteristics

1. **Main Theme**: Normally constructed as a complete small ternary (or sometimes small binary), beginning and ending in the home key with a PAC
2. **Interior Theme**: Creates contrast through modal shift (minore/maggiore) or different tonal region
3. **Return**: Usually brings back the main theme's complete structure, often with ornamental embellishments

## Large Ternary vs. Small Ternary

Despite sharing tripartite organization:
- Large ternary is a **full-movement form**; small ternary is a **theme type**
- The first part of large ternary is usually itself a small ternary; the A section of small ternary is a simple theme
- Large ternary always begins and ends in the home key; small ternary A section may modulate
- Different functional labels are used (main theme/interior theme vs. A/B/A')

## Coda

Following the return of the main theme, a coda may be added that:
- References material from the interior theme
- Serves compensatory functions appropriate to the movement
- Can be quite extensive, especially in Beethoven's works

## Examples

- Haydn, String Quartet in G minor ("Rider"), Op. 74, No. 3, ii
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C, K. 330, ii
- Beethoven, String Quartet in B-flat, Op. 18, No. 6, ii
