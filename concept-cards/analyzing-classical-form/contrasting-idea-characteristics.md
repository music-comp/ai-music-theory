# Quick Definition
The contrasting idea achieves contrast primarily through different melodic-motivic content and must be supported by a cadential progression (unlike the basic idea's tonic prolongation). It often displays continuation-like characteristics: fragmentation, harmonic acceleration, and sequential hints. "Contrasting" means "not a repetition" -- even minimal contrast suffices.

# Core Definition
The contrasting idea achieves its contrast with the basic idea most obviously by means of melodic-motivic content, but contrast may also be achieved or supported by secondary features such as texture, dynamics, and articulation (p. 99). Critically, the basic idea is usually supported by tonic prolongation while the contrasting idea must close with a cadential progression (p. 100). The contrasting idea often has characteristics of continuation function -- fragmentation, accelerated harmonic or surface rhythm, and even a hint of sequential harmonies (p. 101). "Contrasting" must be understood as "not a repetition"; even minimal contrast is sufficient, and when the contrasting idea resembles the basic idea, the return of the b.i. at the start of the consequent clarifies mm. 3-4 as contrasting (p. 101).

# Prerequisites
- **Basic idea** — The c.i. is defined in contrast to the b.i.
- **Antecedent phrase** — The c.i. appears in the antecedent (and consequent)

# Key Properties
1. Different melodic-motivic content from basic idea (primary distinction)
2. Supported by a cadential progression (not prolongational like the b.i.)
3. Often displays continuation-like characteristics (fragmentation, acceleration)
4. Leads to a cadence (weak in antecedent, strong in consequent)
5. "Contrasting" means "not a repetition" -- minimal contrast suffices
6. May share motives with b.i. if contour or directionality differs
7. When supported entirely by cadential progression, resembles a "cadential idea"
8. Consequent's c.i. may or may not resemble antecedent's c.i.

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Check that it is NOT a repetition of the basic idea
2. Look for different melodic content, contour, or directionality
3. Verify cadential progression (not tonic prolongation)
4. Look for continuation-like processes (fragmentation, acceleration)
5. If c.i. resembles b.i., check the harmonic distinction

# Context & Application
The harmonic distinction between basic idea and contrasting idea is crucial: the basic idea prolongs tonic, while the contrasting idea closes with a cadential progression. This is what makes a cadence possible at the end of the antecedent. When the c.i. shares motives with the b.i. (as in Ex. 3.5, Haydn Symphony 99), the underlying harmonies distinguish one from the other. When the c.i. is entirely supported by a cadential progression, it effectively resembles the "cadential idea" that closes a sentence (p. 101). The antecedent as a whole can thus resemble a "mini-sentence."

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 99, Ex. 3.3): Haydn, H. 27, iii -- c.i. brings entirely different material (running sixteenths vs. leaping rhythm), clear contrast.
**Example 2** (p. 100, Ex. 3.5): Haydn, Symphony 99, iv -- c.i. has identical rhythm to b.i.; contrast resides largely in directionality (ascending vs. descending).
**Example 3** (p. 100, Ex. 3.6): Mozart, K. 459, i -- c.i. begins with same rhythmic motive as b.i. but with entirely different harmonic progression (HC), confirming contrast.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon

## Enables
- **Antecedent phrase** — B.i. + c.i. + weak cadence = antecedent
- **Cadential closure** — The c.i.'s cadential progression enables the cadence
## Related
- **Continuation function** — C.i. often displays continuation-like characteristics
- **Cadential progression** — Supports the c.i. (vs. prolongation for b.i.)
## Contrasts With
- **Basic idea** — Prolongational vs. cadential harmony; characteristic vs. often conventional melody

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Requiring the contrasting idea to be dramatically different from the basic idea
  **Correction**: Even minimal contrast suffices; "contrasting" means "not a repetition"

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Identifying a passage as a repetition of the b.i. when it shares motives
  **Clarification**: Check the harmonic progression; if it is cadential rather than prolongational, the passage is a contrasting idea regardless of melodic similarity
