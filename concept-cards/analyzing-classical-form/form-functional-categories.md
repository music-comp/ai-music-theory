# Quick Definition
Formal functions in classical music divide into three temporal categories -- initiating (beginning), medial (middle), and concluding (ending) -- which apply hierarchically from the 2-measure idea level through the phrase level to the theme level and beyond.

# Core Definition
Caplin classifies all formal functions into three temporal categories. Initiating functions express the sense of beginning: presentation, antecedent, and compound basic idea at the phrase level, and basic idea at the idea level. The medial function (continuation) expresses "being in the middle." Concluding functions express ending: cadential and consequent at the phrase level, cadential idea at the idea level. These categories apply at multiple structural levels -- the small ternary's exposition/contrasting middle/recapitulation mirrors the initiating/medial/concluding pattern at the theme level.

# Prerequisites
- **Sentence** — Basic understanding of presentation and continuation functions
- **Period** — Basic understanding of antecedent and consequent functions

# Key Properties
1. Three temporal categories: initiating, medial, concluding
2. Hierarchical application from idea (2-m.) to phrase (4-m.) to theme (8+ m.) levels
3. Initiating: presentation, antecedent, compound basic idea, basic idea
4. Medial: continuation
5. Concluding: cadential, consequent, cadential idea
6. Syntactically valid themes respect the temporal ordering of these categories

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Determine the structural level (idea, phrase, theme)
2. Classify each unit according to its temporal function
3. Verify that the ordering follows temporal logic

# Context & Application
This classification system is foundational to Caplin's theory and explains why certain combinations of functions are syntactical while others are not. It also reveals deep structural parallels across hierarchical levels.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 125): At the phrase level: presentation/antecedent/c.b.i. (initiating) -> continuation (medial) -> cadential/consequent (concluding).
**Example 2**: At the theme level in small ternary: A section (initiating/exposition) -> B section (medial/contrasting middle) -> A' section (concluding/recapitulation).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Applying the categories only at the phrase level
  **Correction**: The categories apply hierarchically at idea, phrase, theme, and larger levels

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking "continuation" is a concluding function because it ends with a cadence
  **Clarification**: Continuation is medial; it fuses with cadential function, but its primary role is "being in the middle"
