# Schoenberg's View of the Coda

Arnold Schoenberg's perspective on the coda's formal function.

## Schoenberg's Statement

"Since many movements have no codas, it is evident that the coda must be considered as an extrinsic addition. The assumption that it serves to establish the tonality is hardly justified; it could scarcely compensate for the failure to establish the tonality in the previous sections. In fact, it would be difficult to give any other reason for the addition of a coda than that the composer wants to say something more."

## Interpretation

Though Schoenberg speaks somewhat flippantly about the coda appearing "merely because the composer has more to say," codas clearly allow composers to say things that could not be appropriately said in earlier sections.

## Significance

This view supports understanding the coda as:
- An extrinsic (not essential) addition
- Not necessary for tonal establishment
- A vehicle for compensatory functions
- An after-the-end expression
