# Quick Definition
The closing ritornello should not be confused with a coda: it brings back material in the same way, lacks loosening devices, rarely references main-theme ideas, and creates frame through matching closing sections. Beethoven's Emperor Concerto confirms the distinction by having a genuine coda after its closing ritornello.

# Core Definition
The ritornello brings back material in much the same way it appeared earlier (coda sets ideas in fresh contexts). A closing ritornello does not normally feature loosening devices. Most codas refer to main-theme ideas for circularity; a closing ritornello rarely does, creating its sense of frame through matching closing sections. The genuine coda at the end of Beethoven's Emperor Concerto confirms how unlike a regular closing ritornello it is (Caplin, pp. 717-718).

# Key Properties
1. Material brought back in same way (vs. coda's fresh contexts)
2. No loosening devices (vs. coda's sequential extensions, cadential expansions)
3. Rarely references main theme (vs. coda's circularity)
4. Frame through matching closing sections (vs. coda's thematic return)
5. Beethoven Emperor Concerto: genuine coda follows closing ritornello, proving distinction
