## The Question

Inasmuch as the closing ritornello follows the end of the recapitulation, we might think it functions as the coda of the movement.

## Why the Closing Ritornello Is Not a Coda

### 1. Material Treatment
- **Ritornello**: Brings back material in much the same way as earlier
- **Coda**: Almost always sets earlier ideas in a **fresh formal context**

### 2. Loosening Devices
- **Ritornello**: Does not normally feature loosening devices
- **Coda**: Typically includes sequential extensions, cadential expansions

### 3. Main-Theme Reference
- **Ritornello**: Rarely contains material from the main theme
- **Coda**: Most codas refer to main-theme ideas for **formal circularity**

### 4. Sense of Closure
- **Ritornello**: Creates formal frame through **matching closing sections** (like a rhyme)
- **Coda**: Creates closure through thematic return and developmental resolution

## Confirmation: Beethoven's Emperor Concerto

The first movement of the *Emperor* Concerto contains a **genuine coda** at the end, following the closing ritornello. This confirms how unlike a regular closing ritornello a true coda is:
- The coda brings different formal organization
- Uses loosening devices
- Creates different sense of closure
- Follows the ritornello as a separate section
