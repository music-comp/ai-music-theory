## General Principle

In classical form, "contrast" usually entails:
- Formal complication
- Increased emotionality

## Evidence at Multiple Levels

### Contrasting Middle (B Section)
- Tends to be more complex and intense than outer parts

### Contrasting Idea
- Exhibits greater intensity in harmony and rhythm
- Compared to the preceding basic idea

### Middle Functions Generally
A "middle function" at any level is more intense than a beginning function:
- Continuation vs. presentation
- Development vs. exposition
- Interior theme vs. main theme
- Rondo couplet vs. refrain

## The Exception: Trio

The trio of minuet/trio form is the **main exception** to this principle:
- Does not bring greater complexity or emotional intensification
- Instead brings **simplification** and **relaxation**
- More diatonic harmony
- More uniform rhythms
- Less dense texture

## Historical Explanation

The term "trio" derives from 17th-century practice:
- Reducing texture in this part of a dance movement
- Using just three instruments
- The lighter scoring tradition continued into classical practice
