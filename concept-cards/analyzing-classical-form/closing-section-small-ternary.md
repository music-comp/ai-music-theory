# Quick Definition
Since the A section of a small ternary closes with a PAC, an optional closing section or codettas may follow before the B section begins, functioning as a postcadential framing element external to the theme proper.

# Core Definition
After the A section achieves its PAC, one or more codettas may appear as postcadential material. This closing section is external to the A section proper and is not counted as part of the 8-measure thematic structure. A typical closing section features multiple codettas with progressive fragmentation (e.g., 2 mm. to 1 m. to 1 m.) and recessive dynamics (dying away). A simpler alternative is a single codetta providing brief confirmation of closure before leading into the B section. Harmonically, closing sections prolong tonic and may include simple I-V-I motion confirming the key established by the PAC.

# Key Properties
1. Appears after the A section's PAC
2. External to the theme proper (a framing function)
3. Not counted in the 8-measure structure
4. May consist of multiple codettas with progressive fragmentation
5. Prolongs tonic harmony
6. Recessive dynamic character (dying away)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate the PAC ending the A section
2. Check for postcadential material before the B section begins
3. Look for tonic prolongation and fragmentation
4. Note that this material is external to the thematic structure

# Context & Application
The closing section in a small ternary A section functions identically to closing sections in other contexts (e.g., after subordinate themes in sonata form). It confirms cadential closure and creates a brief moment of repose before the contrasting middle introduces instability. Its presence or absence does not affect the essential form of the A section.
