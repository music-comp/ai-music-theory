# Quick Definition
Three chromatic pre-dominant harmonies (Italian, German, French sixths) that resolve to V. All contain the raised fourth scale degree (leading tone of V) and are usually built over the lowered sixth degree. They are a subclass of pre-dominants derived from V/V.

# Core Definition
The three varieties of augmented-sixth chords -- the so-called Italian, German, and French sixths -- are an important subclass of pre-dominant harmonies derived from the dominant of the dominant. Though they all contain the raised fourth scale degree (sharp-4), they are usually built over the sixth degree of the natural minor scale (b6). On occasion, these harmonies may be repositioned so that sharp-4 is placed in the bass voice (pp. 32-33). They are notated with their "national" labels: It+6, Gr+6, Fr+6 (p. 46).

# Key Properties
1. Three types: Italian (It+6), German (Gr+6), French (Fr+6)
2. All contain sharp-4 (leading tone of V) and b6
3. The augmented-sixth interval spans from b6 to sharp-4
4. Usually built with b6 in bass, sharp-4 in upper voice
5. May be repositioned with sharp-4 in bass
6. Function as pre-dominants, resolving to V
7. Derived from V/V (secondary dominant of the dominant)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for the characteristic augmented-sixth interval (b6 to sharp-4)
2. Check for b6 in bass (most common) or sharp-4 in bass
3. Italian: augmented sixth + tonic pitch; German: adds b3; French: adds scale degree 2
4. Verify resolution to V

# Context & Application
Augmented-sixth chords function as pre-dominants within cadential progressions, creating intense chromatic approach to the dominant. They are particularly effective in half-cadential contexts (as seen in Mozart K. 332 with Italian sixth) and in the phrygian cadence idiom. The raised-fourth degree's significance is highlighted by its normally being placed in the bass voice, enhancing motion to the dominant root.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Analyzing augmented-sixth chords as independent chord types rather than functional pre-dominants
  **Correction**: They function as pre-dominants within cadential progressions

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Not recognizing the relationship to V/V
  **Clarification**: All augmented sixths contain sharp-4, the leading tone of V, linking them to V/V
- **Confusion**: Confusing the three types
  **Clarification**: Italian = simplest (aug6 + tonic); German = adds b3; French = adds scale degree 2
