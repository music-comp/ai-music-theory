# Quick Definition
Caplin's Figure 8.1 summarizes five distinguishing features: rounded binary always has recapitulation (small binary never); rounded binary always has a contrasting middle (small binary sometimes lacks one); A section ends with PAC (Part 1 may end with HC); rounded binary exhibits variable grouping patterns (small binary is usually 8+8); B section usually begins with new material (Part 2 often begins motivically like Part 1).

# Core Definition
The essential difference is the presence or absence of recapitulation. What truly distinguishes the two theme types is whether the initial basic idea, harmonized by tonic of the home key, returns later. Ratz incorrectly identified the absence of a contrasting middle as the key distinction, but Caplin shows that small binaries may include contrasting middles.

# Key Properties
1. Recapitulation: rounded binary = always; small binary = never (ESSENTIAL)
2. Contrasting middle: rounded binary = always; small binary = sometimes
3. Part 1 cadence: A section = PAC; Part 1 = PAC or HC
4. Grouping: rounded binary = variable; small binary = usually 8+8
5. Part 2 opening: B = new material; Part 2 = often related to Part 1
