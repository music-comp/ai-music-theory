# Quick Definition
Phrase compression shortens a phrase below its normative 4-measure length, typically to 3 measures, by reducing the amount of material needed to express the formal function.

# Core Definition
Compression involves expressing a formal function in fewer measures than normal. A compressed phrase arrives at its goal earlier than expected, creating tighter construction. Compression most commonly affects the continuation phrase, which may be reduced to 3 measures. It can also affect consequent phrases. Compression and extension may occur within the same theme, potentially balancing each other.

# Prerequisites
- **Phrase deviations** — The framework for all deviation techniques

# Key Properties
1. Phrase shortened below normative 4 measures (typically to 3)
2. Achieves tighter construction
3. May create asymmetrical grouping structures
4. Most common in continuation phrases
5. Can compensate for extensions elsewhere in the theme

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Identify a phrase shorter than the expected 4 measures
2. Verify that the function is still expressed (it arrives at its goal, just sooner)
3. Check if compression balances an extension elsewhere

# Context & Application
Compression is the opposite of extension. It is less common than extension but important for creating phrase-structural variety and for the compressed recapitulations discussed in Chapter 7.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 148): A continuation phrase compressed to 3 measures, arriving at the cadence earlier than expected.

## Contrasts With

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Calling any short phrase "compressed" without verifying that the full function is expressed
  **Correction**: Compression implies that the function is complete despite the reduced length; a genuinely incomplete function may indicate a different structural reading
