# Phrase Compression

**Phrase compression** involves shortening a phrase below its normative 4-measure length, typically to 3 measures.

## Definition

A compressed phrase occupies fewer than 4 real measures by eliminating or condensing expected material.

## Result

A compressed phrase:
- Typically occupies 3 measures instead of 4
- May create asymmetrical phrase structures
- Often occurs in the continuation phrase of a sentence

## Common Contexts

Compression frequently occurs in:
- Continuation phrases (3 measures instead of 4)
- Consequent phrases (when theme needs tighter construction)

## Causes of Compression

Compression results from:
- Eliminating expected material (e.g., skipping fragmentation)
- Condensing two expected ideas into one
- Arriving at cadential material earlier than expected

## Relation to Extension

Extension and compression are opposing processes:
- Extension: Adds material, lengthens phrase
- Compression: Removes material, shortens phrase

These processes may occur within the same theme, potentially balancing each other.

## Theme-Level Effects

A compressed phrase alters the overall theme length:
- Compressed continuation: 7-measure sentence instead of 8
- Compressed consequent: 7-measure period instead of 8
