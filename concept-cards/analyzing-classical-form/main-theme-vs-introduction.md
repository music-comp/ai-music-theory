## Main Theme vs. Introduction

The hesitancies and uncertainties projected by some exposition openings can lead to confusion about formal functions. A main theme may seem to possess an "introductory" character.

### Critical Distinction

Do not confuse such main themes with an actual introduction:
- Despite textural, dynamic, and rhythmic instabilities
- The main theme still contains sufficient **tonal, cadential, and phrase-structural solidity**
- It functions as the true formal beginning of the exposition

### Distinguishing Features

**Main Theme** (even with introductory qualities):
- Establishes home key
- Contains cadential articulation
- Has clear phrase structure

**Actual Introduction**:
- May be harmonically unstable
- Lacks full cadential closure
- Prepares for (rather than being) the formal beginning

### Multiple Introduction Types

A main theme may be preceded by:
- **Thematic introduction**: Brief passage before the main theme proper
- **Slow introduction**: More substantial section (different tempo) before the entire exposition
