# Quick Definition
Pre-dominant function prepares and intensifies motion toward the dominant, expressed by a wide variety of chords organized into two main types: those built over scale degree 4 in the bass (II6, IV, bII6) and those derived from V/V (VII7/V, augmented-sixth chords).

# Core Definition
The large number of pre-dominant harmonies within a key generally relate to one of two main types: those built above the fourth degree of the scale in the bass voice and those derived from the dominant of the dominant (V/V). The raised-fourth scale degree (sharp-4), which functions as the leading tone of the dominant, is highlighted by its normally being placed in the bass voice (pp. 32-33). Examination of the classical literature reveals that II6 is more often employed than IV for this function (p. 32).

# Key Properties
1. Two main sub-types: built over scale degree 4, or derived from V/V
2. Built over 4: II6 (most typical), IV, II7, IV7, bII6 (Neapolitan, especially in minor)
3. Derived from V/V: VII7/V (most typical), V6/V, V/V, augmented-sixth chords (It+6, Gr+6, Fr+6)
4. II6 is more commonly employed than IV in classical music
5. Augmented sixths contain sharp-4 but are usually built over b6
6. VI is NOT a pre-dominant; it is a tonic substitute (p. 32)
7. Pre-dominant harmonies can appear in various positions (IV6, II4/3, bII)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify Pre-dominant Function:
1. Look for II, IV, bII, or their inversions and seventh-chord forms
2. Look for chords containing sharp-4 (raised fourth degree) -- these derive from V/V
3. Check for augmented-sixth chords (characteristic augmented sixth interval)
4. Confirm the harmony prepares motion to dominant

# Context & Application
Pre-dominant function appears primarily within cadential progressions, positioned between the initial tonic and the cadential dominant. Complete cadential progressions contain both initial tonic and pre-dominant. Incomplete ones may omit initial tonic more often than pre-dominant, since eliminating the latter results in the loss of a fundamental harmonic function (p. 37). Pre-dominant can also be enriched through modal borrowing and chromatic alteration.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Using IV as the default pre-dominant in analysis
  **Correction**: II6 is more frequently employed in classical music than IV

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Classifying VI as pre-dominant when it progresses to V
  **Clarification**: VI is a tonic substitute; in VI-V, tonic function moves directly to dominant, bypassing pre-dominant
- **Confusion**: Not recognizing V/V-derived pre-dominants as pre-dominant
  **Clarification**: Chords with sharp-4 (including augmented sixths) are pre-dominants, not dominants
