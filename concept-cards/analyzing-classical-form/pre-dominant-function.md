# Quick Definition
The harmonic function that prepares and intensifies motion toward the dominant, expressed by a wide variety of chords including II, IV, bII, secondary dominants of V, and augmented-sixth chords.

# Formal Definition
Pre-dominant harmonies include a wide variety of chords that prepare the dominant. The large number of pre-dominant harmonies within a key generally relate to one of two main types: (1) those built above the fourth degree of the scale in the bass voice, and (2) those derived from the dominant of the dominant (V/V).

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
**Pre-dominants built over scale degree 4:**
- **II6**: The supertonic triad in first inversion (most frequently employed)
- **IV**: The subdominant triad
- **II7, IV7**: Seventh-chord versions
- **bII6** (Neapolitan): Important in minor mode

**Pre-dominants derived from V/V:**
- **VII7/V**: Diminished-seventh of the dominant (most typical)
- **V6/V, V/V**: Less dissonant forms
- **Augmented-sixth chords**: Italian, German, and French sixths

The raised-fourth scale degree (the leading tone of V) is highlighted by its placement in the bass voice, enhancing motion to the dominant.

# Musical Context/Application
Pre-dominant function appears primarily within cadential progressions, positioned between the initial tonic and the cadential dominant:
- Complete cadential progressions contain both initial tonic and pre-dominant
- Incomplete cadential progressions may omit initial tonic more often than pre-dominant
- Eliminating pre-dominant results in the loss of a fundamental harmonic function

Many theorists mistakenly classify VI as pre-dominant when it progresses to V, but it is generally better to understand VI as a tonic substitute that bypasses the pre-dominant function.

# Common Confusions
- Treating VI as a pre-dominant when it typically functions as a tonic substitute
- Confusing the subdominant (IV) as the "most typical" pre-dominant when II6 is actually more common in classical music
- Not recognizing that pre-dominants derived from V/V are a distinct category from those built over scale degree 4
