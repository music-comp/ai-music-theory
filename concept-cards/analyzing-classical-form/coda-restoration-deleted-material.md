# Restoration of Deleted Material in Coda

Returning material in the coda that was eliminated from the recapitulation.

## The Situation

To fulfill its own formal functions, the recapitulation frequently deletes passages from the exposition. The coda offers possibility of "recapitulating" this deleted material.

## Example: Dramatic Tonicization

If a dramatic tonicization of a particular key was deleted because tonal adjustment would place it in a less dramatic key, the coda can restore a comparable harmonic event at its original tonal level.

## Example: Closing Section

If closing section material was deleted to make way for coda, it may return as the coda's own closing section. This creates impression of coda being interpolated in (rather than following) the recapitulation.

## Haydn's Practice

Haydn frequently employs this technique, which, given his tendency to significantly rewrite recapitulations, can create considerable ambiguity about whether the movement contains an independent coda.
