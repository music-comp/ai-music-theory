# Quick Definition
The coda offers the possibility of "recapitulating" passages deleted from the recapitulation, including dramatic harmonic events that could not be transposed into the home key and closing-section material replaced by new material.

# Core Definition
"To fulfill its own particular formal functions, the recapitulation frequently deletes passages that appeared in the exposition. The possibility of 'recapitulating' this deleted material is offered by the coda" (p. 561). This includes dramatic tonicizations that would lose their effect if transposed for tonal adjustment, and closing-section material that was replaced. When closing-section material is restored at the coda's end, "it can give the impression of coda being interpolated in, rather than following on, the recapitulation."

# Key Properties
1. Allows "recapitulation" of material deleted from the recapitulation
2. Dramatic harmonic events can be restored at their original tonal level
3. Closing-section material may be restored as coda's closing section
4. Restoration of closing material creates "interpolation" effect
5. Haydn frequently employs this technique

# Examples
**Example 15.7** (p. 555): Haydn, Piano Trio in C, H. 27, iii — dramatic B-minor tonicization from exposition restored in coda (mm. 214-21) at original pitch level; closing-section material from exposition (deleted in recapitulation) restored at end of coda (mm. 255-61).
