## Definition

All rondo forms in Western art music display the same basic pattern of organization: a principal thematic idea (the "rondo refrain" or "theme") alternates regularly with two or more contrasting passages (variously termed "couplets," "episodes," or "digressions").

## Letter Labeling

Traditional letter labels:
- **A** = Theme or refrain
- **B, C, D** = Couplets of differing melodic-motivic material

Examples:
- ABACA (five-part rondo)
- ABACADA (seven-part rondo)
- ABACABA (sonata-rondo)

## Two Main Classical Types

### 1. Five-Part Rondo (ABACA)
- Three refrains, two couplets
- Used most often for slow movements
- Sometimes as fast finale

### 2. Sonata-Rondo (ABACABA)
- Four refrains, three couplets
- Combines rondo and sonata elements
- Used almost exclusively for fast finales

## Limitations of Letter Labels

Letter labels have serious limitations:
- Tell something about melodic-motivic material
- Indicate little about **formal function**
- Can be confused with small ternary labels (A-B-A')

Better approach: Use labels that:
1. Distinguish refrains from couplets (numbered by position)
2. Specify the formal function of each part
