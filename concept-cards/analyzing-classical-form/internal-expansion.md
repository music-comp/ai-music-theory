# Internal Expansion

**Internal expansion** involves inserting additional material WITHIN a phrase, before it reaches its point of functional completion.

## Definition

Material is embedded within the phrase's boundaries, expanding it from within rather than adding to its end.

## Distinguishing from Extension

- **Extension**: Material added AFTER phrase completion
- **Internal expansion**: Material inserted BEFORE phrase completion

## Characteristics

Internally expanded phrases:
- Are longer than normative
- Contain material that could be removed without disrupting basic function
- Have the insertion occur before the cadence

## Common Techniques

Internal expansion is often achieved through:
- Repetition of a unit within the phrase
- Sequential treatment of an idea
- Delay of expected cadential material

## Example Contexts

Internal expansion occurs in:
- Presentation phrases (basic idea repeated more than once)
- Continuation phrases (fragmented units repeated)
- Antecedent phrases (contrasting idea expanded)

## Analysis

When analyzing internal expansion:
1. Identify where normative phrase would end
2. Locate the inserted material
3. Determine what function the insertion serves
4. Note how the phrase "resumes" its normal course
