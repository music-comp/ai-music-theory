## Definition

A number of slow movements, particularly by Haydn, end with dominant harmony rather than the expected final PAC. This is a remnant of standard high-Baroque practice, where the resolution to tonic occurs only at the beginning of the following movement.

## Effect

- Forges a stronger bond between movements
- May undermine the slow movement's independence
- In extreme cases, the slow movement can seem like an introduction to the subsequent movement (e.g., Beethoven's "Waldstein" Sonata "Introduzione")

## Techniques for Ending on Dominant

### 1. Appended Passage
Movement closes regularly with PAC, then an appended passage leads to a final dominant

### 2. Getting "Stuck"
Music finds itself unexpectedly "stuck" on the dominant, which becomes de facto the ending harmony

### 3. Failed Cadence
Expected closing authentic cadence fails to materialize; HC or dominant arrival appears instead (usually after a deceptive cadence)

## Relation to Interior Theme

These techniques recall the kinds of endings on dominant found at the close of an interior theme in large ternary form:
- A' section fails to achieve PAC
- Form remains incomplete
- Dominant harmony motivates the return of the main theme

## Historical Context

This practice derives from Baroque suite movements where dances were sometimes paired and connected through harmonic openness.
