# Quick Definition
A transition's HC requires the dominant to initially appear as a root-position triad (subsequent prolongation may add seventh or invert). The HC is very frequently approached by a pre-dominant with raised-4 in the bass (vii/V or augmented sixth). If approached by root-position V/V, the effect of modulation to the dominant creates a "reinterpreted" HC.

# Core Definition
A transition can be concluded most definitively by an HC. The dominant must initially appear as a **triad in root position** (subsequent prolongation may introduce a seventh or invert the harmony). The dominant is very frequently approached by a pre-dominant featuring raised-4 in the bass (vii7/V or V65/V). A variant places raised-4 in an upper voice, supported by an **augmented-sixth harmony** as pre-dominant -- a standard signal for the transition's impending end, often arising from a modal shift to the minor mode of the subordinate key. If the HC is approached by root-position V/V, the implied modulation to the dominant creates a "reinterpreted" HC (Caplin, Ch. 11, pp. 350-351).

# Key Properties
1. Dominant must initially appear as root-position triad
2. Subsequent prolongation may add seventh or invert
3. Commonly approached by raised-4 in bass (vii/V, augmented sixth)
4. Augmented sixth approach often from modal shift to minor SK
5. V/V approach may create "reinterpreted" HC
