## Half Cadence in Transition

A transition can be concluded most definitively by means of a **half cadence**.

### Requirements

According to the rule, the dominant of an HC must:
- Initially appear as a **triad in root position**
- Subsequent prolongation may introduce a dissonant seventh
- May even invert the harmony after initial root-position statement

### Common Approach to the HC

The dominant is very frequently approached by a pre-dominant featuring **raised 4** (the "leading tone" of the dominant) in the bass voice.

Common harmonies for this approach:
- vii°7/V or vii°65/V
- V65/V

### Augmented-Sixth Approach

A variant progression places raised 4 in an upper voice, supported by an **augmented-sixth harmony** as pre-dominant.

This standard signal for the impending end of a transition may arise from:
- Modal shift (move to minor mode of subordinate key)
- Natural chromatic approach to the dominant

### Reinterpreted HC

If the HC is approached by root-position V/V:
- The implied effect of modulation to the dominant might occur
- We hear a "reinterpreted" HC (initially sounds like I, becomes V)
