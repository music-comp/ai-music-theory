# Deletion Due to Development Use

When material is deleted from the recapitulation because it was prominently used in the development.

## The Principle

If an idea is highly exploited in the development section, it becomes appropriate (though not necessary) to delete it in the recapitulation.

## Rationale

- Material is already well-known to the listener
- Extensive treatment in development makes further repetition potentially redundant
- Deletion helps maintain formal balance and variety

## Not a Rule

This is a tendency, not a rule. The classical repertory abounds in counterexamples. The relationship is one of appropriateness, not causation.

## Example

An opening phrase from the exposition's subordinate theme may be used in the development as the last part of the pre-core. When this phrase is then deleted from the recapitulation's subordinate theme, the development use helps explain (though not determine) the deletion.
