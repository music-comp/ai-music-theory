# Quick Definition
When material is highly exploited in the development section, it becomes appropriate (though not necessary) to delete it from the recapitulation, since the listener is already thoroughly familiar with it.

# Core Definition
If an idea is highly exploited in the development section, it becomes appropriate -- though not necessary -- to delete it in the recapitulation. The material is already well known to the listener, and extensive treatment in the development makes further repetition potentially redundant. However, this is a tendency, not a rule: the classical repertory abounds in counterexamples where material used extensively in the development is retained in the recapitulation. The relationship is one of appropriateness, not causation.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of development section organization and its treatment of expositional material
- Familiarity with deletion of thematic restatements in the recapitulation

# Key Properties
- Material extensively treated in the development may be deleted from the recapitulation
- This is a tendency or correlation, not a strict rule
- Classical repertory contains many counterexamples
- Development use helps explain but does not determine deletion
- The listener's familiarity with the material provides the rationale
- Helps maintain formal balance and variety

# Construction / Recognition
When analyzing deletions in the recapitulation, check whether the deleted material was prominently treated in the development. If so, the development use provides a plausible motivation for the deletion. However, do not assume deletion must occur simply because material was used in the development.

# Context & Application
This principle is particularly relevant when analyzing why specific passages from the exposition's subordinate theme, main theme, or transition are absent from the recapitulation. Development use is one of several factors that can motivate structural changes in the recapitulation, alongside functional differences and concerns about tonal monotony.

# Examples
- An opening phrase from the exposition's subordinate theme may serve as the last part of the development's pre-core; when this phrase is then deleted from the recapitulation's subordinate theme, the development use helps explain the deletion
- General pattern: ideas prominently used in the development core may be reduced or eliminated in the recapitulation

# Common Errors
- Treating development use as a deterministic rule: "it was in the development, so it must be deleted"
- Ignoring development use as a possible motivation when analyzing recapitulation deletions

# Common Confusions
- Development use is NOT a cause of deletion -- it is a correlation or tendency that makes deletion appropriate but does not require it; many movements retain development-exploited material in the recapitulation
