# Quick Definition
Melodic overlap is a boundary process where the melodic goal of one group occurs on the downbeat beginning the next group, without the full structural overlap characteristic of elision.

# Core Definition
Melodic overlap resembles elision in that the goal of the melody of one group occurs on the downbeat beginning the next group. Unlike elision, however, we do not have the impression that the entire measure defined by this downbeat belongs to the prior group. The measure prior to the melodic overlap is the final measure of the group, while the moment of overlap represents essentially the beginning of the next group. This boundary process is especially common between successive codettas in a closing section.

# Prerequisites
- **Closing section** — The primary context for melodic overlap
- **Codetta** — The units between which overlap occurs
- **Elision** — Must understand to distinguish from overlap

# Key Properties
1. Melodic goal of one group lands on downbeat of next group
2. Less strong than elision
3. Prior measure is definitively the last bar of the first group
4. Especially common between codettas
5. Shown with overlapping brackets in notation

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. In a closing section, check if each codetta's melody ends on the downbeat beginning the next
2. Determine whether it is full elision or just melodic overlap
3. Use overlapping brackets

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 156): Beethoven, Op. 22, ii -- closing section codettas connected by melodic overlap.
**Example 2** (p. 168): Mozart, K. 332, iii -- each codetta begins and ends on tonic scale degree, creating melodic overlaps.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Closing section** — The primary context
## Related
- **Elision** — Similar but stronger boundary process
## Contrasts With
- **Elision** — In elision the overlapping measure belongs to both groups; in melodic overlap it primarily begins the new group
