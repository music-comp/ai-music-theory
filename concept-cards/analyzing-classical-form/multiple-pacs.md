## Multiple PACs in Subordinate-Theme Group

The concept of multiple subordinate themes brings with it the notion that a sonata exposition will contain **multiple perfect authentic cadences** in the new key.

### The Question of Relative Importance

Is one of these PACs more important than the others?

**First PAC emphasis** (Hepokoski-Darcy):
- The first SK: PAC is of greatest structural import
- It achieves the principal tonal goal of the exposition
- They term it the "essential expositional closure"

**Final PAC emphasis** (alternative view):
- The highly dramatic cadential arrival in the final subordinate theme
- Often after massive ECP
- Greatest cadential articulation

### Syntactical vs. Rhetorical Strength

**Syntactical perspective**: All PACs are equally strong
- They all fulfill necessary requirements for thematic closure
- No cadence is formally "more important"

**Rhetorical perspective**: Cadences display a range of expression
- Some seem weak and tame
- Others highly dramatic and assertive
- Varies from piece to piece

### Recommendation

Take each subordinate-theme group on its own terms:
- Analyze rhetorical differences among cadences ad hoc
- Don't assume any PAC is automatically most important
