# Quick Definition
Ornamental changes are surface-level modifications in the recapitulation involving dynamics, instrumentation, register, texture, accompanimental figuration, and melodic embellishments -- motivated by aesthetic concerns for variety and particular expressive values rather than by functional differences.

# Core Definition
Ornamental changes involve dynamics, instrumentation, register, texture, accompanimental figuration, melodic embellishments, and the like. These changes are motivated by general aesthetic concerns for variety and by the particular expressive values that the composer wishes to convey in the movement. Ornamental changes do not involve harmonic-tonal organization, melodic-motivic material (at a deep level), grouping structure, or formal functions. Most analysis focuses on structural changes since they most directly affect formal organization; ornamental changes are observed and explained as they arise.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of the analytical methodology of comparison for recapitulations

# Key Properties
- Involve dynamics, instrumentation, register, texture, accompanimental figuration, melodic embellishments
- Motivated by aesthetic concerns for variety and particular expressive values
- Do NOT involve harmonic-tonal organization, melodic-motivic material, grouping structure, or formal functions
- Surface-level modifications that do not alter fundamental structure
- Observed and explained as they arise in analysis
- May serve to intensify cadential arrivals

# Construction / Recognition
Compare the recapitulation bar by bar with the exposition. Changes that affect only the surface parameters (dynamics, instrumentation, register, texture, accompaniment, embellishment) without altering the underlying harmonic, motivic, grouping, or functional structure are ornamental. Changes that affect the deeper structural parameters are structural, not ornamental.

# Context & Application
While ornamental changes receive less analytical attention than structural changes, they can have significant musical effects. A change of instrumentation may give familiar material a completely new character. A change of register may be necessitated by transposition into the home key. Added melodic embellishments may intensify a cadential approach. These changes reflect the composer's concern for variety and expression within the constraints of formal repetition.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C, K. 545, i, mm. 67-71: cadential phrase in recapitulation features ornamental changes in harmony (added pre-dominant prolongation) and rhythm (added sixteenth notes) creating greater intensity
- General pattern: reorchestration, dynamic changes, or added embellishments in the recapitulation that enhance the character without altering formal structure

# Common Errors
- Ignoring ornamental changes entirely in favor of structural analysis
- Classifying ornamental changes as structural when they do not alter harmonic-tonal organization, motivic material, grouping, or formal function

# Common Confusions
- Some changes blur the boundary between ornamental and structural -- for example, a change that adds a chromatic harmony to intensify a cadence is technically ornamental (it does not alter the cadential function) but approaches structural significance
