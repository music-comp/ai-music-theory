## Premature Dominant Arrival

A **premature dominant arrival** occurs when the harmonic arrival on the dominant does not coordinate with melodic or grouping processes - the music seems to get stuck "too early" on the dominant.

### Non-Coordination

The moment of dominant arrival fails to coordinate with what we hear as the end of:
- Melodic processes
- Grouping processes
- Phrase completion

### With Sequential Progressions

Common with descending-fifth sequences:
- The final link of the sequential chain is the dominant
- But ongoing melodic activity isn't concluded until later
- Harmonic goal considerably precedes melodic goal

### With Cadential Progression

Even if the final harmonic progression seems cadential, factors can obscure closure:
- Cadential six-four is prolonged too long before resolving
- Non-coordination of harmonic arrival and melodic-motivic arrival

### Sequence vs. Cadence

Important distinction:
- A sequential progression ending with II-V-I does not normally create a cadence
- The final unit is a **repetition** of a prior unit, so it doesn't function as a closing unit
- Classical composers usually write a distinct cadential progression **after** the sequence
