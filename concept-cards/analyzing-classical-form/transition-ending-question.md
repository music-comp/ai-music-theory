## Where Does the Transition End?

The final portion of the standing on the dominant can give the impression of an extended upbeat to the subordinate theme, causing confusion about where the transition ends.

### Common Confusion

The drive to resolve dominant onto tonic at subordinate theme's start can lead to:
- Identifying a "point of arrival" at tonic
- Mistakenly identifying subordinate theme entrance as a cadence marking transition's end

### Critical Distinction

Do not confuse:
- "Resolution of dominant to tonic" (harmonic)
- "Arrival at a functional end" (formal)

### The Correct Analysis

The initial tonic of the subordinate theme signals the **beginning** of that unit, **not** the end of the transition.

The transition's **structural end** is marked earlier by the HC.

### Structural End vs. Literal End

| | Structural End | Literal End |
|---|---|---|
| Marked by | HC or dominant arrival | Just before subordinate theme |
| May include | - | Standing on dominant, medial caesura |
| Position | Earlier | Later |

Sometimes these two moments occur simultaneously; often they arise at different points.
