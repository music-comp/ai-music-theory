## Nonconventional Main Theme Types

Many sonata forms begin with main themes that are **nonconventional** in organization; they cannot be easily accommodated by standard categories like sentence, period, hybrid, and small ternary.

### Frequency

First-movement forms are particularly likely to feature nonconventionally organized main themes. Slow-movement and finale main themes are usually conventional.

### Distinction from Deviants

It would be convenient to distinguish a nonconventional theme from a conventional one that "deviates from the norm"; however, the distinction is not always clear-cut.

### Still Tight-Knit

Although thematic nonconventionality is a criterion of loose organization, a nonconventional main theme is usually **more tightly knit** than the subsequent thematic units of the movement. Many are still eight measures in length.

### Analytical Approach (Bottom-Up)

For nonconventional themes, use a **bottom-up** approach:
1. Consider harmonic organization first
2. Group material into basic units on the surface
3. Establish higher-level groupings
4. Assign formal functions to the groups (initiating, medial, concluding)
