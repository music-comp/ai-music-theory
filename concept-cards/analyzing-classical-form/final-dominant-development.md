# Quick Definition
The dominant harmony concluding the development is an "ultimate" dominant that resolves to tonic to initiate the recapitulation, but this V-I resolution is NOT an authentic cadence — it marks a rebeginning, not a close.

# Core Definition
"Seeing as the goal harmony of the development is dominant — an ultimate dominant — we must avoid the temptation to recognize an authentic cadence when it resolves to tonic in order to initiate the recapitulation" (p. 499). "Especially when the opening tonic appears with great dynamic force, it may give the misleading impression that the motion from V to I has a cadential quality." The resolution initiates a new formal section rather than closing a previous one.

# Prerequisites
- **Development section** — The section ending with this dominant
- **Recapitulation section** — The section initiated by its resolution

# Key Properties
1. Development's final harmony is dominant of the home key
2. Termed an "ultimate" dominant
3. Resolution to tonic initiates the recapitulation
4. This V-I motion is NOT cadential
5. Dynamic force (especially Beethoven) can create misleading cadential impression
6. The resolution marks a "rebeginning," not a conclusion

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Labeling the V-I at the recapitulation's start as a PAC
  **Correction**: This motion initiates a new section; it is a beginning, not an ending; authentic cadences close phrases, not open sections
