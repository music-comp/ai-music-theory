# Quick Definition
Mozart and Beethoven typically maintain the subordinate theme's structure in the recapitulation (following the sonata principle), while Haydn typically alters the entire recapitulation significantly, reflecting fundamentally different aesthetic goals.

# Core Definition
"Mozart and Beethoven regularly follow the practice of maintaining the same structure of the subordinate theme in the recapitulation as in the exposition. Only occasionally do their subordinate themes exhibit major structural changes" (p. 516). "Haydn, on the contrary, typically alters this area in the recapitulation," partly due to monothematic expositions but also responding to "particular aesthetic goals, ones that clearly differ from his younger contemporaries, who seem more wedded to the idea of formal symmetry and the sonata principle."

# Key Properties
1. **Mozart/Beethoven**: Maintain subordinate theme structure; value formal symmetry
2. **Haydn**: Typically alters subordinate-theme area; different aesthetic goals
3. Haydn's alterations partly from monothematic tendency
4. Even with contrasting themes, Haydn usually restructures
5. Analytical implication: expect more changes in Haydn
