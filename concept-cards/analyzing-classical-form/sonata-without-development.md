## Definition

Sonata without development is a two-part form consisting of a sonata exposition followed immediately by a recapitulation, to which a coda may optionally be appended. Also known as "sonatina form," "slow-movement form," "overture form," "cavatina form," or Hepokoski and Darcy's "type 1" sonata form.

## Structure

| Section | Function |
|---------|----------|
| Exposition | Main theme, transition, subordinate theme |
| (No Development) | — |
| Recapitulation | Main theme (often altered), transition, subordinate theme (in home key) |
| Coda (optional) | May follow |

## Key Characteristics

### No Exposition Repeat
The exposition is **never repeated** because:
1. Repetition would arouse expectations for a contrasting development
2. Immediate recapitulation after repeated exposition would be redundant

### Retransition
- A retransition **regularly appears** between exposition and recapitulation
- May be relatively long with model-sequence technique and chromatic harmonies
- Must not project a "true development" sense

### Recapitulation Features
- Constructed essentially the same as regular sonata recapitulation
- Main theme and transition often altered to prepare home-key subordinate theme
- **Secondary development** frequently appears in main theme or transition
- May feature a **truncated recapitulation** (subordinate theme eliminated)

## Usage

- Most often in **slow movements**
- Occasionally in fast movements
- Sometimes serves as basis for single-movement **overtures**
- Mozart employs it most often; significant examples in Haydn and Beethoven

## Truncated Recapitulation

When the exposition is followed only by the main theme (transition and subordinate theme eliminated):
- Creates resemblance to large ternary form
- Subordinate-theme complex functions like an interior theme
- Best understood as deviated sonata without development, not large ternary

## Examples

- Haydn, String Quartet in B-flat, Op. 33, No. 4, iii
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in F, K. 332, ii
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in F minor, Op. 2, No. 1, ii
