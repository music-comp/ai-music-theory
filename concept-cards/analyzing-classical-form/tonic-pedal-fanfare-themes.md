# Quick Definition
Some main themes begin with a long tonic pedal and heraldic fanfare gestures, ideal for symphonies and overtures. These passages often obscure the distinction between presentation and continuation functions, making classification as a standard type difficult. They are best regarded as nonconventional.

# Core Definition
Some main themes begin with a long tonic pedal, over which gestures evoke a heraldic, fanfare style. Such powerful passages are ideal for projecting a strong opening, especially in public genres (symphony, overture). They often obscure a clear sense of basic idea and its repetition, or the juxtaposition of a basic idea with a contrasting idea. The lack of a conventional initiating function makes classification difficult. Though such themes have a general sentential quality (obvious cadential function, rhythmic acceleration), it is difficult to distinguish between presentation and continuation. They are best regarded as nonconventional (Caplin, Ch. 10, pp. 314-315).

# Key Properties
1. Long tonic pedal at opening
2. Heraldic, fanfare-style gestures
3. Ideal for public genres (symphony, overture)
4. Obscures basic idea / contrasting idea distinction
5. General sentential quality but not a genuine sentence
6. Best classified as nonconventional

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 314): Haydn, Symphony No. 97, i, mm. 14-25 -- Unisons and octaves with fanfare gestures; cadential function clear but presentation/continuation indistinguishable.
