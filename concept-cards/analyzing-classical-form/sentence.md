# Sentence

The **sentence** is undoubtedly the most important theme type in classical music. The majority of thematic units in this repertoire are structured either as sentences or in ways that resemble the sentence in many respects.

## Structure

The simple sentence is an 8-measure structure built out of two phrases:
- A 4-measure **presentation phrase**
- A 4-measure **continuation phrase**

## Formal Functions

The sentence contains three formal functions:

1. **Presentation function** - articulates a powerful sense of "beginning" the theme
2. **Continuation function** - expresses the sense of "being in the middle" of the thematic process
3. **Cadential function** - creates the necessary conditions for "ending" the theme

The continuation phrase fuses together continuation and cadential functions.

## Basic Design

- Begins with a 2-measure **basic idea** that prolongs tonic harmony
- The basic idea is immediately repeated (creating a 4-measure presentation phrase)
- The presentation sets up expectations for a continuation phrase
- The continuation typically features fragmentation, harmonic acceleration, and closes with a cadence

## Key Characteristics

- One cadence (at the end)
- Can end with PAC, IAC, or HC
- Basic idea is repeated within the presentation phrase
- Projects the character of mobility and forward drive

## Historical Background

The sentence was first identified by Arnold Schoenberg early in the 20th century. It is remarkable that a formal type used with such frequency throughout the 18th and 19th centuries remained undiscovered until the era of tonal composition was largely over.
