# Quick Definition
The most important theme type in classical music: an 8-m. structure with a 4-m. presentation phrase (b.i. + repetition) and a 4-m. continuation phrase (fragmentation, harmonic acceleration, cadential closure). Contains three formal functions: presentation, continuation, and cadential.

# Core Definition
The sentence is undoubtedly the most important theme type in classical music. The majority of thematic units in this repertoire are structured either as sentences or in ways that resemble the sentence. The simple sentence, an 8-m. structure, begins with a 2-m. basic idea that prolongs tonic harmony. The basic idea is then immediately repeated to make a 4-m. presentation phrase, which continues to prolong tonic. The open-ended quality of the presentation sets up expectations for a 4-m. continuation phrase, which can feature fragmentation, harmonic acceleration, and a concluding cadential idea (pp. 55-56). First identified by Arnold Schoenberg early in the 20th century (p. 57).

# Key Properties
1. 8 measures, divided into two 4-m. phrases
2. Presentation phrase: b.i. + repetition (tonic prolongation, no cadence)
3. Continuation phrase: fragmentation, harmonic acceleration, cadential closure
4. Three formal functions: presentation (beginning), continuation (middle), cadential (ending)
5. Cont. and cad. functions are fused within the single continuation phrase
6. One cadence (at the end): PAC (most common), HC, or IAC (rare)
7. Projects character of mobility and forward drive
8. B.i. is repeated within the presentation (not returned after contrasting material)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify a Sentence:
1. Look for a 2-m. b.i. immediately repeated in mm. 3-4 (presentation)
2. Verify tonic prolongation underlies the presentation
3. Check for fragmentation, harmonic acceleration in mm. 5-8 (continuation)
4. Identify the cadence at the end (PAC, HC, or IAC)
5. Confirm one cadence total (at the end of the theme)

# Context & Application
The sentence is the primary theme type in classical music and appears in all formal contexts: main themes, subordinate themes, transitions, developments. Its presentation-continuation structure creates a sense of forward momentum through destabilization of initial stability. The term "continuation phrase" is used (rather than "cadential phrase") because continuation function is usually more salient throughout the phrase.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 56, Ex. 2.1): Beethoven, Piano Sonata Op. 2, No. 1, i, mm. 1-8 -- the most exemplary sentence. B.i. with tonic, response with dominant, fragmentation in mm. 5-6, HC at m. 8.

**Example 2** (p. 60, Ex. 2.2): Mozart, Rondo K. 485, mm. 9-16 -- 8-m. sentence with PAC.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Identifying a cadence at the end of the presentation phrase
  **Correction**: Presentation phrases do not end with cadences; repetition of b.i. reinforces initiation

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming the continuation phrase has only continuation function
  **Clarification**: It fuses continuation and cadential functions; "continuation" is used because it is more salient throughout
