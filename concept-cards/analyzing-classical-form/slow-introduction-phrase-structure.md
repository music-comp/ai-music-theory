# Quick Definition
Slow introductions are usually loose in formal organization, though many are bipartite or tripartite (as articulated by cadences); conventional theme types appear only rarely, and larger introductions may contain four or more distinct parts.

# Core Definition
"As befits their expression of uncertainty and instability, slow introductions are usually loose in formal organization, although tight-knit theme types do appear now and then" (p. 578). Many are "bipartite or tripartite in form (as articulated by cadences), and some larger ones contain four or more distinct parts." The formal situation sometimes resembles a truncated small ternary or small binary, though such labels are approximate given the nonconventional character of slow introductions.

# Key Properties
1. Usually loose in formal organization
2. Tight-knit theme types rare but possible
3. Bipartite or tripartite form common (cadences articulate parts)
4. Larger introductions may have four or more parts
5. May resemble truncated small ternary (when resolution to tonic initiates exposition instead of A')
6. May resemble deviated small binary
7. Irregularities expected given nonconventional formal nature

# Examples
**Example 16.1** (p. 574): Mozart, K. 454 — compound sentence (presentation + continuation); standing on dominant creates contrasting-middle effect; truncated small ternary when tonic at m. 14 initiates exposition.

**Example 16.3** (p. 579): Haydn, Symphony No. 104 — "highly deviated small binary" (mm. 1-7, 7-14) + closing section (14-17); several unusual features (elided parts, stronger closure in closing section than in part 2).
