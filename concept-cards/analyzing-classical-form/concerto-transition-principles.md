# Quick Definition
Two principles guide solo transition material choice: (1) the solo should not reproduce the ritornello's succession of ideas, and (2) an alternative main theme prompts immediate reappearance of ritornello main-theme material. These principles produce four common scenarios.

# Core Definition
Two general principles guide the choice of material to begin the solo transition: (1) the solo section should not reproduce the same succession of ideas found in the ritornello; (2) the use of an alternative main theme prompts the immediate reappearance of material from the ritornello main theme. These yield four common scenarios (Caplin, pp. 708-709).

# Key Properties
1. Principle 1: avoid reproducing ritornello's idea succession
2. Principle 2: alternative main theme prompts ritornello main theme reappearance
3. Scenario A: ritornello MT reused -> new transition material (~50% of Mozart)
4. Scenario B: alternative MT -> ritornello MT begins transition
5. Scenario C: alternative MT -> ritornello MT as second theme -> new transition
6. Scenario D: alternative MT -> ritornello transition (different succession satisfies principle 1)
7. Exception: some concertos restate ritornello in same order (supporting "double exposition")
