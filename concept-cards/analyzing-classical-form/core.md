# Core

The central, most dramatically charged portion of the development section.

## Elements (in order)

1. **Large-scale model**: Usually 4-8 measures long
2. **Sequential repetition**: One or more times
3. **Fragmentation**: Breaking into smaller units (may use model-sequence)
4. **Half cadence or dominant arrival**: In development key or home key
5. **Standing on the dominant**: Postcadential unit

## Emotional Character

- Instability, restlessness, dramatic conflict
- Usually forte dynamic
- Often Sturm und Drang (storm and stress) character
- Marked increase in rhythmic activity
- "Chugging" effect from accompanimental patterns (Alberti bass, murky bass)
- Polyphonic devices may contribute (imitation, canon, fugal entries)

## Historical Background

The term "core" (Kern) was first introduced by Erwin Ratz, though Ratz did not consider the standing on the dominant to belong to the core proper.

## Multiple Cores

A particularly large development may contain two cores - the first ends in a development key, the second usually in the home key.
