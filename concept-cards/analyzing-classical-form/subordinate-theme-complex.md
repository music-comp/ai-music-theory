# Quick Definition
In rondo form, the subordinate-theme complex is a single couplet consisting of transition, subordinate theme (group), closing section, and retransition. The subordinate key is often less emphatically established than in sonata form, and the retransition tends to be the most dramatic element.

# Core Definition
The idea of identifying transition, subordinate theme, closing section, and retransition as a single large-scale unit comes into its own in connection with rondo forms: because main-theme function is so strongly associated with the rondo refrain, all other contrasting material is deemed to constitute a couplet. The tonal conflict of home and subordinate keys tends to be tempered in rondo forms (Caplin, pp. 673-675).

# Prerequisites
- **Couplet** -- The couplet type being described
- **Transition** -- One component of the complex
- **Subordinate theme** -- Another component

# Key Properties
1. Groups transition through retransition as a single couplet
2. Concept unique to rondo analysis (not used in sonata analysis)
3. Subordinate key less emphatically established than in sonata
4. Transition may be entirely omitted
5. Subordinate theme may lack PAC closure (unlike sonata requirement)
6. Subordinate theme can be relatively compressed
7. Retransition is often longer and more elaborate than in sonata; most dramatic element

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate a couplet containing transition, subordinate theme, and retransition
2. Note whether it establishes a subordinate key with PAC
3. Observe the relative emphasis on retransition vs. subordinate theme

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 667): Mozart, Piano Sonata in C, K. 545, iii -- highly compressed subordinate-theme complex with retransition making up a full third of the couplet.
**Example 2** (p. 674): Beethoven, Piano Sonata in D, Op. 10, No. 3, iv -- extremely incomplete subordinate theme with only weak initiating function and brief retransition.
