## Definition

In rondo form, the subordinate-theme complex is a single couplet consisting of a transition, subordinate theme (group), closing section, and retransition. It combines all the expositional material that follows the main theme.

## Components

| Component | Function |
|-----------|----------|
| Transition | Modulates to subordinate key |
| Subordinate theme(s) | Confirms subordinate key with PAC |
| Closing section | Postcadential material |
| Retransition | Prepares return of refrain |

## Unique to Rondo Context

This concept of grouping transition through retransition as a single unit is largely unheard of in sonata form analysis, where:
- Many theories see transition grouping with main theme
- Creating a "two-part" exposition

In rondo:
- Main-theme function is so strongly associated with the refrain
- All other contrasting material constitutes a couplet
- Hence the unified concept of "subordinate-theme complex"

## Differences from Sonata Exposition

The subordinate key in rondo is often **less emphatic** than in sonata:
- Distinct transition sometimes entirely omitted
- Subordinate theme may lack PAC closure
- Subordinate theme tends to be relatively compressed and simple
- Retransition function becomes more significant

## Dramatic Emphasis

The tonal conflict of home and subordinate keys tends to be **tempered** in rondo forms:
- Emphasis thrown on the continual "return" of main theme
- Retransition leading back to refrain is often the most dramatic moment
