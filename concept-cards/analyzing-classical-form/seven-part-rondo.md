# Quick Definition
The seven-part rondo (ABACADA) is an enlarged five-part rondo with an additional couplet and refrain return. Unlike the sonata-rondo (also seven parts), the seven-part rondo does not contain a couplet that recapitulates an earlier subordinate-theme complex.

# Core Definition
When the five-part rondo is enlarged by adding an additional couplet and return of the refrain, it becomes a seven-part rondo (ABACADA). The additional couplet is usually organized as an interior theme. It is important to note that whereas the regular sonata-rondo also has seven parts, a genuine seven-part rondo does not contain a couplet that can be construed as the recapitulation of an earlier subordinate-theme complex (Caplin, p. 686).

# Prerequisites
- **Five-part rondo** -- The form being enlarged

# Key Properties
1. Enlarged five-part rondo: ABACADA
2. Additional couplet usually an interior theme
3. Additional interior theme differs from earlier ones in material, region, and organization
4. NOT a sonata-rondo (no recapitulation of subordinate-theme complex)
5. Distinguished from sonata-rondo by absence of recapitulatory couplet
