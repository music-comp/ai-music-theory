# Quick Definition
When the subordinate theme is transposed from subordinate key to home key, register adjustments are often necessary to keep the melody and accompaniment in appropriate ranges, typically involving partial transposition at different intervals or changes of direction.

# Core Definition
When the subordinate theme is transposed from the subordinate key to the home key, simple transposition may place the melody too high or too low, the accompaniment figures may become awkward, or the overall texture may become unbalanced. Typical solutions include partial transposition (melody transposed down a fifth initially while accompaniment is moved up a fourth, with later adjustments returning to the appropriate register) and change of direction (at certain points, melody transposed up instead of the expected down, preventing descent too low and maintaining appropriate tessitura for instruments). Register adjustment is typically an ornamental change, not affecting the underlying structure, though it may be combined with other alterations.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of tonal adjustment in the recapitulation

# Key Properties
- Necessitated by transposition from subordinate key to home key
- Simple transposition may place melody or accompaniment in awkward range
- Melody may be transposed down a fifth initially
- Accompaniment may be moved up a fourth
- Direction of transposition may change at certain points
- Prevents descent too low or ascent too high
- Maintains appropriate instrumental tessitura
- Typically an ornamental change (not structural)

# Construction / Recognition
Compare the register of the subordinate theme in the exposition and recapitulation. If the transposition has been adjusted at different points (e.g., melody down a fifth for the opening but up a fourth later), register adjustment has occurred. The underlying melodic contour and harmonic structure remain the same; only the octave placement differs.

# Context & Application
Register adjustment is a practical necessity arising from the tonal adjustment of the subordinate theme. It is particularly relevant in keyboard music (where range is fixed), string quartets (where different transposition directions keep parts in comfortable ranges), and orchestral music (where instrument-specific range constraints must be observed). The adjustment is usually seamless and does not call attention to itself.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C, K. 545, i, mm. 58ff.: melody first transposed down a fifth (from G major to C major), accompaniment moved up a fourth; at upbeat to m. 65, melody transposed up a fourth instead to prevent descent too low

# Common Errors
- Treating register adjustments as deliberate motivic alterations rather than practical consequences of transposition
- Failing to recognize that different intervals of transposition at different points are a single coherent adjustment strategy

# Common Confusions
- Register adjustment is NOT a structural change -- the underlying melodic and harmonic structure remains the same; only the octave placement is modified to maintain appropriate ranges
