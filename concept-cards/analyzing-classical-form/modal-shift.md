## Modal Shift in Subordinate Theme

Almost all subordinate themes reside in the **major mode** (even in minor-mode movements, the subordinate theme usually lies in relative major). Nonetheless, a change from major to minor is frequently encountered within a subordinate theme.

### Effect

Such a modal shift:
- Introduces a broader spectrum of pitches and pitch relationships
- Similar to use of chromaticism
- Creates **looser formal expression**
- Confirmed as loosening device by general absence in tight-knit main themes

### Where Modal Shift Occurs

Modal shift can occur almost anywhere except at the final cadence:
- Most commonly with **continuation function**
- At the **initial tonic of an ECP**
- At the **beginning of an expanded repeat** of subordinate theme

### Dramatic Opening Effect

Modal shift is particularly dramatic when it occurs at the **very beginning** of the subordinate theme.

### Resolution

No matter where the modal shift takes place, the **major modality is ultimately restored**, at least by:
- The final cadence of the theme, or
- The final cadence of the last theme in a subordinate-theme group
