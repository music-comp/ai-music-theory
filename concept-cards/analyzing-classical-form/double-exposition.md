## Definition

The "double exposition" is a theoretical concept recognizing that both the opening ritornello and the solo exposition present an ordered succession (rotation) of expositional functions (main theme, transition, subordinate-theme group).

## Analogy to Sonata

In sonata form:
- The exposition is normally presented twice (repeated)

In concerto form:
- Two different "expositions" occur (ritornello then solo)
- Each presents the same succession of thematic functions

## Advantages of the Concept

### Explains Recapitulation Challenge
The solo recapitulation must find a way to "recapitulate" material from **both** expositions in a single section.

### Recognizes Parallel Organization
Both sections follow the same formal plan:
- Main theme
- Transition
- Subordinate theme(s)
- Closing material

## Limitations of the Concept

### Misleading Implication
The term suggests concerto form is a **variant** of sonata form, rather than an **independent form** derived from Baroque antecedents that predate the classical sonata.

### Different Origins
- Concerto form derives from Baroque ritornello form
- Sonata form derives from Baroque binary dance movements
- They developed independently before converging in classical practice

## Terminology Preference

For these reasons, Caplin refers to:
- "Opening ritornello" (not "orchestral exposition")
- "Solo exposition"

This acknowledges the form's independent identity while recognizing its sonata-like aspects.
