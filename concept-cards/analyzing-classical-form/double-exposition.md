# Quick Definition
The "double exposition" concept recognizes that both the opening ritornello and the solo exposition present an ordered succession of expositional functions. This helps explain the recapitulation's challenge of combining material from both. However, the concept misleadingly suggests concerto form is a variant of sonata form rather than an independent form.

# Core Definition
Some theorists speak of a "double exposition" at the beginning of concerto form, seeing both the opening ritornello and the solo exposition as presenting an ordered succession (rotation) of expositional functions. This idea helps explain the solo recapitulation's challenge of combining material from both "expositions." But the notion is misleading because it suggests concerto form is a variant of sonata form, rather than an independent form derived from Baroque antecedents that predate the classical sonata. For this reason, Caplin uses "opening ritornello" rather than "orchestral exposition" (Caplin, pp. 704-705).
