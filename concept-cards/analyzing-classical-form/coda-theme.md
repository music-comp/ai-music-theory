# Quick Definition
A thematic unit within the coda that concludes with a PAC, typically organized with subordinate-theme loosening devices, though the label "coda theme" is functionally neutral — implying only that the unit occurs within the coda.

# Core Definition
"A major distinguishing feature of a coda, as opposed to a closing section consisting of codettas, is the presence of at least one thematic unit that concludes with a perfect authentic cadence" (p. 552). Coda themes are "frequently organized along the lines of a subordinate theme" with loosening devices, especially "a highly expanded cadential area." The label "coda theme" is "functionally neutral," unlike "main theme" or "subordinate theme" which imply specific formal functions. Some coda themes are tight-knit (sentence, period, hybrid), often based on main-theme material. Some conclude with HC, resembling transitions or development structures.

# Prerequisites
- **Coda section** — The section containing coda themes
- **Subordinate theme** — The formal function frequently resembled

# Key Properties
1. Concludes with PAC in the home key (most common)
2. Frequently organized like a subordinate theme (loosening devices)
3. Cadential area especially prone to extension and expansion
4. Functionally neutral label (no specific formal function implied)
5. May be tight-knit when based on main-theme material
6. Some conclude with HC (transition-like or developmental)
7. Multiple coda themes possible in lengthy codas

# Examples
**Example 15.6** (p. 553): Haydn, Symphony No. 104 "London," iv — two coda themes: first with weak tonic prolongation, expanded cadential progression, PAC at m. 287; second with modal shift to minor, internal dominant arrival, second expanded cadential progression, PAC at m. 309.
