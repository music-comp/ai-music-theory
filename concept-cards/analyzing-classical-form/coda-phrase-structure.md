# Quick Definition
Codas contain at least one coda theme (usually subordinate-theme-like with loosening devices and expanded cadential area), though tight-knit themes, transitional passages, and multiple coda themes also appear; lengthy codas may contain developmental model-sequence passages early on.

# Core Definition
Coda themes are "frequently organized along the lines of a subordinate theme" with "standard loosening devices" and a "highly expanded cadential area" (p. 552). Tight-knit themes (sentence, period, hybrid) appear "now and then," often based on main-theme material. Some units conclude with HC (resembling transitions or development), usually "early in a coda, especially when the section starts without any obvious sense of functional beginning." The coda typically ends with a closing section of codettas after the final PAC.

# Key Properties
1. At least one coda theme ending with PAC (defining feature)
2. Coda themes usually subordinate-theme-like (loose, expanded cadential area)
3. Tight-knit themes possible, often from main-theme material
4. HC-ending units early in coda (transitional/developmental character)
5. Multiple coda themes possible in lengthy codas
6. Closing section of codettas typically follows final PAC
