## Subordinate Theme - Omission of Initiating Function

A subordinate theme can acquire significant formal loosening by giving the impression of starting *in medias res* ("in the middle of things").

### Possibilities

The theme may start with:
- **Continuation function** instead of standard initiation
- **Cadential function** instead of standard initiation
- **Standing on the dominant** built over the harmony from end of transition

### Intrinsic vs. Contextual Functionality

**The theoretical puzzle**: How can a passage be "continuation" if not preceded by material it "continues"?

**The answer**: Formal functions are so conventionalized that we can sometimes identify a function **independent** of its actual position. Music can express its *intrinsic* temporal function independent of its *contextual* location.

### Formal Dissonance

When contextual placement differs from intrinsic temporal expression:
- A kind of formal "dissonance" results
- If carefully controlled, it expresses loose organization
- Too great a dissonance produces illogical formal succession

### Most Common Context

Omission of initiation occurs most often with a **second or third** subordinate theme of a group. The new theme can be understood to "continue" the subordinate group as a whole.
