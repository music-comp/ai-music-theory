# Quick Definition
Passages functioning as extensions, interpolations, or links between variations typically occur just before the final variation, breaking the regular succession and drawing attention to the finale.

# Core Definition
Besides creating structural changes within a given variation, the composer can alter the general formal plan by adding passages that function as extensions, interpolations, or links from one variation to the next. Such additions typically occur just before the final variation, breaking the regular succession and drawing attention to the finale by preceding it with something entirely new (Caplin, p. 609).

# Prerequisites
- **Theme and variations** -- The formal context

# Key Properties
1. Passages function as extensions, interpolations, or links
2. Typically placed just before the final variation
3. Break the regular succession of equal-size units
4. Draw attention to the finale
5. May be short or a lengthy developmental passage
6. Often built as continuation phrases
7. Frequently end with fermatas, preparing tempo changes

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for passages between variations that do not follow the theme's form
2. Check if they occur before the final variation or Adagio variation
3. Note continuational features (model-sequence technique, harmonic acceleration)

# Context & Application
These passages are the principal means by which the otherwise regular and symmetrical succession of variations is disrupted, creating formal variety and preparing the weight of the finale.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 609): Mozart, Clarinet Quintet in A, K. 581, iv -- first extension (mm. 81-84) follows the fourth variation with model-sequence technique and dominant arrival under fermata, setting up the Adagio; second extension (mm. 101-105) follows the Adagio with a continuation phrase leading to HC under fermata, preparing the Allegro finale.

- **Error**: Confusing extensions with the coda
