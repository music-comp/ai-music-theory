## Definition

Besides creating structural changes within a given variation, the composer can alter the general formal plan by adding passages that function as extensions, interpolations, or links from one variation to the next.

## Typical Placement

Such additions typically occur **just before the final variation**:
- Break the regular succession of variations
- Draw attention to the finale by preceding it with something entirely new
- Create anticipation for the concluding variation

## Scope

- The added material may be **short**
- Can also be a more **lengthy, developmental passage**

## Example: Mozart, Clarinet Quintet in A, K. 581, iv

### First Extension (mm. 81-84)
- Follows conclusion of fourth variation
- Brief continuational extension
- Model-sequence technique
- Dominant arrival with fermata
- Sets up the Adagio variation

### Second Extension (mm. 101-105)
- Follows conclusion of Adagio variation
- Built as continuation phrase (like the first)
- Leads to genuine HC with fermata
- Prepares for the finale's Allegro (the fastest tempo of the movement)

## Function

Extensions break the rhythmic flow between variations, effectively preparing for:
- A change in tempo
- A change in character
- The formal weight of the finale
