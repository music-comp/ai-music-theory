# Quick Definition
A cadential progression ending on the dominant harmony, which serves as the harmonic goal rather than resolving to tonic.

# Formal Definition
In the half-cadential progression, the dominant itself becomes the goal harmony and occupies the ultimate position. To be sufficiently stable as an ending harmony, the dominant must take the form of a root-position triad (not a seventh chord). The complete form brings: (initial) tonic, pre-dominant, and dominant (triad in root position).

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Complete half-cadential progression: I(6) - II6 - V

Essential requirements:
- Dominant must be a consonant **triad** (not seventh chord)
- Dominant must be in root position
- Dominant represents the harmonic goal (ultimate position)

The dominant usually resolves to tonic, but that tonic initiates a **new** harmonic progression; within the boundaries of the half-cadential progression itself, the dominant possesses sufficient stability to represent a harmonic end.

Special approaches to the half-cadential dominant:
- Descending motion from the sixth degree (usually lowered): bVI - V
- Phrygian motion in bass: iv6 - V (common in minor)

# Musical Context/Application
Half-cadential progressions:
- Create weaker tonal confirmation than authentic cadential progressions
- End contrasting middles and transitions
- The dominant's stability allows it to serve as temporary repose
- Adding a dissonant seventh would overly destabilize the ultimate dominant

Complete progressions include both initial tonic and pre-dominant; incomplete versions omit one of these functions. Even the very incomplete progression I - V is widely used.

# Common Confusions
- Adding a seventh to the ultimate dominant (destroys its stability as ending harmony)
- Confusing the ultimate dominant of a half cadence with the penultimate dominant of an authentic cadence
- Not recognizing special approaches (phrygian, descending from bVI)
- Assuming the following tonic belongs to the half-cadential progression (it begins a new progression)
