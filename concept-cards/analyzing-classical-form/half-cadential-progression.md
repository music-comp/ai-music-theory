# Quick Definition
A cadential progression ending on the dominant, which serves as the harmonic goal (ultimate position). The dominant must be a root-position triad (not seventh chord) to be sufficiently stable as an ending harmony.

# Core Definition
In the half-cadential progression, the dominant itself becomes the goal harmony and thus occupies the ultimate position. To be sufficiently stable as an ending harmony, the dominant must take the form of a root-position triad. Adding a dissonant seventh would overly destabilize the ultimate dominant (pp. 39-40). The dominant usually resolves to tonic, but that tonic initiates a new harmonic progression; within the boundaries of the half-cadential progression itself, the dominant possesses sufficient stability to represent a harmonic end (p. 39).

# Prerequisites
- **Cadential progression** — Half-cadential is one of three kinds of cadential progression

# Key Properties
1. Dominant is the harmonic goal (ultimate position)
2. Must be a root-position triad (not seventh chord)
3. Seventh would overly destabilize the ultimate dominant
4. Complete: I(6)-II6-V; incomplete: omits initial tonic or pre-dominant
5. Even the very incomplete I-V is widely used
6. Special approaches: descending from b6 to 5, phrygian motion (iv6-V)
7. The following tonic begins a new progression, not part of the half-cadential one

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for a cadential progression that ends on root-position V
2. Verify V is a triad (not seventh chord)
3. Check for special approaches (phrygian, descending from b6)
4. Determine completeness
5. Note: the tonic that follows belongs to the next progression

# Context & Application
Half-cadential progressions create weaker tonal confirmation than authentic ones. They are common at the end of antecedent phrases, contrasting middles, and transitions. All authentic cadential paradigms apply (with the adjustments of omitting final tonic and ensuring V is a triad). Several special progressions feature the ultimate dominant approached by descending motion from the sixth degree (usually lowered), especially associated with the half-cadential progression.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Adding a seventh to the ultimate dominant of a half cadence
  **Correction**: Half-cadential dominant must be a consonant triad to serve as ending harmony

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the tonic following V belongs to the half-cadential progression
  **Clarification**: That tonic begins a new progression; within the HC progression, V is the goal
- **Confusion**: Confusing ultimate dominant (HC) with penultimate dominant (authentic)
  **Clarification**: Ultimate dominant is the goal; penultimate dominant drives into the following tonic
