# Quick Definition
Pitches shared between two different chords, explaining functional similarity in substitute harmonies and enabling smooth voice-leading connections.

# Formal Definition
Common tones are pitches that belong to both the original harmony and a substitute harmony. "In such cases, the original harmony and substitute harmony have two chord tones in common, which largely accounts for their functional similarity."

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Common-tone relationships:
- **I and VI**: Share scale degrees 1 and 3 (explains VI as tonic substitute)
- **II and IV**: Share scale degrees 4 and 6 (explains pre-dominant equivalence)
- **I and III**: Share scale degrees 3 and 5

The sharing of common tones:
- Explains why certain chords can substitute for others
- Enables smooth voice-leading through retention
- Underlies the functional groupings of harmonic theory

# Musical Context/Application
Common tones are significant for:
- Understanding substitute harmonies (VI for I, II for IV)
- Voice-leading connections between chords
- Functional similarity within harmonic categories
- Harmonic prolongation through substitution

The chapter explains that VI and I share two tones, "which largely accounts for their functional similarity" (p. 35).

# Common Confusions
- Treating any shared pitch as common-tone relationship
- Assuming common tones always create functional equivalence
- Overlooking common tones when analyzing substitutions
- Confusing common-tone connections with pivot-chord modulation
