# Quick Definition
The harmony sustained through time by intervening subordinate harmonies within a prolongational progression, typically appearing at both the beginning and end of the progression.

# Core Definition
The prolonged harmony is the individual harmony sustained through time by means of intervening subordinate harmonies. In most cases it appears at both the beginning and end of the progression, though exceptions exist: "the subordinate harmony may ... initiate or conclude the progression" (p. 25). The position of the prolonged harmony (root position vs. inversion) determines whether subordinate chords are classified as neighboring or passing.

# Prerequisites
- **Prolongational progression** — The prolonged harmony exists within a prolongational progression

# Key Properties
1. Is the structural harmony being extended through time
2. May be tonic (most common) or built on other scale degrees
3. Typically frames the progression (beginning and end)
4. May remain in same position (with neighboring chords) or change position (with passing chords)
5. Subordinate harmonies may initiate or conclude the progression (exceptions to framing)
6. Most commonly prolongs tonic function

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate the structural harmony that persists throughout a prolongational passage
2. Identify subordinate harmonies that embellish it (passing, neighboring, substitute)
3. Note whether the prolonged harmony appears at both beginning and end
4. Determine whether it stays in the same position (neighboring) or changes (passing)

# Context & Application
Prolonged harmonies most commonly prolong tonic function but can prolong any harmony (dominant, pre-dominant). They support initiating and medial formal functions (not cadential) and create stability without confirming tonality. The position of the prolonged harmony determines the classification of subordinate chords.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Prolongational progression** — The prolonged harmony is the central element of a prolongational progression
## Enables
- **Formal stability** — Prolonged harmony creates stability at phrase beginnings and middles
## Related
- **Subordinate harmony** — Embellishes and extends the prolonged harmony
- **Neighbor motion** — Results when the prolonged harmony stays in the same position
- **Passing motion** — Results when the prolonged harmony changes position
- **Bass arpeggiation** — Connects positions of the prolonged harmony through leaps
## Contrasts With
- **Subordinate harmony** — The prolonged harmony is structural; subordinate harmonies are embellishing

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming the prolonged harmony must always frame the progression
  **Correction**: Subordinate chords may begin or end the progression in exceptional cases

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking only tonic can be prolonged
  **Clarification**: Any harmony may be prolonged, including dominant, pre-dominant, and others
