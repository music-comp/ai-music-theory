# Quick Definition
The harmony that is sustained in time through a prolongational progression, typically appearing at the beginning and end of the progression.

# Formal Definition
The prolonged harmony is the individual harmony that is sustained in time through the use of intervening subordinate harmonies within a prolongational progression. In most cases, the prolonged harmony appears at both the beginning and end of the progression, though exceptions exist where the subordinate harmony may initiate or conclude the progression.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
The prolonged harmony:
- Is the structural harmony being extended through time
- May be tonic (most common) or built on other scale degrees
- Typically frames the progression (beginning and end)
- May remain in same position (with neighboring chords) or change position (with passing chords)

Exceptions to framing structure:
- Subordinate harmony may initiate: V - I - V - I (I is prolonged)
- Subordinate harmony may conclude: I - V - I - V (I is still prolonged)

# Musical Context/Application
Prolonged harmonies:
- Most commonly prolong tonic function
- Can prolong any harmony (dominant, pre-dominant, etc.)
- Support initiating and medial formal functions (not cadential)
- Create stability without confirming tonality

The position of the prolonged harmony (root position vs. inversion) determines whether subordinate chords are classified as neighboring or passing.

# Common Confusions
- Assuming the prolonged harmony must always frame the progression (subordinate chords may begin or end)
- Confusing prolonged harmony with structural harmony at deeper levels (prolongation operates at the foreground)
- Thinking only tonic can be prolonged (any harmony may be prolonged)
