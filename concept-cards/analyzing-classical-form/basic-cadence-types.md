# Basic Cadence Types

There are only **three basic cadences** in tonal music: two kinds of authentic cadence and one half cadence.

## The Three Types

### 1. Perfect Authentic Cadence (PAC)
- Ends with the **tonic scale degree (1)** in the soprano
- Strongest cadential closure
- Achieves complete harmonic and melodic closure

### 2. Imperfect Authentic Cadence (IAC)
- Ends with the **third scale degree (3)** in the soprano
- Moderate strength (stronger than HC, weaker than PAC)
- Results in melodic incompleteness alone

### 3. Half Cadence (HC)
- Ends on **dominant harmony**
- Weakest cadence
- Combination of harmonic and melodic incompleteness

## The Myth of the Plagal Cadence

The motion from IV to I does NOT create a genuine cadence in classical-style music. Most passages labeled "plagal cadence" are actually postcadential **codettas**. There are no genuine plagal cadences in music of the classical style.

## Melodic Content of Cadences

- General melodic contour is **descending** (reflecting Latin "cadere" = to fall)
- Content is typically **conventional** (formulaic, interchangeable)
- Contrasts with the **characteristic** (unique) melody of basic ideas

## Cadential Strength (Syntactical)

For formal analysis, all cadences of the same type have the same **syntactical strength**, regardless of metrical position, dynamics, or texture.
