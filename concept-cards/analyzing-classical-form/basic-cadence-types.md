# Quick Definition
Three basic cadences in tonal music: PAC (soprano ends on scale degree 1, strongest), IAC (soprano on scale degree 3, moderate), HC (ends on dominant, weakest). There are no genuine plagal cadences in classical music. Cadential strength is syntactical (based on type), not rhetorical (based on dynamics/texture).

# Core Definition
There are only three basic cadences in tonal music: two kinds of authentic cadence and one half cadence (HC). Authentic cadences are distinguished by the final scale degree in the soprano: a PAC ends with tonic scale degree, an IAC ends with the third degree (p. 78). Almost every textbook defines a "plagal cadence" as IV-I, but this motion does not create a genuine cadence in classical music; most such passages are postcadential codettas (p. 78). For formal analysis, all cadences of the same type have the same syntactical strength, regardless of metrical position, dynamics, or texture (p. 102).

# Key Properties
1. PAC: authentic cadential progression; soprano on scale degree 1; strongest
2. IAC: authentic cadential progression; soprano on scale degree 3; moderate
3. HC: half-cadential progression; ends on V triad; weakest
4. No genuine plagal cadences in classical music (IV-I passages are codettas)
5. Cadential strength is syntactical (by type), not rhetorical (by dynamics, texture)
6. Melodic content of cadences is typically conventional (descending, formulaic)
7. "Cadence" derives from Latin "cadere" = to fall

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate the cadential progression (authentic or half)
2. For authentic cadences: check the soprano at the moment of tonic arrival
3. Soprano on scale degree 1 = PAC; soprano on scale degree 3 = IAC
4. Ends on V triad (not seventh) = HC
5. Place label (PAC, IAC, HC) where the final harmony first appears

# Context & Application
PAC achieves complete harmonic and melodic closure. IAC produces melodic incompleteness alone. HC has both harmonic and melodic incompleteness, making it the weakest. The syntactical strength hierarchy (PAC > IAC > HC) is determined entirely by type, not by performance factors.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Labeling IV-I as a plagal cadence
  **Correction**: No genuine plagal cadences exist in classical music; such passages are codettas

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking a loud cadence is "stronger" than a soft one of the same type
  **Clarification**: Syntactical strength is determined by type alone; rhetorical strength (dynamics, texture) is separate
