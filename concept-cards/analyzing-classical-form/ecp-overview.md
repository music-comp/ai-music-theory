# Quick Definition
An expanded cadential progression (ECP) in a subordinate theme supports one or more complete phrases with distinct melodic content. The majority comprise all four harmonic functions (I-PD-V-I). Individual harmonies may be prominently expanded: expansion of the dominant (most recognizable, dramatic), pre-dominant (variety of pre-dominant harmonies), or initial tonic (usually I6, embellished by V7/3 neighboring chords).

# Core Definition
Cadential function is enhanced by an ECP supporting one or more complete phrases with distinct melodic ideas and accompanimental textures. The majority comprise all four harmonic functions (initial tonic, pre-dominant, dominant, final tonic). Three types of harmonic expansion: (1) **Expansion of the dominant** -- most recognizable, often with prolonged cadential 6/4 creating dramatic delay; ideal for operatic and concerto genres. (2) **Expansion of the pre-dominant** -- composer employs a variety of pre-dominant harmonies in a single progression. (3) **Expansion of the initial tonic** -- usually I6, the classical composers often use the prominent arrival on I6 as a cue for the onset of an ECP; frequently embellished by neighboring V7/3 chords (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 396-398).

# Key Properties
1. Supports one or more complete phrases with distinct content
2. Four harmonic functions: initial tonic, pre-dominant, dominant, final tonic
3. Dominant expansion: cadential 6/4 prolongation, most dramatic
4. Pre-dominant expansion: variety of pre-dominant harmonies
5. Initial tonic expansion: I6 embellished by V7/3 neighbors; arrival on I6 cues the ECP
6. If sufficiently large, ECP may support several phrases with different content
