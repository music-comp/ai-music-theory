# Quick Definition
An antecedent phrase internally structured like a miniature sentence: the b.i. contains a 1-m. motive repeated (like a "small presentation"), and the c.i. features fragmentation, harmonic acceleration, and cadential closure (like a continuation). Prefer "mini-sentence" or "sentential antecedent" over calling it a genuine sentence.

# Core Definition
The notion of the contrasting idea as continuational becomes even more apparent in cases where the basic idea itself is composed of a 1-m. motive that is immediately repeated, in the manner of a "small presentation." The entire antecedent then resembles the sentence theme type. It is preferable to speak of a mini-sentence, or more formally, of a sentential antecedent. Such miniature sentences rarely appear on their own but rather tend to come in pairs -- antecedent and consequent (pp. 111-112).

# Prerequisites
- **Antecedent phrase** — The sentential antecedent is a variant of the antecedent
- **Sentence** — The sentential antecedent resembles the sentence internally

# Key Properties
1. B.i. contains 1-m. motive + immediate repetition (small presentation)
2. C.i. features fragmentation, acceleration, cadential closure (small continuation)
3. Entire antecedent resembles a miniature sentence
4. Prefer "mini-sentence" or "sentential antecedent" terminology
5. Rarely appears alone; tends to come in pairs (ant. + cons.)
6. Distinguished from genuine 4-m. sentence (which occurs under R = 1/2N)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for a 1-m. motive repeated within the b.i. (small presentation)
2. Check if the c.i. has continuation characteristics (fragmentation, acceleration)
3. The overall 4-m. unit functions as an antecedent (with weak cadence)
4. Its partner (consequent) will have similar structure with strong cadence

# Context & Application
The sentential antecedent supports the general principle that contrasting ideas often contain continuation characteristics. It can be difficult to distinguish from a genuine 4-m. sentence under R = 1/2N conditions; context (tempo, whether paired with a consequent) helps determine the analysis.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 111, Ex. 3.17): Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 331, i, mm. 1-8 -- both antecedent and consequent structured as mini-sentences. B.i. has 1-m. statement + response; c.i. has fragmentation, acceleration, HC. Consequent closes with PAC.
