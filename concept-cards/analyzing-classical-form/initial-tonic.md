# Quick Definition
The tonic harmony that begins a cadential progression, typically in first inversion (I6), signaling the onset of cadential motion.

# Core Definition
Within a cadential progression, the initial tonic precedes the pre-dominant, dominant, and final tonic. It is typically in first inversion (I6), which "plays a special role in articulating harmonic directionality, especially by signaling the onset of a cadential progression" (p. 31). The initial tonic may be omitted in incomplete cadential progressions; "the initial tonic is left out more often than the pre-dominant" (p. 37).

# Prerequisites
- **Cadential progression** — The initial tonic opens the cadential formula
- **Harmonic inversion** — The preference for I6 over I is essential to understanding this concept

# Key Properties
1. Usually in first inversion (I6), sometimes root position
2. Less stable than root-position tonic, signaling forward motion
3. Signals "beginning of the end" -- start of cadential motion
4. May be omitted in incomplete cadential progressions (omitted more often than PD)
5. May be embellished by neighboring V4/3
6. May be chromatically altered to become V/IV or V/II

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate the beginning of a cadential progression
2. Check for I6 (most common) or root-position I
3. Verify that it precedes pre-dominant and dominant functions
4. If absent, classify the cadential progression as incomplete

# Context & Application
The initial tonic begins the cadential formula and sets up forward momentum toward the cadential goal. The preference for first inversion (I6) creates instability that propels the progression forward, contrasting with the stability of the root-position final tonic. Complete cadential progression: I6-II6-V7-I (initial tonic to final tonic).

## Contrasts With
- **Final tonic** — Initial tonic (I6) signals onset; final tonic (I) provides closure

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming initial tonic must be in root position
  **Correction**: First inversion (I6) is more typical and serves a special directional function

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Treating initial and final tonic as equivalent
  **Clarification**: They occupy different positions, inversions, and formal roles within the cadential progression
