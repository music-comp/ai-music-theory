# Quick Definition
The tonic harmony that begins a cadential progression, typically in first inversion (I6), signaling the onset of cadential motion toward a final tonic.

# Formal Definition
Within a cadential progression, the initial tonic is the tonic harmony that begins the progression, preceding the pre-dominant, dominant, and final tonic. It is typically placed in first inversion (I6), which plays a special role in articulating harmonic directionality and signaling the onset of a cadential progression.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Characteristics of the initial tonic:
- Usually in first inversion (I6), sometimes root position
- Less stable than root-position tonic
- Signals "beginning of the end" - start of cadential motion
- May be omitted in incomplete cadential progressions

The preference for I6:
- First inversion creates less stability than root position
- Signals forward motion rather than arrival
- Distinguishes initial tonic from final tonic clearly

Embellishments:
- May be embellished by neighboring V4/3
- May be chromatically altered to become V/IV or V/II

# Musical Context/Application
The initial tonic:
- Begins the cadential formula
- Is omitted more often than pre-dominant in incomplete progressions
- Sets up the forward momentum toward cadential goal
- The I6 form signals that a cadence is approaching

Complete cadential progression: I6 - II6 - V7 - I (initial tonic to final tonic)

# Common Confusions
- Assuming initial tonic must be in root position (first inversion is more typical)
- Treating initial and final tonic as equivalent (different positions, different roles)
- Not recognizing I6 as a signal of cadential onset
- Thinking incomplete progressions lacking initial tonic are defective
