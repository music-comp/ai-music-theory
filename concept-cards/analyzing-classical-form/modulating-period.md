# Modulating Period

A **modulating period** is an 8-measure period whose consequent phrase modulates to a closely related key.

## Structure

- **Antecedent**: Closes with weak cadence in home key (never modulates)
- **Consequent**: Modulates to related key, closes with PAC in new key

## Cadential Strength Across Keys

Even when the two cadences reside in different keys, comparative cadential strength follows the norm: **weak to strong**.

- The HC of the opening key remains weaker than
- The PAC of the related key

## Key Principle

**The syntactical strength of a cadence is determined by its type, not its tonal context.**

A PAC in a subordinate key is still stronger than an HC in the home key.

## Antecedent Never Modulates

Unlike the consequent, the antecedent phrase always:
- Closes in the same key in which it begins
- Does NOT modulate

One exception: The reinterpreted half cadence (discussed separately)

## Typical Key Destinations

From a major home key:
- Dominant region (V)

From a minor home key:
- Relative major (III)
- Minor dominant (v) - less common
