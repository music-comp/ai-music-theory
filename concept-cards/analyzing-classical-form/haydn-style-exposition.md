## Haydn's Expositional Style

Haydn's expositions often differ from those of Mozart and Beethoven in their treatment of formal boundaries.

### Boundary Obscuration

In many Haydn expositions:
- The boundary between transition and subordinate theme is **obscured**
- Less clear demarcation than Mozart or Beethoven
- Poses difficulties for analysis

### Monothematic Tendency

Haydn is well known for:
- Opening subordinate themes with main theme material
- Creating "monothematic expositions"
- Strong motivic unity across sections

### Fusion of Functions

Haydn more frequently creates:
- Fusion of transition and subordinate-theme functions
- Single units embracing both functions
- Ambiguous formal boundaries

### Recapitulation Practice

Haydn's recapitulations typically feature:
- Major structural changes (not just transposition)
- Substantial reworking of subordinate theme
- More extensive recomposition than Mozart or Beethoven

### Analytical Implications

When analyzing Haydn:
- Expect less clear formal boundaries
- Be prepared for form-functional fusion
- Don't force Mozart/Beethoven expectations onto the music
