# Quick Definition
A system for identifying harmonies by the scale degree of their roots, using Roman numerals with figured-bass symbols to indicate inversions.

# Core Definition
"The Roman numeral system identifies individual harmonies in terms of the scale degrees of their roots. The seven Roman numerals indicate these roots with respect to a given key, and additional Arabic numerals indicate (in a manner derived from the figured-bass tradition) the various positions (inversions) of the harmonies" (p. 23). Caplin follows the convention of uppercase Roman numerals exclusively, not distinguishing chord quality in the numeral itself.

# Prerequisites
- **Scale degree** — Roman numerals are based on scale-degree roots
- **Figured bass** — Inversion symbols derive from the figured-bass tradition

# Key Properties
1. Seven Roman numerals indicate chord roots by scale degree
2. Arabic numerals (from figured bass) indicate inversions
3. Caplin uses uppercase exclusively (following Aldwell, Schachter, Cadwallader)
4. Chord quality is not distinguished in the numeral
5. Roman numerals identify chords but do not explain harmonic function
6. Multiple Roman numerals may share the same harmonic function

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Determine the chord root (scale degree)
2. Assign the appropriate uppercase Roman numeral
3. Add figured-bass symbols for inversions (6, 6/4, 7, 6/5, 4/3, 4/2)
4. For nonfunctional chords, use quality symbols in parentheses instead

# Context & Application
Roman numerals provide quick identification of harmonic roots, indication of scale-degree relationships, a framework for comparing progressions across keys, and a basis for understanding harmonic function. However, the system is foundational but limited -- understanding harmonic function (tonic, dominant, pre-dominant) provides deeper analytical insight than Roman numerals alone.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 23): "I = root-position tonic," "IV6 = first-inversion subdominant," "V4/3 = second-inversion dominant seventh."

**Example 2** (p. 24): "For your own analytical work, your instructor will have his or her preferences for harmonic notation, which you should carefully follow."

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating each Roman numeral as representing a distinct harmonic function
  **Correction**: Multiple Roman numerals share the same function (e.g., I and VI both express tonic function)

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming uppercase/lowercase distinguish major/minor chords
  **Clarification**: In Caplin's system, uppercase is used throughout; quality is contextually understood
