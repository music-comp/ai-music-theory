# Quick Definition
Sonata form is the preeminent full-movement form in the classical period, considered the most highly developed and complex compositional design, found in at least one movement (usually the first) of most instrumental cycles (sonatas, quartets, symphonies).

# Core Definition
Sonata form is the preeminent full-movement form in the classical period. Most instrumental cycles (sonatas, quartets, symphonies, etc.) have at least one movement in sonata form -- typically the first movement, though slow movements and finales are often written in sonata form as well. Many historians and theorists consider it to be the period's most highly developed and complex compositional design, the one in which composers reveal their greatest technical skill and expressive potential. Sonata form continued to exert enormous influence over compositional practice in later musical styles and remained a viable form, albeit highly modified, at least until the middle of the 20th century.

# Key Properties
1. Preeminent full-movement form of the classical period
2. Found in most instrumental cycles (sonatas, quartets, symphonies)
3. Usually in the first movement; also in slow movements and finales
4. Considered the most complex compositional design of the period
5. Reveals composers' greatest technical skill and expressive potential
6. Continued to influence composition through the mid-20th century
7. Three required sections: exposition, development, recapitulation

# Context & Application
Sonata form is not merely a template but a dynamic compositional process involving tonal conflict and resolution. The study of sonata form builds directly on the concepts learned in Part I of the textbook (sentence, period, hybrid, compound themes, small ternary), applying the same formal functions at larger scales. The overview chapter uses Beethoven's Piano Sonata Op. 2, No. 1 as the primary illustrative example.
