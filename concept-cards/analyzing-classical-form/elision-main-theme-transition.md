## Elision Between Main Theme and Transition

An **elision** occurs when the cadential arrival of one theme corresponds with the moment when the next theme structurally begins.

### Definition

A single complete measure functions simultaneously as:
- The **ending** of the main theme (cadential arrival)
- The **beginning** of the transition (first structural downbeat)

### Comparison with Accompanimental Overlap

Both techniques may involve new accompaniment at the cadential moment, but:

| Feature | Accompanimental Overlap | Elision |
|---------|------------------------|---------|
| Next theme begins | Bar after cadence | At cadential arrival |
| What overlaps | Only texture | Structure |
| Continuity effect | Moderate | Strong |

### Effect

Elision makes a more powerful effect than accompanimental overlap since the overlap involves not only texture but structure as well.

### Typical Use

Elision is especially effective when:
- New material bursts forth at the cadential moment
- The dramatic effect would be undercut by waiting a measure
