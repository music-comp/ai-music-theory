# Quick Definition
An elision occurs when the cadential arrival of the main theme simultaneously serves as the structural beginning (first downbeat) of the transition. A single measure functions as both ending and beginning, creating a more powerful continuity effect than accompanimental overlap because structure -- not just texture -- overlaps.

# Core Definition
An elision occurs when the cadential arrival of one theme corresponds with the moment when the next theme structurally begins. A single complete measure functions simultaneously as both an **ending** (cadential arrival) and a **beginning** (first structural downbeat of the next unit). Elision creates a more powerful effect than accompanimental overlap since the overlap involves structure, not only texture. Elision is especially effective when new material bursts forth at the cadential moment; the dramatic effect would be undercut by waiting a measure (Caplin, Ch. 11, p. 338).
