# Quick Definition
A postcadential standing on the dominant follows the concluding HC of a contrasting middle, extending the dominant harmony through textural dissolution and motivic anticipation before the recapitulation. It differs from a standing on the dominant that constitutes the entire B section.

# Core Definition
When the B section closes with an HC, a postcadential standing on the dominant may follow, reinforcing the dominant before the recapitulation begins. This standing on the dominant may follow directly after the HC (in the next measure) or elide with it (beginning at the same moment as the cadential arrival). Two preparatory techniques typically appear: (1) textural dissolution -- systematic reduction of texture, "clearing the stage" for the recapitulation's fresh return; (2) motivic anticipation -- motives derived from the upcoming basic idea, preparing the listener for the recapitulation and creating smooth connection to A'. The postcadential standing on the dominant extends the B section's final harmony; the HC marks the structural end of B, and the standing on the dominant is an afterthought or extension.

# Key Properties
1. Follows B section's concluding HC
2. May follow the HC or elide with it
3. Extends B section's final harmony (postcadential function)
4. Features textural dissolution (texture thins progressively)
5. Features motivic anticipation (prepares upcoming basic idea)
6. HC marks structural end of B; standing on dominant is extension

# Construction / Recognition
## Two Formal Contexts for Standing on the Dominant:
| Context | Follows | Function |
|---------|---------|----------|
| Postcadential (this card) | B section's HC | Extends the cadence |
| As complete B section | A section's PAC | IS the B section (no preceding HC) |

# Context & Application
The postcadential standing on the dominant creates a heightened state of expectation for the recapitulation. The combination of textural dissolution and motivic anticipation ensures that the return of the basic idea feels fresh and inevitable. This passage type appears not only in small ternary forms but analogously at the end of development sections in sonata form, where the standing on the dominant prepares the recapitulation's return.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Any dominant prolongation at the end of B is a postcadential standing on the dominant
  **Clarification**: Only if the B section has its own internal harmonic activity leading to an HC; if the entire B section is dominant prolongation, it is a standing on the dominant AS the B section
