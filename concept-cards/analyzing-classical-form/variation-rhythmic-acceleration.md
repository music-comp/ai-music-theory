# Quick Definition
Classical variations movements typically display a systematic acceleration in rhythmic activity through progressively shorter durational values, especially in the early part of the movement.

# Core Definition
A systematic acceleration in rhythmic activity (due to a shortening in the durational values of the notes) is often observable, especially in the early part of the movement. Earlier variations are relatively simple in style and texture, becoming increasingly more complex with each variation (Caplin, p. 592).

# Prerequisites
- **Theme and variations** -- The formal context for this rhythmic phenomenon

# Key Properties
1. Progressive shortening of note durations across variations
2. Most systematic in the early part of the movement
3. Aligns with increasing complexity in style and texture
4. Melodic framework disappears as rhythmic activity increases
5. Harmonic-formal plan remains intact despite surface changes
6. Adagio variation may interrupt the acceleration pattern
7. Not a rigid rule -- individual variations may deviate

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Compare the predominant note values across successive variations
2. Track the trajectory from longer to shorter durations
3. Note where interruptions occur (e.g., Adagio variation)

# Context & Application
This rhythmic pattern provides a broad overarching shape to movements that otherwise resist clear formal categorization. The acceleration creates forward momentum and increasing energy toward the finale.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 592): Earlier variations are relatively simple; later variations feature increasingly shorter durational values.
