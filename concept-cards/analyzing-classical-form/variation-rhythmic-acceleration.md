## Definition

A systematic acceleration in rhythmic activity is often observable in classical theme and variations movements, especially in the early part of the movement. This acceleration is due to a shortening in the durational values of the notes.

## Typical Pattern

| Stage | Rhythmic Values |
|-------|-----------------|
| Theme | Quarter notes, eighth notes |
| Early variations | Eighth notes predominate |
| Middle variations | Sixteenth notes appear |
| Later variations | Continuous sixteenth notes, triplets |

## Progression of Complexity

This rhythmic pattern aligns with a general progression:
1. **Earlier variations**: Relatively simple in style and texture
2. **Progressive variations**: Increasingly complex with each variation
3. **Culmination**: Maximum rhythmic activity before possible deceleration

## Not a Rigid Rule

While rhythmic acceleration is "often observable":
- It is not an absolute requirement
- Individual variations may deviate from the pattern
- The overall trajectory tends toward increased activity

## Relationship to Variation Technique

Rhythmic acceleration connects to variation technique:
- Early variations: Ornamentations of theme's melody (relation to theme evident)
- As variations progress: Melodic framework often disappears
- Fundamental harmonic-formal plan remains intact even as surface rhythm accelerates

## Culmination and Release

- The Adagio variation (slower tempo) often interrupts the acceleration
- The finale then resumes with faster tempo than the theme
- Creates an arch-like rhythmic design in some movements
