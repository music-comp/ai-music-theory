# Quick Definition
Periodic organization (antecedent-consequent) is less common in subordinate themes than sentential organization because periods tend to be tight-knit and symmetrical, working against the dramatic buildup and loose organization required of subordinate themes. When periodic functions appear, the consequent is usually expanded.

# Core Definition
A periodic form may sometimes arise in subordinate themes, though it is considerably less common than sentential organization. Periodic organization is less suited to subordinate themes because periods tend to be relatively tight-knit and symmetrical, and subordinate themes require loose organization. When periodic functions do appear, the antecedent usually retains its conventional size (4 or 8 measures), but the consequent is often expanded (typically through an ECP) to create looser organization. One theme of a subordinate-theme group may take the form of a tight-knit period, creating contrast with looser themes in the group. Structures resembling a small ternary are even rarer: when a subordinate-theme idea yields to new material, the original idea is not normally brought back within the theme itself, distinguishing subordinate-theme procedure from small ternary structure (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 377, 417-418).

# Key Properties
1. Periodic organization is less common than sentential in subordinate themes
2. Periods are inherently tight-knit; subordinate themes need loose organization
3. When used, consequent is usually expanded (often through ECP)
4. One tight-knit period may appear within a group for contrast
5. Small ternary structure is even rarer in subordinate themes
6. Subordinate themes do not normally recapitulate opening material

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for antecedent-consequent structure in the subordinate theme
2. Check whether the consequent is expanded (longer than antecedent)
3. Determine if the period is tight-knit (one theme in a group) or expanded
4. Note the absence of material recapitulation (distinguishes from small ternary)

# Context & Application
The relative rarity of periodic organization in subordinate themes reflects the fundamental tension between the tight-knit character of the period and the loosening requirements of subordinate-theme function. When composers do use periodic structures, they typically modify them through consequent expansion to reconcile these demands. The presence of a tight-knit periodic theme within a larger subordinate-theme group serves as a moment of formal contrast and stability.
