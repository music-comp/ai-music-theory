# Quick Definition
A pre-core constructed from two thematic units, one or both usually incomplete, with the second often consisting of a presentation phrase that groups intimately with the following core.

# Core Definition
"A small number of relatively large pre-cores are constructed out of two thematic units" (p. 468), one or both of which is usually incomplete. A particularly important configuration occurs when the second part is a presentation phrase: the core then functions as a "magnified continuation," creating a sentence-like structure at a higher level. The second-part presentation "undoubtedly groups more intimately with the core than with the first part of the pre-core."

# Prerequisites
- **Pre-core** — The broader category
- **Presentation function** — The common form of the second unit

# Key Properties
1. Consists of two thematic units
2. One or both units typically incomplete
3. First part may be complete (ending with dominant arrival) or incomplete
4. Second part often a presentation phrase
5. When second part is a presentation, core = magnified continuation
6. Creates sentence-like organization at a higher hierarchical level

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for a clear break or change within the pre-core
2. Check if the second part is a presentation that leads directly into the core
3. Note whether the core's model derives from the same material as the second-part presentation

# Examples
**Example 13.8** (p. 468): Beethoven, Op. 14, No. 2 — first part opens like main theme, ends with premature dominant arrival (m. 68); second part brings subordinate-theme compound basic idea (m. 74), left incomplete at the core's outburst (m. 81).

**Example 13.9** (p. 468): Haydn, H. 49 — first part is a transition-like pre-core (mm. 65-80); second part consists of a presentation phrase (mm. 81-84) from the first subordinate theme; core begins at m. 85 as magnified continuation.
