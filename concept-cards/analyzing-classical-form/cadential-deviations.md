# Cadential Deviations

**Cadential deviations** occur when a potential cadence fails to materialize as expected. The expected cadential resolution is altered or denied.

## Types of Cadential Deviations

1. **Deceptive cadence** - Dominant resolves to unexpected harmony (usually vi)
2. **Evaded cadence** - Expected cadential goal replaced by new formal beginning
3. **Abandoned cadence** - Cadential process stops altogether without resolution
4. **Dominant arrival** - Dominant appears without proper HC progression

## Effect

Cadential deviations typically:
- Deny expected closure
- Motivate continuation or repetition
- Create need for "one more try" at achieving cadence
- Extend themes beyond normative length

## Common Contexts

Cadential deviations are particularly common:
- At the end of continuation phrases (motivating extension)
- At major structural junctures
- When composers want to heighten drama

## Cadential Extension

A cadential deviation often leads to **cadential extension** - additional material following the deviation to achieve proper closure.

## Result

The search for genuine cadential closure following a deviation can significantly expand themes and larger formal units.
