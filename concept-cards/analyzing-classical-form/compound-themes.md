# Compound Themes

**Compound themes** are 16-measure theme types constructed from formal functions that are internally more complex than those of simple 8-measure themes.

## Definition

Two compound themes exist:
- **Compound period** (16 measures)
- **Compound sentence** (16 measures)

These are built from 16 **real** measures (not notated measures appearing as 16 due to R = 2N).

## Relation to Simple Themes

The basic functional components of the period and sentence are present in their compound versions, but the structure of these functions is internally more complex.

## Internal Structure

Each 8-measure unit of a compound theme can be structured as:
- A simple sentence
- A simple hybrid theme
- Another appropriate simple form

## Why "Compound"?

The term reflects that:
- The theme is twice the length of simple themes
- Internal units have their own complete structure
- Higher-level functions operate across 8-measure spans

## Phrase Deviations

Like simple themes, compound themes may be subjected to deviation techniques:
- Extension
- Compression
- Internal expansion

## Historical Note

Most textbooks call the compound period a "double period," but this term is avoided here since it implies a period within a period, which rarely occurs.
