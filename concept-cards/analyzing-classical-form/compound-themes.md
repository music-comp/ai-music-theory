# Quick Definition
Compound themes are 16 real-measure theme types -- the compound period and compound sentence -- whose basic functional components mirror the simple period and sentence but with internally more complex structure at each level.

# Core Definition
Two compound themes exist: the compound period (two 8-m. units fulfilling antecedent and consequent functions) and the compound sentence (an 8-m. compound presentation followed by an 8-m. continuation). These are constructed out of 16 real measures, distinguishing them from simple themes that merely appear to be 16 measures due to R = 2N notation. The basic functional components of the period and sentence are present in the compound versions, but the structure of these functions is internally more complex. Like simple themes, compound themes may undergo deviation techniques. Caplin avoids the traditional term "double period" since it implies a period within a period, which rarely occurs.

# Prerequisites
- **Sentence** — Provides the model for compound sentence
- **Period** — Provides the model for compound period
- **Hybrid themes** — Hybrids can serve as internal structures within compound themes

# Key Properties
1. Two types: compound period and compound sentence
2. Normatively 16 real measures
3. Internally more complex than simple themes
4. May undergo phrase deviations
5. "Double period" is avoided as a term
6. R = 2N notation can create confusion about simple vs. compound

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Sentence** and **Period** — The simple theme types that compound themes elaborate
## Enables
- **Large-scale thematic analysis** — Identifying 16-m. structures in symphonies, concertos
## Contrasts With
- **Simple themes** — 8-m. structures with simpler internal organization
