# Quick Definition
Development keys (DK) are the tonal regions explored in the development section, typically minor-mode areas confirmed by an HC (less often by a PAC), contrasting with the exposition's emphasis on major modality.

# Core Definition
In relatively long development sections, one or more development keys will be confirmed, usually by an HC, sometimes by a PAC. In major-mode movements, the development keys are the minor-mode regions of vi, iii, and ii. In minor-mode movements, the development keys are the minor-mode regions of iv and v (see Figure 9.1). In contrast to the exposition (which emphasizes major modality), the development tends to emphasize minor modalities. Toward the end of the development, the home key is restored and usually confirmed with an HC or dominant arrival.

- **Large-scale tonal structure** -- The broader tonal framework

# Key Properties
1. Typically minor-mode regions
2. Major-mode movements: vi, iii, ii
3. Minor-mode movements: iv, v
4. Usually confirmed by HC, less often by PAC
5. Development emphasizes minor modalities (opposite of exposition)
6. Home key restored toward end of development

# Construction / Recognition
## Key Relationships (Figure 9.1):
| Mode | Home Key | Subordinate Key | Development Keys |
|------|----------|-----------------|------------------|
| Major | I (e.g., C major) | V (e.g., G major) | vi, iii, ii (e.g., A minor, E minor, D minor) |
| Minor | i (e.g., C minor) | III or v (e.g., E-flat major, G minor) | iv, v (e.g., F minor, G minor) |

# Context & Application
The development keys intensify the tonal conflict established in the exposition by moving to more remote tonal regions. The emphasis on minor modality in the development creates a dramatic contrast with the exposition's major-mode emphasis, even in minor-mode movements where the subordinate key is typically in the major mode. This tonal contrast is resolved when the recapitulation restores the home key.

# Examples
**Example** (pp. 295-298): Beethoven, Piano Sonata in F minor, Op. 2, No. 1, i -- the development core does not cadentially confirm a development key, but prominently tonicizes the minor-mode regions of iv (B-flat minor) and v (C minor).
