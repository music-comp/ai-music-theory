## Compression

**Compression** occurs when a formal function is expressed in less time than would normally be expected.

### Definition

A formal unit is compressed when:
- It occupies fewer measures than standard
- It achieves its function more quickly
- Essential elements are retained but condensed

### Contrast with Extension

| Technique | Effect |
|-----------|--------|
| Extension | Function takes longer than expected |
| Compression | Function takes less time than expected |

### Context in Transition

Compression in transitions may occur:
- In continuation function (single bar of continuation)
- Leading quickly to cadential function
- Creating urgency toward the dominant goal

### Effect

Compression creates:
- Sense of urgency
- Asymmetry (when combined with extended units)
- Forward momentum
- Relative loosening (departure from expected proportions)

### Following Large-Scale Presentation

When a large-scale presentation is established (as in some Beethoven works):
- The continuation may be "compressed" relative to the expanded presentation
- Creates dramatic contrast in proportions
