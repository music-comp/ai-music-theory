# Quick Definition
Part 1 of a small binary is typically an 8-m. simple theme (sentence, period, or hybrid), often indistinguishable from an A section of a small ternary except that it may also close with an HC (not just PAC), potentially creating a "failed consequent" structure.

# Core Definition
Part 1 is most often built like the exposition of a rounded binary (sentence, period, hybrid). The key difference from an A section is cadential flexibility: Part 1 may close with PAC (like an A section) or with HC (unlike an A section). When Part 1 ends with HC and periodic functions suggest a consequent, but the consequent closes weakly, a "failed consequent" results (e.g., c.b.i. + failed consequent, or antecedent + failed consequent). In many cases, Part 1 is indistinguishable from an A section until Part 2 reveals the lack of recapitulation.

# Key Properties
1. Usually built as sentence, period, or hybrid (8 mm.)
2. May close with PAC or HC (unlike A section: PAC only)
3. Failed consequent arises when periodic expectations are thwarted by HC
4. May modulate to subordinate key
5. Often indistinguishable from A section until Part 2 is heard
