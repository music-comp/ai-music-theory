# Quick Definition
The confirmation of the subordinate key with a perfect authentic cadence is the essential harmonic-formal requirement of the subordinate theme. The typical aesthetic goal is to delay and dramatize this arrival, making the eventual PAC a powerful moment of key confirmation that allows the subordinate key to temporarily "reign supreme."

# Core Definition
Every subordinate theme must close with a PAC in the subordinate key -- this is non-negotiable from a form-functional perspective, and any exception calls for special explanation. Partial confirmation of the subordinate key occurs through the HC at the end of a modulating transition, which establishes the dominant of the subordinate key as a temporary goal but does not fully confirm the key. Full confirmation is achieved only by the PAC at the end of the subordinate theme, which accomplishes complete cadential closure and allows the subordinate key to temporarily "reign supreme" over the home key. The typical aesthetic goal is to delay the arrival of the anticipated PAC and dramatize the struggle to achieve cadential confirmation, making the eventual PAC a powerful moment of key confirmation. This dramatization is achieved through cadential extensions (evaded cadences, deceptive cadences, abandoned cadences, IACs) and expansions (ECPs) (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 375-376).

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate the first PAC in the subordinate key within the exposition
2. Track all cadential attempts and their outcomes (evasion, deception, abandonment, IAC)
3. The final PAC closing the subordinate-theme group is the confirming cadence
4. Note the techniques used to delay and dramatize the arrival

# Context & Application
Subordinate-key confirmation is the fundamental tonal goal of the exposition's second half. The delay and dramatization of this goal is what gives subordinate themes their characteristic dramatic arc -- from initial lyrical statement through increasingly intense cadential attempts to the eventual confirmation. This process explains why subordinate themes are typically longer and more complex than main themes.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating an HC or IAC in the subordinate key as full confirmation
  **Correction**: Only a PAC provides full confirmation; HC provides partial confirmation, IAC provides insufficient closure
