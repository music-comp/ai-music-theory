## Main Theme Character and Affect

### The Traditional View (Rejected)

Since A. B. Marx (mid-19th century), listeners have believed that:
- Main theme: highly charged, aggressively assertive ("masculine")
- Subordinate theme: quietly lyrical and tender ("feminine")

Some sonata forms, especially symphonies or minor-mode works of Sturm und Drang style, do conform to this stereotype.

### The Reality

Examination of the broader classical repertory shows that main themes frequently exhibit:
- **Hesitancy and uncertainty** within their unfolding
- Sudden, striking changes in texture, dynamics, and articulation
- Marked **discontinuities** in rhythmic momentum

### "Getting Under Way"

Often it is not until the beginning of the **transition** that the movement seems finally to "get under way." The main theme may be more tentative than commonly assumed.

### Diversity of Expression

Main themes can also feature:
- Lyrical, songlike character (typically associated with subordinate themes)
- Juxtaposition of "feminine" and "masculine" styles within a single main-theme group

### Main Theme vs. Introduction

Do not confuse main themes with "introductory" character for actual introductions. Despite textural, dynamic, and rhythmic instabilities, the main theme still contains sufficient tonal, cadential, and phrase-structural solidity to function as the true formal beginning.
