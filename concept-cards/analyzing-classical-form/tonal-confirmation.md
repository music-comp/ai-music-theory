# Quick Definition
The establishment of a tonal center through cadential progressions, with authentic cadences providing the strongest confirmation and half cadences providing weaker confirmation.

# Core Definition
Tonal confirmation is achieved through cadential progressions that "confirm a tonal center by bringing the fundamental harmonic functions in this order: (initial) tonic, pre-dominant, dominant, and (final) tonic" (p. 26). Authentic cadential progressions provide the strongest confirmation (V-I in root position); half-cadential progressions provide weaker confirmation (ending on V); deceptive cadential progressions deny expected confirmation. Prolongational progressions do NOT confirm tonality -- they merely extend an existing harmony.

# Prerequisites
- **Cadential progression** — The vehicle for tonal confirmation

# Key Properties
1. Achieved through cadential progressions (not prolongational ones)
2. Strongest: authentic cadential progression (root-position V to root-position I)
3. Weaker: half-cadential progression (ending on root-position V)
4. Denied: deceptive cadential progression (V to substitute)
5. Requires root-position dominant and (for authentic) root-position tonic
6. Prolongational progressions do NOT confirm tonality

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate a cadential progression with proper functional ordering
2. For authentic: verify root-position V resolving to root-position I
3. For half: verify root-position V as the goal
4. Distinguish from prolongational passages (which extend but do not confirm)

# Context & Application
Tonal confirmation establishes the home key at the beginning of pieces, confirms subordinate keys within larger forms, creates phrase endings and formal articulations, and distinguishes cadential from prolongational passages. The study of form depends on understanding cadential progressions for their role in tonal confirmation.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating any V-I motion as tonal confirmation
  **Correction**: Inversion matters; only root-position V to root-position I constitutes authentic cadential confirmation

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming prolongational progressions confirm tonality
  **Clarification**: Prolongational progressions extend an existing harmony but do not confirm a tonal center; only cadential progressions provide confirmation
