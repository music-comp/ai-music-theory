# Quick Definition
The establishment of a tonal center through cadential progressions, with authentic cadences providing the strongest confirmation and half cadences providing weaker confirmation.

# Formal Definition
Tonal confirmation is achieved through cadential progressions that establish a key by bringing the fundamental harmonic functions in their proper order. Cadential progressions "confirm a tonal center by bringing the fundamental harmonic functions in this order: (initial) tonic, pre-dominant, dominant, and (final) tonic."

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Degrees of tonal confirmation:
- **Strongest**: Authentic cadential progression (V-I in root position)
- **Weaker**: Half-cadential progression (ending on V)
- **Denied**: Deceptive cadential progression (V to substitute)

Requirements for confirmation:
- Dominant must be in root position
- Dominant must retain root position until resolution
- Final tonic must be in root position (for authentic)
- Proper functional ordering must be maintained

Prolongational progressions do NOT confirm tonality - they merely extend an existing harmony.

# Musical Context/Application
Tonal confirmation:
- Establishes the home key at the beginning of pieces
- Confirms subordinate keys within larger forms
- Creates phrase endings and formal articulations
- Distinguishes cadential from prolongational passages

The study of form depends on understanding "the harmonic progressions that reside at the very foreground of the musical surface" for their role in tonal confirmation.

# Common Confusions
- Treating any V-I motion as tonal confirmation (inversion matters)
- Assuming prolongational progressions confirm tonality (they do not)
- Thinking half cadences don't confirm tonality at all (they provide weaker confirmation)
- Conflating local tonic emphasis with structural tonal confirmation
