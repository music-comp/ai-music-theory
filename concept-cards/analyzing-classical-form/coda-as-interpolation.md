# Coda as Interpolation

When the coda gives the impression of being inserted into (rather than following) the recapitulation.

## The Effect

If material from the exposition's closing section is deleted in the recapitulation but restored at the end of the coda's closing section, the coda can give the impression of being interpolated within the recapitulation rather than appended after it.

## Haydn's Practice

Haydn frequently employs this technique. Given his tendency to significantly rewrite recapitulations, this can create considerable ambiguity about whether the movement contains an independent coda.

## Analytical Challenge

It becomes difficult to determine:
- Where exactly the coda begins
- Whether the "coda" is truly separate from recapitulation
- Whether material belongs to recapitulation or coda

## Resolution

The analytical rule (coda starts where recapitulation stops corresponding to exposition) helps, but the perceived effect may still be one of interpolation rather than addition.
