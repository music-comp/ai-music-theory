# Quick Definition
Stepwise melodic motion connecting two different chord tones, creating passing chords within prolongational progressions where the prolonged harmony changes position.

# Core Definition
Passing motion refers to stepwise melodic movement connecting two different chord tones of a prolonged harmony. "In the context of harmonic prolongation, passing motion in the bass usually lies between root-position and first-inversion forms of the prolonged harmony, with a variety of chords built over this passing tone" (p. 34). Crucially, "a passing chord may arise without any of the voices literally displaying passing motion" (p. 35) -- the term refers to the position change of the prolonged harmony.

# Prerequisites
- **Prolongational progression** — Passing motion is one technique within prolongational progressions

# Key Properties
1. Stepwise (conjunct) melodic movement connecting structural chord tones
2. Creates passing chords when harmonized
3. In bass: typically connects 1 to 3 (I to I6) or reverse
4. Various harmonies may be built over the passing bass tone
5. A passing chord may arise WITHOUT literal passing tones in the voices
6. The term refers to position change of the prolonged harmony, not necessarily voice motion

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Check whether the prolonged harmony changes position (e.g., I to I6)
2. Identify the subordinate chord connecting the two positions
3. Note that literal passing tones in the voices are typical but not required
4. If the prolonged harmony stays in the same position, the subordinate chord is neighboring, not passing

# Context & Application
Passing motion is central to prolongational progressions, creating smooth bass lines connecting chord positions. The passing tone supports subordinate harmonies that embellish the prolonged harmony. The distinction between "passing" and "neighboring" is based on whether the prolonged harmony changes position, not on the presence of literal passing tones.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Prolongational progression** — Passing motion is a fundamental prolongational technique
## Enables
- **Harmonic prolongation** — Extends a harmony by connecting its different positions
## Related
- **Neighbor motion** — Another prolongational technique where the prolonged harmony stays in the same position
- **Bass arpeggiation** — Connects positions through leaping rather than stepwise motion
- **Contrapuntal processes** — Passing chords may arise from contrapuntal rather than harmonic processes
## Contrasts With
- **Neighbor motion** — Passing motion: position changes; neighbor motion: same position throughout

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming passing motion must involve literal passing tones in all voices
  **Correction**: Passing motion refers to the position change of the prolonged harmony; literal passing tones are common but not required

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing the harmonic concept (position change) with the melodic concept (stepwise motion)
  **Clarification**: The distinction between passing and neighboring chords is about the position of the prolonged harmony, not about whether individual voices move stepwise
