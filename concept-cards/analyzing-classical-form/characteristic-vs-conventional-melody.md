# Characteristic vs. Conventional Melody

Melodic content in classical themes falls into two broad categories: **characteristic** and **conventional**.

## Characteristic Melody

Found primarily in **basic ideas**:
- Unique, distinctive, idiosyncratic
- Defines the theme as an individual
- Memorable and recognizable
- Not interchangeable between pieces

## Conventional Melody

Found primarily in **cadential ideas**:
- Formulaic, generic
- Relatively interchangeable from piece to piece
- Serves functional rather than thematic purposes
- Results from liquidation process

## Progression Through Theme

A typical theme moves from characteristic to conventional:
1. **Basic idea**: Characteristic melody
2. **Continuation**: Mix of characteristic and conventional
3. **Cadential idea**: Conventional, formulaic

## Liquidation Bridge

**Liquidation** is the process connecting these:
- Gradually eliminates characteristic features
- Until only uncharacteristic (conventional) features remain
- Prepares for cadential closure

## Aesthetic Function

- Characteristic melody: Captures attention, establishes identity
- Conventional melody: Signals closure, creates formal punctuation

This contrast is fundamental to the dramatic shape of classical themes.
