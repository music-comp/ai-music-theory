# Analyzing Fragmentation

Recognizing fragmentation requires identifying the **largest** grouping unit defined by repetition, not just surface-level unit sizes.

## The Principle

Fragmentation is identified in relation to the **largest** grouping unit established by repetition in the preceding material.

## Common Analytical Error

Consider a compound presentation with alternating 2-measure ideas:
- If we view mm. 1-8 as four 2-measure units
- We might fail to hear fragmentation when mm. 9-10 continue with 2-measure units

## Correct Approach

In a compound presentation:
- The **compound basic idea** (4 measures) is the largest repeated unit
- When 2-measure fragments appear in the continuation, this IS fragmentation
- Even though individual ideas were 2 measures, the repeated UNIT was 4 measures

## Guideline

When identifying repeated units:
- Look for the largest unit size that exhibits repetition
- Fragmentation occurs when units become smaller than this
- Surface-level groupings may be misleading

## Multiple Levels

Fragmentation can occur at multiple levels:
- 4-measure units → 2-measure units (first stage)
- 2-measure units → 1-measure units (further fragmentation)

Always compare to the largest established unit.
