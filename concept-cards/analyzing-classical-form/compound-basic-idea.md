# Quick Definition
A 4-measure phrase consisting of a basic idea followed by a contrasting idea that does not end with a cadence, functioning as a higher-level initiating unit that is itself a hybrid of antecedent and presentation functions.

# Core Definition
By definition, an antecedent contains a basic idea followed by a contrasting idea leading to a weak cadence. If a cadence does not appear at the end of the phrase, an essential component of antecedent function is lost. The lack of cadential closure creates a sense of open-endedness, giving the phrase the character and function of a higher-level basic idea. Caplin terms this a "compound basic idea." By virtue of its melodic-motivic content (b.i. + c.i.), it resembles an antecedent; by its underlying harmony, which is usually tonic prolongational, it resembles a presentation. The 4-m. phrase thus itself represents a hybrid of antecedent and presentation functions.

# Prerequisites
- **Basic idea** — The 2-m. opening unit
- **Contrasting idea** — The 2-m. unit that follows with different material
- **Antecedent phrase** — Must understand how c.b.i. differs from an antecedent (lack of cadence)
- **Presentation phrase** — Must understand harmonic similarity (tonic prolongation)

# Key Properties
1. Structure: 2-m. basic idea + 2-m. contrasting idea
2. Does NOT end with a cadence (distinguishing it from an antecedent)
3. Underlying harmony is usually (but not always) tonic prolongational
4. Functions as an initiating formal function at the 4-m. level
5. The term "compound" suggests its higher structural level relative to a simple 2-m. basic idea

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for a 2-m. basic idea followed by a 2-m. contrasting idea
2. Check whether the phrase ends with a cadence: if NO, it is a c.b.i.
3. Verify the underlying harmony is predominantly tonic prolongational
4. Note that a dominant appearing at m. 3 and prolonged through m. 4 prevents an HC (no cadential progression)

# Context & Application
The c.b.i. serves as the initiating function in two hybrid types (c.b.i. + continuation, c.b.i. + consequent) and gains even greater significance in compound themes, where a repeated c.b.i. creates a compound presentation in the 16-m. compound sentence. The term "compound basic idea" was chosen to reinforce that this is an initiating function at a higher structural level.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 124): Haydn, Piano Sonata H. 35, i, mm. 1-4 — basic idea followed by contrasting idea, but the dominant first appears at m. 3 and supports the entire c.i., preventing any cadential progression; phrase is a tonic prolongation.
**Example 2** (p. 125): Beethoven, String Quartet Op. 18/2, iv, mm. 21-24 — absence of harmonic motion into m. 24 prohibits recognizing a cadence.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Basic idea** — The compound version operates at the 4-m. level
- **Antecedent phrase** — Shares melodic-motivic structure but lacks cadence
## Enables
- **C.b.i. + continuation** — The opening function of Hybrid 3
- **C.b.i. + consequent** — The opening function of Hybrid 4
- **Compound presentation** — A repeated c.b.i. creates the opening of a compound sentence
## Related
- **Presentation phrase** — Both are tonic-prolongational initiating functions
## Contrasts With
- **Antecedent phrase** — Antecedent ends with a weak cadence; c.b.i. does not
- **Presentation phrase** — Presentation repeats the basic idea; c.b.i. brings contrasting material

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Identifying a c.b.i. as an antecedent when dominant harmony appears but does not create a cadential progression
  **Correction**: A dominant at m. 3 prolonged through m. 4 is tonic-prolongational, not cadential; no HC results

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing c.b.i. with presentation when the contrasting idea is only slightly different from the basic idea
  **Clarification**: This ambiguity is real; Caplin acknowledges that the distinction between "repetition" and "contrasting material" can be ambiguous, and both analyses may be viable
