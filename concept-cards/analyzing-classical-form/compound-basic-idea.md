# Compound Basic Idea

A **compound basic idea** is a 4-measure phrase consisting of a basic idea and a contrasting idea that does NOT end with a cadence. It is itself a hybrid of antecedent and presentation functions.

## Structure

- 2-measure **basic idea** (tonic emphasis)
- 2-measure **contrasting idea** (no cadential closure)

## What Makes It Different

By **definition**, an antecedent contains a basic idea followed by a contrasting idea leading to a weak cadence. If no cadence appears, an essential component of antecedent function is lost.

## Why "Compound Basic Idea"?

The lack of cadential closure creates a sense of **open-endedness**, giving the phrase the character of a higher-level basic idea.

The term emphasizes:
- It is an **initiating** formal function (like a basic idea)
- It resides at a higher structural level (4 measures vs. 2 measures)
- The qualifier "compound" suggests greater complexity

## Hybrid Character

A compound basic idea resembles:
- An **antecedent** by virtue of its melodic-motivic content (basic idea + contrasting idea)
- A **presentation** by virtue of its harmonic support (tonic prolongational, no cadence)

## Harmonic Support

The underlying harmony is usually (but not always) **tonic prolongational**. The entire phrase prolongs tonic, preventing any cadential closure.
