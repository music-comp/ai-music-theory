# Quick Definition
Two exceptions to the standard home-key dominant ending: (1) when the recapitulation does not begin on tonic, the development ends on a harmony leading to the recapitulation's opening; (2) dominant substitutes (V/vi, V/iii) that contain the leading tone.

# Core Definition
"The final harmony of the development is usually the dominant of the home key" (p. 482), but two exceptions arise. First, if the recapitulation does not begin with home-key tonic, the development's final harmony leads most naturally into the recapitulation's opening harmony. Second, V/vi or V/iii can substitute for the normal dominant, since "both of these harmonies contain the leading tone of the home key, a necessary condition for dominant functionality." To mitigate the cross-relation from resolution, "the texture at the very end of the development is usually reduced to a single voice."

# Key Properties
1. Standard ending: home-key dominant
2. Exception 1: recapitulation not starting on tonic -> development ends on harmony leading to that opening
3. Exception 2: V/vi or V/iii as dominant substitute (both contain leading tone)
4. Cross-relation mitigated by texture reduction to single voice
5. Texture reduction part of broader liquidation process common to developments

# Examples
**Example 13.17** (p. 482): Haydn, Symphony No. 92 "Oxford" — recapitulation begins with dominant seventh (as in exposition); development closes with V/V to set up this beginning.

**Example 13.18** (p. 483): Beethoven, Violin Sonata in F "Spring," Op. 24, i — development ends with V/vi, resolving directly to tonic at recapitulation (m. 124); texture reduced to avoid cross-relation.
