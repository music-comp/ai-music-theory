# Framing Functions

**Framing functions** are formal units that "frame" a theme by appearing before its beginning or after its end. They are external to the theme proper.

## Types of Framing Functions

### Before-the-Beginning Functions
- **Introduction** - Material preceding the theme

### After-the-End Functions
- **Closing section** - Multiple codettas following the cadence
- **Codetta** - Brief postcadential unit
- **Standing on the dominant** - Postcadential dominant prolongation (after HC)

## Characteristics

Framing functions:
- Stand OUTSIDE the theme proper
- Are not essential to the theme's form-functional organization
- Could theoretically be removed without destroying the theme
- Add emphasis, preparation, or closure

## Introduction Functions

Introductions:
- Prepare the onset of the theme
- May establish tempo, key, character
- Range from brief to extended

## Closing Functions

Closing functions:
- Confirm and reinforce cadential closure
- Project "recessive dynamic" (dying away)
- May repeat cadential material multiple times

## Notation

In formal diagrams, framing functions are typically indicated separately from the theme's main phrase structure.
