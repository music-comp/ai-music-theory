# Quick Definition
A discrepancy between real measures (as experienced metrically) and notated measures (as written). R = 1/2N: one real measure occupies half a notated measure (slow movements). R = 2N: one real measure embraces two notated measures (fast scherzos). Formal definitions use real measures.

# Core Definition
There are cases where a composer notates what appears to be a 4-m. or 16-m. sentence, yet we still experience the sense that the theme contains eight real measures of music, even though there are more or fewer notated measures (p. 85). Two situations: (1) R = 1/2N -- in slow movements, a real measure occupies half a notated measure; (2) R = 2N -- in fast triple-meter scherzos, real measures embrace two notated measures (pp. 85-86). 18th-century theorists described R = 1/2N as "compound meter" -- two simple meters combined into one measure (p. 86).

# Prerequisites
- **Sentence** — The real-vs-notated distinction is explained in the context of the 8-m. sentence

# Key Properties
1. Formal definitions are given in terms of REAL measures
2. R = 1/2N: common in slow movements (adagio, moderato esp. Haydn)
3. R = 2N: common in fast triple-meter scherzos and fast duple-meter movements
4. A sentence always contains 8 real measures regardless of notation
5. Cadences in R = 1/2N may appear mid-bar (at the midpoint of the notated measure)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Determine:
1. Identify the tempo and meter
2. In slow movements: check if cadences appear mid-bar (suggests R = 1/2N)
3. In fast triple-meter: check if the basic idea spans 4 notated measures (suggests R = 2N)
4. Apply formal definitions based on real measures

# Context & Application
This distinction is essential for accurate formal analysis. A theme notated as 4 measures in a slow movement (R = 1/2N) is really an 8-m. theme. A theme notated as 16 measures in a scherzo (R = 2N) is really an 8-m. theme. Failing to account for this discrepancy leads to misidentification of formal units.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 86): Slow movement with R = 1/2N -- cadences appear at mid-bar points.

**Example 2** (p. 86): Fast triple-meter scherzo with R = 2N -- two notated measures equal one real measure.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Analyzing a 4-bar slow movement passage as a 4-m. half-sentence
  **Correction**: In R = 1/2N, this is actually an 8-m. sentence with each real measure at half a notated measure

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing Caplin's "compound meter" with modern use of "compound meter" (6/8, 9/8)
  **Clarification**: 18th-century "compound meter" refers to two simple meters combined in one bar (R = 1/2N)
