# Real vs. Notated Measures

In classical music, there can be a discrepancy between **real** measures (as experienced) and **notated** measures (as written). Understanding this distinction is essential for accurate formal analysis.

## Definition

- **Real measures**: How the music is actually experienced metrically
- **Notated measures**: How the music appears in the score

The definitions of formal units are given in terms of **real** measures.

## Two Common Situations

### R = ½N (Real = Half of Notated)
- A single real measure occupies only half of a notated measure
- Common in slow movements (adagio) or moderate tempos (especially Haydn)
- **Cue**: Cadences appearing midbar

### R = 2N (Real = Two Notated)
- Real measures embrace two notated measures
- Common in fast triple-meter scherzo movements
- Also found in fast duple-meter outer movements

## Historical Background

18th-century theorists described R = ½N as "compound meter" - two simple meters combined into one measure. They observed that cadences normally occur on downbeats of simple meters, so in compound meters, cadences may appear midbar.

## Practical Application

A sentence always contains eight **real** measures, whether notated as 4, 8, or 16 measures. When analyzing:
- Identify tempo and meter
- Determine the relationship between real and notated measures
- Apply formal definitions based on real measures
