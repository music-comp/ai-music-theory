# Quick Definition
When periodic functions appear in subordinate themes, the antecedent usually retains its conventional size (4 or 8 measures), but the consequent is often expanded -- typically through an expanded cadential progression (ECP) -- to create the looser organization appropriate for subordinate-theme function.

# Core Definition
Antecedent and consequent functions occur less frequently in subordinate themes than the sentential functions of presentation, continuation, and cadential. When periodic functions do appear, the antecedent usually retains its conventional size of four or eight measures, but the consequent is often expanded to create a looser organization. The expansion typically occurs through an expanded cadential progression (ECP) within the consequent. One theme of a subordinate-theme group may alternatively take the form of a tight-knit period, creating contrast with looser themes in the group. Occasionally the opening motive of the subordinate theme's basic idea derives from the contrasting idea of the main theme, though the new tonal context changes the scale-degree functions and may obscure the direct connection (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 417-418).

# Key Properties
1. Antecedent retains conventional size (4 or 8 measures)
2. Consequent is expanded, typically through ECP
3. Creates looser organization appropriate for subordinate themes
4. Less common than sentential organization in subordinate themes
5. One theme in a group may be a tight-knit period for contrast
6. Melodic material may derive from main theme's contrasting idea

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for an antecedent phrase of standard length (4 or 8 mm.)
2. Check whether the following consequent is expanded beyond the antecedent's length
3. Identify the expansion technique (usually ECP)
4. Compare with other themes in the group for tight-knit vs. loose contrast

# Context & Application
Expanded periodic design is one way subordinate themes can achieve the requisite looser organization while using periodic functions. The expansion of the consequent provides the dramatic weight and temporal extension needed for the subordinate key's confirmation, while the antecedent provides a stable point of departure.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 417): Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 576, i, mm. 42-53 -- Second subordinate theme begins with 4-measure antecedent; consequent is expanded by means of an ECP in mm. 48-53. Opening motive derives from contrasting idea of the main theme.
