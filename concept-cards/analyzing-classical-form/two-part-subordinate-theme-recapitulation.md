# Quick Definition
When the exposition has a two-part subordinate theme (with an internal HC and standing on dominant), the recapitulation typically deletes or assimilates the first part to avoid redundant double articulation of the home-key dominant.

# Core Definition
In the exposition, a two-part subordinate theme features an internal HC and standing on the dominant that emphasize dominant harmony of the subordinate key, especially with a nonmodulating transition. If left intact in the recapitulation, two cadential articulations of home-key dominant would result: the HC of the transition and the internal HC of the two-part subordinate theme. This creates potential redundancy. To solve this, either the first part of the subordinate theme is deleted, or portions of the first part are assimilated into the preceding transition. The second part then follows as the actual subordinate theme in the recapitulation. Material that originally functioned as the internal HC and standing on dominant may conclude the transition in the recapitulation rather than beginning the subordinate theme.

# Key Properties
- Exposition has two-part subordinate theme with internal HC and standing on dominant
- Tonal adjustment creates redundant double dominant articulation in the home key
- First part typically deleted or assimilated into the transition
- Second part serves as the subordinate theme in the recapitulation
- Internal HC/standing on dominant may be reassigned to conclude the transition
- Eliminates the redundancy of two successive dominant emphasis passages in the home key

# Construction / Recognition
Compare the subordinate theme group of the exposition and recapitulation. If the exposition has a two-part subordinate theme with an internal HC, check whether the recapitulation retains both parts. If the first part has been deleted or its material incorporated into the transition, note how the second part now functions as the complete subordinate theme. Look for the internal HC/standing on dominant being relocated to conclude the transition.

# Context & Application
This structural change reflects the different tonal context of the recapitulation. In the exposition, the internal HC emphasizes the dominant of the subordinate key, which is a fresh harmonic area. In the recapitulation, this would emphasize the dominant of the home key -- the same harmony already articulated by the transition. The redundancy would create a static, repetitive quality. Deletion or assimilation of the first part avoids this problem and creates a more streamlined subordinate theme.

# Examples
- General pattern: exposition's two-part subordinate theme begins with internal HC and standing on dominant of subordinate key; in recapitulation, this material concludes the transition (now on home-key dominant), and the second part follows as the subordinate theme
- Material from the first part may be assimilated into the preceding transition, blending the functions

# Common Errors
- Assuming both parts of a two-part subordinate theme must always appear in the recapitulation
- Failing to recognize that material from the subordinate theme's first part has been relocated to the transition

# Common Confusions
- The deletion of the first part is NOT a loss of important content -- it eliminates a redundant dominant articulation while preserving the essential cadential and thematic content in the second part
