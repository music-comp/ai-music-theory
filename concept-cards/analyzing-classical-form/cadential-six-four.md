# Quick Definition
A second-inversion tonic triad that functions as an embellishment of dominant harmony in root position within cadential contexts, not as an independent tonic chord.

# Formal Definition
In many cadential situations, tonic harmony in second inversion (I with 6-4 above the bass) functions as an embellishment of dominant harmony in root position. When resolving to a dominant seventh, the progression of the two chords can be notated as V(6-4 to 5-3) or V(6-4 to 7), thus showing that the cadential six-four has dominant harmonic function.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
The cadential six-four:
- Is built on scale degree 5 in the bass
- Contains the pitch content of the tonic triad (scale degrees 1, 3, 5)
- Functions as a melodic embellishment of the dominant (6-4 resolving to 5-3)
- Has **dominant function**, not tonic function
- Must resolve to root-position V before the cadential arrival

Analytical notation: The chord is analyzed as dominant harmony in root position with the 6-4 embellishments and their resolutions placed in parentheses: V(6-4 to 5-3) or V(6-4 to 7).

# Musical Context/Application
The cadential six-four appears:
1. As the principal embellishment of the cadential dominant
2. Within both authentic and half-cadential progressions
3. Before V7 in expanded cadential progressions
4. The 6 resolves to 5, the 4 resolves to 3 (or 7 in seventh chords)

This embellishment adds emphasis and weight to the dominant, delaying its pure consonant form and creating greater tension before resolution.

# Examples
Example 1.12 (p. 38) demonstrates the cadential six-four as the principal dominant embellishment in authentic cadential progressions:
- I6 - II6 - V(6-4 to 5-3) - I shows the complete authentic cadential progression with six-four embellishment
- The six-four is not an independent harmony but part of the dominant's articulation

# Common Confusions
- Treating the cadential six-four as having tonic function because it contains tonic pitches (it functions as dominant)
- Analyzing it as an independent chord rather than as an embellishment of V
- Confusing with passing six-four or other second-inversion contexts (cadential six-four is specifically cadential)
- Forgetting that the bass note (scale degree 5) is what determines function, not the upper voices
