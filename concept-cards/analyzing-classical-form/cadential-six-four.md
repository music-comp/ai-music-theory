# Quick Definition
A second-inversion tonic triad functioning as an embellishment of dominant harmony in root position. It has dominant function, not tonic, and is notated V(6/4-5/3) to show the embellishment and resolution. The principal embellishment of the cadential dominant.

# Core Definition
In many cadential situations, tonic harmony in second inversion (I6/4) functions as an embellishment of dominant harmony in root position. The expression cadential six-four is often used to identify this embellishment. When resolving to a dominant seventh, the progression can be notated as V(6/4-5/3) or V(6/4-7), thus showing that the cadential six-four has dominant harmonic function (p. 31). The chord is built on scale degree 5 in the bass and resolves: 6 to 5, 4 to 3 (or 7).

# Key Properties
1. Contains tonic pitches (scale degrees 1, 3, 5) but has DOMINANT function
2. Bass note is scale degree 5 (the dominant root)
3. 6 resolves to 5, 4 resolves to 3 (or 7 in seventh chords)
4. Notated as V(6/4-5/3) or V(6/4-7), not as I6/4
5. Principal embellishment of the cadential dominant
6. Appears in both authentic and half-cadential progressions
7. Determines the moment of cadential arrival (when the bass first sounds scale degree 5)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for a second-inversion triad on scale degree 5 in the bass
2. Verify it appears in a cadential context (not passing or neighboring)
3. Check that it resolves to root-position V (5/3 or 7)
4. Analyze as dominant function, not tonic

# Context & Application
The cadential six-four adds emphasis and weight to the dominant, delaying its pure consonant form and creating greater tension before resolution. It is the principal embellishment of dominant harmony in cadential contexts. The moment the bass sounds scale degree 5 with the six-four marks the cadential arrival in half cadences, even though the suspensions resolve afterward.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Analyzing the cadential six-four as I6/4 (tonic)
  **Correction**: It has dominant function; analyze as V(6/4-5/3)

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Treating the cadential six-four as tonic because it contains tonic pitches
  **Clarification**: The bass note (scale degree 5) determines function; the upper voices are embellishments of dominant
- **Confusion**: Confusing cadential six-four with passing or neighboring six-four chords
  **Clarification**: Cadential six-four specifically appears in cadential contexts as dominant embellishment
