# Quick Definition
The development's content may make certain recapitulation changes seem particularly appropriate (not causally determined): ideas exploited in the development may be deleted in the recapitulation, and a brief development may prompt added sequential passages.

# Core Definition
"To speak of the development influencing the recapitulation is not to suggest a causal connection" (p. 522). Rather, "it is more a question of the appropriateness of occurrence. When we attend carefully to certain events in the development, we may come to believe that it is particularly fitting that some other event does — or does not — occur in the recapitulation." Compensatory sequences may appear when the development lacks a core; extensively developed ideas may be appropriately deleted.

# Key Properties
1. Not a causal connection — appropriateness, not determination
2. Ideas exploited in development may be deleted in recapitulation
3. Brief/absent development core may prompt secondary development in recapitulation
4. Material prominent in development may need home-key "recapitulation" (possibly in coda)
5. Counterexamples abound — no deterministic relationship

# Examples
**Example 14.5** (p. 509): Mozart, K. 283 — added sequence in recapitulation's main theme compensates for lack of core in the development.

**Example 14.7** (p. 522): Mozart, K. 403 — new model-sequence in transition appropriate given the development's conspicuously absent sequential organization.
