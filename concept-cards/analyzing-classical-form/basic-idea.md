# Basic Idea

The **basic idea** is a 2-measure unit that typically begins classical themes. It is the fundamental melodic-motivic content from which the theme develops.

## Characteristics

- Usually 2 measures in length (embracing two metrical downbeats)
- Contains individual **motives** that may become detached and developed later
- Melodic content is typically **characteristic** (unique, idiosyncratic) rather than conventional
- Usually projects an "opening up" of melodic space through ascending gestures
- Supported by **tonic harmony** when it first appears

## Size and Function

The basic idea is:
- **Small** enough to group with other ideas into phrases and themes
- **Large** enough to be broken down (fragmented) to develop its constituent motives

The 2-measure basic idea is the ideal starting point for both integration into larger formal units and disintegration into smaller motivic elements.

## Boundaries

- The basic idea embraces two metrical downbeats
- Often begins with an **upbeat** (anacrusis) and finishes before the end of the second measure
- Boundaries are a matter of interpretation; use musical instincts rather than relying on bar lines

## Important Distinction

When a 2-measure basic idea consists of a repeated 1-measure motive, do not confuse the 1-measure motive for the basic idea itself. The 2-measure unit remains the basic idea.

## Complete Unit

The basic idea is the complete unit of music including its harmonic, rhythmic, and textural components. The basic idea is much more than just its "tune."
