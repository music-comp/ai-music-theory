# Quick Definition
A core substitute whose dynamics, rhythm, texture, and emotional character strongly resemble a core but whose material is not organized by model-sequence technique.

# Core Definition
A pseudo-core exhibits the expressive qualities of a core — "restlessness, instability, and Sturm und Drang" with loud dynamics, thick textures, and continuous rhythmic activity (p. 473) — but lacks the structural hallmarks. "Such a pseudo-core sometimes features a prominent sequential organization of the harmonies, but they are not used to support the extensive model-sequence repetitions essential to a core proper." Some pseudo-cores take on an improvisatory "fantasia" or "toccata" style, with a prominent linear bass progression providing coherence.

# Prerequisites
- **Core** — The genuine form that the pseudo-core resembles
- **Development without core** — The context for core substitutes

# Key Properties
1. Core-like character: restlessness, instability, Sturm und Drang
2. Loud dynamic, thick textures, continuous rhythmic activity
3. No large-scale model (4-8 measures) for sequencing
4. No systematic model-sequence repetitions
5. May have sequential harmonies (but not supporting model-sequence technique)
6. May be improvisatory (fantasia/toccata style)
7. Linear bass progression may provide coherence

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for core-like character (dynamics, rhythm, texture, emotion)
2. Confirm the absence of a large-scale model that is sequentially repeated
3. Check whether harmonic sequences exist but do not support model-sequence structure

# Examples
**Example 13.14** (p. 474): Mozart, Piano Sonata in B-flat, K. 333, i — continuous Alberti bass, restless running-sixteenth melody; highly suggestive of a core, but no model is sequentially repeated; harmonies suggest descending-fifth sequence at a deep level but are more controlled by chromatic bass oscillation.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Identifying a pseudo-core as a genuine core based on character alone
