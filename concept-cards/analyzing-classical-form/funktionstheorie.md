# Quick Definition
The "theory of functions" developed by Hugo Riemann, classifying harmonies into three main functional categories (tonic, dominant, subdominant/pre-dominant) rather than by individual scale degrees.

# Formal Definition
Funktionstheorie ("theory of functions") is a theoretical approach developed by the late-19th-century German theorist Hugo Riemann that classifies harmonies by their functional role rather than by scale degree. The modern North American adaptation retains Riemann's categories of tonic and dominant functions but usually reconceptualizes his "subdominant" function into a more all-embracing "pre-dominant" function.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Riemann's original three functions:
- **Tonic** (T): Represents stability, home key center
- **Dominant** (D): Creates tension, drives toward tonic
- **Subdominant** (S): Prepares dominant

Modern North American adaptation:
- Tonic and Dominant retained
- Subdominant expanded to "Pre-dominant" (PD)
- Pre-dominant embraces more chord types (II, IV, bII, augmented sixths, V/V)

The functional approach reveals that multiple Roman numeral chords share the same underlying function, providing deeper analytical insight than step-based analysis alone.

# Musical Context/Application
Funktionstheorie provides:
- Simplified classification of harmonic activity
- Understanding of chord substitution within functions
- Framework for understanding cadential progressions
- Basis for the prolongational/cadential distinction

The approach is complementary to Roman numeral analysis (Stufentheorie), providing functional interpretation of individual chords.

# Common Confusions
- Confusing Riemann's "subdominant" with modern "pre-dominant" (related but not identical)
- Treating Funktionstheorie and Stufentheorie as mutually exclusive
- Assuming functional categories are rigid (some chords have context-dependent function)
- Not recognizing the expansion of pre-dominant beyond IV alone
