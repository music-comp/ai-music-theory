# Quick Definition
The harmonic function creating tension and driving toward resolution to tonic, primarily expressed by V and VII chords containing the leading tone.

# Formal Definition
Dominant function is most often represented by the major triad or major-minor seventh chord built on the fifth scale degree. The leading-tone diminished triad in first inversion (VII6) and leading-tone seventh chord (VII7 and its inversions) also have dominant function when they resolve to tonic harmony. In certain limited contexts, the harmony built on the third degree (III) can function as a substitute for the dominant.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Dominant-functioning harmonies include:
- **V**: Major triad on the fifth degree (most common)
- **V7**: Major-minor seventh chord on the fifth degree
- **VII6**: Leading-tone diminished triad in first inversion
- **VII7**: Leading-tone seventh chord in all inversions
- **III** (limited contexts): Can function as dominant substitute (as in III6 - I)

All dominant-functioning harmonies contain the leading tone, which is essential to their tension-creating and resolution-driving character.

Note: VII chords are not considered dominants when, in sequential situations, they progress to nontonic harmonies such as III.

# Musical Context/Application
Dominant function appears in several contexts:
1. **Cadential progressions**: Root-position V is essential for cadential articulation
2. **Prolongational progressions**: V can serve as neighboring or passing chord
3. **Half-cadential goals**: V triad (not seventh) serves as ultimate harmony
4. **Deceptive progressions**: V resolves to VI instead of expected I

The **cadential six-four** (I in second inversion, notated as V with 6-4 embellishment) functions as an embellishment of dominant harmony in root position, not as a tonic chord.

# Common Confusions
- Treating the cadential six-four as tonic function when it actually has dominant function
- Assuming any V-I motion creates a cadence (inversion matters significantly)
- Confusing VII as dominant when it appears in sequential (nonfunctional) contexts
