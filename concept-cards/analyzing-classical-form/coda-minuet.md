## Two Types of Coda

### 1. Coda to Minuet/Trio Form
- Follows the da capo
- Concludes the entire movement
- Typically references contents of the trio

### 2. Coda to Minuet Form (within recapitulation)
- Follows the final cadence of A' section
- Material sufficiently complex to require further cadential closure
- More intimately linked to the recapitulation than in sonata form

## Distinction: Closing Section vs. Coda

- **Closing section**: New material following final PAC, but relatively simple
- **Coda**: Material organized in a manner requiring further cadential closure

## Relationship to Recapitulation

Unlike sonata form (where coda is entirely separate from recapitulation):
- Minuet coda is **included in the repetition** of B and A' sections together
- Coda precedes the double-bar and repeat signs
- More intimately linked to the recapitulation

## When Coda Appears

Codas in minuet form are most likely when:
- Recapitulation is modeled closely on the exposition
- Coda then takes over the role of expanding the form
- Serves same purposes as an enlarged A' section

## Functions

- Provides greater cadential weight to home key
- Uses expanded cadential progression
- Compensates for stronger subordinate-key confirmation in B section
- May repeat material identically for reinforcement
