# Quick Definition
Codas in minuet form may appear at two levels: after the A' section (within the recapitulation) or after the da capo (concluding the whole movement). Unlike sonata codas, minuet codas are included in the repeat of the B+A' sections.

# Core Definition
The overall minuet/trio form may close with a coda following the da capo. More typically, the recapitulation of minuet form may contain a coda following the final cadence of A'. Unlike the sonata, where the coda is entirely separate from the recapitulation, the coda in minuet form is more intimately linked to the latter since it is included in the repetition of the B and A' sections (it precedes the double-bar and repeat signs; Caplin, pp. 649-650).

# Key Properties
1. Two levels: within the recapitulation OR after the da capo
2. Within recapitulation: included in the B+A' repeat (before double bar)
3. After da capo: concludes the entire movement, may reference trio material
4. Most likely when recapitulation closely models the exposition
5. Coda takes over the role of expanding the form
6. Provides greater cadential weight to home key through expanded cadential progressions

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for material after the final PAC of A' that requires further cadential closure
2. Check whether the material is included in the repeat (minuet-level coda) or follows the da capo (movement-level coda)

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 650): Haydn, Symphony No. 87 in A, iii -- coda after A' consists of new 6-m. unit repeated identically, providing expanded cadential progression for home-key reinforcement.
