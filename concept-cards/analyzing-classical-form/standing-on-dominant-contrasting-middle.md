# Standing on the Dominant (Contrasting Middle)

In the simplest cases, the entire B section of a small ternary is supported exclusively by dominant harmony, functioning as a **standing on the dominant**.

## Structure

A typical standing on the dominant:
- Introduces a new 2-measure idea
- Repeats the idea
- May fragment further if section is longer

## No Cadence at End

A standing on the dominant does NOT end with a cadence:
- There is no harmonic change
- No cadential progression can occur
- The section simply "ends" on dominant

## Important Distinction

Do NOT automatically place an HC label at the end. Even with a fermata creating a clear "stop," no cadential closure occurs because:
- A cadence requires a cadential **progression**
- Prolongation of a single harmony = no progression = no cadence

## Two Contexts

Standing on the dominant appears in two formal contexts:

1. **As the complete B section**: Follows A section's PAC
2. **As postcadential function**: Follows B section's HC

In context 1, it does not extend any cadence. In context 2, it extends the HC.

## Preparation for Recapitulation

The standing on the dominant creates strong expectation for:
- Resolution to tonic
- Return of the basic idea
