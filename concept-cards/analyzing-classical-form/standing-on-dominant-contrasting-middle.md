# Quick Definition
In the simplest cases, the entire B section of a small ternary is supported exclusively by dominant harmony, functioning as a standing on the dominant. No cadence occurs at the end because prolongation of a single harmony cannot produce a cadential progression.

# Core Definition
When the entire B section functions as a standing on the dominant, the dominant harmony is prolonged throughout. A typical structure introduces a new 2-measure idea, repeats it, and may fragment further if the section is longer. Crucially, a standing on the dominant does NOT end with a cadence: there is no harmonic change, no cadential progression can occur, and the section simply "ends" on dominant. Even with a fermata creating a clear stop, no cadential closure occurs because a cadence requires a cadential progression, and prolongation of a single harmony produces no progression. This formal context (standing on the dominant as complete B section, following the A section's PAC) is distinct from the postcadential standing on the dominant (which follows the B section's HC and extends that cadence).

# Key Properties
1. Dominant harmony prolonged throughout entire B section
2. Follows A section's PAC
3. No cadence at end (no cadential progression possible)
4. Do NOT label HC at the end, even with fermata
5. Typical structure: new 2-m. idea + repetition + possible fragmentation
6. Creates strong expectation for tonic resolution at A'
7. Distinct from postcadential standing on the dominant

# Construction / Recognition
## Two Formal Contexts Compared:
| Feature | As Complete B Section | As Postcadential Extension |
|---------|----------------------|---------------------------|
| Follows | A section's PAC | B section's HC |
| Extends a cadence? | No | Yes (extends HC) |
| IS the B section? | Yes | No (extends it) |
| Preceded by harmonic activity? | No (only dominant prolongation) | Yes (continuation phrase to HC) |

# Context & Application
The standing on the dominant as complete B section represents the simplest possible contrasting middle: maximum harmonic contrast with the A section's tonic orientation but minimum melodic-motivic complexity. It creates the strongest possible expectation for the recapitulation by building tension through sustained dominant prolongation. The prohibition against labeling an HC at the end is an important analytical principle.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Labeling HC at the end of a standing on the dominant, especially when a fermata is present
  **Correction**: A cadence requires a cadential progression; prolongation of a single harmony cannot produce one, so no HC can be labeled regardless of fermata or clear stopping point
