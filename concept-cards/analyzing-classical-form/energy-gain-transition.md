# Quick Definition
The transition frequently represents a significant energy gain through "brilliant style" passage work (scales, arpeggios), conventional accompanimental patterns (Alberti, drum, murky bass), increased dynamics, and fuller texture. The transition is often where the movement seems to truly "get under way."

# Core Definition
Transitions frequently feature "passage work" -- arpeggiations and scale patterns projecting a "brilliant style" -- rather than the tuneful melodies typical of main themes. Transitions are often the first place where conventional accompanimental patterns (Alberti bass, drum bass, murky bass) appear, bringing continuous rhythmic propulsion. The beginning of the transition is often when the movement seems to be "getting under way," especially when the main theme was hesitant or tentative (Caplin, Ch. 11, pp. 336-337).

# Key Properties
1. "Brilliant style" passage work (scales, arpeggios) rather than tunes
2. Conventional accompanimental patterns: Alberti bass, drum bass, murky bass
3. Continuous rhythmic propulsion and forward drive
4. Dynamic intensification (crescendo, forte)
5. Often where the movement "gets under way"
