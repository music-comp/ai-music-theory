# Antecedent Phrase

The **antecedent phrase** is the opening 4-measure unit of a period, consisting of a basic idea followed by a contrasting idea leading to a weak cadence.

## Structure

- 2-measure **basic idea** (supported by tonic harmony)
- 2-measure **contrasting idea** leading to a weak cadence

## Cadential Requirements

The antecedent must close with a **weak cadence**:
- Usually a **half cadence** (HC) - most common
- Sometimes an **imperfect authentic cadence** (IAC) - less common
- Never a **perfect authentic cadence** (PAC)

## Why No PAC?

A PAC achieves complete harmonic and melodic closure, which would not motivate a consequent phrase. The weak cadence creates the need for stronger closure to follow.

## Non-Modulating

An antecedent phrase closes in the same key in which it begins. It does NOT modulate. (One exception: the reinterpreted half cadence.)

## Formal Function

Antecedent function is an **initiating** formal function that:
- Presents the fundamental melodic material
- Creates partial closure
- Sets up expectations for consequent completion

## No Elision

An antecedent phrase does NOT elide with its consequent. The boundaries between the phrases are clearly separate, preserving the symmetrical phrase structure and the perception of repetition.
