# Quick Definition
The opening 4-m. phrase of a period: b.i. (2 m.) + contrasting idea (2 m.) leading to a weak cadence (HC or IAC, never PAC). The antecedent does not modulate (exception: reinterpreted HC) and does not elide with the consequent.

# Core Definition
An antecedent phrase begins with a 2-m. basic idea (just like in the sentence), followed by a 2-m. contrasting idea leading to a weak cadence, usually a half cadence (HC) but sometimes an imperfect authentic cadence (IAC) (p. 96). A PAC cannot close an antecedent, since this strong cadence achieves complete harmonic and melodic closure (p. 102). An antecedent phrase closes in the same key in which it begins; it does not modulate (exception: reinterpreted HC) (p. 113). An antecedent phrase does not elide with a consequent phrase (p. 109).

# Prerequisites
- **Period** — The antecedent is the first phrase of a period
- **Basic idea** — The antecedent begins with b.i.
- **Contrasting idea** — Follows the b.i. in the antecedent

# Key Properties
1. 4 measures: b.i. (mm. 1-2) + contrasting idea (mm. 3-4)
2. Ends with weak cadence: HC (vast majority) or IAC (less common)
3. Never ends with PAC (complete closure would not motivate consequent)
4. Does not modulate (exception: reinterpreted HC)
5. Does not elide with consequent
6. Antecedent function is an initiating formal function
7. B.i. supported by tonic harmony; c.i. closes with cadential progression

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for b.i. in mm. 1-2 with tonic harmony
2. Check for contrasting idea in mm. 3-4 (not a repetition of b.i.)
3. Verify weak cadence at end (HC or IAC)
4. Confirm clear boundary before consequent (no elision)

# Context & Application
The antecedent's weak cadence creates the need for the consequent to provide stronger closure. The vast majority of antecedents end with HC, to magnify cadential differentiation. The antecedent creates a sense of incomplete statement that motivates repetition with stronger closure.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 96, Ex. 3.1): Mozart, K. 525, ii -- b.i. (mm. 1-2), contrasting idea with brief HC progression I-V (mm. 3-4).

**Example 2** (p. 99, Ex. 3.4): Mozart, K. 281, i -- antecedent ending with the less common IAC.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Identifying a PAC at the end of the antecedent
  **Correction**: PAC cannot close an antecedent; if apparent, it may be a reinterpreted HC

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking any first phrase followed by a second phrase is an antecedent
  **Clarification**: Antecedent specifically requires b.i. + c.i. + weak cadence within a period
