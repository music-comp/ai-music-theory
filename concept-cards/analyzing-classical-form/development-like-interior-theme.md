## Definition

Sometimes the interior theme of a large ternary may function more like a genuine development section, featuring model-sequence technique, exploration of multiple tonal regions, and characteristics of a sonata-form core.

## Characteristics

### Development-like Features
- Model-sequence organization with "dominant version" of opening idea
- Multiple tonal regions explored (e.g., bVI, iv, bII)
- Fragmentation following sequences
- Authentic cadences of "limited scope" within sequential passages
- Standing on the dominant
- Possible false recapitulation

### Interior Theme Features
- Begins with modal shift related to minore
- All tonal regions relate to the minor mode of home key
- Returns to home-key dominant for main theme's return

## Example: Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, ii

The interior theme (mm. 25-50):
1. Begins in A-flat major (bVI region)
2. Features loose model-sequence organization
3. Descending-third sequential motion (A-flat to F minor to D-flat major)
4. Arrives on home-key HC (m. 37)
5. Standing on dominant gets led astray to bVII for "false recapitulation"
6. Returns to home-key dominant arrival

## Analytical Implication

This organization resembles a sonatalike development section:
- Similar to core with pre-core lacking
- Contains standing on the dominant and retransition
- Yet the tonal regions all relate to the minor mode of the home key, maintaining the minore character typical of interior themes
