# Quick Definition
The coda may reference development-section material because the recapitulation cannot treat it; the coda's opening is an especially appropriate location due to its analogous position following the closing section, though the coda's treatment remains nonmodulatory and home-key-bound.

# Core Definition
"Whereas the recapitulation requires the composer to rework the exposition, opportunity is not necessarily found to treat material of the development section within the recapitulation proper" (p. 562). The coda's opening lends itself to this because "both follow upon similar material, namely, the closing section of the exposition and recapitulation." The early coda is also appropriate for destabilizing home-key tonic through model-sequence technique that may resemble development passages, though it remains nonmodulatory.

# Key Properties
1. Recapitulation cannot treat development material (must rework exposition)
2. Coda's opening has analogous position to development's opening
3. Both follow closing sections (of exposition and recapitulation)
4. Model-sequence technique may resemble development but stays in home key
5. Not a "terminal development" — fundamentally different tonal organization

# Examples
**Example 15.4** (p. 550): Mozart, Symphony "Linz," K. 425, i — coda opening (m. 269) derives from development's core (see Ex. 13.6); develops this material entirely in stable home-key context.
