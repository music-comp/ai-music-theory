# Quick Definition
A newly composed passage using model-sequence technique that appears in the main theme or transition of the recapitulation, treating ideas not extensively featured in the actual development section.

# Core Definition
The term, coined by Charles Rosen, describes new model-sequence passages in the recapitulation (p. 507). "This term is handy as an informal description, but it is potentially misleading: the new sequential passage does not usually resemble how sequences are organized in a real development. Unlike in a core, the model of a secondary development is generally short, and the sequential activity is rarely modulatory."

# Prerequisites
- **Recapitulation section** — The formal context
- **Model-sequence technique** — The compositional process employed

# Key Properties
1. Newly composed model-sequence passage in recapitulation
2. Typically in main theme or transition
3. Model is generally short (unlike 4-8 measure core models)
4. Sequential activity rarely modulatory (unlike development core)
5. Harmonies remain closely bound to home key
6. Often treats ideas not featured in the actual development
7. May compensate for a brief or absent core in the development

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for new sequential passages in the recapitulation that do not correspond to the exposition
2. Confirm the models are small and the harmonies stay near the home key
3. Check whether the material was absent from the development section

# Examples
**Example 14.4** (p. 508): Beethoven, Piano Sonata in A, Op. 2, No. 2, i — main theme's final phrase sequenced down a step (m. 244), then up a fifth (m. 248); brief secondary development with flat-side region.

**Example 14.5** (p. 509): Mozart, Piano Sonata in G, K. 283, i — presentation phrase sequenced up a step (mm. 76-79); particularly appropriate because the development lacked a core.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Model-sequence technique** — The compositional process used
## Contrasts With
- **Core** — The genuine development core has larger models and modulatory sequences

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Treating a secondary development as equivalent to a development core
  **Clarification**: The short, non-modulatory models of a secondary development are fundamentally different from the large-scale, modulatory model-sequence of a core
