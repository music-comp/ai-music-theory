# Quick Definition
Transitions frequently feature "passage work" (arpeggiations and scale patterns) projecting a "brilliant style" rather than the tuneful melodic material of main themes. They are often the first place where conventional accompanimental patterns (Alberti bass, drum bass, murky bass) appear, and the beginning of the transition is often when the movement seems to be "getting under way."

# Core Definition
In comparison with main themes, transitions less often contain melodic material that would be characterized as tuneful. Rather, transitions frequently feature "passage work": arpeggiations and scale patterns projecting a "brilliant style." Transitions are often the first place in the movement where a conventional accompanimental pattern, such as an Alberti bass, appears. Such patterns bring continuous rhythmic propulsion and forward drive, helping support the generally increased dynamic momentum generated by the transition. The beginning of the transition is often the moment when the movement seems to be "getting under way." While the 19th-century view (from A. B. Marx) associated "masculine" character with the main theme, it is actually more often the transition that conforms to the stereotypical "masculine" character -- dynamically aggressive and assertive (Caplin, Ch. 11, pp. 336-337).

# Key Properties
1. Less tuneful than main themes; features "passage work"
2. Arpeggiations and scale patterns project "brilliant style"
3. Often first place in movement for conventional accompanimental patterns
4. Creates continuous rhythmic propulsion and forward drive
5. Beginning of transition = moment when movement "gets under way"
6. More "masculine" in character than main themes (correcting Marx)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Note a shift from tuneful melody to passage work (scales, arpeggios)
2. Look for the onset of a continuous accompanimental pattern
3. Listen for increased dynamic energy and forward momentum
4. Observe the "brilliant style" quality of the figuration
5. Note the contrast with the main theme's more lyrical character

# Context & Application
Understanding the transition's characteristic style helps identify where the transition begins, especially when formal boundaries are ambiguous. The shift to passage work, the onset of an accompanimental pattern, and the increase in dynamic momentum are all stylistic markers that complement formal-structural analysis in locating the transition.
