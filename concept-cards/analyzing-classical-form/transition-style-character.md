## Transition Style and Character

Transitions often have distinct stylistic characteristics compared to main themes.

### Melodic Material

- Transitions less often contain melodic material characterized as **tuneful**
- Frequently feature "passage work": arpeggiations and scale patterns
- Project a **brilliant style**

### Accompanimental Patterns

Transitions are often the first place in the movement where a conventional accompanimental pattern appears:
- **Alberti bass**
- **Drum bass**
- **Murky bass**

These patterns bring continuous rhythmic propulsion and forward drive.

### Dynamic Momentum

- Beginning of transition is often when movement seems to be **"getting under way"**
- Supports generally increased dynamic momentum
- Creates forward drive toward the subordinate theme

### Association with "Masculine" Character

While main themes may exhibit hesitancy and uncertainty, it is actually more often the **transition** that conforms to the stereotypical "masculine" character (dynamically aggressive, assertive).
