# Quick Definition
A coda normally consists of at least one coda theme ending with a PAC, but lengthy codas may contain multiple coda themes, each with its own character and cadential goal, allowing for greater formal weight and multiple climactic moments.

# Core Definition
A coda normally consists of at least one thematic unit -- a coda theme -- that ends with a home-key PAC. Multiple coda themes may also be present within a lengthy coda. Unlike other terms that identify themes, "coda theme" does not imply any particular formal function except that the thematic unit occurs within the scope of the coda. It is a functionally neutral label, akin to the "pre-core" of the development section. Coda themes are usually built to resemble the loose organization of a subordinate theme, especially because of a highly expanded cadential area. Occasionally, however, a coda theme is structured as a simple, tight-knit type (sentence, period, or hybrid). The melodic-motivic content of a coda may draw on any prior material from the movement, and main-theme material is particularly common.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of the coda as an after-the-end function

# Key Properties
- At least one coda theme normally present, each ending with a PAC
- Lengthy codas may contain multiple coda themes
- "Coda theme" is a functionally neutral label (no specific formal function implied)
- Usually organized like subordinate themes (loose structure, expanded cadential area)
- Occasionally tight-knit (sentence, period, or hybrid)
- Second coda theme may feature contrasting character (e.g., modal shift to minor)
- Second coda theme may omit initiating function
- Content may draw on any prior material from the movement

# Construction / Recognition
Identify thematic units within the coda by locating PACs. Each unit ending with a PAC is a separate coda theme. Examine the organization of each theme: loose (subordinate-theme-like) or tight-knit (sentence/period). Note whether a second coda theme introduces contrasting material, a modal shift, or different organizational principles from the first.

# Context & Application
Multiple coda themes allow the composer to create a substantial after-the-end section with multiple climactic moments. The first coda theme may end with an HC or PAC, setting up the second, which often provides the more powerful closure. When main-theme material returns in the coda (a primary compensatory function), it often forms the basis of one of the coda themes, lending circular design to the movement.

# Examples
- Haydn, Symphony No. 104 ("London"), iv: first coda theme (mm. 276-87) features weak tonic prolongation in presentation, expanded cadential progression, PAC at m. 287; second coda theme (mm. 287ff.) brings modal shift to minor, omits initiating function, includes internal dominant arrival at m. 293, expanded cadential progression, powerful PAC at m. 309

# Common Errors
- Assuming every coda contains only one thematic unit
- Confusing coda themes with closing section codettas (coda themes end with PACs; codettas prolong tonic after a PAC)

# Common Confusions
- "Coda theme" is NOT analogous to "main theme" or "subordinate theme" in implying a specific formal function -- it is a functionally neutral label indicating only that the thematic unit occurs within the coda
- A coda theme is NOT a codetta -- coda themes are relatively large units containing one or more phrases and ending with a PAC, while codettas are small units that prolong tonic after a PAC
