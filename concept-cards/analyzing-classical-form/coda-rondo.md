# Quick Definition
In the five-part rondo, the coda is optional; in the sonata-rondo, it is required because it includes the final return of the main theme. The coda begins where the music of the recapitulation stops corresponding to the exposition, and the final refrain always appears somewhere within it.

# Core Definition
In the five-part rondo, the coda is optional. In sonata-rondo form, the coda is a required element because the last couplet is followed by a final restatement of the refrain. Like a regular sonata, the coda of a sonata-rondo starts at the point where the music of the recapitulation stops corresponding to the exposition. The rondo refrain always appears somewhere in the coda, often at its very beginning but sometimes only after the coda is under way (Caplin, pp. 682-683).

# Key Properties
1. Five-part rondo: optional; may end with final refrain alone
2. Sonata-rondo: required; includes final refrain
3. Coda begins where recapitulation stops corresponding to exposition
4. Final refrain always appears somewhere in the coda
5. The coda "embraces" the final refrain
6. Five-part rondo coda may be extensive with developmental qualities
