## Coda in Five-Part Rondo

### Optional
- Movement may simply end with the final return of the main theme
- A new closing section may be added

### Full-Fledged Coda
When present, may:
- Be quite extensive
- Have strong developmental qualities
- Introduce new material
- Make reference to earlier couplets
- Bring additional statements of the refrain

## Coda in Sonata-Rondo

### Required Element
- Unlike five-part rondo, the coda is **obligatory**
- The last couplet is followed by a final restatement of the refrain
- This final refrain is understood as part of the coda

### Where Does Coda Begin?

The relation of final refrain to coda is complicated:
- Most theorists see coda as **following** the final main-theme return
- But sometimes the recapitulation leads into music already functioning as coda
- The refrain returns only somewhat later

### Practical Definition
Like a regular sonata, the coda of a sonata-rondo starts at the point where **the music of the recapitulation stops corresponding to the exposition**.

## Coda vs. Final Refrain

The coda embraces the final refrain:
- Coda begins where recapitulation ends
- Final refrain always appears somewhere in the coda
- Often at its very beginning
- Sometimes only after coda is under way
