# Quick Definition
The great majority of cores end on dominant harmony (of either the home key or a development key), most often articulated by a half cadence, sometimes by a dominant arrival, and rarely by an authentic cadence.

# Core Definition
"The great majority of cores have as their harmonic goal a dominant of either the home key or a development key" (p. 453). A core thus tends to end along the lines of a transition. Most often fragmentation leads to an HC; frequently a dominant arrival (sometimes premature) results instead. On rare occasions, the core ends with an authentic cadential progression in a development key (which may be evaded or abandoned). Even more rarely, the core leads to a new section without any concluding function.

# Prerequisites
- **Core** — The formal unit being concluded
- **Half cadence** — The most common concluding articulation
- **Dominant arrival** — An alternative, less formal conclusion

# Key Properties
1. Harmonic goal is dominant (of home key or development key)
2. Core ends like a transition (on dominant harmony)
3. Most common ending: half cadence
4. Frequent alternative: dominant arrival (especially premature)
5. Rare: authentic cadential progression in development key
6. Historical trend: from PAC (Baroque) to HC (classical) to dominant arrival (late classical)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate the dominant harmony that serves as the core's structural endpoint
2. Determine whether it is articulated as HC, dominant arrival, or authentic cadence
3. The standing on the dominant following this harmony marks the core's final boundary
