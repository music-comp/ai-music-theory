# Model-Sequence Technique

**Model-sequence technique** is a form of repetition where a given unit (model) is repeated through complete transposition of its content (sequence).

## Definition

A unit of music (the **model**) is stated, then immediately repeated at a different pitch level (the **sequence**). Both melody and harmony are transposed by the same interval.

## Distinction from Sequential Progression

- **Sequential progression**: A particular way of arranging harmonies according to consistent root motion (e.g., descending fifths, ascending steps)
- **Model-sequence technique**: A form of **repetition** involving complete transposition

Model-sequence technique will always be supported by an underlying sequential progression, but harmonic sequences can occur without model-sequence technique.

## Use in Continuation Phrases

Model-sequence technique typically appears in continuation phrases, where it:
- Contributes to **harmonic destabilization**
- Supports **fragmentation** (the fragmented units are repeated sequentially)
- Expresses continuation function

## Rarity in 8-Measure Sentences

Sequential harmonies appear infrequently in simple 8-measure sentences because:
- Composers typically avoid excessive harmonic destabilization early in a movement
- There is often too little "room" between continuation onset and cadential progression
- Sequential harmonies tend to be used when continuation function is **extended**

## Analytical Notation

When labeling sequences, identify the model with a bracket, then label subsequent transposed units as "sequence" (or seq.).
