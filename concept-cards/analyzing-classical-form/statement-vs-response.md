# Quick Definition
When a tonic compound basic idea is followed by its dominant version, it may seem like an antecedent ending with HC (m. 4) followed by a consequent. The key test: examine whether there is a cadential progression or only prolongational harmony. Statement-response (presentation) is exclusively prolongational with no distinct cadential progression "ending" a prior prolongation. Antecedent-consequent (period) has distinct cadential progressions.

# Core Definition
When a tonic version of a compound basic idea is repeated with a dominant version, the impression that an antecedent (ending with HC at m. 4) is being followed by a consequent (ending with PAC at m. 8) can be strong, especially if the harmonies are in root position (p. 200). But most often in these situations, there is only a single harmonic progression underlying each 4-m. phrase. There is no distinctly cadential progression that "ends" some prior prolongational progression; instead, the entire situation is exclusively prolongational (p. 200). The analyst must observe whether the dominant at m. 4 functions as a genuine cadential goal or merely as part of a broader tonic prolongation. True statement-response creates an open-ended presentation, not a closed periodic structure.

# Key Properties
1. Ambiguity arises when c.b.i. moves I to V (m. 4) then V to I (m. 8)
2. Key test: is the harmony prolongational or cadential?
3. Statement-response: single prolongational progression per phrase; no distinct cadential ending
4. Antecedent-consequent: distinct cadential progression ending a prior prolongation
5. The V at m. 4 may LOOK like an HC but function as part of prolongation
6. True statement-response creates an open-ended presentation
7. Look for root-position harmonies (these strengthen the period impression)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Distinguish:
1. Examine the harmonic progression underlying each 4-m. phrase
2. Ask: is there a prolongational progression followed by a distinct cadential progression?
3. If YES: antecedent-consequent (period) -- two separate harmonic processes
4. If NO (single prolongational progression throughout): statement-response (presentation)
5. Listen for whether the V at m. 4 sounds like a genuine cadential goal or part of ongoing prolongation

# Context & Application
This is one of the most important analytical decisions in compound themes. The compound sentence begins with a compound presentation (statement-response of c.b.i.), while the compound period begins with a compound antecedent. Both can look similar on the surface. The distinction has major formal consequences: a compound presentation is open-ended and leads to a continuation, while a compound antecedent achieves weak closure and leads to a compound consequent. Getting this wrong leads to misidentification of the entire theme type.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 200, Ex. 6.9): Beethoven, Violin Concerto, Op. 61, i, mm. 2-9 -- tonic c.b.i. (mm. 2-5) followed by dominant version (mm. 6-9); exclusively prolongational; therefore compound presentation, not period.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Automatically hearing I-V at m. 4 as an HC closing an antecedent
  **Correction**: Check whether a distinct cadential progression is present; if the situation is purely prolongational, it is statement-response, not antecedent-consequent

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming root-position V always signals an HC
  **Clarification**: Root-position V within a tonic prolongation is not an HC; a genuine HC requires a distinct half-cadential progression at a phrase-structural boundary
