# Quick Definition
Janet Schmalfeldt's term for the technique where, following a cadential deviation, the music "backs up" to repeat prior material and tries for the cadence "one more time." Though coined specifically for evaded cadences, the backing-up behavior also occurs after IACs, deceptive cadences, and abandoned cadences.

# Core Definition
The "one more time" technique was coined by Janet Schmalfeldt specifically for evaded cadences, where the grouping disruption makes the sense of "let's try that one more time and see if we can finally reach the goal" particularly palpable and dramatic. However, returning to prior material after failing to achieve an expected PAC can also occur after an IAC, deceptive cadence, or abandoned cadence. Caplin notes that the evaded cadence produces a more marked effect than the other cadential situations because of its distinct grouping disruption.

# Key Properties
1. Music "backs up" to repeat prior continuation or cadential material
2. Originally coined for evaded cadences (Schmalfeldt)
3. Also occurs after IAC, deceptive cadence, and abandoned cadence
4. Most dramatic effect with evaded cadence due to grouping disruption
5. May occur multiple times before genuine closure

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 153): Extended continuation leads to expected PAC at m. 10; evaded; music returns to earlier material; PAC at m. 13.
**Example 2** (p. 164): Mozart, K. 309, iii -- continuation repeated after evasion.
