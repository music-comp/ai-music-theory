## "One More Time" Technique

Following an evaded cadence, the composer frequently employs the **"one more time" technique** by repeating previously heard ideas and leading them again to a potential cadence.

### How It Works

1. Cadential phrase approaches expected PAC
2. Cadence is evaded
3. Previous cadential material is **repeated**
4. Another attempt at cadence
5. May be evaded again, or finally achieve genuine closure

### Multiple Evasions

The technique can be applied multiple times:
- First attempt: evaded
- Second attempt: evaded again
- Third attempt: finally reaches PAC

### Special Case: Evasion to Root-Position Tonic

A cadential evasion can occur even if cadential dominant moves to **root-position tonic**:
- Sufficient disruption of melody, texture, dynamics, register counters perceiving an end
- "One more time" technique helps project evasion
- When we hear previous cadential material starting over, we realize a cadential arrival has not occurred
