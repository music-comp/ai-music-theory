# Quick Definition
The trio is the second part of minuet/trio form, constructed in minuet form but generally bringing simplification and relaxation rather than the intensification typical of most classical "contrasting" sections. It is usually simpler, shorter, and more symmetrical than the minuet proper.

# Core Definition
The trio must provide a distinct element of contrast while maintaining the same meter and tempo. Unlike many "contrasting" sections of classical form which typically express greater complexity and emotional intensification, the trio generally brings a quality of simplification and relaxation. Its harmonic vocabulary is more diatonic, rhythmic patterns more uniform, and texture less dense (Caplin, pp. 650-651).

# Prerequisites
- **Minuet/trio form** -- The full-movement form containing the trio
- **Minuet form** -- The form used to construct the trio

# Key Properties
1. Provides contrast through simplification, not intensification
2. More diatonic harmony, more uniform rhythms, less dense texture
3. Tends to be shorter and more symmetrically formed than minuet proper
4. Expansions kept under control; codas less common
5. May assume highly symmetrical proportions (e.g., 8+4+4)
6. Most trios in same key as minuet proper; some shift mode or tonality
7. Term derives from 17th-century three-instrument texture reduction

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate the second minuet between the minuet proper and the da capo
2. Note the simplification in texture, harmony, and rhythm
3. Check whether it resides in the same key or shifts mode/tonality
4. Determine whether it is formally complete or structurally dependent

# Context & Application
The trio represents the main exception to the principle that "contrast" in classical form means "intensity." The trio's relaxation serves as foil for the da capo's return.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 652): Haydn, Symphony No. 100 in G ("Military"), iii -- trio shifts to minor mode, truncated (A and B sections only), remains structurally subordinate to surrounding minuets.
