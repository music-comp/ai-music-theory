## Definition

The trio is the second part of minuet/trio form, providing contrast with the minuet proper while maintaining the same meter and tempo. It is constructed in minuet form (or binary minuet form).

## Origin of Name

The term "trio" derives from 17th-century practice of reducing the texture in this part of a dance movement to just three instruments.

## Contrasting Character

Unlike most "contrasting" sections in classical form (which typically express greater complexity and emotional intensification), the trio generally brings:
- **Simplification**
- **Relaxation**

## Stylistic Characteristics

| Parameter | Trio vs. Minuet Proper |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Harmonic vocabulary | More diatonic |
| Rhythmic patterns | More uniform, continuous |
| Texture | Less dense |
| Complexity | Generally simpler |

## Formal Organization

The trio follows norms of minuet form but often differs:
- Tends to be **shorter** and more **symmetrically formed**
- Expansions kept under control
- Codas occur less often
- May assume highly symmetrical proportions (e.g., 8 (A) + 4 (B) + 4 (A'))

## Techniques for Structural Dependence

To forge stronger sense of overall minuet/trio form, the trio may be made dependent on the minuet proper through:

1. **Change of mode or tonality**: Motivates restoration of original key in da capo
2. **Retransition**: Links end of trio to beginning of da capo (especially if tonality changes)
3. **Structural incompleteness**: Remains open on dominant harmony
