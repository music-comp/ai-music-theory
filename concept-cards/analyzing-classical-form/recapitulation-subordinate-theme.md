# Quick Definition
The subordinate theme in the recapitulation returns transposed into the home key, providing perfect authentic closure that resolves the movement's tonal conflict. Mozart and Beethoven bring it largely unaltered; Haydn almost always effects major structural changes.

# Core Definition
As a general rule, the subordinate theme (or theme group) returns as it originally appeared in the exposition, except transposed into the home key. Just as the subordinate theme in the exposition must confirm the subordinate key with a PAC, the recapitulation's subordinate theme must provide perfect authentic closure in the home key. This PAC is often highly dramatized through evaded cadences and expanded cadential progressions, and it is sometimes made even more emphatic than in the exposition so that the home key can emerge as fully victorious. Following the final PAC, the closing section normally returns intact; in the absence of a genuine coda, the closing section may be extended for a more decisive conclusion.

# Key Properties
1. Transposed into the home key
2. Must provide PAC in home key (resolves tonal conflict)
3. Final PAC often more emphatic than in exposition
4. Dramatized through evaded cadences and expanded cadential progressions
5. Mozart and Beethoven: subordinate-theme group largely unaltered (except transposition)
6. Haydn: almost always effects major structural changes to entire recapitulation
7. Closing section normally returns intact after final PAC

# Context & Application
The final PAC of the recapitulation's subordinate theme is the most structurally significant cadence in the entire movement, resolving the tonal conflict that has driven the form since the exposition's modulation. The home key's "victory" is complete when this PAC sounds. Ornamental changes such as register adjustments may occur even in Mozart and Beethoven (e.g., Beethoven adjusting scale passages due to keyboard range limitations).

# Examples
**Example** (pp. 303-305): Beethoven, Op. 2, No. 1, i, mm. 119-140 -- subordinate theme returns with no structural alterations except transposition to home key; ornamental changes involve different registers; descending scales start on tonic degree rather than sixth degree due to keyboard range limitations.
