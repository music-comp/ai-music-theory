## Recapitulation Subordinate Theme

The **subordinate theme in the recapitulation** returns as it originally appeared in the exposition, except that it is now transposed into the home key.

### Cadential Requirement

Just as the subordinate theme in the exposition needs to confirm the subordinate key with a PAC, the subordinate theme in the recapitulation must provide **perfect authentic closure in the home key**.

### Emphasis on Final PAC

Like the exposition, this PAC is often highly dramatized through:
- Evaded cadences
- Expanded cadential progressions

The final PAC of the recapitulation is sometimes made **even more emphatic** than that of the exposition, so that the home key can emerge as fully victorious.

### Composer Differences

**Mozart and Beethoven**: Normally bring their subordinate-theme groups largely unaltered (except for transposition)

**Haydn**: Almost as a rule, effects major structural changes to the entire recapitulation, including substantial reworking of the subordinate theme

### Closing Section

Following the final PAC of the recapitulation, the closing section normally returns intact. In the absence of a genuine coda, the closing section may be extended to impart a more decisive sense of conclusion.
