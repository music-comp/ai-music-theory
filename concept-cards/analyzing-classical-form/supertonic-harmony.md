# Quick Definition
The triad or seventh chord built on the second scale degree (II), functioning as a pre-dominant, with the first-inversion form (II6) being more frequently employed than IV in classical music.

# Core Definition
"Many harmony texts suggest that the subdominant triad (IV) is the most typical pre-dominant harmony. Examination of the classical literature reveals, however, that the supertonic triad in first inversion (II6) is more often employed for this function" (p. 32). Both II6 and IV can be enriched through sevenths and modal borrowing. II6 places scale degree 4 in the bass (like IV), creating strong half-step motion toward the dominant.

# Prerequisites
- **Pre-dominant function** — II6 is the most common pre-dominant
- **Scale degree** — Built on scale degree 2

# Key Properties
1. II6 (first inversion) is the most common cadential pre-dominant in classical music
2. More frequently employed than IV
3. Shares scale degree 4 with subdominant (IV has 4 as root; II6 has 4 in bass)
4. Standard cadential progression: I6-II6-V7-I
5. Can be enriched with seventh (II7) or through mixture (II°, II°7)
6. Diminished in minor mode (II° or II°7)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate a triad or seventh chord built on scale degree 2
2. Note the inversion (II6 = first inversion, with scale degree 4 in bass)
3. Verify pre-dominant function within a cadential progression
4. In minor, note the diminished quality

# Context & Application
II6 is the most frequently used pre-dominant in classical music, appearing in the standard cadential progression I6-II6-V7-I. The recognition that II6 is more common than IV corrects a widespread textbook misemphasis. Both II and IV share scale degree 4 (as bass note in II6, as root in IV), and both serve the same harmonic function.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming IV is the "primary" pre-dominant
  **Correction**: II6 is actually more commonly used in classical music

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Not recognizing that II6 places scale degree 4 in the bass (like IV)
  **Clarification**: Both II6 and IV have scale degree 4 in the bass; the difference is in the root and upper-voice structure
