## Definition

In some slow-movement sonata expositions, the form becomes compressed when the final cadence of the main theme is immediately followed by the subordinate theme, with no transition in between.

## Result of Transition Elimination

When the transition is eliminated:
- The subordinate theme begins directly in the subordinate key
- The emphasis on dominant of the new key is **shifted into the subordinate theme proper**
- Internal HC and standing on dominant appear within the subordinate theme

## Recapitulation Implications

When both main theme and subordinate theme share similar material (Haydn's "monothematicism"):
- The recapitulation must be significantly rewritten
- Avoids redundant, twofold succession of main-theme ideas in the home key

## Example: Haydn, Piano Sonata in D, H. 24, ii

### Exposition
- Main theme closes with HC (m. 6)
- Subordinate theme begins directly in G major with main-theme material
- No transition function present

### Recapitulation
- Main theme begins at m. 33, closes with HC (m. 39)
- Long standing on dominant follows (suggesting main-theme/transition fusion)
- Expectation for subordinate theme is thwarted
- Standing on dominant becomes the actual end of the movement

## Extreme Result: Truncated Recapitulation

In this example, Haydn writes a truncated recapitulation:
- Only main theme returns
- Entire subordinate theme is eliminated
- Standing on dominant ends the movement
- Creates an "astonishing" omission of the subordinate theme
