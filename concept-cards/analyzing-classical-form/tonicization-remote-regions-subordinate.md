# Quick Definition
When a subordinate theme shifts to minor mode, it brings into play tonal regions (bIII, bVI, bVII, bII) that would otherwise be considered remote in a strictly major-mode context. The prominent tonicization of these regions is another loosening device typical of subordinate themes but not of main themes.

# Core Definition
A shift to minor mode in the subordinate theme brings into play tonal regions -- such as bIII, bVI, bVII, and bII -- that would otherwise be considered remote in a strictly major-mode context. The prominent tonicization of these regions is another loosening device typical of subordinate themes (but not of main themes). In extreme cases, ascending-third sequential progressions following a modal shift can reach regions as remote as a tritone from the subordinate key (effectively bIII/bIII), the most distant possible tonal relationship (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 419-420).
