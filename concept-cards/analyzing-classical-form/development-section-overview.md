# Quick Definition
The development section brings about a high degree of tonal and phrase-structural instability along with an intensity of emotional expression, motivating a restoration of stability accomplished by the recapitulation.

# Core Definition
The development largely manipulates and recombines melodic-motivic material from the exposition, but may introduce some new ideas as well. It intensifies the tonal conflict by exploring more remote minor-mode tonal regions and by way of sequential harmonic progressions. In relatively long development sections, one or more development keys will be confirmed, usually by an HC, sometimes by a PAC. Toward the end, the home key returns, confirmed by an HC (or dominant arrival), which is further emphasized by a standing on the dominant marking the literal end of the development section. Development sections are considerably less conventional in their phrase-structural organization than expositions; unlike an exposition, we cannot predict in advance which component parts a given development may contain.

# Key Properties
1. High degree of tonal and phrase-structural instability
2. Manipulates and recombines exposition material
3. May introduce new ideas
4. Explores remote minor-mode tonal regions
5. Sequential harmonic progressions
6. Ends with home-key HC (or dominant arrival) and standing on the dominant
7. Less conventional phrase-structural organization than exposition

# Construction / Recognition
## Pre-Core/Core Technique:
One frequently appearing organizational mode is the pre-core/core technique:
1. **Core**: Establishes a relatively large model (4-8 mm.), sequenced one or more times; subsequent fragmentation leads to HC (or dominant arrival); followed by standing on the dominant
2. **Pre-core**: Passage of lesser emotional intensity preceding the core; difficult to generalize its organization
3. The core projects instability, restlessness, and dramatic conflict
4. The grouping structure of a core often resembles an extended sentence but lacks genuine presentation function (no tonic prolongation)

# Context & Application
The development is the most dramatically intense section of sonata form. Its instability motivates the restoration of stability in the recapitulation. The pre-core/core technique provides one common organizational framework, but many developments defy simple categorization. The standing on the dominant at the end serves as a crucial preparation for the recapitulation's return.

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 296-298): Beethoven, Op. 2, No. 1, i -- pre-core (mm. 49-54) brings basic idea in subordinate key, extended; core (mm. 55-81) establishes 8-m. model based on subordinate-theme material, sequenced, fragmented; tonicizes iv (B-flat minor) and v (C minor).
**Example 2** (pp. 298-299): Beethoven, Op. 2, No. 1, i -- extensive standing on the dominant (mm. 81-100) in two parts, the second reintroducing main theme's sixteenth-note triplet figure.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating the core's sentential grouping structure as a genuine sentence
  **Correction**: The core lacks genuine presentation function because the model and sequence do not prolong tonic harmony of a key; "sentence" should be reserved for tight-knit themes with clear presentation and continuation
