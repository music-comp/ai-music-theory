## Fusion of Transition and Subordinate Theme

In some cases, we cannot identify either a clear concluding function for the transition or an initiating function for the subordinate theme. Yet essential elements of both functions are present. This creates a **fusion** of transition and subordinate-theme functions.

### What Remains Present

Despite the fusion:
- **Transition function**: Modulation to the new key
- **Subordinate-theme function**: Perfect-authentic closure in that key
- Cadential extensions and expansions typical of subordinate themes

### Single Thematic Unit

The concept of form-functional **fusion** means we encounter what appears to be a single thematic unit embracing both functions. It is not possible to find an appropriate initiating moment for the subordinate theme, even in retrospect.

### Analytical Notation

When encountering cases of form-functional fusion:
- There is no clear spot to position the second functional label
- Annotation method: Label beginning with both functions: **transition/subordinate theme**
- The slash (/) indicates fusion of two functions within single unit

### No Boundary Markers

The most extreme case:
- No clear concluding function for transition
- No initiating function for subordinate theme
- A single unit begins clearly as transition, ends as subordinate theme
- Where one ends and other begins is not definable
