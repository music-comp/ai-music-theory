# Quick Definition
A deceptive cadence arises when the final root-position tonic of an implied authentic cadence is replaced by another harmony (usually vi) or by tonic in first inversion, with the replacement event grouping backwards with the preceding cadential progression as the phrase's goal.

# Core Definition
Caplin distinguishes the genuine deceptive cadence from a mere deceptive resolution. A deceptive cadence should be recognized only when there is a truly cadential situation in the making -- when thematic closure is imminent. The event supported by the replacement harmony groups with the preceding music (groups backwards) and functions as the goal of the ongoing phrase. This grouping behavior distinguishes it from the evaded cadence. Following a deceptive cadence, the music typically extends the ongoing function by repeating material (often the contrasting idea) to achieve a genuine PAC.

# Prerequisites
- **Cadential deviations** — The broader category
- **Cadential progression** — Must be present for a genuine deceptive cadence

# Key Properties
1. Final tonic replaced by vi (most common), I6, or other harmony
2. Replacement event groups BACKWARDS with preceding cadential progression
3. The replacement is the GOAL of the phrase (ending event)
4. Must occur in a genuinely cadential context (not any V-vi)
5. Typically extends the function by motivating repetition of prior material

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Confirm a genuine cadential progression is in progress
2. Check if the expected tonic is replaced by another harmony
3. Verify the replacement groups backwards (it is heard as the phrase's ending, not a new beginning)
4. Could you change the vi to I and recognize a genuine cadence at that moment?

# Context & Application
Caplin urges caution: not every V-vi progression is a deceptive cadence. Many V-vi motions are entirely noncadential. Reserve the term "deceptive cadence" only for cases where you could change vi to I and recognize a genuine cadence. Speak freely of deceptive "resolutions"; speak with caution of deceptive "cadences."

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 152): Mozart, K. 310, iii, mm. 1-20 -- period promises PAC at m. 16; dominant resolves to VI instead; deceptive cadence extends consequent with additional contrasting idea statement, reaching PAC at m. 20.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Calling every V-vi progression a "deceptive cadence"
  **Correction**: Only use the term when a genuine cadential situation is imminent; otherwise say "deceptive resolution"

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Not distinguishing deceptive cadence from evaded cadence
  **Clarification**: The key is grouping: deceptive = replacement groups backwards as phrase goal; evaded = what follows groups forward as a new beginning
