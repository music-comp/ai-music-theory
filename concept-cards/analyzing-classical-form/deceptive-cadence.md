# Deceptive Cadence

A **deceptive cadence** occurs when the cadential dominant resolves to an unexpected harmony, most typically vi (submediant), rather than the expected tonic.

## Harmonic Content

- Expected: V → I
- Actual: V → vi (most common)
- Also possible: V → IV6, V → other non-tonic harmonies

## Effect

The deceptive cadence:
- Denies expected authentic cadential closure
- Creates surprise (the "deception")
- Motivates continuation to achieve real closure
- Often leads to phrase extension

## Typical Consequences

Following a deceptive cadence:
- Continuation function resumes
- A second attempt at cadential closure is made
- The phrase becomes extended
- Genuine PAC eventually achieved

## Not a True Cadence

Despite its name, a deceptive cadence does NOT produce genuine cadential closure:
- The formal ending is denied
- The theme/phrase continues
- True closure must still be achieved

## Common Contexts

Deceptive cadences frequently occur:
- At the end of continuation phrases
- Where composers want to delay closure
- To create harmonic interest and surprise
- Before final cadential confirmation
