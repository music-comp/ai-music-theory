# Quick Definition
Three basic cadence types exist in classical music: PAC (harmonically and melodically complete), IAC (harmonically complete, melodically incomplete), and HC (harmonically and melodically incomplete). There is no genuine plagal cadence in classical style. All cadences of the same type have equivalent syntactical strength regardless of rhetorical factors.

# Core Definition
There are only three basic cadences in tonal music: two kinds of authentic cadence and one half cadence (p. 78). A perfect authentic cadence (PAC) ends with the tonic scale degree (1) in the soprano over root-position V-I; it is the strongest. An imperfect authentic cadence (IAC) ends with the third degree in the soprano; it is of moderate strength. A half cadence (HC) ends on the dominant; it is the weakest. The motion IV-I does NOT create a genuine cadence in classical style; such passages are postcadential codettas (p. 78). For formal analysis, all cadences of the same type have the same syntactical strength, regardless of metrical position, dynamic level, texture, or rhetorical emphasis (p. 102).

# Key Properties
1. Only three cadence types: PAC, IAC, HC
2. PAC: root-position V to root-position I, soprano on 1 -- strongest
3. IAC: V to I, soprano on 3 (not 1) -- moderate strength
4. HC: ends on V (root position) -- weakest
5. No genuine plagal cadence (IV-I) in classical style
6. Syntactical strength is determined by type alone, not rhetorical factors
7. Melodic content of cadences is normally conventional (descending contour)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Check the final harmony: V = HC; V-I = authentic cadence
2. For authentic cadences, check soprano: ends on 1 = PAC; ends on 3 = IAC
3. Verify root position for both V and I in authentic cadences
4. Do not label IV-I as a cadence; it is postcadential
5. Place cadence label where the final harmony first appears

# Context & Application
Correct cadence identification is one of the most important tasks in formal analysis. Cadences define the principal goals of thematic organization (p. 77). The distinction between PAC and IAC has formal consequences: PAC achieves complete closure (appropriate for consequent endings), while IAC leaves melodic incompleteness (can function as weak cadence in antecedent). The syntactical vs. rhetorical distinction means that a soft, understated PAC has the same formal weight as a loud, emphatic one.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Labeling IV-I motion as a "plagal cadence"
  **Correction**: In classical style, IV-I is not a genuine cadence; such passages are postcadential codettas

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking a loud, emphatic cadence is "stronger" than a soft one of the same type
  **Clarification**: Syntactical strength is determined solely by cadence type (PAC > IAC > HC); dynamic level, texture, and metrical position affect only rhetorical strength
