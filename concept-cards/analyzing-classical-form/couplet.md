# Quick Definition
A couplet is a large-scale section in rondo form situated between and contrasting with statements of the refrain. Couplets may be built as subordinate-theme complexes, interior themes, or development sections, depending on their position and the type of rondo.

# Core Definition
In rondo form, a couplet is a contrasting section situated between statements of the refrain. Also termed "episodes" or "digressions" in other traditions. Couplets may be organized as subordinate-theme complexes (with transition, subordinate theme, closing section, retransition), as interior themes (in minore, vi, or IV), or as development sections (with pre-core and core technique). The type depends on the rondo form and the couplet's position within it (Caplin, pp. 665-666).

# Key Properties
1. Contrasting section between refrains
2. Three main organizational types: subordinate-theme complex, interior theme, development
3. Provides tonal contrast (subordinate key, minore, vi, IV)
4. In five-part rondo: couplet 1 = subordinate-theme complex or interior theme; couplet 2 = interior theme
5. In sonata-rondo: couplet 1 & 3 = subordinate-theme complex; couplet 2 = development or interior theme

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate sections between refrain statements
2. Determine the organizational type (subordinate-theme complex, interior theme, or development)
3. Note the tonal region and its contrast with the home key

# Context & Application
The couplet concept is central to rondo analysis. The grouping of transition through retransition as a "subordinate-theme complex" is unique to rondo analysis and distinguishes it from sonata-form analysis.
