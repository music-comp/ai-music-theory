## Definition

A couplet is a large-scale section in rondo form situated between, and contrasting with, statements of the refrain. Couplets are also called "episodes" or "digressions" in other theoretical traditions.

## Types of Couplets

### 1. Subordinate-Theme Complex
Contains:
- Modulating transition
- Subordinate theme (group)
- Closing section
- Retransition

Found in:
- First couplet of five-part rondo (especially Mozart)
- First and third couplets of sonata-rondo

### 2. Interior Theme
- Set in minore or submediant (vi) or subdominant (IV)
- Similar to interior theme in large ternary
- May be complete or incomplete

Found in:
- First couplet of five-part rondo (especially Haydn)
- Second couplet of both five-part rondo and sonata-rondo

### 3. Development
- Pre-core and core technique
- Model-sequence activity
- Exploration of tonal regions

Found in:
- Second couplet of sonata-rondo (alternative to interior theme)

## Tonal Contrast

Unlike the refrain (always in home key), couplets provide tonal contrast:
- Subordinate key (V for major, III for minor)
- Minore/maggiore
- Subdominant (IV)
- Submediant (vi)
