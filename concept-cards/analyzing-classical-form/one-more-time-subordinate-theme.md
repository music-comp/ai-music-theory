# Quick Definition
In subordinate themes, the "one more time" technique is a primary means of dramatizing the struggle to achieve the confirming PAC. After an evaded cadence, previously heard cadential material is repeated for another attempt at closure. Multiple evasions and OMT repetitions may precede the eventual PAC, building rhetorical tension appropriate to the subordinate theme's function of confirming the subordinate key.

# Core Definition
The OMT technique is prominently applied in subordinate themes as the principal means of **extending cadential function** through evaded cadences. Following an evasion, previous cadential material returns for another attempt. This may be repeated multiple times before the PAC is finally achieved. The technique is particularly well suited to subordinate themes because dramatizing the subordinate key's cadential confirmation is a principal aesthetic objective. The contrast between the OMT's repetitive cadential attempts and the different behavior after abandoned cadences (which lead to new continuation rather than repetition) is analytically important (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 389-394).

# Key Properties
1. Repeats earlier cadential material after evaded cadence
2. May occur multiple times before final PAC
3. Especially dramatic in subordinate themes (key confirmation at stake)
4. NOT associated with abandoned cadences (which lead to new continuation)
5. A primary loosening device for extending subordinate themes

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 391): Haydn, Op. 64/2, i -- Cadential idea evaded at m. 60; material from m. 58 returns "one more time"; PAC at m. 62.
**Example 2** (p. 393): Beethoven, Op. 2/2, i -- Root-position tonic at m. 88 sounds like evasion; material returns from m. 84; PAC at m. 92.
