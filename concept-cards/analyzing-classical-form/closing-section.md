# Quick Definition
A closing section is a postcadential framing function following a PAC, consisting of a series of codettas that prolong the tonic harmony and melodic scale degree achieved by the cadence, typically exhibiting a recessive dynamic and fragmentation of grouping structure.

# Core Definition
Any theme that closes with a PAC may be followed by a closing section. This unit prolongs both the tonic harmony and the tonic melodic scale degree achieved by the cadence. It comprises a series of codettas, which may begin in the measure following the cadence or elide with the moment of cadential arrival. If the closing section consists of different codettas, fragmentation of the grouping structure is usually observed (each new codetta shorter than the preceding one). Not all closing sections display a recessive dynamic -- sometimes energy is sustained or further increased.

# Key Properties
1. Follows a PAC (never an HC)
2. Comprises a series of codettas
3. Prolongs tonic harmony and tonic scale degree
4. Typically features fragmentation (progressively shorter codettas)
5. Usually recessive dynamic, but not always
6. May begin in the next measure or elide with the cadence
7. Boundary process: melodic overlap often occurs between codettas

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate a PAC
2. Check for postcadential material that prolongs tonic
3. Identify individual codettas
4. Look for fragmentation pattern
5. Determine whether the dynamic is recessive or sustained

# Context & Application
Closing sections appear in a wide variety of formal contexts, not just at the end of simple main themes. They are also found after A sections of small ternaries, after subordinate themes, and at the end of larger formal units.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 156): Beethoven, Op. 22, ii -- closing section from m. 9 with 1-m. codetta repeated, then fragmented to one-beat codettas.
**Example 2** (p. 168): Mozart, K. 330, iii -- closing section elides with PAC at m. 16; triplet sixteenth notes sustain or increase energy (no recessive dynamic).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Calling the final measures of a piece "cadences"
  **Correction**: The final measures are almost always codettas following an earlier PAC, not additional cadences

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking closing sections always have recessive dynamic
  **Clarification**: Frequently enough, energy is sustained or further increased after the cadence
