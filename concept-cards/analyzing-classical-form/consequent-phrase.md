# Quick Definition
The closing 4-m. phrase of a period: return of the b.i. (2 m.) + contrasting idea (2 m.) leading to a strong cadence (almost always PAC). The consequent "repeats" the antecedent with stronger cadential closure. Its cadential progression often begins earlier than the antecedent's.

# Core Definition
The consequent phrase repeats the antecedent by bringing a return of the original basic idea. A contrasting idea, which may or may not resemble the one in the antecedent, leads to a stronger cadence, usually a PAC, or more rarely an IAC (if the antecedent ended with HC) (p. 96). The cadential progression of the consequent often begins earlier within the phrase than that of the antecedent, since the penultimate dominant must be shifted backwards to accommodate the authentic cadential progression (p. 107).

# Prerequisites
- **Period** — The consequent is the second phrase of a period
- **Antecedent phrase** — The consequent repeats the antecedent
- **Return of basic idea** — The consequent begins with the return of b.i.

# Key Properties
1. 4 measures: return of b.i. (mm. 5-6) + contrasting idea (mm. 7-8)
2. Ends with strong cadence: PAC (almost always) or IAC (rare, if ant. had HC)
3. B.i. return can be exact, dominant version, sequential, or varied
4. C.i. may resemble antecedent's c.i. or bring entirely new material
5. Cadential progression often begins earlier than antecedent's (shifted backwards)
6. Greater temporal weight for cadential progression creates more powerful closure
7. When c.i. is entirely supported by cadential progression, it resembles a "cadential idea"

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for return of b.i. at mm. 5-6
2. Check for contrasting idea leading to strong cadence at m. 8
3. Verify cadential differentiation: consequent cadence stronger than antecedent's
4. Note if cadential progression begins earlier than in antecedent

# Context & Application
The consequent acquires a more powerful cadential expression relative to the antecedent, due not only to the type of cadence but also to the greater temporal weight accorded the cadential progression. The consequent is free to modulate (unlike the antecedent). In a modulating period, the two cadences reside in different keys, but the syntactical strength still follows: HC (home key) < PAC (new key).

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 96, Ex. 3.1): Mozart, K. 525, ii -- return of b.i. (mm. 5-6), contrasting idea with PAC (mm. 7-8).

**Example 2** (p. 107, Ex. 3.15): Mozart, K. 459, i -- cadential progression of consequent begins earlier (m. 7) vs. antecedent (mid-m. 3).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Expecting the c.i. of the consequent to match the antecedent exactly
  **Correction**: The c.i. may be entirely new material, as long as it leads to stronger cadence

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the b.i. must return identically
  **Clarification**: The return can be exact, dominant version, sequential, or elaborately varied
