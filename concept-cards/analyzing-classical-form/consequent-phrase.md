# Consequent Phrase

The **consequent phrase** is the closing 4-measure unit of a period. It repeats the antecedent but closes with a stronger cadence.

## Structure

- 2-measure **return of the basic idea**
- 2-measure **contrasting idea** leading to a strong cadence

## Cadential Requirements

The consequent must close with a cadence **stronger** than the antecedent:
- Almost always a **perfect authentic cadence** (PAC)
- Rarely an **imperfect authentic cadence** (IAC) - only if antecedent ended with HC

## Return of the Basic Idea

The consequent begins by bringing back the original basic idea, signaling that the antecedent is being "repeated." This return can take various forms:
- **Exact return** - same harmonic support
- **Dominant version** - basic idea supported by V instead of I
- **Sequential return** - transposed to another scale degree (e.g., up a step)
- **Varied return** - embellished or ornamented

## Contrasting Idea

The contrasting idea of the consequent:
- May resemble that of the antecedent
- May bring entirely new melodic-motivic material
- Must lead to a stronger cadence than the antecedent

## Cadential Emphasis

The cadential progression of the consequent often:
- Begins earlier in the phrase than in the antecedent
- Is longer and more emphatic
- Creates greater temporal weight for closure
