# Fanfare Motive

A majestic, heraldic gesture commonly found in slow introductions.

## Characteristics

- Creates air of dignified solemnity
- Typically sounded by full orchestra (tutti)
- Often features dotted rhythms
- Associated with ceremonial or military character
- Usually forte dynamic

## Typical Features

Fanfare-like basic ideas may include:
- Wide leaps (often octaves, fifths, or triadic intervals)
- Repeated notes
- Dotted rhythmic patterns
- Triadic melodic contour

## Creating Uncertainty

Despite majestic quality, uncertainty may be projected through:
- Fermatas (prohibiting rhythmic continuity)
- Lack of harmonic third (doubt about mode)
- Subsequent hesitant, contrasting ideas
- Discontinuities between phrases

## Role in Introduction

Often opens the slow introduction, establishing solemn character before more uncertain material follows. May return at climactic points within the introduction.
