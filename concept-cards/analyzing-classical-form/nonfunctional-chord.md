# Quick Definition
A chord that arises primarily from contrapuntal or sequential processes rather than from harmonic function, notated with chord quality symbols rather than Roman numerals.

# Formal Definition
In some cases, a chord lacks genuine harmonic function and arises primarily from contrapuntal processes. Such chords receive special notation: they are placed in parentheses and may be labeled with chord quality symbols rather than Roman numerals (e.g., "m7" for minor seventh chord, "°7" for diminished seventh chord).

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Identifying nonfunctional chords:
- Arise from voice-leading or melodic patterns
- Lack clear functional relationship to surrounding harmonies
- May violate standard voice-leading (e.g., incorrect seventh resolution)
- Appear within sequences or passing motion

Analytical notation:
- Placed in parentheses
- Chord quality rather than Roman numeral: (m7), (°7)
- May include labels: "p" for passing, "n" for neighboring

When a chord lacks functional status, using a Roman numeral would misrepresent its role.

# Musical Context/Application
Nonfunctional chords appear:
- In sequential progressions (harmonies linked by pattern, not function)
- As passing chords arising from counterpoint
- As contrapuntal embellishments without harmonic weight

The chapter notes that some passing chords arise "primarily from contrapuntal processes and only minimally from harmonic ones."

# Examples
Example 1.9c (p. 34-35): "The passing chord in Example 1.9c arises entirely out of the counterpoint and thus should not be analyzed as a II7 harmony: the so-called seventh (C) is doubled and incorrectly resolved, thus violating the fundamental voice leading for chordal sevenths. For these reasons, the symbol m7 (minor-seventh chord) is used in place of a Roman numeral."

The Guide to Harmonic Annotation (p. 47): "In some cases, a nonfunctional chord lacks a Roman numeral; in its place, the chord is labeled with its chord quality placed in parentheses, e.g., (°7) for 'diminished seventh chord' or (m7) for 'minor seventh chord.'"

# Common Confusions
- Analyzing every chord with Roman numerals (some chords lack function)
- Ignoring voice-leading evidence of contrapuntal origin
- Treating sequential harmonies as fully functional
- Not recognizing when chord quality notation is more appropriate
