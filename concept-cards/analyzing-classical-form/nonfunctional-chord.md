# Quick Definition
A chord arising primarily from contrapuntal or sequential processes rather than from harmonic function, notated with chord quality symbols in parentheses rather than Roman numerals.

# Core Definition
Some chords lack genuine harmonic function and arise primarily from contrapuntal processes. "In some cases, a nonfunctional chord lacks a Roman numeral; in its place, the chord is labeled with its chord quality placed in parentheses, e.g., (°7) for 'diminished seventh chord' or (m7) for 'minor seventh chord'" (p. 47). Using a Roman numeral for such a chord would misrepresent its role in the music.

# Prerequisites
- **Contrapuntal processes** — Nonfunctional chords arise from voice-leading motion
- **Roman numeral analysis** — Must understand Roman numerals to know when they are inappropriate

# Key Properties
1. Arises from voice-leading or melodic patterns, not harmonic function
2. Lacks clear functional relationship to surrounding harmonies
3. May violate standard voice-leading rules (doubled sevenths, incorrect resolution)
4. Appears within sequences or as passing/neighboring chords
5. Notated with quality symbols in parentheses: (m7), (°7)
6. May include labels: "p" for passing, "n" for neighboring

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Examine whether the chord arises from stepwise voice motion
2. Check for voice-leading irregularities (doubled dissonances, unresolved sevenths)
3. Assess whether the chord has genuine functional relationship to surrounding harmonies
4. If contrapuntal in origin and lacking function, use quality symbols rather than Roman numerals

# Context & Application
Nonfunctional chords appear in sequential progressions (harmonies linked by pattern, not function), as passing chords arising from counterpoint, and as contrapuntal embellishments without harmonic weight. The passing chord in Example 1.9c "arises entirely out of the counterpoint and thus should not be analyzed as a II7 harmony" because its seventh is "doubled and incorrectly resolved."

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 34-35): "The passing chord in Example 1.9c arises entirely out of the counterpoint and thus should not be analyzed as a II7 harmony: the so-called seventh (C) is doubled and incorrectly resolved ... the symbol m7 (minor-seventh chord) is used in place of a Roman numeral."

**Example 2** (p. 47): "In some cases, a nonfunctional chord lacks a Roman numeral; in its place, the chord is labeled with its chord quality placed in parentheses."

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Analyzing every chord with Roman numerals regardless of function
  **Correction**: Some chords lack function and should receive quality symbols (m7, °7) instead

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Ignoring voice-leading evidence of contrapuntal origin
  **Clarification**: Doubled and incorrectly resolved dissonances are strong evidence that a chord is contrapuntal, not harmonic
