# Parallel Period

A **parallel period** is a period in which the consequent phrase begins with material closely resembling the antecedent's opening.

## Definition

In a parallel period:
- The consequent begins with the **same basic idea** as the antecedent
- The return is clear and recognizable
- This is the most common type of period

## Degree of Parallelism

The "parallel" relationship can range from:
- **Exact return**: Identical basic idea
- **Varied return**: Embellished but recognizable
- **Harmonically altered return**: Same melody, different harmony

## Opposite: Contrasting Period

A **contrasting period** would begin the consequent with different melodic material. However, true contrasting periods are rare because:
- The return of the basic idea is essential to consequent function
- Without return, the sense of "repeating" the antecedent is lost

## Harmonic Variations

Even in a parallel period, the return may use:
- **Dominant version** (statement-response across period)
- **Sequential return** (transposed to another degree)
- Same melody with altered harmonization

## Key Point

The term "parallel" refers to the **melodic-motivic** relationship between antecedent and consequent openings, not to the cadential relationship.
