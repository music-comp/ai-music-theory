# Quick Definition
The subordinate theme's cadential area may be more extensively expanded in the recapitulation than in the exposition, ensuring that the most rhetorically powerful cadential emphasis in the entire form confirms the home key rather than the subordinate key.

# Core Definition
The cadential area of the subordinate theme may undergo greater expansion in the recapitulation than in the exposition so that the most powerful cadence in the entire form confirms the home key. In the exposition, cadential expansion serves to establish the subordinate key with sufficient weight. In the recapitulation, even greater expansion can be given to ensure the home key receives the most powerful cadential treatment. This creates a climactic sense of arrival at the movement's fundamental tonal goal -- the essential sonata closure.

# Key Properties
- Cadential area more extensively expanded than in the exposition
- Ensures home key receives most powerful cadential emphasis
- May involve longer expanded cadential progressions
- May include additional deceptive or evaded cadences
- May feature more elaborate pre-cadential preparation
- May contain interpolated passages before final PAC
- Creates climactic arrival at essential sonata closure

# Construction / Recognition
Compare the cadential area of the subordinate theme in the recapitulation with the corresponding passage in the exposition. Look for: (1) longer cadential progressions, (2) additional cadential deviations (deceptive or evaded cadences) that delay the final PAC, (3) more elaborate harmonic embellishment of pre-dominant and dominant, and (4) interpolated passages that increase the weight of the cadential process.

# Context & Application
The expanded cadential area is one of the most significant structural changes possible in the recapitulation's subordinate theme. It transforms the purely tonal matter of transposing the subordinate theme into the home key into a dramatic rhetorical event, where the home key achieves a level of cadential emphasis that surpasses anything in the exposition. This is especially important in movements where the exposition's subordinate key received substantial cadential weight.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C, K. 545, i: cadential phrase in recapitulation (mm. 67-71) adds ornamental changes creating greater intensity, with pre-dominant prolongation and added sixteenth-note motion
- General pattern: recapitulation adds deceptive cadences, expanded cadential progressions, or additional pre-cadential embellishment not present in the exposition

# Common Errors
- Assuming the cadential area must always be expanded in the recapitulation (it is a tendency, not a rule)
- Overlooking subtle expansions that add only a few beats of cadential preparation

# Common Confusions
- Expanded cadential area is NOT the same as ornamental intensification -- expansion involves structural additions to the cadential process (extra deceptive cadences, longer progressions), while intensification involves surface-level embellishment of an existing cadential structure
