# Quick Definition
The basic elements of sonata form were first described by theorists in the 1790s (Koch, Galeazzi, Kollmann), fully developed by Reicha and Marx in the 19th century, and refined in the 20th century by Schoenberg, Ratz, Tovey, and Rosen. Caplin's approach is most influenced by Schoenberg and Ratz.

# Core Definition
Sonata form theory has evolved through several major phases: initial descriptions in the late 18th century (Koch, Galeazzi, Kollmann), the first fully developed account by Antoine Reicha, and the comprehensive theories of A. B. Marx. In the 20th century, Arnold Schoenberg and Erwin Ratz (highly influential on Caplin's textbook) developed formal-functional approaches, while D. F. Tovey and Charles Rosen dominated Anglo-American thinking. The recent treatise by James Hepokoski and Warren Darcy offers a theory that differs from Caplin's in several respects, particularly regarding the medial caesura and the distinction between two-part and continuous expositions (Caplin, Ch. 9, p. 284).

# Key Properties
1. Late 18th-century origins (Koch, Galeazzi, Kollmann)
2. 19th-century systematization (Reicha, Marx)
3. 20th-century functional approaches (Schoenberg, Ratz -- direct influence on Caplin)
4. Anglo-American tradition (Tovey, Rosen)
5. Contemporary alternative (Hepokoski and Darcy)

# Context & Application
Understanding the theoretical lineage helps explain why different analysts may arrive at different conclusions about the same passage. Caplin's approach, rooted in Schoenberg and Ratz, emphasizes identifying constituent formal functions at all levels of structure throughout the entire movement.
