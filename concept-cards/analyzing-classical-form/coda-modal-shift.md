# Quick Definition
A second coda theme may bring a modal shift to minor, functioning as a loosening device that creates dramatic contrast and expressive intensification before the movement's concluding PAC in the home major key.

# Core Definition
In a coda containing multiple themes, a second coda theme may bring a modal shift to minor, creating dramatic contrast with the preceding material and the predominantly major-mode character of the movement. The modal shift functions as a loosening device typical of subordinate-theme-like organization, contributing to the loose character of many coda themes. Despite the shift to minor, the coda ultimately concludes with a return to major mode, a powerful PAC in the home key, and a closing section confirming tonic. The second coda theme featuring a modal shift may also omit an initiating function, beginning directly with model-sequence technique.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of the coda and coda themes
- Knowledge of multiple coda themes

# Key Properties
- Typically appears in a second coda theme following a first coda theme's PAC
- Shifts to minor mode, creating dramatic contrast
- Functions as a loosening device (subordinate-theme-like organization)
- May omit initiating function, beginning directly with model-sequence technique
- Ultimately resolves back to major mode for concluding PAC
- Often leads to internal dominant arrival and expanded cadential progression

# Construction / Recognition
Identify a point in the coda where, after a PAC ending the first coda theme, the mode shifts to minor. This second coda theme typically omits an opening presentation or basic idea, entering directly with sequential or continuational material in the minor mode, then building toward a dominant arrival and powerful concluding cadence in the home major key.

# Context & Application
The modal shift to minor within a coda serves the compensatory function of creating one final moment of tension and uncertainty before the movement's ultimate resolution. It allows the composer to shape a dramatic arc within the coda itself, contrasting the stability of the first coda theme's conclusion with the instability of minor-mode material before achieving the most powerful closure of the entire movement.

# Examples
- Haydn, Symphony No. 104 ("London"), iv: first coda theme ends with PAC at m. 287, which elides with second coda theme bringing modal shift to minor, omitting initiating function, beginning directly with model-sequence technique, leading to internal dominant arrival at m. 293, expanded cadential progression (mm. 301ff.), and powerful PAC at m. 309

# Common Errors
- Assuming the modal shift indicates a real modulation away from the home key
- Failing to recognize the modal shift as a loosening device analogous to subordinate-theme practice

# Common Confusions
- A modal shift in the coda is NOT a genuine change of key -- it is a temporary inflection that serves as a loosening device within the home-key framework, always resolving back to the home major for the final cadence
