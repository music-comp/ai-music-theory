# Quick Definition
The critical distinction between deceptive and evaded cadences lies in grouping structure, not just harmony. In a deceptive cadence, the event after V groups BACKWARD with the preceding cadential progression as the phrase's goal. In an evaded cadence, the event after V groups FORWARD with the next phrase as a new beginning, creating a salient grouping disruption.

# Core Definition
Traditional views focus on harmonic changes in cadential deviations. But Caplin argues that grouping structure is equally important -- indeed, even more so for distinguishing deceptive from evaded cadences. In a deceptive cadence, the replacement harmony belongs to the ongoing cadential process as its final event (groups backward). In an evaded cadence, what appears at the expected goal is heard as belonging to the subsequent phrase (groups forward), and the cadential group fails to finish before a new group begins. This grouping disruption is what allows an evaded cadence to occur even when V literally resolves to root-position I.

# Key Properties
1. Grouping structure is more important than harmony alone
2. Deceptive: replacement groups BACKWARD (is the phrase's goal/ending)
3. Evaded: what follows groups FORWARD (is a new beginning)
4. Evaded cadence can occur even with V-I resolution (if I sounds like a beginning)
5. The cadential group "fails to finish before a new group begins"
