# Quick Definition
The central principle that dominant harmony can acquire cadential status only when it initially appears in root position and retains that position until resolution; any inversion automatically destroys cadential potential.

# Formal Definition
It is a central axiom of the approach to harmony and cadence in this textbook that dominant harmony can acquire cadential status only when it initially appears in root position and retains that position until its resolution to tonic. Any inversion of the dominant automatically destroys its cadential potential.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
The axiom establishes:
- **Cadential V**: Must be in root position from first appearance through resolution
- **Prolongational V**: Any inverted dominant (V6, V4/3, etc.)

This restriction means:
- I - V6 - I is prolongational, not cadential
- I - V - I (with root-position V) may be cadential
- Even momentary inversion destroys cadential status

Exception: V4/2 in the deceptive progression V - V4/2 - I6, where the bass motion 5-4 represents passing motion rather than genuine change of position.

# Musical Context/Application
This axiom is foundational for analyzing classical form because:
- It distinguishes formal cadences from mere harmonic motion
- It explains why inverted dominants appear in prolongational contexts
- It clarifies the special status of root-position V in phrase endings
- It provides an objective criterion for identifying cadential moments

The chapter acknowledges: "This condition may seem overly restrictive to many of you who have been taught that any motion from dominant to tonic creates a cadence. It is to be hoped that the analytical gains from this restrictive definition of cadential progression will more than offset any initial discomfort it may cause."

# Common Confusions
- Treating any V-I motion as cadential (inversion matters)
- Not recognizing the formal significance of this restriction
- Missing the one exception (V4/2 in deceptive progression to I6)
- Confusing the stability of root position with cadential function
