## Tonicization of Remote Regions

A shift to minor mode in the subordinate theme brings into play tonal regions that would otherwise be considered **remote** in a strictly major-mode context.

### Regions Made Available

Through modal shift:
- bIII (flat-three)
- bVI (flat-six)
- bVII (flat-seven)
- bII (flat-two/Neapolitan area)

### Prominent Tonicization

Prominent tonicization of these regions is another **loosening device** typical of subordinate themes (but not of main themes).

### Extreme Cases

Ascending-third sequential progressions at the beginning of the theme can result in tonicization of regions a **tritone removed** from the key (the most distant possible tonal relationship).

### Chromatic ECP

The tonicization of bVI is especially common:
- May be prolonged across several measures
- Often incorporated into an ECP through chromatic variants
- Augmented triad may appear as chromatic variant of initial cadential tonic
- Diminished-seventh may substitute for pre-dominant
