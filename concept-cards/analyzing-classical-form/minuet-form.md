# Quick Definition
Minuet form is the formal organization of an individual minuet (either minuet proper or trio), strongly resembling small ternary but with more complex thematic functionality. It consists of an exposition (A), contrasting middle (B), and recapitulation (A'), and expresses main theme, transition, and subordinate theme functions.

# Core Definition
Most individual minuets are built in a way that strongly resembles the small ternary theme type (about 90%; about 10% resemble small binary). The form uses the functional labels of the small ternary (exposition A, contrasting middle B, recapitulation A') along with their constituent phrase functions. Because minuet form functions as a high-level component of minuet/trio form, we also identify thematic functions of main theme, transition, subordinate theme, and developmental core (Caplin, pp. 631-632).

# Prerequisites
- **Small ternary** -- The theme type that minuet form strongly resembles
- **Minuet/trio form** -- The full-movement form containing individual minuets

# Key Properties
1. ~90% resemble small ternary; ~10% resemble small binary
2. Uses small ternary labels (A, B, A') with phrase functions
3. Also identifies thematic functions (main theme, transition, subordinate theme)
4. Key analytical question: is a subordinate key confirmed, and if so, where?
5. B section usually more complex and longer than in small ternary
6. A' section seldom shorter than A (often significantly expanded)
7. Form-functional fusion widespread (especially transition/subordinate theme)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Confirm the tripartite A-B-A' structure
2. Identify whether the A section modulates
3. Track thematic functions (main theme, transition, subordinate theme) across sections
4. Note where the subordinate key is established (A section or B section)

# Context & Application
Minuet form applies to both the minuet proper and the trio. The critical difference from small ternary is the importance of subordinate-key establishment, since the minuet's A section may represent the principal tonal contrast of the entire section.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 632): Haydn, String Quartet in G, Op. 54, No. 1, iii -- B section contains fused transition and subordinate-theme functions; A' expanded with development and deceptive cadence.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Small ternary** -- The model for minuet form
## Enables
- **Minuet exposition (A section)** -- The first section of minuet form
- **Minuet contrasting middle** -- The B section
- **Minuet recapitulation** -- The A' section
## Contrasts With
- **Small ternary** -- Less complex; the B section is simpler and shorter
- **Binary minuet form** -- Alternative bipartite organization

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating minuet form as identical to small ternary
  **Correction**: Minuet form has more complex thematic functionality, larger B section, and more expanded A' section than typical small ternary
