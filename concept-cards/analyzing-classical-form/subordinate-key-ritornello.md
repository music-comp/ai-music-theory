## Definition

The subordinate-key ritornello is the third section of concerto form, occurring after the solo exposition. It functions as the formal analogue of a closing section in sonata form, allowing the orchestral forces to participate in reinforcing the new key.

## Position in Form

Follows the "cadential trill" marking the end of the solo exposition's subordinate-theme group.

## Functions

1. **Orchestral participation**: Gives orchestra its own music in subordinate-key context
2. **Reintroduces ritornello material**: Often brings back ideas eliminated from solo exposition
3. **Dynamic function**: Sustains or intensifies the climax reached by solo
4. **Rest for soloist**: Allows solo part to rest after virtuosic exposition

## Content

### Material Source
- Usually drawn from opening ritornello
- Often uses material deleted from solo exposition
- Transition material from opening ritornello is a favorite (vigorous, brilliant style)

### Character
- **Forte** material with powerful rhythmic vitality
- Soft, lyrical gestures not found at beginning
- Must sustain/surpass solo's climax (single instrument vs. full orchestra)

## Structure

- Usually complete thematic unit ending with PAC in subordinate key
- Form often nonconventional but relatively tight-knit
- May include brief closing section following final cadence
- May fail to close cadentially before moving to development

## Distinction from Closing Section

Although located where closing section might be expected:
- Not organized as series of codettas
- Dynamic intensity is rarely lessened
- Displays few characteristics of closing-section function
