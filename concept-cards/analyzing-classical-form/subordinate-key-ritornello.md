# Quick Definition
The subordinate-key ritornello replaces the closing section after the solo exposition, allowing the orchestra alone to reinforce the subordinate key. It typically draws on material from the opening ritornello that was eliminated in the solo exposition and sustains or intensifies the solo's dynamic climax.

# Core Definition
In concerto form, the closing section after the solo exposition is replaced by the subordinate-key ritornello, a section designed to allow the orchestral forces to emerge from an accompanimental role and participate alone in the reinforcement of the subordinate key. Its melodic-motivic content is usually taken from material originally presented in the opening ritornello but eliminated in the solo exposition (Caplin, pp. 698-699, 712-713).

# Prerequisites
- **Concerto form** -- The overall form
- **Solo exposition** -- The section the ritornello follows

# Key Properties
1. Replaces closing section after solo exposition
2. Orchestra alone (solo silent until development)
3. Reinforces the subordinate key
4. Draws on material from opening ritornello eliminated in solo exposition
5. Sustains or intensifies the solo's dynamic climax
6. Usually structured as complete thematic unit ending with PAC
7. Often begins with forte material from the ritornello's transition
8. Displays few characteristics of closing-section function

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 699): Mozart, Piano Concerto in E-flat, K. 482, i -- subordinate-key ritornello draws from the second subordinate theme of the opening ritornello.
