# Quick Definition
Melodic motion that departs from and returns to the same pitch, creating neighboring chords within prolongational progressions where the prolonged harmony remains in the same position.

# Core Definition
Neighbor motion refers to melodic movement departing from a chord tone and returning to the same pitch. In harmonic prolongation, "neighboring motion is usually (but not necessarily) present in one or more of the voices when the prolonged harmony remains in the same position from beginning to end of the progression" (p. 33). Crucially, the distinction between neighboring and passing chords is based on the position of the prolonged harmony, not on literal neighbor-tone motion in the voices.

# Prerequisites
- **Prolongational progression** — Neighbor motion is one technique within prolongational progressions

# Key Properties
1. Departs from and returns to the same pitch
2. Creates upper or lower neighbor-tone figures
3. Associated with the prolonged harmony remaining in the same position throughout
4. Literal neighbor-tone motion is "usually (but not necessarily)" present
5. The harmonic concept (neighboring chord) refers to position, not literal voice motion
6. May be diatonic or chromatic

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Check whether the prolonged harmony remains in the same position (e.g., I to I)
2. Identify the subordinate chord between statements of the prolonged harmony
3. Note whether literal neighbor-tone motion appears in the voices (common but not required)
4. If the prolonged harmony changes position (I to I6), the subordinate chord is passing, not neighboring

# Context & Application
Neighbor motion creates embellishment without changing harmonic position and is central to prolongational progressions. The harmonic concept of "neighboring chord" refers to the position of the prolonged harmony (same position throughout), regardless of whether literal neighbor tones appear in individual voices.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming neighboring chords must have literal neighbor-tone motion in the voices
  **Correction**: Literal neighbor-tone motion is typical but "not necessarily" present

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing the melodic concept (neighbor tone) with the harmonic concept (neighboring chord)
  **Clarification**: The distinction between neighboring and passing chords is about the position of the prolonged harmony, not voice motion
