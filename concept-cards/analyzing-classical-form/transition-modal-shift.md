## Modal Shift in Transition

A **modal shift** (moving from major to minor or vice versa) is one of the loosening devices that can be employed in a transition.

### Function

Modal shift helps destabilize the home key by:
- Introducing chromatic pitches
- Creating tonal ambiguity
- Opening up new harmonic possibilities for modulation

### Common Context

Modal shift in transitions often:
- Occurs in continuation function
- Prepares for approach to the HC
- Coincides with augmented-sixth harmonies

### Relationship to Augmented-Sixth Chords

The augmented-sixth approach to the dominant often involves:
- A shift to the minor mode of the subordinate key
- The raised 4 (leading tone of V) and lowered 6 create the augmented sixth
- This chromatic approach signals the impending end of transition

### Minor-Mode Expositions

In minor-mode movements:
- The transition may briefly touch the major mode
- Before returning to minor for the approach to the dominant
- Or may establish major mode of the subordinate key (relative major)
