# Quick Definition
Modal shift (moving from major to minor or vice versa) is a loosening device in transitions that introduces chromatic pitches, creates tonal ambiguity, and often prepares the approach to the HC through augmented-sixth harmonies. A shift to the minor mode of the subordinate key creates raised 4 and lowered 6, yielding the augmented-sixth interval that signals the impending end of the transition.

# Core Definition
A modal shift in the transition helps destabilize the home key by introducing chromatic pitches, creating tonal ambiguity, and opening up new harmonic possibilities for modulation. Modal shift in transitions often occurs in connection with continuation function and prepares the approach to the HC. It frequently coincides with augmented-sixth harmonies: a shift to the minor mode of the subordinate key creates raised 4 (the leading tone of V) and lowered 6, and the resulting augmented-sixth interval is a standard signal for the impending end of a transition. In minor-mode movements, the transition may briefly touch the major mode before returning to minor for the approach to the dominant, or it may establish the major mode of the subordinate key (the relative major) (Caplin, Ch. 11, pp. 350-351).

1. Introduces chromatic pitches and tonal ambiguity
2. Often occurs in continuation function
3. Prepares approach to HC
4. Frequently produces augmented-sixth harmonies
5. Minor mode of subordinate key: raised 4 + lowered 6 = augmented sixth
6. Standard signal for transition's impending end

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Note any change from major to minor (or vice versa) within the transition
2. Check for augmented-sixth harmonies near the approach to HC
3. Identify whether the modal shift introduces raised 4 or lowered 6
4. Note the formal location: typically in continuation function
5. Consider whether the shift prepares the transition's cadential close

# Context & Application
Modal shift is one of the most aurally striking loosening devices in transitions. The sudden introduction of minor-mode coloring creates an immediate contrast with the major-mode stability of the home key or subordinate key, and the resulting augmented-sixth approach to the HC gives transitions a characteristic harmonic sound. Analysts should listen for this shift as a signal that the transition is approaching its structural end.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating modal shift as a modulation to a new key
  **Correction**: Modal shift is a change of mode (major to minor or vice versa), not a modulation to a different tonic; the tonal center remains the same
