# Quick Definition
A harmony (usually VI) that can replace an expected tonic chord while maintaining tonic function, most commonly occurring in deceptive resolutions after a root-position dominant.

# Core Definition
"In most contexts, the triad built on the submediant (VI) degree of the scale has tonic function and can frequently substitute for an expected I chord" (p. 31). This substitution is explained by the sharing of two common tones between I and VI (scale degrees 1 and 3), which "largely accounts for their functional similarity" (p. 35). When following a root-position V, the substitution produces a deceptive resolution. Crucially, VI should NOT be classified as pre-dominant even when progressing to V.

# Prerequisites
- **Tonic function** — Must understand what tonic function means to recognize substitution
- **Common tones** — Common tones between I and VI explain the substitution

# Key Properties
1. VI is the primary tonic substitute in classical harmony
2. Shares scale degrees 1 and 3 with I (common tones explain functional similarity)
3. Commonly appears in deceptive resolutions (V-VI)
4. Participates in prolongational progressions extending tonic function
5. NOT a pre-dominant, even in VI-V progressions (bypasses pre-dominant)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for VI appearing where I would be expected (especially after V)
2. Verify tonic function through common-tone relationship with I
3. In deceptive contexts, note VI replacing the expected final tonic
4. In VI-V progressions, classify VI as tonic (not pre-dominant)

# Context & Application
Tonic substitutes appear in deceptive cadential progressions (V resolves to VI instead of I), prolongational progressions (VI participates in extending tonic function), and direct motion to V (bypassing pre-dominant). The submediant should NOT be classified as pre-dominant even when progressing directly to V; in that case, a tonic-functioning harmony moves directly to dominant, bypassing pre-dominant function entirely.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Classifying VI as pre-dominant when it progresses to V
  **Correction**: In VI-V, a tonic-functioning harmony moves directly to dominant, bypassing pre-dominant

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking tonic substitutes are "weaker" versions of tonic
  **Clarification**: Tonic substitutes serve different formal purposes (e.g., deceptive resolution) rather than being inferior expressions of tonic
