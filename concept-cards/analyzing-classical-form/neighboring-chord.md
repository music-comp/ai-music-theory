# Quick Definition
A subordinate harmony within a prolongational progression where the prolonged harmony remains in the same position (root position or inversion) at beginning and end. The distinction from passing chords is based on the position of the prolonged harmony, not literal voice motion.

# Core Definition
An individual harmony is prolonged by one or more neighboring chords when the prolonged harmony remains in the same position (root position or inversion) from the beginning to the end of the progression. In such cases, melodic neighbor-tone motion is usually (but not necessarily) present in one or more of the voices (p. 34). The terms neighboring and passing were originally used for single-voice embellishing tones; in extending to harmonies, the distinction is based exclusively on the positions of the prolonged harmony (p. 35).

# Prerequisites
- **Prolongational progression** — Neighboring chords are one technique of prolongation

# Key Properties
1. Prolonged harmony retains the same position throughout (e.g., I-V6-I: tonic stays root position)
2. Melodic neighbor-tone motion usually present but not required
3. Distinction from passing: based on position of prolonged harmony, not literal voice motion
4. I-V-I with V in non-root position is often better understood as prolongational (neighboring V)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify a Neighboring Chord:
1. Identify the prolonged harmony at beginning and end
2. Verify it remains in the same position (both root position, or both same inversion)
3. The intervening chord is neighboring regardless of literal voice motion
4. Common pattern: I-V6-I (tonic remains in root position throughout)

# Context & Application
The simple progression I-V-I, often taught as a "cadence formula," is frequently better understood as prolongational when V is in first inversion and the melody embellishes scale degrees 3 or 5. Neighboring chords create embellishment without change of harmonic position.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Classifying I-V6-I as cadential
  **Correction**: When V is inverted, this is prolongational (neighboring), not cadential

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Basing the distinction on literal melodic neighbor motion
  **Clarification**: The distinction is about harmonic position of the prolonged harmony, not voice motion
- **Confusion**: Thinking neighboring always requires stepwise voice motion
  **Clarification**: Neighboring chord status depends only on the prolonged harmony maintaining its position
