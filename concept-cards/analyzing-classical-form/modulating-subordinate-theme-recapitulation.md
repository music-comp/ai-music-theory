# Quick Definition
When the exposition's subordinate theme modulates internally, the tonal adjustment in the recapitulation is more complicated: the theme may begin "off tonic" in a related region and "modulate" back to the home key, or it may simply begin directly on the home-key tonic.

# Core Definition
When the exposition's subordinate theme modulates internally, the tonal adjustment required in the recapitulation is more complicated than the usual simple transposition. Two solutions are available. First, the subordinate theme can still give the impression of modulating by beginning in some other tonal region of the home key (such as the flat-VI region) and eventually returning to emphasize the tonic region -- preserving the modulatory character within the home-key framework. Second, alternatively, the theme may begin directly on the home-key tonic, losing some of its original modulatory character but achieving a simpler and more direct tonal design.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of tonal adjustment in the recapitulation
- Knowledge of the sonata principle

# Key Properties
- Exposition's subordinate theme contains internal modulation
- Simple transposition is insufficient for tonal adjustment
- Solution 1: begin off-tonic, "modulate" back to home key (preserves modulatory character)
- Solution 2: begin directly on home-key tonic (simpler but less faithful to original)
- Choice depends on the degree to which the composer values preserving the exposition's character
- Off-tonic beginning typically uses flat-side region of home key

# Construction / Recognition
Compare the subordinate theme's tonal plan in the exposition and recapitulation. If the exposition's theme modulates, check whether the recapitulation preserves this modulatory character (by beginning in a non-tonic region and returning to tonic) or simplifies it (by beginning directly on tonic). The off-tonic beginning is usually in a flat-side region, consistent with the recapitulation's general tendency to explore flat-side areas.

# Context & Application
This issue is particularly relevant for subordinate themes that modulate from one key area to another within the exposition (as opposed to themes that simply confirm the subordinate key). The recapitulation must find a way to keep everything in the home key while either preserving or sacrificing the theme's original modulatory quality.

# Examples
- General pattern: theme begins in flat-VI region of home key and eventually "modulates" back to tonic by end of first phrase, preserving the impression of tonal motion within the home key
- Alternative pattern: theme begins directly on home-key tonic, simplifying the original modulatory design

# Common Errors
- Assuming the subordinate theme must always begin on tonic in the recapitulation
- Failing to recognize an off-tonic beginning as a tonal adjustment strategy rather than an error

# Common Confusions
- An off-tonic beginning in the recapitulation's subordinate theme is NOT a real modulation away from the home key -- it is a local tonicization that preserves the impression of modulatory activity while remaining functionally within the home key
