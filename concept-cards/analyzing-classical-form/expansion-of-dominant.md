# Quick Definition
The most easily recognizable case of cadential expansion occurs when the dominant is considerably lengthened, delaying its resolution to tonic as long as possible. The "dissonant" cadential six-four embellishment usually receives the greatest expansion, raising powerful expectations for resolution to the five-three sonority.

# Core Definition
In an expanded cadential progression, the dominant harmony can be considerably lengthened to delay resolution to tonic as long as possible. The dominant is typically first sounded with its cadential six-four embellishment, and this "dissonant" sonority usually receives the greatest expansion, raising powerful expectations for its resolution to the five-three position. The heightened dramatic expression inherent in broad dominant expansion makes it ideal for operatic and concerto genres, though it is also found in symphonic, chamber, and solo-sonata repertories. When the dominant expansion is sufficiently large, the same harmony may continue across a phrase boundary, with a new phrase beginning with the return of earlier material while the cadential six-four does not resolve until the very last moment before the PAC (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 396-397).

# Key Properties
1. Dominant is considerably lengthened within the ECP
2. Cadential six-four usually receives greatest expansion
3. Delays resolution to tonic as long as possible
4. Raises powerful expectations for five-three resolution
5. Especially effective in operatic and concerto genres
6. May span a phrase boundary when sufficiently large

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate the cadential six-four within an ECP
2. Measure the temporal span of the dominant harmony
3. Check whether the six-four is prolonged before resolving to five-three
4. Note if a phrase boundary occurs within the dominant expansion
5. Observe the dramatic effect of the delayed resolution

# Context & Application
Dominant expansion is one of three principal ways to expand an ECP (alongside expansion of the initial tonic and the pre-dominant). The broad dominant expansion creates the greatest dramatic tension in the cadential process, making the eventual PAC resolution maximally powerful. When the expansion is so large that it spans a phrase boundary, a new phrase may begin with the return of earlier material while the underlying dominant harmony continues.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating the cadential six-four's resolution to five-three as a separate harmonic event
  **Correction**: The six-four and five-three are both part of the dominant harmony; the six-four is an embellishment, not a separate chord
