## Expansion of the Dominant in ECP

The most easily recognizable case of prominent cadential expansion occurs when the **dominant is considerably lengthened**, delaying as long as possible its resolution to tonic.

### Typical Method

- Dominant is first sounded with its **six-four embellishment** (cadential 6/4)
- This "dissonant" sonority usually receives greatest expansion
- Raises powerful expectation for resolution to five-three sonority

### Dramatic Expression

The heightened dramatic expression inherent in broad dominant expansion makes it ideal for:
- Operatic genres
- Concerto genres
- But also found in symphonic, chamber, and solo-sonata repertories

### Multiple Phrases

When dominant expansion is sufficiently large:
- Same harmony continues across a phrase boundary
- New phrase begins with return of earlier material
- Cadential six-four doesn't resolve until the very last moment before PAC
