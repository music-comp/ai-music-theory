# Liquidation

**Liquidation** is the process of gradually eliminating characteristic motivic features until only uncharacteristic ones remain, which no longer demand a continuation.

## Definition (Schoenberg)

"Liquidation consists in gradually eliminating characteristic features, until only uncharacteristic ones remain, which no longer demand a continuation. Often only residues remain, which have little in common with the basic motive."

## Purpose

Liquidation serves to:
- Prepare for cadential closure
- Transition from characteristic (distinctive) material to conventional (formulaic) material
- Create appropriate conditions for thematic ending

## Context

Liquidation is especially associated with **cadential function**, but may also occur within continuation function. Phrase-structural fragmentation often results in a degree of motivic liquidation when fragmented units eliminate some motivic content of the original basic idea.

## Process

The elimination can be:
- **Gradual** - Most palpable when motives are systematically eliminated over several measures
- **Immediate** - When the continuation phrase begins with entirely contrasting ideas, complete liquidation occurs at once

## Relation to Characteristic vs. Conventional

- **Characteristic melody**: Uniquely defines a theme as an individual (used in basic ideas)
- **Conventional melody**: Relatively interchangeable from piece to piece (used in cadential material)

Liquidation marks the transition from characteristic to conventional melodic content.
