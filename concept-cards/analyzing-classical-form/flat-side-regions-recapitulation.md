# Quick Definition
The recapitulation explores flat-side tonal regions (IV, bIII, bII, bVI) to avoid harmonic monotony and create a sharp-side shift back to the home key that is analogous to the exposition's modulation.

# Core Definition
The recapitulation's main theme or transition may be altered to emphasize "flat-side tonal regions" — "those regions whose key signatures have more flats than that of the home key, such as the subdominant (IV), the supertonic (ii), and regions derived from modal mixture (bVI, bIII, and bII)" (p. 508). Tovey called such inserted passages "purple patches." The flat-side emphasis serves a specific harmonic purpose: "by tonicizing the subdominant region, and thus shifting one step to the flat side of the tonal spectrum, the return back to the home-key dominant represents a shift to the sharp side, which is analogous to the sharp-side shift that takes place in the exposition's transition" (p. 510).

# Key Properties
1. Flat-side regions: IV, ii, bVI, bIII, bII
2. Purpose: avoid harmonic-tonal monotony
3. Subdominant tonicization allows pre-dominant function leading to home-key V
4. Sharp-side shift back to home key parallels exposition's modulation
5. Tovey's term: "purple patches"
6. Tonicizations especially likely in main theme and transition
7. Subordinate theme stays in home key

# Context & Application
The flat-side emphasis is not arbitrary: it creates a harmonic trajectory that makes the home key sound "fresh" upon return, compensating for the lack of modulation that would otherwise make the recapitulation tonally static.
