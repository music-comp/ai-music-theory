# Flat-side Regions in Recapitulation

The use of chromatically lowered tonal regions to create harmonic variety.

## Definition

Flat-side regions are those whose key signatures have more flats than the home key:
- Subdominant (IV)
- Lowered mediant (flat-III)
- Neapolitan (flat-II)
- Lowered submediant (flat-VI)
- Supertonic minor (ii)

## Purpose

The harmonic plan of the main theme or transition is sometimes altered to emphasize flat-side regions to:
- Avoid harmonic-tonal monotony
- Provide chromatic interest
- Allow return to home key to sound "fresh"

## "Purple Patches"

Music historian Donald Francis Tovey referred to extra, inserted passages in flat-side tonal regions as "purple patches."

## Why Flat-side (not Sharp-side)?

Flat-side is emphasized because the return to home-key dominant then represents a sharp-side shift, analogous to the sharp-side shift in the exposition's transition.
