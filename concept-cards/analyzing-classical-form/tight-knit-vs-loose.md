# Tight-Knit vs. Loose Organization

The distinction between **tight-knit** and **loose** formal organization is fundamental to analyzing classical form. First observed by Schoenberg, these terms describe relative structural stability.

## General Principle

- **Tight-knit**: Promotes structural stability
- **Loose**: Promotes structural instability

## Parameters Affecting Organization

| Parameter | Tight-knit | Loose |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| Tonality | Home key | Modulating, distant keys |
| Harmony | Tonic prolongation | Dominant prolongation, sequences |
| Cadence | PAC | HC, evaded, no cadence |
| Grouping | Symmetrical (4+4) | Asymmetrical |
| Functional efficiency | Efficient | Redundant (extensions) |
| Motivic material | Uniform | Diverse |
| Thematic design | Period, sentence | Nonconventional |

## Small Ternary Application

- **A section**: Most tightly knit
- **B section**: Loose
- **A' section**: Variable (depends on structure)

## Relative Nature

These are **relative** distinctions, not absolute:
- A passage is tight-knit only in comparison with another
- Context determines which is tighter or looser
- Multiple criteria may conflict

## Key Insight

There is no absolute condition of being tight-knit or loose; every case is relative to the entire compositional setting.
