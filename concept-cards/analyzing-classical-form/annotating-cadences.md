# Annotating Cadences

Correctly annotating cadences in the score is essential for formal analysis. Cadences define the principal goals of thematic organization.

## Placement of Cadence Labels

Place the label (PAC, IAC, or HC enclosed in a box) at the place where **the final harmony of the cadential progression first appears**. This marks the **cadential arrival**.

## Cadential Arrival

The cadential arrival is the point in time when we recognize: "Here is the cadence; the thematic process has achieved its fundamental closure."

## Melodic vs. Harmonic Closure

The moment of cadential arrival can seem confusing when melodic closure does not correspond to harmonic closure:

- **Half cadence with 6-4 embellishment**: Place HC at the arrival of the dominant root, not when suspensions resolve
- **Triple suspension in authentic cadence**: The root of tonic in the bass signals cadential arrival, even while upper voices resolve afterward

## Key Principle

The symbol for a cadence is placed at the **harmonic** point of arrival, even if the melody reaches its goal after that moment.

## Cadential Progression Brackets

Use horizontal brackets to indicate the boundaries of cadential progressions:
- Place bracket from the initial harmony to the final harmony
- Exception: For **expanded cadential progressions** (ECP), use the abbreviation "ECP" instead of a long bracket
