# Quick Definition
Place the label (PAC, IAC, or HC in a box) where the final harmony of the cadential progression first appears -- the cadential arrival. This is the point of harmonic closure, even if the melody reaches its goal later.

# Core Definition
As a general rule, place the label PAC, IAC, or HC (enclosed in a box) at that place where the final harmony of the cadential progression first appears. This is the moment that marks the cadential arrival, the point in time when we say, "Here is the cadence; the thematic process has achieved its fundamental closure" (p. 80). The moment of cadential arrival can seem confusing when the sense of melodic closure does not correspond to that of harmonic closure (p. 80).

# Prerequisites
- **Basic cadence types** — Must know PAC, IAC, HC
- **Cadential progression** — Must identify the final harmony

# Key Properties
1. Label placed where the FINAL HARMONY first appears
2. This moment = cadential arrival
3. Harmonic closure may precede melodic closure
4. HC with 6-4 embellishment: place HC at arrival of dominant root, not when suspensions resolve
5. Triple suspension in PAC: tonic root in bass signals arrival, upper voices resolve after
6. For ECP: use "ECP" abbreviation instead of long bracket

# Construction / Recognition
## To Annotate:
1. Identify the cadential progression
2. Locate where the final harmony FIRST appears (not when melody resolves)
3. Place PAC, IAC, or HC in a box at that point
4. Mark the cadential progression with horizontal square bracket (or ECP abbreviation)

# Context & Application
Correctly annotating cadences is one of the most important tasks in formal analysis. Cadences define the principal goals of thematic organization. The key principle: the symbol is placed at the harmonic point of arrival, even if the melody reaches its goal later.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 80, Ex. 2.22g): HC with cadential six-four -- HC placed at arrival of dominant root (downbeat), not when suspensions resolve.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Placing the cadence label where the melody resolves
  **Correction**: Place it where the final harmony first appears (harmonic arrival)

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking harmonic and melodic closure always coincide
  **Clarification**: The melody often reaches its goal after the harmonic arrival
