## To Begin Before Having Ended

Many theorists would see the subordinate theme beginning only where a prominent HC, standing on the dominant, and medial caesura mark a clear end of the transition. This view ignores genuine thematic structures that precede such markers.

### The Problem

When analyzing passages like Mozart's C-minor Sonata:
- A genuine thematic structure with beginning, middle, and ending functions appears
- It resides fully in the subordinate key
- It conforms to subordinate theme requirements except for not ending with PAC
- Traditional view: this is "still the transition"

### The Form-Functional Solution

We must learn to accept that something new can **begin** without there having been an **ending** to what precedes this new beginning.

### Formal Disruption, Not Violation

Such a situation creates a "disruption" in the formal syntax, but not necessarily a fundamental violation.

**Analogy**: A speaker begins a sentence but before completing it (even though its ending is implied), moves right on to start another sentence. There is a clear disruption but not complete loss of comprehension.

### Two-Part Subordinate Theme

This understanding leads to the concept of **two-part subordinate theme**:
- Part 1: complete structure except for PAC (ends with internal HC)
- Part 2: new initiation leading to required PAC
