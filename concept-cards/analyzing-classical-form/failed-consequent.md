# Quick Definition
A phrase that resembles a consequent (returns the basic idea, leads toward cadence) but "fails" because it closes with a weak cadence (HC) or modulates instead of achieving the expected PAC, creating a nonconventional structure within Part 1 of a small binary.

# Core Definition
In Part 1 of a small binary, when periodic functions come into play and the second phrase returns the basic idea (suggesting consequent), but that phrase closes with an HC rather than the expected PAC, we speak of a "failed consequent." Although the internal organization (b.i. + c.i. -> HC) resembles an antecedent, in context we much more have the impression that an implied consequent has failed to materialize because of weak cadential closure. The resulting structure is nonconventional: e.g., compound basic idea + failed consequent, or antecedent + failed consequent. The failure often involves modulation to a subordinate key.

# Key Properties
1. Returns basic idea (suggesting consequent) but ends with HC or modulates
2. Context determines function: we expect consequent, get failure
3. Not simply a "second antecedent" -- the contextual expectation matters
4. Creates nonconventional Part 1 structures
5. Especially associated with Part 1 of small binary

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 266): Beethoven, Op. 30/1, iii -- c.b.i. followed by return of b.i. in varied form; second phrase closes with HC instead of PAC; failed consequent.
**Example 2** (p. 267): Haydn, Sonata H. 31, iii -- antecedent followed by phrase that modulates to submediant and closes with HC; failed consequent.
