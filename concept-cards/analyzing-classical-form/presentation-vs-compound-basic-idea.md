# Quick Definition
When an initiating phrase is supported by a straightforward tonic prolongation, the primary difference between a presentation and a compound basic idea lies in whether mm. 3-4 bring a repetition or genuinely contrasting material -- a distinction that can be genuinely ambiguous.

# Core Definition
Caplin identifies this as a key analytical ambiguity. In many cases the distinction is clear-cut: either the basic idea is obviously repeated (presentation) or significantly different material brings a contrasting idea (c.b.i.). But when the melodic "background" of the two ideas is similar -- e.g., the same underlying scale-degree motion with different surface figuration -- the distinction becomes ambiguous. Both analyses can be viable, so long as the criteria supporting each interpretation are identified.

# Prerequisites
- **Presentation phrase** — Must understand repeated basic idea over tonic prolongation
- **Compound basic idea** — Must understand b.i. + contrasting idea without cadence

# Key Properties
1. Ambiguity arises only when underlying harmony is tonic prolongational (shared by both)
2. Surface: ideas may appear different (favoring c.b.i.)
3. Background: underlying melodic scaffold may be consistent (favoring presentation)
4. Neither interpretation is necessarily "correct"
5. Both can be supported with different criteria

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Compare the melodic-motivic content of mm. 1-2 and mm. 3-4
2. Look at surface differences (rhythm, contour, figuration)
3. Look at underlying similarities (scale-degree motion, melodic framework)
4. If genuinely ambiguous, acknowledge both viable readings

# Context & Application
This ambiguity illustrates Caplin's broader point that formal analysis is not a mechanical procedure. Recognizing and exploring ambiguities leads to richer analytical understanding than forcing a single label.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 134): Beethoven, Piano Sonata Op. 31/3, iii, mm. 1-4 — surface: prominent leaps vs. stepwise motion suggests c.b.i.; background: descending stepwise motion 1-7 matched by 4-3 suggests varied repetition (presentation).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Insisting on one "correct" label when the situation is genuinely ambiguous
  **Correction**: Both readings can be valid; identify the criteria supporting each

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking any surface difference makes a contrasting idea
  **Clarification**: Consider the underlying melodic framework; varied repetition with a similar scaffold may still be a presentation
