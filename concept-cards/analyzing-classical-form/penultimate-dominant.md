# Quick Definition
The dominant harmony occupying the "before-the-last" position in an authentic cadential progression, creating dynamic impulse into the final tonic.

# Core Definition
"In the authentic cadential progression, the dominant occupies the penultimate ('before-the-last') position and thus creates a powerful dynamic impulse into the final tonic" (p. 39). "Whether a dominant is characterized as penultimate or ultimate has nothing to do with harmony per se; rather, it is a matter of how the dominant groups with its surrounding harmonies" (p. 39). The penultimate dominant groups WITH the following tonic as part of the same progression.

# Prerequisites
- **Cadential progression** — The penultimate dominant exists within an authentic cadential progression
- **Dominant function** — Must understand dominant harmony

# Key Properties
1. Precedes the final tonic in authentic cadential progressions
2. Groups WITH the following tonic as part of the same progression
3. May be a triad or seventh chord (V or V7)
4. V7 is appropriate here -- dissonance enhances drive toward resolution
5. Must be in root position for cadential status
6. May be embellished by cadential six-four

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate the dominant within an authentic cadential progression
2. Verify that it is followed by a root-position tonic
3. Confirm that the dominant and tonic group together as part of one progression
4. The dominant is penultimate if it drives forward into the final tonic

# Context & Application
The penultimate dominant creates the characteristic tension before cadential resolution. The distinction from the ultimate dominant is about formal grouping: the penultimate dominant groups forward with the tonic, while the ultimate dominant ends the progression. Adding a seventh is appropriate and common for the penultimate position, as the dissonance enhances the drive toward resolution.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing penultimate dominant (authentic cadence) with ultimate dominant (half cadence)
  **Correction**: Penultimate precedes and groups with tonic; ultimate IS the goal

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the distinction relates to chord structure rather than grouping
  **Clarification**: "Whether a dominant is characterized as penultimate or ultimate has nothing to do with harmony per se; rather, it is a matter of how the dominant groups with its surrounding harmonies"
