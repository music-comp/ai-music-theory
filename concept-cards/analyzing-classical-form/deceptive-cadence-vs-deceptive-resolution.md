# Quick Definition
Caplin insists on distinguishing a deceptive resolution (any V-vi motion) from a genuine deceptive cadence (V-vi occurring only when thematic closure is truly imminent). The test: could you change vi to I and recognize a genuine cadence at that moment? If so, it is a deceptive cadence. "Speak freely of deceptive resolutions; speak with caution of deceptive cadences."

# Core Definition
Most students first learn about the "deceptive cadence" in elementary harmony, where the term is used whenever V resolves to vi. But it is important to distinguish a deceptive resolution (the harmonic progression V-vi, which occurs in many noncadential contexts) from a genuine deceptive cadence (which should be recognized only when a truly cadential situation is in the making -- when circumstances for thematic closure are imminent). The test: substitute I for vi -- would you recognize a genuine cadence?

# Key Properties
1. Deceptive resolution: any V-vi, in any context
2. Deceptive cadence: V-vi only when cadential closure is imminent
3. Test: change vi to I -- would it be a genuine cadence?
4. Many V-vi progressions are entirely noncadential
5. "Speak freely of deceptive resolutions; speak with caution of deceptive cadences"
