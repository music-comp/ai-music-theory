# Phrase Deviations

**Phrase deviations** are alterations to the normative phrase structure that make phrases longer or shorter than their default 4-measure length.

## Types of Phrase Deviations

1. **Extension** - Adding material AFTER the phrase is functionally complete
2. **Compression** - Shortening the phrase (usually to 3 measures)
3. **Internal expansion** - Inserting material WITHIN the phrase
4. **Interpolation** - Inserting foreign material that disrupts formal continuity

## Normative Phrase Length

Phrases in classical themes are normatively 4 real measures in length. Deviations alter this norm by:
- Adding material (extension, internal expansion)
- Removing material (compression)
- Inserting parenthetical material (interpolation)

## Effect on Theme Size

When phrase deviations occur, the actual size of a theme will differ from the model size. For example:
- A sentence with extended continuation: 10 measures instead of 8
- A period with compressed consequent: 7 measures instead of 8

## Analysis Approach

When analyzing themes with phrase deviations:
1. Identify the normative phrase structure
2. Locate where the deviation occurs
3. Determine the type of deviation
4. Understand the formal effect

Recognizing phrase deviations helps us understand how composers manipulate conventional structures.
