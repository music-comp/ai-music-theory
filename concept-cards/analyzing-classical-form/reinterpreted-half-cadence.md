# Reinterpreted Half Cadence

A **reinterpreted half cadence** occurs when an antecedent modulates to the dominant region, closing with what is literally a PAC in that key, but which is understood retrospectively as a kind of half cadence when the home key immediately returns.

## The Situation

A presumed antecedent phrase:
1. Modulates to the dominant region
2. Closes there with a PAC (in the new key)

This would seem to violate two principles:
- Antecedent should close with weak cadence
- Antecedent should not modulate

## The Reinterpretation

When the home key is immediately reinstated at the beginning of the consequent:
- The sense of modulation is instantly canceled
- The PAC (V/V → I/V) is retrospectively understood as HC (V of home key)
- The final dominant harmony is "reinterpreted"

## Notation

**PAC⇒HC** (PAC "becomes" HC)

The double arrow (⇒) represents retrospective reinterpretation.

## Both Interpretations Valid

- At the moment of the cadence: A genuine PAC in the new key
- In the broader context of the period: A reinterpreted HC in the home key

## Frequency

- **Rare** in simple 8-measure periods
- **More frequent** in compound 16-measure periods
