# Period

The **period** is a fundamental 8-measure theme type based on the idea that a musical unit of weak cadential closure is repeated to produce stronger cadential closure.

## Structure

The period is built out of two phrases:
- A 4-measure **antecedent phrase** (weak cadence)
- A 4-measure **consequent phrase** (strong cadence)

## Formal Functions

The period contains two formal functions:
- **Antecedent** - an initiating function
- **Consequent** - a concluding function

Unlike the sentence, the period does NOT bring a specific medial formal function.

## Cadential Differentiation

The essential feature of the period is cadential differentiation:
- Antecedent ends with a **weak** cadence (usually HC, sometimes IAC)
- Consequent ends with a **strong** cadence (usually PAC, rarely IAC)

## Key Characteristics

- Two cadences (one per phrase)
- Cannot end with an HC
- Basic idea **returns** at the start of the consequent phrase
- Projects character of balance and repose

## Historical Background

The term "period" arose in 18th-century theory. The modern concept of two symmetrical phrases with differing cadential strength was introduced by Adolph Bernhard Marx in the mid-19th century.

## Comparison with Sentence

- Both are 8 measures with two phrases
- Both begin with a basic idea
- Period has two cadences; sentence has one
- In period, basic idea returns; in sentence, basic idea is repeated
