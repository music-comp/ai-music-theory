# Quick Definition
A fundamental 8-m. theme type based on cadential differentiation: a 4-m. antecedent phrase (b.i. + c.i. leading to weak cadence) is followed by a 4-m. consequent phrase (return of b.i. + c.i. leading to strong cadence). Two formal functions: antecedent (initiating) and consequent (concluding). No medial function.

# Core Definition
The period has at its basis the idea that a musical unit of weak cadential closure is repeated so as to produce stronger cadential closure. The period is an 8-m. theme built out of two phrases: a 4-m. antecedent phrase, followed by a 4-m. consequent phrase. The period contains two formal functions: an initiating antecedent and a concluding consequent. Unlike the sentence, the period does not bring a specific medial formal function (pp. 95-96). The concept used in this text, in the tradition of Schoenberg, narrows the definition to cases where the first and second phrases begin with similar material and exhibit cadential differentiation of weak to strong (p. 98).

# Key Properties
1. 8 measures, divided into two 4-m. phrases
2. Antecedent: b.i. + contrasting idea leading to weak cadence (HC or IAC)
3. Consequent: return of b.i. + contrasting idea leading to strong cadence (PAC, rarely IAC)
4. Two cadences (one per phrase); cadential differentiation: weak to strong
5. Two formal functions: antecedent (initiating) and consequent (concluding)
6. No medial function (unlike the sentence)
7. Consequent "repeats" the antecedent by bringing a return of the original b.i.
8. Projects character of balance and repose (vs. sentence's mobility)
9. Antecedent does NOT elide with consequent

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify a Period:
1. Look for b.i. in mm. 1-2 followed by contrasting idea (not repetition) in mm. 3-4
2. Identify weak cadence at end of m. 4 (HC or IAC)
3. Check for return of b.i. at mm. 5-6
4. Identify strong cadence at end of m. 8 (PAC)
5. Confirm cadential differentiation: weak to strong

# Context & Application
The period is one of two fundamental 8-m. theme types (with the sentence). It is not always possible to predict from the opening b.i. whether a sentence or period will follow -- this depends on what happens in mm. 3-4 (repetition = sentence; contrast with cadence = period). The period form does NOT include "contrasting periods" (where the consequent begins with different material); those are treated as hybrid themes.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 96, Ex. 3.1): Mozart, Eine kleine Nachtmusik, K. 525, ii, mm. 1-8 -- b.i., contrasting idea with HC, return of b.i., contrasting idea with PAC.

**Example 2** (p. 98, Ex. 3.2): Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 311, ii, mm. 1-8 -- another clear period.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Identifying a "contrasting period" (consequent with new material)
  **Correction**: In Caplin's system, this is a hybrid theme, not a period

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Using "period" for any two-phrase unit
  **Clarification**: Period requires similar material at the start of both phrases AND cadential differentiation (weak to strong)
