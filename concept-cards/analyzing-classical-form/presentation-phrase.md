# Presentation Phrase

The **presentation phrase** is the opening 4-measure unit of a sentence, consisting of a basic idea and its repetition.

## Structure

- 2-measure **basic idea** (tonic version/statement)
- 2-measure **repetition** of the basic idea

## Harmonic Foundation

The underlying harmony of the entire presentation phrase is **tonic prolongational**. The lack of a distinct cadential progression prohibits the formation of a cadence to end the phrase.

## Formal Function

Presentation function creates a solid structural beginning for the theme by:
- Establishing the melodic-motivic content
- Providing a stable harmonic-tonal environment
- Setting forth the fundamental material through initial statement
- Fully "presenting" it through immediate repetition

## Types of Repetition

The basic idea can be repeated in three ways:

1. **Exact repetition** - same harmonic support (I-I or I-V, V-I)
2. **Statement-response repetition** - statement emphasizes tonic, response emphasizes dominant
3. **Sequential repetition** - model is completely transposed (rare in presentations)

## Key Principle

A presentation phrase does NOT end with a cadence, even if the harmonies might suggest cadential potential. The repetition of the basic idea reinforces formal initiation rather than creating any sense of ending.

## Open-Ended Quality

The open-ended quality of the presentation sets up strong expectations for a continuation phrase to follow.
