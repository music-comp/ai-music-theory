# Quick Definition
The opening 4-m. phrase of a sentence, consisting of a 2-m. b.i. and its immediate repetition (exact, statement-response, or sequential). Supported by tonic prolongation with no cadential closure. Creates a solid structural beginning by presenting the theme's melodic-motivic content.

# Core Definition
A presentation phrase begins with a 2-m. basic idea. The first appearance emphasizes tonic harmony, usually in root position (tonic version). The basic idea is then repeated (exact, statement-response, or sequential repetition). The underlying harmony of the entire presentation phrase is tonic prolongational; the lack of a distinct cadential progression prohibits the formation of a cadence (pp. 56, 58).

# Prerequisites
- **Sentence** — The presentation phrase is the first phrase of the sentence
- **Basic idea** — The presentation is built from the b.i. and its repetition

# Key Properties
1. 4 measures: b.i. (mm. 1-2) + repetition (mm. 3-4)
2. Tonic prolongational harmony throughout
3. No cadential closure -- b.i. repetition reinforces initiation, not ending
4. Three repetition types: exact, statement-response, sequential (rare)
5. Open-ended quality creates expectations for continuation
6. Tonic prolongation may extend into the beginning of the continuation phrase

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for b.i. in mm. 1-2 supported by tonic harmony
2. Identify the repetition type in mm. 3-4 (exact, statement-response, or sequential)
3. Verify tonic prolongational harmony throughout
4. Confirm no cadence at the end of m. 4

# Context & Application
The presentation sets up expectations for continuation. Its repetition of the b.i. and tonic stability create the "beginning" quality. Even when harmonies at the end of mm. 3-4 have cadential potential (V7-I), no cadence occurs because the repetition of the b.i. reinforces formal initiation rather than creating ending (p. 82).

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 56, Ex. 2.1): Beethoven, Op. 2, No. 1 -- tonic version (mm. 1-2) and dominant version (mm. 3-4), statement-response repetition.

**Example 2** (p. 63, Ex. 2.6): Mozart, K. 330, i -- exact repetition with identical harmonic support.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Identifying a cadence at the end of the presentation phrase
  **Correction**: Even with V-I at mm. 3-4, no cadence occurs; b.i. repetition reinforces initiation

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking sequential repetition is common in presentations
  **Clarification**: Sequential repetition is rare because tonic prolongation is required; exact and statement-response are far more common
