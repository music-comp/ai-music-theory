# Quick Definition
Development sections are the most analytically challenging part of sonata form due to the enormous variety of techniques employed; when standard categories fail, an ad hoc descriptive approach may be necessary.

# Core Definition
"Formal analysis of development sections can be extremely difficult, since the variety of techniques employed by the classical composers is enormous and highly diverse" (p. 484). The generalities offered "only begin to scratch the surface." Analysts should "be prepared to encounter situations that do not entirely conform" and "be flexible when using the tools." When standard labels fail, "adopt an ad hoc approach and simply describe, as best as you can, the general harmonic/tonal plan and the grouping structure."

# Key Properties
1. Development analysis can be extremely difficult
2. Variety of techniques is enormous and diverse
3. Standard categories may not apply to all cases
4. Flexibility is essential
5. Ad hoc description of harmonic plan and grouping structure is acceptable
6. Goal: account for how fundamental units present themselves in time

# Context & Application
This caveat from Caplin is important for preventing analytical paralysis. Not every development section fits neatly into the pre-core/core framework, and forcing inadequate labels is worse than honest descriptive analysis.
