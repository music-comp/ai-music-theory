## Three-Key Exposition

A **three-key exposition** occurs when the exposition moves through three distinct tonal areas rather than the standard two (home key and subordinate key).

### Standard vs. Three-Key Exposition

**Standard Exposition**:
- Home key (HK) → Subordinate key (SK)
- Subordinate key confirmed by PAC

**Three-Key Exposition**:
- Home key (HK) → Intermediate key → Final subordinate key (SK)
- The intermediate key may be tonicized but not fully confirmed
- Final key receives the confirming PAC

### Common Scenarios

In minor-mode movements, the three-key exposition often involves:
- Home key (minor)
- Relative major (III) - often a "false" subordinate key
- Minor dominant (v) - the true subordinate key

### Analytical Considerations

When analyzing a three-key exposition:
- Identify which key receives the essential PAC
- Determine whether intermediate keys are truly confirmed or merely tonicized
- Consider how the tonal plan affects the recapitulation
