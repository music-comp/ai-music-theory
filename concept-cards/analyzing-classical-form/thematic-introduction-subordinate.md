# Quick Definition
A subordinate theme may be preceded by a brief thematic introduction of two types: (1) a dominant prolongation continuing from the transition's end, resolved to tonic at the theme's structural beginning; or (2) an anacrusis-like introduction where tonic harmony and a new accompanimental pattern start on the downbeat but the basic idea begins later in the measure, making the next downbeat the theme's true "first" bar.

# Core Definition
The structural beginning of a subordinate theme may be preceded by a brief thematic introduction. Two types are common: (1) the underlying harmony is the dominant, prolonged from the end of the transition and resolved to tonic at the beginning of the subordinate theme; (2) tonic harmony of the subordinate key and a new accompanimental pattern start on the downbeat of the measure following the transition's end, but the basic idea begins later in that measure, so that the downbeat of the next measure must be seen as the actual "first" bar of the theme. The first type connects the transition's ending directly to the subordinate theme's beginning through dominant prolongation. The second type (anacrusis-like introduction) is rarely encountered with main themes, though Mozart's Symphony No. 40 in G minor is a notable exception (Caplin, Ch. 12, p. 422).

# Key Properties
1. Two types: dominant prolongation and anacrusis-like introduction
2. Type 1: dominant from transition resolved to tonic at theme's start
3. Type 2: tonic + accompaniment on downbeat, basic idea enters later
4. Type 2: next downbeat is the theme's true "first" bar
5. Type 2 is rare with main themes (exception: Mozart Symphony No. 40)
6. Both types precede the structural beginning of the theme

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Check whether the subordinate theme's basic idea begins on the downbeat or later
2. If a new accompanimental pattern and tonic harmony precede the basic idea's entrance: anacrusis-like introduction
3. If dominant harmony continues from transition into the measure before the theme: dominant-prolongation introduction
4. Determine the true structural downbeat of the theme

# Context & Application
Thematic introductions to subordinate themes are subtler than introductions to main themes. The anacrusis-like type is particularly easy to overlook in analysis but can affect how one counts measures and identifies phrase structure. Recognizing these introductions is important for correctly locating the structural beginning of the subordinate theme.
