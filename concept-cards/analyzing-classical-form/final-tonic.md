# Quick Definition
The root-position tonic harmony that concludes an authentic cadential progression, representing the harmonic goal and point of cadential arrival.

# Core Definition
In an authentic cadential progression, the final tonic is the root-position tonic that concludes the progression. Both the dominant and final tonic harmonies must be in root position for an authentic cadential progression. If the final tonic is inverted or replaced by a substitute, a deceptive cadential progression arises: VI replacing I creates a standard deceptive resolution; I6 replacing I creates a deceptive variant (Caplin, pp. 37, 40-41).

# Prerequisites
- **Cadential progression** — The final tonic is the goal of an authentic cadential progression
- **Harmonic inversion** — Root position vs. first inversion determines cadential validity

# Key Properties
1. Must be in root position (I, not I6) for authentic cadential closure
2. Follows root-position dominant (V or V7)
3. Represents the harmonic goal of the cadential progression
4. Creates maximum stability and closure
5. When altered: VI instead of I = deceptive; I6 instead of I = deceptive variant

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate the tonic that concludes a cadential progression
2. Verify root position (scale degree 1 in bass)
3. Confirm it follows a root-position dominant
4. If inverted or substituted, classify as deceptive cadential progression

# Context & Application
The contrast between initial tonic (typically I6) and final tonic (I in root position) articulates the directional quality of cadential motion. The instability of first inversion at the outset gives way to the maximum stability of root position at the conclusion, creating the sense of harmonic arrival that defines authentic cadential closure.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 37): Example 1.11 shows the basic authentic cadential progression ending with root-position final tonic: I6-II6-V7-I.

**Example 2** (pp. 40-41): Example 1.16 shows what happens when the final tonic is altered: (a-c) VI replaces I (deceptive); (d-e) I6 replaces I (deceptive variant); (f) V7/IV replaces I.

# Relationships

- **Tonal confirmation** — Root-position final tonic provides the strongest tonal confirmation
## Related
- **Penultimate dominant** — Immediately precedes the final tonic

## Contrasts With
- **Initial tonic** — Initial tonic (I6) signals onset; final tonic (I) provides arrival

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating I6 as equivalent to I for cadential purposes
  **Correction**: Only root-position I creates authentic cadential arrival; I6 produces a deceptive variant

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Not recognizing that deceptive progressions arise from altering the final tonic
  **Clarification**: Deceptive cadential progressions result specifically from replacing or inverting the expected final tonic
