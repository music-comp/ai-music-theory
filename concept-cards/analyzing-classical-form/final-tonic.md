# Quick Definition
The root-position tonic harmony that concludes an authentic cadential progression, representing the harmonic goal and point of arrival.

# Formal Definition
In an authentic cadential progression, the final tonic is the root-position tonic harmony that concludes the progression. Both the dominant and final tonic harmonies must be placed in root position for an authentic cadential progression. If the final tonic is inverted or otherwise altered harmonically, a deceptive cadential progression arises.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Requirements for final tonic:
- Must be in root position (I, not I6)
- Follows root-position dominant (V or V7)
- Represents the harmonic goal of the cadential progression
- Creates maximum stability and closure

When altered:
- I6 instead of I → deceptive cadential progression
- VI instead of I → standard deceptive resolution
- V7/IV instead of I → converts to secondary dominant

The final tonic's root position creates the stability and closure that defines authentic cadential arrival.

# Musical Context/Application
The final tonic:
- Marks the cadential arrival in authentic cadences
- Creates the sense of harmonic conclusion
- Must be preceded by root-position dominant
- Represents the most stable harmonic state

The contrast between initial tonic (I6) and final tonic (I) articulates the directional quality of cadential motion.

# Examples
Example 1.11 (p. 37): The basic authentic cadential progression ends with root-position final tonic: I6 - II6 - V7 - I.

Example 1.16 (pp. 40-41) shows what happens when the final tonic is altered:
- (a-c) VI replaces I (deceptive)
- (d-e) I6 replaces I (deceptive variant)
- (f) V7/IV replaces I (creates instability)

# Common Confusions
- Treating I6 as equivalent to I for cadential purposes (only I creates authentic arrival)
- Not recognizing that deceptive progressions arise from altering the final tonic
- Confusing final tonic with any tonic following dominant
- Overlooking the contrast between initial tonic (I6) and final tonic (I)
