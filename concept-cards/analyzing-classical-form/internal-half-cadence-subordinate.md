# Quick Definition
An internal half cadence (or internal dominant arrival) occurs when a subordinate theme seems to end with dominant harmony rather than the required PAC. Since more subordinate-theme material must follow to achieve the PAC, the HC is "internal" to a single theme. It is often motivated by a nonmodulating or ending-less transition that left the SK dominant unemphasized.

# Core Definition
Sometimes a subordinate theme seems to end with an HC or dominant arrival, which may be prolonged by a standing on the dominant. Since a more conclusive PAC must eventually follow, we speak of an **internal** half cadence within a single subordinate theme. An internal HC is often explained by how the transition ends: if the transition is nonmodulating (lacking emphasized SK dominant) or lacks an ending function, the internal HC rectifies this by providing the subordinate-key dominant emphasis. Two main strategies follow: (1) **resumption of continuation or cadential function** leading to PAC; (2) establishment of a **new initiating function** to begin the second part of a two-part subordinate theme (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 398-403).

# Key Properties
1. Subordinate theme seems to end with HC but more material must follow (PAC required)
2. HC is "internal" to a single subordinate theme
3. Often motivated by nonmodulating transition or transition lacking ending function
4. Two follow-up strategies: (a) continuation/cadential resumption, (b) new initiation (two-part theme)
5. Standing on the dominant may follow the internal HC
6. Most expositions feature SK dominant emphasis somewhere before the final PAC

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. A subordinate-theme passage ends on dominant with standing on the dominant
2. But no PAC has yet occurred in the subordinate key
3. More subordinate-theme material follows
4. Determine whether what follows is continuation/cadential (single theme) or new initiation (two-part theme)
