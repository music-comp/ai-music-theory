# Quick Definition
The sentence is an 8-m. theme built from two phrases: a 4-m. presentation phrase (b.i. + repetition, tonic prolongation, no cadence) followed by a 4-m. continuation phrase (fragmentation + cadence, fusing continuation and cadential functions). It is the most important theme type in classical music.

# Core Definition
The sentence is undoubtedly the most important theme type in classical music (p. 55). It is an 8-m. theme built from two phrases: a 4-m. presentation phrase and a 4-m. continuation phrase (p. 56). The presentation begins with a 2-m. basic idea that prolongs tonic harmony, then immediately repeats it (exact, statement-response, or sequential). The continuation phrase destabilizes the presentation through fragmentation, harmonic acceleration, increased surface rhythm, and/or sequential progressions, then closes with a cadence (p. 58). The sentence contains three formal functions: presentation (beginning), continuation (middle), and cadential (ending). The continuation and cadential functions are fused within the single continuation phrase; it is labeled "continuation phrase" because continuation function is usually more salient throughout the entire phrase than cadential function, which normally appears later (p. 57).

# Key Properties
1. Most important theme type in classical music
2. 8 measures: presentation (4 m.) + continuation (4 m.)
3. Presentation: b.i. (2 m.) + repetition (2 m.) + tonic prolongation
4. Continuation: fragmentation + cadence (fusing continuation and cadential functions)
5. Three formal functions: presentation, continuation, cadential
6. Continuation and cadential fused in second phrase
7. Can end with any cadence type: PAC (most common), HC, IAC (rare)
8. One cadence only (unlike the period's two)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for a 2-m. basic idea immediately repeated in mm. 3-4
2. Verify tonic prolongation supports the first 4 measures
3. Check for continuation characteristics starting at m. 5 (fragmentation, acceleration)
4. Identify the cadence at the end of the theme
5. Confirm only ONE cadence (two cadences suggest a period)

# Context & Application
The sentence projects the character of mobility and forward drive because of the open-ended nature of the presentation and the destabilizations offered by the continuation (p. 115). This contrasts with the period's character of balance and repose. The sentence was first identified by Schoenberg early in the 20th century (p. 57). Key differences from the period: the sentence has one cadence (period has two); the b.i. is repeated within the presentation (in the period, the b.i. returns at the start of the consequent); the sentence can end with HC (the period cannot end with HC) (p. 114).

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 56, Ex. 2.1): Beethoven, Op. 2, No. 1, i -- the most exemplary sentence: b.i. (mm. 1-2), dominant-version repetition (mm. 3-4), fragmentation to 1-m. units (mm. 5-6), HC at m. 8.
**Example 2** (p. 60, Ex. 2.2): Mozart, Rondo K. 485 -- another standard sentence.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking the continuation phrase has only continuation function
  **Correction**: It fuses continuation AND cadential functions; the label reflects the more salient function

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing the sentence with the period because both are 8-m. themes with two phrases
  **Clarification**: Sentence: b.i. repeated in mm. 3-4, one cadence; period: c.i. in mm. 3-4, two cadences
