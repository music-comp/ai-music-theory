## Main Theme Basics

The **main theme** presents the principal melodic-motivic material of the sonata form in a stable tonal and phrase-structural context.

### Tonal Stability

Tonal stability is achieved by:
- Beginning and ending in the home key
- Always closing with one of the three cadence types:
  - Most often: PAC
  - Sometimes: HC
  - Rarely: IAC

### Phrase-Structural Stability

Phrase-structural stability is achieved by the main theme acquiring a generally **tight-knit organization** relative to the more loosely organized transition and subordinate theme.

### Theme Types

Many main themes are structured as conventional theme types:
- **Sentence**: Particularly suitable for opening a sonata exposition (dynamic momentum toward a single cadence)
- **Period**: Used less often (symmetrical phrasing emphasizing closure)
- **Small ternary**: Relatively uncommon
- **Small binary**: Rarely begins a sonata-form movement

### Framing Functions

If the main theme ends with a PAC, a **closing section** (codettas) may follow. A main theme ending with an HC may lead to a **standing on the dominant**. A main theme may also be preceded by a **thematic introduction**.
