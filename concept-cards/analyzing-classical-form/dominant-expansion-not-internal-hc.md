## Dominant Expansion vs. Internal HC

Not every passage involving expanded dominant harmony in a subordinate theme can be considered an internal HC with standing on the dominant. For this identification, the dominant must be the **ultimate** harmony of an ongoing cadential progression.

### Penultimate vs. Ultimate Dominant

**Penultimate dominant** (in ECP):
- Part of an expanded cadential progression
- Will resolve to tonic to complete the cadence
- NOT an internal HC

**Ultimate dominant** (internal HC):
- The harmonic goal itself
- Creates sense of ending/closure
- Standing on the dominant may follow

### Passing Dominant Expansion

Sometimes a dominant is neither ultimate nor penultimate:
- Undergoes major expansion
- But functions as **passing chord** in a larger pattern
- Example: vii°7 (dominant function) expanded 9 measures, but passes between I and I6
- Not an internal HC

### Dramatic Gesture vs. Structural Function

A highly dramatic gesture on expanded diminished-seventh (dominant function):
- Harmonic stasis on dissonant sonority
- Violent disruptions of dynamics and register
- Creates powerful expectations for resolution
- But NOT necessarily an internal HC or standing on the dominant

The distinction depends on whether the harmony is the **goal** or merely a **passing element**.
