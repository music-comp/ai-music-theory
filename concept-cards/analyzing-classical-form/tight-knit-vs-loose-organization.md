## Tight-Knit vs. Loose Organization

The distinction between **tight-knit** and **loose** formal organization is of major prominence throughout the study of sonata form.

### Key Principle

The main theme defines a standard of tight-knit organization for the movement as a whole, to which all other sections can be compared as more or less loosely organized.

### Relative, Not Absolute

The expression of tight-knit and loose form is not absolute but always **relative** to a given movement. What matters is the degree to which a main theme is tight-knit in relation to the units that follow (transition, subordinate theme, development section).

### Tight-Knit Characteristics

- Harmonic and tonal stability
- Clear establishment of home-key tonic
- Cadential confirmation
- Unified melodic material (motives from basic idea)
- Symmetrical grouping structure
- Compact, efficient functional expression

### Loose Characteristics

- Harmonic and tonal instability
- Asymmetrical grouping structure
- Extended or expanded functions
- Redundancy in functional expression
- Modulation and chromaticism

### Application to Sonata Form

| Section | Organization |
|---------|--------------|
| Main Theme | Tight-knit (reference point) |
| Transition | Looser |
| Subordinate Theme | Loosest |
| Development | Very loose |
