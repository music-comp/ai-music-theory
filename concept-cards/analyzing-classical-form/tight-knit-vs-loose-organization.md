# Quick Definition
A fundamental distinction (first observed by Schoenberg) between structural stability (tight-knit) and instability (loose), determined by seven parameters. The main theme of a sonata defines a standard of tight-knit organization for the movement, to which all other sections are compared as more or less loosely organized. The distinction is always relative, never absolute.

# Core Definition
As a general rule, the main theme defines a standard of tight-knit organization for the movement as a whole. The expression of tight-knit and loose form is not absolute but always **relative** to a given movement. What matters is the degree to which a given main theme is tight-knit in relation to the units that follow (transition, subordinate theme, development) and how these units are rendered looser in relation to that initial main theme (Caplin, Ch. 9, p. 286).

# Key Properties
## Seven Parameters (from Ch. 7, pp. 225-227)
1. **Tonality**: home key (tight) vs. modulating/distant keys (loose)
2. **Harmony**: tonic prolongation (tight) vs. sequential/chromatic (loose)
3. **Cadence**: PAC (tight) vs. no cadence (loose)
4. **Grouping structure**: symmetrical 4+4 (tight) vs. asymmetrical (loose)
5. **Functional efficiency**: efficient (tight) vs. redundant with extensions (loose)
6. **Motivic material**: uniform (tight) vs. diverse (loose)
7. **Thematic conventionality**: period (most tight-knit) vs. nonconventional (loose)

## General Principles
- Period is the most tight-knit conventional type; sentence is less so
- The distinction is always RELATIVE to the given movement
- Comparing a main theme to other movements' main themes has little analytical worth
- Structural loosening does NOT reflect compositional weakness -- deft handling is a sign of skill
- Parameters may conflict (some tight, some loose) within a single passage
- First observed by Arnold Schoenberg
- A section of small ternary: tight-knit; B section: loose; A': variable

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Analyze the main theme first to establish the movement's tight-knit standard
2. Compare subsequent sections (transition, subordinate theme) to that standard
3. Note specific loosening devices: extensions, asymmetry, harmonic instability, modal shifts
4. Recognize that loosening is compositionally appropriate for transition and subordinate-theme functions

# Context & Application
This distinction, first introduced in Chapter 7 on small ternary, assumes major prominence throughout the study of sonata form. Loosening techniques are entirely appropriate to the transition's functions (destabilizing the home key, effecting modulation) and the subordinate theme's functions (dramatizing cadential confirmation).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating tight-knit vs. loose as an absolute quality
  **Correction**: The distinction is always relative to the specific movement being analyzed
- **Error**: Treating tight-knit and loose as absolute categories
  **Correction**: Every case is relative to the compositional setting; a sentence may be tight in one context and loose in another

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking loosening implies compositional weakness
  **Clarification**: Loosening techniques are entirely appropriate and their skillful deployment is a sign of mastery
