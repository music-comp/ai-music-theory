# Quick Definition
Voice-leading procedures that generate chords through melodic motion (passing, neighboring) rather than through harmonic functions, often producing weakly functional or nonfunctional sonorities.

# Core Definition
Contrapuntal processes are compositional techniques where harmonic content arises from the motion of individual melodic lines rather than from functional harmonic relationships. Some chords arise "primarily from contrapuntal processes and only minimally from harmonic ones" (p. 35). Such chords may violate standard voice-leading rules (e.g., doubled and incorrectly resolved sevenths) because they are products of melodic patterns, not harmonic syntax.

# Prerequisites
- **Voice leading** — Contrapuntal processes are fundamentally voice-leading phenomena
- **Passing motion** — Passing chords are a primary product of contrapuntal processes
- **Neighbor motion** — Neighboring chords similarly arise from contrapuntal motion

# Key Properties
1. Chords arise from stepwise voice motion, not from functional harmonic relationships
2. Resulting chords may lack genuine harmonic function
3. Voice-leading may not follow standard rules (doubled sevenths, incorrect resolutions)
4. Such chords are labeled with quality symbols (m7, °7) rather than Roman numerals
5. Passing ("p") and neighboring ("n") labels indicate contrapuntal origin

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Examine whether the chord can be explained by stepwise motion in the voices
2. Check for voice-leading irregularities (doubled dissonances, unresolved sevenths)
3. If the chord lacks clear functional relationship to surrounding harmonies, consider contrapuntal origin
4. Use quality symbols rather than Roman numerals for contrapuntally derived chords

# Context & Application
The distinction between harmonically and contrapuntally generated chords is crucial for accurate analysis. Contrapuntal processes create texture and melodic interest, generate subordinate harmonies in prolongational progressions, and explain apparent voice-leading "errors" in certain chords. When a chord arises from counterpoint, assigning it a Roman numeral would misrepresent its role.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 35): "The passing chord in Example 1.9c arises entirely out of the counterpoint and thus should not be analyzed as a II7 harmony: the so-called seventh (C) is doubled and incorrectly resolved."

**Example 2** (p. 35): The chapter notes that some passing chords "arise primarily from contrapuntal processes and only minimally from harmonic ones," receiving quality labels like (m7) instead of Roman numerals.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Analyzing contrapuntal passing chords with Roman numerals
  **Correction**: Use chord quality symbols (m7, °7) when the chord lacks genuine harmonic function

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Treating all chords as harmonically functional
  **Clarification**: Some chords arise from voice-leading motion and should be analyzed as contrapuntal rather than harmonic events
