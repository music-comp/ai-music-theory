# Quick Definition
Voice-leading procedures that generate chords through melodic motion (passing, neighboring) rather than through purely harmonic functions, often resulting in weakly-functional or nonfunctional sonorities.

# Formal Definition
Contrapuntal processes are compositional techniques that generate harmonic content through the motion of individual melodic lines (counterpoint) rather than through functional harmonic relationships. Some chords arise "primarily from contrapuntal processes and only minimally from harmonic ones."

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Contrapuntal origin of harmonies:
- Passing chords arising from stepwise voice motion
- Neighboring chords arising from neighbor-tone motion
- Chords resulting from voice-leading procedures (5-6, 7-6)
- Sonorities created by suspensions and other melodic embellishments

When contrapuntally derived:
- Chords may lack genuine harmonic function
- Voice-leading may not follow standard rules (doubled sevenths, incorrect resolutions)
- Labeled with chord quality (m7, °7) rather than Roman numerals
- Receive "p" or "n" labels in analysis

# Musical Context/Application
Contrapuntal processes:
- Create texture and melodic interest
- Generate subordinate harmonies in prolongational progressions
- Solve voice-leading problems in sequences
- Explain "incorrect" voice leading in certain chords

The distinction between harmonic and contrapuntal origins is important for accurate analysis.

# Examples
Example 1.9c (p. 35): "The passing chord arises entirely out of the counterpoint and thus should not be analyzed as a II7 harmony."

The chapter notes that some passing chords "arise primarily from contrapuntal processes and only minimally from harmonic ones" (p. 35).

# Common Confusions
- Treating all chords as harmonically functional
- Analyzing contrapuntal passing chords with Roman numerals
- Not recognizing voice-leading origins of certain sonorities
- Applying standard voice-leading rules to contrapuntally-derived chords
