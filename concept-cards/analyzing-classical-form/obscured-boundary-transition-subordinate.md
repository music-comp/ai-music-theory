## Obscured Boundary Between Transition and Subordinate Theme

Most sonata-form expositions (especially Mozart and Beethoven) feature an unmistakable demarcation of transition and subordinate theme. In some expositions (especially Haydn), the boundary is **obscured**.

### Clear Boundary Markers

Normally present:
- Transition clearly ends with HC (or dominant arrival)
- Subordinate theme begins with obvious initiating function (presentation, CBI)
- Medial caesura aids in articulation

### Three Situations Creating Obscured Boundaries

**1. Transition lacks concluding function**:
- No clear sense of ending
- Subordinate theme must begin with clearly defined initiating function to signal boundary

**2. Subordinate theme lacks initiating function**:
- No clear sense of beginning
- Sufficient contrast in texture and rhythm must articulate boundary

**3. Both lacking**:
- Neither clear end nor clear beginning
- May be impossible to determine where one function ends and other begins

### Analytical Implications

When boundaries are obscured:
- Harmonic considerations should normally take preference
- Prolongation of SK: I projects stronger initiation than SK: I6
- Retrospective understanding often required
