# Quick Definition
The intervening chords (neighboring, passing, or substitute harmonies) that serve to extend a prolonged harmony within a prolongational progression, notated in parentheses.

# Core Definition
Subordinate harmonies are the intervening chords within a prolongational progression that have a meaning different from the prolonged harmony. They serve to sustain the prolonged harmony through time by functioning as neighboring, passing, or substitute harmonies. Their structural status is reduced compared to the prolonged harmony. Within pedal-point situations, "the bass note of subordinate harmonies is replaced by the pedal note," significantly reducing their structural status (Caplin, pp. 25-26, 33).

# Prerequisites
- **Prolongational progression** — Subordinate harmonies exist within prolongational progressions
- **Prolonged harmony** — Subordinate harmonies embellish the prolonged harmony

# Key Properties
1. Three types: neighboring chords, passing chords, substitute harmonies
2. Placed in parentheses in analytical notation
3. Structural status is reduced compared to the prolonged harmony
4. In pedal-point contexts, cannot be assigned a specific inversion
5. May include dominant, subdominant, and other functional chords
6. Lose much of their independent functional status when subordinate

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate the prolonged harmony within a prolongational progression
2. Identify chords that embellish the prolonged harmony
3. Classify as neighboring (same position), passing (position change), or substitute
4. Place in parentheses in analytical notation
5. In pedal-point contexts, note that inversion cannot be determined

# Context & Application
Subordinate harmonies are placed in parentheses to highlight the prolonged harmony. In pedal-point contexts, their bass voice is replaced by the pedal, making it impossible to determine their inversion. The distinction between subordinate and prolonged harmonies is essential for understanding harmonic rhythm and phrase structure. Some subordinate harmonies may stand outside parentheses when they significantly contribute to harmonic rhythm.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Prolongational progression** — Subordinate harmonies are components of prolongational progressions
## Enables
- **Harmonic prolongation** — Subordinate harmonies enable the prolonged harmony to persist through time
## Related
- **Neighbor motion** — Neighboring chords are one type of subordinate harmony
- **Passing motion** — Passing chords are another type
- **Pedal point** — Pedal points affect how subordinate harmonies are analyzed
## Contrasts With
- **Prolonged harmony** — Subordinate harmonies embellish; the prolonged harmony is structural

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Analyzing subordinate harmonies as having full functional weight
  **Correction**: Their structural status is reduced; they serve the prolonged harmony

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assigning inversions to subordinate harmonies within pedal points
  **Clarification**: When the bass is replaced by the pedal note, the inversion of subordinate harmonies cannot be determined
