# Quick Definition
A cadence becomes "abandoned" when the cadential root-position dominant is inverted or eliminated, causing the cadential progression to lose direction. Unlike an evaded cadence, an abandoned cadence typically leads to new continuation function (not "one more time" repetition), and its aesthetic effect is less dramatic -- the music wanders off before eventually returning to a genuine cadential attempt.

# Core Definition
A genuine cadence fails to materialize when the cadential root-position dominant is inverted or eliminated. Three methods: (1) dominant initially root-position but altered to first/second inversion before resolution; (2) expected dominant appears initially inverted; (3) dominant omitted entirely. Following an abandoned cadence, the music typically acquires **new continuation function** (OMT technique tends not to apply). Eventually a genuine cadential progression appears. The aesthetic effect is **less dramatic** than an evaded cadence -- the progression loses its sense of direction and the music "wanders off" -- but the resulting formal loosening is appropriate for subordinate themes (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 395-396).

# Key Properties
1. Cadential dominant is inverted or eliminated (not merely evaded)
2. Three methods: late inversion, initial inversion, omission
3. Followed by NEW continuation (not OMT repetition)
4. Less dramatic than evaded cadence
5. Cadential progression loses direction; music "wanders off"
6. Important exception: V7 before I6 in evaded cadence is NOT an abandoned cadence
