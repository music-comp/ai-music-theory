## Abandoned Cadence in Subordinate Theme

A genuine cadence can fail to materialize if the cadential root-position dominant is **inverted or eliminated**. A promised cadence becomes "abandoned."

### Methods of Abandonment

**1. Dominant inverted after initial appearance**:
- Dominant initially appears in root position (as expected)
- Then altered to first or second inversion before resolving to tonic

**2. Dominant initially inverted**:
- Expected cadential dominant appears initially in inversion
- Cannot function as true cadential dominant

**3. Dominant omitted entirely**:
- Cadential progression skips the dominant altogether

### Important Exception

When the root-position dominant moves to V7 before proceeding to I6 in an **evaded cadence**:
- The chordal seventh in bass is a "passing tone"
- V7 is understood as embellishment of root-position dominant
- This is **not** an abandoned cadence - it's an evaded cadence

### What Follows

After an abandoned cadence:
- Music typically acquires a **new continuation function**
- "One more time" technique tends **not** to be associated with abandonment
- Eventually a genuine cadential progression appears leading to PAC

### Aesthetic Effect

The abandoned cadence is **less dramatic** than evaded cadence:
- Cadential progression loses sense of direction
- Music wanders off somewhere else
- But the resulting formal loosening is appropriate for subordinate themes
