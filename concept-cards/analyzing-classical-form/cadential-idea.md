# Quick Definition
The final 2-m. unit of a sentence (mm. 7-8), supported by a cadential progression. Features conventional, formulaic melodic content resulting from liquidation of the b.i.'s characteristic motives. May stand apart from preceding material or emerge directly from continuational processes.

# Core Definition
The final unit of the phrase, the cadential idea, is supported by a cadential progression. The melodic-motivic material of the theme is often liquidated toward the end of the continuation phrase, such that the content of the cadential idea is fairly formulaic, rarely resembling the basic idea (p. 58). The cadential idea can be identified by its unique formal function (p. 62).

# Prerequisites
- **Cadential function** — The c.i. expresses cadential function
- **Liquidation** — The c.i. typically results from motivic liquidation

# Key Properties
1. Final 2-m. unit of the sentence (typically mm. 7-8)
2. Supported by cadential progression
3. Melodic content: conventional, formulaic, descending
4. Rarely resembles the b.i. (result of liquidation)
5. May stand apart or emerge directly from continuational processes
6. General contour: descending (reflecting "cadere" = to fall)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate the final 2 measures of the sentence
2. Check for cadential progression support
3. Note the conventional, descending melodic material
4. Compare with b.i.: should be markedly different (liquidated)

# Context & Application
The c.i. provides formulaic closure material, contrasting with the characteristic opening of the b.i. Standard cadence formulas include descending melodic patterns (3-to-1, 5-to-1) and galant patterns. In some themes the c.i. is distinct; in others, cadential material grows naturally from the continuation.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 79, Ex. 2.22): Standard classical cadence formulas: PAC with 3-to-1 descent, 5-to-1 descent, galant cadence, Prinner pattern, phrygian HC.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon

- **Liquidation** — The c.i. is the result of motivic liquidation
## Related
- **Contrasting idea** — In periods, the c.i. may overlap with the c.i.
## Contrasts With
- **Basic idea** — Characteristic/ascending vs. conventional/descending

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Expecting the c.i. to resemble the b.i.
  **Correction**: The c.i. is typically formulaic and conventional, unlike the characteristic b.i.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing the c.i. with a contrasting idea (in periods)
  **Clarification**: The c.i. is defined by cadential function; contrasting idea is defined by contrast with b.i.
