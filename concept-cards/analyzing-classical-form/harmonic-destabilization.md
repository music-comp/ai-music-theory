# Quick Definition
The process of loosening tonal stability through sequential progressions, creating movement away from or between tonal centers.

# Core Definition
Harmonic destabilization occurs through sequential progressions, which "destabilize harmonic activity by bringing a consistent pattern of root motion" (p. 27). Individual harmonies within the sequence lack clear functional relation to the prevailing key, as the pattern-based root motion overrides functional relationships. "Sequential progressions are thus especially appropriate for destabilizing harmonic activity within a given key or for effecting a modulation from one key to another" (p. 42).

# Prerequisites
- **Prolongational progression** — Understanding stability (prolongation) is needed to understand its opposite (destabilization)
- **Cadential progression** — Cadential progressions confirm tonality; sequences destabilize it

# Key Properties
1. Consistent intervallic patterns between roots override functional relationships
2. Individual harmonies within the sequence lack clear functional relation to the key
3. May lead to a new tonal center (modulation) or return to the original key
4. Initial and final harmonies of the sequence retain functional meaning
5. Intermediate harmonies serve the pattern rather than harmonic function

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for a series of chords following a consistent root-motion pattern
2. Verify that individual chords lack independent functional weight
3. Note where the passage begins (stable context) and ends (new key or return)
4. Classify by root-motion type (descending fifth, ascending second, etc.)

# Context & Application
Harmonic destabilization creates contrast with stable prolongational and cadential passages. It facilitates modulation to new keys, appears between prolongational and cadential sections, and is essential for development sections and transitions. Sequential progressions represent the primary means of destabilization within the classical harmonic system.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Sequential progression** — Sequences are the primary mechanism of destabilization
## Enables
- **Modulation** — Destabilization facilitates movement between tonal centers
## Related
- **Root motion** — Consistent root-motion patterns drive the destabilizing effect
- **Circle of fifths** — The descending-fifth sequence is the most common destabilizing pattern
## Contrasts With
- **Tonal confirmation** — Cadential progressions confirm; sequences destabilize
- **Prolonged harmony** — Prolongation maintains stability; destabilization loosens it

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating sequential harmonies as fully functional
  **Correction**: Within sequences, individual harmonies serve the pattern rather than expressing independent harmonic function

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming destabilization equals modulation
  **Clarification**: Destabilization may lead to modulation or may return to the original key; the two are not synonymous
