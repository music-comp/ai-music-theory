# Quick Definition
The process of loosening tonal stability through sequential progressions, creating movement away from or between tonal centers.

# Formal Definition
Harmonic destabilization occurs through sequential progressions, which "destabilize harmonic activity by bringing a consistent pattern of root motion." Sequential progressions are "especially appropriate for destabilizing harmonic activity within a given key or for effecting a modulation from one key to another."

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Destabilization through sequences:
- Pattern-based root motion overrides functional relationships
- Individual harmonies within the sequence lack clear functional relation
- Creates sense of harmonic "drift" or movement
- May lead to new tonal center (modulation) or return to original key

Characteristics of destabilized passages:
- Consistent intervallic patterns between roots
- Reduced functional weight of individual harmonies
- Initial and final harmonies retain functional meaning
- Intermediate harmonies serve pattern rather than function

# Musical Context/Application
Harmonic destabilization:
- Creates contrast with stable, cadential passages
- Facilitates modulation to new keys
- Appears between prolongational and cadential sections
- Essential for development sections and transitions

Sequential progressions represent the primary means of destabilization within the classical harmonic system.

# Common Confusions
- Treating sequential harmonies as fully functional
- Assuming destabilization equals modulation (it may return to original key)
- Not recognizing the formal role of destabilization
- Confusing harmonic instability with chromatic complexity
