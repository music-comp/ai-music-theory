# Quick Definition
The period is an 8-m. theme type built from two 4-m. phrases: an antecedent (b.i. + c.i. + weak cadence) followed by a consequent (b.i. return + c.i. + strong cadence). It contains two formal functions (antecedent and consequent) but no distinct medial function. Its essential feature is cadential differentiation: weak to strong.

# Core Definition
The period has at its basis the idea that a musical unit of weak cadential closure is repeated so as to produce stronger cadential closure; as a result, the two units group together to form a higher-level whole (p. 95). Like the sentence, the period is an 8-m. theme built out of two 4-m. phrases: a 4-m. antecedent phrase followed by a 4-m. consequent phrase (p. 95). The labels antecedent and consequent refer to both the phrases and the two formal functions of the period: an initiating antecedent and a concluding consequent. Unlike the sentence, the period does not bring a specific medial formal function (p. 95). Caplin (following Schoenberg) restricts "period" to cases where the consequent begins with similar material and exhibits weak-to-strong cadential differentiation (p. 98).

# Prerequisites
- **Basic idea** — Both antecedent and consequent begin with a b.i.
- **Contrasting idea** — Follows the b.i. in both phrases
- **Cadence classification** — Must understand weak vs. strong cadences

# Key Properties
1. 8 measures: antecedent (4 m.) + consequent (4 m.)
2. Antecedent: b.i. (2 m.) + c.i. (2 m.) + weak cadence (HC or IAC)
3. Consequent: b.i. return (2 m.) + c.i. (2 m.) + strong cadence (usually PAC)
4. Two formal functions only: antecedent (initiating) and consequent (concluding)
5. No medial function (unlike the sentence)
6. Essential relationship: weak cadence followed by strong cadence
7. Consequent "repeats" antecedent with stronger closure
8. Projects balance and repose through symmetrical construction

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for two cadences within 8 measures
2. Verify weak-to-strong cadential differentiation (HC/IAC then PAC)
3. Check that m. 5 brings a return of the opening basic idea
4. Confirm b.i. + c.i. structure in both phrases
5. Distinguish from sentence (sentence has one cadence; period has two)

# Context & Application
The period projects the character of balance and repose owing to the similar construction of antecedent and consequent, both of which bring a degree of cadential closure (p. 115). This contrasts with the sentence's forward-driving character. Key differences from the sentence: the period has two cadences (sentence has one); the period cannot end with HC (sentence can end with any cadence); in the period, the b.i. returns at m. 5 (in the sentence, the b.i. is repeated at m. 3) (p. 114).

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 96, Ex. 3.1): Mozart, K. 525, ii -- b.i. (mm. 1-2), c.i. with HC (mm. 3-4), b.i. return (mm. 5-6), c.i. with PAC (mm. 7-8).
**Example 2** (p. 98, Ex. 3.2): Mozart, K. 311, ii -- another standard 8-m. period.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Calling any two-phrase theme a "period"
  **Correction**: Period requires specific structure: b.i. + c.i. + weak cadence, then b.i. return + c.i. + strong cadence; other two-phrase structures may be hybrids

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the period has a medial function
  **Clarification**: Unlike the sentence (which has continuation as medial function), the period contains only antecedent (initiating) and consequent (concluding); no medial function
