# Quick Definition
Bass motion that outlines the chord tones of a harmony through arpeggiated movement, often used to prolong a harmony by connecting root position and inversions.

# Formal Definition
Bass arpeggiation is a technique where the bass voice moves through chord tones of a prolonged harmony, creating melodic interest while maintaining harmonic stability. This motion typically connects different positions of the same chord (root position to inversions) and contributes to prolongational progressions.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Characteristics of bass arpeggiation:
- Outlines chord tones of the prolonged harmony
- Connects different inversions (e.g., I to I6)
- Creates melodic activity without changing harmony
- Contributes to prolongational effect

In the context of harmonic prolongation, bass arpeggiation serves a similar function to neighbor and passing motion, but through leaping rather than stepwise movement.

# Musical Context/Application
Bass arpeggiation:
- Commonly used in Alberti bass patterns and similar accompaniments
- Creates rhythmic vitality while maintaining harmonic stability
- May combine with other prolongational techniques
- Distinguishes between inversion changes within one harmony

The conventional accompaniment pattern called "Alberti bass" consists of arpeggiation figurations.

# Common Confusions
- Confusing arpeggiation (within one harmony) with root motion (between different harmonies)
- Not recognizing arpeggiation as a prolongational technique
- Treating each arpeggiated note as a different harmony
