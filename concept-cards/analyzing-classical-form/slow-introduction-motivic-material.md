# Slow Introduction Motivic Material

The melodic-motivic content of slow introductions and its relationship to the rest of the movement.

## Haydn and Mozart

Many slow introductions present melodic-motivic material having no immediately obvious relation to the rest of the movement.

## Late Works and Beethoven

In some late works of Haydn and Mozart, and in many by Beethoven:
- Slow introduction hints at ideas more fully realized later
- Creates motivic connections across the movement
- Anticipatory motives foreshadow exposition material

## Fanfare Gestures

Common feature - stately, heraldic fanfare motives that may:
- Stand independent of later material
- Be transformed into main theme ideas
- Return at climactic moments

## Anticipatory Techniques

Introduction may end by:
- Anticipating opening harmony of main theme
- Anticipating opening melody of main theme
- Both harmonic and melodic anticipation

These techniques create smooth transition into exposition while maintaining introduction's distinct identity.
