# Quick Definition
The major-minor seventh chord built on the fifth scale degree (V7), the most common form of dominant harmony, with the seventh adding dissonance that intensifies the drive toward tonic resolution.

# Formal Definition
Dominant function is most often represented by the major triad or the major-minor seventh chord built on the fifth scale degree. The dominant seventh (V7) adds a dissonant seventh to the dominant triad, creating a stronger pull toward tonic resolution. This added dissonance is appropriate for the penultimate dominant in authentic cadential progressions.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Characteristics of V7:
- Major triad with minor seventh added
- Contains both leading tone and chordal seventh
- Strong resolution tendency to tonic
- Seventh typically resolves downward by step

Usage in cadential progressions:
- **Authentic cadence**: V7 appropriate (penultimate position)
- **Half cadence**: V7 NOT appropriate (seventh destabilizes ultimate dominant)

The seventh adds "appropriate" dissonance for the penultimate position but would "overly destabilize" the ultimate dominant of a half cadence.

# Musical Context/Application
V7 appears in:
- Authentic cadential progressions (most common)
- Prolongational contexts (as subordinate harmony)
- Sequences (may carry less functional weight)

The chapter emphasizes: "Adding a dissonant seventh, so appropriate for the penultimate position within an authentic cadential progression, would overly destabilize the ultimate dominant of a half-cadential progression" (p. 39-40).

# Examples
Example 1.11 (p. 37): The basic authentic cadential progression shows I6-II6-V7-I.

Example 1.12 (p. 38): V7 combined with cadential six-four embellishment: V(6-4 to 7).

The notation V(6-4 to 7) shows the six-four embellishment resolving to a dominant seventh.

# Common Confusions
- Using V7 in half cadences (the seventh destabilizes the ultimate position)
- Not recognizing the difference between penultimate and ultimate dominant requirements
- Treating all V7 appearances as equally cadential (context matters)
- Forgetting that the seventh must resolve down by step
