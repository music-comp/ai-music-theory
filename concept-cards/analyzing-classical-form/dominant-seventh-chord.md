# Quick Definition
The major-minor seventh chord built on the fifth scale degree (V7), the most common form of dominant harmony, whose dissonant seventh intensifies the drive toward tonic resolution but is inappropriate for the ultimate position in half cadences.

# Core Definition
Dominant function is most often represented by the major triad or major-minor seventh chord built on the fifth scale degree. "Adding a dissonant seventh, so appropriate for the penultimate position within an authentic cadential progression, would overly destabilize the ultimate dominant of a half-cadential progression" (pp. 39-40). The seventh adds a dissonance that enhances cadential drive in authentic progressions but undermines the stability required for half-cadential endings.

# Prerequisites
- **Dominant function** — V7 is the primary representative of dominant function
- **Penultimate dominant** — V7 is appropriate in the penultimate position

# Key Properties
1. Major triad with minor seventh added (major-minor seventh chord)
2. Contains both leading tone and chordal seventh
3. The seventh resolves downward by step
4. Appropriate for penultimate dominant (authentic cadence)
5. NOT appropriate for ultimate dominant (half cadence) -- seventh destabilizes
6. In cadential notation: V(6-4 to 7) shows cadential six-four embellishment resolving to V7

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate a chord built on scale degree 5 with an added seventh
2. Verify the quality: major triad + minor seventh
3. Determine cadential position: penultimate (appropriate) vs. ultimate (inappropriate)
4. Check for cadential six-four embellishment: V(6-4 to 5-3) or V(6-4 to 7)

# Context & Application
V7 appears in authentic cadential progressions (penultimate position), in prolongational contexts as subordinate harmony, and in sequences where it may carry less functional weight. The critical distinction is between its appropriateness in penultimate position (where dissonance drives resolution) and its inappropriateness as ultimate dominant (where stability is needed).

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Dominant function** — V7 is the most common dominant sonority
## Enables
- **Authentic cadential progression** — V7 drives powerfully toward the final tonic
## Related
- **Cadential progression** — V7 occupies the penultimate position in authentic progressions
## Contrasts With
- **Ultimate dominant** — The ultimate dominant must be a consonant triad (not V7)

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Using V7 in half cadences (as the ultimate dominant)
  **Correction**: The seventh "overly destabilize[s]" the ultimate dominant; use a consonant V triad

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Treating all V7 appearances as equally cadential
  **Clarification**: V7 in prolongational or sequential contexts carries less functional weight than V7 in a cadential progression
