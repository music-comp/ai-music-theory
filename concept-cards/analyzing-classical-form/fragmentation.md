# Fragmentation

**Fragmentation** is the immediate breaking down of the established unit size into smaller segments. It is the most typical sign of continuation function.

## Process

The process of fragmentation shortens the grouping units:
- The 2-measure units established in the presentation are reduced to 1-measure units
- Further fragmentation into half-measure units may occur

## How Fragments Are Defined

Individual fragments are often made clear through **repetition**, which helps define their boundaries. A common situation:
- Measure 5 of the theme becomes a fragment by virtue of being repeated in measure 6

## Relation to Melodic Content

Fragmentation exclusively concerns the **length** of musical units, regardless of how the melodic content of the fragments relates to the preceding material:
- Sometimes fragments contain motives derived from the basic idea
- Sometimes fragments bring entirely new melodic-motivic material

## Analogy

Think of cereal boxes in a supermarket: the difference in box size (grouping structure) is independent of the contents (melodic-motivic material). Fragmentation relates exclusively to the "packaging" of the material.

## Delayed Fragmentation

Sometimes fragmentation does not occur until after the continuation phrase has already begun:
- The continuation may start with 2-measure groups maintained
- Fragmentation may be bypassed to half-bar units directly

## Key Point

Fragmentation can be identified when grouping units become shorter, no matter what the melodic-motivic relationships among the groups may be.
