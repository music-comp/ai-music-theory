# Quick Definition
Sonata form consists of three required sections -- exposition, development, and recapitulation -- with optional slow introduction and coda as framing functions, in a repeat structure (||: Exposition :|| ||: Development-Recapitulation :||) that resembles the rounded binary version of small ternary.

# Core Definition
Sonata form consists of three required sections, each with a unique section function: exposition (presents thematic material and establishes tonal conflict), development (explores and intensifies material and tonal areas), and recapitulation (resolves tonal conflict by returning material to the home key). The exposition is almost always repeated (with repetition signs, though sometimes written out to introduce variations). The development and recapitulation together are sometimes repeated. The resulting structure ||: Exposition :|| ||: Development-Recapitulation :|| thus resembles the "rounded binary" version of the small ternary, with the three sections corresponding to the A, B, and A' sections. Two optional framing functions may frame the form: a slow introduction before the exposition and a coda after the recapitulation.

# Key Properties
1. Three required sections: exposition, development, recapitulation
2. Exposition almost always repeated
3. Development + recapitulation sometimes repeated
4. Repeat structure: ||: Exposition :|| ||: Development-Recapitulation :||
5. Resembles rounded binary (small ternary) at large scale
6. Two optional framing functions: slow introduction (before) and coda (after)

# Construction / Recognition
## Landmarks of Sonata Form:
1. **Beginning of exposition**: obvious (even after slow introduction, signaled by tempo change)
2. **End of exposition**: clearly articulated by repetition structure (repeat signs or written-out repeat)
3. **Beginning of development**: immediately after exposition closes
4. **End of development / beginning of recapitulation**: return of main-theme materials in home key
5. **End of recapitulation / beginning of coda**: where recapitulation no longer corresponds with exposition (can be difficult to determine)

# Context & Application
The structural parallel between sonata form and small ternary is deliberate: the same functional logic of exposition-contrast-return operates at both scales. Understanding small ternary form (Ch. 7) provides direct preparation for understanding sonata form. The landmarks described above are the analyst's primary tools for navigating a sonata-form movement on first encounter.
