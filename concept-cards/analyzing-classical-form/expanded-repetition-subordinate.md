# Quick Definition
When a complete, relatively tight-knit subordinate theme (ending with PAC) is repeated with significant expansion -- through internal repetition, extension, modal shift, tonicization, and cadential evasion -- the expanded repetition makes the theme considerably looser. A dilemma arises over whether to count this as a new theme or a repetition of the same theme.

# Core Definition
If a complete subordinate theme ending with PAC first appears as relatively tight-knit, it will sometimes be repeated and undergo **significant expansion** to render it considerably looser. This expanded repetition is especially likely when the original theme omits an initiating function (and thus seems abrupt at its close). Expansion techniques include: internal repetition extending model and sequences, extension of cadential idea through evaded cadences, "one more time" technique, modal shift, and tonicization of remote regions. It seems more practical to give the expanded repetition the **same numerical label** as the original, showing the motivic relationship even if the literal number of themes is not fully reflected (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 417-419).
