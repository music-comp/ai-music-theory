# Quick Definition
Three types of repetition occur within the presentation phrase, distinguished by harmonic context (not melody): (1) exact repetition -- same harmony; (2) statement-response -- tonic version then dominant version; (3) sequential -- complete transposition of melody AND harmony. The melody alone cannot determine the type. All three must support overall tonic prolongation.

# Core Definition
Three types of repetition of the basic idea can be identified within the presentation phrase (p. 63): exact, statement-response, and sequential. The distinction is essentially based on the harmonic context of the restated units, not on their melodic content (p. 63). In exact repetition, the underlying harmony remains essentially the same (allowing minor variants); the melody may be varied or even transposed if supportable by the original harmony (p. 63). In statement-response repetition, the statement emphasizes tonic and the response emphasizes dominant; the differing emphases are signaled primarily by their initial harmonies (p. 64). In sequential repetition (model-sequence technique), both melody and harmony are completely transposed by the same interval (p. 66). Regardless of type, the entire presentation must support tonic prolongation (p. 67).

# Prerequisites
- **Presentation phrase** — All three repetition types occur within the presentation
- **Basic idea** — The unit being repeated
- **Tonic prolongation** — Must be maintained regardless of repetition type

# Key Properties
1. Three types: exact, statement-response, sequential
2. Determined by HARMONIC context, not melody
3. Exact: same harmony; melody may be varied or transposed
4. Statement-response: I then V (signaled by initial harmonies); variant uses IV for response
5. Sequential: complete transposition of both melody and harmony
6. Cannot identify type from melody alone
7. All three must maintain overall tonic prolongation
8. Sequential is rarest (threatens prolongational stability)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Examine the harmonic support of both the basic idea and its repetition
2. Same harmony = exact repetition (even if melody differs)
3. Initial harmony changes from I to V = statement-response
4. Both melody AND harmony transposed = sequential
5. Do NOT identify type from melodic changes alone

# Context & Application
Statement-response is especially common and creates a harmonic dialogue within the tonic prolongation. It is easy to confuse statement-response with sequential repetition when the melody is transposed by a step; the harmonic context is what distinguishes them (p. 67). Sequential repetition is rare in presentations because it threatens the tonic prolongational stability required by presentation function; when it appears, a broader tonic prolongation usually emerges at some later point (p. 67). These same three return types apply to the return of the basic idea at the start of the consequent in a period (pp. 103-105).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Identifying repetition type from melody alone
  **Correction**: Melody transposed by a step could be either statement-response or sequential; always check the harmonic context (p. 67)

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing statement-response with sequential when melody rises by a step
  **Clarification**: In statement-response, the harmony changes from I to V; in sequential, both melody and harmony are transposed by the same interval
