# Repetition Types Summary

The three types of repetition that can occur within a presentation phrase are distinguished primarily by their **harmonic context**.

## Comparison Table

| Type | Harmonic Character | Example Pattern |
|------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Exact | Same harmony | I-V, I-V |
| Statement-Response | Tonic→Dominant | I, V or I-V, V-I |
| Sequential | Complete transposition | I-ii, ii-iii |

## Exact Repetition

- Harmony essentially unchanged
- Most stable expression
- Strongly reinforces tonic

## Statement-Response

- Statement emphasizes tonic (I)
- Response emphasizes dominant (V)
- Creates harmonic dialogue within prolongation

## Sequential Repetition

- Complete transposition of melody AND harmony
- Creates less stable expression
- Rare in presentations due to destabilizing effect

## Key Principle

**Cannot identify type from melody alone.**

The harmonic context determines the type:
- Same melody, different harmonies = could be any type
- The HARMONY (not melody) defines the category

## Presentation Requirement

Regardless of type, the entire presentation must support **tonic prolongation**. This is why sequential repetition is rare (it threatens prolongational stability).
