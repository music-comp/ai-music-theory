## Definition

A cadence of limited scope is a cadential idea whose function is limited to the unit of form that it closes (usually a codetta), without participating in any broader sense of thematic closure.

## Context

These cadences typically appear:
- Within closing sections
- Within codettas
- Within sequential passages
- In developmental contexts where formal closure is not the goal

## Distinction from Structural Cadences

| Structural Cadence | Cadence of Limited Scope |
|-------------------|-------------------------|
| Closes a theme or section | Closes only a local unit |
| Participates in larger formal plan | Function limited to immediate context |
| Creates formal articulation | Does not create broader articulation |

## Example Context

In a development section featuring model-sequence technique:
- Authentic cadences may occur within the sequence
- These cadences close local phrases
- But they do not create the broader closure that ends the development
- They are cadences "of limited scope"

## Analytical Implication

When encountering what appears to be a PAC:
- Consider whether it participates in broader formal closure
- Or whether its scope is limited to the immediate passage
- This distinction affects how we understand the formal structure
