# Quick Definition
A cadence of limited scope occurs when a 4-measure codetta contains its own internal cadential progression; this cadence is genuine within the boundaries of the codetta but does not affect the broader cadential structure of the theme.

# Core Definition
If an individual codetta occupies the length of a full 4-m. phrase, it may itself be concluded with a brief cadential idea. Within the limited scope of the codetta, this cadential figure has genuine cadential function. From the perspective of the theme as a whole, however, it participates within a broader postcadential function and thus cannot be considered a true cadence. Analytical notation: cadences of limited scope are placed in parentheses rather than in a box. Cadences of limited scope can arise in some other formal contexts beyond closing sections (development, sequential passages), but their appearance in closing sections is by no means rare.

# Prerequisites
- **Codetta** — The postcadential unit that may contain a cadence of limited scope
- **Closing section** — The larger context

# Key Properties
1. Occurs within a 4-m. codetta (rarely shorter)
2. Has genuine cadential function within the codetta
3. Does NOT function as a true cadence from the perspective of the theme
4. Notation: cadential abbreviation in parentheses, not a box
5. Can arise in various formal contexts, not just closing sections

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate a codetta that is 4 measures long
2. Check if it ends with a cadential progression
3. If so, note it as a cadence of limited scope (parenthetical notation)
4. Do not treat it as a structural cadence of the theme

# Context & Application
Cadences of limited scope help analysts avoid over-counting structural cadences. They appear in closing sections, development sections, and sequential passages.
