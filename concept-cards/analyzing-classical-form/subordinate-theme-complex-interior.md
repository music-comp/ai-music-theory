## Definition

Sometimes the interior theme of a large ternary does not relate to the small ternary (or binary) model of most such themes. Instead, it can give the impression of being a subordinate-theme complex within the tonal framework of the minore.

## Components

When organized this way, the interior theme contains:
1. **Transition-like material**: Modulatory passage from minore
2. **Subordinate theme**: Theme in a secondary key area (within minore framework)
3. **Retransition**: Return to home-key dominant

## Key Distinction

Unlike a true subordinate-theme complex:
- Set within the minore framework (not the standard subordinate key)
- Does not establish V (major) or III (minor) as in regular sonata form
- Secondary keys are related to the minor mode of the home key

## Tonal Framework

| Standard Subordinate-Theme Complex | Interior Theme as Subordinate-Theme Complex |
|-----------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| Moves to V (major) or III (minor) | Stays within minore tonal framework |
| Confirms subordinate key with PAC | May or may not achieve strong cadential closure |
| Represents tonal conflict | Represents modal contrast |

## Analytical Recognition

When encountering this type of interior theme:
1. Identify the minore framework
2. Note transition-like and subordinate-theme-like functions
3. Track how the material returns to home-key dominant
4. Distinguish from both typical interior theme and regular sonata exposition
