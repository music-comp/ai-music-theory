# Quick Definition
Ornamental changes in the recapitulation's cadential phrase -- such as additional harmonic embellishment, faster rhythmic motion, and fuller texture -- create greater intensity for the cadential arrival at the essential sonata closure.

# Core Definition
The most important cadence in the form (the essential sonata closure) should receive the most powerful treatment. The cadential phrase in the recapitulation's subordinate theme may feature ornamental changes that create greater intensity for the cadential arrival. These changes include harmonic embellishment (such as prolonging the pre-dominant with additional harmonies like a diminished seventh of V), more chromatic pre-dominant preparation, extended dominant prolongation, faster rhythmic motion approaching the cadence (added sixteenth notes where quarters appeared, more elaborate trills or ornaments), and textural changes (fuller texture, additional voices or doublings, increased dynamic level).

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of essential sonata closure
- Knowledge of ornamental changes in the recapitulation

# Key Properties
- Pre-dominant prolonged by additional harmonies (e.g., vii diminished seventh/V)
- More chromatic pre-dominant preparation
- Extended dominant prolongation
- Faster rhythmic motion approaching cadence (added sixteenth notes)
- More elaborate trills or ornaments
- Fuller texture at cadential arrival
- Additional voices or doublings
- Increased dynamic level
- Creates appropriate sense of arrival and climax

# Construction / Recognition
Compare the cadential phrase of the subordinate theme in the recapitulation with the corresponding passage in the exposition. Look for added harmonic embellishments (especially in pre-dominant and dominant areas), increased rhythmic activity, and changes in texture or dynamics that make the recapitulation's cadence more powerful than the exposition's.

# Context & Application
Cadential intensification is an ornamental change that serves a structural purpose: ensuring that the most important cadence in the entire movement receives the most powerful treatment. Even when the underlying cadential structure remains the same as in the exposition, surface-level embellishments can transform the effect from routine confirmation to dramatic climax.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C, K. 545, i, mm. 67-71: pre-dominant ii6 prolonged by vii diminished seventh/V, sixteenth notes in m. 69 make the following trill more climactic, creating greater intensity for the cadential arrival than in the exposition

# Common Errors
- Treating cadential intensification as a structural change when it is typically ornamental
- Overlooking subtle embellishments that significantly increase cadential weight

# Common Confusions
- Intensification is NOT the same as expansion -- intensification embellishes the existing cadential structure with surface-level changes, while expansion adds new structural elements (additional deceptive cadences, longer progressions) to the cadential process
