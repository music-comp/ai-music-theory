# Quick Definition
When a compound sentence ends with an HC, the arrival on dominant harmony may be reinforced by a postcadential standing on the dominant. Unlike a closing section (recessive dynamic after PAC), the standing on the dominant reinforces the dynamic energy of the unstable HC ending. It is very rare in simple sentences but appears in compound sentences, often restoring 16-m. balance when the continuation is compressed.

# Core Definition
If the compound sentence ends with an HC, the arrival on dominant harmony may be reinforced by a postcadential standing on the dominant, a formal unit analogous to a closing section that follows a PAC (p. 204). The use of a standing on the dominant at the end of a simple sentence is very rare (p. 204). Unlike a closing section, which typically brings a recessive dynamic following the cadential goal, a standing on the dominant tends to reinforce the dynamic energy associated with the instability of "ending" on dominant harmony (p. 204). It is structured as a series of repeated one-bar units, like the codettas of a closing section, and it prolongs dominant harmony exclusively.

# Key Properties
1. Follows an HC at the end of a compound sentence
2. Prolongs dominant harmony exclusively
3. Reinforces dynamic energy (progressive, not recessive)
4. Structured as repeated one-bar units (like codettas)
5. Analogous to closing section but with opposite dynamic character
6. Very rare in simple sentences
7. Often restores balance: compressed 4-m. continuation + 4-m. standing = 8 m. continuation area
8. Creates strong expectation for tonic resolution

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate an HC ending a compound sentence
2. Check for sustained or arpeggiated dominant harmony following the HC
3. Look for repeated one-bar units prolonging V
4. Verify the material is postcadential (after the theme has technically ended)
5. Note the progressive dynamic character (reinforcing energy, not dying away)

# Context & Application
A typical scenario: a compound sentence with an 8-m. presentation followed by a compressed 4-m. continuation ending with HC. The 4-m. standing on the dominant then extends the theme to 16 m., restoring balance with the presentation (p. 204). The standing on the dominant is postcadential -- the theme has technically ended at the HC, and the dominant prolongation frames the theme's end, creating powerful expectation for the resolution that follows (typically the start of a new section or theme).

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 204, Ex. 6.11): Haydn, Symphony No. 83, i, mm. 1-16 -- compressed 4-m. continuation leads to HC at m. 12; 4-m. standing on the dominant (mm. 13-16) extends to 16 measures, restoring balance with the presentation; structured as repeated one-bar units.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Labeling the standing on the dominant as part of the continuation
  **Correction**: The standing on the dominant is postcadential (after the HC); the theme has technically ended; it is a framing function, not a continuation

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Expecting a recessive dynamic (dying away) after the HC
  **Clarification**: Unlike closing sections after PAC, the standing on the dominant reinforces energy and instability, creating anticipation for resolution
