# Quick Definition
Subordinate themes employ six main loosening devices: (1) extending/repeating the presentation, (2) weakening the opening tonic prolongation, (3) extending continuation with model-sequence technique, (4) extending cadential function through evaded cadences/"one more time" technique, (5) expanding cadential function via ECP, and (6) omitting initiating function. Additional harmonic loosening includes modal shift, chromaticism, and tonicization of remote regions.

# Core Definition
Six principal loosening devices make subordinate themes almost always considerably longer than main themes: (1) **extending or repeating presentation** (additional repetition of basic idea, or repeating entire presentation); (2) **weakening tonic prolongation** of presentation (dominant pedal undermining but not obliterating tonic); (3) **extending continuation** with model-sequence technique; (4) **extending cadential function** through evaded cadences and "one more time" technique; (5) **expanding cadential function** through expanded cadential progressions (ECP); (6) **omitting initiating function** (beginning in medias res). Additional harmonic-tonal loosening: modal shift, chromaticism, tonicization of remote regions (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 376-397, 412-417).

# Key Properties
1. Six phrase-structural loosening devices
2. Three additional harmonic-tonal loosening techniques
3. All contribute to theme's characteristic length
4. Different devices may be distributed among themes in a group
5. Continuation and cadential functions often occupy distinct phrases (unlike tight-knit sentence)
6. These techniques generate dramatic tension around the confirming PAC
