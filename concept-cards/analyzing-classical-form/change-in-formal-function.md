# Quick Definition
When a recapitulation is compressed, expanded, or even the same length as the exposition, the internal formal functions (sentence, period, presentation, continuation) may change, representing a significant structural alteration.

# Core Definition
The recapitulation may alter the formal functions used within the theme relative to the exposition. In compression, functions may be eliminated (e.g., the antecedent is dropped, leaving a single consequent phrase). In expansion, new continuation material or motivic development may extend phrases. Even when A' has the same number of measures as A, different formal functions may be used: for example, a period in the exposition may become sentential in the recapitulation, with the basic idea followed by continuation with fragmentation rather than by a contrasting idea. Such changes are structural, not merely ornamental.

# Key Properties
1. Formal functions may change even when phrase length is preserved
2. Compression may eliminate functions (e.g., dropping antecedent)
3. Expansion may add continuation material or developmental passages
4. A period (A) may become sentential (A')
5. These changes are structural, not ornamental
6. Identifying such changes reveals compositional choices

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Determine the formal functions in the exposition (e.g., antecedent + consequent = period)
2. Compare with the recapitulation's internal organization
3. Check whether phrase types have changed (presentation vs. antecedent, continuation vs. contrasting idea)
4. Note whether changes are due to compression, expansion, or reconfiguration at same length

# Context & Application
Change in formal function is one of the most sophisticated structural changes a composer can make in a recapitulation. It demonstrates that the relationship between exposition and recapitulation goes beyond surface-level variation; the very logic of phrase construction may be rethought. This analytical observation applies equally to small ternary forms and to sonata-form recapitulations.

# Examples
**Example pattern** (p. 248): Exposition as period (antecedent: b.i. + c.i. to HC; consequent: b.i. + c.i. to PAC) becomes recapitulation as sentential structure (basic idea 2 mm. + continuation with fragmentation 6 mm. to PAC). Same 8-measure length, entirely different formal logic.
