# Quick Definition
A slow introduction may resemble a truncated small ternary: an opening section (A) reaches cadential closure, a standing-on-the-dominant passage functions as a contrasting middle (B), but the expected return of A-prime is truncated when the resolution to tonic instead initiates the exposition's main theme.

# Core Definition
A slow introduction's form may resemble an incomplete small ternary. The opening section presents prominent thematic material (A-like) and reaches cadential closure (usually a PAC). A subsequent standing-on-the-dominant passage resembles a typical contrasting middle (B section). The implied small ternary becomes truncated when the expected resolution to tonic for the A-prime return instead initiates the exposition of the movement, with the main theme taking the place of the expected A-prime return. The resolution to tonic at the beginning of the exposition simultaneously provides the elided cadence ending the introduction and initiates the main theme as a new beginning.

# Key Properties
- Opening section (A) with prominent thematic material
- Cadential closure (usually PAC)
- Standing on dominant functions as contrasting middle (B)
- Expected A-prime return is truncated
- Resolution to tonic initiates exposition instead of A-prime
- Main theme of exposition takes place of expected A-prime return
- Creates sense that exposition "interrupts" the introduction
- Or that introduction flows seamlessly into main theme

# Construction / Recognition
Identify a slow introduction whose opening section reaches cadential closure, followed by a standing-on-the-dominant passage. If the expected return of opening material is replaced by the beginning of the exposition's main theme (at the resolution of the dominant), a truncated ternary has occurred. The exposition "interrupts" the introduction's implied ternary design.

# Context & Application
The truncated ternary is one of the most elegant ways to connect the slow introduction to the exposition. The introduction sets up the expectation for a formal return (A-prime), but instead of fulfilling this expectation within the introduction's own terms, the resolution to tonic launches the fast movement. This technique exploits the listener's formal expectations to create a powerful sense of inevitability at the transition from slow introduction to fast exposition. The "interruption" effect heightens the dramatic impact of the exposition's entrance.

# Examples
- Mozart, Violin Sonata in B-flat, K. 454, i: opening fanfare section reaches PAC at m. 9; remaining music brings standing on dominant (contrasting middle); implied small ternary becomes truncated when resolution to tonic at m. 14 initiates the exposition rather than bringing a return of the opening fanfares

# Common Errors
- Treating the truncated ternary as a complete small ternary by counting the exposition's main theme as the A-prime section
- Failing to recognize the standing on dominant as a contrasting middle function

# Common Confusions
- The truncated ternary is NOT a defective form -- the truncation is a deliberate compositional strategy that exploits formal expectations to create a dramatic transition to the exposition
- The main theme is NOT the A-prime section -- it is a new formal beginning that replaces the expected A-prime return
