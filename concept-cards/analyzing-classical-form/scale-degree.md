# Quick Definition
A position within the diatonic scale, numbered 1 through 7, serving as the basis for identifying chord roots and understanding harmonic relationships.

# Core Definition
Scale degrees are the seven positions of the diatonic scale. "Most North American textbooks identify individual harmonies in terms of the scale degrees of their roots" (p. 23), using Roman numerals to represent these positions. Chromatic alterations of scale degrees (raised 4, lowered 2, lowered 6) create additional harmonic resources beyond the diatonic vocabulary.

# Prerequisites
- None; scale degrees are foundational

# Key Properties
1. Seven diatonic positions, numbered 1-7
2. Serve as the foundation of Roman numeral analysis
3. Each degree generates a characteristic harmony
4. Chromatic alterations: raised 4 (V/V), lowered 2 (Neapolitan), lowered 6 (augmented sixths)
5. Enable transposition of analytical insights across keys
6. Connect melodic and harmonic analysis

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Determine the key of the passage
2. Number each pitch position within the diatonic scale (1-7)
3. Note any chromatic alterations (raised or lowered degrees)
4. Identify chord roots by their scale-degree position

# Context & Application
Scale degrees form the foundation of Roman numeral analysis, determine chord roots and harmonic identification, and enable comparison of progressions across keys. Understanding scale degrees is prerequisite to understanding harmonic function. Each degree has a traditional name (tonic, supertonic, mediant, subdominant, dominant, submediant, leading tone).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Conflating scale degree (melodic position) with harmonic function
  **Correction**: Each scale degree identifies a chord root, but function (tonic, dominant, pre-dominant) groups multiple degrees together

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing scale degree notation with figured bass
  **Clarification**: Scale degrees identify positions in the scale; figured bass indicates intervals above the bass note
