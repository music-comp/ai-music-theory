# Quick Definition
A position within the diatonic scale, numbered 1 through 7, which serves as the basis for identifying chord roots and understanding harmonic relationships.

# Formal Definition
Scale degrees are the seven positions of the diatonic scale, numbered with Arabic numerals or represented with caret notation. Most North American textbooks identify individual harmonies in terms of the scale degrees of their roots, using Roman numerals to indicate these roots with respect to a given key.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Scale degrees and their typical harmonies:
- **1 (tonic)**: I chord - stability, home
- **2 (supertonic)**: II chord - pre-dominant function
- **3 (mediant)**: III chord - ambiguous function
- **4 (subdominant)**: IV chord - pre-dominant function
- **5 (dominant)**: V chord - dominant function
- **6 (submediant)**: VI chord - tonic substitute
- **7 (leading tone)**: VII chord - dominant function

Chromatic alterations:
- Raised 4: Leading tone of V (V/V, augmented sixths)
- Lowered 2: Neapolitan
- Lowered 6: Phrygian/augmented-sixth harmonies

# Musical Context/Application
Scale degrees:
- Form the foundation of Roman numeral analysis
- Determine chord roots and harmonic identification
- Enable transposition of analytical insights across keys
- Connect melodic and harmonic analysis

Understanding scale degrees is prerequisite to understanding harmonic function.

# Common Confusions
- Conflating scale degree (melodic position) with harmonic function
- Assuming each scale degree creates a distinct function
- Treating altered scale degrees as separate from their diatonic versions
- Confusing scale degree notation with figured bass
