# Quick Definition
Concerto form is a full-movement form derived from Baroque ritornello form and infused with sonata elements, consisting of six principal sections: opening ritornello, solo exposition, subordinate-key ritornello, solo development, solo recapitulation, and closing ritornello. It is used in the first movement of all classical concertos.

# Core Definition
A defining feature of the classical concerto is its employment of a formal scheme derived from the Baroque ritornello form and strongly infused with elements of classical sonata form. Concerto form consists of six principal sections. It is used in the first movement of all classical concertos. The form does not contain a section that functions like a sonata-form coda (Caplin, pp. 694-695).

# Prerequisites
- **Sonata form** -- Provides the exposition/development/recapitulation framework
- **Ritornello** -- The orchestral passages that frame the form

# Key Properties
1. Six sections: opening ritornello, solo exposition, subordinate-key ritornello, solo development, solo recapitulation, closing ritornello
2. Derived from Baroque ritornello form + classical sonata elements
3. First movement of all classical concertos (required)
4. Sometimes in slow movements; rarely in finales
5. No coda section (closing ritornello is NOT a coda)
6. Balances solo instrument against full orchestra

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for an opening orchestral section (ritornello) preceding the solo entrance
2. Identify the solo exposition with its modulation to subordinate key
3. Check for orchestral ritornellos after the exposition and at the end
4. Note the cadenza within the closing ritornello

# Context & Application
The form balances two aesthetic goals: highlighting the solo instrument (through new material, modulatory action, and cadenza) and ensuring the orchestra is not mere accompaniment (through ritornellos providing a textural frame).

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Treating concerto form as a variant of sonata form
  **Clarification**: Concerto form is an independent form derived from Baroque ritornello form, not a variant of sonata form, despite sharing many features
