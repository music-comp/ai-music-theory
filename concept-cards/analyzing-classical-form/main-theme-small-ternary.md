# Quick Definition
A main theme can take the form of a small ternary (A-B-A'), though this is relatively uncommon because the form's internal completeness and complexity can reduce forward momentum. Small binary is even rarer. Ternary main themes are more likely in slow movements and finales.

# Core Definition
The small ternary and small binary are used much less frequently as main-theme types than the sentence, period, or hybrid, owing most likely to their formal complexity. The sentence form -- with its dynamic momentum pushing toward a single cadence -- is particularly suitable for opening a sonata exposition. The period form -- with its symmetrical phrasing that emphasizes closure -- is used less often. Many main themes are structured as conventional theme types, and the choice often relates to movement position: sentences and sentential hybrids suit first movements; periods suit finales. Slow movements show no particular preference. Small ternary and small binary main themes are relatively uncommon overall.

# Key Properties
1. Small ternary as main theme is relatively uncommon
2. Small binary as main theme is even rarer
3. Sentence is the preferred type for first movements (forward drive)
4. Period is more common in finales (symmetrical, lighter affect)
5. No preference for slow movements
6. Form's internal completeness may reduce forward momentum

# Context & Application
The choice of main-theme type relates directly to the aesthetic needs of the movement. First movements favor the sentence because sonata form is inherently the most dynamic of full-movement forms, and the sentence's forward-striving character suits this dynamic quality. First-movement main themes are also more likely to feature phrase-structural deviations (extensions and expansions) than finale main themes.
