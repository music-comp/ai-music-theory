# Quick Definition
The "theory of steps" developed by Simon Sechter, organizing harmonic progressions based on roots placed above the seven degrees of the diatonic scale, foundational to Schoenberg's and Schenker's approaches.

# Core Definition
"A more systematic presentation of harmonic progressions based on roots placed above the seven 'steps' of the diatonic scale was proposed by the mid-19th-century Viennese theorist Simon Sechter. His Stufentheorie ('theory of steps') was highly influential on both Arnold Schoenberg and Heinrich Schenker, whose theories have had enormous impact on modern North American approaches to harmony" (p. 25).

# Prerequisites
- **Scale degree** — Stufentheorie is organized around the seven scale degrees

# Key Properties
1. Organizes harmony by the seven diatonic "steps" (Stufen)
2. Each step generates a characteristic harmony
3. Progressions understood through root relationships
4. Developed by Simon Sechter (mid-19th-century Vienna)
5. Influenced Schoenberg's harmonic theory
6. Influenced Schenker's analytical approach
7. Foundational to Roman numeral analysis

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. When encountering scale-degree-based harmonic analysis, recognize its roots in Stufentheorie
2. Roman numeral analysis is the practical application of step theory
3. The "step" concept treats each scale degree as generating a family of related harmonies

# Context & Application
Stufentheorie provides the conceptual foundation for Roman numeral analysis but may be enriched by Funktionstheorie, which groups multiple steps into functional categories. The two approaches are complementary rather than opposed: Stufentheorie identifies chords by root position; Funktionstheorie reveals shared functions among different roots.

## Contrasts With
- **Funktionstheorie** — Step theory identifies individual roots; function theory groups them into categories

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating Stufentheorie and Funktionstheorie as opposed theories
  **Correction**: They are complementary: step theory identifies chords; function theory interprets their roles

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming all seven steps have equal functional weight
  **Clarification**: Function theory reveals that multiple steps share the same function (e.g., I and VI both express tonic)
