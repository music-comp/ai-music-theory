# Quick Definition
The "theory of steps" developed by Simon Sechter, presenting harmonic progressions based on roots placed above the seven degrees of the diatonic scale, foundational to Schoenberg and Schenker's approaches.

# Formal Definition
Stufentheorie ("theory of steps") is a systematic presentation of harmonic progressions based on roots placed above the seven "steps" (Stufen) of the diatonic scale. Proposed by the mid-19th-century Viennese theorist Simon Sechter, this approach was highly influential on both Arnold Schoenberg and Heinrich Schenker, whose theories have had enormous impact on modern North American approaches to harmony.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Key features:
- Focuses on scale-degree roots (Stufen)
- Organizes harmony by the seven diatonic steps
- Each step generates a characteristic harmony
- Progressions understood through root relationships

Historical context:
- Developed by Simon Sechter (mid-19th century Vienna)
- Influenced Schoenberg's harmonic theory
- Influenced Schenker's analytical approach
- Foundational to Roman numeral analysis

The "step" (Stufe) concept treats each scale degree as generating a family of related harmonies.

# Musical Context/Application
Stufentheorie relates to modern practice through:
- Roman numeral analysis (derived from step theory)
- Understanding of root motion
- Scale-degree based harmonic description
- Framework for analyzing chord progressions

The theory provides the conceptual foundation for Roman numeral analysis but may be enriched by function theory (Funktionstheorie), which groups multiple steps into functional categories.

# Common Confusions
- Treating Stufentheorie and Funktionstheorie as opposed theories (they can be complementary)
- Not recognizing Roman numeral analysis as derived from step theory
- Assuming all seven steps have equal functional weight
- Conflating historical theory with modern analytical practice
