# Quick Definition
The home key (HK) is the primary tonal center of a sonata-form movement, confirmed in the exposition by a cadence (usually PAC), abandoned during the development, and restored and ultimately reconfirmed with a PAC in the recapitulation.

# Core Definition
The home key establishes the fundamental point of reference for all tonal activity in sonata form. The exposition begins in the home key, confirmed by a cadence (usually a PAC, but possibly an IAC or HC). The exposition then modulates to the subordinate key. The development typically moves away from the home key, exploring development keys, but restores it toward the end, usually confirmed with an HC or dominant arrival. The recapitulation begins in the home key and remains there throughout, with a move toward the subdominant region (not confirmed as a genuine key) typically occurring early in the section. The recapitulation eventually reconfirms the home key with a PAC.

# Key Properties
1. Primary tonal center of the movement
2. Exposition: confirmed by cadence (usually PAC), then abandoned
3. Development: moves away; restored toward end (HC or dominant arrival)
4. Recapitulation: begins and remains in home key throughout
5. Subdominant emphasis in recapitulation's transition (not a genuine modulation)
6. Final PAC in home key resolves the tonal conflict

# Construction / Recognition
## Role in Each Section:
| Section | Home Key Function | Confirmation |
|---------|-------------------|-------------|
| Exposition | Established, then abandoned | PAC, IAC, or HC |
| Development | Absent; restored at end | HC or dominant arrival |
| Recapitulation | Present throughout | Final PAC |

# Context & Application
The home key's journey -- from establishment through abandonment to restoration -- constitutes the fundamental tonal drama of sonata form. The tonal conflict between home key and subordinate key drives the entire form. The home key's final confirmation by PAC in the recapitulation represents the resolution of this conflict, with the home key emerging "victorious."
