# Quick Definition
An augmented-sixth chord containing four pitches -- lowered 6, tonic, scale degree 2, and raised 4 -- creating a whole-tone-based sonority that functions as pre-dominant.

# Core Definition
The French sixth (Fr+6) is one of three varieties of augmented-sixth chords functioning as pre-dominants. It adds the second scale degree to the Italian sixth's three pitches, creating a four-note sonority whose pitches all belong to the same whole-tone scale. This gives it a distinctive, somewhat unstable color. The French sixth is classified as "an important subclass" of pre-dominants related to the dominant of the dominant (p. 32).

# Prerequisites
- **Dominant of the dominant** — The French sixth belongs to the V/V pre-dominant category
- **Raised fourth scale degree** — Contains raised 4 as the defining chromatic element

# Key Properties
1. Pitch content (in C): Ab (lowered 6), C (1), D (2), F# (raised 4)
2. All four pitches belong to the same whole-tone scale
3. Contains two tritones: Ab-D and C-F#
4. Lowered 6 resolves down to 5; raised 4 resolves up to 5
5. Scale degree 2 can remain as part of the following dominant
6. No parallel-fifth problem (unlike the German sixth)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for the augmented sixth interval (lowered 6 to raised 4)
2. Verify the presence of scale degree 2 (distinguishes from Italian and German)
3. Check that all four pitches form a whole-tone subset
4. Confirm pre-dominant function resolving to V

# Context & Application
The French sixth functions as pre-dominant within cadential progressions. The absence of the lowered third degree (present in the German sixth) avoids the parallel-fifth problem, allowing more direct resolution to V without needing a cadential six-four. Its distinctive whole-tone color sets it apart from the other augmented sixths.

## Contrasts With
- **Italian sixth** — French adds scale degree 2 to the Italian's three pitches
- **German sixth** — French has scale degree 2; German has lowered 3

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing the French sixth with the German sixth
  **Correction**: French has scale degree 2; German has lowered 3

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Not recognizing the whole-tone subset quality
  **Clarification**: All four pitches of the French sixth belong to one whole-tone scale, creating its distinctive sound
