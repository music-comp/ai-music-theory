# Quick Definition
An augmented-sixth chord containing four pitches: the lowered sixth degree in the bass, the tonic pitch, the second scale degree, and the raised fourth degree, creating a sonority based on the whole-tone scale.

# Formal Definition
The French sixth (Fr+6) is one of three varieties of augmented-sixth chords functioning as pre-dominants. It adds the second scale degree to the Italian sixth's three pitches, creating a four-note sonority whose pitches all belong to the whole-tone scale. This gives the French sixth its distinctive, somewhat unstable color compared to the other augmented sixths.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Pitch content (in C major/minor):
- Ab (lowered 6) - typically in bass
- C (tonic/1)
- D (scale degree 2)
- F# (raised 4) - typically in an upper voice

Whole-tone character:
- All four pitches (Ab, C, D, F#) belong to the same whole-tone scale
- Creates distinctive, floating quality
- Two tritones: Ab-D and C-F#

Resolution:
- Lowered 6 resolves down to 5
- Raised 4 resolves up to 5
- Scale degree 2 can remain as part of the following dominant or resolve
- Less voice-leading difficulty than German sixth (no parallel fifth issue)

# Musical Context/Application
The French sixth:
- Functions as pre-dominant within cadential progressions
- Can resolve directly to V without cadential six-four
- Creates distinctive whole-tone color
- The second scale degree connects smoothly to dominant harmony

The absence of the lowered third degree (present in the German sixth) avoids the parallel fifth problem, allowing more direct resolution to V.

# Common Confusions
- Confusing with German sixth (French has scale degree 2, not lowered 3)
- Not recognizing the whole-tone subset quality
- Treating as independent harmony rather than pre-dominant function
- Missing the two tritones that characterize the sonority
