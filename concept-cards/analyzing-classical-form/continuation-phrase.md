# Quick Definition
The second 4-m. phrase of a sentence, fusing continuation and cadential functions. Continuation function destabilizes the presentation through fragmentation, harmonic acceleration, increased surface rhythm, and/or sequential progressions. Closes with a cadence (PAC, HC, or IAC).

# Core Definition
In the continuation phrase, continuation function destabilizes the harmonic, tonal, and rhythmic processes established in the presentation, and cadential function brings closure (p. 58). Continuation function is characterized by four processes: (1) phrase-structural fragmentation, (2) acceleration in harmonic change, (3) increase in surface rhythmic activity, and (4) sequential harmonic progressions. At least one, but often several, of these will be found. The phrase is labeled "continuation" because continuation function is usually more salient throughout the entire phrase than cadential function, which normally appears later (p. 57).

# Prerequisites
- **Sentence** — The continuation phrase is the second phrase of the sentence
- **Presentation phrase** — The continuation follows and destabilizes the presentation

# Key Properties
1. 4 measures (mm. 5-8 of the sentence)
2. Fuses continuation and cadential functions
3. Four characteristics: fragmentation, harmonic acceleration, surface-rhythm increase, sequential harmony
4. At least one characteristic must be present; often several are found
5. Ends with a cadence: PAC (most common), HC, or IAC (rare)
6. Cadential idea typically in mm. 7-8, supported by cadential progression
7. Labeled "continuation" because cont. function is more salient than cad. function
8. The two functions are fused (not separate sections)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for destabilization after the presentation (mm. 5-8)
2. Check for fragmentation (units shorter than 2 m.)
3. Check for harmonic acceleration (faster rate of harmonic change)
4. Check for increased surface rhythmic activity
5. Check for sequential harmonies
6. Identify the cadence at the end

# Context & Application
The continuation phrase is where the theme acquires momentum and drive. The four processes work together to create a medial formal function -- "being in the middle." Cadential function emerges from the continuation processes, creating formal closure. The cadential idea often features liquidation of characteristic motives toward conventional, formulaic material.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 56, Ex. 2.1): Beethoven, Op. 2, No. 1 -- fragmentation (mm. 5-6), harmonic acceleration, increased rhythmic activity, HC at m. 8.

**Example 2** (p. 70, Ex. 2.14): Mozart, K. 330, i -- clear fragmentation and cadential idea.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Expecting fragmentation to always begin at m. 5
  **Correction**: Fragmentation may be delayed; the continuation may begin by maintaining 2-m. units

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the continuation phrase has only continuation function
  **Clarification**: It fuses continuation and cadential functions; both are present
