# Quick Definition
A two-part transition combines a nonmodulating first part (ending HK: HC) with a modulating second part (ending SK: HC). The second part begins in the home key, often alludes to main-theme material, and frequently omits initiating function by starting with continuation via model-sequence technique.

# Core Definition
The process of tonal destabilization sometimes takes place in two distinct stages: a nonmodulating first part ending HK: HC, followed by a modulating second part ending SK: HC. The second part begins in the home key (though not necessarily on tonic) and often alludes to the opening material of the main theme. It frequently omits initiating function, starting with continuation signaled by model-sequence technique (Caplin, Ch. 11, pp. 361ff, also pp. 343-344 for Ex. 11.8).

# Prerequisites
- **Transition basics** -- Must understand the three tonal categories
- **Modulating / Nonmodulating transition** -- Two-part combines both

# Key Properties
1. First part: nonmodulating, ends HK: HC
2. Second part: modulating, ends SK: HC
3. Second part often uses main-theme material
4. Second part frequently begins with continuation (model-sequence)
5. Combines nonmodulating and modulating approaches

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 343): Haydn, H. 30, i, mm. 15-44 -- First part begins with false closing section, ends HK: HC; second part modulates to SK.
