## Elision in Subordinate-Theme Group

To maintain rhythmic continuity among multiple subordinate themes, the end of one theme may **elide** with the beginning of the next.

### Definition

The final tonic of the PAC functions simultaneously as:
- The **ending** of the closing theme (cadential arrival)
- The **initial tonic** of the subsequent theme (structural beginning)

### Effect

Elision is particularly effective in:
- **Reinvigorating rhythmic activity** for the second subordinate theme
- Following long note values of pre-dominant and dominant harmonies of an ECP
- Creating seamless connection between themes

### Compared to Accompanimental Overlap

| Feature | Elision | Accompanimental Overlap |
|---------|---------|------------------------|
| What overlaps | Structure | Only texture |
| Next theme begins | At cadential arrival | Bar after cadence |
| Continuity effect | Strong | Moderate |

### Formal Implication

Elision creates:
- A single harmonic event serving two formal functions
- Strong forward momentum across the group
- Emphasis on continuity over articulation
