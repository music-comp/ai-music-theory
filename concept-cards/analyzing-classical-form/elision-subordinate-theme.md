# Quick Definition
Between subordinate themes in a group, the final tonic of one theme's PAC can function simultaneously as the initial tonic of the next theme (elision). This is particularly effective in reinvigorating rhythmic activity after the long note values of an ECP's pre-dominant and dominant harmonies.

# Core Definition
To maintain rhythmic continuity among multiple subordinate themes, the end of one theme may elide with the beginning of the next. The final tonic of the PAC functions simultaneously as the ending of the prior theme and the initial tonic of the subsequent theme. Elision is particularly effective in reinvigorating rhythmic activity for the second subordinate theme following the long note values of pre-dominant and dominant harmonies closing the first theme. An accompanimental overlap (where new accompanimental figuration enters at the PAC, but the new theme's first downbeat is in the following measure) provides a more typical, less forceful alternative (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 404-405).

1. Final tonic of PAC = initial tonic of next theme (simultaneous)
2. Especially effective after long ECP (reinvigorates rhythm)
3. Creates seamless connection between themes
4. Accompanimental overlap is the more common, less forceful alternative
