# Quick Definition
The sentence's forward-striving character (from its continuation's compression and acceleration) makes it especially suited for opening the dynamic sonata form. The period's symmetrical repose is better suited for finales and rondos. Slow movements show no particular preference.

# Core Definition
The period has a symmetrical structure creating a "repose in itself" due to its balanced halves. The sentence has a "forward-striving character" due to increased activity and compression in its continuation, making it fundamentally different. Since sonata form is inherently the most dynamic full-movement form, the sentence is especially well suited to initiate it. Fast first movements particularly favor the sentence; finales favor the period (often in rondo form); slow movements show no preference (Caplin, Ch. 10, p. 309, citing Ratz).

# Prerequisites
- **Sentence** -- Must understand its continuation-driven dynamic

# Key Properties
1. Period: symmetrical, "repose in itself," balanced halves
2. Sentence: forward-striving, compressed continuation, dynamic momentum
3. First movements: favor sentence or sentential hybrids
4. Finales: favor period (lighter, more relaxed affect)
5. Slow movements: no particular preference

# Context & Application
This aesthetic distinction, attributed to Erwin Ratz, explains compositional choices at the movement level. It connects theme-type selection to the expressive goals of different movements within a cycle.
