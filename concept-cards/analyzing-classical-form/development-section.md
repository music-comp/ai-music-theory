# Quick Definition
The second large-scale section of sonata form, standing between the exposition and recapitulation, functioning to generate the greatest degree of tonal and phrase-structural instability within the movement.

# Core Definition
According to Caplin, the development "functions to generate the greatest degree of tonal and phrase-structural instability within the movement and thus motivates the restoration of stability that is accomplished by the recapitulation" (p. 442). Unlike the exposition, the development is considerably less conventional in its formal organization.

# Prerequisites
- **Exposition** — Must understand what material is being developed and destabilized
- **Contrasting middle** — The development is a higher-level analogue to this small-ternary function
- **Sequential progression** — The primary harmonic technique driving developmental process

# Key Properties
1. Features looser organization than the exposition
2. Emphasizes sequential progressions throughout
3. Avoids authentic cadential closure in the home key
4. Typically ends on dominant harmony of the home key
5. Can sometimes exceed the size of the entire exposition
6. Usually contains multiple thematic units
7. Explores minor-mode tonal regions as development keys

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate the section between the exposition's closing section and the recapitulation
2. Observe emphasis on sequential progressions and tonal instability
3. Look for the pre-core/core technique as the most common phrase-structural process
4. Identify the harmonic goal (usually home-key dominant)

# Context & Application
The development section creates maximum instability to motivate the recapitulation's restoration of tonal order. It typically begins in the subordinate key and explores minor-mode regions before returning to the home key.

# Examples
**Example 13.1** (p. 446): Mozart, Piano Sonata in A minor, K. 310, i, mm. 50-81 — pre-core begins with main-theme material in subordinate key; core sets up 4-m. model sequenced twice down a perfect fifth; fragmentation at m. 70; HC at m. 74 followed by standing on the dominant.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Contrasting middle** — Higher-level analogue with greater length and complexity
- **Exposition** — Develops material first presented there
## Enables
- **Recapitulation** — Motivates the restoration of stability
## Related
- **Pre-core** — The opening section of the development
- **Core** — The central, most dramatically charged portion
## Contrasts With
- **Exposition** — Development is less conventional, more unstable
- **Contrasting middle** — Greater length, complexity, and tonal exploration

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming the development will follow a predictable formal plan like the exposition
  **Correction**: The development is the least conventional section; the listener rarely knows in advance how it will be organized

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Conflating "development" as a section with "development" as a motivic process
  **Clarification**: The term has two meanings — the central section of sonata form, and the systematic manipulation/transformation of musical ideas (which can occur anywhere in the form)
