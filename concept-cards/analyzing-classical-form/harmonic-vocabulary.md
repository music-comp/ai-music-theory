# Quick Definition
Individual harmonies are identified by uppercase Roman numerals indicating the scale degree of their root, with Arabic numerals (from figured-bass tradition) showing inversions. Caplin uses uppercase Roman numerals exclusively, without distinguishing chord quality.

# Core Definition
Most North American textbooks identify individual harmonies in terms of the scale degrees of their roots. The seven Roman numerals indicate these roots with respect to a given key, and additional Arabic numerals indicate the various positions (inversions) of the harmonies. Caplin follows the system (such as Aldwell, Schachter, and Cadwallader) that does not distinguish chord quality and uses uppercase Roman numerals exclusively (p. 24). The modern theory of harmony was founded by Rameau in the early 18th century; the Roman numeral system was introduced by Abbe Vogler and popularized by Gottfried Weber in the early 19th century (p. 24).

# Key Properties
1. Roman numerals indicate the scale degree of the harmonic root
2. Arabic numerals (figured-bass derived) indicate inversions
3. Uppercase Roman numerals are used exclusively regardless of chord quality (major, minor, diminished)
4. Examples: I = root-position tonic, IV6 = first-inversion subdominant, V4/3 = second-inversion dominant seventh
5. Harmonies group into three functional categories rather than seven fully distinct entities

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify a Harmony:
1. Determine the root of the chord
2. Identify which scale degree that root occupies in the current key
3. Assign the corresponding uppercase Roman numeral
4. Add figured-bass symbols for any inversion

# Context & Application
This notation system is foundational to all harmonic analysis in the text. Caplin's choice to use uppercase numerals exclusively simplifies notation while relying on context and key signature to convey chord quality. The system derives from a long tradition: Rameau founded the modern theory of harmony, Vogler introduced Roman numerals, Weber popularized them, and Sechter's Stufentheorie influenced Schoenberg and Schenker.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Using lowercase Roman numerals for minor triads
  **Correction**: Caplin uses uppercase exclusively; chord quality is understood from context

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking uppercase numerals imply major chord quality
  **Clarification**: In this system, uppercase is used for all harmonies regardless of quality; quality is determined by key context
- **Confusion**: Treating all seven scale degrees as producing equally distinct harmonies
  **Clarification**: Harmonies group into three functional categories (tonic, dominant, pre-dominant)
