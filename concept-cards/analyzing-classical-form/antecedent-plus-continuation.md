# Antecedent + Continuation (Hybrid Type 1)

**Antecedent + continuation** is the most frequently found hybrid type in the repertoire. It begins like a period but ends like a sentence.

## Structure

- **First phrase (mm. 1-4)**: Standard **antecedent** (basic idea + contrasting idea → weak cadence)
- **Second phrase (mm. 5-8)**: **Continuation** (fragmentation, acceleration → PAC)

## Characteristics

The second phrase does NOT bring a return of the basic idea. Instead, it:
- Begins with new material
- Features fragmentation
- May modulate to a subordinate key
- Projects continuation function (destabilization, forward drive)

## Cadential Pattern

Unlike the sentence (one cadence), this hybrid almost always closes with a PAC to complement the weaker cadence ending the antecedent.

Typical pattern:
- Antecedent: HC (m. 4)
- Continuation: PAC (m. 8)

## Continuational Features

The continuation phrase may not feature marked harmonic acceleration (since the contrasting idea already sped up the harmony toward the HC). Clear **fragmentation** will usually signal the continuation function.

## Modulation

Like the sentence and period, the continuation phrase may modulate to a closely related key.
