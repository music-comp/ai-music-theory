## Definition

An early authentic cadence is a home-key PAC that appears before the end of the minuet's A section. It is followed by another thematic unit that either begins again in the home key and modulates, or begins directly in the subordinate key.

## Significance

The early authentic cadence:
- Marks the end of **main-theme function** within the exposition
- Creates stronger expression of main-theme function
- Distinguishes minuet form from small ternary theme type

## What Closes with Early PAC

Usually one of these:
- A single phrase of exclusively cadential function
- A phrase with formal characteristics of a consequent
- A complete "main theme" consisting of several phrases (in complex A sections)

## Comparison: Minuet Form vs. Small Ternary

| Feature | Minuet Form | Small Ternary |
|---------|-------------|---------------|
| Early authentic cadence | Typical | Rarely found |
| Reason | Highlights thematic functionality | Would create too great a conflict |

In small ternary:
- The juxtaposition of two strong cadences (home key, then subordinate key) would create too dramatic a conflict early in a unit that functions as main theme in a larger context

In minuet form:
- The early authentic cadence helps highlight a broader expression of thematic functionality
- The presence of main-theme function is emphasized

## Example

Beethoven, Violin Sonata in F ("Spring"), Op. 24, iii: First phrase is a 4-m. consequent closing with early PAC; second phrase modulates and closes with PAC in subordinate key.
