# Quick Definition
An early authentic cadence is a home-key PAC appearing before the end of the minuet's A section, marking the end of main-theme function and creating the strongest expression of that function. It is typical of minuet form but rarely found in small ternary.

# Core Definition
The sense of main-theme function is strongest when the opening music closes with a PAC before the end of the section. This early authentic cadence is followed by another thematic unit that begins again in the home key and then modulates, or one that begins directly in the subordinate key. The early authentic cadence is typical of minuet form but rarely found in small ternary, where the juxtaposition of two strong cadences would create too great a dramatic conflict so early in a unit functioning as main theme (Caplin, pp. 636-637).

# Prerequisites
- **Minuet exposition (A section)** -- The section containing this cadence

# Key Properties
1. Home-key PAC before the A section ends
2. Marks the end of main-theme function
3. Followed by material expressing transition and/or subordinate-theme function
4. Typical of minuet form; rare in small ternary
5. Usually closes a single cadential phrase, a consequent, or a complete main theme

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for a home-key PAC that is not the final cadence of the A section
2. Check whether additional material follows in a different key
3. Note the contrast with small ternary, where this would be unusual

# Context & Application
The early authentic cadence highlights the broader expression of thematic functionality within the minuet's A section, distinguishing it from the simpler small ternary.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 639): Beethoven, Violin Sonata in F ("Spring"), Op. 24, iii -- first phrase closes with early PAC; second phrase modulates to subordinate key.
**Example 2** (p. 640): Haydn, Symphony No. 98 in B-flat, iii -- 4-m. cadential phrase creates early PAC at m. 4.
