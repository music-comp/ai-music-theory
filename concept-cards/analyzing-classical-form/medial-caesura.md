# Quick Definition
The medial caesura (MC) is a textural gap -- a moment of silence or fermata -- that often accompanies the final moment of the transition, preceding the subordinate theme. Caplin restricts the term to literal silences or fermatas, and does not view it as a prerequisite for subordinate-theme function (unlike Hepokoski-Darcy).

# Core Definition
The medial caesura is a prominent "gap" in the musical texture that precedes the onset of the subordinate theme. Hepokoski and Darcy give special prominence to this device, proposing that it is fundamental for distinguishing "two-part" from "continuous" expositions. Caplin's form-functional perspective acknowledges the MC as an important characteristic but does **not** see it as a prerequisite for subordinate-theme function. All expositions contain a subordinate theme (or its major elements); the two-part vs. continuous distinction is merely textural and rhythmic, with little functional significance. Caplin **restricts** the term to cases of a literal silence or a fermata on the final sonority (Caplin, Ch. 11, pp. 332-333).

# Key Properties
1. A literal silence or fermata at the end of the transition
2. Precedes the onset of the subordinate theme
3. Caplin: important but NOT required for subordinate-theme function
4. Hepokoski-Darcy: fundamental prerequisite (different view)
5. Caplin restricts the term to literal gaps (not "filled-in" caesuras)
6. Not all transitions end with an MC (especially in slow movements)

# Context & Application
The MC serves as an appropriate (but not required) textural cue for the subordinate theme's entrance. Slow movements in particular may lack sufficient dynamic tension to prompt a postcadential unit or MC.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming every exposition must have a medial caesura
  **Correction**: Many expositions lack a literal MC, especially in slow movements

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Using "medial caesura" for any textural thinning at the transition's end
  **Clarification**: Caplin restricts the term to literal silence or fermata
