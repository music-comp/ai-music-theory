## Medial Caesura

The **medial caesura** is a textural gap (silence) that often accompanies the final moment of the transition, preceding the onset of the subordinate theme.

### Definition

A prominent "gap" in the musical texture that precedes the onset of the subordinate theme. The term was given special prominence by Hepokoski and Darcy.

### Characteristics

- A moment of **silence** or
- A **fermata** on the final sounding sonority of the transition

### Hepokoski-Darcy Theory

These theorists propose:
- The medial caesura is fundamental for distinguishing "two-part" from "continuous" expositions
- The gap is necessary for "opening up" space to permit subordinate theme appearance

### Caplin's Perspective

The form-functional approach:
- Acknowledges the medial caesura as an important characteristic
- Does not see it as a **prerequisite** for subordinate-theme function
- All expositions contain a subordinate theme (or its major elements)
- The distinction between two-part and continuous is textural/rhythmic, with little functional (syntactical) significance

### Usage in This Text

The term "medial caesura" is restricted to cases where:
- A literal caesura is created by a moment of silence, or
- A fermata on the final sounding sonority of the transition
