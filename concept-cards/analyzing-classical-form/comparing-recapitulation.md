# Quick Definition
The principal analytical method for the sonata-form recapitulation involves systematic bar-by-bar comparison with the exposition, noting all structural and ornamental changes and asking the "why" question to suggest compositional rationales.

# Core Definition
As with the A' section of the small ternary, the principal analytical method for the sonata-form recapitulation involves comparison. One works through the section bar by bar, comparing how the various parts relate to the exposition, noting all structural changes (alterations in harmonic-tonal organization and phrase structure) and ornamental changes (instrumentation, dynamics, accompanimental patterning, texture). Some changes are mandated by the general requirements of sonata form (such as adjusting the transition and transposing the subordinate theme), but many others are specific to the individual work.

# Key Properties
1. Principal method is comparison, bar by bar
2. Note all structural changes (harmony, tonality, phrase structure)
3. Note all ornamental changes (instrumentation, dynamics, texture)
4. Ask the "why" question for compositional rationales
5. Some changes are required by sonata form (transition adjustment, subordinate theme transposition)
6. Many changes are specific to the individual work

# Construction / Recognition
## Analytical Steps:
1. Work through recapitulation bar by bar alongside the exposition
2. Note every structural change (required and individual)
3. Note every ornamental change
4. Ask why each change was made
5. Consider whether changes are mandated by sonata form or composer-specific

# Context & Application
This method extends directly from the comparative approach used for small ternary recapitulations (Ch. 7). A significant difference among the classical composers: Mozart and Beethoven normally bring their subordinate-theme groups largely unaltered (except for transposition), while Haydn almost as a rule effects major structural changes to the entire recapitulation, including substantial reworking of the subordinate theme.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming the recapitulation is simply a transposed copy of the exposition
  **Correction**: Even when largely unaltered, ornamental changes are likely; in Haydn, major structural reworking is the norm
