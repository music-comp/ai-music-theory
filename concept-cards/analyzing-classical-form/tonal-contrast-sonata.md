## Tonal Contrast in Sonata Form

The fundamental **tonal contrast** of sonata form is the dramatic conflict between home key and subordinate key.

### The Drama

1. **Exposition**: Conflict is **established**
   - Main theme confirms home key
   - Transition destabilizes home key
   - Subordinate theme confirms rival key

2. **Development**: Conflict is **intensified**
   - Exploration of additional tonal areas
   - Often minor-mode regions
   - Away from both HK and SK

3. **Recapitulation**: Conflict is **resolved**
   - Subordinate theme returns in home key
   - Home key emerges victorious

### Thematic vs. Tonal Contrast

The principal source of contrast between main and subordinate themes lies in **tonality**:
- Main theme resides in home key
- Subordinate theme resides in subordinate key

Melodic-motivic contrast (traditionally emphasized) is actually **secondary** to tonal contrast.

### Even in Monothematic Expositions

When subordinate theme uses main-theme material:
- Tonal contrast still creates formal differentiation
- Different key creates different function
- Monothematic ≠ mono-tonal
