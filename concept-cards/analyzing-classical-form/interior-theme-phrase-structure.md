# Quick Definition
The interior theme is usually modeled on the small ternary (or small binary), but is frequently left structurally incomplete through progressive distortions: the A' section may lack cadential closure, be entirely eliminated (truncated small ternary), or both B and A' sections may be absent.

# Core Definition
The formal organization of an interior theme can usually be related to the small ternary (or small binary), specifically one whose A section modulates to a subordinate key of the minore. The small ternary is frequently left structurally incomplete, often in ways that significantly distort the form (Caplin, pp. 598-604).

# Prerequisites
- **Interior theme** -- The formal function whose phrase structure is detailed
- **Small ternary** -- The model on which interior themes are based
- **Truncated small ternary** -- A specific distortion type

# Key Properties
1. Based on small ternary or small binary, with A section modulating (usually to relative major)
2. Complete form: closes with PAC, followed by retransition
3. A' section initially closed then reopened (via deceptive cadence on repeat)
4. A' section lacks cadential closure (ends with premature dominant arrival)
5. A' section eliminated = truncated small ternary (A-B only)
6. Both B and A' eliminated = most extreme distortion (A section + retransition only)
7. Final harmony is usually dominant of home key

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Identify the A section and its modulation (typically to relative major in minore)
2. Look for B section (contrasting middle) features
3. Check whether A' section exists and whether it achieves cadential closure
4. Distinguish retransition from B section: retransition typically elides with end of A section

# Context & Application
The progressive distortions of the interior theme's form are a hallmark of Caplin's analytical approach. The distinction between retransition and B section is experientially significant: a B section does not typically elide with the end of the A section, while a retransition often does.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 599): Haydn, Piano Trio in A-flat, H. 14, ii -- A' initially closed, then reopened by deceptive cadence in second ending.
**Example 2** (p. 601): Haydn, String Quartet in B-flat, Op. 64, No. 3, ii -- Truncated small ternary (A+B only); B section followed directly by main theme return.
**Example 3** (p. 602): Haydn, String Quartet in E, Op. 54, No. 3, ii -- Both B and A' eliminated; A section followed directly by retransition.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Labeling a retransition as a B section
  **Correction**: A B section forms an independent unit, while a retransition typically elides with the A section and is more directly attached to it
