## Basic Model

The formal organization of an interior theme can usually be related more or less to the basic plan of the small ternary (or small binary), specifically one whose A section modulates to a subordinate key of the minore (usually relative major, sometimes dominant or bVI).

## Complete Form

When the small ternary/binary follows the norm:
- Ends with PAC in opening key of interior theme
- Usually followed by brief retransition
- Retransition leads to home-key dominant
- Prepares return of main theme

## Progressive Distortions

Interior themes are frequently distorted in several ways:

### 1. A' Section Initially Closed, Then Reopened
- First ending: PAC achieved
- Second ending: Deceptive cadence reopens the form
- Additional music leads to main theme's return without further cadential closure

### 2. A' Section Lacks Cadential Closure
- A' section begins normally
- Music leads to dominant arrival instead of PAC
- Gets "stuck" on dominant harmony
- Main theme returns, showing interior theme "ended" with premature dominant

### 3. A' Section Eliminated (Truncated Small Ternary)
- A section + B section only
- B section ends on home-key dominant
- A' section completely eliminated
- Main theme returns directly after B section

### 4. B and A' Sections Eliminated
- Modulating A section only
- Followed directly by retransition (often substantial)
- Most extreme distortion

## Retransition Boundary

The distinction between B section and retransition can be subtle but significant:
- B section does not typically elide with end of A section
- Retransition often begins with such an elision
- Retransition seems more directly attached to A section
