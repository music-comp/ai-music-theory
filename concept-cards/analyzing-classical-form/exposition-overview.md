## Exposition Overview

The **exposition** presents most of the melodic-motivic material used throughout the entire movement and establishes the fundamental tonal conflict between home key and subordinate key.

### Principal Thematic Functions

The exposition contains three principal thematic functions:

1. **Main Theme** - introduces principal melodic-motivic material; closes in home key
2. **Transition** - destabilizes home key; loosens formal organization
3. **Subordinate Theme** - confirms subordinate key with PAC

### Function of Each Component

| Thematic Function | Role |
|-------------------|------|
| Main Theme | Initiating function; establishes tight-knit organization |
| Transition | Medial function; effects modulation or prepares for it |
| Subordinate Theme | Concluding function; confirms new key |

### Landmarks to Identify

When listening to an exposition, identify these landmarks:
- A home-key cadence (usually PAC) marking the end of the main theme
- A subordinate-key HC and standing on the dominant marking the end of a modulating transition
- An SK: PAC marking the end of a subordinate theme
- Any additional SK: PACs marking additional subordinate themes
- A closing section made up of codettas
- A retransition leading back to the home key for the repeat
