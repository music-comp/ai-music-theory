# Quick Definition
Extension occurs when a formal function is expressed over a longer span than would normally be expected, through repetition or elaboration of its constituent elements. It is a primary criterion of loose organization, creating functional redundancy, asymmetrical grouping, and greater dramatic weight.

# Core Definition
A formal unit is extended when it occupies more measures than standard, takes longer to achieve its function, and includes elements that are repeated or elaborated beyond the minimum. Extension is a primary criterion of loose organization in Caplin's theory. In transitions, continuation extension is particularly common: model-sequence technique extends the continuation, fragments are repeated more than necessary, and harmonic sequences prolong the motion. Cadential extension occurs through evaded cadences requiring further attempts, "one more time" technique, and expanded cadential progressions (ECPs). Extension creates rhetorical emphasis on the extended function, greater dramatic weight, delay of the expected goal, and looser formal expression. It frequently produces asymmetrical grouping when other units remain at standard length (Caplin, Ch. 11, pp. 331, 346).

# Prerequisites
- **Continuation** -- The function most commonly extended
- **Tight-knit vs. loose organization** -- Extension is a criterion of loosening

# Key Properties
1. Function occupies more measures than standard
2. Elements repeated or elaborated beyond the minimum
3. Primary criterion of loose organization
4. Creates functional redundancy
5. Produces asymmetrical grouping when combined with standard-length units
6. Common in continuations (model-sequence) and cadential functions (evasion, OMT)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Compare the length of each phrase function to norms
2. A continuation occupying more bars than the presentation suggests extension
3. Look for model-sequence repetitions, cadential evasions, or OMT technique
4. Note the asymmetry created when one function is extended and others are not

# Context & Application
Extension of continuation function is listed as the first loosening device for transitions. It is equally important in subordinate themes, where cadential extension (through evaded cadences, deceptive cadences, and abandoned cadences) is a primary means of delaying the confirming PAC and building dramatic weight. Extension is appropriate for all medial and concluding thematic functions (transition, subordinate theme) and is generally avoided in tight-knit main themes.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing extension with expansion
  **Correction**: Extension adds material within a single function; expansion typically refers to the broadening of a specific harmonic stage (as in an ECP)
