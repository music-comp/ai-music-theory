# Quick Definition
A genuine PAC requires: (1) root-position V, (2) root-position I, (3) soprano ending on scale degree 1, and (4) a proper cadential progression. It is the strongest cadence type, achieving both harmonic and melodic completeness. If the soprano ends on 3 instead of 1, the result is an IAC.

# Core Definition
Authentic cadences are distinguished on the basis of the final scale degree in the soprano voice: a perfect authentic cadence (PAC) ends with the tonic scale degree in the soprano; an imperfect authentic cadence (IAC) ends with the third degree (p. 78). The PAC achieves complete harmonic closure (root-position V resolving to root-position I) and complete melodic closure (soprano arriving on scale degree 1, often approached by step from 2). This combination of harmonic and melodic completeness makes the PAC the strongest of the three cadence types. The general melodic contour of a cadence is descending, reflecting the original sense of cadence as "to fall" (Latin, cadere) (p. 78). A cadential progression must precede the dominant for the cadence to be genuine.

# Key Properties
1. Root-position V resolving to root-position I
2. Soprano ends on scale degree 1
3. Strongest cadence type (harmonically and melodically complete)
4. Often approached by step: 2 to 1 in soprano
5. Melodic contour is typically descending
6. Cadential material often conventional/formulaic (liquidated from characteristic motives)
7. A proper cadential progression must precede the V

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate V-I with both chords in root position
2. Check soprano: must end on scale degree 1
3. If soprano ends on 3 instead, label as IAC
4. Verify a cadential progression precedes the dominant
5. Place label at the point where the final tonic first appears

# Context & Application
The PAC achieves complete closure, which is why it cannot close an antecedent phrase (complete closure would not motivate a consequent) (p. 102). It is the strongest cadence for closing a consequent, a continuation phrase, or any theme that requires full thematic closure. The PAC is the most common cadence for ending sentences and consequent phrases. Its complete closure also makes it the prerequisite for a closing section (postcadential codettas).

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 79, Ex. 2.22a): Beethoven, Op. 2, No. 3, iii -- simple PAC with melodic descent from 3 to 1, complete harmonic progression beginning with root-position I.
**Example 2** (p. 79, Ex. 2.22b): Haydn, H. 16, iii -- PAC with melodic descent from 5 to 1, first supported by I6.
**Example 3** (p. 79, Ex. 2.22d): Haydn, H. 32, ii -- galant cadence, conventional PAC figure beginning on leading tone.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Labeling a cadence as PAC when soprano ends on 3
  **Correction**: Soprano on 3 = IAC; always verify soprano position before labeling

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking any V-I in root position is a PAC
  **Clarification**: A V-I within a tonic prolongation (e.g., end of a presentation) is not a cadence; proper cadential progression and phrase-structural context are required
