# Quick Definition
A subordinate theme may employ a deceptive cadence (V resolving to vi or similar) in place of the expected PAC, extending cadential function by requiring the phrase to be taken up again and eventually brought to the required PAC closure.

# Core Definition
Now and then, a subordinate theme brings a deceptive cadence in place of an expected authentic cadence. Like the IAC, the deceptive cadence provides a temporary goal but one that is insufficiently conclusive -- the listener knows more must happen. The deceptive cadence provides even less sense of closure than an IAC, feeling more interrupted than merely incomplete. Following a deceptive cadence, the ongoing phrase is usually taken up again, often leading to an expanded cadential progression with expanded pre-dominant, and ultimately reaching the required PAC. The deceptive cadence may also trigger a resumption of continuation function before a new cadential attempt (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 386-387).

# Key Properties
1. V resolves to vi (or similar) instead of expected I
2. Less conclusive than even an IAC
3. Phrase is usually repeated or taken up again
4. May lead to expanded pre-dominant in subsequent attempt
5. Eventually reaches required PAC
6. Appropriate loosening device for subordinate themes

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate a cadential progression approaching what seems like a PAC
2. Check the resolution: does V resolve deceptively to vi?
3. Observe what follows: does the phrase resume or repeat?
4. Track subsequent cadential attempts until PAC is achieved

# Context & Application
The deceptive cadence is one of several methods by which cadential function is extended in subordinate themes, alongside evaded cadences, IACs, and abandoned cadences. Its use adds dramatic weight by delaying the confirming PAC and increasing the rhetorical force of the eventual cadential closure.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating a deceptive cadence as equivalent to an evaded cadence
  **Correction**: A deceptive cadence has a specific harmonic identity (V-vi); an evaded cadence involves the expected tonic arriving but failing to function as a cadential goal
