# Quick Definition
The contrasting middle of a small ternary can be built as a continuation phrase, featuring sequential harmonic activity and closing with a half cadence, often followed by a postcadential standing on the dominant.

# Core Definition
When the B section takes the form of a continuation phrase, it typically includes model-sequence technique and sequential harmonic progressions, creating the harmonic destabilization appropriate for a contrasting middle. The section usually closes with a half cadence, which sets up strong expectations for the tonic resolution at the recapitulation. Following the HC, a postcadential standing on the dominant may be added to reinforce the dominant and create stronger expectation for the return. This standing on the dominant may follow directly after the HC or elide with it (beginning at the same moment as the cadential arrival).

# Key Properties
1. B section organized as continuation phrase
2. Features model-sequence technique and sequential harmonic progressions
3. Creates harmonic destabilization appropriate for contrasting middle
4. Usually closes with half cadence
5. Postcadential standing on the dominant may follow the HC
6. Standing on the dominant may elide with the cadence

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for sequential activity in the B section
2. Check for continuation characteristics: fragmentation, sequential progression, acceleration
3. Locate the closing HC
4. Check for postcadential standing on the dominant after the HC
5. Distinguish from B sections that are entirely standing on the dominant (no preceding HC)

# Context & Application
The continuation-based B section represents a middle ground of complexity: more elaborate than a simple standing on the dominant but less complex than a fully modulating B section. The sequential activity creates forward momentum toward the recapitulation while the HC at the end provides a clear structural goal. The optional standing on the dominant after the HC creates heightened expectation for the return of the basic idea.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: A B section ending on dominant must be a standing on the dominant
  **Clarification**: If the B section has harmonic activity leading TO an HC, it is a continuation phrase ending with a cadence, not a standing on the dominant; the latter involves prolongation of dominant throughout
