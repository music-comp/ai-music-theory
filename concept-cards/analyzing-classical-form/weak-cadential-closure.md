# Quick Definition
HC and IAC are considered weak cadences because each leaves some process unclosed. HC (harmonic + melodic incompleteness) is decidedly weaker than IAC (melodic incompleteness alone). Weak cadential closure is essential for antecedent function: it creates the need for the consequent's stronger closure.

# Core Definition
Both the half cadence and the imperfect authentic cadence can be considered weak, because each leaves unclosed some harmonic or melodic process. Of the two, the HC, with its combination of harmonic and melodic incompleteness, is decidedly weaker than the IAC, which results in melodic incompleteness alone. The vast majority of antecedent phrases end with an HC, no doubt to magnify the sense of cadential differentiation. A perfect authentic cadence cannot be used to close an antecedent phrase, since this strong cadence achieves complete harmonic and melodic closure (p. 102).

# Key Properties
1. HC: harmonic AND melodic incompleteness (weakest)
2. IAC: melodic incompleteness alone (moderately weak)
3. HC is decidedly weaker than IAC
4. Vast majority of antecedents end with HC
5. IAC ending antecedent is less common
6. PAC cannot close an antecedent (complete closure would eliminate need for consequent)
7. Exception: reinterpreted HC (PAC=>HC)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. HC: ends on V (root-position triad)
2. IAC: ends on root-position I with scale degree 3 in soprano
3. Both are weak; PAC is strong

# Context & Application
Weak cadential closure is essential for creating the period's effect: incomplete closure motivates the consequent to provide completion. The vast preference for HC over IAC maximizes the sense of cadential differentiation.
