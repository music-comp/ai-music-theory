# Weak Cadential Closure

**Weak cadential closure** refers to cadences that leave some harmonic or melodic process unclosed. Both the half cadence and imperfect authentic cadence are considered weak.

## The Two Weak Cadences

### Half Cadence (HC)
- Ends on dominant harmony
- Combination of harmonic AND melodic incompleteness
- Decidedly weaker than IAC
- Most common for ending antecedent phrases

### Imperfect Authentic Cadence (IAC)
- Ends with tonic but third degree (3) in soprano
- Melodic incompleteness alone (harmonic process complete)
- Less common for ending antecedent phrases

## Essential for Antecedent

A weak cadence is essential for antecedent function. Without it, the phrase cannot properly set up the consequent's stronger closure.

## PAC Cannot End Antecedent

A perfect authentic cadence cannot be used to close an antecedent because:
- It achieves complete harmonic and melodic closure
- No "need" for a consequent would be created
- Exception: The reinterpreted half cadence

## Cadential Strength: Syntactical vs. Rhetorical

**Syntactical strength** is determined by cadence type:
- PAC = strongest
- IAC = moderate
- HC = weakest

**Rhetorical strength** is determined by:
- Metrical position
- Dynamic intensity
- Texture

Two cadences of varying rhetorical strength may still have equivalent syntactical strength if they are of the same type.
