# Quick Definition
The resolution of a root-position dominant to a tonic substitute (usually VI, with bass ascending from 5 to 6) rather than to the expected tonic. The term describes the harmonic motion itself, distinct from the formal deceptive cadential progression.

# Core Definition
In most contexts, the triad built on the submediant (VI) has tonic function and can frequently substitute for an expected I chord, especially when following a root-position V, a situation that can be termed a deceptive resolution of the dominant (p. 31). The deceptive resolution is distinct from the deceptive cadential progression: the resolution describes the V-VI harmonic motion itself, while the cadential progression refers to the complete formal structure within which it occurs.

# Prerequisites
- **Dominant function** — V must resolve for deception to occur
- **Tonic function** — VI functions as tonic substitute

# Key Properties
1. V resolves to VI instead of expected I
2. Bass ascends stepwise from scale degree 5 to 6
3. VI functions as tonic substitute (not as pre-dominant)
4. The leading tone typically resolves up to scale degree 1 (the third of VI)
5. Distinct from deceptive cadential progression (which is a complete formal structure)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for root-position V resolving to VI
2. Check for stepwise bass ascent (5 to 6)
3. Verify VI is functioning as tonic substitute

# Context & Application
Deceptive resolutions can occur in cadential contexts (forming part of a deceptive cadential progression) or in prolongational contexts (VI extending tonic function). When in cadential context, it often prompts continuation or repetition of cadential material.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Labeling every V-VI motion as a "deceptive cadence"
  **Correction**: Distinguish the harmonic resolution (V-VI) from the formal cadence type

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing deceptive resolution with deceptive cadence
  **Clarification**: The resolution is the harmonic motion; the cadence is the formal event in which it occurs
