# Quick Definition
The resolution of a dominant harmony to a tonic substitute (usually VI) rather than to the expected tonic chord.

# Formal Definition
A deceptive resolution occurs when a root-position dominant resolves to some harmony that substitutes for tonic, most commonly the submediant (VI). This resolution maintains the sense of harmonic motion while denying the expected arrival on tonic, creating a sense of harmonic surprise that often leads to continuation or repetition of cadential material.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
The most common deceptive resolution:
- V resolves to VI (stepwise bass motion from 5 to 6)
- The VI chord functions as a tonic substitute
- The leading tone typically resolves up to scale degree 1 (now the third of VI)
- Creates need for continuation toward genuine tonic resolution

The deceptive resolution is distinct from the deceptive cadential progression: the resolution describes the harmonic motion itself, while the cadential progression refers to the complete formal structure.

# Musical Context/Application
Deceptive resolutions appear in:
1. **Deceptive cadential progressions**: The formal context where V-VI replaces V-I
2. **Prolongational contexts**: VI as tonic substitute extends tonic function
3. **Formal articulation**: Often requires continuation or "one more time" technique

The deceptive resolution creates formal ambiguity - the cadential progression ends (with VI), but the expected tonic arrival is denied, often prompting repetition of cadential material.

# Common Confusions
- Confusing deceptive resolution (the V-VI motion) with deceptive cadence (the formal event)
- Thinking any V to non-I motion is deceptive (V-I6 is a different type of deception)
- Assuming deceptive resolution always involves VI (other substitutes are possible but less common)
- Confusing deceptive resolution with evaded cadence (deceptive completes the progression; evaded abandons it)
