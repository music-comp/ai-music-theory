# Quick Definition
Many musicians fail to distinguish between cadences and codettas. The very final measures of a work rarely bring cadences -- they are almost always codettas following an earlier-appearing PAC. The so-called "plagal cadence" (I-IV-I) is almost always a codetta, not a cadence.

# Core Definition
Caplin explicitly addresses this common confusion. When a codetta is supported by a cadential progression (especially if its melody resembles the PAC that closes the theme), it is easy to confuse codettas with cadences. But if you are tempted to speak of "additional cadences" following the final cadence of a theme, consider instead whether these are better understood as codettas. The "plagal cadence" (often as an "Amen" figure) is another instance -- almost all such cases involve a codetta (I-IV-I) whose emphasized subdominant is typical of an after-the-end function.

# Key Properties
1. Final measures are almost always codettas, not cadences
2. Codettas may have cadential-sounding progressions but function postcadentially
3. "Plagal cadence" = typically a codetta with subdominant emphasis
4. A genuine cadence requires a cadential function within the theme; postcadential material is external

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate the theme's structural cadence (the PAC that closes the theme)
2. Everything after that PAC is postcadential (codettas or closing section)
3. Even if postcadential material has cadential-sounding harmony, its function is postcadential

# Context & Application
This distinction is essential for accurate formal analysis. Misidentifying codettas as cadences leads to incorrect identification of formal boundaries and cadential weight.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 175): Caplin discusses how many musicians speak of "the piece finishing with a cadence repeated a number of times" when these are actually codettas.
**Example 2** (p. 172): Mozart, K. 496, ii, mm. 8-12 -- codettas use same material as cadence; first has deceptive resolution, second has authentic version; but both are postcadential.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Speaking of "the final cadence" at the very end of a piece
  **Correction**: The structural cadence usually occurs earlier; final measures are codettas

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Calling I-IV-I a "plagal cadence"
  **Clarification**: This is almost always a codetta with characteristic subdominant emphasis, not a genuine cadence
