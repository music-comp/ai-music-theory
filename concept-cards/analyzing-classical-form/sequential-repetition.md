# Sequential Repetition

**Sequential repetition** (also called **model-sequence technique**) is a type of repetition in which the original unit (model) is completely transposed to another scale degree to create a sequence.

## Definition

Both the melody and the harmony (as well as all accompanying material) are transposed by the **same interval and direction**.

## Harmonic Patterns

Typical harmonic plans:

| Model | Sequence |
|-------|----------|
| I | ii |
| I | viio |

## Rarity in Presentations

Sequential repetition is rarely used at the beginning of a sentence because:
- The overall harmonic support of a presentation phrase must be **tonic prolongational**
- A sequential progression implies harmonic instability
- When used, the sense of broader tonic prolongation usually emerges at some later point within the theme

## Distinction from Statement-Response

Compare situations where melody is transposed by a step:
- **Statement-response**: The harmony changes from I to V (different progression types)
- **Sequential**: Both melody AND underlying harmony are transposed (same progression type at new pitch level)

## Key Principle

You cannot tell the type of repetition by looking at the melody alone. You must consider the **harmonic context** to make an accurate identification.

## Result

Sequential repetition often delays the completion of the tonic prolongation until the continuation phrase is under way.
