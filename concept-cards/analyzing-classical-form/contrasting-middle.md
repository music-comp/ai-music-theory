# Contrasting Middle (B Section)

The **contrasting middle** (B section) achieves contrast with the A section through harmonic, tonal, and phrase-structural means.

## Primary Means of Contrast

Contrast is achieved primarily through:
1. **Harmonic-tonal**: Emphasizes dominant of home key
2. **Phrase-structural**: Looser organization than A section

Secondary contrast may come from:
- New melodic-motivic material
- Changes in texture, dynamics, articulation

## Dominant Emphasis

The B section:
- Often begins with V (dominant)
- Almost always ends with V
- May consist entirely of dominant prolongation

## Typical Phrase Structures

The B section is typically built as:
1. **Standing on the dominant** (simplest case)
2. **Continuation phrase** ending with HC

Other options: sentential structure, sequential passages

## No Elision with A

The contrasting middle always starts in the measure following the A section. The A and B sections **never elide**.

## Loose Organization

The B section is decidedly **looser** than the exposition:
- Less symmetrical
- May undergo extensions/expansions
- Nonconventional design
- Weak or no cadential closure
