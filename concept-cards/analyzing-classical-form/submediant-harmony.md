# Quick Definition
The triad built on the sixth scale degree (VI), which typically functions as a tonic substitute rather than as a pre-dominant, even when it progresses directly to the dominant.

# Core Definition
"The submediant (VI) is often thought to be a pre-dominant harmony, especially when it progresses directly to the dominant. It is generally better, however, to continue viewing VI as a tonic substitute and to recognize instead that, in the progression VI-V, a tonic functioning harmony moves directly to a dominant one, thus bypassing a pre-dominant" (p. 32). VI shares two common tones with I (scale degrees 1 and 3), which accounts for their functional similarity.

# Prerequisites
- **Tonic function** — VI functions as tonic (not pre-dominant)
- **Common tones** — Shared pitches between I and VI explain functional similarity

# Key Properties
1. Built on scale degree 6
2. Primary role: tonic substitute (NOT pre-dominant)
3. Shares scale degrees 1 and 3 with tonic (I)
4. Most common in deceptive resolutions (V-VI)
5. In VI-V, tonic function moves directly to dominant, bypassing pre-dominant
6. Caplin explicitly argues against the common textbook view of VI as pre-dominant

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate a triad built on scale degree 6
2. Classify as tonic substitute (not pre-dominant)
3. In deceptive contexts (V-VI), recognize VI as replacing the expected I
4. In VI-V progressions, understand that pre-dominant is bypassed

# Context & Application
VI appears in deceptive resolutions (V-VI, where VI substitutes for I), deceptive cadential progressions, prolongational contexts extending tonic function, and direct motion to V (bypassing pre-dominant). The chapter explicitly argues against the common textbook view of VI as pre-dominant.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating VI as a pre-dominant when it progresses to V
  **Correction**: In VI-V, a tonic-functioning harmony moves directly to dominant, bypassing pre-dominant

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming VI must be pre-dominant because it can precede V
  **Clarification**: Caplin explicitly argues that VI retains tonic function even when followed by V; the progression simply lacks a pre-dominant
