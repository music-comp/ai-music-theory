# Quick Definition
The triad built on the sixth scale degree (VI), which typically functions as a tonic substitute rather than as a pre-dominant, especially when following a root-position dominant.

# Formal Definition
The submediant (VI) is often thought to be a pre-dominant harmony, especially when it progresses directly to the dominant. It is generally better, however, to continue viewing VI as a tonic substitute and to recognize that in the progression VI-V, a tonic-functioning harmony moves directly to a dominant one, thus bypassing a pre-dominant.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Submediant function:
- Primary role: **Tonic substitute**
- Shares two common tones with tonic (scale degrees 1 and 3)
- Most common in deceptive resolutions (V-VI)
- Can participate in tonic prolongation

The VI-V progression:
- VI is NOT a pre-dominant here
- VI retains tonic function
- The progression bypasses pre-dominant entirely
- Represents direct tonic-to-dominant motion

# Musical Context/Application
Submediant appears in:
1. **Deceptive resolutions**: V-VI (VI substitutes for I)
2. **Deceptive cadential progressions**: Full cadential context with VI replacing final I
3. **Prolongational contexts**: VI as tonic substitute extending tonic function
4. **Direct motion to V**: Bypassing pre-dominant (not true pre-dominant function)

The chapter explicitly argues against the common textbook view of VI as pre-dominant.

# Common Confusions
- Treating VI as a pre-dominant (it's a tonic substitute)
- Assuming VI-V must involve pre-dominant function (it bypasses PD)
- Conflating VI's common tones with tonic as evidence of tonic function (this IS the evidence)
- Not recognizing VI's role in deceptive progressions
