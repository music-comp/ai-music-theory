## Rationale

The sonata-rondo normally includes **four** appearances of the refrain. The composer may feel that this many statements is:
- Unnecessarily redundant
- Overburdens the form

Omitting one refrain does not significantly impair the rondo effect.

## Option 1: Omit Refrain 3 (Mozart's Preference)

Couplet 2 moves directly to couplet 3 (the recapitulation).

### How It Works
- End of couplet 2 brings material from **transition** of couplet 1
- Couplet 3 can begin directly with subordinate-theme group
- The recapitulatory sense of "beginning again" is conveyed by subordinate-theme materials

### Advantage
- Subordinate-theme materials often convey "recapitulation" more effectively than transition materials
- Creates smoother connection between development and recapitulation

## Option 2: Omit Refrain 4 (When Refrain 3 Is Retained)

Mozart usually eliminates the basic structure of refrain 4 within the coda.

### How It Works
- Opening motives of refrain may still pervade the coda texture
- Gives at least minimal sense of "rondo-theme return"
- But the full thematic structure is not present

## Mozart's Tendencies

- Where Mozart retains refrain 3: usually eliminates refrain 4
- Where Mozart omits refrain 3: coda typically contains clearer refrain return
