## Conventional Accompanimental Patterns

The classical period developed several conventionalized rhythmic-contour patterns to accompany the distinctive upper-voice melody.

### Three Common Patterns

**Alberti Bass**: Broken chord pattern (e.g., C-G-E-G repeated)
- Named after Domenico Alberti
- Most famous classical accompaniment pattern

**Drum Bass**: Repeated notes or octaves in regular rhythm
- Creates rhythmic drive
- Often used in energetic passages

**Murky Bass**: Alternating bass and higher chord tones
- Creates a "murky" or oscillating texture
- Similar to but distinct from Alberti bass

### Usage Context

These patterns can appear at any point in a classical movement, including the main theme. They are typically associated with:
- **Medial phrase functions**: continuation and contrasting middle
- **Medial thematic functions**: transition and core of development

### Formal Implications

Their presence often signals:
- Forward momentum
- Continuation function
- Formal loosening
