# Quick Definition
The harmonic function representing stability and repose, primarily expressed by the I chord and its substitute VI.

# Formal Definition
Tonic function is represented by the major or minor triad built on the first scale degree (tonic) of a key, along with the submediant (VI) chord which can substitute for tonic in certain contexts. The tonic triad in root position (I) is considerably more stable than that triad in first inversion (I6), which plays a special role in signaling the onset of cadential progressions.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Tonic-functioning harmonies include:
- **I** (root position): Maximum stability, serves as goal of authentic cadences
- **I6** (first inversion): Less stable, typically initiates cadential progressions
- **VI**: Tonic substitute, especially following root-position V (deceptive resolution)

The first-inversion tonic plays a special role in articulating harmonic directionality, especially by signaling the onset of a cadential progression.

# Musical Context/Application
Tonic function appears in several formal contexts:
1. **At cadential goals**: Root-position I marks authentic cadential arrival
2. **Initiating cadential progressions**: I6 typically begins the cadential formula
3. **Prolongational progressions**: Tonic is extended through subordinate harmonies
4. **Deceptive resolution**: VI substitutes for expected I after V

The stability of root-position tonic makes it the ultimate harmonic goal in authentic cadences, while its first-inversion form signals "beginning of the end" within cadential contexts.

# Common Confusions
- Confusing the role of I6 (which often initiates cadential motion) with the goal I (which concludes cadences)
- Treating VI as a pre-dominant when it typically functions as a tonic substitute
- Assuming all appearances of I have equal structural weight regardless of position
