# Quick Definition
Tonic function represents harmonic stability and repose, primarily expressed by I and its substitute VI. Root-position I is considerably more stable than I6, which plays a special role in signaling the onset of cadential progressions.

# Core Definition
Tonic function is usually represented by the major or minor triad built on the first scale degree of a key. The tonic triad in root position (I) is considerably stabler than that triad in first inversion (I6). The first-inversion tonic plays a special role in articulating harmonic directionality, especially by signaling the onset of a cadential progression. The submediant (VI) has tonic function and can frequently substitute for an expected I chord, especially following root-position V -- a situation termed a deceptive resolution (p. 31).

# Prerequisites
- **Harmonic function** — Must understand the three-function system to classify tonic harmonies

# Key Properties
1. Primary chord: I (major or minor triad on scale degree 1)
2. Root-position I is maximally stable; serves as the goal of authentic cadences
3. First-inversion I6 is less stable; typically initiates cadential progressions
4. VI functions as tonic substitute, especially after root-position V (deceptive resolution)
5. VI is a tonic substitute, NOT a pre-dominant, even when progressing directly to V (p. 32)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify Tonic Function:
1. Look for I or VI chords
2. Root-position I at a cadential goal signals authentic cadential arrival
3. I6 at the start of a harmonic phrase often signals the beginning of a cadential progression
4. VI following V signals deceptive resolution (tonic substitute)

# Context & Application
Tonic function appears in several formal contexts: at cadential goals (root-position I marks authentic cadential arrival), initiating cadential progressions (I6 begins the cadential formula), within prolongational progressions (tonic extended through subordinate harmonies), and in deceptive resolutions (VI substitutes for expected I).

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 37, Ex. 1.11): Cadential progression I6-II6-V7-I shows initial tonic in first inversion and final tonic in root position.

**Example 2** (p. 28, Ex. 1.4): Mozart, K. 332, mm. 50-53 -- tonic prolongation through neighboring V6 chord.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating I6 as having the same structural weight as root-position I
  **Correction**: I6 is less stable and often signals the onset of cadential motion, not a point of arrival

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Classifying VI as pre-dominant when it progresses to V
  **Clarification**: VI is a tonic substitute; in VI-V, tonic function moves directly to dominant, bypassing pre-dominant (p. 32)
