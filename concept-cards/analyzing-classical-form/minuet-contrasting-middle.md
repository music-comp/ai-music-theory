## Definition

The B section of minuet form brings many characteristics of a contrasting middle in small ternary form but is usually more complex and often lasts considerably longer than the preceding A section.

## Shared Characteristics with Small Ternary

- Loose, nonconventional organization
- Emphasis on home-key dominant
- Sequential harmonies
- Closure with HC or dominant arrival in home key

## Distinctive Features in Minuet

### Greater Complexity
Techniques rarely found in small ternary's modest scope:
- **Interpolated episode**: New material in relatively remote tonal region
- **False recapitulation**: Near end of B section
- **Model-sequence technique**: Resembling a developmental core

## Organization Depends on Preceding A Section

### If A Section Modulates
- B section can express rudimentary development
- May explore additional tonal regions
- Brief tonicizations common (rarely cadentially confirmed)
- Simplest case: B section consists entirely of standing on dominant

### If A Section Does Not Modulate
- B section usually provides fundamental tonal contrast
- Moves to and confirms subordinate key
- Employs phrase-structural processes of transition and subordinate-theme functions
- May include interpolated episode and significant model-sequence technique

## Typical Techniques

- Quick tonicization of supertonic region using minuet opening material
- Interpolated episode with new melodic idea and abrupt dynamic shift
- Return to home key confirmed by HC
- Standing on dominant prepares recapitulation
