# Quick Definition
The B section of minuet form shares characteristics with the small ternary's contrasting middle but is usually more complex and longer. It may feature interpolated episodes, false recapitulations, and model-sequence technique. Its organization depends on whether the preceding A section modulates.

# Core Definition
The B section of the minuet brings many characteristics of a contrasting middle in small ternary form: loose nonconventional organization, emphasis on the home-key dominant, sequential harmonies, closure with HC or dominant arrival. But the minuet's B section is usually more complex and often lasts considerably longer. Certain techniques appear there that rarely find a place in the modest scope of the small ternary (Caplin, pp. 643-646).

# Key Properties
1. Shares characteristics with small ternary B section (loose organization, dominant emphasis)
2. Usually more complex and longer than small ternary B section
3. May feature interpolated episodes, false recapitulations, model-sequence technique
4. Organization depends on whether the A section modulates
5. If A modulates: B section is free to explore other regions or return to home key
6. If A does not modulate: B section usually provides transition and subordinate-theme functions

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Determine what the preceding A section established tonally
2. If A modulated: look for developmental character, standing on dominant
3. If A did not modulate: look for transition and subordinate-theme functions
4. Check for interpolated episodes and model-sequence technique

# Context & Application
The B section's role varies significantly depending on the A section's tonal behavior, making the A-B relationship a key analytical consideration in minuet form.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 644): Haydn, Symphony No. 98 in B-flat, iii -- after modulating A, B section tonicizes supertonic, brings interpolated episode in A-flat major, returns to home key with HC.
**Example 2** (p. 646): Haydn, Symphony No. 87 in A, iii -- after nonmodulating A, B section provides transition and genuine subordinate theme with distinct presentation and continuation.
