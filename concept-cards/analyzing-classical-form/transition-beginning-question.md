# Quick Definition
The formal beginning of a transition is not at the point where the music departs from the expected path or where the modulation takes place; that moment stands in the middle of a formal process. The transition's beginning is typically at the start of a new presentation phrase, even if that phrase initially seems like a consequent to the main theme. The identification often happens retrospectively.

# Core Definition
Students may be tempted to locate the beginning of the transition at the point where the music departs from the expected path or where the modulation takes place. This is a mistake: that moment marks the end of main-theme material, but from a strictly formal point of view it represents neither an end (of the main theme) nor a beginning (of the transition). Rather, it stands very much in the middle of a formal process, one that clearly begins earlier with a presentation phrase. The transition's formal beginning is typically at the start of that presentation phrase, even if the phrase initially seems like a consequent to the main theme. The identification happens retrospectively: the initial impression may be that a consequent of a compound period is beginning, but once the music modulates, the listener gives up the idea of the compound period and reinterprets the presentation as the beginning of the transition, confirming that the prior HC truly closed the main theme (Caplin, Ch. 11, pp. 333-334).

# Key Properties
1. Modulation point is NOT the transition's beginning
2. Transition begins at the start of a presentation phrase
3. Modulation point is in the middle of a formal process
4. Initial impression may be that a consequent is starting
5. Retrospective reinterpretation clarifies the true boundary
6. Prior HC is confirmed as main theme's ending

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Do NOT equate the modulation point with the transition's beginning
2. Look backward from the modulation to find the preceding presentation phrase
3. The transition begins at the start of that presentation
4. Consider the prior cadence type: HC suggests dissolving continuation
5. Accept that the identification may require retrospective understanding

# Context & Application
This principle is essential for accurate formal analysis of expositions. Mislocating the transition's beginning leads to incorrect assessment of both the main theme (treating it as longer than it is) and the transition (treating it as shorter). The retrospective nature of the identification reflects a fundamental property of classical form: formal functions are often understood only after subsequent events clarify their true identity.

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 333-334): Beethoven, Piano Sonata Op. 49/1, i -- Main theme (sentence) closes with HC at m. 8; sentence begins to be repeated at m. 9 (seeming consequent); music modulates at m. 12; transition formally begins at m. 9 (not m. 12).
