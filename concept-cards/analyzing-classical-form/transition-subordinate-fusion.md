## Definition

Transition/subordinate-theme fusion is the combining of transition and subordinate theme functions into a single thematic unit. The two functions are traversed more rapidly than when they occupy distinct groups.

## What Gets Eliminated

- Half cadence ending the transition
- Standing on the dominant after transition
- Initiating unit (new basic idea) for the subordinate theme

## How to Identify

The formal boundary between transition and subordinate theme:
- Lies "in the middle" and is recognizable only after the fact
- Fails to be associated with a sense of "ending" (of transition) or "beginning" (of subordinate theme)
- Often occurs at the point where modulation completes and cadential confirmation begins

## Frequency

- Occurs with greater frequency in **slow movements** than in fast movements
- Also regularly found in **minuet form**

## Typical Process

1. Unit begins with identifiable basic idea
2. Model-sequence activity brings modulation to subordinate key
3. New key is confirmed by cadential progressions
4. No clear moment separates "transition ending" from "subordinate theme beginning"

## Example

Beethoven, Piano Sonata in D, Op. 10, No. 3, ii, mm. 9-17:
- Transition begins after main theme closes
- Model-sequence brings modulation to C major
- Key confirmed by IAC and PAC
- Middle of m. 13 stands as boundary, but only recognizable after the fact
