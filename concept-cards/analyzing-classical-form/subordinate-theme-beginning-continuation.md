# Quick Definition
A subordinate theme can begin directly with continuation function, bypassing any initiating unit. The most effective expression is through sequential harmonic progressions (model-sequence technique), since other continuational features (fragmentation, harmonic acceleration) require an immediately preceding initiating function for their effect. This most often occurs with a second or third theme in a group; when it opens the first theme, the effect is especially bold.

# Core Definition
The most effective way of expressing continuation function at the start of a subordinate theme is through **sequential harmonic progressions**, usually with model-sequence technique. Other continuational characteristics (fragmentation, harmonic acceleration, increased surface rhythm) create their most palpable effect in relation to an immediately preceding initiating function, so they are infrequently used alone. A continuation replaces a true structural beginning most often with a **second or third subordinate theme**, where the start "continues" the group as a whole. When it begins the first theme, the effect is especially bold; a more tightly knit second theme may compensate by emphasizing its own presentation (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 413-414).
