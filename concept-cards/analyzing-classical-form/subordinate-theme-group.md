## Subordinate-Theme Group

Many classical sonata expositions contain **multiple subordinate themes**, each of which ends with a PAC in the subordinate key. This creates a **subordinate-theme group**.

### Typical Size

- A typical group consists of **two themes**
- Many large expositions contain **three or four** subordinate themes

### Purpose

Multiple subordinate themes are a principal means for enlarging formal dimensions:
- Greater number of loosening techniques can be employed
- More opportunities for diverse material
- Extends the confirmation of subordinate key

### Labeling System

A functionally neutral numbering system is used:
- "First subordinate theme"
- "Second subordinate theme"
- etc.

This avoids trying to characterize specific functions beyond the order of appearance.

### Boundary Processes

To maintain rhythmic continuity among multiple themes:
- **Elision**: Final tonic of PAC functions simultaneously as initial tonic of next theme
- **Accompanimental overlap**: New accompaniment enters at PAC; theme proper begins next measure
