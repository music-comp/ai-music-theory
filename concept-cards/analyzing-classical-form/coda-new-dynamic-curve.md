# Shaping a New Dynamic Curve in Coda

Using the coda to create a different dynamic trajectory than the recapitulation provides.

## The Normal Situation

If sonata movement ends with recapitulation's closing section, final dynamic curve conforms to that established by exposition's closing section.

## The Opportunity

Sometimes how the exposition ends dynamically is unsuitable for ending complete movement. The composer may use coda to shape a new dynamic curve.

## Progressive Dynamic (Crescendo)

Most striking examples in Beethoven:
- Extensive passages of progressive dynamic
- Lead to powerful climax in movement
- Third Symphony and Seventh Symphony codas feature enormous orchestral crescendos

## Recessive Dynamic (Diminuendo)

Coda can also close with marked recessive dynamic:
- Leading to complete dissolution of texture
- Example: Beethoven's Coriolanus overture

## Example: Mozart K. 457

Exposition and recapitulation close in forte. Coda's opening theme continues forte, but suddenly shifts to piano at PAC. Closing section continues piano, final codetta pianissimo - completely changing the dynamic curve.
