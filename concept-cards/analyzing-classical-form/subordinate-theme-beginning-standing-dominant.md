# Quick Definition
The boundary between transition and subordinate theme can blur when the dominant from the transition is held over to support a standing on the dominant at the subordinate theme's opening. The standing then yields directly to continuation or cadential function (not a standard initiating function). A marked change of melodic-motivic material helps indicate where the transition ends and the subordinate theme begins.

# Core Definition
The boundary between transition and subordinate theme can become blurred when the dominant from the end of the transition is held over to provide harmonic support for a standing on the dominant at the beginning of the subordinate theme. Such a standing would NOT be followed by a standard initiating function (presentation, compound basic idea), because that would suggest the standing belongs to the transition. Instead, the standing yields directly to continuation or cadential functions. A **marked change of melodic-motivic material** helps indicate where the transition ends and the subordinate theme begins. The distinction from a weakened tonic prolongation depends on whether the melodic material implies dominant or tonic prolongation (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 415-417).

# Key Properties
1. Dominant from transition held over into subordinate theme
2. Standing does NOT lead to standard initiating function
3. Instead yields to continuation or cadential functions
4. Change of melodic material signals new theme despite retained harmony
5. Distinguish from weakened tonic: melody implies dominant = standing; tonic = weakened presentation
