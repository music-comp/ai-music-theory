# Quick Definition
The five-part rondo consists of three refrains alternating with two couplets (ABACA). The refrain always appears in the home key, the first couplet is either a subordinate-theme complex or an interior theme, and the second couplet is usually an interior theme. An optional coda may follow.

# Core Definition
The five-part rondo consists of five principal sections: three appearances of the refrain alternate with two couplets, yielding an ABACA pattern. The refrain always appears in the home key, while the couplets are set in contrasting keys or the minore. The first couplet is built either as a subordinate-theme complex (Mozart) or as an interior theme (Haydn). The second couplet is usually an interior theme differing from the first in material, tonal region, and formal plan (Caplin, pp. 664-666).

# Key Properties
1. Five sections: refrain 1, couplet 1, refrain 2, couplet 2, refrain 3
2. Refrain always in home key; couplets in contrasting key or minore
3. First couplet: subordinate-theme complex (Mozart) or interior theme (Haydn)
4. Second couplet: usually interior theme in minore, IV, or vi
5. Optional coda after refrain 3
6. Most often for slow movements; sometimes fast finales; rarely fast openings

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Count three statements of the refrain with two contrasting couplets
2. Determine whether couplet 1 is a subordinate-theme complex or interior theme
3. Confirm couplet 2 differs in material, key, and formal plan from couplet 1

# Context & Application
The five-part rondo is used most often for slow movements within instrumental cycles. It is the preferred rondo type for slow movements by all three major classical composers, though Haydn and Mozart differ in how they construct the first couplet.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 667): Mozart, Piano Sonata in C, K. 545, iii -- first couplet as subordinate-theme complex with highly compressed transition and subordinate theme.
