# Quick Definition
Phrase deviations may result in symmetrical grouping structures (5+5, 6+6) or asymmetrical ones (4+6). In periods, deviations frequently maintain symmetry between antecedent and consequent; in asymmetrical cases, the first phrase almost always keeps its normative length while the second is altered.

# Core Definition
Caplin distinguishes symmetrical deviations (where both phrases are altered to maintain equilibrium, e.g., 5+5 or 6+6) from asymmetrical deviations (where typically only the second phrase is altered, e.g., 4+6). In the period, the 4+4 grouping suggests symmetrical organization more strongly than in the sentence, since the consequent repeats the antecedent. For this reason, phrase deviations in periods frequently maintain equilibrium: if the antecedent is lengthened, the consequent is likely altered similarly (though possibly by different deviation techniques). Despite apparent symmetry in grouping (5+5), cadence points may still define an asymmetrical pattern (4+6).

# Key Properties
1. Symmetrical: both phrases altered (5+5, 6+6)
2. Asymmetrical: typically only second phrase altered (4+6)
3. Periods favor symmetrical deviations due to parallel construction
4. First phrase almost always normative length in asymmetrical cases
5. Grouping symmetry may mask cadential asymmetry

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Count the measures of each phrase
2. Compare to normative 4+4
3. If both phrases deviate: symmetrical
4. If only one deviates: asymmetrical (almost always the second)
5. Check cadence points -- they may reveal asymmetry within apparent grouping symmetry

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 163): Haydn, Op. 54/1, iii, mm. 1-10 -- 5+5 grouping (symmetrical); HC extended by 1 m. in antecedent, contrasting idea expanded in consequent. But cadence points define 4+6 (asymmetrical).
