## Hallmark of Concerto Style

Enormous expanded cadential progressions are a **hallmark of the concerto**. They provide the structural space for virtuosic display by the soloist.

## The Rhythmic and Dynamic Climax

- The rhythmic and dynamic **climax of the exposition** is normally reached during expansion of the final cadential dominant
- Features the shortest durational values
- Culminates in the conventional **"cadential trill"**
- The cadential trill is the conventional sign for the close of the solo exposition

## Function

Extended sequences and expanded cadential progressions:
1. Loosen the structure appropriately for subordinate-theme function
2. Open up considerable **structural space** for virtuosic display
3. Allow exploration of extreme ranges of the instrument
4. Show off the performer's technique

## Passage Work

These places in the form are usually filled with:
- Figurational passage work
- Scales
- Arpeggiations
- Other conventional virtuosic patterns

## Genre Influence

The concerto style permeated other genres of the period:
- Solo sonata
- String quartet

The appearance of a broad ECP leading to a cadential trill **immediately brings the idea of concerto to mind**, regardless of the actual genre of the work.

## Comparison to Sonata

While sonata form also uses ECPs, the concerto:
- Uses them more extensively
- Stretches them to greater lengths (dominant held for 10+ measures)
- Makes virtuosic display their primary purpose
