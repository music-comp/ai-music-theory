# Coda Section

An optional section that follows the recapitulation, expressing an "after-the-end" function.

## Definition

The coda is an optional section following the recapitulation and fully distinct from it. The fundamental melodic, harmonic, and tonal processes of the movement are completed within the recapitulation - the coda has no "essential" function to serve.

## Schoenberg's View

"Since many movements have no codas, it is evident that the coda must be considered as an extrinsic addition... it would be difficult to give any other reason for the addition of a coda than that the composer wants to say something more."

## Key Characteristics

- Remains in the home key (rarely initiates key changes)
- Contains prominent tonicizations and sequential progressions early on
- Ascending-stepwise sequence particularly popular
- Brings PAC in home key
- Typically ends with closing section (codettas)

## Determining the Start

The coda starts at that point where the music of the recapitulation stops corresponding to the exposition.
