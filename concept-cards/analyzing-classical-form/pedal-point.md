# Quick Definition
The most forceful way of prolonging a harmony, in which the root of the prolonged harmony is sustained in the bass throughout the progression while subordinate harmonies sound above. Subordinate harmonies lose their bass voice, so their inversions cannot be determined.

# Core Definition
The most forceful way of prolonging a harmony arises by means of pedal point. The pedal, which lies in the bass voice throughout the progression, contains the root of the prolonged harmony. Most often, this harmony appears at the beginning and end of the progression. The bass note of the subordinate harmonies is replaced by the pedal note, thus significantly reducing the structural status of these harmonies (p. 33).

# Prerequisites
- **Prolongational progression** — Pedal point is one of four prolongation techniques

# Key Properties
1. Bass voice sustains or repeats the root of the prolonged harmony throughout
2. Most forceful type of prolongation
3. Subordinate harmonies lose their bass voice (replaced by pedal)
4. Inversions cannot be determined for subordinate harmonies (analyzed in root position unless context implies otherwise)
5. Prolonged harmony typically frames the progression (beginning and end)
6. Notation: "ped." after Roman numeral; subordinate harmonies in parentheses

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify a Pedal Point:
1. Look for a sustained or repeated bass note
2. Verify that the bass note is the root of the prolonged harmony
3. Note that chords above the pedal do not have their own bass voice
4. Label: I ped.-(IV-V7)-I

# Context & Application
Pedal points create strong prolongation through bass stability. Common at openings of themes (tonic pedal) and in development sections (dominant pedal). Because subordinate harmonies lose their bass voice, their position (inversion) cannot be determined -- they are analyzed in root position unless context implies otherwise.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assigning inversions to subordinate harmonies within pedal points
  **Correction**: Their bass is replaced by the pedal; inversions are indeterminate

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking pedal must always be on tonic
  **Clarification**: Dominant pedals and other pedals also exist
- **Confusion**: Confusing pedal point with sustained bass notes in non-prolongational contexts
  **Clarification**: Pedal point specifically involves prolongation with subordinate harmonies above
