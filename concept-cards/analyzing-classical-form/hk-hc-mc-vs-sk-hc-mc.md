## I:HC MC vs. V:HC MC

In the Hepokoski-Darcy theory (which Caplin acknowledges but modifies), two types of half-cadence/medial-caesura patterns are distinguished.

### I:HC MC (Home Key Half Cadence)

- Transition ends with HC **in the home key**
- Nonmodulating transition
- Home key is destabilized by arriving on its dominant
- Subordinate theme then begins in subordinate key through direct modulation

### V:HC MC (Subordinate Key Half Cadence)

- Transition ends with HC **in the subordinate key**
- Modulating transition
- Already in the new key when standing on dominant occurs
- Subordinate theme begins with tonic of subordinate key

### Caplin's View

While acknowledging this terminology from Hepokoski-Darcy:
- The distinction refers to whether transition is modulating or nonmodulating
- The medial caesura itself is a textural feature, not a prerequisite
- Both types lead to a subordinate theme; the difference is in tonal path

### Implications for Analysis

| Type | Transition | Subordinate Theme Entry |
|------|-----------|------------------------|
| I:HC MC | Nonmodulating | Direct modulation at start |
| V:HC MC | Modulating | Already in new key |
