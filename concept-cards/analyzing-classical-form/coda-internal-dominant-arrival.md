# Quick Definition
An internal dominant arrival within a coda theme creates a structure resembling a subordinate theme, with a premature dominant arrival, standing on the dominant, and a new continuation leading to the final PAC.

# Core Definition
A coda theme may feature an internal dominant arrival that functions analogously to the premature dominant arrival found in subordinate themes. The dominant arrival is followed by a standing on the dominant, after which a new continuation brings a clear continuation function leading eventually to an expanded cadential progression and the final PAC. When this structure appears, the coda theme is organized less like a development core and more like a subordinate theme with internal dominant arrival. Although model-sequence passages at the beginning of a coda may initially suggest a core, the harmonies remain closely allied with the home key, distinguishing the passage from true developmental activity.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of the coda and its phrase-structural organization
- Knowledge of dominant arrivals and standing on the dominant

# Key Properties
- Premature dominant arrival within the coda theme
- Standing on the dominant follows the arrival
- New continuation function leads to expanded cadential progression
- Final PAC provides powerful closure
- Harmonies remain closely allied with home key (unlike development cores)
- Creates subordinate-theme-like organization within the coda

# Construction / Recognition
Identify a passage in the coda where the music arrives on the dominant before any cadential process has been initiated. If standing on the dominant follows, and a new continuation then leads to an expanded cadential progression and PAC, the coda theme is structured like a subordinate theme with internal dominant arrival.

# Context & Application
This structure appears in coda themes that need to build significant weight and momentum toward the movement's final cadential goal. The subordinate-theme-like organization, with its loosening devices and expanded cadential area, allows the coda to achieve a powerful sense of closure that matches or exceeds the essential sonata closure in the recapitulation.

# Examples
- Beethoven, String Quartet in F, Op. 18, No. 1, i: dominant remains "stuck" at m. 290 for four measures (premature dominant arrival and standing on dominant), then clear continuation leads to expanded cadential progression (mm. 298-302) and final PAC
- Haydn, Symphony No. 104 ("London"), iv: second coda theme features internal dominant arrival at m. 293, standing on dominant, then expanded cadential progression (mm. 301ff.) and powerful PAC at m. 309

# Common Errors
- Analyzing a coda theme's model-sequence passage as a genuine development core
- Failing to recognize the subordinate-theme-like organization when internal dominant arrival is present

# Common Confusions
- A coda theme with model-sequence technique is NOT a "terminal development" -- the harmonies remain bound to the home key and the overall organization resembles a subordinate theme, not a development core
