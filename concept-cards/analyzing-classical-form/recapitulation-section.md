# Recapitulation Section

The third section of sonata form, functioning to resolve the principal tonal and melodic processes left incomplete in earlier sections.

## Definition

The final section of the form (unless a coda follows), providing symmetry and balance by restating the melodic-motivic material of the exposition.

## Basic Principle

The recapitulation brings back, in the same order, the three basic thematic units of the exposition:
- Main theme
- Transition
- Subordinate theme (group)

## Key Functions

1. Resolve fundamental tonal conflict (subordinate key returns to home key)
2. Provide formal symmetry and balance
3. Restate melodic-motivic material
4. Typically remains throughout in the home key

## Relationship to Small Ternary A' Section

Like an A' section, the recapitulation:
- Begins in home key with basic idea of main theme
- Adjusts material to remain in home key
- Confirms home key with concluding PAC
- May modify formal organization

Unlike an A' section, the recapitulation is roughly the same length as the exposition.
