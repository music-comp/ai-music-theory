# Quick Definition
The chromatically raised fourth degree of the scale, functioning as the leading tone of the dominant and defining the entire category of V/V-type pre-dominants and augmented-sixth chords.

# Core Definition
"These harmonies feature the chromatic raised-fourth scale degree, which functions as the leading tone of the dominant. The significance of the raised-fourth degree is highlighted by its normally being placed in the bass voice, so that the motion to the root of the following dominant is all the more enhanced" (p. 32). The raised fourth defines the entire category of pre-dominants derived from V/V, including augmented-sixth chords.

# Prerequisites
- **Scale degree** — Must understand diatonic scale degree 4 to recognize the alteration
- **Chromatic alteration** — The raised fourth is a specific chromatic alteration

# Key Properties
1. Functions as the leading tone of V (half-step below dominant root)
2. Typically placed in bass voice to emphasize resolution to V
3. Defines VII7/V (most typical V/V-type pre-dominant)
4. Defines V/V and V6/V (less dissonant alternatives)
5. Present in all three augmented-sixth chords (It+6, Gr+6, Fr+6)
6. Distinguishes V/V-type pre-dominants from IV-type pre-dominants

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for a chromatically raised fourth scale degree (sharp on the natural 4)
2. Check whether it appears in the bass (most common, most emphatic placement)
3. Verify resolution upward to scale degree 5 (dominant root)
4. Classify the containing chord: VII7/V, V/V, or augmented sixth

# Context & Application
The raised fourth intensifies motion toward the dominant, creating a chromatic half-step approach to V. Its bass placement creates particularly strong resolution. The raised fourth unifies an entire category of pre-dominants (V/V, VII7/V, augmented sixths) that might otherwise appear unrelated. Augmented-sixth chords are "usually built over the sixth degree of the natural minor scale" but share the raised 4 as a defining element.

## Enables
- **Dominant of the dominant** — Raised 4 is the defining element of this pre-dominant category

## Related
- **Italian sixth** — Contains raised 4 as one of its three essential pitches
- **German sixth** — Contains raised 4 alongside lowered 6, 1, and lowered 3
- **French sixth** — Contains raised 4 alongside lowered 6, 1, and natural 2
## Contrasts With
- None specific; contrasts with the natural fourth degree in IV-type pre-dominants

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Not recognizing raised 4 as the unifying element of V/V-type pre-dominants
  **Correction**: The raised fourth defines and connects VII7/V, V/V, and all three augmented-sixth types

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Missing the connection between raised 4 and augmented-sixth chords
  **Clarification**: Augmented-sixth chords are "an important subclass" of the raised-fourth pre-dominant category
