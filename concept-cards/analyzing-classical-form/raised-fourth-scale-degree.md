# Quick Definition
The chromatically raised fourth degree of the scale, which functions as the leading tone of the dominant and is the defining characteristic of V/V and augmented-sixth harmonies.

# Formal Definition
The raised fourth scale degree functions as the leading tone of the dominant within pre-dominant harmonies derived from the dominant of the dominant (V/V). The significance of the raised-fourth degree is highlighted by its normally being placed in the bass voice, so that the motion to the root of the following dominant is all the more enhanced.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
The raised fourth degree appears in:
- **VII7/V**: Most typical pre-dominant of this type
- **V/V, V6/V**: Less dissonant forms
- **Augmented-sixth chords**: Italian, German, French sixths

Characteristics:
- Functions as leading tone of V
- Creates half-step motion to dominant root
- Typically placed in bass to emphasize motion
- Chromatic alteration of the diatonic fourth degree

The raised fourth defines the entire category of pre-dominants derived from V/V.

# Musical Context/Application
The raised fourth:
- Intensifies motion toward the dominant
- Creates chromatic approach to V
- Distinguishes V/V-type pre-dominants from IV-type pre-dominants
- In bass, creates particularly strong resolution to V

The augmented-sixth chords feature this degree "usually built over the sixth degree of the natural minor scale" but contain raised 4 as a defining element.

# Common Confusions
- Not recognizing raised 4 as the unifying element of V/V-type pre-dominants
- Missing the connection between raised 4 and augmented-sixth chords
- Overlooking the preference for bass placement
- Confusing with the natural fourth degree in IV-type pre-dominants
