# Quick Definition
The transition is the medial thematic function of the exposition, functioning to destabilize the home key so the subordinate key can emerge as a rival tonality, and to loosen the formal organization established by the main theme. Most transitions modulate to the subordinate key and close with an HC or dominant arrival.

# Core Definition
The transition functions to destabilize the home key in order for the subordinate key to emerge as a rival tonality, and it begins to loosen the formal organization initially defined by the tight-knit main theme. Most transitions modulate to the subordinate key, closing there with an HC or dominant arrival. A minority of transitions are nonmodulating, closing instead on dominant harmony of the home key. The phrase-structural organization is usually based on sentential functions (presentation, continuation, cadential); periodic formations are rarely found in transitions. Loosening is achieved through harmonic and tonal instability, asymmetrical grouping structure, extended continuation function, and obscured cadential articulation.

# Key Properties
1. Destabilizes the home key
2. Loosens formal organization relative to main theme
3. Most transitions modulate to subordinate key
4. Minority are nonmodulating (close on home-key dominant)
5. Usually sentential phrase structure (not periodic)
6. Typically concludes with standing on the dominant
7. Loosening through harmonic instability, asymmetry, extended continuation, obscured cadences

# Construction / Recognition
## Loosening Techniques:
| Technique | Main Theme | Transition |
|-----------|------------|------------|
| Tonal stability | Stable (home key) | Unstable (modulation or disruption) |
| Grouping symmetry | Symmetrical (4+4) | Asymmetrical (2+6+4) |
| Functional efficiency | Compact and efficient | Extended, redundant |
| Cadential clarity | Clear cadence | Obscured cadential articulation |
| Presentation phrase | Full, solid | May be absent or abbreviated |

# Examples
**Example** (pp. 288-289): Beethoven, Op. 2, No. 1, i, mm. 9-20 -- begins with main theme's basic idea in lower voice over C-minor harmony (disrupting home key); basic idea not repeated (no presentation); continuation extended by 2 mm. with descending-fifth sequence; HC in A-flat at m. 16; standing on the dominant with repeated HC idea (mm. 17-20). Loosening through: C-minor harmony disrupting home key, asymmetrical grouping (2+6+4), extended continuation, obscured cadential articulation.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Formal loosening in the transition reflects compositional weakness
  **Clarification**: Loosening techniques are entirely appropriate to the transition's function; deft handling of loosening devices is a sure sign of compositional skill (Caplin, p. 289)
