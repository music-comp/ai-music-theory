## Transition Overview

The **transition** is the medial thematic function of the exposition, functioning to destabilize the home key and loosen the formal organization.

### Primary Functions

1. **Destabilize the home key** so the subordinate key can emerge as a rival tonality
2. **Loosen the formal organization** (phrase-structural destabilization)

### Tonal Organization

- **Modulating transition**: Most transitions modulate to the subordinate key, closing there with an HC (or dominant arrival)
- **Nonmodulating transition**: Some transitions remain in the home key, closing on dominant harmony of the home key

### Phrase Structure

- Usually based on sentential functions (presentation, continuation, cadential)
- Periodic formations are rarely found in transitions

### Loosening Techniques

Compared to the main theme, the transition acquires a looser organization through:
- Harmonic and tonal instability
- Asymmetrical grouping structure
- Extended continuation function
- Obscured cadential articulation

### Standing on the Dominant

Most transitions conclude with a postcadential **standing on the dominant**, which liquidates motives and texture to prepare for the subordinate theme.
