# Quick Definition
Sturm und Drang ("storm and stress") describes the dramatic, emotionally intense character typical of development cores, achieved through forte dynamic, increased rhythmic activity, "chugging" accompaniment patterns, polyphonic complexity, minor modality, and chromaticism.

# Core Definition
The core of a development section typically projects an emotional quality of instability, restlessness, and dramatic conflict -- a character often described as Sturm und Drang (storm and stress). The dynamic level is usually forte, and the general character is achieved through a marked increase in rhythmic activity projected by conventionalized accompanimental patterns (such as an Alberti bass or a murky bass), which impart a kind of "chugging" effect. Polyphonic devices -- imitation, canon, fugal entries -- can contribute further to the complexity of the texture. Minor modality and chromaticism intensify the sense of dramatic conflict. If the core often bursts out with Sturm und Drang, the pre-core can be likened to the calm before the storm: more relaxed, hesitant, anticipatory, with softer dynamics.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of the development section and its core organization

# Key Properties
- Forte dynamic level
- Marked increase in rhythmic activity
- "Chugging" accompanimental patterns (Alberti bass, murky bass)
- Polyphonic complexity (imitation, canon, fugal entries)
- Minor modality and chromaticism
- Sudden dynamic contrasts
- Instability, restlessness, and dramatic conflict
- Contrasts with the more relaxed, hesitant pre-core

# Construction / Recognition
Identify the core of the development section (large-scale model, sequential repetition, fragmentation, HC or dominant arrival). The Sturm und Drang character manifests as a dramatic increase in intensity compared to the pre-core: louder dynamic, faster rhythmic activity, more complex texture, and more intense harmonic language (minor mode, chromaticism). The contrast with the preceding pre-core is the primary marker.

# Context & Application
The term Sturm und Drang derives from a late 18th-century German literary movement emphasizing emotional extremes and individual subjectivity, which influenced musical aesthetics of the period. In sonata form analysis, it describes the specific emotional quality that development cores achieve through their combination of rhythmic, textural, and harmonic intensification. The contrast between the "calm before the storm" (pre-core) and the "storm" itself (core) is one of the most dramatic formal events in the Classical style.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in A minor, K. 310, i, mm. 58ff.: core sets up 4-measure model of Sturm und Drang character, sequenced twice down a perfect fifth
- General pattern: pre-core is piano, hesitant, anticipatory; core bursts out forte with driving rhythmic activity and minor-mode intensity

# Common Errors
- Applying the term Sturm und Drang to any dramatic passage outside the development section context
- Treating Sturm und Drang as an all-or-nothing quality rather than a matter of degree

# Common Confusions
- Sturm und Drang in the musical context is NOT identical to the literary movement -- it describes a specific emotional quality of development cores characterized by intensity, conflict, and instability, borrowing the literary term by analogy
- The Sturm und Drang character of the core is NOT simply "loud music" -- it requires the specific combination of rhythmic intensification, textural complexity, and harmonic intensity
