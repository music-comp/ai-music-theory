# Harmonic Acceleration

**Harmonic acceleration** refers to a faster rate of harmonic change in the continuation phrase compared to the presentation phrase.

## Identification

In the clearest cases, harmonic acceleration is evident when:
- A single harmony supports each basic idea in the presentation
- Multiple harmonies appear within fragments of the continuation

## Complexity in Analysis

It can be tricky to determine harmonic acceleration when the basic idea contains multiple harmonies. In such cases:
- Some harmonies within the basic idea may be merely **ornamental**
- They may not affect the broader sense of harmonic rhythm
- The "effective" harmonic rhythm of the presentation may be slower than literally notated

## Judging Harmonic Acceleration

Identifying harmonic acceleration is not a mechanical procedure but relies on musical judgment and experience. Consider:
- The structural weight of harmonies (root position vs. embellishing)
- The perception of harmonic change rate
- The context of the musical phrase

## Compensation for Lack of Fragmentation

Harmonic acceleration is particularly effective when used to express continuation function in the absence of fragmentation. Even ornamental harmonies in the continuation can project harmonic acceleration compared to the presentation.

## Rarity as Sole Indicator

Harmonic acceleration alone is rarely the sole indicator of continuation function. It is typically used in conjunction with other continuational criteria:
- Fragmentation
- Faster surface rhythm
- Sequential progressions
