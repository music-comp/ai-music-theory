# Quick Definition
A codetta is a postcadential unit that follows a PAC, prolonging the achieved tonic harmony and reinforcing the sense of cadential arrival. Codettas typically feature a tonic pedal, conventionalized melodic material (scales, arpeggios), and a recessive dynamic character, with successive codettas undergoing fragmentation.

# Core Definition
Within the closing section of a sonata exposition, codettas prolong the tonic harmony achieved by the preceding PAC. They are characterized by tonic pedal support, melodies that often descend from the tonic scale degree, a generally recessive dynamic character, and conventionalized material such as scales and arpeggios. The closing section typically contains several codettas: a first codetta (usually 2-4 measures) establishes a pattern, which is then repeated; subsequent codettas are usually shorter (through fragmentation), creating an overall sense of compression. The melodic content of codettas usually contrasts with the cadential ideas that closed the subordinate theme, though occasional significant motivic references to main-theme material may appear. A retransition sometimes follows the final codetta, leading back to the home key for the exposition repeat (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 410-412).

# Key Properties
1. Postcadential units following a PAC
2. Prolong tonic harmony, usually with tonic pedal
3. Melody often descends from tonic scale degree
4. Generally recessive dynamic character
5. Conventionalized material (scales, arpeggios)
6. Successive codettas undergo fragmentation (compression)
7. Melodic content usually contrasts with preceding cadential ideas

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate the PAC closing the subordinate theme
2. Look for tonic pedal and conventionalized melodic material following the PAC
3. Check for repetition and fragmentation of an opening idea
4. Note the recessive dynamic character
5. Track the compression of successive codettas

# Context & Application
Codettas function as the building blocks of the closing section, which follows the confirming PAC of the subordinate theme (or theme group). The general sense of compression through fragmentation is typical, though expansion is rare. A retransition may grow out of the final codetta or introduce different material to prepare the exposition repeat.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing a false closing section with genuine codettas
  **Correction**: A false closing section looks like codettas but is retrospectively understood as the initiating unit (presentation) of the transition; genuine codettas truly prolong the preceding cadential arrival
