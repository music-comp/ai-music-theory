# Quick Definition
The dominant harmony concluding the B section is sustained into the following phrase rather than resolving immediately to tonic. The dominant often resolves deceptively to VI, after which the phrase concludes with a PAC. The resulting phrase defies conventional functional labeling.

# Core Definition
Another common procedure for Part 2: the goal dominant of the contrasting middle is prolonged well into the subsequent phrase. The dominant does not resolve to tonic at the start of the final phrase; instead, it continues to be prolonged, often resolving deceptively to VI before the closing PAC. This is NOT a deceptive cadence -- it is simply a deceptive resolution within the phrase. The resulting phrase cannot be easily labeled with conventional function names, since it lacks initial tonic support (weakening consequent function).

# Key Properties
1. Dominant from B section extends into final phrase
2. No tonic at start of final phrase (unlike most patterns)
3. Often resolves deceptively to VI before PAC
4. Deceptive RESOLUTION, not deceptive CADENCE
5. Phrase defies conventional labeling

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 273): Haydn, Sonata H. 22, iii -- contrasting middle ends with HC; dominant continues; deceptive resolution to VI at m. 14; PAC at m. 16.
