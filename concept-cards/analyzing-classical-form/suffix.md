# Quick Definition
A suffix is postcadential material following a PAC that is more elaborate than a simple codetta, potentially introducing new melodic ideas while still confirming tonic closure. Note: Caplin does not use the term "suffix" -- his framework uses "closing section" composed of "codettas." The concept of a more elaborate postcadential unit is captured by Caplin's description of codettas that can range from a single chord up to a full 4-m. phrase with varied melodic content.

# Core Definition
Caplin's framework describes postcadential material after a PAC as a closing section comprising codettas (p. 156). Individual codettas can be as short as a single chord or as long as a 4-m. phrase (p. 169). Their motivic content often differs from the theme's cadential material, and they may introduce varied melodic content while still prolonging tonic harmony and confirming closure. The term "suffix" is not used by Caplin but appears in other formal theories to describe more elaborate postcadential material. In Caplin's terms, such material would be a more extended codetta or series of codettas within a closing section. A closing section may begin in the measure following the cadence or else elide with the moment of cadential arrival (p. 156).

# Key Properties
1. Postcadential: follows a PAC (external to the theme proper)
2. More elaborate than a simple codetta (potentially new melodic material)
3. Still functions to confirm tonic closure
4. Prolongs tonic harmony (often with tonic pedal)
5. Not counted in the theme's measure structure
6. Melody typically revolves around or approaches scale degree 1
7. Codettas within a closing section often show fragmentation
8. Dynamic character may be recessive or sustained

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate a PAC ending the theme
2. Check for material following the PAC that prolongs tonic
3. Assess whether the material is simple (single chord, basic figuration) or elaborate (new melodic ideas, extended phrase)
4. Verify it does not reopen melodic space as a new basic idea would
5. Note that the material is postcadential and external to the theme

# Context & Application
Closing sections with more elaborate codettas appear frequently in subordinate themes and at the ends of larger formal sections. The codetta's melody tends to revolve around scale degree 1 or has that degree as its melodic goal, avoiding the "opening up" of melodic space that would suggest a new basic idea (p. 157). Codettas often use tonic pedal for maximum stability. When a closing section contains multiple codettas, fragmentation of the grouping structure is common (p. 157). The dynamic may be recessive (dying away) but is not always so (p. 168).

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 156, Ex. 5.7): Beethoven, Op. 22, ii -- PAC at m. 9; 1-m. codetta with descending melody from 5 to 1 over V-I; repeated down an octave; then fragmentation to one-beat codettas.
**Example 2** (p. 169, Ex. 5.18): Mozart, K. 331, iii -- closing section codettas built exclusively on root-position tonic harmony.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating elaborate postcadential material as a new theme
  **Correction**: If it follows a PAC, prolongs tonic, and does not reopen melodic space, it is postcadential (closing section) regardless of elaborateness

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming "suffix" is Caplin's term
  **Clarification**: Caplin uses "closing section" and "codetta"; "suffix" comes from other theoretical traditions. In Caplin's framework, what might be called a suffix is an elaborate codetta or extended closing section
