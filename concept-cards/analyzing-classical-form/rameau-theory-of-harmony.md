# Quick Definition
The foundational theoretical system established by Jean-Philippe Rameau in the early 18th century that forms the basis of modern harmonic theory, including the concepts of chord roots and systematic harmonic organization.

# Core Definition
"The modern theory of harmony was founded by Jean-Philippe Rameau early in the 18th century" (p. 24). His theoretical work established foundational concepts including the fundamental bass (chord root) and the systematic organization of harmonic progressions. His work preceded and influenced all subsequent harmonic theory: the Roman numeral system (Abbe Vogler, Gottfried Weber), Stufentheorie (Simon Sechter), and Funktionstheorie (Hugo Riemann).

# Prerequisites
- None; Rameau's theory is the historical starting point

# Key Properties
1. Founded the modern theory of harmony in the early 18th century
2. Established the concept of fundamental bass (chord root)
3. Systematized understanding of chord inversions
4. Influenced all subsequent harmonic theory
5. Preceded Roman numeral analysis, Stufentheorie, and Funktionstheorie

# Context & Application
Rameau's legacy pervades all modern harmonic analysis. All Roman numeral analysis derives from his root-based approach, the concept of harmonic function builds on his foundations, and contemporary analytical practice continues to use his basic concepts. Caplin positions Rameau as the historical starting point for the theoretical tradition he draws upon.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Attributing Roman numerals to Rameau
  **Correction**: Roman numerals came later, introduced by Abbe Vogler and popularized by Gottfried Weber

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming modern analysis simply applies Rameau's original system
  **Clarification**: Modern analysis has evolved substantially through Sechter, Riemann, Schoenberg, Schenker, and others
