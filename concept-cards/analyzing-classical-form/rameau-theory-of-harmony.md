# Quick Definition
The foundational theoretical system established by Jean-Philippe Rameau in the early 18th century that forms the basis of modern harmonic theory.

# Formal Definition
The modern theory of harmony was founded by Jean-Philippe Rameau early in the 18th century. His theoretical work established foundational concepts that continue to influence harmonic analysis, including the notion of chord roots and the systematic organization of harmonic progressions.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Rameau's contributions (as referenced in historical context):
- Founded the modern theory of harmony
- Established the concept of fundamental bass (chord root)
- Systematized understanding of chord inversions
- Influenced all subsequent harmonic theory

His work preceded and influenced:
- The Roman numeral system (Abbe Vogler, Gottfried Weber)
- Stufentheorie (Simon Sechter)
- Funktionstheorie (Hugo Riemann)

# Musical Context/Application
Rameau's legacy:
- All modern Roman numeral analysis derives from his root-based approach
- The concept of harmonic function builds on his foundations
- Contemporary analytical practice continues to use his basic concepts
- His ideas form the starting point for 18th-19th century theoretical development

# Common Confusions
- Attributing Roman numerals to Rameau (they came later, from Vogler/Weber)
- Conflating Rameau's specific theories with modern function theory
- Not recognizing the historical development of harmonic theory
- Assuming modern analysis simply applies Rameau's original system
