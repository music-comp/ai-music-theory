# Quick Definition
The recapitulation may be expanded relative to the exposition through motivic development, internal expansion, phrase extension, or cadential deviation, creating a looser, more developmental character in the A' section.

# Core Definition
The A' section of a small ternary can be lengthened beyond the exposition through several techniques: internal expansion (material inserted within phrases), phrase extension (material added after phrases), and cadential deviation (deceptive cadence requiring extension). The recapitulation may also "develop" motives from prior sections through stepwise sequences elaborating basic-idea material and new continuational passages. Such expansion typically renders the recapitulation looser than the exposition, with asymmetrical phrase structure, sequential activity, and chromatic enrichment. Expansion constitutes a structural change, not merely ornamental.

# Key Properties
1. A' is longer than A
2. Expansion techniques: internal expansion, phrase extension, cadential deviation
3. Motivic development may elaborate basic-idea material
4. Creates looser organization than exposition
5. Sequential activity and chromatic enrichment typical
6. Constitutes structural change (not ornamental)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Compare length of A' to A
2. Locate inserted or added material
3. Check for motivic development (sequences, fragmentation)
4. Note cadential deviations requiring extension
5. Assess whether the expanded material alters grouping structure or formal functions

# Context & Application
Expanded recapitulations demonstrate that the recapitulation is not a mere repeat of the exposition. Development is not confined to the B section; A' can be significantly developmental. When A' is heavily expanded, both B and A' may appear loosely organized, making comparison between them complex with different loosening criteria applying to each.
