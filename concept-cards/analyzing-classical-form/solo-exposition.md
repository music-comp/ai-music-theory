## Definition

The solo exposition is the second major section of concerto form. It functions essentially the same as a sonata exposition, containing main theme, transition, and subordinate-theme group, and effecting modulation to the subordinate key.

## Key Differences from Sonata Exposition

1. **Does not conclude with closing section**: Ends with final PAC of subordinate-theme group
2. **Subordinate-key ritornello follows**: Takes the place of closing section
3. **Rarely repeats the opening ritornello**: Almost always contains new material
4. **May already be "developmental"**: Contains greater sequential activity

## Solo Main Theme

### Option 1: Based on Ritornello Main Theme
- Same formal plan as orchestral version
- May be altered through loosening devices or new material
- Often includes variation procedures for virtuosic character

### Option 2: Alternative Main Theme
When ritornello main theme is highly orchestral (loud fanfares, polyphonic textures):
- New theme introduced that is more idiomatic for solo instrument
- Example: Mozart, Piano Concerto in D minor, K. 466

## Solo Transition

Principles guiding material choice:
1. Solo section should not reproduce same succession of ideas as ritornello
2. Alternative main theme prompts immediate reappearance of ritornello main theme

## Solo Subordinate-Theme Group

- Contains at least two themes (three is common)
- One or more themes based on ritornello material
- New material almost always added
- First subordinate theme is often entirely new (allows solo its own melodic profile)
- Responsible for substantial loosening of form
- Enormous expanded cadential progressions (hallmark of concerto style)
