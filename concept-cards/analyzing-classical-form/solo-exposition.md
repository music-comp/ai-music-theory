# Quick Definition
The solo exposition functions like a sonata exposition (effecting modulation to the subordinate key) but does not end with a closing section (replaced by subordinate-key ritornello), rarely repeats the ritornello's material in the same order, and almost always contains new material for the solo.

# Core Definition
The solo exposition functions essentially as a sonata exposition, containing main theme, transition, and subordinate-theme group. It does not conclude with a closing section but ends with the final PAC of the subordinate-theme group (the subordinate-key ritornello follows as the formal analogue of a closing section). The solo exposition almost always contains new material and already represents a "development" of earlier material (Caplin, pp. 706-711).

# Prerequisites
- **Concerto form** -- The overall form

# Key Properties
1. Functions like sonata exposition with modulation
2. Ends with final PAC (no closing section -- ritornello follows)
3. Almost always contains new material for the solo
4. Rarely repeats the ritornello in the same order
5. Solo main theme may use ritornello material or be entirely new (alternative main theme)
6. Subordinate-theme group usually has at least 2-3 themes
7. Contains greater sequential activity than typical sonata exposition
8. Enormous expanded cadential progressions with cadential trill (hallmark of concerto style)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for the solo entrance after the opening ritornello
2. Identify the modulation to the subordinate key
3. Note new material introduced by the solo
4. Check for the cadential trill marking the end

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 711): Mozart, Piano Concerto in E-flat, K. 482, i -- bravura theme with continuous sixteenth notes; first presented as 8-m. sentence, then repeated and expanded.
