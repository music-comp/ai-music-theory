# Quick Definition
The point in the recapitulation's transition where the music diverges from the exposition's path to effect the tonal adjustment, keeping the music in the home key rather than modulating to the subordinate key.

# Core Definition
The crux is the moment where the recapitulation's transition must alter the exposition's harmonic plan. In the exposition, the transition modulates from the home key to the subordinate key; in the recapitulation, it must be "adjusted to remain in the home key, ending there with dominant harmony, so that the subordinate theme can enter with tonic of that key" (p. 500). The most typical adjustment shifts toward the flat-side subdominant region, so that the tonicized IV can function as pre-dominant leading to the home-key dominant. This creates an analogous sharp-side shift back to the home key, comparable to the exposition's modulation.

# Key Properties
1. Point where recapitulation diverges from exposition
2. Can occur early, middle, or late in the transition
3. Typically involves shift to flat-side subdominant region
4. Subdominant functions as pre-dominant leading to home-key dominant
5. Creates sharp-side shift analogous to exposition's modulation
6. In exposition: I -> V (sharp-side); in recapitulation: IV -> I (flat to sharp)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Compare the recapitulation's transition bar by bar with the exposition's
2. The crux is where the two paths diverge
3. Note the direction of tonal adjustment (typically toward subdominant)
4. Identify how the music returns to the home-key dominant

# Context & Application
Understanding the crux is central to the comparative analytical method Caplin advocates for recapitulations. The flat-side shift to subdominant serves a dual purpose: it avoids tonal monotony and it sets up the home-key dominant through a pre-dominant function analogous to the exposition's modulation.
