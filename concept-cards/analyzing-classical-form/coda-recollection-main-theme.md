# Recollection of Main-theme Ideas in Coda

The most common compensatory function of the coda.

## The Aesthetic Goal

The notion that musical material at the beginning of a movement should return toward its end, lending circularity to the overall form.

## Comparison to Other Forms

Some forms (large ternary, five-part rondo, sonata-rondo) require return of main theme toward close.

Sonata and minuet forms call for main theme return only at beginning of recapitulation. If composers wish to bring back main-theme material late in movement, they normally must place it in a coda.

## Most Common Compensatory Function

Most codas refer at some point to main-theme ideas. This is perhaps the coda's principal compensatory function.

## Functional Transformation

When main-theme material returns in coda:
- May be organized as tight-knit theme (like original)
- Or loosened through deceptive cadences, expansions
- Or function as closing section (if exclusively tonic support)
