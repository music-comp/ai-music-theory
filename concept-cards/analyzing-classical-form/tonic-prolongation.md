# Quick Definition
Tonic prolongation is a harmonic progression that extends or sustains the sense of tonic harmony over a span of music. It is the defining harmonic characteristic of a presentation phrase. The lack of a distinct cadential progression within the prolongation prohibits cadence formation, which is why the presentation never ends with a cadence.

# Core Definition
By definition, a presentation phrase is supported by a progression that prolongs tonic harmony (usually in root position) (p. 67). The underlying harmony of the entire presentation phrase is tonic prolongational; the lack of a distinct cadential progression within the phrase prohibits the formation of a cadence to end the phrase (p. 58). Tonic prolongation may include root-position tonic chords, neighboring dominants (V returning to I), embellishing pre-dominants, and passing chords -- all subordinate harmonies serving to prolong the fundamental tonic (p. 67). The boundaries of the prolongation may complete within the presentation itself, at the downbeat of the continuation (if the response is entirely on V), or even within the continuation phrase (pp. 67-68).

- **Presentation phrase** — Tonic prolongation is the defining harmonic feature of presentation

# Key Properties
1. Extends/sustains tonic harmony over a span of music
2. Defining harmonic characteristic of presentation function
3. Prohibits cadence formation (no distinct cadential progression within it)
4. May include neighboring V, embellishing pre-dominants, passing chords
5. All subordinate harmonies serve the fundamental tonic
6. Boundaries may extend into the continuation phrase
7. Provides harmonic stability for the sense of "beginning"
8. Root-position tonic is typical but not the only form

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Check whether the harmony of mm. 1-4 prolongs tonic
2. Look for I as the governing harmony, with other chords as embellishments
3. Neighboring V (returning to I) is prolongational, not cadential
4. If the response is on V, the prolongation may complete at the downbeat of m. 5
5. The absence of a cadential progression confirms prolongational status

# Context & Application
Tonic prolongation provides the requisite harmonic stability for presentation function, which creates a solid structural beginning for the theme (p. 69). This is why sequential repetition is rare in presentations: sequential progressions threaten the prolongational stability required by presentation function (p. 67). Even a clear V7-I progression at the end of a presentation does not create a cadence because the context of repetition (the basic idea returning) prevents the V7-I from functioning as a cadential goal (p. 88). The tonic prolongation of the continuation may be less stable than that of the presentation (p. 75).

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 56, Ex. 2.1): Beethoven, Op. 2, No. 1, i -- tonic version (mm. 1-2, on I) followed by dominant version (mm. 3-4, on V65); tonic prolongation completes when V resolves to I at the downbeat of m. 5.
**Example 2** (p. 63, Ex. 2.6): Mozart, K. 330, i -- both statements of the basic idea supported entirely by tonic; prolongation complete within the presentation.
**Example 3** (p. 68, Ex. 2.13): Beethoven, Op. 14, No. 2, i -- sequential repetition pushes the remaining harmonies of the prolongation (V-I) into the continuation; prolongation completed at m. 6.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Hearing V7-I at the end of a presentation as a cadence
  **Correction**: In a presentation context (supporting repetition of b.i.), V7-I is part of tonic prolongation, not a cadential progression; no cadence occurs

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking any passage with a dominant chord is no longer tonic prolongational
  **Clarification**: Neighboring dominants (V returning to I) are part of tonic prolongation; the key is whether V serves as an embellishment of I or as the goal of a cadential progression
