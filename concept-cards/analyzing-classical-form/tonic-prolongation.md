# Tonic Prolongation

**Tonic prolongation** is a harmonic progression that extends or sustains the sense of tonic harmony over a span of music. It is the defining harmonic characteristic of a presentation phrase.

## Definition

By definition, a presentation phrase is supported by a progression that prolongs tonic harmony (usually in root position).

## Scope of Prolongation

The boundaries of the tonic prolongation may occur in different ways:

1. **Within the presentation itself** - The prolongation is complete by the end of measure 4
2. **At the downbeat of the continuation** - If the response is supported entirely by dominant, the tonic prolongation completes when the continuation begins with I
3. **Within the continuation** - Sometimes the prolongation extends into the continuation phrase before completing

## Harmonic Content

A tonic prolongation may include:
- Root-position tonic chords
- Neighboring dominants (V returning to I)
- Embellishing pre-dominants
- Passing chords

All of these subordinate harmonies serve to prolong the fundamental tonic harmony.

## Key Principle

The lack of a distinct **cadential progression** within a tonic prolongation prohibits the formation of a cadence. This is why presentation phrases do not end with cadences.

## Relation to Formal Function

Tonic prolongation provides the requisite harmonic stability for **presentation function**, which creates a solid structural beginning for the theme.
