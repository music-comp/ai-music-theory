# Quick Definition
Caplin avoids the traditional term "double period" for 16-m. periodic structures because it falsely implies a structure made of two periods or a period within a period, which rarely occurs. The term "compound period" is preferred because the 8-m. units function as compound antecedent and consequent, not as independent periods.

# Core Definition
Most textbooks call the compound period a "double period," implying two periods or a period embedded within a period. But the 8-m. units are not themselves periods -- they are a compound antecedent (closing with a weak cadence) and compound consequent (closing with a strong cadence). True embedding of a simple period within a compound period rarely arises.

# Key Properties
1. "Double period" implies period-within-period (rarely occurs)
2. "Compound period" more accurately describes the structure
3. The 8-m. units are compound antecedent and consequent, not periods
