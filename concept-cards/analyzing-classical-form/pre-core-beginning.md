# Quick Definition
The opening of the pre-core conventionally draws its melodic-motivic material from the main theme's basic idea, closing section codettas, or new material not found in the exposition.

# Core Definition
Caplin identifies three typical sources for pre-core opening material (p. 463): (1) the main theme's basic idea, which creates an effective variation on the link between the exposition's end and main theme's beginning; (2) closing section material, which promotes continuity of motive, rhythm, and texture from the end of the exposition; (3) new material, especially favored by Mozart but rarely used by Haydn and Beethoven. Transition and subordinate-theme material rarely appears at the very beginning of the pre-core.

# Prerequisites
- **Pre-core** — The formal unit being initiated
- **Basic idea** — The main theme element most commonly used
- **Closing section** — Another common source of opening material

# Key Properties
1. Main theme's basic idea reforges the link between exposition ending and main theme in new tonal context
2. Closing section material promotes rhythmic and textural continuity from exposition
3. New material may appear (especially Mozart), though it sometimes derives from earlier ideas through motivic transformation
4. Transition and subordinate-theme material almost never opens a pre-core

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Compare the pre-core's opening material to the main theme's basic idea
2. If not from the main theme, check against the exposition's closing section codettas
3. If neither, the material is "new" (common in Mozart)

# Context & Application
The choice of opening material affects the perceived continuity between the exposition and the development. Using main-theme material reinforces the link that the exposition repeat originally created; closing-section material ensures smooth textural continuity.

# Examples
**Example 13.8** (p. 463): Beethoven, Op. 14, No. 2 — main theme's basic idea returns at beginning of pre-core, marked by change of mode to minor.

**Example 13.9** (p. 464): Haydn, Piano Sonata in E-flat, H. 49 — pre-core develops a right-hand motive from the closing section (m. 62), metrically displaced by one beat.

- **Error**: Assuming the pre-core always begins with main-theme material
