# Lead-in

A **lead-in** is a brief melodic idea that links the end of one phrase with the beginning of the next, generating rhythmic continuity and flow.

## Definition

A lead-in (term introduced by William Rothstein) connects:
- The end of the antecedent with the beginning of the consequent
- Or other adjacent formal units

## Function

The lead-in functions largely to generate:
- **Rhythmic continuity** from one phrase to the next
- Smooth **flow** between formal units

## Characteristics

The melodic material of a lead-in:
- Belongs neither to the weak cadence (end of antecedent) nor to the return of the basic idea (beginning of consequent)
- Could easily be eliminated without upsetting melodic, harmonic, and formal organization
- May be simple or elaborately embellished

## Distinction from Accompaniment

The effect of rhythmic continuity can also be produced by:
- Maintaining accompanimental figuration through the half cadence
- Steady accompaniment continuing without pause into the consequent

When there is a clear rest in the melody part, we would not speak of a lead-in, even if the accompaniment continues.

## Context

Lead-ins are not confined to periods. They may be used in various formal contexts wherever phrase boundaries need to be bridged while maintaining musical momentum.
