## Definition

A ritornello is a section in concerto form written for the orchestra alone (tutti), without the soloist. The term means "little return" in Italian, referring to the recurring orchestral passages that frame the form.

## Three Ritornellos in Classical Concerto Form

| Ritornello | Position | Key | Function |
|------------|----------|-----|----------|
| Opening | Beginning | Home key | Introduces thematic material; creates frame |
| Subordinate-key | After solo exposition | Subordinate key | Reinforces new key; provides orchestral moment |
| Closing | End | Home key | Completes frame; includes cadenza |

## Historical Background

### Baroque Ritornello Form
- **Tutti passages** (ritornellos) alternate with **solo passages**
- Opening ritornello brings primary melodic-motivic content
- Subsequent ritornellos transpose parts of opening into related keys
- Closing ritornello brings back content in home key

### Classical Adaptation
- Retains alternation of tutti and solo
- Integrates sonata-form principles
- Reduces number of ritornellos to three
- Gives solo greater developmental and modulatory responsibility

## Function in Classical Concerto

Ritornellos ensure that:
- The orchestra is not reduced to mere accompaniment
- A textural "frame" exists for opening and closing
- The orchestra can assert its identity in relation to the solo
