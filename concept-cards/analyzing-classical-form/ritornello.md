# Quick Definition
A ritornello is a section in concerto form written for the orchestra alone (tutti), without the soloist. Classical concerto form contains three ritornellos: opening (home key), subordinate-key (reinforces new key), and closing (home key, includes cadenza).

# Core Definition
In concerto form, ritornellos are played by the orchestra alone; they frame the form and alternate with solo sections. The classical concerto reduces the Baroque practice to three ritornellos: opening (in the home key, introducing thematic material), subordinate-key (reinforcing the new key after the solo exposition), and closing (in the home key, completing the frame with cadenza; Caplin, pp. 694-695).

# Key Properties
1. Orchestra alone (tutti), no soloist
2. Three in classical concerto: opening, subordinate-key, closing
3. "Little return" (Italian) -- refers to recurring orchestral passages
4. Frame the form (opening + closing create textural frame)
5. Ensure orchestra is not reduced to mere accompaniment
6. Derived from Baroque ritornello form
