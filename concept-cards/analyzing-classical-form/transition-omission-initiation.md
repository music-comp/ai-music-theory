## Transition Omission of Initiating Function

In some cases, the transition acquires a markedly **looser formal expression** by starting directly with continuation function, bypassing a clear sense of formal initiation.

### Most Evident Expression

The most evident way of beginning with continuation is when the music presents a **model for sequential repetition**.

### Without Model-Sequence

In absence of model-sequence technique, continuation can be expressed by:
- Relatively **short groups** (less than two bars)
- Relatively **fast harmonic changes**
- Highly **active surface rhythm**
- Fragmentation-like groupings (1-measure units repeated)

### Fragmentation at the Start

Repeated one-bar units at the start of a new thematic unit may signal continuation function even without prior initiation. We are so accustomed to understanding repeated one-bar units as fragments that their appearance signals continuation.

### Effect

The lack of a full presentation phrase renders the transition's beginning less solid, and thus looser in expression, than the opening of the main theme.
