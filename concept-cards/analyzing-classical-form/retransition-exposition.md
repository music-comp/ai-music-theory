# Quick Definition
A retransition is a passage following the final codetta of the closing section that brings the music back to the home key, leading smoothly to a repeat of the exposition. Its material may grow out of the final codetta, be entirely different, or anticipate main-theme motives.

# Core Definition
The final codetta of the closing section is sometimes followed by a **retransition**, a passage that functions to bring the music back to the home key and lead smoothly to a repeat of the exposition. The material may grow directly out of the final codetta, be quite different from the closing section, or anticipate main-theme motives to prepare for the repeated exposition (Caplin, Ch. 12, p. 412).
