# Realization of Unrealized Implications in Coda

Using the coda to resolve implications generated earlier in the movement.

## The Principle

Many compositional processes imply particular modes of continuation:
- Some implications realized immediately
- Others deferred until later in movement
- Still others may never be realized at all

The coda gives the composer last opportunity to realize earlier implications that would not find appropriate realization elsewhere.

## Typical Source

Implications usually arise in relation to the main theme, particularly a "problem" or "disturbance" not resolved until the coda.

## Example: Modal Disturbance

An expressive turn to minor in the exposition's main theme is unusual and implies later return without this minor-mode disturbance. The coda version eliminates explicit modal shift.

## Example: Deleted Cadence

If main theme's PAC was eliminated in recapitulation (where modal shift led to remote key), the coda version can restore authentic cadential closure.

## Compositional Continuation

Implications might be exploited (minor-mode pitch becomes significant tonal region) before ultimate resolution in coda.
