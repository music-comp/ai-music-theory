# Cadential Differentiation

**Cadential differentiation** is the essential characteristic of the period form: the antecedent ends with a weaker cadence than the consequent.

## The Principle

Period form depends on:
- Antecedent: **Weak** cadence (HC or IAC)
- Consequent: **Strong** cadence (PAC)

This weak-to-strong pattern is fundamental.

## Why It Matters

Cadential differentiation creates the period's effect:
- The weak cadence is "incomplete"
- This motivates the consequent
- The strong cadence provides "completion"
- The pattern creates satisfying closure

## Relative Strength

Cadential strength hierarchy:
1. PAC (strongest)
2. IAC (moderate)
3. HC (weakest)

The consequent must be stronger than the antecedent:
- HC → PAC (most common)
- HC → IAC (acceptable)
- IAC → PAC (acceptable)

## What Cannot Work

Violations of cadential differentiation:
- PAC → anything (already complete)
- Strong → weak (illogical)

These patterns cannot create period form.

## Expanded Cadential Emphasis

The consequent often **expands** its cadential progression relative to the antecedent, creating even greater differentiation.
