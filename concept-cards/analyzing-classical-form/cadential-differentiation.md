# Quick Definition
The essential defining characteristic of the period: the antecedent ends with a weaker cadence than the consequent. The strength hierarchy (PAC > IAC > HC) is syntactical (determined by type), not rhetorical (determined by dynamics or texture). Typical: HC -> PAC.

# Core Definition
The period has at its basis the idea that a musical unit of weak cadential closure is repeated so as to produce stronger cadential closure (p. 95). The syntactical strength of a cadence is determined on the basis of its type, not on its tonal context (p. 112). Other factors (metrical position, dynamics, texture) determine rhetorical strength but do not alter syntactical strength. Two cadences of varying rhetorical strength may still function to create equivalent formal closure if they have the same syntactical strength (p. 102).

# Key Properties
1. Antecedent: weak cadence (HC or IAC); consequent: strong cadence (PAC, rarely IAC)
2. Syntactical strength hierarchy: PAC (strongest) > IAC (moderate) > HC (weakest)
3. Common patterns: HC -> PAC (most common); HC -> IAC; IAC -> PAC
4. Invalid: PAC -> anything (already complete); strong -> weak
5. Syntactical strength is determined by type alone, not by rhetorical factors
6. Rhetorical strength (dynamics, texture, metrical position) is separate from syntactical
7. In modulating periods, HC (home key) remains weaker than PAC (new key)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Identify the cadence type at end of each phrase
2. Verify weak-to-strong progression
3. Note: syntactical strength only (ignore dynamics, texture)

# Context & Application
Cadential differentiation is what creates the period's characteristic sense of balance and completion. The weak cadence motivates the consequent; the strong cadence provides closure. The cadential progression of the consequent often begins earlier and carries greater temporal weight.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 96, Ex. 3.1): Mozart, K. 525, ii -- HC (ant.) -> PAC (cons.), the most typical pattern.

**Example 2** (p. 106, Ex. 3.13): Mozart, K. 466, ii -- HC (ant.) -> IAC (cons.), a rare pattern.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Judging cadential strength by dynamics or texture
  **Correction**: Syntactical strength is determined by cadence type alone

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking HC in the home key is stronger than PAC in a secondary key (modulating period)
  **Clarification**: Syntactical strength is by type, not tonal context: HC < PAC regardless of key
