# Quick Definition
In some expansive Beethoven works, the main theme has overall sentential form but with initiating units enlarged far beyond a typical compound basic idea. Their immediate repetition creates a large presentation-like unit, followed by a continuation often "compressed" in relation to the presentation's size.

# Core Definition
In some expansive works by Beethoven, the main theme acquires an overall sentential form, but the initiating units are enlarged beyond a typical compound basic idea. Their immediate repetition (occasionally sequential) creates a large presentation-like unit, upon which a continuation follows. This continuation is often "compressed" relative to the grouping size proposed by the presentation. The opening unit may be large enough to appear to close with its own cadence, but never a PAC in the home key (which would suggest theme completion); such a cadence should be seen as one of "limited scope" (Caplin, Ch. 10, pp. 316-317).

# Key Properties
1. Initiating units enlarged beyond compound basic idea size
2. Immediate repetition creates large-scale presentation
3. Continuation often compressed relative to presentation
4. Internal "cadences" are of limited scope (never HK: PAC)
5. Particularly associated with Beethoven's expansive works

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 317): Beethoven, "Kreutzer" Sonata, Op. 47, i, mm. 19-45 -- 9-m. unit repeated (18 mm. presentation) + 9-m. compressed continuation to PAC.
