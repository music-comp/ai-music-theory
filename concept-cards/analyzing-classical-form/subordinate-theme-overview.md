## Subordinate Theme Overview

The **subordinate theme** is the concluding thematic function of the exposition, serving to confirm fully the subordinate key with a PAC.

### Primary Functions

1. **Confirm the subordinate key** with a required PAC
2. **Provide contrast** with main theme through different material
3. **Loosen the formal organization** through extensions and expansions

### Characteristics

- Usually brings contrasting material to the main theme
- Distinctly looser form than main theme
- Almost always considerably longer than main theme
- Delays and dramatizes the arrival of the anticipated PAC

### Aesthetic Goal

The typical aesthetic goal is to delay and dramatize the arrival of the PAC so that its eventual appearance provides a powerful sense of confirming the subordinate key, allowing it (temporarily) to gain structural superiority over the home key.

### Multiple Subordinate Themes

Many expositions contain multiple subordinate themes, each closing with a PAC, creating a **subordinate-theme group**.

### Postcadential Elements

- **Closing section**: follows the final PAC, consisting of codettas
- **Retransition**: may follow the closing section to return to the home key for the repeat
