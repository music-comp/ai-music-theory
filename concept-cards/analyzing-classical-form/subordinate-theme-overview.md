# Quick Definition
The subordinate theme is the concluding thematic function of the exposition, functioning to confirm fully the subordinate key with a required PAC. It usually brings contrasting material in a distinctly looser form than the main theme, and is almost always considerably longer due to extensions, expansions, and the dramatization of cadential arrival.

# Core Definition
The subordinate theme functions to confirm fully the subordinate key, requiring a PAC in that key. It usually brings contrasting material to the main theme and shapes it into a distinctly looser form. The various loosening devices often involve extensions and expansions, making the subordinate theme almost always considerably longer than the main theme. A typical aesthetic goal is to delay and dramatize the arrival of the anticipated PAC so that its eventual appearance provides a powerful sense of confirming the subordinate key, allowing it temporarily to gain structural superiority over the home key. Many expositions contain multiple subordinate themes, each closing with a PAC, creating a subordinate-theme group. The final cadence is usually followed by a closing section of codettas and in some cases a retransition.

# Key Properties
1. Must confirm subordinate key with PAC
2. Usually contrasting material to main theme
3. Distinctly looser form than main theme
4. Almost always considerably longer than main theme
5. Delays and dramatizes cadential arrival
6. Multiple subordinate themes possible (subordinate-theme group)
7. Followed by closing section (codettas) and possibly retransition

# Construction / Recognition
## Loosening Techniques:
1. Continuation and cadential functions given distinct phrases (not fused as in tight-knit sentence)
2. Extended continuation beyond typical 4-measure length
3. Cadential expansion and evasion
4. Harmonic complication (modal mixture, dominant pedals undermining tonic)
5. Redundant repetitions and extensions

# Examples
**Example** (pp. 290-293): Beethoven, Op. 2, No. 1, i, mm. 20-48 -- subordinate theme begins with new basic idea (inverted variant of main theme's); presentation complicated by two harmonic levels; continuation extended well beyond normal length; cadential phrase brings expanded cadential progression; first cadence evaded at m. 37; entire cadential phrase repeated for PAC at m. 41; closing section of three 2-m. codettas follows.

# Context & Application
The subordinate theme's looseness is not compositional weakness but serves the form's dramatic function. The delay and dramatization of the confirming PAC create a powerful sense of arrival when the subordinate key is finally established. The built-up energy is then released through the closing section, whose slower rhythmic values and disjunct textures dissipate accumulated tension. The distinction between tight-knit main theme and loose subordinate theme is fundamental to understanding sonata form.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Expecting the subordinate theme to be as concise and clearly organized as the main theme
  **Correction**: The subordinate theme's looseness (extensions, expansions, cadential dramatization) is an essential formal feature, not a deficiency
