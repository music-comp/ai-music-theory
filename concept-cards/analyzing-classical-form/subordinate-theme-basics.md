# Quick Definition
The subordinate theme is the concluding thematic function of the exposition, serving to contrast with the main theme, loosen the formal organization through extensions and expansions, and confirm the subordinate key with a PAC. It is almost always considerably longer than the main theme.

# Core Definition
The subordinate theme is the large-scale **concluding** thematic function of the exposition, characterized by contrast with the main theme, looser formal organization, and required ending with a PAC in the subordinate key. Three primary functions: (1) to **contrast** with the main theme, supporting the tonal contrast of home and subordinate keys; (2) to **loosen the formal organization** through extensions and expansions, giving rhetorical and dramatic weight to the subordinate key; (3) to **confirm** the subordinate key with a PAC. Most subordinate themes are built from sentential functions (presentation, continuation, cadential), with periodic forms less common. A typical aesthetic goal is to delay and dramatize the PAC so its arrival provides a powerful sense of key confirmation (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 375-381).

# Key Properties
1. Three functions: contrast, loosen, confirm (PAC required)
2. Contrast: usually new material, but Haydn uses main-theme material ("monothematic")
3. Opening often tuneful and lyrical in piano dynamic; end often energetic and aggressive
4. Diverse motivic ideas (unlike main theme's unified motives)
5. Almost always considerably longer than main theme
6. Multiple subordinate themes create a subordinate-theme group
7. Closing section follows the final PAC

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. New thematic material (usually) after the transition's HC/standing on dominant
2. Sentential organization: presentation, continuation, cadential -- in loosened forms
3. Look for loosening devices: extensions, expansions, cadential evasions, modal shifts
4. Required PAC in subordinate key marks the theme's end
5. Closing section (codettas) follows the final PAC

# Context & Application
The subordinate theme's loosening devices generate considerable length and dramatic tension. The PAC is often highly dramatized through evaded cadences and expanded cadential progressions, making the eventual closure feel hard-won. The energy accumulated in achieving this goal is then released through the closing section.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 290): Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 20-48 -- Subordinate theme more than 3x longer than main theme, with separated continuation and cadential phrases, cadential evasion, expanded cadential progression, closing section.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming subordinate themes must be lyrical throughout
  **Correction**: Opening may be lyrical, but the end is typically energetic and dramatically charged

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking a single exposition can have only one subordinate theme
  **Clarification**: Many expositions contain two, three, or even four subordinate themes
