## Subordinate Theme Basics

The **subordinate theme** is the third thematic function of the sonata-form exposition, serving as the large-scale concluding thematic function. It is characterized by contrast with the main theme, looser formal organization, and a required ending with a PAC in the subordinate key.

### Three Primary Functions

1. **To contrast** with the main theme, supporting thematically the tonal contrast of home and subordinate keys
2. **To loosen the formal organization** through extensions and expansions, giving rhetorical and dramatic weight to the subordinate key
3. **To confirm** the subordinate key with a perfect authentic cadence

### Contrast with Main Theme

- Usually provided by new melodic-motivic material
- Opening material is often tuneful and lyrical, set in *piano* dynamic
- Haydn often opens subordinate themes with main theme material ("monothematic exposition")
- Subordinate themes display diverse motivic ideas
- Toward the end: highly energetic, dramatically charged, dynamically aggressive

### Phrase-Structural Organization

- Most subordinate themes are **sentential** (presentation, continuation, cadential)
- Periodic form may sometimes arise (usually with extended consequent)
- Small ternary structures rarely occur
