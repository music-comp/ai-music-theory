# Quick Definition
The A' section of minuet form recapitulates A section material but, unlike small ternary, is seldom shorter and is more likely to be significantly expanded through loosening devices and deceptive cadences.

# Core Definition
The A' section of minuet form recapitulates the material initially presented in the A section. Unlike a small ternary, which frequently contains a compressed recapitulation, that of a minuet is seldom shorter than the earlier section. In fact, the minuet's recapitulation is more likely to be significantly expanded and frequently includes a new closing section not found in the exposition (Caplin, pp. 646-648).

# Key Properties
1. Seldom shorter than the A section (unlike small ternary)
2. Often significantly expanded
3. May include new closing section
4. Deceptive cadence is a favorite extension device (more common than in sonata)
5. Loosening devices typical of subordinate-theme function
6. May include interpolated episodes
7. Functions that were fused in A may become distinct in A'

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Compare A' to A section for structural correspondence
2. Note expansions, extensions, and new material
3. Look for deceptive cadences as extension devices
4. Check for new closing section or coda

# Context & Application
The expansion compensates for tonal imbalance (if B section confirmed a subordinate key more strongly than A' confirms the home key) and provides a kind of "recapitulation of subordinate theme" through looser phrase-structural techniques.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 648): Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, iii -- A' expanded so that transition and subordinate-theme functions become distinct units (fused in A section).
