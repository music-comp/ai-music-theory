## Definition

The A' section of minuet form recapitulates material from the A section. Unlike small ternary, the minuet's recapitulation is seldom shorter and is more likely to be significantly expanded.

## Comparison to Small Ternary

| Feature | Small Ternary | Minuet Form |
|---------|---------------|-------------|
| A' length | Often compressed | Seldom shorter than A |
| Expansion | Rare | Common, often significant |
| New closing section | Less common | Frequently includes new closing section |

## Expansion Techniques

### Loosening Devices
- Devices typically associated with subordinate theme function
- New material may appear in interpolated episode

### Deceptive Cadence
- A favorite device for extending the form
- Found more often in minuet than in sonata
- Sonata uses evaded cadences more commonly

## Why Expansion Occurs

### Tonal Balance Argument
If A section is shorter than B section:
- Equally short A' section might not give home key enough space
- Expanded recapitulation with cadential reinforcements restores tonal stability
- New closing section (and coda) helps home-key predominance

### "Recapitulation of Subordinate Theme" Argument
If nonmodulating A section represents main-theme function and B section brings subordinate theme:
- Simply bringing back main theme in A' leaves subordinate theme unrecapitulated
- Expansion in A' section provides a kind of "recapitulation of subordinate theme"
- Uses looser phrase-structural techniques to suggest this recapitulation

## Thematic Functional Expansion

Example (Beethoven, Op. 7, iii): Recapitulation expanded such that transition and subordinate-theme functions are no longer fused (as in A section) but given distinct thematic units.
