## Dissolving Continuation

When a main theme ends with a half cadence, the transition often begins with material that seems to continue the main theme - this creates a **dissolving** effect where continuation function retrospectively becomes transition.

### How It Works

1. Main theme ends with HC (not PAC)
2. New material enters, seeming to continue the main theme
3. Listener initially expects a consequent-like phrase
4. But the music modulates or moves toward new key
5. The "continuation" is retrospectively understood as beginning of transition

### The "Dissolving" Quality

The continuation function "dissolves" into transition function:
- Initially heard as belonging to main theme
- Retrospectively understood as transitional
- The boundary between main theme and transition is obscured

### Why It Works

After an HC, listeners expect continuation to PAC. When the music departs from this expectation:
- The anticipated "completion" of the main theme is thwarted
- The music is revealed to have moved on to new formal function
- A seamless connection is created between main theme and transition
