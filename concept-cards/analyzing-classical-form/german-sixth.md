# Quick Definition
An augmented-sixth chord containing four pitches: the lowered sixth degree in the bass, the tonic pitch, the lowered third degree, and the raised fourth degree, creating a sonority equivalent to a dominant seventh chord.

# Formal Definition
The German sixth (Gr+6) is one of three varieties of augmented-sixth chords functioning as pre-dominants. It adds the lowered third scale degree to the Italian sixth's three pitches, creating a four-note sonority that is enharmonically equivalent to a dominant seventh chord. Despite this equivalence, it functions as a pre-dominant resolving to V, not as a dominant.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Pitch content (in C major/minor):
- Ab (lowered 6) - typically in bass
- C (tonic/1)
- Eb (lowered 3)
- F# (raised 4) - typically in an upper voice

Enharmonic equivalence:
- Spelled as Ab-C-Eb-F# = sounds like Ab dominant seventh (Ab-C-Eb-Gb)
- Despite sounding like V7/Db, it functions as pre-dominant to V in the home key

Resolution:
- Typically resolves through cadential six-four to avoid parallel fifths
- Lowered 6 and lowered 3 moving to 5 would create parallels without intervention
- Gr+6 - V(6-4) - V - I is common resolution path

# Musical Context/Application
The German sixth:
- Functions as pre-dominant within cadential progressions
- Often resolves through cadential six-four to avoid parallel fifths
- Creates rich chromatic texture with four distinct pitches
- The dominant-seventh sound creates strong pull toward resolution

The voice-leading challenge (potential parallel fifths) distinguishes its usage from the Italian sixth.

# Common Confusions
- Treating as a dominant seventh chord rather than pre-dominant function
- Not recognizing the need for cadential six-four to avoid parallel fifths
- Confusing with Italian sixth (German has lowered 3)
- Confusing with French sixth (French has natural 2 instead of lowered 3)
