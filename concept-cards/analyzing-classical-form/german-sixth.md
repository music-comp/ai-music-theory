# Quick Definition
An augmented-sixth chord containing four pitches -- lowered 6, tonic, lowered 3, and raised 4 -- enharmonically equivalent to a dominant seventh chord but functioning as pre-dominant.

# Core Definition
The German sixth (Gr+6) is one of three varieties of augmented-sixth chords functioning as pre-dominants. It adds the lowered third scale degree to the Italian sixth's three pitches, creating a four-note sonority enharmonically equivalent to a dominant seventh chord. Despite this equivalence, it functions as pre-dominant, not dominant. It typically resolves through the cadential six-four to avoid the parallel fifths that would arise from the lowered 6 and lowered 3 both resolving to scale degree 5 (Caplin, pp. 32-33).

# Prerequisites
- **Dominant of the dominant** — The German sixth belongs to the V/V pre-dominant category
- **Raised fourth scale degree** — Contains raised 4 as the defining chromatic element

# Key Properties
1. Pitch content (in C): Ab (lowered 6), C (1), Eb (lowered 3), F# (raised 4)
2. Enharmonically equivalent to Ab dominant seventh (Ab-C-Eb-Gb)
3. Functions as pre-dominant despite dominant-seventh sound
4. Typically resolves through cadential six-four to avoid parallel fifths
5. Lowered 6 and lowered 3 both pull toward scale degree 5, creating potential parallels

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for the augmented sixth interval (lowered 6 to raised 4)
2. Verify the presence of lowered 3 (distinguishes from Italian and French)
3. Note the dominant-seventh sonority (but classify as pre-dominant)
4. Check for resolution through cadential six-four

# Context & Application
The German sixth functions as pre-dominant within cadential progressions. The voice-leading challenge (potential parallel fifths between lowered 6 to 5 and lowered 3 to 5) typically requires resolution through the cadential six-four, distinguishing its usage from the Italian and French sixths. Its rich, four-note chromatic texture creates a powerful approach to the dominant.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating the German sixth as a dominant seventh chord
  **Correction**: Despite the enharmonic equivalence, it functions as pre-dominant resolving to V

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Not recognizing the need for cadential six-four to avoid parallel fifths
  **Clarification**: Direct resolution of Gr+6 to V creates parallel fifths between lowered 6-to-5 and lowered 3-to-5; the cadential six-four intervenes to solve this
