# Quick Definition
A progression that confirms a tonal center by bringing the fundamental harmonic functions in order: (initial) tonic, pre-dominant, dominant, and (final) tonic.

# Formal Definition
Cadential progressions confirm a tonal center by bringing the fundamental harmonic functions in a specific order: (initial) tonic, pre-dominant, dominant, and (final) tonic. Essential to cadential progressions is the requirement that the dominant must first appear, and then remain, in root position.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Three kinds of cadential progression:

1. **Authentic**: Ends with root-position tonic (V - I)
2. **Half**: Ends with root-position dominant triad, not seventh chord (... - V)
3. **Deceptive**: Final tonic replaced by another harmony, usually VI (V - VI)

**Complete vs. Incomplete:**
- Complete: Contains all component functions (I6 - II6 - V7 - I)
- Incomplete: Lacks initial tonic and/or pre-dominant (II6 - V7 - I or V7 - I)

There is nothing defective about incomplete progressions; compositional context determines which form is appropriate.

# Central Axiom
It is a central axiom that dominant harmony can acquire cadential status only when it initially appears in root position and retains that position until its resolution to tonic. Any inversion of the dominant automatically destroys its cadential potential.

# Musical Context/Application
Cadential progressions:
- Are essential for formal closure
- Define phrase endings and thematic boundaries
- The initial tonic typically appears in first inversion (I6)
- The pre-dominant is typically II6, though IV is also used

Analytical notation: The harmonies of cadential progressions are embraced by a horizontal square bracket to distinguish them from prolongational progressions.

# Common Confusions
- Assuming any V-I motion creates a cadential progression (inverted dominants cannot be cadential)
- Thinking complete progressions are "stronger" than incomplete ones (both are fully viable)
- Confusing cadential progression (harmonic) with cadence type (formal event)
- Forgetting that half-cadential dominants must be triads, not seventh chords
