# Restatement, Repetition, Return

In most formal contexts, important melodic-motivic material is stated more than once. It is useful to distinguish among terms for "saying something again."

## Restatement (General Term)

**Restatement** is the general term for any instance of presenting prior material again:
- "The opening basic idea is restated many times throughout the piece."

## Repetition (Immediate)

**Repetition** refers to restatement that occurs **immediately**, without any intervening material:
- "The opening basic idea is repeated in the two following measures."
- Used for the basic idea within a presentation phrase
- No intervening contrasting material

## Return (After Contrast)

**Return** refers to restatement that occurs **following** some intervening, contrasting material:
- "The basic idea returns at the start of the consequent phrase."
- The return follows the contrasting idea of the antecedent
- Intervening material separates the two statements

## Usage in Period Analysis

"The consequent phrase **repeats** the antecedent by bringing a **return** of the original basic idea."

This expresses:
- Nothing stands between antecedent and consequent (repeat)
- The two statements of the basic idea are separated by intervening material (return)

## Practical Application

- **Sentence**: Basic idea is **repeated** (mm. 1-2 → mm. 3-4)
- **Period**: Basic idea **returns** (mm. 1-2 → mm. 5-6, after contrasting idea)
