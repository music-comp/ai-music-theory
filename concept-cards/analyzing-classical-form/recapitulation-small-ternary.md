# Quick Definition
The A' section must fulfill two requirements: (1) begin by restating the initial basic idea from the A section in the home key, and (2) close with a PAC in the home key. It may be identical to, compressed from, or expanded relative to the exposition, and may undergo tonal adjustment if the A section modulated.

# Core Definition
The recapitulation brings back the fundamental melodic-motivic material entirely in the home key. If the A section modulated, the A' section requires tonal adjustment (often turning toward the subdominant). The recapitulation may be identical to the exposition (when A is nonmodulating), compressed (most commonly to a single 4-m. consequent phrase), or expanded (through motivic development, deceptive cadences, etc.). Changes may be ornamental (dynamics, texture, instrumentation, embellishment) or structural (harmony, grouping, formal functions). The analytical procedure: compare A and A' bar by bar, note every change, classify as ornamental or structural, and ask why.

# Key Properties
1. Two requirements: return of basic idea in home key + PAC in home key
2. Tonal adjustment required if A modulated
3. May be identical, compressed, or expanded
4. Ornamental vs. structural changes
5. Motivic development may occur in A' (not confined to B section)
6. The why question is essential for analysis
