# Quick Definition
Four distinct compositional devices characterize continuation function: (1) phrase-structural fragmentation, (2) acceleration of harmonic rhythm, (3) increase in surface rhythmic activity, and (4) sequential harmonic progressions. None is strictly required, but at least one is typically present. They are distinct and independent processes.

# Core Definition
Continuation function is characterized by four processes (p. 70): (1) phrase-structural fragmentation -- reduction in the length of constituent units relative to the prior phrase; (2) acceleration in the rate of harmonic change; (3) increase in surface rhythmic activity, such as shorter durational values compared to the prior phrase; (4) sequential harmonic progressions, with the added possibility of model-sequence technique. Although often closely related to each other, these are distinct and independent processes. Moreover, none of them is a necessary condition of the function (p. 70). At least one, but often a number, of these characteristics will be found in a continuation phrase (p. 58).

# Prerequisites
- **Continuation phrase** — The phrase within which these characteristics operate
- **Presentation phrase** — The characteristics are defined relative to the presentation

# Key Properties
1. Fragmentation: reduction of grouping units (e.g., 2 m. to 1 m.)
2. Harmonic acceleration: faster rate of harmonic change than presentation
3. Surface rhythm acceleration: shorter note values, greater density
4. Sequential progressions: harmonic sequences, possibly with model-sequence technique
5. All four are distinct and independent processes
6. None is a necessary condition -- at least one must be present
7. Multiple characteristics often reinforce each other
8. Fragmentation is the most typical indicator

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Compare continuation to the preceding presentation
2. Check for fragmentation (are grouping units shorter?)
3. Check for harmonic acceleration (does harmony change faster?)
4. Check for increased surface rhythmic activity (shorter note values?)
5. Check for sequential harmonic progressions
6. Any ONE characteristic is sufficient to identify continuation function

# Context & Application
When one characteristic is absent, others often compensate. For example, if fragmentation is absent, harmonic acceleration alone can project continuation function (Ex. 2.19, p. 74). Surface rhythm acceleration is particularly effective in the absence of harmonic acceleration (p. 75). Sequential harmonies appear infrequently in simple 8-m. sentences because there is often too little "room" between continuation onset and the cadential progression; they tend to appear when continuation function is extended (p. 77). These same characteristics can appear in miniature within a contrasting idea of a period's antecedent phrase (p. 101).

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 56, Ex. 2.1): Beethoven, Op. 2, No. 1, i -- fragmentation (mm. 5-6), harmonic acceleration, and increased surface rhythm all present.
**Example 2** (p. 74, Ex. 2.19): Mozart, K. 402, i -- harmonic acceleration compensates for absence of fragmentation.
**Example 3** (p. 71, Ex. 2.16): Mozart, K. 311, iii -- surface rhythm acceleration is particularly effective.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Requiring all four characteristics to be present
  **Correction**: None is necessary; at least one is sufficient; they are independent processes

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking fragmentation must always begin at m. 5
  **Clarification**: Fragmentation may be delayed; the continuation may begin by maintaining 2-m. units before fragmenting (e.g., Ex. 2.13, p. 72)
