# Quick Definition
Repeated material from the exposition -- such as repeated main themes, A-prime sections of small ternaries, or repeated continuation phrases -- is often eliminated in the recapitulation because the material is well known by this point in the movement.

# Core Definition
Since the main theme's melodic-motivic material is well known by the recapitulation, restatements that were appropriate in the exposition (where material is heard for the first time) are often eliminated. This is especially likely when those ideas were prominently exploited in the development section. If the entire main theme was repeated in the exposition, the recapitulation usually states the theme just once. If the main theme was originally constructed as a small ternary, the A-prime section alone is likely to be used in the recapitulation. Repeated continuation phrases may also be eliminated. The effect is formal compression while maintaining essential thematic content.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of recapitulation harmonic-tonal organization
- Knowledge of recapitulation as formal rotation through exposition material

# Key Properties
- Restatements appropriate in the exposition become redundant in the recapitulation
- Entire repeated main themes are typically stated only once
- Small ternary main themes may be reduced to A-prime section alone
- Repeated continuation phrases may be eliminated
- Deletion is especially likely when material was exploited in the development
- Creates formal compression while preserving essential content

# Construction / Recognition
Compare the recapitulation's main theme with the exposition's. Look for: (1) omission of a full repeat of the main theme, (2) reduction of a small ternary to its A-prime section, (3) elimination of repeated continuation phrases. Confirm that the deleted material was indeed a restatement (not unique content) and consider whether it appeared prominently in the development.

# Context & Application
Deletion of thematic restatements is one of the most common structural changes in the recapitulation's main theme. It reflects the changed formal function of the main theme: in the exposition, the theme introduces and fixes melodic-motivic ideas in the listener's mind; in the recapitulation, the material is already familiar and needs only to signal "return." Excessive repetition would be redundant and could make the recapitulation feel sluggish.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in D, K. 576, i: main theme repeat deleted in recapitulation (except for polyphonic opening), which is then redirected into transitional function
- General pattern: exposition repeats main theme in full; recapitulation states it once, then moves directly to transition

# Common Errors
- Assuming all main theme material must return in full in the recapitulation
- Failing to recognize that a shortened main theme represents a deliberate deletion, not a different theme

# Common Confusions
- Deletion of restatements does NOT mean the main theme is absent from the recapitulation -- the basic idea and essential content always return; only redundant repetitions are eliminated
