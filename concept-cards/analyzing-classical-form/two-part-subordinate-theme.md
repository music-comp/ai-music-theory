# Quick Definition
When material following an internal HC sounds like the beginning of another theme (with a new initiating function like a presentation), it creates a two-part subordinate theme. Rather than calling these two separate themes (one ending HC, one PAC), Caplin maintains the principle that all subordinate themes must close with an authentic cadence, treating the structure as a single two-part theme.

# Core Definition
At times, the material following an internal HC sounds more like the beginning of another subordinate theme than continuation of the ongoing one, because it presents a **new initiating function** (presentation or compound basic idea). Rather than speaking of two subordinate themes -- one ending with HC, the other with PAC -- it is more theoretically sound to maintain the principle that all subordinate themes must close with an authentic cadence, identifying a single **two-part subordinate theme**. Part 1 begins in the subordinate key and leads to an internal HC and standing on the dominant. Part 2 provides a new initiating function, eventually leading to the required PAC (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 402-403).

# Key Properties
1. Internal HC divides the theme into two parts
2. Part 2 begins with new initiating function (presentation or CBI)
3. Theoretically a single theme (not two separate ones)
4. Principle: all subordinate themes must end with authentic cadence
5. Part 1 has clear beginning, middle, end -- but no PAC
6. The lack of PAC in Part 1 motivates Part 2
7. Relatively rare in the classical repertory
