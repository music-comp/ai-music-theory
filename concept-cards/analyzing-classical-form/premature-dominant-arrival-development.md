# Quick Definition
A dominant arrival in the core that occurs before a genuine cadence materializes, with the harmonic goal arriving "too early" in relation to the phrase-structural processes, typically extended as a standing on the dominant.

# Core Definition
"Frequently, though, a genuine cadence fails to materialize, and a dominant arrival, sometimes a premature one, results instead" (p. 453). In retrospect, the dominant at a certain point is understood as the harmonic goal of the core, marking a premature dominant arrival. This technique is "seen more in later works of the classical era than in earlier ones," especially in Beethoven, reflecting the historical trend toward greater loosening of the development section.

# Prerequisites
- **Core** — The formal unit in which the premature arrival occurs
- **Dominant arrival** — The harmonic event itself (not a genuine cadence)

# Key Properties
1. Dominant arrives before phrase-structural processes reach a genuine cadence
2. Recognized in retrospect as the harmonic goal of the core
3. Typically extended as a standing on the dominant
4. More common in later classical works (especially Beethoven)
5. Part of historical trend: PAC (Baroque) -> HC (classical) -> dominant arrival (late classical)

# Examples
**Example 13.7** (p. 461): Beethoven, Op. 14, No. 2, i — dominant of E-flat reached at m. 93 during what seems like further sequential activity; extended to fermata at m. 98; understood in retrospect as the core's premature harmonic goal.
