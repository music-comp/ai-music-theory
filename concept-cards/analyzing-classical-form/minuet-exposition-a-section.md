# Quick Definition
The A section (exposition) of minuet form is usually a tight-knit, conventional form (sentence, period, or hybrid) closing with a PAC. It may either remain in the home key (expressing only main-theme function) or modulate to a subordinate key (expressing main theme, transition, and subordinate theme functions).

# Core Definition
As with the exposition of a small ternary, the A section of minuet form is usually constructed as a relatively tight-knit, conventional form and closes with a PAC. It may either remain entirely in the home key or modulate to a closely related subordinate key. If it modulates, thematic functionality is more complex, potentially expressing main theme, transition, and subordinate theme functions. At times the A section is highly expanded and may resemble an entire sonata-form exposition (Caplin, pp. 635-636).

# Key Properties
1. Tight-knit, conventional form (sentence, period, hybrid)
2. Closes with PAC
3. Two options: nonmodulating (main theme only) or modulating (more complex)
4. If nonmodulating: tonal contrast deferred to B section
5. If modulating: transition and subordinate-theme functions may appear
6. Form-functional fusion widespread in modulating cases
7. May include early authentic cadence, closing section

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Determine whether the A section modulates
2. If nonmodulating, identify main-theme function exclusively
3. If modulating, locate transition and subordinate-theme functions
4. Check for early authentic cadence (home-key PAC before the section ends)

# Context & Application
The decision whether to modulate in the A section has consequences for the entire minuet: if the A section stays in the home key, the B section usually provides the subordinate key; if it modulates, the B section is free to explore other regions or emphasize the dominant.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 638): Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, iii -- modulating A section with compound antecedent; transition/subordinate-theme fusion.
**Example 2** (p. 641): Mozart, Piano Sonata in A, K. 331, ii -- early authentic cadence at m. 10, followed by subordinate theme directly in new key.
**Example 3** (p. 642): Mozart, String Quartet in A, K. 464, ii -- A section resembles miniature sonata exposition with distinct main theme, transition, and subordinate theme.
