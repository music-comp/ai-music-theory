## Definition

The A section (exposition) of minuet form is usually constructed as a relatively tight-knit, conventional form (sentence, period, or hybrid) and closes with a PAC.

## Two Options

### 1. Nonmodulating A Section
- Remains entirely in home key
- Ends with home-key PAC (exceptionally, HC)
- Thematic expression: exclusively **main theme**
- Expectations: B section will bring tonal contrast

### 2. Modulating A Section
- Modulates to subordinate key
- Thematic functionality more complex
- Main theme, transition, and subordinate theme functions may all appear

## Scale and Complexity

- Sometimes highly expanded, embracing more than one thematic unit
- Each unit may end with PAC
- Most extreme cases: resembles an entire sonata-form exposition

## Subordinate Key Significance

Unlike small ternary (where modulation is of minor concern because the form serves as main theme in a larger context):
- In minuet form, subordinate key may represent **principal tonal contrast** of the minuet
- Presence or absence of new key is particularly important

## When Both Minuets Are Nonmodulatory

Rarely is a subordinate key not established somewhere in complete minuet/trio form. If entire minuet is nonmodulatory:
- Companion minuet (minuet proper or trio) usually expresses subordinate-key function
