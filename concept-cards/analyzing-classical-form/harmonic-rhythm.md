# Quick Definition
The rate at which harmonies change in a musical passage, which influences analytical notation decisions about subordinate harmonies.

# Core Definition
Harmonic rhythm refers to the rate of harmonic change within a musical passage. Caplin notes that in actual musical analysis, "a greater flexibility in the use of parentheses will help differentiate levels of harmonic organization, and in many cases the subordinate harmonies will stand outside of the parentheses in order better to reflect the harmonic rhythm of the passage" (p. 26). The analytical representation should clarify the perceived rate of harmonic change.

# Prerequisites
- **Prolongational progression** — Harmonic rhythm depends on distinguishing structural from embellishing harmonies
- **Subordinate harmony** — The notation of subordinate harmonies reflects harmonic rhythm

# Key Properties
1. Measures the frequency of chord changes
2. Distinguishes structural harmonies from embellishing ones
3. Influences analytical notation (parentheses for subordinate harmonies)
4. Typically accelerates approaching cadential arrivals
5. Slows during prolongational passages

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Note the frequency of chord changes in the passage
2. Distinguish between structural and ornamental harmonic changes
3. Consider whether subordinate harmonies contribute to the perceived rhythm
4. Adjust analytical notation (parentheses) to reflect harmonic rhythm accurately

# Context & Application
Harmonic rhythm relates to phrase structure and formal articulation, energy and momentum in musical passages, and the distinction between structural and ornamental harmony. When analyzing, the notation should "better reflect the harmonic rhythm of the passage" -- some subordinate harmonies may remain outside parentheses if they significantly contribute to the rhythmic feel.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating all chord changes as equal contributors to harmonic rhythm
  **Correction**: Distinguish structural harmonies from subordinate/embellishing ones

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming faster harmonic rhythm always indicates greater tension
  **Clarification**: Harmonic rhythm interacts with other parameters; its significance depends on formal context
