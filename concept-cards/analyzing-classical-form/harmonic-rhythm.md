# Quick Definition
The rate at which harmonies change in a musical passage, typically accelerating as cadential arrivals approach and slowing during prolongational passages.

# Formal Definition
Harmonic rhythm refers to the rate of harmonic change within a musical passage. The analytical notation of subordinate harmonies within prolongational progressions allows greater flexibility "in order better to reflect the harmonic rhythm of the passage."

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Factors affecting harmonic rhythm perception:
- Frequency of chord changes
- Relationship between chord changes and metric accent
- Distinction between structural and embellishing harmonies
- Placement of cadential progressions

Notation considerations:
- Subordinate harmonies may or may not be parenthesized depending on their contribution to perceived harmonic rhythm
- Local embellishing harmonies may not significantly contribute to the basic harmonic rhythm
- The analytical representation should clarify levels of harmonic organization

# Musical Context/Application
Harmonic rhythm relates to:
- Phrase structure and formal articulation
- Energy and momentum in musical passages
- The distinction between structural and ornamental harmony
- Cadential approach (often features acceleration)

When analyzing, the notation should "better reflect the harmonic rhythm of the passage" - meaning some subordinate harmonies might remain outside parentheses if they significantly contribute to the rhythmic feel.

# Examples
The chapter notes: "In the analysis of actual musical passages, a greater flexibility in the use of parentheses will help differentiate levels of harmonic organization, and in many cases the subordinate harmonies will stand outside of the parentheses in order better to reflect the harmonic rhythm of the passage" (p. 26).

# Common Confusions
- Treating all chord changes as equal contributors to harmonic rhythm
- Not distinguishing between structural and embellishing harmonic motion
- Assuming faster harmonic rhythm always means more tension
- Ignoring metric placement of harmony changes
