# Quick Definition
The last basic theme type, consisting of two parts (each typically 8 real measures, repeated), distinguished from the small ternary by one essential feature: the small binary does NOT recapitulate its opening materials -- the initial basic idea, harmonized by tonic of the home key, does not return later in the theme.

# Core Definition
The small binary resembles the rounded binary externally (two repeated parts of similar length) but differs fundamentally: no recapitulation. The two parts are labeled neutrally as "Part 1" and "Part 2" (letter labels A-B or A-A' are inappropriate because they carry functional meanings). Part 2 often begins with ideas motivically related to Part 1's beginning, which dampens expectations for recapitulation. The small binary emphasizes structural symmetry (8+8) more than any other theme type and infrequently undergoes phrase deviations. It is especially appropriate for variations and rondos, where the basic idea's return is reserved for the beginning of each new variation or refrain.

# Prerequisites
- **Small ternary** — Must understand what recapitulation means to recognize its absence

# Key Properties
1. Essential difference: no recapitulation (basic idea harmonized by tonic does not return)
2. Two parts labeled "Part 1" and "Part 2" (not A-B or A-A')
3. Part 2 often begins motivically like Part 1
4. Usually symmetrical 8+8; phrase deviations rare
5. Appropriate for variations and rondos
6. Part 1 may end with PAC or HC
7. Part 2 always ends with PAC in home key
8. Part 2 may or may not contain a contrasting middle

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 263): Haydn, Piano Trio H. 14, ii, mm. 1-16 -- Part 1 as sentence modulating to V; Part 2 opens with "dominant version" of b.i. as B section, followed by continuation (not recapitulation).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Using A-B or A-A' labels for small binary parts
  **Correction**: These labels carry functional meanings (exposition, contrasting middle, recapitulation) that don't apply; use Part 1 and Part 2

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing small binary with compound period when Part 1 ends with HC
  **Clarification**: If a presumed compound antecedent is fully repeated (with repeat signs) and ends with HC again, it is a small binary Part 1, not a compound antecedent
