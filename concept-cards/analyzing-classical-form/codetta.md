# Quick Definition
A codetta is a brief postcadential unit (typically 1-2 measures) that follows a PAC, prolonging tonic harmony and the tonic scale degree, often emphasizing subdominant harmony as a dynamic counterweight to the preceding cadential dominant.

# Core Definition
Codettas are the building blocks of closing sections. Their size is variable (from a single chord to a full 4-m. phrase). Harmony is most often tonic prolongational, with tonic pedal frequently employed. Subdominant emphasis (often with tonicization, V/IV) is highly characteristic -- it functions as a dynamic counterweight to the cadential dominant, creating relaxation. Melody tends to revolve around the tonic scale degree (1) or has it as a melodic goal. The codetta avoids opening up melodic space in a manner that would signal a new basic idea. If a codetta occupies a full 4-m. phrase, it may itself close with a brief cadential idea -- a "cadence of limited scope."

# Key Properties
1. Size: variable (single chord to 4-m. phrase; typically 1-2 mm.)
2. Harmony: tonic prolongational, tonic pedal, I-V-I alternation
3. Subdominant emphasis is highly characteristic (IV, V/IV)
4. Melody: revolves around or descends to tonic scale degree
5. Avoids opening melodic space (no new basic idea)
6. May use cadential progression (cadence of limited scope)
7. May use deceptive progression followed by authentic version
8. So-called "plagal cadence" (I-IV-I) is typically a codetta, not a cadence

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate material following a PAC
2. Check if it prolongs tonic (harmony and melody)
3. Note subdominant emphasis if present
4. Verify it does not open new melodic space

# Context & Application
Many musicians fail to distinguish cadences from codettas. The final measures of a work rarely bring cadences -- they are almost always codettas following an earlier PAC. The so-called "plagal cadence" (Amen figure) is almost always a codetta with characteristic subdominant emphasis.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 169): Mozart, K. 331, iii, mm. 122-27 -- codettas built exclusively on root-position tonic.
**Example 2** (p. 171): Mozart, K. 428, ii, mm. 92-96 -- codetta with tonic pedal and chromatic line emphasizing subdominant.
**Example 3** (p. 174): Beethoven, Op. 135, iii -- cadential melody taken up by cello as first codetta.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Calling the final measures of a piece "cadences"
  **Correction**: These are almost always codettas; true cadences occur earlier

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Identifying "plagal cadences" (I-IV-I) as genuine cadences
  **Clarification**: Almost all such cases are codettas with characteristic subdominant emphasis, not cadences
