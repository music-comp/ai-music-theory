# Quick Definition
The transition is frequently quite short -- often less than eight measures and sometimes only four -- far from the one-third of the exposition that students might expect. Many passages traditionally identified as belonging to the transition are better understood, from the form-functional perspective, as belonging to the opening part of the subordinate-theme group.

# Core Definition
Knowing that the transition constitutes one of three principal formal functions of the exposition, students may believe it should occupy about one-third of the exposition as a whole. Very rarely is this the case. Rather, the transition is frequently quite short, often taking up less than eight measures and even sometimes only four. Many passages that more traditional approaches to sonata form have identified as belonging to the transition are better understood, from the form-functional perspective advocated by Caplin, as belonging to the opening part of the subordinate-theme group. The transition's functions -- destabilizing the home key, effecting modulation, leading to dominant -- can often be accomplished efficiently, without requiring extensive length (Caplin, Ch. 11, p. 350).

# Key Properties
1. Transitions often less than eight measures
2. Sometimes only four measures
3. Rarely approaches one-third of the exposition
4. Traditional analyses often overestimate transition length
5. Material assigned to transition may actually be subordinate-theme opening
6. Transition functions can be accomplished efficiently

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Do not assume the transition must be long
2. Identify the transition's structural beginning and end (HC or dominant arrival)
3. Material after the transition's end that is in the subordinate key may belong to the subordinate theme
4. Look for subordinate-theme characteristics (initiating function in subordinate key) that might be mistakenly attributed to the transition

# Context & Application
This observation has significant analytical implications. When analysts assign too much material to the transition, they correspondingly misidentify the subordinate theme as beginning later than it actually does. Caplin's form-functional approach often reveals subordinate-theme structures beginning earlier than traditional analyses suggest, with the transition being shorter and more focused.
