# Quick Definition
An ultimate dominant is the harmonic goal of a half-cadential progression (must be a root-position triad). A penultimate dominant precedes the final tonic in an authentic cadential progression (may be triad or seventh chord). The distinction is about grouping, not chord content.

# Core Definition
Whether a dominant can be characterized as ultimate or penultimate has nothing to do with harmony per se. Rather, it is a matter of how the dominant groups with its surrounding harmonies. In the case of a penultimate dominant (authentic cadential progression), the dominant groups with the following tonic as part of the progression. In the case of an ultimate dominant (half-cadential progression), the dominant does not group with the following tonic, since the latter signals the start of a new progression (pp. 39-40).

# Key Properties
1. Ultimate dominant: harmonic goal of HC progression; must be root-position triad (no seventh)
2. Penultimate dominant: "before-the-last" in authentic cadential progression; may have seventh
3. The distinction is about grouping, not chord structure
4. Ultimate V does NOT group with following tonic (which begins new progression)
5. Penultimate V DOES group with following tonic as part of same progression
6. Adding a seventh to ultimate dominant overly destabilizes it as an ending harmony
7. Adding a seventh to penultimate dominant appropriately enhances drive toward resolution

# Construction / Recognition
## To Distinguish:
1. Does the dominant end the progression? -- Ultimate (HC)
2. Does the dominant drive into a following tonic within the same progression? -- Penultimate (authentic)
3. Ultimate: must be consonant triad
4. Penultimate: may be seventh chord; often embellished by cadential six-four

# Context & Application
This distinction is fundamental to identifying cadence types. The ultimate dominant ends formal units (antecedent phrases, transitions, contrasting middles). The penultimate dominant creates characteristic tension before cadential resolution. Both must be in root position for cadential status.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Adding a seventh to the ultimate dominant
  **Correction**: Ultimate dominant must be a consonant triad

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the distinction relates to chord structure
  **Clarification**: It is entirely about how the dominant groups with surrounding harmonies
- **Confusion**: Including the following tonic in the half-cadential progression
  **Clarification**: The tonic after an ultimate dominant begins a new progression
