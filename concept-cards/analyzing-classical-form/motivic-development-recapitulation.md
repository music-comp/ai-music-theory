# Quick Definition
Although motivic development is typically associated with contrasting middles, it can also occur significantly in the recapitulation, with the A' section developing motives through stepwise sequences, new continuation phrases, fragmentation, and chromatic enrichment.

# Core Definition
Development is not confined to the B section: the A' section may extensively develop motives from prior sections, drawing new consequences from melodic material. Developmental techniques in A' include stepwise sequences elaborating basic-idea material, new continuation phrases exploring motives, fragmentation of previously unified material, and chromatic enrichment of harmonic language. Such development typically expands the recapitulation, changes the theme's character, creates structural changes (not merely ornamental), and may make A' looser than A with phrase-structural asymmetry, prominent sequential passages, and extensive chromatic harmonies.

# Key Properties
1. Development not confined to B section; A' can be significantly developmental
2. Techniques: stepwise sequences, new continuation, fragmentation, chromatic enrichment
3. Typically expands the recapitulation
4. Changes the theme's character
5. Creates structural changes (not ornamental)
6. May make A' looser than A
7. When both B and A' are developmental, comparison between them becomes complex

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Compare A' with A bar by bar
2. Look for sequential elaboration of basic-idea motives
3. Check for new continuation material not present in exposition
4. Note fragmentation of previously unified phrases
5. Identify chromatic enrichment beyond the exposition's harmonic language
6. Assess whether the development expands the section's length

# Context & Application
A heavily developmental recapitulation blurs the traditional boundary between "contrasting middle" (development) and "recapitulation" (return). Both B and A' may be loosely organized, but different criteria contribute to the loosening in each: the B section's looseness comes from harmonic instability and dominant emphasis, while A''s looseness comes from motivic elaboration and phrase-structural expansion. This anticipates the more extensive developmental recapitulations found in sonata form.
