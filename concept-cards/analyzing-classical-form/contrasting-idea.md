# Quick Definition
The 2-m. unit following the b.i. in an antecedent phrase, achieving contrast primarily through melodic-motivic content and leading to a weak cadence. "Contrasting" means "not a repetition." The c.i. often contains continuation characteristics (fragmentation, harmonic acceleration) and must close with a cadential progression.

# Core Definition
The contrasting idea achieves its "contrast" with the basic idea most obviously by means of melodic-motivic content. In the clearest cases, it introduces distinctly different motives. Contrast may also be achieved by secondary features such as texture, dynamics, and articulation (p. 99). The notion of contrasting must be understood as "not a repetition" -- the difference may be striking or minimal (p. 101). The basic idea usually has tonic harmony at its basis; the contrasting idea must close with a cadential progression (p. 100). A contrasting idea often contains characteristics of continuation function: fragmentation, accelerated harmonic or surface rhythm, and sequential harmonies (p. 101).

# Prerequisites
- **Basic idea** — The c.i. contrasts with the b.i.
- **Antecedent phrase** — The c.i. follows the b.i. in the antecedent

# Key Properties
1. 2-m. unit following the b.i. in the antecedent
2. "Contrasting" = not a repetition (contrast may be striking or minimal)
3. Achieves contrast through melodic-motivic content primarily
4. Must close with a cadential progression (weak: HC or IAC)
5. Often contains continuation characteristics (fragmentation, harmonic acceleration)
6. B.i. has tonic harmony; c.i. closes with cadential progression
7. When entirely supported by cadential progression, resembles a "cadential idea"
8. Also called "complementary idea"

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Check mm. 3-4 of the antecedent for material that is NOT a repetition of mm. 1-2
2. Verify it closes with a cadential progression
3. Note any continuation characteristics
4. In ambiguous cases: the return of b.i. at m. 5 retroactively confirms mm. 3-4 as contrasting

# Context & Application
The contrasting idea appears in both antecedent and consequent phrases. The consequent's c.i. may resemble the antecedent's c.i. or bring entirely new material. In cases where b.i. and c.i. share motives, the underlying harmonies distinguish them (b.i. = tonic prolongation; c.i. = cadential progression).

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 99, Ex. 3.3): Haydn, H. 27, iii -- c.i. with entirely different motives from b.i.

**Example 2** (p. 100, Ex. 3.5): Haydn, Symphony No. 99, iv -- c.i. very similar to b.i., distinguished mainly by contour direction.

**Example 3** (p. 100, Ex. 3.6): Mozart, K. 459, i -- same motive returns but with completely different harmonic progression (HC instead of tonic prolongation).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Identifying a c.i. when the b.i. is being repeated (that would indicate a presentation, not an antecedent)
  **Correction**: If mm. 3-4 repeat the b.i., this is a presentation phrase (sentence), not an antecedent (period)

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Expecting the c.i. to be completely different from the b.i.
  **Clarification**: Minimal contrast suffices; "contrasting" means "not a repetition"
