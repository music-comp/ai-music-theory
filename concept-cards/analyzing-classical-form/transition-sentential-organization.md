# Quick Definition
Most transitions employ sentential functions (presentation, continuation, cadential) but in a loose manner. When the length and complexity vastly exceed an 8-measure sentence, describe the organization as "sentential" or "sentence-like" rather than calling it a sentence; reserve the term "sentence" for tightly knit manifestations.

# Core Definition
Most transitions employ the phrase functions associated with the sentence theme type (presentation, continuation, cadential), but in a **loose** manner through phrase-structural means (extension, expansion, compression, redundant repetitions) or harmonic-tonal destabilizations (modal shift, chromaticism, tonicization). When length and complexity vastly differ from a simple 8-m. sentence, speak more generally of a **sentential** or **sentence-like** organization. Reserve the term **sentence** for more tightly knit manifestations (Caplin, Ch. 11, pp. 346-347).

1. Presentation, continuation, cadential functions typical

4. "Sentential" vs. "sentence" is a terminological distinction of precision
5. Reserve "sentence" for tight-knit themes; use "sentential" for looser structures
