# Quick Definition
Some main themes are organized nonconventionally yet still occupy eight measures, retaining a sense of tight-knit organization through their standard length despite irregular internal grouping structures such as 2+3+3, 3+3+2, or 3+2+3.

# Core Definition
Some main themes cannot be easily accommodated by standard categories (sentence, period, hybrid, small ternary) yet still occupy the standard eight measures. Because of this standard length, the nonconventional theme retains a distinct sense of tight-knit organization despite formal irregularities. Most often, the grouping structure is asymmetrical (e.g., 2+3+3, 3+3+2, 3+2+3), yet the internal units nonetheless manifest a definite expression of phrase functionality. Even with irregular grouping, units typically express initiating function, continuational characteristics, and cadential function for closure. Caplin recommends a bottom-up analytical approach: consider harmonic organization, group material into basic units, establish higher-level groupings, and then assign functional labels.

# Key Properties
1. Cannot be classified as sentence, period, hybrid, or ternary
2. Still occupies standard 8-measure length
3. Retains tight-knit quality despite irregularities
4. Asymmetrical internal grouping structure
5. Internal units still express recognizable phrase functions
6. Bottom-up analysis recommended (not top-down)

# Construction / Recognition
## Bottom-Up Analytical Approach:
1. Consider harmonic organization first
2. Group material into basic surface-level units
3. Establish higher-level groupings that embrace the lower-level ones
4. Assign formal function to groups (initiating, medial, concluding)
5. Identify any standard functions (basic idea, continuation, cadential idea)
6. What makes the theme nonconventional is that functions do not organize in standard ways

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 311): Haydn, String Quartet Op. 74/3 ("Rider"), iv, mm. 1-8 -- grouping 2+3+3; opening 2 mm. = basic idea (1+1); next 3 mm. = continuation (bass rises, rhythmic activity increases); final 3 mm. = cadential function closing with HC. More sentential than periodic, but no presentation phrase and continuation/cadential occupy distinct groups.
**Example 2** (p. 313): Haydn, Piano Sonata H. 34, i, mm. 1-8 -- grouping 3+3+2 (or 3+2+3); mm. 1-3 clearly initiating over root-position tonic; mm. 4-6 continuation and cadential; mm. 7-8 postcadential standing on the dominant. Related to sentence but insufficiently expressed.
