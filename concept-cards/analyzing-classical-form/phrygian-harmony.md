# Quick Definition
A term applied to the Neapolitan sixth chord (bII6), referencing the characteristic half-step descent in the bass from lowered 6 to 5, reminiscent of the Phrygian mode.

# Formal Definition
The "Neapolitan" or "phrygian" harmony in first inversion (bII6) is an important pre-dominant, especially in minor. The term "phrygian" refers to the characteristic melodic motion associated with this harmony: the half-step bass descent that evokes the Phrygian mode's distinctive lowered second scale degree.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
The phrygian connection:
- Phrygian mode features half-step between scale degrees 1 and 2
- bII6 has lowered 2 in its root
- When bII6 moves to V, bass descends 4-leading tone-5
- The half-step descent (from lowered 6 to 5) evokes Phrygian character

Phrygian approaches to half cadence:
- iv6-V (common in minor)
- bVI-V (with bass descending b6 to 5)
- These create "phrygian" half cadences

# Musical Context/Application
Phrygian harmony:
- Functions as pre-dominant (usually bII6)
- Creates distinctive chromatic color
- Especially common in minor mode
- May be referenced in half-cadential approaches

The Phrygian quality arises from the characteristic half-step descent, whether in the Neapolitan or in other progressions approaching V from scale degree b6.

# Common Confusions
- Treating Phrygian harmony as modulation to Phrygian mode (it remains in the home key)
- Not recognizing the half-step descent as the defining characteristic
- Confusing with other chromatic pre-dominants
- Limiting phrygian motion to only the Neapolitan (other progressions share this quality)
