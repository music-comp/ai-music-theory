# Quick Definition
A term applied to the Neapolitan sixth chord (bII6), referencing the characteristic half-step descent in the bass that evokes the Phrygian mode, functioning as an important pre-dominant especially in minor.

# Core Definition
"The 'Neapolitan' or 'phrygian' harmony in first inversion (bII6) is another important pre-dominant, especially in minor" (p. 32). The term "phrygian" refers to the characteristic half-step descent evocative of the Phrygian mode. Progressions "in which the ultimate dominant is approached by descending motion from the sixth degree (usually lowered), are especially associated with the half-cadential progression" (p. 40).

# Prerequisites
- **Pre-dominant function** — bII6 functions as pre-dominant
- **Scale degree** — Understanding lowered scale degree 2 and its harmonic context

# Key Properties
1. bII6 = Neapolitan sixth, a major triad on lowered 2 in first inversion
2. "Phrygian" name references the half-step descent characteristic of Phrygian mode
3. Especially important in minor mode
4. Functions as pre-dominant, resolving to V
5. The half-step bass descent (from lowered 6 to 5) creates distinctive phrygian cadential approach
6. Phrygian half cadences: iv6-V or bVI-V with bass descending b6 to 5

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for a major triad built on lowered scale degree 2 in first inversion
2. Note the half-step descent in the bass approaching the dominant
3. Check for the characteristic phrygian motion: b6 descending to 5 in the bass
4. Confirm pre-dominant function within a cadential progression

# Context & Application
Phrygian harmony functions as pre-dominant, creating distinctive chromatic color especially in minor mode. The phrygian quality arises from the characteristic half-step descent, whether in the Neapolitan chord or in other progressions approaching V from scale degree b6. Phrygian half cadences (iv6-V, bVI-V) are especially associated with the half-cadential progression.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating phrygian harmony as modulation to Phrygian mode
  **Correction**: The phrygian element is a characteristic half-step descent within the home key, not a modal shift

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Limiting phrygian motion to only the Neapolitan chord
  **Clarification**: Other progressions approaching V from b6 (iv6-V, bVI-V) also share the phrygian quality
