## Transition Unusual Opening Strategies

Beyond the four typical opening strategies, some other procedures are employed occasionally.

### 1. Later Portions of Main Theme

Rather than beginning with material from the main theme's opening:
- Transition starts with material from **continuation phrase**
- Or takes up the **cadential idea** of main theme
- Develops this material to lead elsewhere

### 2. B Section Material

If main theme is a small ternary:
- Transition starts with material from the **B section**
- Creates impression that B and A' sections will be repeated
- Instead modulates to new key

### 3. A' Section "Becomes" Transition

Main theme forms as small ternary, but:
- The A' section departs from the path of prior A section
- Instead of closing with home-key PAC, leads to dominant closure
- A' section is understood retrospectively to have "become" a transition

### Distinguishing A' → Transition from Two-Part Transition

Two factors help distinguish:
1. A and B sections of small ternary must **not elide** - if they do, it's two-part transition
2. B section often emphasizes home-key dominant at beginning; transition tends to emphasize tonic
