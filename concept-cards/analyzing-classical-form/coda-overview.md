# Quick Definition
A coda is an optional framing function that may follow the recapitulation in sonata form, extending beyond the material of the exposition to provide additional emphasis on the home key and a more decisive sense of conclusion.

# Core Definition
The coda is one of two optional framing functions in sonata form (the other being the slow introduction). It follows the recapitulation's final PAC and extends beyond the corresponding material of the exposition. The recapitulation ends when it no longer corresponds with the exposition; material beyond that point constitutes a coda. In the absence of a genuine coda, the closing section may be extended to impart a more decisive sense of conclusion. The boundary between an extended closing section and a coda can sometimes be unclear.

# Prerequisites
- **Sonata form large-scale structure** -- The three required sections the coda frames
- **Recapitulation subordinate theme** -- The final PAC after which the coda appears

# Key Properties
1. Optional framing function (not required)
2. Follows the recapitulation's final PAC
3. Extends beyond material corresponding to the exposition
4. Provides additional emphasis on the home key
5. Creates a more decisive sense of conclusion
6. Boundary with extended closing section can be unclear

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate the final PAC of the recapitulation
2. Check whether the closing section corresponds to the exposition's closing section
3. Material that goes beyond the exposition's scope = coda
4. If the closing section is merely extended, the distinction may be debatable

# Context & Application
The coda balances the slow introduction at the other end of the movement. While the slow introduction prepares the beginning, the coda reinforces the ending. In movements without a coda, the closing section often receives extension to serve a similar conclusive purpose. Chapters 15 and 16 provide detailed treatment of coda and slow introduction respectively.

# Examples
**Example** (p. 305): Beethoven, Op. 2, No. 1, i -- the closing section in the recapitulation begins like the exposition's but is extended to form a complete sentence, whose continuation is stretched to eight measures. The resulting structure could even be considered a "second" subordinate theme.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Any postcadential material in the recapitulation is a coda
  **Clarification**: Material that corresponds to the exposition's closing section is still closing section; only material beyond the exposition's scope is coda
