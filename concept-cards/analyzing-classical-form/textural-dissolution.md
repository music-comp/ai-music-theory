# Quick Definition
Textural dissolution is the systematic reduction of musical texture -- voices progressively dropping out, potentially concluding with a single melodic line -- typically occurring within a standing on the dominant to "clear the stage" before the recapitulation.

# Core Definition
Textural dissolution is one of two preparatory techniques (the other being motivic anticipation) typically appearing within a postcadential standing on the dominant before the recapitulation. The process involves a multi-voice texture gradually thinning, with voices dropping out progressively, potentially concluding with a single melodic line or minimal activity. Against this background of reduced texture, the recapitulation sounds more prominent, the return of the basic idea is highlighted, and a fresh, new character is achieved. The technique "clears the stage" for the recapitulation's return.

# Prerequisites
- **Postcadential standing on the dominant** -- The formal context where dissolution occurs

# Key Properties
1. Systematic reduction of texture
2. Voices drop out progressively
3. May conclude with single melodic line
4. Occurs within standing on the dominant
5. Positioned just before A' begins
6. Creates contrast that highlights the recapitulation's return
7. Often combined with motivic anticipation

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate the standing on the dominant (postcadential or as B section)
2. Check for progressive thinning of texture
3. Note whether voices drop out gradually
4. Look for minimal texture just before the recapitulation begins
5. Check whether the reduced texture combines with motivic anticipation of A' material

# Context & Application
Textural dissolution works in conjunction with motivic anticipation to prepare the recapitulation. While dissolution creates a textural "clearing" that makes the return sound fresh, motivic anticipation creates a thematic connection to the upcoming material. The same technique appears at the end of sonata-form development sections, where the standing on the dominant before the recapitulation often features textural dissolution (as in Beethoven, Op. 2, No. 1, i, mm. 93-94).
