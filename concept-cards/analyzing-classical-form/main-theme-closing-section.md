# Quick Definition
If the main theme ends with a PAC, an optional closing section consisting of codettas may follow, prolonging tonic before the transition begins. If the main theme ends with an HC, a standing on the dominant may occur instead.

# Core Definition
A closing section following the main theme only occurs after a PAC (not after HC or IAC) and is optional. It prolongs the achieved tonic through postcadential material before the transition begins. If the main theme ends with an HC, a closing section strictly cannot follow; instead, a standing on the dominant may occur. Material that initially seems to be a closing section may retrospectively be understood as the beginning of the transition (a false closing section). Unlike subordinate themes, main themes only occasionally include a closing section because less energy is built up in achieving the main-theme cadence, so less postcadential dissipation is needed.

# Key Properties
1. Only after PAC (not HC or IAC)
2. Optional, not required
3. Prolongs tonic harmony
4. Less common than subordinate-theme closing sections
5. HC ending leads to standing on the dominant instead
6. May be retrospectively reinterpreted as false closing section (beginning of transition)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Determine main theme's cadence type (PAC required for closing section)
2. Look for postcadential material prolonging tonic
3. Check whether the material truly functions as postcadential or begins transitional activity
4. If HC: look for standing on the dominant rather than closing section

# Context & Application
The main-theme closing section is far less prominent than the closing section after the subordinate theme. Since the main theme's cadence is less dramatically achieved (less energy buildup), there is correspondingly less need for postcadential dissipation. The concept of false closing section is important: what seems like a codetta may turn out to be the transition's opening strategy.
