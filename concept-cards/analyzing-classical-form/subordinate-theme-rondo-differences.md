# Quick Definition
In rondos, the subordinate key is established less emphatically than in sonata: the transition may be omitted, the subordinate theme may lack PAC closure, and the theme tends to be more compressed. The tonal conflict is tempered because emphasis falls on the return of the refrain.

# Core Definition
The establishment and confirmation of a subordinate key in a rondo are often less emphatic than in sonata: a distinct transition is sometimes entirely omitted; the subordinate theme may lack cadential closure; and the subordinate theme can be relatively compressed and simple. The tonal conflict of home and subordinate keys tends to be tempered in rondo forms (Caplin, pp. 673-675).

# Prerequisites
- **Subordinate-theme complex** -- The rondo structure containing subordinate themes

# Key Properties
1. Transition sometimes entirely omitted (subordinate theme begins directly in new key)
2. PAC not always required (unlike sonata, where subordinate theme must close with PAC)
3. Subordinate theme can be relatively compressed and simple
4. Tonal conflict tempered compared to sonata
5. Emphasis on return of refrain rather than subordinate-key drama
6. Retransition function more significant than in sonata

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 674): Beethoven, Piano Sonata in D, Op. 10, No. 3, iv -- extremely incomplete subordinate theme with only weak initiating function and brief retransition; continuation and cadential functions entirely eliminated.
