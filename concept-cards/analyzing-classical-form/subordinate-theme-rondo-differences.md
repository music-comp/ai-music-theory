## Key Differences from Sonata

The establishment and confirmation of a subordinate key in a rondo are often **less emphatic** than in a sonata:

### 1. Transition May Be Omitted
- End of main theme immediately followed by subordinate theme
- Subordinate theme begins directly in new key
- Internal HC or dominant arrival provides emphasis on subordinate-key dominant

### 2. PAC Not Always Required
- Sonata's subordinate theme **must** close with PAC
- Rondo's subordinate theme may lack cadential closure
- Music may move without interruption into retransition
- Recapitulation's subordinate theme may also end without PAC (leading into coda)

### 3. Relative Compression
- Sonata: Subordinate-theme group usually complex and expansive
- Rondo: Subordinate theme can be relatively compressed and simple

## The Underlying Principle

*The tonal conflict of home and subordinate keys—so often dramatized in sonata form—tends to be tempered in rondo forms.*

## Example: Beethoven, Piano Sonata in D, Op. 10, No. 3, iv

- Transition ends with HC in new key (m. 16)
- Subordinate theme: compound basic idea supported by vi6-V6/4
- Phrase begins to repeat but V6/4 converts to VI of home key
- Following fermata: return of rondo refrain
- Extremely incomplete subordinate theme: only weak initiating function + brief retransition
- Continuation and cadential functions entirely eliminated
