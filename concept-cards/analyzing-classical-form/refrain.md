## Definition

The refrain is the initial section of any rondo form, functioning as a main theme. It is usually built as a small ternary or small binary and always appears in the home key.

## General Characteristics

### Compared to Sonata Main Themes
Rondo refrains are generally **more tightly knit**:
- Always close with PAC (never HC)
- Tend to be conventional and symmetrical
- Looser, nonconventional themes (common in sonata) are rare

### Theme Type Preference
- Periodic forms favored over sentences or sentence-like hybrids
- The 8-measure period frequently appears
- May serve as first part of small ternary/binary or as stand-alone theme

## Additional Elements

- May be followed by a closing section
- A second main theme in home key may sometimes appear as part of opening refrain

## Returns of the Refrain

All returns are set in the **home key**. They may:
- Bring back the complete structure of the opening
- Include ornamental changes (melodic embellishment, textural enrichment)
- Be **abridged**: Only A (or A') section of an original ternary
- Be **incomplete**: Lack closing PAC or conclude in another tonal region

## Tonal Stability

The refrain's consistent home-key setting:
- Provides tonal anchor throughout the movement
- Creates sense of "coming home" with each return
- Contrasts with the tonal variety of the couplets
