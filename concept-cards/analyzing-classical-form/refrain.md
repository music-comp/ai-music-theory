# Quick Definition
The refrain is the recurring main theme of any rondo form, always set in the home key. It is more tightly knit than a sonata main theme: it always closes with a PAC, favors periodic forms, and is typically built as a small ternary or small binary.

# Core Definition
The rondo refrain functions as a main theme, returning after each contrasting couplet. It is almost always a conventional, tight-knit theme closing in the home key with a PAC. The small ternary theme type is commonly used. Main themes in rondo forms are generally more tightly knit than those in sonata form: they always close with a PAC (never HC), favor periodic forms, and shun sentences or sentence-like hybrids (Caplin, pp. 665, 672).

# Key Properties
1. Always in the home key
2. Always closes with PAC (never HC, unlike sonata)
3. Conventional and symmetrical
4. Periodic forms favored over sentences
5. 8-measure period frequently used (stand-alone or part of small ternary)
6. May be followed by closing section or second main theme
7. Returns may be complete, abridged, or incomplete

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Identify the main theme that returns multiple times
2. Confirm it is in the home key with PAC closure
3. Note the tight-knit, periodic organization

# Context & Application
The refrain's repeated appearances require clear, memorable thematic identity and strong cadential closure. The emphasis on returns distinguishes rondo from sonata aesthetics.
