## Sequence vs. Cadence

A sequential progression that ends with ii-V-I does **not** normally create a cadence.

### Why Sequences Don't Create Cadences

The final unit of a sequence is a **repetition** of a prior unit:
- It functions as part of the sequential chain
- It does not function as a distinct closing unit
- No unique cadential material is present

### The Classical Solution

Classical composers usually write a **distinct cadential progression after** the sequence completes:
- The sequence creates the harmonic motion
- A separate unit then provides cadential closure

### Premature Dominant Arrival

When the final link of a sequence lands on the dominant:
- The harmonic goal may be achieved
- But melodic and grouping processes may not be complete
- This creates a "premature" dominant arrival
- The dominant functions as end of sequence, not true cadence

### Analytical Implication

When analyzing sequential passages:
- Don't assume the sequence "cadences"
- Look for a distinct cadential unit following the sequence
- Note if the sequence ends on dominant (which may still need resolution)
