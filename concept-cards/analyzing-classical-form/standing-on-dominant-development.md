# Quick Definition
The postcadential prolongation of dominant harmony following the HC or dominant arrival that closes a core, often highly extended and serving to prepare for the recapitulation.

# Core Definition
"The half cadence (or dominant arrival) closing a core is usually followed by a standing on the dominant" (p. 453). This postcadential unit is "often highly extended by means of several distinct sections, each with its own melodic-motivic content." When it occurs at the end of the development, "anticipatory motives derived from the basic idea of the main theme often appear to help prepare for the beginning of the recapitulation."

# Prerequisites
- **Core** — The formal unit whose conclusion generates the standing on the dominant
- **Standing on the dominant** — The general formal function
- **Half cadence** — The cadential articulation it follows

# Key Properties
1. Follows the HC or dominant arrival closing the core
2. Often highly extended with multiple sections
3. Each section may have its own melodic-motivic content
4. Anticipatory main-theme motives prepare for recapitulation
5. Marks the final boundary of the core (and often of the entire development)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate the HC or dominant arrival ending the core
2. The standing on the dominant is the subsequent passage prolonging that dominant
3. Look for anticipatory motives from the main theme near the end

# Context & Application
Traditional theories often call this a "retransition," but Caplin restricts that term to passages whose primary function is the actual modulation back home. By the time the standing on the dominant begins, the home key has already been achieved by the cadential articulation. The standing on the dominant is thus postcadential, not transitional.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Core concluding function** — The standing on the dominant follows the core's cadential close
## Enables
- **Recapitulation** — The standing on the dominant builds expectation for the recapitulation's arrival
## Related
- **Retransition** — A distinct passage that modulates back home, before the standing on the dominant

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Calling the standing on the dominant a "retransition"
  **Clarification**: The retransition (if present) occurs before the home-key dominant is reached; the standing on the dominant occurs after it, as a postcadential prolongation
