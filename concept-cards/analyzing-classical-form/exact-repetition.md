# Exact Repetition

**Exact repetition** is one of three types of repetition that can occur within a presentation phrase. In this type, the underlying harmony of the restatement is essentially the same as the original.

## Definition

The basic idea is repeated with the same harmonic support, allowing for minor variants. The melody of an exact repetition may be varied or even transposed to other scale degrees that are supportable by the original harmony.

## Harmonic Patterns

Typical harmonic plans in exact repetition:

| Basic Idea | Exact Repetition |
|------------|------------------|
| I | I |
| I - V | I - V |
| I - V - I | I - V - I |

## Key Principle

The type of repetition is determined by the **harmonic context**, not the melody alone. Even when the melody changes, if the harmony remains essentially the same, it is still exact repetition.

## Examples

- Melody identical with same harmonic support
- Melody transposed up/down a third but same underlying harmony (I-V, I-V)
- Melodic ornamentation added but harmonic foundation unchanged

## Distinction from Other Types

- **Statement-response**: Changes from tonic emphasis to dominant emphasis
- **Sequential**: Complete transposition of both melody and harmony

Exact repetition maintains harmonic stability and strongly reinforces the tonic prolongation of the presentation phrase.
