# Quick Definition
One of three types of b.i. repetition in the presentation phrase: the underlying harmony of the restatement is essentially the same as the original. The melody may be varied or even transposed, as long as the harmonic context remains the same.

# Core Definition
In this type, the underlying harmony of the restatement is essentially the same as the original (allowing for minor variants). Note that the melody of such an exact repetition may be varied, even transposed to other scale degrees that are supportable by the original harmony of the basic idea (p. 63). The distinction among repetition types is essentially based on the harmonic context of the restated units, not on their melodic content (p. 63).

# Prerequisites
- **Basic idea** — The unit being repeated
- **Presentation phrase** — Context of the repetition

# Key Properties
1. Harmonic support remains essentially the same
2. Melody may be varied, ornamented, or transposed within the same harmony
3. Distinction is based on HARMONIC context, not melody
4. Typical harmonic plans: I-I; I-V, I-V; I-V-I, I-V-I
5. Maintains harmonic stability; reinforces tonic prolongation

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Compare the harmonic support of mm. 1-2 with mm. 3-4
2. If essentially the same harmonies: exact repetition
3. Do NOT rely on melodic comparison alone

# Context & Application
Exact repetition is common in presentation phrases, reinforcing tonic prolongation and providing maximum stability. It strongly establishes the b.i. as the fundamental material.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 63, Ex. 2.6): Mozart, K. 330, i -- identical melody and harmony.

**Example 2** (p. 62, Ex. 2.5): Haydn, H. 41, ii -- melody a third higher but same harmony (exact repetition).

**Example 3** (p. 64, Ex. 2.7): Beethoven, Op. 119, No. 1 -- melody transposed down a third but same I-V harmony.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Classifying a repetition as non-exact because the melody changes
  **Correction**: Repetition type is determined by harmonic context, not melody

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing exact repetition with statement-response when melody is transposed by a step
  **Clarification**: Check the harmony: if it shifts from I to V emphasis, it is statement-response, not exact
