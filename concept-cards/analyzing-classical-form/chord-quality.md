# Quick Definition
The intervallic structure of a chord (major, minor, diminished, augmented, etc.), which in Caplin's notation is not distinguished by Roman numeral case but may be indicated separately for nonfunctional chords.

# Formal Definition
Chord quality refers to the type of triad or seventh chord based on its intervallic structure (major triad, minor triad, diminished triad, major-minor seventh, etc.). Many textbooks distinguish chord quality using different forms of Roman numerals (uppercase for major, lowercase for minor, etc.), but Caplin follows texts that use uppercase Roman numerals exclusively and do not distinguish chord quality in the numeral itself.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Chord qualities in classical harmony:
- **Major triad**: Major third + minor third
- **Minor triad**: Minor third + major third
- **Diminished triad**: Minor third + minor third
- **Augmented triad**: Major third + major third
- **Major-minor seventh** (dominant seventh): Major triad + minor seventh
- **Diminished seventh**: Diminished triad + diminished seventh
- **Half-diminished seventh**: Diminished triad + minor seventh

Notation:
- Uppercase Roman numerals used regardless of quality
- Nonfunctional chords: quality symbols in parentheses (m7, °7)
- Augmented sixths: national labels (It+6, Gr+6, Fr+6)

# Musical Context/Application
Chord quality:
- Is contextually understood from the key and scale degree
- Does not require notation in uppercase-only systems
- May be indicated explicitly for nonfunctional chords
- Affects voice leading but not necessarily Roman numeral labeling

# Examples
The chapter notes: "Many textbooks distinguish chord 'quality' by using different forms of the Roman numerals: uppercase for 'major,' lowercase for 'minor,' lowercase with a superscript o for 'diminished.' Here, we will follow those texts (such as the popular one by Aldwell, Schachter, and Cadwallader) that do not distinguish chord quality and instead use uppercase Roman numerals exclusively" (pp. 23-24).

For nonfunctional chords: "(m7) for 'minor seventh chord'" and "(°7) for 'diminished seventh chord'" (p. 47).

# Common Confusions
- Expecting case to indicate quality in Caplin's system (it does not)
- Using chord quality symbols for functional harmonies (use Roman numerals)
- Forgetting that quality is contextually clear from key and scale degree
- Not recognizing augmented-sixth notation as quality-indicating
