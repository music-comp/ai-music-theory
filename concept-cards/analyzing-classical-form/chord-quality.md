# Quick Definition
The intervallic structure of a chord (major, minor, diminished, augmented), which in Caplin's notation is not distinguished by Roman numeral case but may be indicated separately for nonfunctional chords.

# Core Definition
Chord quality refers to the type of triad or seventh chord based on its intervallic content (major, minor, diminished, augmented, etc.). Caplin follows texts such as Aldwell, Schachter, and Cadwallader that "do not distinguish chord quality and instead use uppercase Roman numerals exclusively" (p. 24). For nonfunctional chords lacking Roman numerals, quality symbols in parentheses are used: "(m7) for 'minor seventh chord'" and "(°7) for 'diminished seventh chord'" (p. 47).

# Prerequisites
- **Scale degree** — Chord roots are identified by scale degree; quality is contextually understood from the key

# Key Properties
1. Major, minor, diminished, and augmented triads each have distinct intervallic structures
2. Seventh chords add further quality distinctions (major-minor, fully diminished, half-diminished)
3. In Caplin's system, uppercase Roman numerals are used regardless of quality
4. Quality is contextually clear from the key and scale degree in most cases
5. Nonfunctional chords receive quality symbols in parentheses instead of Roman numerals
6. Augmented-sixth chords use national labels (It+6, Gr+6, Fr+6)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Determine the intervallic content of the chord (stack of thirds)
2. In Caplin's system, identify the chord's Roman numeral using uppercase only
3. If the chord is nonfunctional or contrapuntal, use a quality symbol in parentheses
4. For augmented-sixth chords, use the appropriate national label

# Context & Application
Chord quality is contextually understood from the key signature and scale degree in functional harmonic analysis. The decision to use uppercase Roman numerals exclusively simplifies notation and places emphasis on harmonic function rather than quality. Quality symbols become necessary only when a chord lacks genuine harmonic function and would be misrepresented by a Roman numeral.

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 23-24): "Many textbooks distinguish chord 'quality' by using different forms of the Roman numerals: uppercase for 'major,' lowercase for 'minor,' lowercase with a superscript o for 'diminished.' Here, we will follow those texts ... that do not distinguish chord quality and instead use uppercase Roman numerals exclusively."

**Example 2** (p. 47): For nonfunctional chords: "(m7) for 'minor seventh chord'" and "(°7) for 'diminished seventh chord'" are used in place of Roman numerals.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Using lowercase Roman numerals for minor chords in Caplin's system
  **Correction**: Caplin uses uppercase exclusively; quality is understood from context

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Expecting case to indicate quality in Caplin's system
  **Clarification**: Uppercase is used uniformly; chord quality is contextually determined from key and scale degree
