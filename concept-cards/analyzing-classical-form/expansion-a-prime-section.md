# Quick Definition
The A' section of a minuet tends to expand for tonal balance (giving the home key more weight) and to suggest a "recapitulation of subordinate theme" through looser techniques such as deceptive cadences, interpolated episodes, and new development.

# Core Definition
The tendency to expand the recapitulation can be explained on tonal balance grounds (the home key needs enough space to overcome the subordinate-key confirmation) and as providing a kind of "recapitulation of the subordinate theme" -- not literally recapitulating the B section's material, but employing looser phrase-structural techniques that suggest it (Caplin, pp. 647-648).

# Key Properties
1. Tonal balance: expanded recapitulation restores home-key predominance
2. Pseudo-recapitulation of subordinate theme through looser techniques
3. Deceptive cadences: favorite extension device (more common in minuet than sonata)
4. Interpolated episodes with new material in contrasting key
5. Development of exposition motives with sequential organization
6. Thematic functions that were fused in A may become separate in A'

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Compare A' length and complexity to A section
2. Look for deceptive cadences as extension points
3. Note interpolated episodes and developmental passages
4. Check whether fused functions become distinct

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 648): Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, iii -- shift to minor, deceptive resolution to bVI, interpolated episode confirming bVI as development key, retransition, new presentation; transition and subordinate-theme functions given distinct units.
