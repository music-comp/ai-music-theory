## Tendency

The recapitulation of a minuet tends to be expanded significantly compared to the exposition, unlike small ternary where compression is more common.

## Explanations for Expansion

### 1. Tonal Balance Argument

If A section is shorter than B section:
- Bringing equally short A' section might not give home key enough space
- Home key needs to "win the battle of conflicting tonalities"
- Expanded recapitulation with cadential reinforcements restores tonal stability
- New closing section (and coda) helps home-key predominance

### 2. "Recapitulation of Subordinate Theme" Argument

If nonmodulating A section = main theme, and B section brings subordinate theme:
- Simply repeating main theme in A' leaves subordinate theme unrecapitulated
- Expansion doesn't literally recapitulate earlier subordinate theme
- But looser phrase-structural techniques can **suggest** such a recapitulation

## Typical Expansion Techniques

1. **Loosening devices**: Associated with subordinate-theme function
2. **Interpolated episode**: New material in contrasting key
3. **Deceptive cadences**: Favorite device for extending form
   - More common in minuet than evaded cadences (which dominate in sonata)
4. **New development**: Of exposition motives with hint of sequential organization
5. **Thematic separation**: Functions that were fused in A section become distinct units

## Example: Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, iii

In the recapitulation:
- Sudden shift to minor at m. 51
- Deceptive resolution at m. 54 brings bVI
- Development into tonicized region
- Interpolated episode with IAC confirming bVI as development key
- Retransitional passage back to home key
- Result: Transition and subordinate-theme now given distinct thematic units
