# Quick Definition
A false closing section occurs when codettas following a main theme's PAC are retrospectively understood to function as the beginning (presentation) of the transition, not as a postcadential closing section. The same technique also operates in subordinate-theme groups.

# Core Definition
A main theme ending with a PAC may be followed by codettas (tonic pedal, descending melody from tonic scale degree) that initially suggest a closing section. When this material develops beyond typical codetta scope -- acquiring continuation function, modulating, or leading to an HC -- the codettas are retrospectively understood as a **presentation phrase** for the transition. The closing section thus proves "false." This same technique can begin a subordinate theme within a subordinate-theme group (Caplin, Ch. 11, pp. 342-343; Ch. 12, pp. 411-412).

# Key Properties
1. Material initially appears postcadential (codetta-like)
2. Tonic pedal, descending melody, recessive character
3. Material is repeated, creating what retrospectively becomes a presentation
4. Subsequent continuation function reveals the transition has begun
5. The symbol => indicates retrospective reinterpretation
6. Also used to begin subordinate themes (Ch. 12)
