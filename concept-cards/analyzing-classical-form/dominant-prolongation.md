# Quick Definition
The extension of dominant harmony through subordinate chords, following the same principles as tonic prolongation but with the dominant as the prolonged harmony.

# Formal Definition
Dominant prolongation applies the same techniques used for tonic prolongation - pedal point, neighboring chords, passing chords, and substitute harmonies - to extend dominant harmony over time. The chapter notes that "most of the progressions discussed and illustrated below prolong tonic harmony; however, many of them can prolong harmonies on other scale degrees as well."

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Techniques for dominant prolongation:
- **Dominant pedal**: Scale degree 5 sustained in bass throughout
- **Neighboring chords**: Chords embellishing V that return to V
- **Passing chords**: Chords connecting different positions of V
- **Substitute harmonies**: VII functioning similarly to V

Dominant prolongation often appears:
- At the end of development sections (standing on the dominant)
- In retransitions preparing main theme returns
- After half cadences extending the ultimate dominant

# Musical Context/Application
Dominant prolongation:
- Creates expectation for tonic resolution
- Builds tension before structural returns
- May extend across substantial musical passages
- Often features dominant pedal point

While tonic prolongation establishes stability, dominant prolongation creates and maintains tension awaiting resolution.

# Common Confusions
- Thinking prolongation only applies to tonic
- Confusing dominant prolongation with half-cadential progression
- Not recognizing subordinate harmonies within dominant prolongations
- Missing the formal significance of extended dominant passages
