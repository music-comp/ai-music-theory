# Quick Definition
The extension of dominant harmony through subordinate chords, following the same prolongational principles as tonic prolongation but with the dominant as the prolonged harmony.

# Core Definition
Dominant prolongation applies the same techniques used for tonic prolongation -- pedal point, neighboring chords, passing chords, and substitute harmonies -- to extend dominant harmony over time. Caplin notes that "most of the progressions discussed and illustrated below prolong tonic harmony; however, many of them can prolong harmonies on other scale degrees as well" (p. 33). Dominant prolongation creates and maintains tension awaiting resolution.

# Prerequisites
- **Prolongational progression** — Dominant prolongation is a specific application of prolongational technique
- **Dominant function** — Understanding what constitutes dominant harmony

# Key Properties
1. Uses the same techniques as tonic prolongation (pedal, neighbor, passing, substitute)
2. Dominant pedal (scale degree 5 sustained in bass) is a primary technique
3. Creates expectation for tonic resolution
4. Builds tension before structural returns
5. May extend across substantial musical passages

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate a passage where dominant harmony persists over multiple beats or measures
2. Check for dominant pedal point in the bass
3. Identify subordinate harmonies (neighboring, passing) embellishing the dominant
4. Verify that the passage maintains dominant function throughout despite surface activity

# Context & Application
Dominant prolongation often appears at the end of development sections ("standing on the dominant"), in retransitions preparing main theme returns, and after half cadences extending the ultimate dominant. While tonic prolongation establishes stability, dominant prolongation creates and sustains tension.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking prolongation only applies to tonic
  **Correction**: Any harmony can be prolonged, with dominant prolongation being especially common in formal contexts

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing dominant prolongation with a half-cadential progression
  **Clarification**: A half-cadential progression arrives at the dominant; dominant prolongation extends it after arrival
