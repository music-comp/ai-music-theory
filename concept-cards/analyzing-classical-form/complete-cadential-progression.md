# Quick Definition
A cadential progression containing all of its component harmonic functions: initial tonic, pre-dominant, dominant, and (for authentic cadences) final tonic.

# Core Definition
A cadential progression is complete if it contains all of its component harmonic functions. For an authentic cadential progression: I6-II6-V7-I (initial tonic, pre-dominant, dominant, final tonic). For a half-cadential progression: I6-II6-V (initial tonic, pre-dominant, dominant). "Whether a composer employs a complete or incomplete cadential progression normally depends on circumstances unique to the given context" -- there is nothing inadequate about incomplete progressions (Caplin, p. 37).

# Prerequisites
- **Initial tonic** — The tonic harmony that begins the cadential progression
- **Final tonic** — The root-position tonic that concludes an authentic cadential progression
- **Penultimate dominant** — The dominant that precedes the final tonic

# Key Properties
1. All harmonic functions are present: initial tonic, pre-dominant, dominant, (final tonic)
2. Complete authentic: I6-II6-V7-I
3. Complete half: I6-II6-V or I6-IV-V
4. Completeness describes harmonic content, not structural strength
5. Neither superior nor inferior to incomplete progressions

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate the cadential progression boundaries
2. Check for initial tonic (usually I6)
3. Check for pre-dominant (II6 or IV)
4. Check for dominant (V or V7)
5. For authentic: check for final tonic (root-position I)
6. If all functions are present, the progression is complete

# Context & Application
The choice between complete and incomplete cadential progressions is a matter of compositional design, not hierarchical quality. Complete progressions present the full functional sequence, but incomplete progressions are equally viable and carry no deficiency requiring later "correction."

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming complete progressions are "stronger" or preferable to incomplete ones
  **Correction**: Both are equally viable; the choice depends on compositional context

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking incomplete progressions are "defective" and require a following complete progression
  **Clarification**: There is nothing inadequate about incomplete cadential progressions; they do not need resolution
