## Definition

The final cadence of a minuet exposition may be followed by a closing section. This postcadential passage typically consists of codettas.

## Content Options

### 1. Entirely New Material
- Most common scenario
- Closing section brings fresh melodic-motivic content
- Clearly distinguishes cadential from postcadential function

### 2. Prior Cadential Ideas
- Sometimes closing section consists of previous cadential material
- Obscures distinction between cadential and postcadential functions
- Especially common in **scherzos**

## Why Scherzos Reuse Cadential Material

In a scherzo:
- Sudden change of material could disrupt the intensity of prevailing rhythmic drive
- Maintaining rhythmic continuity takes priority
- The boundary between cadential and postcadential functions becomes blurred

## Example Issue

When the same idea appears both at the cadence and after:
- If understood as the goal of the phrase, it functions as cadential
- If repeated and extended, it functions as codetta
- Interpretation may depend on rhythmic correspondence and harmonic analysis

## Where Closing Sections Appear

1. **After A section**: Following subordinate-key PAC (if modulating)
2. **After A' section**: Following home-key PAC in recapitulation
3. **New closing section**: May appear only in recapitulation, not in exposition
