# Quick Definition
The final cadence of a minuet exposition may be followed by a closing section. In scherzos, the closing section often consists of prior cadential ideas, blurring the distinction between cadential and postcadential functions to maintain rhythmic drive.

# Core Definition
The final cadence of a minuet exposition is sometimes followed by a closing section. In most cases, its content is entirely new. Sometimes, however, the closing section consists of prior cadential ideas, obscuring the distinction between cadential and postcadential functions. This happens especially in a scherzo, where a sudden change of material could disrupt the intensity of the prevailing rhythmic drive (Caplin, p. 637).

# Key Properties
1. Follows the final cadence of the A section
2. Usually consists of entirely new material
3. In scherzos: may reuse prior cadential ideas
4. Scherzo reuse blurs cadential/postcadential boundary
5. May also appear in the A' section (recapitulation)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for material following the A section's final PAC
2. Determine whether the material is new or reuses cadential ideas
3. In scherzos, note the blurred boundary due to rhythmic continuity

# Context & Application
The closing section in minuet form functions similarly to closing sections elsewhere, but the scherzo's need for rhythmic continuity creates a distinctive analytical challenge.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 637): In scherzos, a sudden change of material for the closing section could disrupt rhythmic drive; prior cadential ideas are therefore reused.
