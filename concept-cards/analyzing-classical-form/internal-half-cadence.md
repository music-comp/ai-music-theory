## Internal Half Cadence

Sometimes a subordinate theme seems to end with a **half cadence or dominant arrival** rather than a PAC. Since a PAC is required, we call this an **internal half cadence**.

### Definition

An HC (or dominant arrival) that occurs within a subordinate theme:
- A certain sense of ending is present
- But a more conclusive PAC must eventually follow
- More subordinate-theme material will invariably be heard

### Why It Occurs

The appearance of an internal HC is often explained by how the transition ends:

**If transition is nonmodulating**: The absence of emphasized SK dominant at end of transition is rectified by internal HC within subordinate theme.

**If modulating transition lacks ending function**: SK dominant is also left unemphasized, motivating subsequent internal HC.

### Key Principle

Most sonata-form expositions feature a **partial confirmation** of subordinate key through HC closure at some point prior to full confirmation by PAC:
- Typically at end of modulating transition
- Failing that: within the subordinate theme as internal HC

### Two Strategies After Internal HC

1. **Resumption of continuation or cadential function** leading to final PAC
2. **Establishment of new basic idea** (presentation) to begin second part of a two-part subordinate theme
