# Quick Definition
An internal half cadence occurs when a subordinate theme reaches an HC (or dominant arrival) within its boundaries rather than the required PAC. Since a PAC must eventually follow, more subordinate-theme material will invariably be heard, and the HC is understood as "internal" to the ongoing subordinate-theme process.

# Core Definition
Sometimes a subordinate theme seems to end with a half cadence or dominant arrival rather than a PAC. Since a PAC in the subordinate key is required, this HC is labeled "internal": a certain sense of ending is present, but a more conclusive PAC must eventually follow, and more subordinate-theme material will invariably be heard. The appearance of an internal HC is often explained by how the transition ends: if the transition is nonmodulating (ending with a home-key HC), the absence of emphasized subordinate-key dominant is rectified by an internal HC within the subordinate theme. Similarly, if a modulating transition lacks ending function, the subordinate-key dominant is left unemphasized, motivating a subsequent internal HC. Most sonata-form expositions feature partial confirmation of the subordinate key through HC closure at some point -- typically at the end of the modulating transition, or failing that, within the subordinate theme itself (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 398-399).

# Key Properties
1. HC (or dominant arrival) occurs within subordinate theme, not at its end
2. PAC must still follow to fulfill subordinate-theme requirements
3. More subordinate-theme material invariably follows
4. Often motivated by transition lacking subordinate-key dominant emphasis
5. Provides partial confirmation of subordinate key
6. Usually followed by standing on the dominant

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Locate an HC within a subordinate-theme area
2. Confirm that a PAC in the subordinate key has not yet been achieved
3. Check what follows: does the music continue with subordinate-theme material?
4. Consider whether the transition's ending explains why the internal HC occurs
5. Look for a standing on the dominant following the internal HC

# Context & Application
The internal HC creates a two-part structure within the subordinate theme. Following the internal HC (and typically a standing on the dominant), two strategies are possible: (1) resumption of continuation or cadential function leading to the final PAC, or (2) establishment of a new basic idea (presentation) to begin the second part of a two-part subordinate theme. The internal HC is related to the concept of "to begin before having ended" -- the idea that a new formal process can begin without a prior process having achieved full closure.

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 398-399): Subordinate theme after nonmodulating transition -- absence of subordinate-key dominant emphasis at transition's end motivates an internal HC within the subordinate theme, providing the partial key confirmation that the transition did not supply.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating the internal HC as the end of the subordinate theme
  **Correction**: The internal HC is not the end; a PAC must still follow. The subordinate theme's requirements are not fulfilled by an HC.
