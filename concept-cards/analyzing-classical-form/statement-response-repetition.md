# Statement-Response Repetition

**Statement-response repetition** is a type of repetition in which the statement emphasizes tonic harmony while the response emphasizes dominant harmony.

## Terminology

- **Statement** = **Tonic version** of the basic idea
- **Response** = **Dominant version** of the basic idea

## Identification

The differing harmonic emphases are signaled primarily by the **initial** harmonies:
- Statement begins with I (tonic)
- Response begins with V (dominant)

## Harmonic Patterns

Typical harmonic plans:

| Statement | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| I | V |
| I - V | V - I |
| I - ii (or IV) | V - I |
| I | IV (subdominant substitute) |

## Melodic Adjustment

The melody of the response is frequently transposed stepwise to accommodate the change in harmonic support. A typical melodic alteration sees the melody rise by a step in the response.

## Subdominant Variant

A variant of this type sees the **subdominant (IV)** substituting for the dominant to create the response.

## Important Distinction

You cannot identify the type of repetition by looking at the melody alone. The **harmonic context** determines whether it is statement-response or sequential repetition. Even when the melody is transposed by a step:
- If harmony changes from I to V = statement-response
- If both melody and harmony are transposed by the same interval = sequential
