# Quick Definition
A type of b.i. repetition where the statement (tonic version) emphasizes I and the response (dominant version) emphasizes V. The differing harmonic emphases are signaled primarily by the initial harmonies: statement begins on I, response begins on V. Melody is often transposed stepwise to accommodate the harmonic shift.

# Core Definition
In this type, the statement emphasizes tonic harmony, whereas the response emphasizes dominant. The statement and response can also be termed a tonic version and a dominant version respectively. The differing harmonic emphases are signaled primarily by their initial harmonies: the statement begins with I, the response with V. The melody of the response is frequently transposed stepwise to accommodate the change in harmonic support. A variant sees IV substituting for V to create the response (pp. 64-66).

# Key Properties
1. Statement = tonic version (begins on I); response = dominant version (begins on V)
2. Harmonic shift from tonic emphasis to dominant emphasis
3. Melody often transposed stepwise to accommodate harmonic change
4. Variant: IV substituting for V in the response
5. Typical patterns: I/V; I-V/V-I; I-II(or IV)/V-I
6. Distinction from sequential: determined by harmonic context, not melody

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Check initial harmony of each version: statement starts on I, response on V
2. Verify the melody is adapted to the new harmonic context (not a complete transposition)
3. Distinguished from sequential: in statement-response, harmony changes from I to V; in sequential, both melody and harmony are transposed by the same interval

# Context & Application
Statement-response repetition creates a complementary pairing within the presentation, establishing a statement-response character. The tonic prolongation is typically completed when the dominant resolves to tonic at the beginning of the continuation phrase.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 56, Ex. 2.1): Beethoven, Op. 2, No. 1 -- tonic version (mm. 1-2), dominant version (mm. 3-4) with V6/5.

**Example 2** (p. 65, Ex. 2.8): Mozart, K. 402, i -- statement on I, response on V exclusively.

**Example 3** (p. 66, Ex. 2.11): Mozart, K. 309, iii -- IV substitutes for V in the response.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Identifying as sequential because the melody is transposed by a step
  **Correction**: Check the harmony: I-to-V = statement-response; complete transposition of both = sequential

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing statement-response with sequential when melody rises by step
  **Clarification**: Compare Exs. 2.10 and 2.3: both have stepwise melodic transposition, but 2.10 is statement-response (I to V) while 2.3 is sequential (complete transposition up a step) (p. 67)
