## Definition

One way of limiting the length of a slow-movement sonata is to reduce the scope of the development section. The composer forgoes a full-fledged core while potentially including brief model-sequence activity.

## Characteristics of Reduced Development

1. **No full core**: Core technique is abbreviated or absent
2. **Brief sequences**: May have some model-sequence activity
3. **No confirmed development key**: Development key is not cadentially confirmed
4. **Resembles contrasting middle**: May function more like a B section of small ternary

## Proportional Comparison

In fast-movement sonata forms by Haydn:
- Development tends to be roughly the same length as exposition and recapitulation

In slow-movement sonata forms:
- Development can be less than one-third as long as the exposition

## Example: Haydn, Piano Trio in A, H. 9, i

- Development is less than one-third as long as exposition
- Briefly touches on three tonal regions (vi, IV, ii)
- Uses descending-third sequential pattern
- Final dominant so weakly articulated it may not be perceived as an "ending" harmony

## Functional Implication

When development is reduced to this extent:
- The section loses its typical dramatic weight
- Resembles the contrasting middle of small ternary more than true sonata development
- The movement as a whole becomes more compact while retaining sonata organization
