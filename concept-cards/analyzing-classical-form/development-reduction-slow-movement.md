# Quick Definition
One compression technique for slow-movement sonatas involves reducing the development section's scope: the composer forgoes a full-fledged core, avoids cadentially confirming a development key, and may produce a section so simple it resembles a contrasting middle of a small ternary.

# Core Definition
One way of limiting the length of a slow-movement sonata is to reduce the scope of the development. The composer forgoes a full-fledged core (although there may be some brief model-sequence activity) and normally does not allow a development key to be cadentially confirmed. At times, the development may be so simple as to resemble a contrasting middle of the small ternary form (Caplin, pp. 615-616).

# Prerequisites

# Key Properties
1. No full-fledged core
2. Brief model-sequence activity may be present
3. Development key not cadentially confirmed
4. May resemble a contrasting middle of small ternary
5. Can be less than one-third the length of the exposition
6. The dominant at the end may be so weakly articulated it is barely perceived as an ending

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Compare the development's length to the exposition's length
2. Check for the absence of genuine core technique
3. Note whether any development key receives cadential confirmation
4. Assess whether the section resembles a simple contrasting middle more than a true development

# Context & Application
This is the third compression technique for slow-movement sonatas. The reduction can be so extreme that the development barely fulfills its normal dramatic function, yet the overall form remains recognizably sonata.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 616): Haydn, Piano Trio in A, H. 9, i -- development less than one-third the exposition's length; briefly touches three tonal regions (vi, IV, ii) via descending-third sequential pattern; final dominant so weakly articulated it may not be perceived as an ending.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming a very short development means the form is not really a sonata
  **Correction**: A reduced development still qualifies as sonata form; if it were eliminated entirely, the form would be sonata without development
