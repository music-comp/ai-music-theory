# Quick Definition
Traditional theories refer to a "closing theme" after the second subject, but this concept is imprecisely defined. In Caplin's categories, what is traditionally called a closing "theme" is usually either a true subordinate theme (the last of a group) or a collection of codettas (a closing section). Since no distinct "closing" thematic function can be identified apart from subordinate-theme function, Caplin does not adopt the notion of a closing "theme."

# Core Definition
Traditional theories refer to a "closing theme" following the "second subject," but the concept is usually not defined with precision. Hepokoski and Darcy also develop a notion of closing theme (defined as occurring after their "essential expositional closure"), which is more rigorously formulated. In Caplin's form-functional categories, what is traditionally called a closing **theme** can most often be identified as either: (1) a true subordinate theme (usually the last of a group), or (2) a collection of codettas (a closing **section**). Since it is not normally possible to identify a specific thematic function that is "closing" as opposed to "subordinate theme," the notion of closing "theme" is not adopted (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 410-411).
