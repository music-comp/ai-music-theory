## Closing Section vs. "Closing Theme"

Traditional theories of sonata form refer to a "closing theme" directly following the "second subject." This concept is usually not defined with precision.

### Traditional Usage

The term "closing theme" tends to be applied indiscriminately to various formal contexts:
- Sometimes a subordinate theme (usually the last of a group)
- Sometimes a collection of codettas

### Hepokoski-Darcy Definition

Their theory also develops a notion of closing theme:
- More rigorously formulated than most accounts
- Defined as occurring after their "essential expositional closure" (first SK: PAC)

### Caplin's Position

Using form-functional categories established in this study:
- What is traditionally called a closing **theme** can most often be identified as either:
  1. A true subordinate theme (usually the last of a group), or
  2. A collection of codettas (a closing **section**)

### Why No "Closing Theme" Category

It is not normally possible to identify a specific thematic function that can be considered "closing" as opposed to "subordinate theme." For this reason, the notion of closing **theme** is not adopted in this study.
