## Intrinsic vs. Contextual Functionality

The notion of a theme "beginning" with a medial or concluding function poses a theoretical conundrum that requires distinguishing between intrinsic and contextual functionality.

### The Theoretical Problem

How can a passage be a "continuation" if not preceded by material it "continues"? How can an opening phrase be "cadential" if there's nothing to close?

### The Answer: Intrinsic Functionality

Since formal functions essentially involve how music expresses its logical location in a temporal spectrum (beginnings, middles, ends), music can express its **intrinsic** temporal function independent of its **contextual** location.

### Why This Works

Formal functions are so **conventionalized** and **well defined** by their specific characteristics that we can sometimes identify a function without taking into account its actual position in a theme.

### Formal Dissonance

When contextual placement differs from intrinsic temporal expression:
- A kind of formal "dissonance" results
- If carefully controlled: expresses loose organization
- If too great: produces illogical succession of formal functions

### Example

A subordinate theme beginning with model-sequence technique:
- **Intrinsic function**: continuation (sequences are inherently continuational)
- **Contextual position**: beginning of theme
- **Result**: "formal dissonance" projecting loose organization
