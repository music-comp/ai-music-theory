# Quick Definition
Five regularly observed compensatory functions allow the coda to address compositional matters that could not be appropriately treated in earlier sections: recollection of main-theme ideas, restoration of deleted material, reference to development, new dynamic curve, and realization of unrealized implications.

# Core Definition
"The coda does not normally involve these primary processes — and thus appears after-the-end — those other, secondary processes often attain closure only in the coda, thus finally allowing the movement to conclude" (p. 560). The recapitulation is "highly constrained" in how it handles earlier material (same order, no main-theme recall late, no development reference), so the coda offers opportunity to compensate. Five compensatory functions are regularly observed (p. 521): (1) recollection of main-theme ideas for circular design, (2) restoration of material deleted from recapitulation, (3) reference to development section ideas, (4) shaping a new dynamic curve, (5) realization of unrealized implications.

# Prerequisites
- **Coda section** — The section serving these functions
- **Recapitulation section** — The constrained section the coda compensates for

# Key Properties
1. **Recollection of main-theme ideas** — Circular design; most codas refer to main-theme ideas
2. **Restoration of deleted material** — Recapitulating material deleted from the recapitulation
3. **Reference to development** — Especially at coda's start (analogous position); nonmodulatory
4. **New dynamic curve** — Different trajectory than recapitulation/exposition endings
5. **Realization of unrealized implications** — Resolving "problems" or "disturbances" from earlier

# Examples
**Example 15.1** (p. 544): Beethoven, Op. 18, No. 1 — coda restores main-theme ideas (mm. 294-99) deleted from recapitulation.

**Example 15.4** (p. 550): Mozart, Symphony "Linz," K. 425 — coda references core material from the development, now in home key.

**Example 15.10** (p. 565): Mozart, Symphony No. 39, K. 543, ii — coda realizes implication of minor-mode disturbance in main theme; eliminates modal shift that occurred in exposition.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Seeing the coda's compensatory functions as contradicting its after-the-end status
  **Clarification**: Primary processes (tonal, formal) are completed in the recapitulation (after-the-end); secondary processes attain closure in the coda (conclusion)
