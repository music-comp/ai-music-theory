# Quick Definition
The initial tonic of an expanded cadential progression is usually placed in first inversion (I6). When expanded, it is frequently embellished by a neighboring V4/3, whose voice-leading demands resolution back to I6. The prominent arrival on I6 serves as a cue for the onset of cadential function.

# Core Definition
The initial tonic of an expanded cadential progression is usually placed in first inversion (I6), and classical composers often use the prominent arrival on I6 as a cue for the onset of an ECP. When the initial tonic is expanded, it is frequently embellished by a neighboring dominant seventh in third inversion (V4/3), which works especially well because the voice-leading rule demands its resolution back to I6, creating elegant neighbor-note motion. When a subordinate theme is supported by a single ECP, the entire theme may begin with an expanded I6, and the functional label "cadential" can be assigned to the whole theme. Nonetheless, the expanded I6 possesses a degree of harmonic stability, and the opening measures project a sense of structural beginning despite the overall cadential function (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 397-398).

1. Initial tonic placed in first inversion (I6)
2. Prominent arrival on I6 cues ECP onset

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for a prominent arrival on I6 (first-inversion tonic)
2. Check for neighboring V4/3 embellishment
3. Note whether the I6 arrival signals the start of cadential function
4. If the entire theme is supported by one ECP, the expanded I6 constitutes the opening

# Context & Application
Expansion of the initial tonic is one of three principal ways to expand an ECP (alongside expansion of the dominant and the pre-dominant). When a subordinate theme begins with an expanded I6, a theoretical tension arises: the passage is functionally cadential but the stable I6 also projects a sense of initiation. This formal "dissonance" is appropriate for the looser organization of subordinate themes.
