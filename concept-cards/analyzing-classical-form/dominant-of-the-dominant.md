# Quick Definition
A group of pre-dominant harmonies functioning as secondary dominants of V, featuring the chromatic raised-fourth scale degree as the leading tone of the dominant.

# Core Definition
"One group of pre-dominants relates to harmonies that function as a secondary (or applied) dominant of the dominant. These harmonies feature the chromatic raised-fourth scale degree, which functions as the leading tone of the dominant. The significance of the raised-fourth degree is highlighted by its normally being placed in the bass voice, so that the motion to the root of the following dominant is all the more enhanced" (pp. 32-33). Despite containing dominant-quality sonority, these chords function as pre-dominants.

# Prerequisites
- **Raised fourth scale degree** — The defining chromatic element of this pre-dominant category
- **Cadential progression** — V/V-type harmonies appear within cadential progressions as pre-dominants

# Key Properties
1. All contain raised scale degree 4 (leading tone of V)
2. Raised 4 is typically placed in bass to enhance motion to V
3. VII7/V is the most typical pre-dominant of this type (diminished seventh)
4. V6/V and V/V are "less dissonant" alternatives
5. Augmented-sixth chords (It+6, Gr+6, Fr+6) are "an important subclass"
6. Function as pre-dominant, NOT as dominant

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for raised scale degree 4 (chromatic sharp on the fourth degree)
2. Check whether it appears in the bass voice (most common placement)
3. Verify that the chord resolves to V (pre-dominant function)
4. Identify the specific form: VII7/V, V/V, V6/V, or augmented sixth

# Context & Application
The dominant of the dominant serves as a pre-dominant, creating a powerful chromatic approach to V. This pre-dominant category is distinct from those built over scale degree 4 in the bass (II6, IV, bII6). The raised 4 in bass creates half-step motion to the dominant root, intensifying the cadential drive. Two consecutive diminished-seventh chords can prolong pre-dominant function in this category.

## Contrasts With
- **Subdominant harmony** — IV-type pre-dominants have scale degree 4 (natural) in bass
- **Supertonic harmony** — II6 also has scale degree 4 in bass but without chromatic alteration

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating V/V as a true dominant rather than as pre-dominant function
  **Correction**: Despite containing dominant-quality sonority, V/V functions as pre-dominant within the cadential progression

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Not recognizing the connection between augmented sixths and V/V
  **Clarification**: Augmented-sixth chords are "an important subclass" of the V/V pre-dominant category, all sharing the raised fourth scale degree
