# Quick Definition
A group of pre-dominant harmonies that function as secondary dominants of V, featuring the chromatic raised-fourth scale degree as the leading tone of the dominant.

# Formal Definition
One group of pre-dominants relates to harmonies that function as a secondary (or applied) dominant of the dominant. These harmonies feature the chromatic raised-fourth scale degree, which functions as the leading tone of the dominant. The significance of the raised-fourth degree is highlighted by its normally being placed in the bass voice, so that the motion to the root of the following dominant is all the more enhanced.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Harmonies in this category:
- **VII7/V**: The most typical pre-dominant of this type (diminished-seventh chord)
- **V6/V**: Less dissonant form (first-inversion V of V)
- **V/V**: Root position dominant of the dominant
- **Augmented-sixth chords**: Italian, German, French (special subclass)

Characteristics:
- All contain raised scale degree 4 (leading tone of V)
- Raised 4 typically placed in bass to enhance motion to V
- Function as pre-dominants within cadential progressions
- Create chromatic intensification before the cadential dominant

# Musical Context/Application
The dominant of the dominant:
- Serves as pre-dominant function (not dominant function)
- Creates powerful chromatic approach to V
- The raised 4 in bass creates half-step motion to dominant root
- May appear in expanded cadential progressions

This pre-dominant category is distinct from those built over scale degree 4 in the bass (II6, IV, bII6).

# Common Confusions
- Treating V/V as a true dominant rather than as pre-dominant function
- Not recognizing the raised-fourth degree as the defining characteristic
- Confusing with other secondary dominants (V/V has special pre-dominant status)
- Missing the connection between augmented sixths and V/V
