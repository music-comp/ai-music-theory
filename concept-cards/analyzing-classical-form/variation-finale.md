## Definition

The final variation (the "finale") of a theme and variations movement is frequently set in a faster tempo than that of the theme, providing a climactic conclusion to the movement.

## Typical Characteristics

1. **Faster tempo**: Creates sense of culmination and energy
2. **Usually preceded by**: A distinctly slower variation (often marked Adagio)
3. **May be followed by**: A brief closing section or full-fledged coda

## Tempo Progression Pattern

A common progression in classical variations:

| Variation Type | Tempo |
|----------------|-------|
| Theme | Original tempo |
| Early variations | Same or slightly varied |
| Adagio variation | Distinctly slower |
| Finale | Faster than theme |

## Formal Treatment

The finale may feature:
- Greater rhythmic activity
- More virtuosic figuration
- Structural expansions not found in earlier variations
- An appended closing section or coda

## Example Context

In Mozart's Piano Sonata in A, K. 331, i:
- Variation 6 serves as the finale
- Quick-paced character of many finale movements
- Creates culminating energy after the preceding slower variation

## Movement-Type References

Individual variations can sometimes reference standard movement types of an instrumental cycle:
- Minuet/trio character (pastoral quality)
- Slow-movement style (opera seria)
- Quick-paced finale character
