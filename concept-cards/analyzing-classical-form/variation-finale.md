# Quick Definition
The final variation (the "finale") of a theme and variations movement is frequently set in a faster tempo than the theme, providing a climactic conclusion that may be followed by a closing section or coda.

# Core Definition
The last variation -- the finale -- is frequently set in a faster tempo than that of the theme. It may have appended to it a brief closing section or even be followed by a full-fledged coda. A given variation, especially one that changes tempo and meter, can sometimes reference one of the standard movement types of an instrumental cycle (Caplin, p. 592).

# Key Properties
1. Frequently set in faster tempo than the theme
2. Often preceded by a distinctly slower Adagio variation
3. May reference a standard movement type (e.g., finale character)
4. May be followed by closing section or coda
5. Creates a sense of culmination for the movement

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for a tempo increase in the final variation
2. Check for greater rhythmic activity and virtuosic figuration
3. Note whether a closing section or coda follows

# Context & Application
The finale variation creates the final climactic gesture of the movement, often preceded by the contrast of the Adagio variation, creating a dramatic slow-fast trajectory.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 592): In Mozart's Piano Sonata in A, K. 331, i, variation 6 has the quick-paced character of many finale movements.
