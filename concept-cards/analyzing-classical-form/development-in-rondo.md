# Quick Definition
The second couplet of sonata-rondo is often a development section with transition-like pre-core and one or more cores. It connects to refrain 2 in various ways: the refrain may be complete (followed by pre-core), or incomplete (merging with transitional material). False recapitulations are more common in rondos than in sonatas.

# Core Definition
The second couplet of sonata-rondo form is often built as a development section containing a transition-like pre-core followed by one or more cores (or core substitutes). The pre-core is usually based on prior ideas, often bringing back the original transition from couplet 1 or having the refrain start over and shift to minor. A number of Beethoven's sonata-rondo developments contain false recapitulations near the end (Caplin, pp. 678-682).

# Prerequisites
- **Sonata-rondo** -- The form containing this development
- **Development** -- The section type being used as couplet 2

# Key Properties
1. Transition-like pre-core followed by core(s)
2. Three connections to refrain 2: complete refrain + pre-core, antecedent-only refrain becoming pre-core, refrain merging with transitional material
3. Pre-core may use original transition, refrain material shifted to minor, or new material
4. Shift to minor typically signals that exposition is not being repeated
5. False recapitulations more common in rondo than sonata
6. The five-part rondo second couplet may also have development-like aspects

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 679): Beethoven, Violin Sonata in D, Op. 12, No. 1, iii -- refrain 2 appears intact, then begins to repeat but shifts to minor, revealing sonata-rondo; mm. 60-76 function as transition-like pre-core.
