## Definition

The second couplet of sonata-rondo form is often built as a development section in the sense of a regular sonata. It normally contains a transition-like pre-core followed by one or more cores (or core substitutes).

## Structure

| Component | Function |
|-----------|----------|
| Pre-core | Transition-like unit beginning in home key |
| Core(s) | Model-sequence technique, tonal exploration |
| Retransition | Standing on dominant preparing refrain return |

## Connection to Refrain 2

### If Refrain is Complete
- Development begins with unit functioning as pre-core
- Organization resembles a transition
- Opens in home key, modulates to new region for core

### If Refrain is Incomplete (Antecedent Only)
- Presumed consequent begins but departs from antecedent's course
- Becomes transition-like pre-core

### If Refrain Merges with Development
- Refrain fails to achieve cadential closure
- Merges with transitional material
- Difficult to discern clear dividing line

## Pre-core Material Options

1. **Original transition**: From couplet 1, leading to new tonal region
2. **Refrain material**: Starts over but shifts to minor, signaling new couplet
3. **New material**: Entirely different content

## False Recapitulation

- More common in rondo movements than in sonatas
- Main-theme material reappears in "wrong" key
- Particularly effective because rondo emphasizes the return of refrain
- Initial appearance in wrong key, corrected later in right key
