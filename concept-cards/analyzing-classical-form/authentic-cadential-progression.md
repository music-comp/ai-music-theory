# Quick Definition
A cadential progression ending with a root-position tonic, achieving the strongest tonal confirmation.

# Formal Definition
In an authentic cadential progression, both the dominant and final tonic harmonies must be placed in root position. The complete form brings, in order, the harmonic functions of tonic (usually in first inversion), pre-dominant, dominant (in root position), and tonic (in root position). If the final tonic is inverted or otherwise altered harmonically, a deceptive cadential progression arises instead.

# Musical/Theoretical Formulation
Complete authentic cadential progression: I6 - II6 - V(7) - I

The three harmonies preceding the final tonic can be varied and embellished:

**Initial tonic embellishments:**
- Usually in first inversion (I6), sometimes root position
- May be embellished by neighboring V4/3
- May be chromatically altered to become V/IV or V/II

**Pre-dominant embellishments:**
- II6 (most typical) or IV
- bII6 (Neapolitan), especially in minor
- VII7/V or augmented-sixth chords over raised 4
- May be built on other scale degrees (II, VI)

**Dominant embellishments:**
- Addition of seventh (V7)
- Cadential six-four: V(6-4 to 5-3)

# Musical Context/Application
The authentic cadential progression:
- Creates the strongest tonal confirmation
- Typically concludes themes and formal units
- The dominant occupies the penultimate position
- Creates a powerful dynamic impulse into the final tonic

Either the initial tonic or the pre-dominant may be omitted for an incomplete progression. The initial tonic is omitted more often than the pre-dominant, since eliminating the latter results in loss of a fundamental harmonic function.

# Common Confusions
- Assuming complete progressions are "stronger" than incomplete ones (both are fully viable)
- Forgetting that V must remain in root position throughout (inversion destroys cadential potential)
- Thinking the initial tonic must be in root position (first inversion is more typical)
- Confusing authentic cadential progression with perfect authentic cadence (the cadence also requires soprano on 1)
