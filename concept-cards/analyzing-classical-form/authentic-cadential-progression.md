# Quick Definition
A cadential progression in which both the dominant and final tonic are in root position: (initial) I6-PD-V-I. The strongest form of tonal confirmation. May be complete (all functions present) or incomplete (omitting initial tonic and/or pre-dominant).

# Core Definition
In an authentic cadential progression, both the dominant and final tonic harmonies must be placed in root position. If the final tonic is inverted or otherwise altered harmonically, then a deceptive cadential progression arises instead (p. 37). The basic complete form is: I6-II6-V(7)-I. Either the initial tonic or the pre-dominant may be omitted, yielding an incomplete cadential progression; the initial tonic is left out more often than the pre-dominant, since eliminating the latter results in the loss of a fundamental harmonic function. Even the very incomplete V-I succession represents an entirely viable cadential progression (p. 37).

# Prerequisites
- **Cadential progression** — Authentic is one of three kinds of cadential progression

# Key Properties
1. Both V and final I must be in root position
2. Complete form: I6-II6-V(7)-I
3. Initial tonic usually in first inversion (I6), sometimes root position
4. Pre-dominant typically II6, also IV, bII6 (Neapolitan), VII7/V, augmented sixths
5. Dominant embellished by cadential six-four: V(6/4-5/3)
6. Initial tonic embellished by neighboring V4/3 or chromaticized as V/IV, V/II
7. Incomplete forms omit initial tonic, pre-dominant, or both -- all fully viable

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for the functional succession ending on root-position I
2. Verify V is in root position from first appearance through resolution
3. Determine completeness: initial tonic present? Pre-dominant present?
4. Mark with horizontal square bracket in analysis
5. Note any embellishments (cadential six-four, chromaticized initial tonic, etc.)

# Context & Application
The authentic cadential progression creates the strongest tonal confirmation and typically concludes themes and formal units. The dominant occupies the penultimate position and creates a powerful dynamic impulse into the final tonic. There is nothing defective about incomplete progressions; compositional context determines which form is appropriate (p. 37).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming the initial tonic must be in root position
  **Correction**: First inversion (I6) is more typical for the initial tonic

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing authentic cadential progression with PAC
  **Clarification**: PAC also requires soprano on scale degree 1; authentic cadential progression is the harmonic event, PAC is the formal cadence type
- **Confusion**: Thinking complete progressions are "stronger" than incomplete
  **Clarification**: Both are fully viable; context determines which form is appropriate
