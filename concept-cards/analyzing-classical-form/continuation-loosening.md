# Quick Definition
In subordinate themes, continuation and cadential functions typically occupy distinct phrases with different content (unlike the tight-knit sentence where they fuse into a single "continuation phrase"). Continuation is loosened primarily through extension via model-sequence technique and delayed fragmentation.

# Core Definition
In tight-knit main themes (sentences), continuation and cadential functions normally fuse into a single "continuation phrase." In subordinate themes, these two functions typically occupy **distinct groups** with different melodic-motivic content, rhythmic patterning, and accompanimental figuration. Continuation achieves loose expression primarily through **extension**: constituent units (fragments) repeated more than necessary, and model-sequence technique is especially suitable. Another method is **delayed fragmentation**: initial continuation units match presentation size (no fragmentation yet), with continuation expressed through harmonic acceleration, quicker rhythms, and sequence; fragmentation appears later, extending continuation across multiple phrases (Caplin, Ch. 12, pp. 385-387).

6. This separation is a key marker of loose vs. tight-knit organization
