# Surface Rhythm Acceleration

**Surface rhythm acceleration** refers to an increase in rhythmic activity at the musical surface, typically through the use of shorter durational values in the continuation phrase compared to the presentation.

## Definition

The durational patterns formed by the attack points of every note create varying rates of activity. In comparison to the presentation, continuation function frequently features:
- Shorter note values
- Greater quantity of the same note values
- Increased motion in the surface rhythm

## Contribution to Continuation Function

Both fragmentation and harmonic acceleration bring a general sense of increased rhythmic action appropriate for:
- Giving the continuation a sense of thematic mobility
- Creating the impression of truly being "in the middle" of things

## When Particularly Effective

Increasing surface rhythm is particularly effective in the **absence** of harmonic acceleration. When harmonic rhythm doesn't speed up, faster surface rhythm can still project continuation function.

## Examples

- Change from predominantly quarter notes in the presentation to eighth notes in the continuation
- Elimination of longer note values in favor of shorter, more active patterns
- Focus on faster rhythmic motives that were only briefly introduced earlier

## Coordination with Other Processes

Surface rhythm acceleration typically works together with:
- Fragmentation
- Harmonic acceleration
- Sequential progressions

to create the overall effect of continuation function.
