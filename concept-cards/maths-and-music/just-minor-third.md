# Quick Definition
The just minor third is the interval with frequency ratio 6/5, approximately 315.64 cents, about 16 cents sharp of the equal-tempered minor third.

# Formal Definition
The just minor third is the rational interval corresponding to the ratio 6/5 = 2 * 3 / 5 in Q+. It is defined as the difference between the just fifth and the just major third: 3/2 / (5/4) = 6/5. Its prime factorization is 2^1 * 3^1 * 5^(-1), making it a 5-limit interval with the prime 5 in the denominator. Measured in cents: 1200 * log2(6/5) ~ 315.64.

# Mathematical Context
The ratio 6/5 emerges naturally as the interval "between" the just major third (5/4) and the just fifth (3/2) within the harmonic series. It is the octave complement of the just major sixth: 2 / (5/3) = 6/5. The just minor third is the first common just interval whose denominator involves the prime 5.

# Musical Context
The just minor third appears in justly tuned chords in several important ways: it is the interval from the third to the fifth of a major triad (4:5:6), and the interval from the root to the minor third in the just minor triad (10:12:15). In the just intonation chromatic scale, the flat third is tuned as a just minor third above the tonic. The 16-cent sharpness relative to equal temperament is noticeable, particularly in sustained chords.

# Examples
- 1200 * log2(6/5) ~ 315.64 cents (tempered minor third = 300 cents, difference ~ 15.64 cents)
- Just fifth minus just major third: (3/2) / (5/4) = 6/5
- Appears as the interval between harmonics 5 and 6
- In the just minor triad 10:12:15, the ratio 10:12 = 5:6 gives the minor third

# Common Confusions
- The just minor third (6/5 ~ 316 cents) is sharper than the tempered minor third (300 cents), while the just major third (5/4 ~ 386 cents) is flatter than the tempered major third (400 cents) -- they deviate in opposite directions
- The just minor third (6/5) should not be confused with the septimal minor third (7/6 ~ 267 cents), which is a substantially different interval
- The just minor third (6/5) is not the same as the Pythagorean minor third (32/27 ~ 294 cents)
