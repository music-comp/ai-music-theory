# Quick Definition

The just minor third is the interval with frequency ratio 6/5, approximately 315.64 cents, about 16 cents sharp of the equal-tempered minor third.

# Core Definition

The just minor third is the rational interval corresponding to the ratio 6/5 = 2 * 3 / 5 in Q+. Wright defines it via the just fifth and just major third: "By virtue of the equality 3/2 / (5/4) = 6/5, the ratio 6/5 gives the interval I 'between' the just major third and the just fifth, i.e., I is (additively) a just fifth minus a just major third" (p. 142). Its prime factorization is 2^1 * 3^1 * 5^(-1). Measured in cents: 1200 * log2(6/5) ~ 315.64.

# Prerequisites

- **Just fifth** -- The just minor third is defined as the difference between the just fifth and the just major third
- **Just major third** -- The other component in the defining relationship

# Key Properties

1. Ratio: 6/5
2. Cents: ~315.64
3. Deviation from equal temperament: ~15.64 cents sharp
4. Prime factorization: 2^1 * 3^1 * 5^(-1) (5-limit interval)
5. Equals just fifth minus just major third: (3/2) / (5/4) = 6/5
6. Octave complement of just major sixth: 2 / (5/3) = 6/5
7. Interval between the 5th and 6th harmonics

# Construction / Recognition

1. Start with the just fifth (3/2)
2. Subtract the just major third (5/4): (3/2) / (5/4) = 6/5
3. Result: the just minor third at ~315.64 cents
4. Alternatively, identify as the interval from harmonic 5 to harmonic 6

# Context & Application

The just minor third appears in several important chord tunings: it is the interval from the third to the fifth of a major triad (4:5:6), and the interval from the root to the third in the just minor triad (10:12:15). In the just intonation chromatic scale, the flat third is tuned as a just minor third above the tonic. The 16-cent sharpness relative to equal temperament is noticeable, particularly in sustained chords.

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 142): 1200 * log2(6/5) ~ 315.64 cents; tempered minor third = 300 cents, about 16 cents sharp.

**Example 2** (p. 142): Just fifth minus just major third: (3/2) / (5/4) = 6/5.

**Example 3** (p. 147): In the just minor triad 10:12:15, the ratio 10:12 = 5:6 gives the minor third.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming the just minor third is flatter than the tempered minor third (by analogy with the just major third being flatter)
  **Correction**: The just minor third (316 cents) is sharper than the tempered minor third (300 cents); the deviations go in opposite directions

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Confusing the just minor third (6/5) with the septimal minor third (7/6)
  **Clarification**: These differ by about 49 cents; the septimal version is substantially flatter

- **Confusion**: Confusing the just minor third (6/5 ~ 316 cents) with the Pythagorean minor third (32/27 ~ 294 cents)
  **Clarification**: These are different intervals in different tuning systems, separated by about 22 cents
