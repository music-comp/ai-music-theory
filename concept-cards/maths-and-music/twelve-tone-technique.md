# Quick Definition
A method of musical composition developed by Arnold Schoenberg that uses ordered arrangements of all twelve chromatic note classes, largely abandoning traditional consonance in favor of combinatorics.

# Formal Definition
Twelve-tone technique (also called dodecaphony or serialism) constructs music from ordered sets of all 12 pitch classes modulo octave. A composition is based on a row chart: a $12 \times 12$ array where each row and column contains each of the 12 note classes exactly once. All entries derive from the original (prime) row through inversion, transposition, and retrograde operations.

# Mathematical Context
The technique is heavily based on the structure of $\mathbb{Z}_{12}$. The number of possible original rows is $12! = 479,001,600$. Using modular arithmetic, the entry at position $(i, j)$ in the row chart is $a_j - a_i$ in $\mathbb{Z}_{12}$, where $a_1 = [0], a_2, \ldots, a_{12}$ are the elements of the original row. This formula simultaneously generates all transpositions and inversions.

# Musical Context
Developed in the 1920s by Arnold Schoenberg and continued by Anton Webern, Alban Berg, and Milton Babbitt. This is a form of serial music that constructs compositions from ordered sets of note classes. Since there is little feeling of tonal center, twelve-tone music is often written in the key of C. The technique can be generalized to n-tone row charts for any $n$.

# Examples
- An original row such as E, G, F$\sharp$, A, G$\sharp$, C, F, D, D$\sharp$, C$\sharp$, B, B$\flat$ generates a complete $12 \times 12$ row chart
- Notes from sequences can be applied horizontally (melodically) or vertically (harmonically)
- The technique generalizes to n-tone composition: a 7-tone row chart uses $\mathbb{Z}_7$ arithmetic with detuned white keys

# Common Confusions
- Twelve-tone music is not atonal in the sense of being random; it follows strict combinatorial rules
- The spelling of notes (sharps vs. flats) may vary within a composition and differ from the row chart
- The method can be extended to any n-chromatic scale, not just 12
