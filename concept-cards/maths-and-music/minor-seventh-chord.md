# Quick Definition
The minor seventh chord is a four-note chord defined by the interval sequence (3, 4, 3, 2) in semitones, built by adding a minor seventh above the root of a minor triad.

# Formal Definition
The minor seventh chord is defined by the sequence of modular intervals (3, 4, 3, 2) in Z_12:

root --(3)--> third --(4)--> fifth --(3)--> seventh --(2)--> (root)

This sequence admits no non-trivial cyclic symmetries, so the root, third, fifth, and seventh are uniquely determined. The chord contains the minor triad with the same root, third, and fifth.

# Mathematical Context
The interval sequence (3, 4, 3, 2) sums to 12. Although the sequence contains two 3s, they are not adjacent in a way that creates cyclic symmetry -- the four cyclic permutations (3,4,3,2), (4,3,2,3), (3,2,3,4), (2,3,4,3) are all distinct.

# Musical Context
The minor seventh chord is labeled with suffix m7 (e.g., Dm7, F#m7). It appears naturally on several scale degrees in the major mode. The chord is prominent in jazz and popular music. In the key of D major, an F#m7 would be labeled IIIm7; in the key of G minor, it would be #VIIm7.

# Examples
- Dm7: D-F-A-C, with intervals (3, 4, 3, 2)
- F#m7: F#-A-C#-E
- Am7: A-C-E-G

# Common Confusions
- The minor seventh chord (3, 4, 3, 2) should not be confused with the seventh chord (4, 3, 3, 2) -- the former has a minor triad base, the latter a major triad base
- The "seventh" in both the minor seventh and dominant seventh chords is a minor seventh interval above the root; the distinction is in the triad (minor vs. major)
- Do not confuse with the major seventh chord (4, 3, 4, 1)
