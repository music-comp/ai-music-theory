# Quick Definition

Overtone singing is a vocal technique in which the singer manipulates the mouth, tongue, and jaw to isolate specific overtones, making individual harmonics audible as distinct pitches while holding a constant fundamental.

# Core Definition

"Overtone singing is a type of singing in which the singer manipulates the resonating cavities in the mouth by moving the tongue and jaw so as to isolate specific overtones one at a time. The isolated overtone then becomes clearly audible. While holding a constant fundamental pitch the singer can thereby 'play a tune' with the overtones" (Wright, Ch. 10, p. 127).

# Prerequisites

- **Harmonics and Overtones** -- Understanding overtones as components of a tone
- **Overtone Series** -- The available pitches are from the overtone series

# Key Properties

1. The singer maintains a constant fundamental frequency $F$
2. Resonating cavities are adjusted to amplify one overtone at a time
3. The available melody pitches are $2F, 3F, 4F, 5F, \ldots$
4. Effectively creates a narrow, movable formant
5. The isolated overtone is a real frequency component, not an illusion

# Construction / Recognition

## How overtone singing works (mathematically):
1. In the Fourier series $g(t) = C + \sum d_k\sin(2\pi Fkt + \beta_k)$
2. The singer manipulates amplitudes $d_k$ by creating resonance conditions
3. One particular $d_k$ is dramatically boosted
4. The boosted harmonic becomes perceptually separated from the timbre

# Context & Application

While holding a constant fundamental, the singer can "play a tune" using the overtone series. The technique is practiced in Tuvan throat singing and Mongolian khoomei. It demonstrates that overtones are real frequency components, not mathematical abstractions.

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 127): A singer holding $F_2$ as fundamental can produce audible overtones at $F_3, C_4, F_4, A_4, C_5$, etc.

**Example 2**: The melody follows the overtone series, constrained to integer multiples of the fundamental.

**Example 3**: Higher overtones (11th, 13th harmonics) produce "in the cracks" intervals.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Thinking overtone singing involves two separate vocal sources
  **Correction**: The singer produces one fundamental pitch; the overtone is a natural component selectively amplified through resonance manipulation

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking overtones are abstract mathematical concepts
  **Clarification**: Overtone singing demonstrates that overtones are real, physically present frequency components that can be individually heard
