# Quick Definition

The Pythagorean major third is the interval with ratio 81/64, approximately 407.82 cents, formed by two Pythagorean whole tones (or four just fifths minus two octaves). It is about 22 cents sharp of the just major third and about 8 cents sharp of the tempered major third.

# Core Definition

The Pythagorean major third is the rational interval 81/64 = 3^4/2^6 in Q+, arising as the interval from scale tone 1 to scale tone 3 in the Pythagorean scale. Wright calculates: "(3/2)^4 * (1/2)^2 = 3^4/2^6 = 81/64. This interval, measured in cents is 1200 * log2(81/64) ~ 407.88, about 8 cents sharp of the tempered major third, and about 22 cents sharp of the just major third" (p. 153).

# Prerequisites

- **Pythagorean scale** -- The Pythagorean major third arises within this scale
- **Greater whole tone** -- Two greater whole tones compose to 81/64

# Key Properties

1. Ratio: 81/64 = 3^4/2^6
2. Cents: ~407.82
3. Sharp of tempered major third (400 cents) by ~7.82 cents
4. Sharp of just major third (5/4 ~ 386.31 cents) by the comma of Didymus (~21.51 cents)
5. Prime factorization: 2^(-6) * 3^4 (3-limit interval)
6. Equals two greater whole tones: (9/8)^2 = 81/64
7. Equals four just fifths minus two octaves: (3/2)^4 / 4 = 81/64

# Construction / Recognition

1. Iterate four just fifths from a starting note: (3/2)^4 = 81/16
2. Bring down two octaves: 81/16 / 4 = 81/64
3. Result: ~407.82 cents

# Context & Application

Wright states: "A significant weakness of this scale is its poor representation of the major third as 81/64, two Pythagorean whole tones. This ratio is even greater than the tempered major third, and is sharp of the just major third by precisely the comma of Didymus" (p. 154). The sharpness is easily perceived, and the resulting dissonance in the major triads I, IV, and V renders Pythagorean tuning unacceptable for music requiring consonant thirds.

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 153): (3/2)^4 / 4 = 81/64; 1200 * log2(81/64) ~ 407.82 cents.

**Example 2** (p. 154): The Pythagorean major third is sharp of the just major third by exactly the comma of Didymus: (81/64) / (5/4) = 81/80.

**Example 3** (p. 153): Two greater whole tones: (9/8)^2 = 81/64.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Expecting the Pythagorean major third to be close to the just major third
  **Correction**: The ~22-cent discrepancy (comma of Didymus) is clearly audible

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the Pythagorean major third is a "bad" just major third
  **Clarification**: It is not a just major third at all -- it is a 3-limit interval that cannot match the 5-limit just major third (5/4) because it lacks the prime 5

- **Confusion**: Assuming the Pythagorean major third is worse than the tempered major third
  **Clarification**: The Pythagorean version (408 cents) is even sharper than the tempered version (400 cents), which is itself ~14 cents sharp of just (386 cents)
