# Quick Definition
The Pythagorean major third is the interval with ratio 81/64, approximately 407.82 cents, formed by two Pythagorean whole tones. It is about 22 cents sharp of the just major third and about 8 cents sharp of the tempered major third.

# Formal Definition
The Pythagorean major third is the rational interval corresponding to the ratio 81/64 = 3^4/2^6 in Q+. It equals four just fifths minus two octaves: (3/2)^4 / 2^2 = 81/64. Equivalently, it is two greater whole tones: (9/8)^2 = 81/64. It is a 3-limit interval, involving only the primes 2 and 3. Measured in cents: 1200 * log2(81/64) ~ 407.82.

# Mathematical Context
The Pythagorean major third exceeds the just major third (5/4 = 80/64) by the comma of Didymus: 81/64 / (5/4) = 81/80 ~ 21.51 cents. This discrepancy is the fundamental weakness of Pythagorean tuning -- the system optimizes fifths at the expense of thirds. The ratio 81/64 arises inevitably from 3-limit tuning, since 5/4 requires the prime 5.

# Musical Context
The sharpness of the Pythagorean major third is easily perceived, and the resulting dissonance in the major triads I, IV, and V renders Pythagorean tuning unacceptable for music requiring a high level of consonance in thirds. This was a significant limitation until thirds became important in Western harmony during the 14th-15th centuries, which drove the development of 5-limit tuning systems (just intonation and mean-tone temperament).

# Examples
- 1200 * log2(81/64) ~ 407.82 cents (tempered major third = 400 cents, just major third ~ 386 cents)
- Two greater whole tones: (9/8)^2 = 81/64
- Four just fifths minus two octaves: (3/2)^4 / 4 = 81/64
- Sharp of just major third by comma of Didymus: 81/64 / (5/4) = 81/80

# Common Confusions
- The Pythagorean major third (81/64 ~ 408 cents) is sharper than both the tempered (400 cents) and just (386 cents) major thirds -- it is the worst of the three from a consonance standpoint
- The fact that Pythagorean tuning gives poor thirds is not a flaw in the math but an inherent consequence of restricting to 3-limit tuning
- The Pythagorean major third is still a rational interval, just not a very consonant one
