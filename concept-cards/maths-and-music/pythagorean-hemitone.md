# Quick Definition
The Pythagorean hemitone is the half-step interval in the Pythagorean scale with ratio 256/243 (= 2^8/3^5), approximately 90.22 cents, about 10 cents flat of the equal-tempered semitone.

# Formal Definition
The Pythagorean hemitone is the rational interval corresponding to the ratio 256/243 = 2^8/3^5 in Q+. It is a 3-limit interval, arising as the interval remaining when five just fifths minus three octaves are subtracted from the Pythagorean whole tone. In the Pythagorean scale, it appears as both half-step intervals: between scale degrees 3-4 and 7-8. Measured in cents: 1200 * log2(256/243) ~ 90.22.

# Mathematical Context
The hemitone can be computed as the complement of five greater whole tones within the octave. Since five Pythagorean whole tones (9/8) span 5 * 203.91 ~ 1019.55 cents, the two hemitones together must account for 1200 - 1019.55 ~ 180.45 cents, giving ~90.22 cents each. The hemitone is notably smaller than half a Pythagorean whole tone (which would be (9/8)^(1/2) ~ 101.96 cents).

# Musical Context
Pythagoras called this interval a hemitone, though it is not exactly half of any whole tone. It is narrower than the equal-tempered semitone (100 cents) by about 10 cents, and narrower than the just semitone (16/15 ~ 111.73 cents) by about 22 cents. This narrow half-step gives the Pythagorean scale a distinctive character, with wide whole steps and narrow half steps compared to equal temperament.

# Examples
- 256/243 = 2^8/3^5 ~ 90.22 cents
- Compare: tempered semitone = 100 cents, just semitone (16/15) ~ 111.73 cents
- Half of Pythagorean whole tone: (9/8)^(1/2) ~ 101.96 cents (the hemitone is smaller)
- Appears at intervals 3-to-4 and 7-to-8 in the Pythagorean scale

# Common Confusions
- The hemitone is not exactly half the Pythagorean whole tone -- it is called "hemitone" by convention but is actually narrower than half a whole tone
- The hemitone (256/243 ~ 90 cents) is a different interval from the just semitone (16/15 ~ 112 cents) -- they belong to different tuning systems (3-limit vs. 5-limit)
- Despite its complex-looking ratio, the hemitone involves only the primes 2 and 3
