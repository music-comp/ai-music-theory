# Quick Definition

The wolf fifth is the anomalously large fifth that appears in the mean-tone chromatic scale, approximately two-fifths of a semitone sharp of a just or tempered fifth, named after the animal's howl for its harsh dissonance.

# Core Definition

In the classical mean-tone scale, eleven fifths are tuned to 5^(1/4) (~696.58 cents each), but twelve such fifths fall short of seven octaves by 128/125 (~41.06 cents). One position must accommodate a "large fifth" that compensates. Wright states this large fifth "was long ago dubbed the wolf fifth, after the animal's howl" (p. 157). It measures approximately 696.58 + 41.06 ~ 737.64 cents.

# Prerequisites

- **Mean-tone scale** -- The wolf fifth is a structural consequence of mean-tone tuning
- **Lesser diesis** -- The interval (128/125 ~ 41 cents) that the wolf fifth absorbs

# Key Properties

1. Approximately 737.64 cents (~37.64 cents sharp of tempered fifth)
2. About 35.68 cents sharp of the just fifth
3. Absorbs the entire lesser diesis (128/125 ~ 41 cents)
4. Nearly twice as disruptive as the Pythagorean weak fifth (~23 cents)
5. Named for the harsh, howling quality of its beating
6. Placement on the circle is a design choice
7. Typically placed between 8 and 9 o'clock positions (e.g., between G# and Eb)

# Construction / Recognition

1. Tune eleven fifths at 5^(1/4) ~ 696.58 cents each
2. Total of eleven fifths: 11 * 696.58 ~ 7662.36 cents
3. Seven octaves = 8400 cents
4. Wolf fifth = 8400 - 7662.36 ~ 737.64 cents
5. Identify by its harsh, beating quality when played

# Context & Application

The wolf fifth represents the unavoidable cost of optimizing major thirds in the mean-tone system. Strategic placement away from commonly used keys was essential for practical music-making. If placed between G# and Eb, keys using those notes as fifths sound terrible while keys near C sound excellent. This key-dependence was a major motivation for the eventual adoption of equal temperament.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Thinking the wolf fifth exists in equal temperament
  **Correction**: It exists only in unequally tempered systems; equal temperament distributes the discrepancy evenly

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the wolf fifth is similar in size to the Pythagorean weak fifth
  **Clarification**: The wolf fifth (~41 cents excess) is much worse than the Pythagorean weak fifth (~23 cents deficit), and they go in opposite directions (sharp vs. flat)

- **Confusion**: Thinking the wolf fifth is fixed to a specific position
  **Clarification**: Its position is a design choice; moving it changes which keys sound best
