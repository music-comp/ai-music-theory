# Quick Definition
The mean-tone fifth is the interval with ratio 5^(1/4) (~1.49535), approximately 696.58 cents, deliberately flattened from the just fifth so that four such fifths yield an exact just major third.

# Formal Definition
The mean-tone fifth is the irrational interval with ratio x = 5^(1/4), determined by the requirement x^4 = 5 (i.e., four mean-tone fifths equal two octaves plus a just major third). Measured in cents: 1200 * log2(5^(1/4)) = 300 * log2(5) ~ 696.58. It lies about 3.42 cents flat of the tempered fifth (700 cents) and about 5.38 cents flat of the just fifth (701.96 cents).

# Mathematical Context
The mean-tone fifth is flat of the just fifth by exactly one-quarter of the comma of Didymus. Since the Pythagorean major third exceeds the just major third by the comma of Didymus (81/80), shrinking each of the four fifths that compose the major third by one-quarter of this comma produces an exact 5/4. The ratio 5^(1/4) is irrational, as can be proved using unique factorization (if it were rational, its fourth power 5 would need to factor as p1^(4a1) * ..., but 5 = 5^1 cannot have this form).

# Musical Context
The mean-tone fifth is "tolerably close" to the just fifth -- the ~5 cent flatness is at the threshold of perceptibility for most listeners. This makes it an acceptable compromise for achieving pure major thirds. However, the cumulative effect of twelve such fifths falling short of seven octaves by 128/125 (~41 cents) creates the wolf fifth, which must be placed at a musically inconvenient location on the circle of fifths.

# Examples
- 5^(1/4) ~ 1.49535 (compare: 3/2 = 1.5, 2^(7/12) ~ 1.49831)
- Four mean-tone fifths: (5^(1/4))^4 = 5 = 4 * (5/4) (two octaves + just major third)
- 300 * log2(5) ~ 696.58 cents
- Flat of just fifth by (81/80)^(1/4) ~ 5.38 cents (one-quarter comma of Didymus)

# Common Confusions
- The mean-tone fifth is irrational (5^(1/4)), unlike the just fifth (3/2) which is rational
- "Flat of the just fifth by one-quarter of the comma of Didymus" means each fifth absorbs 1/4 of the syntonic comma, not the Pythagorean comma
- The closeness of 5^(1/4) ~ 1.49535 to 3/2 = 1.5 is what makes mean-tone temperament viable
