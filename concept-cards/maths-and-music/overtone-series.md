# Quick Definition
For a given fundamental frequency F, the overtone series is the infinite sequence of pitches F, 2F, 3F, 4F, 5F, ..., formed by all positive integer multiples of the fundamental.

# Formal Definition
Given a fundamental frequency F, the overtone series is the sequence {kF : k = 1, 2, 3, ...}. The term kF is called the k-th harmonic. For k >= 2, it is also called the (k-1)-th overtone. The fundamental F is the 1st harmonic (and is sometimes called the 0th overtone, though this usage varies).

# Mathematical Context
The overtone series is simply the positive integer multiples of F. Each member kF forms the interval ratio k:1 with the fundamental. The sequence of intervals between consecutive overtones narrows: the interval from kF to (k+1)F has ratio (k+1)/k, which decreases as k grows.

# Musical Context
The overtone series is fundamental to Western harmony. Starting from F2 as the fundamental, the first 13 harmonics approximate (on the keyboard): F2, F3, C4, F4, A4, C5, (Eb5), F5, G5, A5, (between Bb5 and B5), C6, (between C#6 and D6). The notes in parentheses have poor keyboard approximations.

# Examples
- 1st harmonic (fundamental): F
- 2nd harmonic (1st overtone): 2F -- one octave above
- 3rd harmonic (2nd overtone): 3F -- octave + fifth above
- 4th harmonic: 4F -- two octaves above
- 5th harmonic: 5F -- two octaves + major third above
- 6th harmonic: 6F -- two octaves + fifth above
- 7th harmonic: 7F -- two octaves + "natural seventh" (poorly tempered)

# Common Confusions
The k-th harmonic is the (k-1)-th overtone: these indexing conventions differ by one. The 1st harmonic IS the fundamental; the 1st overtone is the 2nd harmonic (2F). This off-by-one issue is a perennial source of confusion.
