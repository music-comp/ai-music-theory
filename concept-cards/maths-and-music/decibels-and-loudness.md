# Quick Definition

Loudness is a subjective perception that varies from person to person and is not directly proportional to amplitude. Physics attempts to measure it using sound pressure in decibels, which also depends on frequency.

# Core Definition

"The term 'loudness' used above is subjective and difficult to quantify, as it varies from person to person. It is not directly proportional to mere amplitude. Physics attempts to measure it as a function of 'sound pressure', measured in *decibels*, as well as frequency" (Wright, Ch. 10, p. 134).

# Prerequisites

This is a foundational concept with no prerequisites within this source.

# Key Properties

1. Loudness is subjective and varies between individuals
2. Not directly proportional to amplitude
3. Depends on both sound pressure and frequency
4. Measured in decibels (dB), a logarithmic scale
5. Vertical stretching of a waveform adjusts amplitude with "very little effect, if any, on the timbre"

# Construction / Recognition

## Key relationships:
1. The decibel scale is logarithmic
2. The ear is most sensitive to frequencies in the 1-4 kHz range
3. Very low and very high frequencies require more amplitude to sound equally loud
4. Doubling amplitude does not double perceived loudness

# Context & Application

The distinction between amplitude and loudness is important for understanding how formant amplification affects the perceived volume of specific harmonics. Vertical stretching of a waveform changes loudness without significantly affecting timbre.

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 134): Doubling amplitude does not double perceived loudness.

**Example 2**: A soft clarinet and a loud clarinet have similar timbre (same harmonic profile) but different loudness.

**Example 3**: The ear is less sensitive to bass frequencies, which is why bass instruments need more power to sound equally loud.

# Relationships

## Related

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Equating amplitude directly with loudness
  **Correction**: The relationship is nonlinear and frequency-dependent

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking all frequencies at the same amplitude sound equally loud
  **Clarification**: The ear has frequency-dependent sensitivity; a 100 Hz tone at a given amplitude sounds much quieter than a 2000 Hz tone at the same amplitude
