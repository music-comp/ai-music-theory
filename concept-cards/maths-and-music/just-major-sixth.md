# Quick Definition
The just major sixth is the interval with frequency ratio 5/3, approximately 884.36 cents, about 16 cents flat of the equal-tempered major sixth.

# Formal Definition
The just major sixth is the rational interval corresponding to the ratio 5/3 in Q+. Its prime factorization is 3^(-1) * 5^1, making it a 5-limit interval. It involves a denominator with the prime 3 (unlike the simpler just intervals whose denominators are powers of 2). Measured in cents: 1200 * log2(5/3) ~ 884.36.

# Mathematical Context
The ratio 5/3 can be decomposed as 5/4 * 4/3, i.e., a just major third plus a just fourth. Equivalently, it is 2 / (6/5), meaning the octave complement of the just minor third. In the just intonation scale, 5/3 is the ratio of scale tone 6 to scale tone 1, and is determined by the requirement that the IV chord (4:5:6) be justly tuned.

# Musical Context
The just major sixth appears in the just intonation scale as the interval from the tonic to the sixth scale degree. It arises naturally from requiring the subdominant triad (IV) to be justly tuned as 4:5:6 -- the sixth scale degree must be a just major third above the fourth scale degree. The 16-cent discrepancy from equal temperament is significant and audible in sustained harmonies.

# Examples
- 1200 * log2(5/3) ~ 884.36 cents (tempered major sixth = 900 cents, difference ~ 15.64 cents)
- Just major third + just fourth = just major sixth: 5/4 * 4/3 = 5/3
- Octave complement of just minor third: 2 / (6/5) = 5/3
- In the just intonation scale, scale tone 6 has ratio 5/3 to scale tone 1

# Common Confusions
- The just major sixth (5/3) differs from the Pythagorean major sixth (27/16 ~ 905.87 cents) by the comma of Didymus
- The 16-cent flatness relative to equal temperament is the same magnitude as the just minor third's sharpness, since they are octave complements
