# Quick Definition

The just major sixth is the interval with frequency ratio 5/3, approximately 884.36 cents, about 16 cents flat of the equal-tempered major sixth.

# Core Definition

The just major sixth is the rational interval corresponding to the ratio 5/3 in Q+. Wright states: "The fraction 5/3 gives us an interval ~ 884.36 measured in cents. Since it is only about 16 cents flat of the keyboard major sixth, we call it the just major sixth" (p. 142). Its prime factorization is 3^(-1) * 5^1, making it a 5-limit interval.

# Prerequisites

- **Just interval** -- The just major sixth is a specific just interval defined by small-integer ratio

# Key Properties

1. Ratio: 5/3
2. Cents: ~884.36
3. Deviation from equal temperament: ~15.64 cents flat
4. Prime factorization: 3^(-1) * 5^1 (5-limit interval)
5. Equals just major third + just fourth: 5/4 * 4/3 = 5/3
6. Octave complement of just minor third: 2 / (6/5) = 5/3
7. In just intonation scale, ratio of scale tone 6 to scale tone 1

# Construction / Recognition

1. Compose a just major third (5/4) with a just fourth (4/3): 5/4 * 4/3 = 5/3
2. Alternatively, compute the octave complement of the just minor third: 2 / (6/5) = 5/3
3. Result: ~884.36 cents

# Context & Application

The just major sixth appears in the just intonation scale as the interval from the tonic to the sixth scale degree. It arises from requiring the subdominant triad (IV) to be justly tuned as 4:5:6 -- the sixth scale degree must be a just major third above the fourth scale degree. The 16-cent discrepancy from equal temperament is significant and audible in sustained harmonies.

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 142): 1200 * log2(5/3) ~ 884.36 cents; tempered major sixth = 900 cents, difference ~ 15.64 cents.

**Example 2** (p. 142): Just major third + just fourth = just major sixth: 5/4 * 4/3 = 5/3.

**Example 3**: Octave complement of just minor third: 2 / (6/5) = 5/3.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Computing the just major sixth independently rather than deriving it from chord requirements
  **Correction**: In the just intonation scale, the sixth is determined by the need for the IV chord to be 4:5:6

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the just major sixth and Pythagorean major sixth are similar
  **Clarification**: They differ by the comma of Didymus (~21.5 cents): just major sixth 5/3 ~ 884 cents vs. Pythagorean 27/16 ~ 906 cents

- **Confusion**: Not recognizing the complementary relationship with the just minor third
  **Clarification**: The deviation from ET is the same magnitude (~16 cents) but in opposite directions, since they are octave complements
