# Quick Definition

The two modes that became standard by the 18th century: major (Ionian, interval pattern $1, 1, \frac{1}{2}, 1, 1, 1, \frac{1}{2}$) and minor (Aeolian, interval pattern $1, \frac{1}{2}, 1, 1, \frac{1}{2}, 1, 1$), with each key signature representing one of each.

# Core Definition

The *major* mode is the Ionian mode (the standard scale). The *minor* mode is the Aeolian mode. With only these two modes in use, each key signature determines exactly two possibilities: a major key and a minor key. The minor key sharing a key signature with a given major key is called the *relative minor*. The tonic of the relative minor lies a minor third (3 semitones) below the tonic of the corresponding major key (Wright, p. 27).

# Key Properties

1. Major mode = Ionian; minor mode = Aeolian
2. Each key signature determines exactly one major key and one minor key
3. Total: $12 \times 2 = 24$ major/minor keys
4. Relative minor tonic lies a minor third (3 semitones) below the major tonic
5. The relative minor relationship defines a bijection between major and minor keys
6. The "character of music" determines which mode prevails

# Construction / Recognition

## To find the relative minor:

1. Start with the major key's tonic
2. Count down a minor third (3 semitones)
3. The resulting note is the relative minor's tonic
4. Both keys share the same key signature

# Context & Application

The restriction to major and minor modes became standard during the Common Practice Period (roughly 1600-1900). Major mode is often perceived as bright or happy; minor mode as dark or sad. The distinction between major and minor is one of the most fundamental in Western tonal music.

# Examples

- No sharps or flats: C major or A minor (A is a minor third below C) (p. 27)
- One sharp ($F^\sharp$): G major or E minor
- Three flats ($B^\flat, E^\flat, A^\flat$): $E^\flat$ major or C minor
- The "character of music" — cadences, melodic tendencies, harmonic progressions — determines which mode applies (p. 27)

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Confusing relative minor (shares key signature) with parallel minor (shares tonic)
  **Correction**: C major and A minor are relative (same signature); C major and C minor are parallel (same tonic)

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the natural minor (Aeolian) is the only form of minor
  **Clarification**: Harmonic and melodic minor are later modifications not discussed in this chapter
- **Confusion**: Assuming "standard scale" means any diatonic scale
  **Clarification**: Wright uses "standard scale" to mean specifically the major (Ionian) scale
