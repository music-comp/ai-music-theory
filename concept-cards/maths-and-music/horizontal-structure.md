# Quick Definition
The temporal aspects of music -- note durations, sequences of events, and formal organization -- represented visually by left-to-right progression on the staff, analogous to the horizontal axis in mathematics.

# Formal Definition
In mathematics, time is parameterized by a horizontal axis ($x$-axis or $t$-axis). In a musical staff, progression from left to right represents the passing of time, while the vertical axis designates pitch. The temporal aspects of music -- durations of sustained notes, sequences of events and episodes -- constitute its horizontal structure. This contrasts with vertical structure (harmony, simultaneity).

# Mathematical Context
The analogy between musical time and the mathematical $x$-axis or $t$-axis is foundational: a musical score is essentially a two-dimensional graph with time on the horizontal axis and pitch on the vertical axis. Temporal patterns in music relate to mathematical concepts including powers of 2 (note durations), geometric series (dotted notes), and geometric transformations (melodic repetition and variation).

# Musical Context
Horizontal structure encompasses everything about how music unfolds in time: note durations, rhythm, meter, melodic sequences, repetition patterns, and large-scale form. Music strives to interest the listener through temporal patterns that are satisfying and cohesive. The notation and organization of horizontal structure contain numerous relationships with basic mathematical concepts.

# Examples
- Note durations are based on powers of 2 (whole, half, quarter, eighth, etc.)
- Dotted notes involve geometric series summation
- Melodic repetition corresponds to translation (horizontal shift)
- Musical form (AABA, binary, ternary) organizes large-scale temporal structure

# Common Confusions
- "Horizontal" refers to time, not pitch -- pitch is the vertical dimension on a staff
- Horizontal structure is not just rhythm; it includes melody, form, and all aspects of temporal organization
- The horizontal/vertical distinction in music maps directly to the $x$/$y$ axes in mathematics
