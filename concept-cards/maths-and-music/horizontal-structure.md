# Quick Definition

The temporal aspects of music -- note durations, sequences of events, and formal organization -- represented visually by left-to-right progression on the staff, analogous to the horizontal axis in mathematics.

# Core Definition

In mathematics, time is parameterized by a horizontal axis ($x$-axis or $t$-axis). In a musical staff, progression from left to right represents the passing of time, while the vertical axis designates pitch. The temporal aspects of music -- durations of sustained notes, sequences of events and episodes -- constitute its *horizontal structure* (Wright, p. 30).

# Prerequisites

- **Functions and Graphs** — The horizontal axis analogy comes from function graphs

# Key Properties

1. Horizontal = time; vertical = pitch on a musical staff
2. Encompasses note durations, rhythm, meter, melodic sequences, form
3. Musical scores are essentially two-dimensional graphs (time vs. pitch)
4. Temporal patterns relate to powers of 2, geometric series, and geometric transformations
5. Music strives to interest listeners through satisfying temporal patterns

# Construction / Recognition

## To identify horizontal structure elements:

1. Note durations — how long each note is held
2. Rhythm — how time is organized within measures
3. Meter — how beats are grouped into measures
4. Melody — the succession of notes over time
5. Form — the sequence of larger sections

# Context & Application

Horizontal structure encompasses everything about how music unfolds in time. The notation and organization of horizontal structure contain numerous relationships with basic mathematical concepts: powers of 2 (note durations), geometric series (dotted notes), and geometric transformations (melodic repetition and variation). This contrasts with vertical structure (harmony, simultaneity), discussed in Chapter 3.

# Examples

- Note durations are based on powers of 2 (whole, half, quarter, eighth, etc.) (p. 30)
- Dotted notes involve geometric series summation (pp. 32-34)
- Melodic repetition corresponds to translation (horizontal shift) (p. 38)
- Musical form (AABA, binary, ternary) organizes large-scale temporal structure (pp. 40-41)

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Thinking "horizontal" refers to pitch range
  **Correction**: Horizontal refers to time; pitch is the vertical dimension

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking horizontal structure is just rhythm
  **Clarification**: It includes melody, form, and all aspects of temporal organization
- **Confusion**: Equating the musical horizontal with only left-right spatial position
  **Clarification**: The horizontal/vertical distinction in music maps directly to the $t$/$y$ axes in mathematics
