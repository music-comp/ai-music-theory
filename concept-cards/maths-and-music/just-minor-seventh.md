# Quick Definition
The just minor seventh is the interval with frequency ratio 9/5, approximately 1017.60 cents, as distinct from the septimal minor seventh (7/4 ~ 968.83 cents). It produces a less consonant seventh chord (20:25:30:36) compared to the septimal version (4:5:6:7).

# Formal Definition
The just minor seventh is the rational interval corresponding to the ratio 9/5 = 3^2 / 5 in Q+. It is a 5-limit interval. A seventh chord tuned with this interval has the ratios 20:25:30:36, where the root-to-seventh ratio is 36/20 = 9/5. This is decidedly less consonant than the septimal seventh chord 4:5:6:7 (where root-to-seventh = 7/4). Measured in cents: 1200 * log2(9/5) ~ 1017.60.

# Mathematical Context
The ratio 9/5 can be decomposed as (3/2) * (6/5), i.e., a just fifth plus a just minor third. Alternatively, it is a just fourth plus a just major third: (4/3) * (5/4) * (3/2) * ... The 5-limit seventh chord 20:25:30:36 uses larger integers than the septimal 4:5:6:7, reflecting its lower consonance. The interval 9/5 is closer to the tempered minor seventh (1000 cents) than the septimal seventh (7/4), being about 18 cents sharp versus 31 cents flat.

# Musical Context
In the just intonation scale (5-limit), seventh chords use the just minor seventh (9/5) rather than the septimal seventh (7/4), since 7-limit intervals are excluded. This makes just-intonation seventh chords (20:25:30:36) less perfectly consonant. The V7 chord in just intonation is even worse, tuned as 36:45:54:64, because the interval from scale degree 2 to scale degree 4 is not even a just minor third.

# Examples
- 1200 * log2(9/5) ~ 1017.60 cents (tempered minor seventh = 1000 cents, difference ~ 17.60 cents)
- 5-limit seventh chord: 20:25:30:36 (less consonant than septimal 4:5:6:7)
- V7 in just intonation: 36:45:54:64 (poorly consonant)
- Compare: septimal seventh 7/4 ~ 968.83 cents vs. just minor seventh 9/5 ~ 1017.60 cents (gap ~ 49 cents)

# Common Confusions
- The just minor seventh (9/5) and the septimal minor seventh (7/4) are substantially different intervals (~49 cents apart) despite both being called "minor sevenths"
- The septimal version (4:5:6:7) is more consonant despite using a higher prime, because its chord ratios involve smaller integers overall
- Neither version matches the tempered minor seventh (1000 cents) closely
