# Quick Definition

The just minor seventh is the interval with frequency ratio 9/5, approximately 1017.60 cents. It produces a less consonant seventh chord (20:25:30:36) compared to the septimal minor seventh (7/4), which gives the more consonant 4:5:6:7 chord.

# Core Definition

The just minor seventh is the rational interval corresponding to the ratio 9/5 = 3^2 / 5 in Q+. Wright introduces it by contrasting it with the septimal seventh: "A less consonant tuning of the seventh chord is given by 20:25:30:36. Here the interval from root to seventh is 9:5, and interval which is called the just minor seventh (as opposed to the septimal minor seventh)" (p. 149). It is a 5-limit interval.

# Prerequisites

- **Just fifth** -- The just minor seventh can be composed as a just fifth plus a just minor third
- **Just minor third** -- Component of the composition

# Key Properties

1. Ratio: 9/5
2. Cents: ~1017.60
3. Deviation from equal temperament: ~17.60 cents sharp
4. Prime factorization: 3^2 * 5^(-1) (5-limit interval)
5. Equals just fifth + just minor third: (3/2) * (6/5) = 9/5
6. Gives the 5-limit seventh chord 20:25:30:36
7. About 49 cents sharper than the septimal minor seventh (7/4 ~ 968.83 cents)

# Construction / Recognition

1. Start with the just fifth (3/2)
2. Add a just minor third (6/5): (3/2) * (6/5) = 9/5
3. Result: ~1017.60 cents

# Context & Application

In the just intonation scale (5-limit), seventh chords must use the just minor seventh (9/5) rather than the more consonant septimal seventh (7/4), since 7-limit intervals are excluded. The resulting chord tuning 20:25:30:36 uses notably larger integers than the septimal 4:5:6:7. The V7 chord in just intonation is even worse, tuned as 36:45:54:64, because the interval from scale degree 2 to scale degree 4 is not even a just minor third.

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 149): The 5-limit seventh chord is tuned as 20:25:30:36, with root-to-seventh ratio 9/5.

**Example 2** (p. 149): Compare with the septimal seventh chord 4:5:6:7, which is "laser-like in its perfect consonance."

**Example 3** (Ch. 12, p. 156): V7 in just intonation is tuned as 36:45:54:64, even less consonant.

**Example 4**: 1200 * log2(9/5) ~ 1017.60 cents; septimal seventh 7/4 ~ 968.83 cents; gap ~ 49 cents.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming the 5-limit seventh chord is more consonant because it avoids the "exotic" prime 7
  **Correction**: The septimal chord 4:5:6:7 is actually more consonant because it uses smaller integers overall

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Conflating "just minor seventh" (9/5) with "septimal minor seventh" (7/4)
  **Clarification**: These are about 49 cents apart and produce very different chord qualities

- **Confusion**: Thinking neither version matches the tempered minor seventh
  **Clarification**: The septimal seventh (969 cents) is 31 cents flat; the just minor seventh (1018 cents) is 18 cents sharp; neither is close
