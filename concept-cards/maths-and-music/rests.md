# Quick Definition
Notational symbols indicating silence for a specific duration, with rest types corresponding to the same durational hierarchy as notes (whole rest, half rest, quarter rest, etc.).

# Formal Definition
A rest is a notational symbol indicating silence for a duration determined by the type of rest. Rests come in the same durational types as notes ($\frac{1}{2^n}$-th rests for non-negative integers $n$), distinguished by their visual appearance rather than by noteheads and stems. The whole rest is a rectangle attached on the underside of a staff line; other rests have distinct symbols for each duration level.

# Mathematical Context
Rests follow the same power-of-2 durational structure as notes. A rest of type $\frac{1}{2^n}$ represents silence for $\frac{1}{2^n}$ of a whole note's duration. Rests can also receive dots (extending duration by the same geometric series formula as dotted notes) and participate in the arithmetic of measure completion.

# Musical Context
Standard rest symbols include whole rest, half rest, quarter rest, eighth rest, sixteenth rest, thirty-second rest, and sixty-fourth rest. The vertical position on the staff is generally standard but may vary when two parts share a staff (e.g., soprano and alto on the same staff, with a rest appearing in only one part). Rests are essential for rhythmic articulation and musical phrasing.

# Examples
- A whole rest in $\frac{4}{4}$ time fills an entire measure (4 beats of silence)
- A quarter rest in $\frac{4}{4}$ time represents 1 beat of silence
- An eighth rest represents $\frac{1}{2}$ beat (in $\frac{4}{4}$ time)
- The whole rest hangs from a line; the half rest sits on a line (a common visual distinction)

# Common Confusions
- Whole rest vs. half rest: visually similar rectangles, but the whole rest hangs below the line while the half rest sits above the line
- A whole rest can also mean "fill the entire measure with silence" regardless of time signature, not just 4 beats
- Rest position on the staff may vary for multi-voice notation
