# Quick Definition

Notational symbols indicating silence for a specific duration, with rest types corresponding to the same durational hierarchy as notes (whole rest, half rest, quarter rest, etc.).

# Core Definition

A *rest* is a notational symbol indicating silence for a duration determined by the type of rest. Rests come in the same durational types as notes ($\frac{1}{2^n}$-th rests for non-negative integers $n$), distinguished by their visual appearance rather than by noteheads and stems (Wright, p. 32).

# Prerequisites

- **Note Durational Values** — Rests follow the same power-of-2 durational system as notes

# Key Properties

1. Rests follow the same power-of-2 durational structure as notes
2. A rest of type $\frac{1}{2^n}$ represents silence for $\frac{1}{2^n}$ of a whole note's duration
3. Each rest type has a distinct visual symbol
4. Rests can also receive dots (same geometric series formula as dotted notes)
5. Vertical position on staff is generally standard but may vary for multi-voice notation

# Construction / Recognition

## To identify rest types:

1. Whole rest: rectangle hanging below a staff line
2. Half rest: rectangle sitting on top of a staff line
3. Quarter rest: a distinctive zigzag symbol
4. Eighth rest and shorter: angled marks with increasing number of flags/hooks

# Context & Application

Standard rest symbols include whole rest, half rest, quarter rest, eighth rest, sixteenth rest, thirty-second rest, and sixty-fourth rest. Rest position on the staff may vary when two parts share a staff, with rests placed at different vertical positions to avoid ambiguity. Rests are essential for rhythmic articulation and musical phrasing.

# Examples

- A whole rest in $\frac{4}{4}$ time fills an entire measure (4 beats of silence) (p. 32)
- A quarter rest in $\frac{4}{4}$ time represents 1 beat of silence
- An eighth rest represents $\frac{1}{2}$ beat in $\frac{4}{4}$ time
- The whole rest hangs from a line; the half rest sits on a line (p. 32)

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Confusing whole rest and half rest visually
  **Correction**: Whole rest hangs below the line; half rest sits above the line

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking a whole rest always means 4 beats of silence
  **Clarification**: A whole rest can also mean "fill the entire measure with silence" regardless of time signature
- **Confusion**: Assuming rest position on the staff is always fixed
  **Clarification**: In multi-voice notation, rests may be placed at different vertical positions
