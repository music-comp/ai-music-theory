# Quick Definition
The succession of single pitches with prescribed durations that forms the most prominent and recognizable line in a musical composition, defined equally by its pitch sequence and its rhythm.

# Formal Definition
Melody is the succession of notes (single pitches with prescribed duration) that are most prominent in a musical composition and serve to define and characterize the piece. A melody is defined and made recognizable not only by its sequence of pitches but by its rhythm.

# Mathematical Context
A melody can be modeled as a function $f: T \to P$ from a discrete set of time points $T$ to a set of pitches $P$, where each time point also has an associated duration. Alternatively, it is a finite sequence of ordered pairs $\{(p_i, d_i)\}$ where $p_i$ is a pitch and $d_i$ is a duration. Melodic transformations (translation, transposition, retrogression) correspond to geometric transformations of the pitch-vs-time graph.

# Musical Context
In a song, the melody is what the solo vocalist sings while other notes are played in accompaniment. In a symphony, the melody is often (but not always) played by the highest instrument, typically the first violin section. Music achieves cohesion through repetition of melodic patterns, with variation and embellishment. These repetitions correspond to mathematical transformations.

# Examples
- A descending C major scale played in even eighth notes is not recognizable as any particular song
- The same descending scale set to the rhythm of "Joy To The World" is immediately recognized -- demonstrating that rhythm is essential to melodic identity
- In "When The Saints Go Marching In," the melodic sequence F-A-B$\flat$-C appears three times consecutively (melodic translation)

# Common Confusions
- Melody is not just a sequence of pitches -- rhythm is equally important for melodic identity
- Melody is the foreground line, not the harmony or accompaniment
- A melody can be transformed (transposed, translated, retrograded) and still be recognizably related to the original
