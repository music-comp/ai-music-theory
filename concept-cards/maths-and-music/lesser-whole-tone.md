# Quick Definition

The lesser whole tone is the interval with frequency ratio 10/9, approximately 182.40 cents, about 18 cents flat of the equal-tempered whole step.

# Core Definition

The lesser whole tone is the rational interval corresponding to the ratio 10/9 = 2 * 5 / 3^2 in Q+. Wright states: "The ratio 10/9 gives another interval approximated by the keyboard step. We have 1200 * log2(10/9) ~ 182.40, showing this interval to be about 18 cents flat of the keyboard's step" (p. 142). It is a 5-limit interval involving the primes 2, 3, and 5.

# Prerequisites

- **Just interval** -- The lesser whole tone is a specific just interval
- **Rational interval** -- Understanding frequency ratios as elements of Q+

# Key Properties

1. Ratio: 10/9
2. Cents: ~182.40
3. Deviation from equal temperament: ~17.60 cents flat
4. Prime factorization: 2^1 * 3^(-2) * 5^1 (5-limit interval)
5. Differs from the greater whole tone by the comma of Didymus: (9/8) / (10/9) = 81/80
6. Greater whole tone + lesser whole tone = just major third: 9/8 * 10/9 = 5/4
7. The tempered step (200 cents) lies between the lesser and greater whole tones, closer to the latter

# Construction / Recognition

1. Divide the just major third (5/4) by the greater whole tone (9/8): (5/4) / (9/8) = 10/9
2. Result: the lesser whole tone at ~182.40 cents

# Context & Application

In the just intonation scale, the lesser whole tone appears at intervals 2-to-3 and 5-to-6, while the greater whole tone appears at 1-to-2, 4-to-5, and 6-to-7. This means the just intonation scale has two different sizes of whole steps, unlike the Pythagorean scale (which uses only the greater whole tone) or equal temperament (which has only one size). The existence of two whole tones is a fundamental feature of 5-limit tuning.

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 142): 1200 * log2(10/9) ~ 182.40 cents; tempered step = 200 cents, difference ~ 17.60 cents.

**Example 2** (p. 142): The cents scale shows: lesser whole tone (182) < tempered step (200) < greater whole tone (204).

**Example 3** (p. 142): Greater whole tone / lesser whole tone = comma of Didymus: (9/8) / (10/9) = 81/80.

**Example 4** (p. 142): Greater whole tone * lesser whole tone = just major third: 9/8 * 10/9 = 5/4.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating the lesser whole tone as interchangeable with the greater whole tone
  **Correction**: They differ by ~21.5 cents (the comma of Didymus), a clearly audible difference

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Assuming equal temperament's single whole step is closer to the lesser whole tone
  **Clarification**: The tempered step (200 cents) is much closer to the greater whole tone (204 cents) than to the lesser (182 cents)

- **Confusion**: Thinking the lesser whole tone is a 3-limit interval like the greater whole tone
  **Clarification**: The lesser whole tone (10/9) is a 5-limit interval involving the prime 5, while the greater whole tone (9/8) is 3-limit
