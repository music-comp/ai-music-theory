# Quick Definition

The justly tuned major triad comprises harmonics 4, 5, and 6 of a fundamental tone, with the ratio 4:5:6 giving a just major third from root to third and a just fifth from root to fifth.

# Core Definition

A just rendition of the major triad is given by the ratio 4:5:6, meaning the three pitches have frequencies in the proportion 4:5:6. The interval from root to third is 5/4 (just major third), from root to fifth is 6/4 = 3/2 (just fifth), and from third to fifth is 6/5 (just minor third). With the root doubled in "tenth voicing" (third a tenth above the lowest root), the chord is realized as 2:3:4:5. The presence of this chord among the low harmonics explains why it is "so pleasing and so basic in music" (Wright, pp. 146-147).

# Prerequisites

- **Major Triad** -- Must understand the major triad as a chord type defined by interval sequence (4, 3, 5)
- **Interval as Frequency Ratio** -- Must understand intervals as frequency ratios to work with the 4:5:6 relationship

# Key Properties

1. The major triad 4:5:6 appears as consecutive harmonics starting at the 4th harmonic
2. Root to third: ratio 5/4 (just major third, approximately 386 cents)
3. Root to fifth: ratio 3/2 (just fifth, approximately 702 cents)
4. Third to fifth: ratio 6/5 (just minor third)
5. All component intervals involve only primes 2, 3, and 5 (5-limit harmony)
6. The equal-tempered major third (400 cents) is about 14 cents sharp compared to the just version

# Construction / Recognition

## To Construct a Justly Tuned Major Triad

1. Choose a root frequency f
2. The third has frequency (5/4)f
3. The fifth has frequency (3/2)f
4. These three frequencies are in the proportion 4:5:6

# Context & Application

The major triad's natural occurrence in the harmonic series provides the physical explanation for its consonance. In the just intonation scale, the I, IV, and V chords are all tuned to 4:5:6, making them perfectly consonant. The equal-tempered major triad approximates this but with a notably sharp third. Performers with pitch flexibility naturally gravitate toward the 4:5:6 tuning (Wright, pp. 146-147).

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 146): C major triad in just intonation: frequencies in ratio 4:5:6.

**Example 2** (p. 147): F2 harmonics 4, 5, 6 give an F major triad. Doubled root in tenth voicing gives harmonics 2, 3, 4, 5.

**Example 3** (p. 147): The minor triad for comparison requires ratio 10:12:15 (larger integers, less consonant).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming the equal-tempered major triad produces the 4:5:6 ratio
  **Correction**: The tempered third is about 14 cents sharp (400 vs. 386 cents); only just intonation produces 4:5:6

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the 4:5:6 ratio applies regardless of voicing
  **Clarification**: The 4:5:6 ratio describes the chord in root position within an octave; other voicings have different frequency ratios
