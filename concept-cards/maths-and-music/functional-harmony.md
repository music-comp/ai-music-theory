# Quick Definition
Functional harmony is the system in which chords serve specific roles (tonic, dominant, predominant, etc.) within a key, and progressions follow patterns that create tension and resolution toward the tonic.

# Formal Definition
In functional harmony, chords are understood not merely as isolated sonorities but as fulfilling harmonic functions relative to a tonic. The primary functions are tonic (rest/stability, typically I), dominant (tension/drive toward tonic, typically V or V7), and predominant (preparation for the dominant, typically II or IV). Chord progressions are organized to create directed harmonic motion, often following the circle of fifths toward resolution on the tonic.

# Mathematical Context
Functional harmony can be understood through the algebraic structure of Z_12 and the circle of fifths. The dominant-to-tonic resolution (V -> I) corresponds to a counter-clockwise step on the circle of fifths (motion by 5 semitones). Extended progressions like VI7 -> IIm -> V7 -> I trace a path along the circle. The Roman numeral labeling system encodes functional relationships: the numeral identifies the chord's position relative to the tonic, and common functional patterns emerge as arithmetic regularities.

# Musical Context
A melody often suggests chords by offering sequences of notes that lie mostly within a certain chord -- this is "implied harmony." However, this basic harmony can be artistically altered or enhanced. The text shows that a simple melody in F major can be harmonized as I -> V7 -> I, but also as I -> IIm -> IIIm, I -> IV -> V, I -> bVII -> I, or I -> bVII -> V, each giving the passage a different personality. The dominant seventh chord (V7) plays a central role in functional harmony due to its strong resolution tendency.

# Examples
- I -> V7 -> I: the most basic tonic-dominant-tonic progression
- VI7 -> IIm -> V7 -> I: extended circle-of-fifths progression
- Multiple valid harmonizations of the same melody demonstrate that harmony involves artistic choice within functional constraints
- The seventh chord in a "dominant" role should always be spelled as a seventh chord, not as an augmented sixth

# Common Confusions
- Functional harmony is not the only harmonic system; much twentieth-century and non-Western music operates outside it
- "Function" refers to the chord's role in the key, not its acoustic properties
- Multiple harmonizations of the same melody can all be "correct" -- functional harmony provides a framework, not a single right answer
- The dominant "function" can be served by chords other than V (e.g., VII)
