# Quick Definition
The just fifth is the interval with frequency ratio 3/2, approximately 701.96 cents, about 2 cents sharp of the equal-tempered fifth.

# Formal Definition
The just fifth is the rational interval corresponding to the ratio 3/2 in Q+. It is the integral interval 3 (the third harmonic) lowered by one octave. Its prime factorization is 2^(-1) * 3^1, making it the simplest interval involving the prime 3. Measured in cents: 1200 * log2(3/2) ~ 701.96.

# Mathematical Context
The ratio 3/2 is the generator of the Pythagorean tuning system (3-limit). Iterating the just fifth generates the circle of fifths, but twelve just fifths do not exactly equal seven octaves -- they overshoot by the comma of Pythagoras (3^12/2^19 ~ 23.46 cents). This fundamental discrepancy is what prevents perfect tuning and motivates the various temperament systems.

# Musical Context
Pythagoras regarded the 3:2 ratio as representing the perfection of the universe. The just fifth is the most consonant interval after the octave, and is the foundation of Pythagorean tuning. The equal-tempered fifth at 700 cents is an excellent approximation, being only about 2 cents flat -- close enough that most listeners cannot distinguish them in isolation. The just fifth is also the interval between the 2nd and 3rd harmonics of any sustained tone.

# Examples
- 1200 * log2(3/2) ~ 701.96 cents (tempered fifth = 700 cents, difference ~ 1.96 cents)
- Composing with its complement: 3/2 * 4/3 = 2 (just fifth + just fourth = octave)
- Two just fifths minus an octave: (3/2)^2 / 2 = 9/8 (the greater whole tone)
- Twelve just fifths: (3/2)^12 = 531441/4096 ~ 129.75, vs. seven octaves = 128

# Common Confusions
- The just fifth (3/2 ~ 701.96 cents) is not the same as the tempered fifth (2^(7/12) = 700 cents), though the difference is very small
- The just fifth is not the same as the mean-tone fifth (5^(1/4) ~ 696.58 cents), which is deliberately flattened to improve thirds
- Twelve just fifths do not close the circle -- they overshoot by the comma of Pythagoras
