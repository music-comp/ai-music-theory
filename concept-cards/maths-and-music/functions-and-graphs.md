# Quick Definition
A function maps elements from a domain to a range, and its graph is the visual representation of this mapping in the coordinate plane, with the independent variable on the horizontal axis and the dependent variable on the vertical axis.

# Formal Definition
A function from some subset of $\mathbb{R}$ into $\mathbb{R}$ is expressed as $y = f(x)$, where $x$ is the independent variable and $y$ is the dependent variable. The graph is the set of points $\{(x, f(x)) \mid x \in \text{domain}\}$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$. When the independent variable parameterizes time, it is often denoted $t$, giving $y = f(t)$.

# Mathematical Context
Key examples include: $y = mx + b$ (linear, with slope $m$ and $y$-intercept $b$), $y = x^2$ (parabola with vertex at the origin), and the trigonometric functions $y = \sin x$ and $y = \cos x$. The concepts of domain and range are assumed. Functions can be transformed by shifts and stretches (see Geometric Transformations of Graphs).

# Musical Context
In music, the horizontal axis ($x$ or $t$) typically represents time, and the vertical axis represents pitch or amplitude. Sound waves are modeled by sinusoidal functions $y = \sin(2\pi ft)$ where $f$ is frequency. The graph of a musical waveform shows amplitude vs. time. Musical scores themselves function as a type of graph: horizontal position encodes time, vertical position encodes pitch.

# Examples
- $y = \sin x$ and $y = \cos x$ model pure tones (sound waves)
- $y = mx + b$ represents a linear function with slope $m$
- $y = x^2$ is a parabola, used to illustrate geometric transformations in the text
- A musical staff acts analogously to a graph: left-to-right is time, up-down is pitch

# Common Confusions
- The variable $t$ (time) and $x$ are interchangeable as independent variables; the choice is contextual
- A graph is the visual representation of a function, not the function itself
