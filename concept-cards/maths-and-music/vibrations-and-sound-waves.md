# Quick Definition
A vibration is an oscillation with a repeating pattern of period P, producing a sound wave transmitted through air as contraction and expansion, perceived by the ear as a musical tone of frequency F = 1/P Hz.

# Formal Definition
A vibration is a periodic function y = f(t) where y is the position of a vibrating object at time t. The period P is measured in seconds, and the frequency (pitch) is F = 1/P Hz. The vibration is transmitted through air as a sound wave and received when the eardrum vibrates in the same pattern.

# Mathematical Context
For a periodic function to represent a vibration suitable for Fourier analysis, it must satisfy two conditions: (1) f has only finitely many discontinuities on [0, P), and (2) f is bounded (there exist b, B such that b < f(t) < B for all t). Discontinuities are interpreted as moments of very rapid position change.

# Musical Context
Vibrating motion arises from violin strings, air columns inside trumpets, and human vocal cords. The brain interprets the received vibration as a musical tone. The pitch is determined by the period, the loudness by the amplitude, and the timbre by the relative strengths of the harmonics in the Fourier decomposition.

# Examples
- A string vibrating at P = 1/440 seconds produces A4 (440 Hz)
- sin(880*pi*t) gives a pure tone at A4 since its period is 2*pi/(880*pi) = 1/440 seconds
- A tuning fork produces a nearly pure sinusoidal vibration
- A violin string produces a complex vibration with many harmonics

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes confuse the vibration (the physical motion of an object) with the sound wave (the pressure variation transmitted through air). They have the same pattern, but the vibration is a mechanical motion while the sound wave is a pressure wave.
