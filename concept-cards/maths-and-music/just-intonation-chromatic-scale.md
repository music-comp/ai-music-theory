# Quick Definition
The just intonation chromatic scale extends the 5-limit diatonic scale to all twelve notes by tuning additional triads (flat-VI, flat-III, VI, VIIm) as justly as possible, yielding many just chords but also some poorly tuned ones.

# Formal Definition
The just intonation chromatic scale extends the diatonic just intonation scale by the following tuning decisions: (1) flat-6 and flat-3 are tuned so that the flat-VI triad is justly tuned (flat-6 is a just major third below the octave; flat-3 is a just minor third above the tonic); (2) flat-7 is a just minor third above scale degree 5, making flat-III a just major triad; (3) flat-2 (= sharp-1) is tuned so that VI is justly tuned; (4) flat-5 (= sharp-4) is a just fourth below scale degree 7, making VIIm a just minor triad.

# Mathematical Context
With this chromatic extension, many major triads, minor triads, and minor seventh chords are justly tuned (Im7 and IVm7 are just), but the major triads II and III are both poor -- II has a flat fifth and III has a sharp third. This precludes well-tuned chords along any extensive circle-of-fifths root movement. The system remains 5-limit, so no septimal intervals are available, and seventh chords use the less-consonant 9:5 ratio rather than the septimal 7:4.

# Musical Context
The chromatic just intonation scale represents an attempt to maximize the number of justly tuned chords available on a fixed-pitch instrument. While it succeeds for many common chords, the poorly tuned II and III triads are problematic for standard harmonic progressions (e.g., ii-V-I in jazz). The absence of septimal intervals means that dominant seventh chords (especially V7, tuned as 36:45:54:64) lack the resonance of the septimal seventh chord (4:5:6:7).

# Examples
- Justly tuned major triads: I, IV, V, flat-VI, flat-III (all as 4:5:6)
- Justly tuned minor triads: IIIm, VIm, VIIm (all as 10:12:15)
- Justly tuned minor sevenths: IIIm7, VIm7, Im7, IVm7 (as 10:12:15:18)
- Poorly tuned: II major (flat fifth), III major (sharp third)
- V7 chord: 36:45:54:64 (worse than the already imperfect I7 at 20:25:30:36)

# Common Confusions
- The chromatic extension is not uniquely determined -- the choices above are conventional but other extensions are possible
- "Just intonation" does not mean all chords are just -- some are significantly out of tune
- The absence of 7-limit intervals is a choice inherent to 5-limit tuning, not a limitation that could be fixed within the system
- The V7 chord problem is especially severe because V7 is the most harmonically important seventh chord
