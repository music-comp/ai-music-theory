# Quick Definition

The just intonation chromatic scale extends the 5-limit diatonic scale to all twelve notes by tuning additional triads (flat-VI, flat-III, VI, VIIm) as justly as possible, yielding many just chords but also some poorly tuned ones.

# Core Definition

Wright describes the chromatic extension through four tuning decisions (pp. 155-156): (1) flat-6 and flat-3 are tuned so that flat-VI is justly tuned (flat-6 is a just major third below 8-hat; flat-3 is a just minor third above 1-hat); (2) flat-7 is a just minor third above 5-hat, making flat-III a just major triad; (3) flat-2 = sharp-1 is tuned so that VI is justly tuned; (4) flat-5 = sharp-4 is a just fourth below 7-hat, making VIIm a just minor triad. All intervals remain 5-limit.

# Prerequisites

- **Just intonation scale** -- The diatonic scale being extended

# Key Properties

1. 5-limit tuning throughout (no septimal intervals)
2. Just major triads: I, IV, V, flat-VI, flat-III (all 4:5:6)
3. Just minor triads: IIIm, VIm, VIIm (all 10:12:15)
4. Just minor sevenths: IIIm7, VIm7, Im7, IVm7 (all 10:12:15:18)
5. Poorly tuned: II major (flat fifth), III major (sharp third)
6. V7 tuned as 36:45:54:64 (poorly consonant)
7. No septimal seventh chords available

# Construction / Recognition

1. Start with the just intonation diatonic scale
2. Tune flat-6 = 2/(5/4) = 8/5 (just major third below octave)
3. Tune flat-3 = 6/5 (just minor third above tonic)
4. Tune flat-7 = (3/2)(6/5) = 9/5 (just minor third above 5th)
5. Tune flat-2 so VI triad is just
6. Tune flat-5 = (15/8)/(4/3) = 45/32 (just fourth below 7th)

# Context & Application

The chromatic extension maximizes the number of justly tuned chords available but cannot avoid some poorly tuned triads. The II and III major triads are problematic for standard harmonic progressions (e.g., ii-V-I). The absence of septimal intervals means dominant seventh chords lack the resonance of the 4:5:6:7 chord. This "precludes well-tuned chords along any extensive circle-of-fifths root movement" (p. 156).

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 156): I7 chord tuned as 20:25:30:36 (with 9:5 seventh) -- less consonant than septimal 4:5:6:7.

**Example 2** (p. 156): V7 chord tuned as 36:45:54:64 -- even worse, because 2-hat to 4-hat is not a just minor third.

**Example 3** (p. 156): Im7 and IVm7 are just, but II and III major triads are both poor.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming all chords in the just intonation chromatic scale are justly tuned
  **Correction**: II and III major triads and V7 are poorly tuned

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the chromatic extension is uniquely determined
  **Clarification**: The choices above are conventional; other extensions are possible

- **Confusion**: Assuming the V7 problem can be fixed within 5-limit tuning
  **Clarification**: The V7 is inherently poorly tuned because the interval 2-hat to 4-hat is not a just minor third; this is structural, not a design flaw
