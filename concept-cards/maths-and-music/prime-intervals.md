# Quick Definition
Musical intervals whose frequency ratio is a prime number, serving as the irreducible building blocks from which all rational intervals can be constructed through composition and inversion.

# Formal Definition
A prime interval is an interval whose interval ratio is a prime integer. By the Unique Factorization Theorem, every integer $n \geq 1$ factors uniquely as $n = p_1^{\alpha_1} \cdots p_r^{\alpha_r}$, so every integral interval decomposes uniquely as a composition of prime intervals. More generally, every rational interval (ratio $= m/n \in \mathbb{Q}^+$) can be written as a composition of prime intervals and their opposites (inverses).

# Mathematical Context
The set of prime intervals does NOT form a monoid under composition (the composition of two prime intervals has a composite ratio, e.g., $2 \cdot 3 = 6$). However, the set of rational intervals (ratios in $\mathbb{Q}^+$) forms a group under composition. The unique factorization theorem guarantees that the decomposition of any rational interval into prime intervals and their inverses is unique.

# Musical Context
The musically most important prime intervals are: ratio 2 (octave, 1200 cents), ratio 3 ($\approx$ 1902 cents, octave + fifth), ratio 5 ($\approx$ 2786 cents, two octaves + major third), and ratio 7 ($\approx$ 3369 cents, two octaves + minor seventh). These are the prime intervals underlying just intonation and the harmonic series. The octave (ratio 2) is the most fundamental prime interval.

# Examples
- Ratio 2 (prime): the octave
- Ratio 3 (prime): approximately an octave-and-a-fifth
- Ratio 5 (prime): approximately two octaves and a major third
- Ratio 6 = 2 $\times$ 3 is NOT a prime interval; it decomposes as octave + interval-of-ratio-3
- The just fifth (ratio 3/2) = prime interval 3 composed with the opposite of prime interval 2

# Common Confusions
- "Prime interval" here means an interval with a prime-number ratio, NOT the "prime" form of a twelve-tone row
- Prime intervals do NOT form a group or even a monoid; their compositions have composite ratios
- A "just fifth" (ratio 3/2) is not itself a prime interval, but it decomposes into two prime intervals: ratio 3 up, then ratio 2 down
