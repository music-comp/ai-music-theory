# Quick Definition

An ideal is a subset of a ring closed under addition and under multiplication by any ring element. A principal ideal is one generated by a single element. Every ideal in $\mathbb{Z}$ is principal, making $\mathbb{Z}$ a principal ideal domain.

# Core Definition

"A subset $J \subseteq R$ is called an *ideal* if it is a subgroup of the additive group $(R, +)$ and if whenever $a \in R$ and $d \in J$, then $ad \in J$" (Wright, Ch. 8, p. 101). A *principal ideal* is one of the form $aR = \{ab \mid b \in R\}$ for some generator $a \in R$. Both $\{0\} = 0R$ and $R = 1R$ are principal ideals.

# Prerequisites

- **Ring** -- Ideals are substructures of rings

# Key Properties

1. An ideal $J$ is a subgroup of $(R, +)$
2. An ideal absorbs multiplication: if $a \in R$ and $d \in J$, then $ad \in J$
3. The zero ideal $\{0\}$ and the full ring $R$ are always ideals
4. A principal ideal $aR$ consists of all multiples of $a$
5. A non-zero ideal in $\mathbb{Z}$ has exactly two generators: $n$ and $-n$, hence a unique positive generator

# Construction / Recognition

## To check if J is an ideal of R:
1. Verify $J$ is non-empty and closed under subtraction (subgroup of $(R, +)$)
2. Verify: for all $a \in R$ and $d \in J$, the product $ad \in J$

## To find the generator of an ideal in $\mathbb{Z}$:
1. If $J = \{0\}$, the generator is $0$
2. Otherwise, find the smallest positive integer $n \in J$
3. $J = n\mathbb{Z}$ (all multiples of $n$)

# Context & Application

The ideal structure of $\mathbb{Z}$ is the algebraic foundation for the greatest common divisor: $\gcd(m, n)$ is the positive generator of the ideal $m\mathbb{Z} + n\mathbb{Z}$. This connects to music through the theory of generating intervals and polyrhythmic patterns.

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 101): The even integers $2\mathbb{Z} = \{\ldots, -4, -2, 0, 2, 4, \ldots\}$ form a principal ideal in $\mathbb{Z}$.

**Example 2** (p. 103): $12\mathbb{Z} + 15\mathbb{Z} = 3\mathbb{Z}$ since $\gcd(12, 15) = 3$.

**Example 3** (p. 103): $5\mathbb{Z} + (-20)\mathbb{Z} = 5\mathbb{Z}$ since $\gcd(5, 20) = 5$.

**Example 4** (p. 101): The only ideals in $\mathbb{Q}$ are $\{0\}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ itself.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Confusing ideals with subrings
  **Correction**: An ideal must absorb multiplication by any ring element, not just by elements of itself. Every ideal is a subring, but not every subring is an ideal.

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking every ring has the property that all ideals are principal
  **Clarification**: This is a special property of $\mathbb{Z}$ (and other PIDs); polynomial rings in two or more variables typically have non-principal ideals

- **Confusion**: Thinking a non-zero ideal in $\mathbb{Z}$ has many generators
  **Clarification**: It has exactly two generators: $n$ and $-n$, giving a unique positive generator
