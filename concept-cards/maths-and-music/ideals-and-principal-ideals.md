# Quick Definition
An ideal is a subset of a ring closed under addition and multiplication by any ring element; a principal ideal is one generated by a single element. Every ideal in $\mathbb{Z}$ is principal.

# Formal Definition
A subset $J \subseteq R$ of a ring is an ideal if: (1) $J$ is a subgroup of $(R, +)$, and (2) whenever $a \in R$ and $d \in J$, then $ad \in J$. A principal ideal is one of the form $aR = \{ab \mid b \in R\}$ for some $a \in R$. The element $a$ is called a generator of the ideal. The zero ideal $\{0\} = 0R$ and the full ring $R = 1R$ are principal ideals. If every ideal in an integral domain is principal, it is called a principal ideal domain (PID).

# Mathematical Context
**Theorem:** $\mathbb{Z}$ is a principal ideal domain. Proof: Let $J$ be a non-zero ideal. Since $J$ contains a positive integer (if $n \in J$, then $-n \in J$), let $n$ be the smallest positive integer in $J$ (by WOP). For any $m \in J$, write $m = qn + r$ with $0 \leq r < n$. Then $r = m - qn \in J$, so by minimality of $n$, $r = 0$. Thus $J = n\mathbb{Z}$. A non-zero ideal has a unique positive generator (since generators $n$ and $-n$ are the only generators).

# Musical Context
The ideal structure of $\mathbb{Z}$ is the algebraic foundation for the greatest common divisor: $\gcd(m, n)$ is the positive generator of the ideal $m\mathbb{Z} + n\mathbb{Z}$. This connects to music through the theory of generating intervals: the gcd determines which intervals generate all chromatic note classes and which polyrhythmic patterns complete their cycles.

# Examples
- The even integers $2\mathbb{Z} = \{\ldots, -4, -2, 0, 2, 4, \ldots\}$ form a principal ideal in $\mathbb{Z}$
- $12\mathbb{Z} + 15\mathbb{Z} = 3\mathbb{Z}$ (since $\gcd(12, 15) = 3$)
- $5\mathbb{Z} + (-20)\mathbb{Z} = 5\mathbb{Z}$ (since $\gcd(5, 20) = 5$)
- The only ideals in $\mathbb{Q}$ are $\{0\}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ itself

# Common Confusions
- An ideal is NOT the same as a subring; an ideal must absorb multiplication by any ring element, not just by elements of itself
- Every ideal in $\mathbb{Z}$ is principal, but this is a special property of $\mathbb{Z}$; not all rings have this property
- A non-zero ideal in $\mathbb{Z}$ has exactly two generators: $n$ and $-n$; the unique POSITIVE generator is $n$
