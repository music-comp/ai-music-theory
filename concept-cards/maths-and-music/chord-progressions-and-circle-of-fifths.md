# Quick Definition
A chord progression is the procedure from one chord to the next in time. The circle of fifths is a cyclic arrangement of all twelve chromatic pitch classes by intervals of a fifth, and counter-clockwise (up a fourth) movement around it is a fundamental classical progression pattern.

# Formal Definition
A progression is the movement from one chord to the next in a harmonic sequence. The circle of fifths arranges the twelve chromatic pitch classes so that each adjacent pair is separated by a fifth (7 semitones clockwise, 5 semitones counter-clockwise). Since gcd(7, 12) = 1, the cycle visits all twelve pitch classes before returning to the start. The classical circle-of-fifths progression moves counter-clockwise: each chord's root is a fourth (5 semitones) above the preceding chord's root.

# Mathematical Context
The circle of fifths is an isomorphism between Z_12 under addition and the cyclic group generated by 7 (mod 12). Since 7 is coprime to 12, repeated addition of 7 generates all of Z_12, ensuring every chromatic pitch class occupies exactly one position on the circle. Counter-clockwise motion corresponds to adding 5 (mod 12), which is the additive inverse of 7 in Z_12 (since 7 + 5 = 12 ≡ 0).

# Musical Context
The circle-of-fifths progression is one of the most common harmonic patterns in Western music. A classical example is VI7 -> IIm -> V7 -> I, where each root moves counter-clockwise (up a fourth). The progression creates a strong sense of harmonic momentum toward the tonic. Many familiar harmonic patterns follow portions of this circle. The half-diminished seventh chord also often resolves around the circle of fifths.

# Examples
- Classical circle-of-fifths progression: VI7 -> IIm -> V7 -> I
- Clockwise on the circle: each step goes up a fifth (or down a fourth)
- Counter-clockwise: each step goes up a fourth (or down a fifth)
- In the key of C: Am7 -> Dm -> G7 -> C follows this pattern

# Common Confusions
- Clockwise motion on the circle goes up a fifth; counter-clockwise goes up a fourth -- the classical progression moves counter-clockwise
- The circle of fifths is about pitch classes, not specific pitches; it works modulo octave
- Not all progressions follow the circle of fifths; it is a common pattern, not a rule
