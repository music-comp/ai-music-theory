# Quick Definition
A rhythmic convention, common in jazz, where pairs of eighth notes are performed as if the first were two-thirds and the second one-third of a beat (triplet feel), though notated simply as even eighth notes.

# Formal Definition
Swing rhythm occurs when the figure consisting of an eighth-note triplet with the first two notes tied is pervasive in a piece. Rather than writing the cumbersome triplet notation throughout, the figure is simply denoted by two eighth notes. This is indicated by the words "swing rhythm" or a marking showing even eighth notes equaling a tied-triplet-eighth-plus-triplet-eighth pattern. The rhythm can be notated precisely using compound time signatures $\frac{3n}{8}$, writing a quarter note followed by an eighth note.

# Mathematical Context
In swing, the beat is divided into the ratio $2:1$ rather than the standard $1:1$. Using triplet subdivision, the first "eighth note" gets $\frac{2}{3}$ of the beat and the second gets $\frac{1}{3}$. This is equivalent to a compound time feel where each beat divides into three equal parts. The notation introduces a discrepancy between written and performed durations -- a notational convention (shorthand) rather than a literal duration specification.

# Musical Context
Swing is the defining rhythmic characteristic of jazz from the 1920s onward. The "swing feel" creates a lilting, forward-moving groove distinct from straight eighth notes. The degree of swing can vary: some performers play nearly even eighth notes ("light swing") while others exaggerate the $2:1$ ratio. Swing rhythm illustrates that musical notation is sometimes a convention rather than a precise specification.

# Examples
- Two written eighth notes in swing: performed as a quarter-note triplet tied to an eighth-note triplet (duration ratio $2:1$)
- A piece marked "swing" in $\frac{4}{4}$ time sounds equivalent to $\frac{12}{8}$ time with quarter-eighth patterns
- Jazz standards like those of the Great American Songbook typically use swing feel
- The same melody played straight vs. swing has a fundamentally different character

# Common Confusions
- Written eighth notes in swing are NOT performed as equal durations -- the notation is a shorthand
- Swing is not the same as dotted-eighth + sixteenth ($3:1$ ratio); swing is approximately $2:1$
- Not all jazz uses swing; bossa nova, jazz-rock fusion, and some modern jazz use straight eighth notes
- The compound time signature $\frac{12}{8}$ can precisely notate swing, but convention favors $\frac{4}{4}$ with a swing marking
