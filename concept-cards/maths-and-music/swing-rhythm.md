# Quick Definition

A rhythmic convention, common in jazz, where pairs of eighth notes are performed as if the first were two-thirds and the second one-third of a beat (triplet feel), though notated simply as even eighth notes.

# Core Definition

Swing rhythm occurs when the figure consisting of an eighth-note triplet with the first two notes tied is pervasive in a piece. Rather than writing the cumbersome triplet notation throughout, the figure is simply denoted by two eighth notes. This is indicated by the words "swing rhythm" or a marking showing even eighths equaling a tied-triplet pattern. The rhythm can be notated precisely using compound time signatures $\frac{3n}{8}$, writing a quarter note followed by an eighth note (Wright, pp. 36-37).

# Key Properties

1. The beat divides in ratio $2:1$ rather than $1:1$
2. The first "eighth note" gets $\frac{2}{3}$ of the beat, the second gets $\frac{1}{3}$
3. The notation is a shorthand — written rhythm differs from performed rhythm
4. Equivalent to compound time feel where each beat divides into three parts
5. Can be notated precisely in $\frac{3n}{8}$ compound time

# Construction / Recognition

## To interpret swing rhythm:

1. Look for the "swing" marking or equivalent notation at the beginning of the piece
2. Read pairs of eighth notes as if the first = $\frac{2}{3}$ beat and second = $\frac{1}{3}$ beat
3. Equivalently, hear each beat as divided into triplets with the first two tied

# Context & Application

Swing is the defining rhythmic characteristic of jazz from the 1920s onward. The degree of swing can vary: some performers play nearly even eighth notes ("light swing") while others exaggerate the $2:1$ ratio. Swing rhythm illustrates that musical notation is sometimes a convention rather than a precise specification.

# Examples

- Two written eighth notes in swing: performed as a quarter-note triplet tied to an eighth-note triplet (ratio $2:1$) (p. 37)
- A piece marked "swing" in $\frac{4}{4}$ time sounds equivalent to $\frac{12}{8}$ time with quarter-eighth patterns
- The compound time signature $\frac{12}{8}$ can precisely notate what swing notation abbreviates

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Performing written eighth notes in swing as equal durations
  **Correction**: In swing, the first eighth gets $\frac{2}{3}$ of the beat, the second gets $\frac{1}{3}$

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking swing is the same as dotted-eighth + sixteenth ($3:1$ ratio)
  **Clarification**: Swing is approximately $2:1$, not $3:1$
- **Confusion**: Assuming all jazz uses swing
  **Clarification**: Bossa nova, jazz-rock fusion, and some modern jazz use straight eighth notes
- **Confusion**: Thinking $\frac{12}{8}$ and "swing in $\frac{4}{4}$" are identical
  **Clarification**: $\frac{12}{8}$ can precisely notate swing, but convention favors $\frac{4}{4}$ with a swing marking
