# Quick Definition
The Pythagorean scale is a 3-limit tuning system that tunes all diatonic intervals using only the primes 2 and 3, achieved by making all fifths between diatonic notes just fifths (ratio 3:2).

# Formal Definition
The Pythagorean scale uses 3-limit tuning: all interval ratios between scale tones have the form 2^a * 3^b for integers a, b. It is constructed by tuning each interval in the sequence of fifths 4-hat -> 1-hat -> 5-hat -> 2-hat -> 6-hat -> 3-hat -> 7-hat to be 3:2 (a just fifth). The diatonic scale ratios to the tonic are:

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1/1 | 9/8 | 81/64 | 4/3 | 3/2 | 27/16 | 243/128 | 2/1 |

# Mathematical Context
All five whole-step intervals (1-2, 2-3, 4-5, 5-6, 6-7) are the greater whole tone (9/8), while both half-step intervals (3-4, 7-8) are the Pythagorean hemitone (256/243 = 2^8/3^5). The scale extends to a chromatic scale by continuing just fifths around the circle, but the comma of Pythagoras (3^12/2^19 ~ 23.46 cents) prevents closing the circle. A "small fifth" must be placed somewhere, often between scale degree 7 and flat-5 or between flat-5 and flat-2.

# Musical Context
Named after Pythagoras (c. 540-510 BC), who considered the 3:2 fifth a symbol of universal perfection. The Pythagorean scale gives excellent fifths (just, at 3:2) almost everywhere, but its thirds are poor: the major third at 81/64 is sharp of the just major third by the comma of Didymus (~22 cents), producing audible dissonance in major triads. This system was adequate for medieval music based on parallel fifths but became unacceptable as thirds gained importance in the 14th-15th centuries.

# Examples
- Scale tone 2 = two just fifths minus one octave: (3/2)^2 / 2 = 9/8
- Scale tone 3 = four just fifths minus two octaves: (3/2)^4 / 4 = 81/64
- Pythagorean hemitone: 256/243 = 2^8/3^5 ~ 90.22 cents
- Adjacent intervals: 9:8, 9:8, 256:243, 9:8, 9:8, 9:8, 256:243
- The comma must be placed on the circle of fifths, creating one "wolf" fifth

# Common Confusions
- The Pythagorean scale has only one size of whole tone (9/8), unlike just intonation which has two (9/8 and 10/9)
- The Pythagorean hemitone (256/243) is not the same as the just semitone (16/15) -- they differ by about 22 cents
- The comma of Pythagoras is accommodated by one "bad" fifth, not spread across all fifths (that would be equal temperament)
- Pythagorean tuning is 3-limit, meaning it has no 5-limit intervals at all -- it cannot produce a just major third
