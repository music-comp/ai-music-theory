# Quick Definition
The just intonation scale is a 5-limit tuning system that tunes the diatonic scale so that the three primary major triads (I, IV, V) are all justly tuned as 4:5:6.

# Formal Definition
The just intonation scale employs 5-limit tuning such that the diatonic major triads I, IV, and V are justly tuned in root position within an octave as 4:5:6. Since each diatonic note appears in at least one of these three chords, this requirement uniquely determines the tuning. The resulting scale ratios to the tonic are:

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1/1 | 9/8 | 5/4 | 4/3 | 3/2 | 5/3 | 15/8 | 2/1 |

# Mathematical Context
The adjacent intervals alternate between three sizes: greater whole tone (9/8) at 1-2, 4-5, 6-7; lesser whole tone (10/9) at 2-3, 5-6; and just semitone (16/15) at 3-4, 7-8. The pattern is: 9:8, 10:9, 16:15, 9:8, 10:9, 9:8, 16:15. Unlike the Pythagorean scale, this scale has two different whole-tone sizes, introducing the comma of Didymus (81/80) as the ratio between them.

# Musical Context
The just intonation scale gives justly tuned major triads I, IV, and V, plus justly tuned minor triads IIIm and VIm, and justly tuned minor sevenths IIIm7 and VIm7. The chromatic extension tunes flat-6 and flat-3 to make flat-VI just, flat-7 for just flat-III, and so on. However, major triads II and III are poorly tuned (II has a flat fifth, III has a sharp third), precluding well-tuned chords in extended circle-of-fifths progressions. The system is inherently key-dependent.

# Examples
- I chord (C-E-G): 4:5:6 (perfectly just)
- IV chord (F-A-C): 4:5:6 (perfectly just)
- V chord (G-B-D): 4:5:6 (perfectly just)
- IIIm chord (E-G-B): 10:12:15 (perfectly just minor triad)
- II chord (D-F#-A): poorly tuned (flat fifth)
- V7 chord: 36:45:54:64 (poorly consonant -- worse than the just I7 at 20:25:30:36)
- No septimal intervals available (5-limit only)

# Common Confusions
- Just intonation does not mean "all intervals are just" -- only certain chords are perfectly tuned, while others (II, III) are noticeably out of tune
- The scale has two different whole-tone sizes (9/8 and 10/9), which means transposing a melody can change its interval structure
- Just intonation is key-dependent: a keyboard tuned to just intonation in C gives poor results in other keys
- The V7 chord in just intonation is not well-tuned (36:45:54:64), which is a serious limitation for tonal harmony
