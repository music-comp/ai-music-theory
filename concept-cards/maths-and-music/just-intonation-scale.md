# Quick Definition

The just intonation scale is a 5-limit tuning system that tunes the diatonic scale so that the three primary major triads (I, IV, V) are all justly tuned as 4:5:6.

# Core Definition

Wright defines: "This scale employs 5-limit tuning in such a way that the diatonic major triads I, IV, and V are justly tuned, meaning that when these chords are voiced within an octave in root position, the ratios of root, third, and fifth are 4:5:6" (p. 155). Since each diatonic note appears in at least one of these three chords, this uniquely determines the tuning. The resulting scale ratios are:

| 1-hat | 2-hat | 3-hat | 4-hat | 5-hat | 6-hat | 7-hat | 8-hat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1/1 | 9/8 | 5/4 | 4/3 | 3/2 | 5/3 | 15/8 | 2/1 |

# Prerequisites

- **p-limit tuning** -- The just intonation scale is 5-limit
- **Just fifth** -- The ratio 3/2 appears as the fifth scale degree
- **Just major third** -- The ratio 5/4 appears as the third scale degree
- **Greater whole tone** -- One of two whole-step sizes in this scale
- **Lesser whole tone** -- The other whole-step size
- **Just semitone** -- The half-step size in this scale

# Key Properties

1. 5-limit tuning: all ratios involve only primes 2, 3, and 5
2. I, IV, and V major triads are exactly 4:5:6 (justly tuned)
3. IIIm and VIm minor triads are exactly 10:12:15 (justly tuned)
4. Two sizes of whole tone: greater (9/8) at 1-2, 4-5, 6-7; lesser (10/9) at 2-3, 5-6
5. One size of half step: just semitone (16/15) at 3-4 and 7-8
6. Adjacent intervals: 9:8, 10:9, 16:15, 9:8, 10:9, 9:8, 16:15
7. II and III major triads are poorly tuned (II has flat fifth, III has sharp third)

# Construction / Recognition

## To Build a Just Intonation Scale

1. Require I triad as 4:5:6: this gives 3-hat = 5/4 and 5-hat = 3/2
2. Require V triad as 4:5:6: 5-hat to 7-hat = 5/4, so 7-hat = 3/2 * 5/4 = 15/8; 5-hat to 2-hat = 3/4 (down a fourth), so 2-hat = 3/2 * 3/4 = 9/8
3. Require IV triad as 4:5:6: this gives 4-hat = 4/3 and 6-hat = 5/3
4. Result: 1/1, 9/8, 5/4, 4/3, 3/2, 5/3, 15/8, 2/1

# Context & Application

The just intonation scale provides perfectly consonant I, IV, and V triads plus IIIm and VIm minor triads. However, the II and III major triads are poorly tuned, precluding well-tuned chords in extended circle-of-fifths progressions. The V7 chord is tuned as 36:45:54:64 -- poorly consonant. Since the scale is 5-limit, no septimal intervals are available. The system is inherently key-dependent: a keyboard tuned to just intonation in C gives poor results in other keys.

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 155): I chord (C-E-G) as 4:5:6, with 1-hat to 3-hat = 5/4 and 1-hat to 5-hat = 3/2.

**Example 2** (p. 156): Adjacent intervals: 9:8, 10:9, 16:15, 9:8, 10:9, 9:8, 16:15.

**Example 3** (p. 156): IIIm and VIm are justly tuned as 10:12:15; IIIm7 and VIm7 as 10:12:15:18.

**Example 4** (p. 156): V7 chord in just intonation: 36:45:54:64, because 2-hat to 4-hat is not a just minor third.

# Relationships

## Builds Upon
- **p-limit tuning** -- The just intonation scale is the paradigmatic 5-limit system
- **Just fifth** and **Just major third** -- The fundamental intervals that define the scale

## Enables
- **Just intonation chromatic scale** -- Extended to twelve notes by tuning additional triads

## Related
- **Justly tuned chords** -- The scale is designed to produce justly tuned I, IV, V triads
- **Comma of Didymus** -- The scale introduces two whole-tone sizes differing by this comma

## Contrasts With
- **Pythagorean scale** -- 3-limit; one whole-tone size; just fifths but poor thirds
- **Mean-tone scale** -- Achieves just thirds via irrational fifths; one whole-tone size

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming "just intonation" means all intervals are just
  **Correction**: Only certain chords are perfectly tuned; others (II, III) are noticeably out of tune

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the just intonation scale has only one whole-tone size
  **Clarification**: It has two: greater (9/8 ~ 204 cents) and lesser (10/9 ~ 182 cents); transposing a melody can change its interval structure

- **Confusion**: Assuming just intonation works equally well in all keys
  **Clarification**: Just intonation is key-dependent; a keyboard tuned to just intonation in C gives poor results in D

- **Confusion**: Thinking the V7 chord is well-tuned in just intonation
  **Clarification**: V7 is tuned as 36:45:54:64, which is poorly consonant -- a serious limitation
