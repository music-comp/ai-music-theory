# Quick Definition
The mean-tone whole tone has ratio sqrt(5)/2, approximately 193.16 cents, equal to two mean-tone fifths minus one octave, about 7 cents flat of the equal-tempered whole step.

# Formal Definition
The mean-tone whole tone is the interval with ratio (5^(1/4))^2 / 2 = 5^(1/2) / 2 = sqrt(5)/2, obtained by iterating two mean-tone fifths and subtracting one octave. It is an irrational interval. Measured in cents: 1200 * log2(sqrt(5)/2) ~ 193.16. The mean-tone whole tone lies between the lesser whole tone (10/9 ~ 182.40 cents) and the greater whole tone (9/8 ~ 203.91 cents), closer to the geometric mean.

# Mathematical Context
The ratio sqrt(5)/2 ~ 1.11803 is the geometric mean of the two just whole tones in a specific sense: the mean-tone system unifies the two different whole-tone sizes of just intonation into a single whole-tone size. Two mean-tone whole tones equal one just major third: (sqrt(5)/2)^2 = 5/4. This contrasts with the Pythagorean scale (where two greater whole tones give 81/64) and just intonation (where one greater plus one lesser whole tone gives 5/4).

# Musical Context
With the wolf fifth placed away from diatonic positions, each diatonic whole step in the mean-tone scale is equal to the mean-tone whole tone. This uniformity of whole steps (unlike just intonation's two sizes) simplifies melodic relationships while maintaining just major thirds. At ~193 cents, it is about 7 cents flat of the tempered step, a difference that is subtle but contributes to the distinctive character of mean-tone tuning.

# Examples
- sqrt(5)/2 ~ 1.11803
- 1200 * log2(sqrt(5)/2) ~ 193.16 cents (tempered step = 200 cents)
- Two mean-tone whole tones = just major third: (sqrt(5)/2)^2 = 5/4
- Compare: greater whole tone (9/8 ~ 204 cents), lesser whole tone (10/9 ~ 182 cents)
- Mean-tone whole tone lies roughly between the two just whole tones

# Common Confusions
- The mean-tone whole tone is not the arithmetic mean of the two just whole tones -- it is determined by the requirement that two of them equal a just major third
- It is irrational (sqrt(5)/2), unlike the greater (9/8) and lesser (10/9) whole tones which are rational
- All diatonic whole steps in the mean-tone scale are the same size, unlike just intonation which has two sizes
