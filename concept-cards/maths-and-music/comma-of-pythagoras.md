# Quick Definition
The comma of Pythagoras is the small interval 3^12/2^19 (= 531441/524288), approximately 23.46 cents, representing the discrepancy between twelve just fifths and seven octaves.

# Formal Definition
The comma of Pythagoras is the rational interval corresponding to the ratio (3/2)^12 / 2^7 = 3^12 / 2^19 = 531441/524288 in Q+. It measures the amount by which twelve consecutive just fifths exceed seven octaves. Since (3/2)^12 = 531441/4096 ~ 129.75 and 2^7 = 128, the overshoot is 531441/524288 ~ 1.01364. Measured in cents: 1200 * log2(3^12/2^19) ~ 23.46.

# Mathematical Context
The comma of Pythagoras demonstrates that the circle of fifths cannot be closed using just fifths. If we plot twelve just fifths on a circle representing seven octaves, we overshoot the starting point by the comma (~23.46 cents). This is a consequence of the fact that no power of 3 equals any power of 2 -- a direct corollary of unique prime factorization. The comma is approximately a quarter of a semitone, "not quite negligible."

# Musical Context
Pythagoras (c. 540-510 BC) discovered this discrepancy and found it greatly disturbing, since it meant that the perfection of the 3:2 fifth could not be maintained uniformly around the circle of fifths. Any tuning system based on just fifths must accommodate this comma somewhere. In the Pythagorean scale, the comma is placed as a "small fifth" between two adjacent positions on the circle (often between scale degree 7 and flat-5, or between flat-5 and flat-2). This is the fundamental reason why perfect tuning in all keys is impossible.

# Examples
- (3/2)^12 = 531441/4096 ~ 129.75 versus 2^7 = 128
- 531441/524288 ~ 1.01364
- 1200 * log2(3^12/2^19) ~ 23.46 cents (roughly a quarter of a semitone)
- The tempered fifth (700 cents) distributes this comma equally: 12 * 700 = 8400 = 7 * 1200 exactly
- Just fifths: 12 * 701.96 = 8423.46, overshooting 8400 by ~23.46 cents

# Common Confusions
- The comma of Pythagoras (~23.46 cents) is not the same as the comma of Didymus (~21.51 cents) -- though similar in size, they measure different tuning discrepancies
- The comma does not mean Pythagorean tuning is "wrong" -- it means that just fifths are mathematically incompatible with octave closure
- Equal temperament "solves" the comma by making each fifth slightly flat (by 1/12 of the comma ~ 1.96 cents), distributing the error evenly
