# Quick Definition

The Pythagorean chromatic scale extends the Pythagorean diatonic scale to all twelve notes by continuing to tune just fifths around the circle, but must accommodate the comma of Pythagoras by placing one "small" fifth somewhere on the circle.

# Core Definition

Wright explains: "The Pythagorean scale can be extended to a chromatic scale by continuing to tune just fifths around the circle of fifths, but, as we have seen, the comma of Pythagoras prevents us from completing the circle using only just fifths. The comma is accommodated by allowing a 'small' fifth between some two adjacent positions in the circle. This is often placed at one of the bottom clock positions, either between 7-hat and flat-5 or between flat-5 and flat-2" (p. 154).

# Prerequisites

- **Pythagorean scale** -- The diatonic scale that is being extended
- **Comma of Pythagoras** -- The discrepancy that must be accommodated

# Key Properties

1. Eleven of twelve fifths are exact just fifths (3:2 ~ 701.96 cents)
2. One "small" fifth absorbs the entire comma of Pythagoras (~23.46 cents)
3. The small fifth measures approximately 678.49 cents
4. All intervals remain 3-limit (ratios of form 2^a * 3^b)
5. All major thirds are 81/64 (~22 cents sharp of just)
6. Comma placement is a design choice affecting which keys sound best

# Construction / Recognition

1. Start with the seven diatonic notes tuned as the Pythagorean scale
2. Continue tuning just fifths clockwise around the circle of fifths
3. At the 12th position, the comma of Pythagoras prevents closure
4. Place the "small" fifth at a musically inconvenient position (e.g., between flat-5 and flat-2)
5. Result: 11 just fifths + 1 small fifth = exactly 7 octaves

# Context & Application

The Pythagorean chromatic scale was adequate for medieval music based primarily on fifths and fourths. Its significant weakness is the poor representation of the major third as 81/64, which is even sharper than the tempered major third and sharp of the just major third by the comma of Didymus. Wright notes: "The dissonance heard in the major triads I, IV, and V when played in Pythagorean tuning render this system unacceptable for music in which a high level of consonance is desired" (p. 154).

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 154): Eleven just fifths (701.96 cents each) plus one small fifth (~678.49 cents) = 8400 cents = 7 octaves.

**Example 2** (p. 154): Comma typically placed between flat-5 and flat-2 on the circle.

**Example 3** (Exercise 8, p. 160): Some major triads with thirds crossing the comma have slightly better thirds.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Thinking the comma can be eliminated by clever placement
  **Correction**: The comma must go somewhere; placement only determines which keys are most affected

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the Pythagorean chromatic scale has no just major thirds at all
  **Clarification**: It has no just major thirds by design (3-limit excludes the prime 5), though some thirds crossing the comma are slightly improved (see Exercise 8)
