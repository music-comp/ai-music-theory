# Quick Definition
The Pythagorean chromatic scale extends the Pythagorean diatonic scale to all twelve notes by continuing to tune just fifths around the circle, but must accommodate the comma of Pythagoras by placing one "small" fifth somewhere on the circle.

# Formal Definition
The Pythagorean chromatic scale extends the 3-limit diatonic scale by continuing to tune just fifths (3:2) around the remaining positions on the circle of fifths. Since twelve just fifths overshoot seven octaves by the comma of Pythagoras (3^12/2^19 ~ 23.46 cents), one position must have a fifth that is flat of just by this comma. This "small fifth" is often placed between scale degree 7 and flat-5, or between flat-5 and flat-2 -- positions that are musically less prominent.

# Mathematical Context
With the comma placed between flat-5 and flat-2, eleven of the twelve fifths are exact just fifths (3:2), while the remaining one is 3/2 * 2^19/3^12 = 2^18/3^11 ~ 678.49 cents, about 23.46 cents flat of a just fifth. All intervals remain 3-limit (ratios of form 2^a * 3^b). The chromatic scale inherits the Pythagorean diatonic scale's weakness in thirds: all major thirds are 81/64, about 22 cents sharp of the just major third.

# Musical Context
The Pythagorean chromatic scale was adequate for medieval music based primarily on fifths and fourths. The dissonance of the Pythagorean major third (81/64) in the I, IV, and V triads renders this system unsatisfactory for music requiring consonant thirds. Interestingly, major triads whose third lies across the comma from the root and fifth have slightly better thirds (an exercise explores this). The placement of the comma is a design choice that affects which keys sound best.

# Examples
- Eleven just fifths (3:2 ~ 701.96 cents each) plus one small fifth (~678.49 cents)
- Total: 11 * 701.96 + 678.49 ~ 8400 = 7 * 1200 (exactly seven octaves)
- Comma typically placed at 7-to-flat-5 or flat-5-to-flat-2 on the circle
- All major thirds: 81/64 ~ 407.82 cents (~22 cents sharp of just)

# Common Confusions
- The comma must go somewhere -- it cannot be eliminated, only placed strategically
- The "small fifth" is still a usable interval (~678 cents), but noticeably worse than the other eleven fifths
- The Pythagorean chromatic scale differs from the just intonation chromatic scale in that it is 3-limit (no prime 5), so it has no just major thirds at all
