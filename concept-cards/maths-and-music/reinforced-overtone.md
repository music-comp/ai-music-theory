# Quick Definition

A reinforced overtone is a pitch that appears in the overtone series of two or more notes in a chord simultaneously. This reinforcement can make the overtone audible as a distinct pitch, causing the chord to "ring."

# Core Definition

"Another situation where overtones can become audible occurs when a certain pitch appears as a *reinforced overtone*, i.e., is an overtone of two or more notes in a well-tuned chord" (Wright, Ch. 10, p. 128). The coincidence of the overtone in multiple harmonic series amplifies its audibility.

# Prerequisites

- **Harmonics and Overtones** -- Understanding what overtones are
- **Overtone Series** -- The sequence of harmonics from each note

# Key Properties

1. A pitch $p = k_1 F_1 = k_2 F_2$ appears in the overtone series of both $F_1$ and $F_2$
2. The reinforcement makes the overtone more likely to be perceived as a distinct pitch
3. Audibility is enhanced if the overtone falls within a formant
4. The effect is strongest when the chord is well-tuned (close to just intonation)

# Construction / Recognition

## To identify reinforced overtones in a chord:
1. List the fundamentals of all chord tones
2. For each pair, find common multiples of their frequencies
3. The lowest common multiples are the most audible reinforced overtones
4. Check if any fall within formant ranges of the instrument/voice

# Context & Application

When a reinforced overtone is audible, a cappella singers describe the chord as "ringing." The phenomenon is most noticeable in well-tuned chords in resonant spaces (churches, cathedrals).

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 128): Root $C_3$ (frequency $F$) and fifth $G_3$ (frequency $\approx 3F/2$). The pitch $G_4$ (frequency $3F$) is the 3rd harmonic of $C_3$ and the 2nd harmonic of $G_3$. This reinforcement makes $G_4$ sometimes audible as a distinct pitch.

**Example 2** (p. 128): "Singers of a cappella music often say the chord 'rings' when this phenomenon is experienced."

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Thinking reinforced overtones are separately played notes
  **Correction**: They emerge from the overtone series of the notes being sounded, not from an additional source

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Confusing reinforced overtones with combination tones (difference tones)
  **Clarification**: These arise from different mechanisms; reinforced overtones are actual harmonics present in multiple series, while combination tones arise from nonlinear interactions

- **Confusion**: Expecting reinforcement in equal temperament to be as strong as in just intonation
  **Clarification**: Reinforcement requires the overtone frequencies to be close to identical, which is better achieved in just intonation (off by ~2 cents for the fifth)
