# Quick Definition
Chord labeling identifies a chord by its root (either a note class name or a Roman numeral scale degree) followed by a suffix indicating the chord type.

# Formal Definition
A chord label consists of two parts: (1) the root identifier, which is either a specific note class (e.g., D, Bb) or a scale tone indicated by Roman numeral (possibly preceded by # or b, e.g., III, bVI), and (2) a suffix indicating chord type. The standard suffixes are:
- Major triad: no suffix
- Minor triad: m
- Augmented: aug or +
- Diminished: dim or ^0
- Seventh: ^7
- Minor seventh: m^7
- Major seventh: M^7
- Diminished seventh: ^o7
- Half-diminished seventh: ^ø7

# Mathematical Context
Chord labeling combines two pieces of information: a position in Z_12 (the root) and a chord type (determined by the interval sequence). Roman numeral labeling further relates the root to a position in the diatonic scale of the ambient key, incorporating the mode. The label is well-defined for chords with discernible roots; for symmetric chords (augmented, diminished seventh), the root is assigned by convention.

# Musical Context
Two parallel labeling systems are in common use. Note-class labeling (e.g., G7, F#m) identifies chords absolutely. Roman numeral labeling (e.g., V7, IIm) identifies chords relative to a key, making it easy to see harmonic function and to transpose. An alternate system uses uppercase/lowercase to indicate major/minor thirds (e.g., iv for a minor triad on scale degree 4, ii^0 for diminished on scale degree 2).

# Examples
- C major triad: labeled "C" (no suffix) or "V" in the key of F major, or "III" in A minor
- F#m7: minor seventh chord rooted on F#; in D major it would be IIIm7; in G minor it would be #VIIm7
- Bb aug: augmented triad with Bb as declared root
- The alternate system writes iv for a minor triad on scale degree 4

# Common Confusions
- No suffix means major triad, not "no chord type"
- Roman numeral labeling requires knowing the mode/key
- For symmetric chords, the label depends on voicing or spelling, not on inherent structure
- The alternate labeling system using uppercase/lowercase is common but not universal
