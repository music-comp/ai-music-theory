# Quick Definition
Chords can be identified by sequences of integer ratios. Sets of adjacent notes in the overtone series approximate familiar chord types, revealing the mathematical basis of harmony.

# Formal Definition
A chord expressed as integer ratios n1:n2:n3:... specifies the frequency relationships between its notes. The keyboard approximations of consecutive integers in the overtone series produce recognizable chord structures from Chapter 3 of the text.

# Mathematical Context
The integer ratio representation of chords is related to the overtone series. For a fundamental F, the sequence of pitches F, 2F, 3F, 4F, 5F, ... produces ratios 1:2:3:4:5:... Any subset of consecutive terms gives a chord whose quality depends on the specific integers involved.

# Musical Context
The exercises in Chapter 9 ask students to identify chords from integer ratio sequences, connecting abstract number theory to concrete harmonic structures. For example, 2:3:4:5 approximates a major chord with an added octave, and 4:5:6 gives a just-intoned major triad.

# Examples
From the chapter exercises:
- 2:3:4:5 -- starting from a note below the chord, this approximates a major triad with octave doubling
- 5:6:7:8 -- involves the problematic ratio 7, producing an unusual chord
- 10:12:15:18 -- can be analyzed by reducing ratios between adjacent notes

# Common Confusions
Integer ratio chords represent just intonation, not equal temperament. The chord 4:5:6 is a pure major triad with a 5:4 major third (386 cents), which differs from the tempered major third (400 cents) by 14 cents.
