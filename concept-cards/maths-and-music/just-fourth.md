# Quick Definition
The just fourth is the interval with frequency ratio 4/3, approximately 498.04 cents, about 2 cents flat of the equal-tempered fourth.

# Formal Definition
The just fourth is the rational interval corresponding to the ratio 4/3 = 2^2/3 in Q+. It is the complement of the just fifth within the octave: 4/3 * 3/2 = 2. Equivalently, it is one octave minus a just fifth. Its prime factorization is 2^2 * 3^(-1). Measured in cents: 1200 * log2(4/3) ~ 498.04.

# Mathematical Context
The ratio 4/3 is the multiplicative inverse of 3/2 modulo the octave (i.e., within the quotient group R+/<2>). As a 3-limit interval, it involves only the primes 2 and 3. The complementary relationship 4/3 * 3/2 = 2 exemplifies the general principle that an interval and its octave complement multiply to give the octave ratio.

# Musical Context
The just fourth is one of the most consonant intervals, historically grouped with the octave and fifth as a "perfect" consonance. It was one of the earliest intervals used in Western harmony (parallel fourths were common in medieval music). The just fourth appears in the just intonation scale as the ratio of scale tone 4 to scale tone 1, and is used to define the tuning of scale tone 4 in both the Pythagorean and just intonation scales.

# Examples
- 1200 * log2(4/3) ~ 498.04 cents (tempered fourth = 500 cents, difference ~ 1.96 cents)
- Complement of the just fifth: 4/3 * 3/2 = 2 (one octave)
- In just intonation, the interval from scale tone 1 to scale tone 4 is 4/3
- The just fourth is the interval from the 3rd to the 4th harmonic

# Common Confusions
- The just fourth (4/3 ~ 498 cents) and tempered fourth (500 cents) differ by the same amount as the just fifth and tempered fifth (~2 cents), since they are complementary intervals
- The just fourth is not the inversion of the just fifth in the additive sense of cents -- it is the octave complement (1200 - 701.96 = 498.04)
