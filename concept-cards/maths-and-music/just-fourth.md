# Quick Definition

The just fourth is the interval with frequency ratio 4/3, approximately 498.04 cents, about 2 cents flat of the equal-tempered fourth. It is the octave complement of the just fifth.

# Core Definition

The just fourth is the rational interval corresponding to the ratio 4/3 = 2^2/3 in Q+. Wright notes that "this interval, call it I, is complementary to the just fifth, since 4/3 * 3/2 = 2. This says I is given additively as one octave minus a just fifth, which means it is about 2 cents flat of a keyboard fourth" (p. 141). Its prime factorization is 2^2 * 3^(-1), making it a 3-limit interval.

# Prerequisites

# Key Properties

1. Ratio: 4/3
2. Cents: ~498.04
3. Deviation from equal temperament: ~1.96 cents flat
4. Prime factorization: 2^2 * 3^(-1) (3-limit interval)
5. Octave complement of just fifth: 4/3 * 3/2 = 2
6. Interval from 3rd to 4th harmonic in the overtone series

# Construction / Recognition

1. Start with the octave (ratio 2)
2. Subtract a just fifth: 2 / (3/2) = 4/3
3. Result: frequency ratio 4/3
4. Alternatively, identify as the interval from the 3rd to the 4th harmonic

# Context & Application

The just fourth is one of the most consonant intervals, historically grouped with the octave and fifth as "perfect" consonances. It appears in both the Pythagorean scale and the just intonation scale as the ratio of scale tone 4 to scale tone 1. The complementary relationship 4/3 * 3/2 = 2 exemplifies the principle that an interval and its octave complement multiply to give the octave ratio.

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 141): 1200 * log2(4/3) ~ 498.04 cents; the keyboard fourth is 500 cents, about 2 cents sharp.

**Example 2** (p. 141): Complement of the just fifth: 4/3 * 3/2 = 2 (one octave).

**Example 3** (Ch. 12, p. 155): In the just intonation scale, the interval from scale tone 1 to scale tone 4 is 4/3.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Computing the just fourth as the inverse (reciprocal) of the just fifth
  **Correction**: The just fourth is the octave complement (2 / (3/2) = 4/3), not the reciprocal (2/3, which would be a descending fifth)

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the just fourth and tempered fourth differ by a different amount than the just fifth and tempered fifth
  **Clarification**: Both differ by exactly ~1.96 cents, since they are octave complements and the tempered versions are also octave complements (500 + 700 = 1200)
