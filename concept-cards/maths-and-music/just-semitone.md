# Quick Definition
The just semitone is the interval with frequency ratio 16/15, approximately 111.73 cents, about 12 cents sharp of the equal-tempered semitone.

# Formal Definition
The just semitone is the rational interval corresponding to the ratio 16/15 = 2^4 / (3 * 5) in Q+. It is a 5-limit interval and the first common just interval whose denominator involves more than one odd prime (both 3 and 5). By virtue of having larger numerator and denominator than previously discussed just intervals, it is considered "less just" and expected to be less consonant. Measured in cents: 1200 * log2(16/15) ~ 111.73.

# Mathematical Context
The ratio 16/15 can be derived as the difference between a just fourth and a just major third: (4/3) / (5/4) = 16/15. Equivalently, it is the interval remaining when a just major third is subtracted from a just fourth. In the just intonation scale, it appears as both half-step intervals: 3-to-4 and 7-to-8.

# Musical Context
The just semitone is the half-step interval in the just intonation scale, appearing between scale degrees 3-4 and 7-8. It is noticeably wider than the equal-tempered semitone (100 cents) by about 12 cents. In the Pythagorean scale, the half-step is instead the hemitone (256/243 ~ 90.22 cents), a narrower interval. The just semitone contributes to the distinctive character of just intonation, where half steps are wider and whole steps come in two sizes.

# Examples
- 1200 * log2(16/15) ~ 111.73 cents (tempered semitone = 100 cents, difference ~ 11.73 cents)
- Just fourth minus just major third: (4/3) / (5/4) = 16/15
- In just intonation scale: the intervals 3-to-4 and 7-to-8 are both 16/15
- Compare with Pythagorean hemitone: 256/243 ~ 90.22 cents

# Common Confusions
- The just semitone (16/15 ~ 112 cents) is sharper than the tempered semitone (100 cents), not flatter
- The Pythagorean hemitone (256/243 ~ 90 cents) is a different interval from the just semitone -- both are "half steps" but in different tuning systems
- The primes 17 and 19 give intervals closer to the tempered semitone (17/16 ~ 105 cents) and tempered minor third (19/16 ~ 298 cents) than the just semitone and just minor third
