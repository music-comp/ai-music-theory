# Quick Definition
The diatonic scale is the seven-note standard scale (e.g., C D E F G A B) with a specific pattern of whole and half steps; the chromatic scale contains all twelve notes within an octave.

# Formal Definition
The standard (diatonic) scale based on C is the sequence C D E F G A B, with the interval pattern (in whole and half steps): $1, 1, \frac{1}{2}, 1, 1, 1, \frac{1}{2}$. The chromatic scale contains all 12 notes modulo octave. A scale is defined as a subsequence of the chromatic scale notes. In a given key, diatonic notes are those lying within the diatonic scale, forming a 7-element subset of the 12-element chromatic scale. A standard scale is any sequence of eight consecutive notes equivalent (same interval pattern) to the C scale.

# Mathematical Context
The diatonic scale is characterized by its interval sequence $1, 1, \frac{1}{2}, 1, 1, 1, \frac{1}{2}$, which has no non-trivial cyclic permutation equal to itself. This means the seven modes obtained by cyclic permutation are all distinct. The scale contains two equivalent tetrachords (four-note sequences bounded by a perfect fourth): C-D-E-F and G-A-B-C, each with interval pattern $1, 1, \frac{1}{2}$.

# Musical Context
The diatonic scale corresponds to the white keys on the keyboard (in the key of C). The pattern of intervals is built into musical notation, making C the "default" key -- there is nothing in the notation itself to indicate that E to F is a half step while F to G is a whole step. The chromatic scale uses all keys (black and white). Diatonic notes are the "natural" notes of a given key; chromatic notes require accidentals.

# Examples
- C major diatonic scale: $C \xrightarrow{1} D \xrightarrow{1} E \xrightarrow{1/2} F \xrightarrow{1} G \xrightarrow{1} A \xrightarrow{1} B \xrightarrow{1/2} C$
- The $E^\flat$ major scale $E^\flat, F, G, A^\flat, B^\flat, C, D, E^\flat$ is a standard scale (same interval pattern)
- Tetrachord equivalence: C-D-E-F and G-A-B-C have the same interval pattern $1, 1, \frac{1}{2}$
- The chromatic scale has 12 notes modulo octave; the diatonic scale has 7

# Common Confusions
- "Standard scale" in Wright means specifically a scale with the interval pattern $1, 1, \frac{1}{2}, 1, 1, 1, \frac{1}{2}$ (i.e., the major/Ionian scale), not any diatonic mode
- The notation does not visually distinguish half steps from whole steps; the pattern must be memorized
- "Chromatic" notes are key-dependent: $F^\sharp$ is diatonic in G major but chromatic in C major
