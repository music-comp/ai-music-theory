# Quick Definition
The period P of a vibration is the time for one complete cycle (in seconds). The frequency F = 1/P is the number of cycles per second, measured in Hertz (Hz). Frequency determines the perceived pitch of a musical tone.

# Formal Definition
For a periodic function f(t) with period P (measured in seconds), the frequency is F = 1/P, measured in Hertz (Hz = cycles per second). The relationship is reciprocal: longer period means lower frequency (lower pitch), shorter period means higher frequency (higher pitch).

# Mathematical Context
If f(t) has period P, then the horizontal compression f(ct) has period P/c and frequency c/P = cF. Doubling the frequency (c = 2) halves the period and raises the pitch by one octave. The relationship F = 1/P is fundamental to connecting the mathematical description of periodic functions to their musical interpretation.

# Musical Context
Concert pitch A4 is standardized at 440 Hz, meaning the sound wave completes 440 cycles per second (period = 1/440 ~ 0.00227 seconds). Human hearing spans approximately 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz. The piano ranges from about 27.5 Hz (A0) to 4186 Hz (C8).

# Examples
- A4 = 440 Hz: period = 1/440 seconds
- sin(t) has period 2*pi seconds, frequency = 1/(2*pi) ~ 0.159 Hz (far below audibility)
- sin(880*pi*t) has period 2*pi/(880*pi) = 1/440 seconds, frequency = 440 Hz = A4
- Middle C ~ 262 Hz: period ~ 0.00382 seconds

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes confuse angular frequency (omega = 2*pi*F, in radians per second) with frequency (F, in Hz). For sin(alpha*t), the frequency is alpha/(2*pi) Hz, not alpha Hz. The factor of 2*pi arises because one cycle of sine corresponds to 2*pi radians.
