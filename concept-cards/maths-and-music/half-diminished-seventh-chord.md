# Quick Definition
The half-diminished seventh chord is a four-note chord defined by the interval sequence (3, 3, 4, 2) in semitones. It has a discernible root and an unstable, tension-laden quality that suggests resolution.

# Formal Definition
The half-diminished seventh chord is defined by the sequence of modular intervals (3, 3, 4, 2) in Z_12:

root --(3)--> third --(3)--> fifth --(4)--> seventh --(2)--> (root)

This sequence has no non-trivial cyclic permutations that reproduce it, so the root is uniquely identifiable.

# Mathematical Context
The interval sequence (3, 3, 4, 2) sums to 12. Its four cyclic permutations -- (3,3,4,2), (3,4,2,3), (4,2,3,3), (2,3,3,4) -- are all distinct, confirming a discernible root. The chord can be seen as a diminished triad (3, 3) with the final interval 6 split into 4 + 2 by the seventh.

# Musical Context
The half-diminished seventh chord has a somewhat unstable aura and suggests the need for resolution. Like the dominant seventh chord, it often resolves around the circle of fifths. It is labeled with the suffix "ø7" (e.g., Bø7). It commonly appears on scale degree VII in the major mode or scale degree II in the minor mode, where it functions as a predominant chord.

# Examples
- Bø7: B-D-F-A, with intervals (3, 3, 4, 2)
- The chord often resolves around the circle of fifths
- In C major, the VII chord is Bø7

# Common Confusions
- "Half-diminished" is distinct from "fully diminished" (diminished seventh): half-diminished is (3, 3, 4, 2), fully diminished is (3, 3, 3, 3)
- Unlike the diminished seventh, the half-diminished seventh has a discernible root
- The half-diminished shares the first two intervals (3, 3) with the diminished seventh but differs in the last two
