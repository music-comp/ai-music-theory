# Quick Definition

In unequal temperaments, the comma (discrepancy preventing the circle of fifths from closing) must be placed at a specific position on the circle, creating one anomalous fifth that is either too small (Pythagorean) or too large (mean-tone).

# Core Definition

Since neither twelve just fifths nor twelve mean-tone fifths exactly equal seven octaves, every unequal temperament based on fifths must accommodate a comma somewhere on the circle. In the Pythagorean scale, the comma of Pythagoras (~23.46 cents) creates one fifth that is ~23 cents flat. In the mean-tone scale, the lesser diesis (~41.06 cents) creates a wolf fifth that is ~41 cents sharp. Wright notes placement "is often placed at one of the bottom clock positions" (p. 154) -- a strategic choice affecting which keys sound best.

# Prerequisites

- **Comma of Pythagoras** -- The Pythagorean comma that must be placed
- **Wolf fifth** -- The mean-tone comma's manifestation

# Key Properties

1. The comma cannot be eliminated -- only placed or distributed
2. Pythagorean comma creates a flat fifth (~23 cents deficit)
3. Mean-tone comma creates a sharp (wolf) fifth (~41 cents excess)
4. The two commas go in opposite directions (flat vs. sharp)
5. Keys near the comma sound poor; keys far from it sound excellent
6. Equal temperament is the limiting case: comma spread equally across all twelve fifths
7. Rotating the comma changes which keys are well-tuned

# Construction / Recognition

## Common Placements

For Pythagorean:
- Between 7-hat and flat-5 on the circle
- Or between flat-5 and flat-2

For Mean-tone:
- Between 8 and 9 o'clock positions (e.g., G# to Eb)
- Away from commonly used diatonic note pairs

# Context & Application

Strategic comma placement was essential for practical music-making in pre-equal-temperament eras. Certain keys had distinct "characters" in unequal temperaments: keys near the comma sounded rougher, keys far from it sounded purer. This key character was a valued aesthetic feature of Baroque music. Wright notes that rotating the mean-tone comma one position clockwise would fix the VII triad but break another (p. 158).

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 154): Pythagorean comma placed between flat-5 and flat-2.

**Example 2** (p. 157): Mean-tone wolf fifth placed between 8 and 9 o'clock positions.

**Example 3**: Equal temperament: each fifth absorbs 1/12 of the Pythagorean comma (~1.96 cents flat).

**Example 4** (p. 158): Mean-tone with comma placed away from diatonic notes gives "tolerably tuned major triads on each of the diatonic roots except VII."

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Thinking the comma can be eliminated by clever placement
  **Correction**: The comma is a mathematical necessity; placement only determines which keys are affected

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking equal temperament eliminates the comma
  **Clarification**: Equal temperament distributes the comma equally (~1.96 cents per fifth) rather than concentrating it

- **Confusion**: Thinking Pythagorean and mean-tone commas work the same way
  **Clarification**: The Pythagorean comma produces a flat fifth; the mean-tone comma produces a sharp (wolf) fifth -- opposite directions
