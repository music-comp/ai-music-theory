# Quick Definition
In unequal temperaments, the comma (discrepancy preventing the circle of fifths from closing) must be placed at a specific position on the circle, creating one anomalous fifth that is either too small (Pythagorean) or too large (mean-tone).

# Formal Definition
Since neither twelve just fifths nor twelve mean-tone fifths exactly equal seven octaves, every unequal temperament based on fifths must accommodate a comma somewhere on the circle of fifths. In the Pythagorean scale, the comma of Pythagoras (3^12/2^19 ~ 23.46 cents) creates one fifth that is ~23 cents flat. In the mean-tone scale, the lesser diesis (128/125 ~ 41.06 cents) creates one wolf fifth that is ~41 cents sharp. The placement of this anomalous fifth is a design choice affecting which keys are well-tuned.

# Mathematical Context
For the Pythagorean scale: if twelve just fifths (each 3/2) are plotted on a 7-octave circle, they wrap around and end up ~23.46 cents past the starting point. The tempered fifth (2^(7/12)) distributes this comma equally across all twelve fifths, each being 1/12 of the comma flat of just. For the mean-tone scale: the deficit is larger (128/125 ~ 41.06 cents), making the anomalous position more disruptive and its placement more critical.

# Musical Context
Strategic comma placement is essential for practical music-making in unequal temperaments. The comma is typically placed between notes that are rarely used together as a fifth. In the Pythagorean scale, common placements are between scale degree 7 and flat-5, or between flat-5 and flat-2. In the mean-tone scale, it is often placed between the 8 and 9 o'clock positions (e.g., between G# and Eb). Keys near the comma sound poor; keys far from it sound excellent. This is why certain keys had distinct "characters" in pre-equal-temperament music.

# Examples
- Pythagorean: comma (~23.46 cents) often between 7-hat and flat-5, or flat-5 and flat-2
- Mean-tone: wolf fifth (~41 cents excess) often between 8 and 9 o'clock positions
- Equal temperament: comma distributed equally across all 12 fifths (~1.96 cents each)
- Key character: keys near the comma sound "rougher"; keys far from it sound "purer"
- Rotating the comma one position clockwise in mean-tone can fix the VII triad but break another

# Common Confusions
- Equal temperament is essentially the limiting case where the comma is spread equally across all twelve fifths rather than concentrated in one place
- The comma cannot be eliminated -- it is a mathematical necessity. It can only be distributed or placed
- Different comma placements create different sets of "good" and "bad" keys, not different overall quality
- The Pythagorean comma is absorbed by a flat fifth; the mean-tone comma produces a sharp (wolf) fifth -- opposite directions
