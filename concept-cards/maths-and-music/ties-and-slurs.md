# Quick Definition
A tie is a curved line connecting two notes of the same pitch, combining their durations into one sustained note; a slur looks similar but connects notes of different pitches, indicating smooth, connected performance.

# Formal Definition
A tie connects two notes of the same pitch with a curved line, indicating they are to be treated as one note whose duration is the sum of the two tied notes' durations. A slur connects notes of different pitches, indicating they should be performed with minimal rearticulation (e.g., in one bow stroke for a string instrument).

# Mathematical Context
Ties implement addition of durations: if two notes of durations $d_1$ and $d_2$ are tied, the resulting duration is $d_1 + d_2$. Combined with the power-of-2 note system and dots, ties allow representation of any rational duration that can be expressed as a sum of dyadic rationals (fractions with powers of 2 in the denominator). Exercise 8 asks which fractions of a whole note can be achieved using $\frac{1}{2^n}$-notes with dots and ties.

# Musical Context
Ties are essential when a note's duration must span across a bar line or when the desired duration cannot be expressed by a single note value (with or without dots). Slurs are performance instructions affecting articulation: a violinist plays slurred notes in one bow stroke, a wind player in one breath, a singer on one syllable. Despite their visual similarity, ties and slurs have fundamentally different musical meanings.

# Examples
- A quarter note ($d_1 = 1$ beat) tied to a dotted sixteenth ($d_2 = \frac{3}{8}$ beat) gives duration $1 + \frac{3}{8} = \frac{11}{8}$ beats
- A half note tied across a bar line to a quarter note gives 3 beats of sustained sound
- A slur over C-D-E indicates all three notes played smoothly and connectedly
- Accidental rule: when an altered note is tied across a bar line, the alteration applies to the tied note but not to other notes of the same class in the new measure

# Common Confusions
- Ties connect notes of the SAME pitch (combining durations); slurs connect notes of DIFFERENT pitches (performance instruction) -- they look nearly identical
- A tied note across a bar line carries its accidental, but the accidental does NOT apply to other notes of the same class in the new measure
- Ties are not redundant with dots: some durations (like $\frac{11}{8}$ beats) can only be expressed with ties
