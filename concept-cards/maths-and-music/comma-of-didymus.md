# Quick Definition
The comma of Didymus (also called the syntonic comma) is the small interval 81/80, approximately 21.51 cents, representing the difference between the greater whole tone (9/8) and the lesser whole tone (10/9).

# Formal Definition
The comma of Didymus is the rational interval corresponding to the ratio 81/80 = 3^4 / (2^4 * 5) in Q+. It is defined as (9/8) / (10/9) = 81/80. Equivalently, it is the difference between the Pythagorean major third (81/64) and the just major third (5/4): (81/64) / (5/4) = 81/80. Measured in cents: 1200 * log2(81/80) ~ 21.51.

# Mathematical Context
The comma of Didymus measures the fundamental incompatibility between 3-limit tuning (Pythagorean) and 5-limit tuning (just intonation). It arises because four just fifths minus two octaves (= Pythagorean major third = 81/64) does not equal the just major third (5/4). Its prime factorization 3^4 * 2^(-4) * 5^(-1) shows that it necessarily involves the prime 5 -- it vanishes only in 3-limit tuning where 5 is absent.

# Musical Context
The comma of Didymus explains why the Pythagorean scale has poor thirds: the Pythagorean major third (81/64) is sharp of the just major third (5/4) by this comma (~21.5 cents), an easily perceptible discrepancy. The mean-tone scale addresses this by flattening each fifth by one-quarter of the comma of Didymus, so that four such fifths yield an exact just major third. The comma is named after Didymus the Musician (1st century BC), who first described this discrepancy.

# Examples
- (9/8) / (10/9) = 81/80 (difference between the two whole tones)
- (81/64) / (5/4) = 81/80 (Pythagorean major third minus just major third)
- 1200 * log2(81/80) ~ 21.51 cents (roughly one-fifth of a semitone)
- The mean-tone fifth is flat of the just fifth by (81/80)^(1/4) ~ 5.38 cents
- The comma of Didymus + one octave = two just fifths minus a lesser whole tone

# Common Confusions
- The comma of Didymus (81/80 ~ 21.51 cents) should not be confused with the comma of Pythagoras (3^12/2^19 ~ 23.46 cents) -- they are different intervals arising from different tuning problems
- The comma of Didymus is not negligible -- at ~21.5 cents it is roughly one-fifth of a semitone and clearly audible
- The comma measures the gap between 3-limit and 5-limit tuning, not a flaw in any single tuning system
