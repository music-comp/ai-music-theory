# Quick Definition
The large-scale organization of a musical composition into distinct sections, often labeled with letters (A, B, C) to indicate patterns of repetition and contrast, with section lengths frequently being powers of 2.

# Formal Definition
Form is the sequence of larger sections into which music may be organized. Sections are distinguished by different rhythmic and melodic character. The number of measures in a section is often a power of 2 (commonly 8 or 16). Sections are labeled with letters: same letter for sections with similar material, different letters for contrasting sections. A prime mark (') indicates a section bearing strong resemblance to another.

# Mathematical Context
Musical form can be represented as a string over an alphabet $\{A, B, C, \ldots\}$, where identical letters denote equivalent (or near-equivalent) sections. The prevalence of powers of 2 in section lengths connects to the binary structure of the durational system. Form analysis identifies the algebraic structure of repetition and variation at the highest organizational level.

# Musical Context
Common forms include:
- **Binary form** (AABB): two repeated sections, common in minuets and scherzos of the late 18th-19th centuries
- **Ternary form** (ABA): three sections with the first and third the same or similar, found in Chopin nocturnes and Brahms piano pieces
- **AABA**: common in American popular music and Tin Pan Alley songs (e.g., "Five Foot Two, Eyes of Blue")
- **AA'BA'**: variant where A' is similar but not identical to A (e.g., "Edelweiss")
- Ragtime: typically 3-4 sections of 16 measures each (e.g., Joplin's "Maple Leaf Rag": AA BB A CC DD)

# Examples
- Scott Joplin's "Maple Leaf Rag" (1868-1917): form AA BB A CC DD
- "Five Foot Two, Eyes of Blue": AABA form
- "Edelweiss": AA'BA' form
- "O Tannenbaum": AABA form with internal symmetries in each section
- Most American popular songs divide into 8-bar segments

# Common Confusions
- Form labels (A, B, C) describe large sections, not individual phrases or measures
- A' means "similar to A but not identical" -- the degree of similarity is a judgment call
- Binary form (AABB) is not simply "two sections" -- the repetition of each section is part of the form
- "Ternary form" (ABA) is distinct from "rounded binary" (||:A:||:BA':||) despite their surface similarity
