# Quick Definition
The just major third is the interval with frequency ratio 5/4, approximately 386.31 cents, about 14 cents flat of the equal-tempered major third.

# Formal Definition
The just major third is the rational interval corresponding to the ratio 5/4 in Q+. It is the integral interval 5 (the fifth harmonic) lowered by two octaves. Its prime factorization is 2^(-2) * 5^1, making it the simplest interval involving the prime 5. Measured in cents: 1200 * log2(5/4) ~ 386.31.

# Mathematical Context
The ratio 5/4 is a 5-limit interval, requiring the prime 5 in its factorization. This distinguishes it from the Pythagorean major third (81/64 ~ 407.82 cents), which uses only primes 2 and 3. The difference between these two thirds is precisely the comma of Didymus: 81/64 / (5/4) = 81/80 ~ 21.51 cents. Three just major thirds do not equal an octave: (5/4)^3 = 125/64 < 2, falling short by the interval 128/125 ~ 41.06 cents.

# Musical Context
The just major third is a cornerstone of 5-limit tuning and just intonation. It forms the middle voice of the justly tuned major triad (4:5:6). The equal-tempered major third at 400 cents is noticeably sharper than the just version -- this 14-cent discrepancy is one of the most audible compromises of equal temperament. The acceptance of thirds into Western music in the 14th-15th centuries drove the development of mean-tone temperament, which prioritizes just major thirds.

# Examples
- 1200 * log2(5/4) ~ 386.31 cents (tempered major third = 400 cents, difference ~ 13.69 cents)
- Just major third + just minor third = just fifth: 5/4 * 6/5 = 3/2
- Greater whole tone + lesser whole tone = just major third: 9/8 * 10/9 = 5/4
- Pythagorean major third (81/64) is sharper than just major third by the comma of Didymus (81/80)

# Common Confusions
- The just major third (5/4 ~ 386 cents) is significantly different from the tempered major third (400 cents) -- a 14-cent gap that trained ears can detect
- The Pythagorean major third (81/64 ~ 408 cents) is even sharper than the tempered version and is considered a weakness of Pythagorean tuning
- Three just major thirds do not make an octave -- they fall short by about 41 cents
