# Quick Definition

A horizontal shift in music where a melodic or rhythmic pattern is repeated at a later time, analogous to the mathematical graph transformation $y = f(x - c)$.

# Core Definition

*Translation* is a horizontal shift, corresponding to replacing $y = f(x)$ by $y = f(x - c)$ in the graph of a function. In music, translation appears as the repetition of a pitch sequence (melodic translation) or a rhythmic pattern (rhythmic translation) at a different point in time. A translation preserves the intervals and/or durations exactly (Wright, p. 38).

# Prerequisites

- **Geometric Transformations of Graphs** — Translation is a horizontal shift of a function graph
- **Melody** — Translation operates on melodic and rhythmic patterns

# Key Properties

1. Translation is an isometry (distance-preserving transformation) of the time axis
2. Rhythmic translation preserves the pattern of durations
3. Melodic translation preserves both pitches and intervals
4. Translation can occur with or without simultaneous pitch changes (transposition)
5. It is one of the fundamental devices for creating musical cohesion

# Construction / Recognition

## To identify translation in music:

1. Look for a melodic or rhythmic pattern that repeats at a later time
2. Check if the pitch sequence is preserved (melodic translation) or only the rhythm (rhythmic translation)
3. Note whether transposition (pitch shift) occurs simultaneously

# Context & Application

Translation is one of the most fundamental devices for creating musical cohesion. It can be purely rhythmic (same rhythm, different pitches) or melodic (same pitches and rhythm). Repetition of sections in musical form (e.g., AABA) is a large-scale instance of translation. Motivic development often begins with exact or near-exact translation of short phrases.

# Examples

- Rhythmic translation: the rhythm of the first two bars is repeated twice while pitches vary (p. 38)
- In "When The Saints Go Marching In," the melodic sequence F-A-B$\flat$-C appears three times consecutively — melodic and rhythmic translation (p. 38)
- Sequential patterns in Baroque music repeat a melodic figure at the same pitch level

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Confusing translation with transposition
  **Correction**: Translation is horizontal (same pitch level, later time); transposition is vertical (different pitch level)

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking musical translation must be exact
  **Clarification**: In practice, slight variations and embellishments may occur while maintaining the essential pattern
- **Confusion**: Thinking rhythmic translation requires the same pitches
  **Clarification**: Rhythmic translation preserves only the durational pattern; pitches may vary
