# Quick Definition
A horizontal shift in music where a melodic or rhythmic pattern is repeated at a later time, analogous to the mathematical graph transformation $y = f(x - c)$.

# Formal Definition
Translation is a horizontal shift, corresponding to replacing $y = f(x)$ by $y = f(x - c)$ in the graph of a function (see Chapter 1). In music, translation appears as the repetition of a pitch sequence (melodic translation) or a rhythmic pattern (rhythmic translation) at a different point in time. A translation preserves the intervals and/or durations exactly.

# Mathematical Context
In the function $y = f(x - c)$, the parameter $c > 0$ shifts the graph to the right by $c$ units, corresponding to a later occurrence in time. Translation is an isometry (distance-preserving transformation) of the time axis. Rhythmic translation preserves the pattern of durations; melodic translation preserves the pattern of both pitches and intervals. Translation can occur with or without simultaneous pitch changes (transposition).

# Musical Context
Translation is one of the most fundamental devices for creating musical cohesion. It can be purely rhythmic (same rhythm, different pitches) or melodic (same pitches and rhythm). Repetition of sections in musical form (e.g., AABA) is a large-scale instance of translation. Motivic development often begins with exact or near-exact translation of short phrases.

# Common Confusions
- Translation (horizontal shift, same pitch level) vs. transposition (vertical shift, different pitch level) -- both involve repetition but in different dimensions
- Pure rhythmic translation preserves only the durational pattern, not the pitches
- Translation in music is not necessarily exact: slight variations and embellishments may occur while maintaining the essential pattern
