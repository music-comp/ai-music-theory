# Quick Definition
The set of all integral intervals (intervals with positive integer frequency ratios) forms a monoid under composition, meaning it is closed under combining intervals, the operation is associative, and unison (ratio 1) serves as the identity element.

# Formal Definition
The set of integral intervals under composition forms a monoid identifiable with (Z+, *), where Z+ is the set of positive integers and * is multiplication. The identity element is 1 (unison). The monoid is commutative. It is not a group because inverses (ratios 1/n for n > 1) are not integers.

# Mathematical Context
A monoid is an algebraic structure with an associative binary operation and an identity element. Unlike a group, a monoid does not require inverses. The integral intervals form a monoid because: (1) composing two integer ratios m and n gives the integer ratio m * n (closure); (2) multiplication is associative; (3) the ratio 1 is the identity. By the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, this monoid is freely generated by the prime intervals.

# Musical Context
The monoid structure captures how integer intervals combine: stacking an octave (2) with a twelfth (3) gives the interval 6, which is two octaves plus a fifth. The factorization structure explains why composite intervals inherit their musical character from their prime components.

# Examples
- 1 * n = n for any n (unison composed with any interval yields that interval)
- 2 * 3 = 6 (octave + twelfth = two octaves + fifth)
- 3 * 3 = 9 (twelfth + twelfth = three octaves + major second)
- 2 * 2 * 2 = 8 (three octaves)

# Common Confusions
Students may wonder why this is a monoid rather than a group. The key is that downward intervals (ratios like 1/3) are not integers, so inverses do not exist within the structure. The full group of interval ratios is (R+, *), which contains this monoid.
