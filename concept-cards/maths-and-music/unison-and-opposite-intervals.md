# Quick Definition
The unison interval is the "zero interval" where f_1 = f_2, corresponding to ratio 1. Every interval f_2 : f_1 has a unique opposite interval f_1 : f_2, which spans the same distance in the opposite direction and has ratio r^(-1).

# Formal Definition
The unison interval is given by the ratio f : f for any f in R+, corresponding via phi to the number 1. For any interval with ratio r = f_2/f_1, its opposite interval has ratio r^(-1) = f_1/f_2. If the original interval is upward (r > 1), the opposite is downward (r^(-1) < 1), and vice versa. The unison interval is its own opposite (1^(-1) = 1).

# Mathematical Context
In the multiplicative group (R+, *), the unison interval r = 1 is the identity element, and the opposite interval r^(-1) is the group inverse. This gives (R+, *) the structure of an abelian group, where:
- Identity: 1 (unison)
- Inverse of r: r^(-1) (opposite interval)
- The group operation (multiplication) corresponds to juxtaposing intervals

The orientation partition divides R+ into three sets: {r < 1} (downward), {1} (unison), {r > 1} (upward).

# Musical Context
The unison interval means "no change in pitch." The opposite interval reverses direction: if up a fifth has ratio 3/2, then down a fifth has ratio 2/3. By default, when an interval's orientation is not stated, an upward interval is meant (e.g., "the interval of a fourth" means "the upward interval of a fourth").

# Examples
- Unison: ratio 1 (same pitch)
- Up an octave: ratio 2; down an octave (opposite): ratio 1/2
- Up a fifth (~ratio 3/2); down a fifth (opposite): ratio 2/3
- The convention is that unspecified orientation means upward

# Common Confusions
- The opposite interval is the multiplicative inverse (r^(-1)), not the additive negation (-r)
- Unison is ratio 1, not ratio 0 -- there is no ratio 0 in this system
- "Opposite" refers to direction reversal, not to any notion of complementary interval
