# Quick Definition
The way in which time is organized within measures -- the specific pattern of note and rest durations that fills each measure and gives music its temporal character.

# Formal Definition
Rhythm is the organization of time within measures, created by the specific sequence of durational notes and rests. A melody is defined and made recognizable not only by its sequence of pitches but by its rhythm. Rhythms can be straightforward or subtle; jazz often obscures the meter using complex sequences.

# Mathematical Context
Rhythm can be modeled as a partition of a measure's total duration into an ordered sequence of note and rest values, where each value is a dyadic rational (possibly modified by dots or tuplets). The constraint is that the sum of all durations in a measure equals the total beats specified by the time signature. Rhythmic patterns can be analyzed using the concept of translation (repeating patterns) from geometric transformations.

# Musical Context
Rhythm is distinct from meter: meter provides the framework (beats per measure), while rhythm fills that framework with specific patterns. The same sequence of pitches set to different rhythms can produce entirely different musical effects. Swing rhythm is a notable example where the written rhythm differs from the performed rhythm: pairs of eighth notes are performed as if the first were two-thirds and the second one-third of a triplet beat. Swing is sometimes indicated by notation or by the marking "swing rhythm."

# Examples
- A descending C major scale in even eighth notes evokes no particular song, but the same pitches set to the rhythm of "Joy To The World" are immediately recognizable
- Swing rhythm: written eighth notes are performed as a tied triplet quarter + triplet eighth, notated precisely in $\frac{3n}{8}$ compound time
- Rhythmic translation: the rhythm of the first two bars of a passage repeated in subsequent bars, even while the pitches vary

# Common Confusions
- Rhythm vs. meter: meter is the underlying beat structure, rhythm is how durations are arranged within that structure
- Swing rhythm is not literally what is written -- the notation is a shorthand for a triplet-based feel
- Rhythm is as essential to a melody's identity as its pitch sequence -- the same pitches with different rhythms create a different melody
