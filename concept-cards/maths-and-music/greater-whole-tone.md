# Quick Definition
The greater whole tone (also called the Pythagorean whole tone) is the interval with frequency ratio 9/8, approximately 203.91 cents, about 4 cents sharp of the equal-tempered whole step.

# Formal Definition
The greater whole tone is the rational interval corresponding to the ratio 9/8 = 3^2/2^3 in Q+. It equals two just fifths minus one octave: (3/2)^2 * (1/2) = 9/8. Its prime factorization involves only the primes 2 and 3, making it a 3-limit interval. Measured in cents: 1200 * log2(9/8) ~ 203.91.

# Mathematical Context
The ratio 9/8 arises naturally as the interval between two consecutive just fifths brought down an octave. It is the whole step interval in the Pythagorean scale, where it appears as every whole-step interval between adjacent scale tones. The greater whole tone exceeds the lesser whole tone (10/9) by the comma of Didymus: 9/8 / (10/9) = 81/80. Two of these whole tones compose a Pythagorean major third: (9/8)^2 = 81/64.

# Musical Context
This interval is called the "Pythagorean whole tone" because it arises in the Pythagorean scale as the interval between adjacent diatonic scale tones separated by a whole step. It is called the "greater" whole tone to distinguish it from the lesser whole tone (10/9 ~ 182.40 cents). The tempered whole step (200 cents) lies between these two, closer to the greater whole tone. In just intonation, the greater whole tone appears at intervals 1-to-2, 4-to-5, and 6-to-7.

# Examples
- 1200 * log2(9/8) ~ 203.91 cents (tempered step = 200 cents, difference ~ 3.91 cents)
- Two just fifths minus an octave: (3/2)^2 / 2 = 9/4 / 2 = 9/8
- Greater whole tone + lesser whole tone = just major third: 9/8 * 10/9 = 5/4
- Pythagorean scale uses 9/8 for all five whole-step intervals

# Common Confusions
- The greater whole tone (9/8 ~ 204 cents) and lesser whole tone (10/9 ~ 182 cents) are both approximated by the single tempered step (200 cents) -- equal temperament merges two distinct intervals into one
- Two greater whole tones make a Pythagorean major third (81/64), not a just major third (5/4) -- the difference is the comma of Didymus
- "Pythagorean whole tone" and "greater whole tone" are the same interval
