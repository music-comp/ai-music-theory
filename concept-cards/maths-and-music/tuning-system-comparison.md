# Quick Definition

The four main tuning systems -- Pythagorean, just intonation, mean-tone, and equal temperament -- each optimize different properties, reflecting the fundamental mathematical impossibility of achieving perfect tuning in all respects simultaneously.

# Core Definition

Wright presents three historical tuning systems in Chapter 12, each with "certain advantages" (p. 158), implicitly compared with equal temperament. No single system can simultaneously achieve just fifths, just thirds, octave closure, and key uniformity. The root cause is unique prime factorization: powers of different primes never coincide.

# Key Properties

| Property | Pythagorean | Just Intonation | Mean-Tone | Equal Temperament |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fifth | 3/2 (just) | 3/2 (just) | 5^(1/4) (~697 cents) | 2^(7/12) (700 cents) |
| Major third | 81/64 (~408 cents) | 5/4 (~386 cents) | 5/4 (~386 cents) | 2^(1/3) (400 cents) |
| Key uniform | No | No | No | Yes |
| All rational | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Comma size | ~23.5 cents | varies | ~41 cents | 0 (distributed) |
| p-limit | 3-limit | 5-limit | mixed | N/A (irrational) |

# Construction / Recognition

To compare tuning systems, evaluate these criteria:
1. Quality of fifths (how close to 3/2?)
2. Quality of thirds (how close to 5/4?)
3. Key uniformity (do all keys sound the same?)
4. Comma size and placement (how large and where?)
5. Historical appropriateness (what era's music is served?)

# Context & Application

Historically, each system served the music of its era: Pythagorean for medieval music (focused on fifths), just intonation for Renaissance vocal music (consonant triads), mean-tone for Baroque keyboard music (balancing thirds and fifths), equal temperament for Classical/Romantic/Modern music (free modulation). The progression reflects increasing harmonic complexity demanding greater key flexibility.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Ranking tuning systems as "better" or "worse" in absolute terms
  **Correction**: Each optimizes different musical properties; the best choice depends on the musical context

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking equal temperament made other systems obsolete
  **Clarification**: Unequal temperaments remain relevant for historical performance practice and are used by period-instrument ensembles

- **Confusion**: Assuming the progression was purely about tuning quality
  **Clarification**: The shift to equal temperament was driven by changing compositional needs (more modulation, more chromaticism), not by a pure improvement in tuning
