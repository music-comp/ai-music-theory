# Quick Definition
The four main tuning systems -- Pythagorean, just intonation, mean-tone, and equal temperament -- each optimize different properties, reflecting the fundamental mathematical impossibility of achieving perfect tuning in all respects simultaneously.

# Formal Definition
The four systems differ in their prime limits, interval accuracy, and key uniformity:
- Pythagorean (3-limit): all ratios 2^a * 3^b. Just fifths (3:2), poor thirds (81/64). Comma of Pythagoras (~23.46 cents) at one position.
- Just intonation (5-limit): all ratios 2^a * 3^b * 5^c. Just fifths, just thirds, many just chords, but key-dependent. Some triads (II, III) poorly tuned.
- Mean-tone (mixed rational/irrational): fifth = 5^(1/4). Perfect major thirds (5/4), but wolf fifth (~41 cents excess). Key-dependent.
- Equal temperament (all irrational): all intervals 2^(k/12). No just intervals except octave, but all keys identical.

# Mathematical Context
The comparison reveals a fundamental trade-off theorem: no fixed-pitch tuning system can simultaneously achieve (1) just fifths, (2) just thirds, (3) octave closure, and (4) key uniformity. Pythagorean achieves (1) and (3); just intonation achieves (1), (2), and (3) in some keys; mean-tone achieves (2) and (3); equal temperament achieves (3) and (4). The root cause is that powers of different primes never coincide (unique factorization).

# Musical Context
Historically, each system served the music of its era: Pythagorean for medieval music (focused on fifths), just intonation for Renaissance vocal music (focused on consonant triads), mean-tone for Baroque keyboard music (balancing thirds and fifths), and equal temperament for Classical/Romantic/Modern music (requiring free modulation). The progression reflects the increasing harmonic complexity of Western music demanding greater key flexibility.

# Common Confusions
- No single system is "best" -- each optimizes different musical properties
- Equal temperament did not make other systems obsolete -- they remain relevant for historical performance practice
- The progression from Pythagorean to equal temperament was not purely about tuning quality but about the changing needs of musical composition
- Just intonation remains the natural tuning for voices and unfretted strings, even in modern performance
