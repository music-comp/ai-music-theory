# Quick Definition
Pitch is the perceived quality of a musical tone determined by the frequency of its sound wave vibration, measured in hertz (cycles per second).

# Formal Definition
A musical tone results from a regular vibration transmitted through air as a sound wave. The pitch of the tone is the frequency of the vibration, measured in hertz (Hz), named after Heinrich Hertz (1857-1894). The set of all pitches is placed in one-to-one correspondence with $\mathbb{R}^+$, where a positive real number $x$ corresponds to frequency $x$ Hz.

# Mathematical Context
The identification of pitches with $\mathbb{R}^+$ means pitch is a continuous quantity, even though musical practice uses discrete pitches (keyboard notes). The range of human audibility is approximately 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz, but mathematically we allow any $x \in \mathbb{R}^+$. This identification enables the use of real analysis, functions, and transformations on the set of pitches.

# Musical Context
Standard tuning places A above middle C ($A_4$) at 440 Hz. Pitches between adjacent keyboard notes exist but are not assigned specific note names in standard Western music. The vertical position of a note on a musical staff indicates its pitch. In equal temperament, the frequency doubles with each octave, so frequencies are related by powers of $2^{1/12}$ per semitone.

# Examples
- $A_4 = 440$ Hz (standard tuning reference pitch)
- Human audible range: approximately 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz
- Middle C ($C_4$) is approximately 261.63 Hz in equal temperament
- The pitch continuum $\mathbb{R}^+$ includes all frequencies, not just those of keyboard notes

# Common Confusions
- Pitch (perceptual/frequency) vs. note (notational symbol on a staff): a note specifies a pitch, but pitches exist that are not represented by standard notes
- There are pitches between adjacent keyboard notes (the set of pitches is $\mathbb{R}^+$, not a discrete set)
- Hertz measures cycles per second, not the wave's amplitude (loudness)
