# Quick Definition
Tonal music is generally conceived in four voices (soprano, alto, tenor, bass), which nicely accommodate triads and seventh chords; three notation formats exist: open score, short score (chorale style), and keyboard style.

# Core Definition
Common-practice tonal music was generally conceived in a four-voice framework: soprano, alto, tenor, and bass (SATB). This represents a "happy medium" because it accommodates both triads (three pitches + one doubled) and seventh chords (four pitches). Three notation formats exist: **open score** (each voice on its own staff), **short score/chorale style** (SA on treble staff, TB on bass staff, with soprano/tenor stems up, alto/bass stems down), and **keyboard style** (SAT in treble, B in bass, with SAT fitting within an octave) (Laitz, Ch. 8, pp. 191-192).

# Prerequisites
- **Triads** -- the harmonies being voiced

# Key Properties
1. Four voices: soprano (highest), alto, tenor, bass (lowest)
2. Short score is the standard format for chorale-style writing
3. Soprano and tenor stems up; alto and bass stems down
4. In keyboard style, three upper voices fit within an octave in the right hand
