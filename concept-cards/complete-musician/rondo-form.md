# Quick Definition
Rondo form alternates sections of recurring material (refrains: A1, A2, A3) with sections of contrasting material (episodes: B, C), creating either a five-part (ABACA) or seven-part (ABACABA) structure.

# Core Definition
Rondo form is a composite form that extends the principle of ternary form by incorporating additional episodes and refrains. The refrain (A) always occurs in the home key and is typically the first thing heard. Episodes provide tonal and thematic contrast. Two main types exist: the five-part rondo (A1-B-A2-C-A3) and the seven-part rondo (A1-B1-A2-C-A3-B2-A4). The rondo originated in French Baroque instrumental music (rondeau) by composers such as Couperin and Rameau, and became a common final movement form in Classical sonatas, symphonies, and chamber works (Laitz, Ch. 32).

# Prerequisites
- **Ternary Form** -- rondo can be understood as an extension of ternary
- **Binary Form** -- sections often contain nested binary forms

# Key Properties
1. Alternation of refrains (A) and episodes (B, C)
2. Refrain always in the home key
3. Episodes provide tonal and thematic contrast
4. May include transitions, retransitions, and codas
5. Often a compound form with nested binary structures
6. Themes often folk- or popular-like

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Identify recurring refrain material in the home key
2. Identify contrasting episodes in other keys
3. Determine whether the form is five-part or seven-part
4. Look for transitions, retransitions, and coda
5. Check for nested binary forms within sections

# Context & Application
Rondos are common final movements in Classical sonatas, chamber pieces, and symphonies. Their lighter, folk-like themes provide contrast to more complex first movements. They demonstrate the balance between variety (episodes) and return (refrains) fundamental to human musical experience.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 731): Francois Couperin, "Air tendre" -- A five-part rondeau (A1-B-A2-C-A3) with episodes in III and v.

**Example 2** (p. 733): Clementi Piano Sonatina -- Five-part rondo with episodes in V and i, retransitions, and coda; each section in nested rounded binary form.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming double bars always mark section boundaries in rondos
  **Correction**: Double bars do not always appear; use changes in key, motive, and texture to identify sections

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Seven-part rondo vs. large ternary form
  **Clarification**: The seven-part rondo (A1-B1-A2 | C | A3-B2-A4) can sound like a ternary (A|B|A). Context helps: rondos favor the 18th century, faster tempos, and nested forms.
