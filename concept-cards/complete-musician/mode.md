# Quick Definition
Mode refers to the specific pattern of half and whole steps in a scale; in tonal music, the two primary modes are major (bright, stable) and minor (dark, flexible).

# Core Definition
Although the term **mode** has been used since antiquity to refer to many scalar constructions, in common-practice tonal music it refers to the two types of scales that occur regularly: **major** and **minor**. The name of a key provides two kinds of information: the tonic pitch and the mode. The major mode is characterized by a brighter sound and half steps between ^3-^4 and ^7-^1. The minor mode has a darker sound, with a lowered ^3 and variable ^6 and ^7.

# Prerequisites
- **Diatonic Scale** -- modes are specific arrangements of a diatonic scale

# Key Properties
1. Major mode: W-W-H-W-W-W-H; considered brighter and more stable
2. Minor mode: W-H-W-W-H-W-W (natural form); considered darker and more flexible
3. A key name specifies both tonic and mode (e.g., "D major" = tonic D, major mode)
4. The critical difference is the placement of the mediant: ^3 is a half step lower in minor

# Context & Application
Mode is one of the two defining characteristics of a key (along with tonic). Composers exploit the contrast between major and minor for expressive purposes, sometimes shifting between them within a single composition.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 10): Schubert's "Gute Nacht" (Example 1.10) presents the same passage first in D major (bright) then in D minor (dark), illustrating modal contrast.
