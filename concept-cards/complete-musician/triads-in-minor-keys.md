# Quick Definition
In minor keys, the variable ^6 and ^7 produce multiple triad possibilities on some degrees; the most common pattern uses raised ^7: i (minor), ii-deg (diminished), III (major), iv (minor), V (major), VI (major), vii-deg (diminished).

# Core Definition
Because the minor scale has variable ^6 and ^7, more triad types are possible. The most common forms (incorporating the leading tone) are: **i** (minor), **ii-deg** (diminished), **III** (major), **iv** (minor), **V** (major, with raised ^7), **VI** (major), and **vii-deg** (diminished, with raised ^7). The lowered ^7 produces v (minor) and VII (major), which are less common. Two different triads (with different roots) are possible on ^7.

# Key Properties
1. i = minor, ii-deg = diminished, III = major, iv = minor, V = major, VI = major, vii-deg = diminished
2. Variable ^6 affects triads on ^2, ^4, and ^6
3. Variable ^7 affects triads on ^5 and ^7
4. V and vii-deg (with raised ^7) are the most common forms
5. Lowered ^7 produces v and VII (subtonic), used in specific contexts
