# Quick Definition
The first-inversion variant of the D3 sequence, creating a stepwise descending bass with one voice (often tenor) alternating between forming a fifth and then a sixth above the bass. Much more common than the root-position D3.

# Core Definition
The descending 5-6 sequence is the D3 (-4/+2) + 6/3 variant, in which six-three chords smooth the bass into a stepwise descent. One voice remains stationary against the falling bass, forming alternating intervals of fifth and sixth (5-6 motion). The bass and soprano move in parallel tenths. This is much more common than the root-position D3. (Laitz, Ch. 22, pp. 504-505)

# Key Properties
1. Stepwise descending bass
2. Parallel tenths between bass and soprano
3. Stationary voice alternates 5-6 against bass
4. More common than root-position D3
