# Quick Definition
Moving from one perfect fifth, octave, or unison to another in parallel or contrary motion between any pair of voices is forbidden -- it causes one voice to drop out of the texture.

# Core Definition
**Parallel fifths and octaves** occur when two voices move from one perfect interval (P1, P5, or P8) to the same type of perfect interval on different pitches. This is the most absolute voice-leading rule: do not move from one unison, octave, or perfect fifth to another in parallel or contrary motion between any pair of voices. The effect is that one voice seems to drop out of the texture. Sustained perfect intervals on the same pitches (no actual motion) are not parallels (Laitz, Ch. 8, pp. 194-195).

# Prerequisites
- **Voice leading** -- this is the most inviolable voice-leading rule

# Key Properties
1. Applies between any pair of the six possible voice combinations
2. Cannot be broken for any reason (absolute rule)
3. Sustained perfect intervals without motion are not parallels
4. Parallel thirds and sixths are perfectly acceptable
5. Close position helps avoid parallels (potential P5s become harmless P4s)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Check all six voice pairs (S-A, S-T, S-B, A-T, A-B, T-B)
2. Identify any pair moving from P5 to P5, P8 to P8, or P1 to P1
3. Even contrary motion to the same perfect interval is forbidden
