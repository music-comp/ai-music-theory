# Quick Definition
ii is the most common pre-dominant chord: ii-V proceeds by descending fifth (strongest root motion), ii provides modal contrast, and ii-V-I often sets ^2-^7-^1 in the soprano (a powerful double-neighbor encircling ^1).

# Core Definition
The **supertonic** (ii in major, iio in minor) is the most common pre-dominant chord for three reasons: (1) ii-V proceeds by descending fifth, the strongest root motion; (2) ii introduces modal contrast (minor ii against major I and V in major keys; diminished iio in minor); (3) ii-V-I often sets ^2-^7-^1 in the soprano, a double-neighbor motion encircling ^1 that creates a powerful cadence. In minor, always use first inversion (iio6) to avoid the exposed root-position tritone. ii6 combines the best of both ii and IV: modal contrast, descending-fifth root relation, AND smooth ^4-^5 bass voice leading (Laitz, Ch. 12, pp. 285-288).

# Key Properties
1. Most common pre-dominant chord
2. ii-V = descending-fifth root motion (strongest)
3. Modal contrast: minor ii vs. major I and V
4. Soprano ^2-^7-^1 = powerful double-neighbor cadential motion
5. In minor: always use iio6 (root-position iio exposes tritone)
6. ii6 combines ii's modal contrast with IV's smooth ^4-^5 bass
7. Double ^4 (the bass) when writing ii6
