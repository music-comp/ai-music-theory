# Quick Definition
The most common sequence type, in which each copy descends by step from the model, with root motion alternating between descending fifths and ascending fourths. Labeled D2 (-5/+4). Follows the circle of fifths: I-IV-vii^o-iii-vi-ii-V-I.

# Core Definition
The D2 (-5/+4) sequence, also called the descending-fifth sequence, follows the circle of fifths. The root motion from chord to chord alternates between descending fifth (-5) and ascending fourth (+4), creating a deeper-level descent by second. The complete diatonic cycle is I-IV-vii^o-iii-vi-ii-V-I. A bass tritone leap occurs near the beginning in major and near the end in minor. The first-inversion variant, D2 (-5/+4) + 6/3, creates a smoother bass with outer-voice counterpoint of 10-6. (Laitz, Ch. 22, pp. 498-502)

# Prerequisites
- **Harmonic sequence** — D2 is the most common sequence type

# Key Properties
1. Root motion: -5/+4 (or +4/-5)
2. Overall descent by step
3. Complete cycle traverses all seven diatonic chords
4. Bass tritone leap required (beginning in major, end in minor)
5. Root-position diminished triads acceptable within the pattern
6. First-inversion variant (+6/3s) creates 10-6 outer-voice counterpoint
