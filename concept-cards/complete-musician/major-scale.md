# Quick Definition
The major scale is a seven-note diatonic scale with the interval pattern W-W-H-W-W-W-H, producing a bright, stable sound characteristic of the major mode.

# Core Definition
The **major scale** consists of a collection of seven different pitch classes with the intervallic order W-W-H-W-W-W-H. Half steps occur between ^3 and ^4 and between ^7 and ^1. The major mode is generally considered brighter and more stable than the minor mode. A major scale can be constructed on each of the 12 pitch classes by applying the same pattern of half and whole steps, a process called **transposition**.

# Prerequisites
- **Diatonic Scale** -- the major scale is a specific type of diatonic scale
- **Half Step and Whole Step** -- the pattern W-W-H-W-W-W-H defines the major scale

# Key Properties
1. Half steps fall between ^3-^4 and ^7-^1
2. The major scale uses every letter name once and only once
3. The name of a key provides two pieces of information: the tonic pitch and the mode
4. The major scale serves as the reference for identifying interval qualities: intervals above ^1 are perfect (P1, P4, P5, P8) or major (M2, M3, M6, M7)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Write each letter name in order from the chosen tonic
2. Apply W-W-H-W-W-W-H by adding sharps or flats as needed
3. Verify that each letter name appears exactly once

## To Recognize:
1. Check for the H-W pattern: half steps only between ^3-^4 and ^7-^1
2. Verify all seven letter names are present

# Context & Application
The major mode dominates Western tonal music and serves as the default reference scale for interval identification.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 11): A-major scale: A-B-C#-D-E-F#-G#-A (three sharps).
**Example 2** (p. 11): F-major scale: F-G-A-Bb-C-D-E-F (one flat).
**Example 3** (p. 10): Schubert's "Gute Nacht" (Example 1.10A) in D major demonstrates the bright major-mode character.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing the major scale with other modes.
  **Correction**: The major scale is equivalent to the Ionian mode. Its defining feature is half steps between ^3-^4 and ^7-^1; natural minor has half steps between ^2-^3 and ^5-^6.
