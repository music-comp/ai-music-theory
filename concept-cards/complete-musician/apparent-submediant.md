# Quick Definition
A vi chord that arises as a byproduct of 5-6 voice-leading motion rather than functioning as an independent harmony -- the vi6 is merely a contrapuntal sonority, not a chord change.

# Core Definition
An apparent submediant occurs when a 5-6 motion above the bass creates what looks like a vi6 chord but functions only as a voice-leading sonority, not an independent harmony. This is analogous to the IV-ii complex: when I moves to ii, a 5-6 motion creates an apparent vi6 that simply avoids parallel fifths. There is no real "chord change" to vi. (Laitz, Ch. 18, pp. 437-438)

# Prerequisites
- **5-6 technique** — The voice-leading motion that generates the apparent chord
- **Contextual analysis** — Must interpret chords by function, not just by label

# Key Properties
1. Arises from 5-6 motion above the bass
2. Not an independent harmony -- a voice-leading byproduct
3. Analogous to the IV-ii complex
4. The "vi6" avoids parallel fifths in the progression

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 438): Wagner, "Mogst du mein Kind" from The Flying Dutchman -- apparent vi6 between I and ii created by 5-6 motion.
