# Quick Definition
Harmonic paradigms are short, memorizable chord-progression models (typically three chords) that represent the basic contrapuntal expansions of tonic and dominant found across all common-practice music.

# Core Definition
**Harmonic paradigms** are short models (usually three chords) representing the basic contrapuntal expansions that underlie all common-practice music. These include neighboring (I-V6-I), passing (I-viio6-I6), chordal skip (I-I6), and their equivalents using V7 inversions and viio7. These paradigms are crucial to all music from the Baroque through popular music today. Composers from different style periods flesh out these same skeletons with distinctive figurations, producing vastly different surfaces over identical underlying structures (Laitz, Ch. 11, pp. 253-254).

# Key Properties
1. Short, memorizable models (typically 3 chords)
2. Must be recognized regardless of key, mode, meter, instrumentation, or texture
3. Same paradigms underlie music across four centuries of common-practice music
4. Types: neighboring, passing, chordal skip, and combinations
5. Include paradigms for both tonic and dominant expansion

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 253-254): Chopin's Etude op. 10 no. 8 -- an eight-measure phrase that is entirely a tonic expansion using I-V6/5-I as a neighboring paradigm, despite the brilliant surface figuration.
