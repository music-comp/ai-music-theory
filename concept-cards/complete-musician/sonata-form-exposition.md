# Quick Definition
The sonata form exposition presents the main thematic and tonal material of the movement, moving from the FTA in the tonic to the STA in a contrasting key, followed by a closing section.

# Core Definition
The exposition is the first main section of sonata form, usually marked by repeat signs. It contains: the FTA in the tonic, a transition (dependent or independent) modulating to the new key, the STA in the contrasting key (V in major, III in minor), and a closing section reinforcing the new key. A codetta may follow the closing section. The exposition typically repeats to balance the longer development and recapitulation (Laitz, Ch. 33).

# Prerequisites
- **Sonata Form** -- the overarching structure
- **First Tonal Area and Second Tonal Area** -- the two key-defined sections

# Key Properties
1. Components: FTA - Transition - STA - Closing Section - (Codetta)
2. FTA establishes tonic; STA establishes contrasting key
3. Transition modulates from FTA to STA
4. Usually marked by repeat signs
5. Medial caesura often separates transition from STA

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Look for repeat signs or double bar marking the end of the exposition
2. Identify FTA material in the tonic
3. Find the transition (look for accidentals in major; removal of accidentals in minor)
4. Locate the STA and its new key
5. Identify the closing section after the STA's PAC

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 757): Beethoven, op. 49, no. 1 -- Exposition: FTA (m. 1, i), DTr (m. 9), STA (m. 16, III), Cl (m. 29, III).

**Example 2** (p. 771): Haydn, Sonata in C -- FTA (mm. 1-19, I), ITr (mm. 20-35), STA (mm. 36-45, V), Cl (mm. 46-62, V), Codetta (mm. 62-67, V).
