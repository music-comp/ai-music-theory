# Quick Definition
The development is the freest section of sonata form, analogous to the digression in binary form, where material from the exposition is transformed through fragmentation, sequence, and exploration of remote key areas.

# Core Definition
The development is usually the freest section in a sonata form, analogous to the digression in a binary form. Composers may introduce new themes, explore remote harmonic areas, and develop thematic and motivic material through fragmentation and sequence. There is often a complete absence of regular phrasing and periodicity. Despite the chaotic surface, a logical unfolding of tonal and melodic events provides coherence. The retransition (RTr) is the final area of the development, where the dominant prepares the return of the tonic in the recapitulation (Laitz, Ch. 33).

# Key Properties
1. Freest section of sonata form
2. Transforms exposition material through fragmentation and sequence
3. Explores remote key areas
4. May introduce new themes
5. Absence of regular phrasing and periodicity
6. Ends with retransition preparing V for the recapitulation
7. Underlying logical tonal plan despite surface complexity

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 758): Beethoven, op. 49, no. 1 -- Development begins with STA variation, introduces new melody in VI, drives to V and interruption.

**Example 2** (p. 772): Haydn, Sonata in C -- Development explores A minor and F major via sequences before retransition.
