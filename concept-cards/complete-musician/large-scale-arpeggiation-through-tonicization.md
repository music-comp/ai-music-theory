# Quick Definition
Large-scale arpeggiation through tonicization occurs when successive tonicizations outline the notes of the tonic triad at a deep structural level, creating a large-scale harmonic arpeggiation.

# Core Definition
When extended tonicizations target harmonies whose roots outline the tonic triad (e.g., i, III, and v in minor), the result is a large-scale arpeggiation. The tonicized harmonies retain their structural functions but together create a deep-level unfolding of the tonic harmony. This technique reveals how surface-level chromaticism can serve underlying tonal coherence (Laitz, Ch. 25, p. 565).

# Prerequisites
- **Extended Tonicization** -- the mechanism creating the arpeggiation

# Key Properties
1. Successive tonicizations target chord tones of the tonic triad
2. Creates structural unity at a deep level
3. Each tonicized harmony retains its local function
4. Common pattern in minor: i-III-v outlines the tonic triad

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 565): Schumann, "Sangers Trost" -- tonicizations of G minor (i), B-flat major (III), and D minor (v) outline the G-minor tonic triad.
