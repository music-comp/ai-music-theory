# Quick Definition
A motive reduced to its most elemental form -- a single characteristic interval (such as a descending second or ascending sixth) that pervades a composition at multiple structural levels.

# Core Definition
A single-interval motive is a motive consisting of just one characteristic interval that pervades the composition at surface and structural levels. Despite its simplicity, a single interval can be a powerful unifying device when it appears in multiple voices, structural levels, and contexts. The interval ^6-^5 in the bass and soprano, for example, can function simultaneously as a surface motive and as a structural melodic motion. (Laitz, Ch. 16, pp. 378-380)

# Prerequisites
- **Motive** — Understanding the concept of motive as a recurring pattern

# Key Properties
1. Consists of only one interval (two pitches)
2. Appears at multiple structural levels
3. Often involves scale-degree specific intervals (e.g., ^6-^5)
4. Can participate simultaneously in melodic, harmonic, and structural domains

# Context & Application
Single-interval motives demonstrate that even the simplest melodic element can generate unity across an entire composition. They are particularly important in analysis because they connect surface events to deep structure.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 378): The interval ^6-^5 appears as a bass motive (vi-V progression) and simultaneously as the soprano's melodic descent, unifying surface and harmonic domains.
