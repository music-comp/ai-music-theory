# Quick Definition
Two ways sentence structure integrates with period form: (1) each phrase of the period is itself a sentence (repeated sentence), or (2) a single sentence spans the entire period, with A/A' as the antecedent and B as the consequent.

# Core Definition
Sentence structure can integrate with period form in two ways: (1) **repeated sentence** -- each phrase of the period is cast as its own sentence, so the period contains two 1:1:2 structures; (2) **single sentence over the period** -- the antecedent phrase forms A and A', and the consequent phrase forms B, so one 1:1:2 structure spans the entire period. The second technique is more dramatic because the sentence and period structures reinforce each other. (Laitz, Ch. 21, pp. 481-482)

# Key Properties
1. Two integration strategies: repeated sentence or single-sentence period
2. In repeated sentence: both phrases have 1:1:2 structure
3. In single-sentence period: antecedent = A+A', consequent = B
4. Single-sentence period is more dramatic
