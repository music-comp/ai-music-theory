# Quick Definition
Binary form describes a complete work that can be parsed into two sections, almost always marked by repeat signs, with classification based on harmonic structure (sectional or continuous) and thematic design (simple, rounded, or balanced).

# Core Definition
Binary form is a two-part structure traceable to well before 1700, common in Baroque suite movements, Classical minuets, and variation themes. The two sections are usually marked by double bars with repeat signs (hence "two-reprise form"). Binary form is classified by two criteria: (1) harmonic structure -- sectional (first section closes on tonic) or continuous (first section closes away from tonic); (2) thematic design -- simple (no material recurs), rounded (opening material returns after a digression), or balanced (closing material from the first section recurs transposed at the end of the second) (Laitz, Ch. 26, pp. 587-593).

# Prerequisites
- **Phrase Model** -- binary form consists of phrases and periods
- **Cadence Types** -- cadences define the sections
- **Modulation** -- continuous binary forms modulate

# Key Properties
1. Two sections, usually marked by double bars with repeats
2. Harmonic structure: sectional (both sections tonally closed) or continuous (first section ends away from tonic)
3. Thematic design: simple, rounded, or balanced
4. A formal conflict can arise between the three-part melodic design and two-part tonal structure in rounded forms
5. The structure resembles a parallel interrupted period at the deepest level

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Identify the two sections (double bars)
2. Determine harmonic structure: does the first section close on tonic (sectional) or not (continuous)?
3. Determine thematic design: does opening material return (rounded)? Does closing material recur (balanced)? Or is the second section entirely new (simple)?

# Context & Application
Binary form is fundamental to Baroque dances (sarabande, gigue, minuet), Classical minuets and scherzos, and variation themes. Understanding binary form provides the foundation for understanding ternary and sonata forms.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 587): Hummel, Bagatelle in C major -- simple sectional binary form (no recurring material, both sections close on tonic).

**Example 2** (p. 588): Weber, Ecossaise no. 1 -- simple continuous binary form (first section tonicizes dominant).

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking "binary" means only two phrases
  **Clarification**: Binary form refers to two large sections, each potentially containing multiple phrases
