# Quick Definition
The repetition of a melodic motive at successively higher or lower pitch levels, creating a pattern of transposition that may or may not involve harmonic sequential motion.

# Core Definition
A melodic sequence is a type of motivic repetition in which a motive is stated at one pitch level and then restated at one or more successive pitch levels, usually ascending or descending by a consistent interval. Melodic sequences may be tonal (following the diatonic intervals of the key) or real (preserving exact intervals). A melodic sequence is distinct from a harmonic sequence: the former involves only the transposition of a melodic pattern, while the latter involves the transposition of a complete contrapuntal/harmonic model. (Laitz, Ch. 16, p. 370; Ch. 22, p. 495)

# Prerequisites
- **Motive** — The pattern being sequenced
- **Tonal vs. real transposition** — Understanding diatonic versus chromatic transposition

# Key Properties
1. A motive is restated at successive pitch levels
2. Can be tonal (diatonic) or real (chromatic)
3. Usually involves two or three restatements
4. Creates directional momentum (ascending or descending)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Identify a melodic pattern that repeats at different pitch levels
2. Determine if the transposition is tonal or real
3. Note the direction and interval of transposition

# Context & Application
Melodic sequences are one of the most common forms of motivic development and appear at all levels of structure, from surface figuration to large-scale thematic design.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 370): A motive repeated at successively higher diatonic pitch levels, with interval qualities changing to match the key (tonal sequence).

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Motivic repetition types** — Melodic sequence is a systematic form of transposed repetition

## Enables
- **Harmonic sequence** — When melodic sequences are harmonized, they can generate harmonic sequences

## Contrasts With
- **Harmonic sequence** — A harmonic sequence involves a complete two-chord contrapuntal model, not just a melodic pattern

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Equating melodic sequence with harmonic sequence
  **Correction**: Melodic sequences involve only a melodic pattern; harmonic sequences involve a full contrapuntal/harmonic model with copies
