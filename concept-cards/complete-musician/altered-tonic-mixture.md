# Quick Definition
Altered tonic mixture occurs when a major tonic is made minor (I becomes i), creating modal ambiguity that often signals an upcoming chromatic modulation to bVI or bIII.

# Core Definition
In addition to b6, b3 can create melodic mixture in altered tonic harmonies. Because making a major tonic minor calls the mode of the entire piece into question, the minor tonic is more often implied rather than literally stated in eighteenth-century music. By the nineteenth century, Schubert and others saturated major-mode pieces with minor elements to such a degree that the 24 keys fused into 12 "major-minor" keys. The modal shift from I to i often signals an upcoming chromatic modulation, as the minor tonic loses its anchoring power and begins to act as a PD for an upcoming bVI or bIII (Laitz, Ch. 27, pp. 621-622; Ch. 28, p. 639).
