# Quick Definition
The characteristic acceleration of harmonic rhythm in the B section of a sentence, where chords change more rapidly than in the A and A' sections, creating a paradox of longer spans with faster internal motion.

# Core Definition
In sentence structures, the A and A' sections typically have slow harmonic rhythm (one or two harmonies per subphrase). The B section, though proportionally longer, features faster harmonic rhythm. This creates a musical paradox: as the subphrases lengthen from A to B, the harmonic motion accelerates internally. The effect generates forward drive toward the cadence. (Laitz, Ch. 21, pp. 479-480)

# Key Properties
1. A/A': slow harmonic rhythm
2. B: fast harmonic rhythm (acceleration)
3. Creates paradox: longer span with faster internal motion
4. Generates forward drive toward cadence

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 479): Beethoven, Piano Sonata no. 1 -- A/A' have one harmony per two measures; B changes harmony every measure or more frequently.
