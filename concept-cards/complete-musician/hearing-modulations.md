# Quick Definition
Hearing modulations involves three strategies: listening for new persistent chromatic pitches, comparing opening and closing modes, and physically sensing whether the closing tonic pitch is higher or lower than the opening.

# Core Definition
Three techniques for detecting modulations aurally: (1) Listen for new chromatic pitches that persist through the end of the phrase. (2) If the first key is major and the closing key is minor (or vice versa), a modulation has occurred. (3) Sing the opening tonic, then sing the closing tonic and compare. If the closing tonic is higher or lower, a modulation has occurred. In major-to-major shifts, the destination is likely V; in minor-to-minor, likely v (Laitz, Ch. 25, p. 570).

# Prerequisites
- **Modulation** -- the concept being detected aurally

# Key Properties
1. Major opening + minor closing = modulation to iii or vi
2. Minor opening + major closing = modulation to III or VI
3. Major to major = likely V
4. Minor to minor = likely v
5. Process of elimination narrows choices
