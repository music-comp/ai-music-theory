# Core Definition
The **chromatic neighbor** (CN) is a neighbor tone that includes one or more nondiatonic pitches. It creates a half-step embellishment that is highly dissonant yet beautiful. Like diatonic neighbors, chromatic neighbors can be unaccented or accented. They appear frequently in variation sets of the late Classical and Romantic periods, where chromatic embellishment provides contrast and drama to earlier diatonic settings (Laitz, Ch. 13, pp. 309-310).

# Key Properties
1. Includes nondiatonic pitch(es) in the neighbor figure
2. Creates half-step motion (more intense than diatonic)
3. Can be accented or unaccented
4. Highly dissonant yet beautiful
5. Common in variations and Romantic-era music
