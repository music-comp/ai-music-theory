# Quick Definition
The insertion of additional musical material (typically a sequence) into the interior of a phrase, extending the phrase without altering its underlying harmonic progression.

# Core Definition
Interpolation occurs when additional chords are inserted between existing structural harmonies of a phrase, extending its length. Sequences are the most common interpolated material. The added chords fit within the phrase and serve to prolong the tonic function or connect tonic to pre-dominant. The underlying T-PD-D-T progression remains intact despite the extended interior. (Laitz, Ch. 22-23, pp. 495-496, 514-517)

# Key Properties
1. Material inserted into the phrase interior
2. Sequences are the most common interpolated material
3. Extends phrase length without altering basic harmonic plan
4. Prolongational or transitional function

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 496): Vivaldi, Concerto Grosso in C minor -- bracketed sequence interpolated between i6 and ii^o-V-i cadence.
