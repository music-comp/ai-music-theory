# Quick Definition
The chromatic D2 (-P5/+P4) sequence uses exclusively perfect fifths (no tritone), descends by whole step, and requires 12 chromatic steps for a complete cycle, making it more goal-directed than the D2 (-P4/+m3).

# Core Definition
The diatonic D2 (-5/+4) sequence is asymmetrical because it contains perfect fifths and a tritone. The chromatic form, D2 (-P5/+P4), replaces the tritone with a perfect fifth, requiring 12 chromatic steps for a complete cycle (versus 7 diatonic steps). To avoid sequential stasis, composers use partial statements or three-or-more-chord models. A variant, D2 (-P5/+A4), descends by minor second by following the perfect fifth with a tritone ascent. The D2 (-P5/+P4) is more goal-directed than the D2 (-P4/+m3) because it moves more naturally by descending perfect fifths (Laitz, Ch. 36).

# Key Properties
1. Uses exclusively perfect fifths (no tritone)
2. Descends by major second (whole tone)
3. Requires 12 steps for complete cycle
4. More goal-directed than D2 (-P4/+m3)
5. Variant D2 (-P5/+A4) descends by minor second
6. Often uses three-chord models to reduce repetitions
