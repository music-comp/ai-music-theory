# Quick Definition
A chromatic pivot-chord modulation uses a mixture chord in the original key as the pivot, since mixture introduces triads common to both the home key and a chromatically related key.

# Core Definition
In a modulation to a chromatic key resulting from modal mixture (such as bIII or bVI), the pivot chord must be a mixture chord in the original key. Without mixture, there are often no triads common to both keys. By permitting modal mixture to enter the equation, potential pivot chords emerge. For example, between C major and Ab major (bVI), no diatonic triads are shared; but adding C minor's triads reveals four potential pivots (Laitz, Ch. 28, pp. 637-639).

# Prerequisites
- **Pivot Chord** -- the general concept of common chords between keys

# Key Properties
1. Pivot must be a mixture chord in the original key
2. The minor tonic is the most effective pivot (becomes vi or iii in the new key)
3. Use the new key signature for clarity after the pivot
4. Expand PD in the new key to give the ear time to adjust
5. To return home, use a chord in the new key that is a mixture chord in the original

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 640): C major to Ab major (bVI) -- F-minor chord serves as pivot: iv in C major, vi in Ab major.

**Example 2** (p. 639): E major to C major (bVI) -- E-minor chord (i in E) becomes iii in C major.
