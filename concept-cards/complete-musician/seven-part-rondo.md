# Quick Definition
The seven-part rondo (A1-B1-A2-C-A3-B2-A4) adds another episode and refrain to the five-part rondo, creating a symmetrical arch form.

# Core Definition
The seven-part rondo creates a symmetrical form likened to a musical arch. Typical key schemes: in major, A1(I)-B1(V or i or IV)-A2(I)-C(i, IV, or vi)-A3(I)-B2(I or i)-A4(I). In minor, B1 in iv, IV, III, VI, or I; C varies; B2 usually in I. As in the five-part rondo, sections are often cast in rounded binary forms. Because the C section may be longer and distinct from the flanking ABA sections, the seven-part rondo can sound like a giant ternary (Laitz, Ch. 32).

# Prerequisites
- **Rondo Form** -- the overarching form
- **Five-Part Rondo** -- the shorter rondo type

# Key Properties
1. Structure: A1-B1-A2-C-A3-B2-A4
2. Symmetrical arch form
3. Four refrains and two episodes plus a central C section
4. B2 often in the tonic (unlike B1 in a contrasting key)
5. Sections often contain nested rounded binary forms

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Identify four refrain statements and at least two different episodes
2. Look for the symmetrical arrangement around the central C section
3. Distinguish from large ternary by considering period, tempo, and nested forms

# Context & Application
Seven-part rondos appear in Classical instrumental finales. They can be confused with large ternary forms; distinguishing criteria include period of composition (18th century favors rondo), tempo (rondos are usually spirited), and the presence of nested binary forms.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 740): Haydn, String Quartet in D major, op. 33, no. 6, Finale -- Seven-part rondo with written-out ornamented repeats in A2.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Five-Part Rondo** -- adds B2 and A4

## Contrasts With
- **Ternary Form** -- can sound like a large ABA but has more internal structure
- **Five-Part Rondo** -- shorter, with fewer returns

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Seven-part rondo vs. large ternary
  **Clarification**: Consider period (rondos in 18th c.), tempo (rondos usually fast), nested forms (more common in rondos), and that seven-part rondos can actually be shorter than five-part rondos due to fewer transitions and codas.
