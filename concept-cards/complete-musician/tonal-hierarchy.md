# Quick Definition
Tonal hierarchy is the ranking of pitches by structural importance within a key, with the tonic as most important, followed by the dominant and mediant, while other pitches serve as connective material.

# Core Definition
**Hierarchy** of pitch and meter is a defining feature of tonal music. The tonic is the most important pitch, supported by satellite pitches (primarily the dominant and mediant) that form the tonic triad. Remaining pitches function as connective material between these structural pillars. Hierarchy is established through multiple types of accent: metric placement, frequency, duration (agogic accent), and melodic patterning.

# Prerequisites
- **Tonality** -- hierarchy exists within the tonal system

# Key Properties
1. Tonic (^1) is the primary tonal pillar
2. Dominant (^5) is the secondary tonal pillar
3. Mediant (^3) completes the tonic triad
4. Hierarchy is reinforced by multiple accent types working together
5. Longer durations (agogic accent) and strong metric placement emphasize structural pitches

# Context & Application
Understanding tonal hierarchy enables analysis of melodic structure, phrase construction, and large-scale form. Pitches that receive multiple types of accent simultaneously create particularly strong structural points.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 28-30): In Bach's Violin Partita no. 3, Prelude, E is the tonic, with G# and B as satellites. The remaining pitches serve as connective material within melodic gestures. Example 1.23 shows two levels of melodic activity: sweeping gestures and stationary patterns.
