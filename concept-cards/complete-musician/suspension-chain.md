# Quick Definition
A suspension chain is a patterned series of interlocking suspensions (typically 7-6) derived from a series of falling 6/3 chords, with outer voices in parallel tenths and suspensions disguising parallel fifths.

# Core Definition
A **suspension chain** is a patterned series of interlocking suspensions, most commonly 7-6 suspensions. It is derived from a series of falling parallel 6/3 chords (which would contain parallel fifths if left undisguised). The suspensions resolve the inner-voice fifths into alternating sixths, with outer voices typically moving in parallel tenths. The chain begins by moving the fifth of a chord up to a sixth, which is then tied over to become the suspended seventh in the next chord, and the pattern continues. Composers may break the chain at a cadence to incorporate a V7 (Laitz, Ch. 13, pp. 319-320).

# Prerequisites
- **Suspension** -- chains are series of individual suspensions
- **Suspension types** -- 7-6 is the most common chain type

# Key Properties
1. Series of interlocking suspensions (typically 7-6)
2. Derived from parallel 6/3 chords (disguising parallel 5ths)
3. Outer voices in parallel tenths
4. Chain often broken at cadence for V7
5. More contrapuntal/melodic than harmonic

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 319-320): Haydn's Piano Sonata Hob. XVI/22: 7-6 suspension chain from m. 63 to m. 67, with outer voices in parallel tenths and alto suspensions. Chain breaks at m. 67 for cadential V7 on G major.
