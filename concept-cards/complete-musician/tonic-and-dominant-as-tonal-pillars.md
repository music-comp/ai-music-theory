# Quick Definition
Tonic and dominant chords form the harmonic foundation of tonal music; the tonic is the ultimate goal, and the dominant creates a powerful expectation of the tonic's return.

# Core Definition
Tonic and dominant chords provide the harmonic foundation of tonal music. By themselves, they can define a key. The tonic stated at the opening need not progress to any particular chord, but the dominant inevitably leads back to the tonic. The V chord's tendency to resolve to I stems from the relationship between its chordal members and those of the tonic: ^7 wants to ascend to ^1, ^2 can move to ^1 or ^3, and ^5 (though stable in the tonic) is unstable as the root of V and tends to return to ^1 (Laitz, Ch. 8, p. 183).

# Prerequisites
- **Consonance and dissonance** -- V contains unstable, tendency tones
- **Triads** -- must understand chord construction

# Key Properties
1. I and V can define a key by themselves
2. Tonic is stable; dominant is unstable and seeks resolution
3. V contains tendency tones (^7, ^2) that drive toward tonic
4. Compositions generally begin on I, progress through non-tonic chords, and return to I
5. The I-V-I progression is the basis for much tonal music

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 184): Wagner's Das Rheingold opens with 136 measures of a single E-flat major tonic triad -- no forward harmonic motion, yet rhythmic growth creates interest.

**Example 2** (p. 185): Beethoven's Eroica Finale, mm. 328-348, sustains dominant harmony with increasing instability as the minor seventh is added, creating powerful expectation of tonic resolution.

**Example 3** (p. 186): Beethoven's Fifth Symphony Finale opens with simple I-V alternation, yet creates forceful, imaginative music through rhythmic and melodic variety.

# Relationships
## Enables
- **Cadence types** -- cadences are defined by I-V relationships
- **Voice leading** -- rules for connecting I and V smoothly
- **Tendency tones** -- active scale degrees in V that resolve to I

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating I and V as interchangeable in function
  **Correction**: I is stable and can begin a phrase without obligation; V is unstable and must resolve
