# Quick Definition
A rhetorical formal device following a short-short-long pattern (1:1:2 proportion): a musical idea is stated (A), restated with variation (A'), then elaborated in a continuation twice as long (B) that leads to a cadence.

# Core Definition
A sentence structure unfolds as a proportional additive pattern of 1:1:2. The first segment (A) presents a basic idea; the second segment (A') restates it with slight variation (often transposed up a step); the third segment (B) is exactly twice as long and develops the material, culminating in a cadence. In an eight-measure sentence, this yields 2+2+4. The B section typically features harmonic rhythm acceleration -- the longer unit is internally fragmented even as the proportional span doubles. Sentences occur as single phrases, as individual phrases within periods, or spanning entire periods. (Laitz, Ch. 21, pp. 477-482)

# Prerequisites
- **Phrase model** — The sentence is a way of organizing harmonic content within phrases
- **Subphrase** — A and A' are subphrases

# Key Properties
1. Proportional structure: 1:1:2 (typically 2+2+4 or 1+1+2)
2. A' often transposes A up a step (tonal transposition)
3. B develops A/A' material, not merely new material
4. Harmonic rhythm typically accelerates in B
5. B often fragments the motivic material
6. Analogous to spoken rhetoric: statement, restatement, elaboration/conclusion

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Write a short musical idea (A) -- typically 2 measures
2. Restate with variation (A') -- same length, often up a step
3. Write a continuation (B) -- twice as long, developing A/A' material
4. Include a cadence at the end of B

## To Recognize:
1. Look for the 1:1:2 proportional pattern
2. Check if the first two segments are related (A and A')
3. Verify that the third segment develops (not merely contrasts) the material
4. Note whether harmonic rhythm accelerates in the longer segment

# Context & Application
Sentence structure is found across all genres: folk tunes ("Clementine"), blues, and art music from the 17th century to the present. It provides a natural rhetorical shape analogous to human speech patterns.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 478): Beethoven, "Lustig-Traurig" in C major, WoO 54 -- A (mm. 1-2, I to V), A' (mm. 3-4, V6/5 to I transposed up), B (mm. 5-8, continuation to cadence).

**Example 2** (p. 479): Beethoven, Piano Sonata no. 1 in F minor, op. 2 -- eight-measure sentence with harmonic rhythm acceleration in B; nested sentence within B (1+1+2 within the 4-measure B).

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Phrase model** — Sentence organizes the phrase's harmonic content

## Enables
- **Nesting of sentences** — B can itself contain a miniature 1:1:2 structure
- **Sentence within period** — Periods can be built from sentence-structured phrases

## Related
- **Fragmentation** — B often fragments A/A' material for development

## Contrasts With
- **Period** — A period is defined by cadential relationships (antecedent-consequent); a sentence by proportional structure (1:1:2). They can coexist.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating the B section as completely new material
  **Correction**: B must develop the material from A/A', not merely introduce contrast

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: A sentence and a period are mutually exclusive
  **Clarification**: A sentence can span a period, or each phrase of a period can be a sentence; they describe different structural principles
