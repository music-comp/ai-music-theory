# Quick Definition
Chromatic alteration is the process by which a chromatic pitch replaces its diatonic counterpart, creating applied chords or other chromatic harmonies.

# Core Definition
Chromaticism colors diatonicism; chromatic pitches are generally not integral to the underlying diatonic structure. Instead, chromatic pitches take the place of diatonic pitches in a process called chromatic alteration. Diatonic chords can be altered chromatically, changing them into applied dominants. The alteration changes the quality of the chord while maintaining voice-leading connections to the surrounding diatonic harmony (Laitz, Ch. 24, p. 538).

# Prerequisites
- **Diatonic and Chromatic Scales** -- understanding which pitches are diatonic vs. chromatic

# Key Properties
1. Chromatic pitches replace diatonic counterparts rather than adding new tones
2. A chromatically raised note typically functions as a temporary leading tone
3. A chromatically lowered note typically functions as a chordal seventh
4. Chromatic alteration can transform diatonic triads into applied dominants

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Identify the diatonic chord to alter
2. Raise or lower the appropriate pitch(es) to create the desired chromatic chord quality

## To Recognize:
1. Look for accidentals not belonging to the key signature
2. Determine whether the altered pitch is a raised leading tone or lowered seventh

# Context & Application
Chromatic alteration serves both melodic and harmonic purposes. Melodically, chromatic passing tones animate texture and provide color. Harmonically, when a chromatic passing tone is supported with its own chord, it becomes an applied dominant that eliminates voice-leading problems (such as parallel fifths) present in the unharmonized version.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 538): In D minor, a C# chromatic passing tone between the tonic and ii chords is harmonized by an A-major chord, creating V/ii and eliminating parallel octaves and fifths.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Adding accidentals without considering voice-leading implications
  **Correction**: Each chromatic alteration creates tendency tones that must resolve properly

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking chromatic alteration changes the underlying key
  **Clarification**: Chromatic alteration operates within the prevailing key; it colors but does not replace the diatonic framework
