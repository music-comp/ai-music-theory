# Quick Definition
Close position packs the three upper voices within an octave; open position spans an octave or more. Adjacent upper voices (S-A, A-T) must stay within an octave of each other.

# Core Definition
**Close position** occurs when the three upper voices (soprano, alto, tenor) are packed within a single octave with no additional chord tones insertable between adjacent voices. **Open position** occurs when the total spacing of the upper voices spans an octave or more. Rule 4: keep adjacent upper voices (S-A and A-T) within an octave of each other; there may be more than an octave between tenor and bass. Begin exercises in close position; it is acceptable to shift to open position, but try to return, since close position makes parallel fifths easier to avoid (potential P5s become harmless P4s) (Laitz, Ch. 8, pp. 196-197).

# Key Properties
1. Close position: upper voices within an octave (no insertable chord tones)
2. Open position: upper voices span more than an octave
3. S-A and A-T must stay within an octave (Rule 4)
4. T-B may exceed an octave
5. Begin in close position for easier part writing
6. Shifting between C and O provides contrasting sonority
