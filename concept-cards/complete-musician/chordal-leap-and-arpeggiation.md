# Quick Definition
A chordal leap (CL) is a leap between two members of the same harmony; three or more successive chord tones form an arpeggiation (ARP).

# Core Definition
**Chordal leaps** (also called **consonant leaps**) are nonstepwise motions between pitches that are both members of the underlying harmony. When three or more tones from the same harmony appear in succession, they form an **arpeggiation** (ARP). Unlike passing tones and neighbor tones, chordal leaps and arpeggiations are consonant embellishments -- they are chord members, not nonchord tones. Laitz therefore prefers the umbrella term "tones of figuration" rather than "nonharmonic tones" for all melodic embellishments (Ch. 7, p. 164).

# Prerequisites
- **Triads** -- must know chord membership
- **Consonance and dissonance** -- chordal leaps are consonant

# Key Properties
1. Leaps between members of the same chord
2. Consonant -- both pitches belong to the underlying harmony
3. Do not require stepwise resolution
4. Arpeggiations involve three or more successive chord tones
5. Permitted only when pitches are members of the underlying harmony

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Identify the underlying harmony
2. Leap between any two or more members of that chord

## To Recognize:
1. Identify a leap in the melody
2. Verify both pitches are members of the same underlying harmony
3. If three or more chord tones appear in succession, label as arpeggiation

# Context & Application
Chordal leaps and arpeggiations are fundamental to constructing melodies and accompaniment patterns. They establish the harmonic context without moving to new harmonies and are especially prominent in opening gestures ("Clementine" arpeggiates the tonic triad).

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 164): In Example 7.4, leaps within the C-major triad (C-E, E-G, etc.) are consonant leaps, while three successive triad members form arpeggiations.

**Example 2** (p. 167): "Clementine" opens with consonant skips and leaps that arpeggiate the F-major tonic triad (F4-A4-C5) on the strong beats of mm. 1-3.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating all melodic embellishments as nonchord tones
  **Correction**: Chordal leaps are consonant embellishments that ARE chord members

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Leaps are always problematic in voice leading
  **Clarification**: Consonant leaps within a single harmony are perfectly acceptable; only dissonant leaps or leaps that cause voice-leading problems are restricted
