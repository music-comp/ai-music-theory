# Quick Definition
The progression I-vi-IV, in which the submediant bridges the tonic and pre-dominant through descending-third root motion, with bass ^1-^6-^4 arpeggiating a triad.

# Core Definition
In the descending-thirds progression I-vi-IV, the submediant provides a way station connecting tonic to pre-dominant. The three roots ^1-^6-^4 arpeggiate a triad, hence the term "harmonic arpeggiation." The vi shares two common tones with I and two with IV, making the voice leading smooth. This progression is characteristic of 1950s rock and roll and remains common across all genres. (Laitz, Ch. 18, pp. 425-427)

# Prerequisites
- **Submediant** — vi is the bridging chord
- **Root motions** — Descending third is one of the three basic root motions

# Key Properties
1. Root motion: I (down 3rd) vi (down 3rd) IV
2. Bass arpeggiates ^1-^6-^4
3. Two common tones at each step; keep them and move the remaining voice up by step
4. vi acts simultaneously as tonic extension and pre-pre-dominant
