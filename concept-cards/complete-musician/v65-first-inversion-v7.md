# Quick Definition
V6/5 places the leading tone (^7) in the bass; it functions as a neighboring chord to tonic (like V6) with the added intensity of the chordal seventh.

# Core Definition
**V6/5** (first inversion of V7) places the chordal third (^7, the leading tone) in the bass. It functions like V6 -- primarily as a **neighboring chord** to root-position tonic. The key difference from V6 is the presence of the chordal seventh (^4), which often appears in the soprano and resolves by step down. The seventh commonly enters as a passing tone (soprano ^5-^4-^3), either accented or unaccented. V6/5 can also follow V or V6, since adding the seventh intensifies the triad. The seventh often participates in a neighboring configuration in contrary motion with the bass (Laitz, Ch. 11, pp. 247-249).

# Prerequisites
- **V7** -- V6/5 is its first inversion
- **Neighboring chord** -- V6/5 functions as a neighbor to I

# Key Properties
1. Bass = ^7 (leading tone, must resolve up to ^1)
2. Functions as neighboring chord (I-V6/5-I)
3. Seventh (^4) resolves down to ^3
4. Often enters as passing seventh (8-7 motion)
5. Can follow V or V6 (intensification)
6. Complete chords preferred for V7 inversions
