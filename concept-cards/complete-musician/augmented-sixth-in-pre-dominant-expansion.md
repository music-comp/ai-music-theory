# Quick Definition
Augmented sixth chords frequently combine with other pre-dominant harmonies (iv, ii, bVI) to expand the pre-dominant function, typically appearing as the last event before the dominant.

# Core Definition
Given that two of its voices are so goal-directed toward scale-degree 5, the augmented sixth chord is usually the last event before the dominant, following either iv(6) or bVI. The augmented sixth often participates in PD expansions involving voice exchanges: iv through a passing 6/4 to iv6, or iv to augmented sixth via chromatic voice exchange. The chord also appears in chromaticized bass descents as the final step before the dominant (Laitz, Ch. 30, pp. 685-688).

# Prerequisites
- **Augmented Sixth Chord** -- the chord being used in expansion

# Key Properties
1. Usually the final PD chord before V
2. Commonly follows iv(6) or bVI
3. Participates in diatonic and chromatic voice exchanges
4. Appears at the end of chromaticized bass descents
5. bVI can be converted to Ger65 by adding #4

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 687): Beethoven, Piano Sonata op. 27 no. 1 -- Ger65-V reiterated seven times with sf markings after iv6.

**Example 2** (p. 688): Chromatic voice exchange between iv and It6: the F and Ab of iv swap to Ab and F# of the It6.
