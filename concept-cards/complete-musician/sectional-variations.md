# Quick Definition
In a sectional variation set, the theme and variations are usually in binary form and tonally closed, creating clear divisions between variations with more substantive changes in harmony, texture, and mode.

# Core Definition
In a sectional variation set, the theme and variations are usually in binary form (often rounded continuous binary) that is tonally closed, and therefore they are clearly separated from one another. Sectional variations feature more substantive changes among variations than continuous variations, including changes in figuration, reharmonization, tonicization of chromatic keys, and changes between major and minor modes. The form of the theme, including its proportions and length, tends not to be altered (Laitz, Ch. 26, pp. 602-603).

# Key Properties
1. Each variation is in binary form (usually rounded continuous)
2. Tonally closed -- clear separations between variations
3. More substantive changes than continuous variations
4. Changes may include figuration, reharmonization, mode change, tonicization
5. Form and proportions of the theme are preserved

# Context & Application
Composers use two strategies to create larger-scale structure: (1) rhythmic crescendo or increasingly elaborate textures leading to a climax; (2) grouping variations into larger units distinguished by mode change or character.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 603): Beethoven, Variations on "Nel cor piu non mi sento" -- six variations demonstrating progressive intensification with a mode change (to parallel minor) in Variation IV.
