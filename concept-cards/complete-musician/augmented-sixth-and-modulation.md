# Quick Definition
The augmented sixth chord's powerful half-step tendencies toward scale-degree 5 make it exceptionally effective for reinforcing and securing a new key after a modulation's pivot chord.

# Core Definition
Because of its half-step tendencies to 5, the augmented sixth chord contains more linear drive toward the dominant than any other PD harmony. Its appearance after a pivot chord is particularly helpful in securing a new key. In some cases, composers juxtapose radically different chromatic harmonies and then confirm the key of the second by means of an augmented sixth chord. For example, bVI can be converted to a Ger65 by substituting #4 for one of its shared pitches, enabling smooth motion to V (Laitz, Ch. 30, pp. 690-692).

# Prerequisites
- **Augmented Sixth Chord** -- the chord being used
- **Modulation** -- the process being reinforced

# Key Properties
1. The augmented sixth provides the strongest possible PD approach to V in the new key
2. bVI can be converted to Ger65 (they share three of four pitch classes)
3. Useful when chromatic harmonies need immediate tonal confirmation
4. Particularly effective after bold chromatic juxtapositions

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 690): Haydn, String Quartet op. 55 no. 1, Menuetto -- tonic serves as pivot (I = IV6 in new key), leading to Ger65 in the new key.

**Example 2** (p. 691): Schubert, Sonatina D. 384 -- bold move from D major to bII (F major), then F converted to augmented sixth resolving to V of A (V).
