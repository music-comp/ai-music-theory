# Quick Definition
The tonic is the central, most stable pitch in a key, serving as the gravitational center toward which all other pitches are drawn.

# Core Definition
The tonic (^1) is the first scale degree and the primary tonal pillar in tonal music. It is the pitch around which the entire tonal hierarchy is organized. A single pitch, labeled using letters A through G plus modifiers (flat, sharp, major, minor), the tonic gives its name to the key.

# Prerequisites
- **Tonality** -- understanding that pitches are organized hierarchically

# Key Properties
1. The tonic is established through frequency of occurrence, metric placement, duration, and function as a point of arrival
2. The tonic gives its name to the key (e.g., E is the tonic of E major and E minor)
3. It functions as the point of rest and stability in a composition
4. The dominant (^5) is the next most important pitch; the mediant (^3) is third

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Listen for the pitch that conveys a sense of arrival, "home," or rest
2. Look for the pitch that appears most prominently on strong beats
3. Identify which pitch opens and closes phrases or sections

# Context & Application
The tonic typically opens and closes musical phrases, sections, and entire compositions. It serves as the "home base" from which musical journeys depart and to which they return.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 28-30): In Bach's Violin Partita no. 3, Prelude, E is the tonic, established through metric prominence, frequent occurrence after bar lines, and its role as a focal point of several types of musical accent. The satellites G# and B form the tonic triad around it.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Tonality** -- the tonic is the central element of the tonal system

## Enables
- **Key** -- the tonic defines the key
- **Scale Degrees** -- all other degrees are named relative to the tonic

## Contrasts With
- **Dominant** -- the dominant (^5) is the secondary tonal pillar, less stable than the tonic

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing the tonic pitch with the tonic triad.
  **Correction**: The tonic pitch is ^1 alone; the tonic triad is the chord built on ^1 (I or i).

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming the first note of a piece is always the tonic.
  **Clarification**: Pieces can begin on any scale degree; the tonic is determined by harmonic context and function, not merely by order of appearance.
