# Quick Definition
Voicing refers to how chord members are distributed in register: close position (within an octave above the bass) or open position (wider spacing); doubling adds a fourth voice by repeating one chord member, usually the root.

# Core Definition
**Close position** arranges chordal members directly above the root within a tight spacing. **Open position** distributes pitches across a wider range, generally wider in the bass for clarity. **Doubling** increases the number of voices from three to four by repeating one member. The most stable member, the **root**, is most often doubled, though fifths and thirds may also be doubled.

# Prerequisites
- **Triads** -- voicing and doubling apply to triad presentation

# Key Properties
1. Close position: all upper voices within an octave span
2. Open position: wider distribution, especially in the bass register
3. Root is the most common note to double
4. Tightly spaced triads in the bass register are hard to hear clearly
5. Spacing affects the perceived quality and clarity of the chord
