# Quick Definition
Chromatic contrary motion extends an underlying harmony by moving outer voices by half step in opposite directions, with inner voices remaining stationary, creating chromatic prolongation of tonic, dominant, or pre-dominant.

# Core Definition
Contrary-motion expansions containing chromaticism prolong underlying harmonies with outer voices moving by half step in opposite directions while inner voices remain stationary. For best harmonization, the initial interval between bass and soprano should comprise an even number of half steps (unison, M2, M3, tritone, m6, m7, etc.). If the initial interval has an odd number of half steps, one voice must be suspended while the other adjusts. Depending on which portion of the chromatic contrary motion is used, tonic, dominant, or pre-dominant can be prolonged (Laitz, Ch. 36).

# Key Properties
1. Outer voices move by half step in opposite directions
2. Inner voices remain stationary
3. Initial interval should have even number of half steps for best results
4. Can prolong tonic, dominant, or pre-dominant
5. Creates tonal ambiguity at the surface
6. Nonfunctional passing chords (e.g., Ger7, Ger-6/5) fill between diatonic chords
