# Quick Definition
A chromatic voice exchange is a voice exchange in which one or both of the exchanged pitches are chromatically altered, producing cross relations that are softened by intervening passing chords.

# Core Definition
A chromatic voice exchange occurs when two voices swap pitches across chords, but the exchanged pitches involve chromatic alteration. For example, in C major, the A/F of a IV6 chord exchange to become the F/A-flat of a ii°65 chord. Chromatic voice exchanges necessarily involve strong cross relations, but intervening passing chords diminish any harshness. They are particularly common in chromatic step-descent bass lines and in augmented sixth chord progressions (Laitz, Ch. 27, p. 626; Ch. 30, p. 688).

# Key Properties
1. Outer voices swap pitches with chromatic alteration
2. Creates cross relations (unavoidable)
3. Intervening passing chords soften the cross relations
4. Common in chromatic bass descents and augmented sixth contexts
5. The exchange is not exact (pitches are enharmonically or chromatically altered)
