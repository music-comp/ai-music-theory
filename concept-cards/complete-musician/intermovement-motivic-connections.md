# Quick Definition
The presence of shared motivic material across different movements of a multi-movement work, creating unity across the entire composition.

# Core Definition
Intermovement motivic connections occur when the same motive, interval pattern, or contour appears in multiple movements of a multi-movement work, linking movements that may otherwise differ in tempo, key, and character. These connections can be surface-level (shared melodic patterns) or structural (shared tonal trajectories). They create a sense of large-scale organic unity. (Laitz, Ch. 16, pp. 388-390)

# Prerequisites
- **Motivic parallelism** — Understanding multi-level motivic presence

# Key Properties
1. Same motivic material appears in different movements
2. Can be surface or structural connections
3. Creates unity across the entire multi-movement work
4. May involve transformations (inversion, augmentation, etc.)

# Context & Application
Intermovement connections are particularly important in analyzing sonatas, symphonies, and string quartets. They demonstrate that a composer conceived the entire work as an organic whole, not just a collection of separate movements.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 389): Bach, Partita for Solo Violin in B minor -- the Corrente and its Double share identical compound-melody structures and harmonic implications despite wildly different surface figurations.
