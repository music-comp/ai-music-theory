# Quick Definition
Writing applied-chord sequences follows four rules: construct the model carefully, replicate voice leading in copies, keep chord roots diatonic, and write complete seventh chords in inversion.

# Core Definition
There are no new voice-leading procedures for writing applied-chord sequences. The four guidelines are: (1) Construct the two-chord model carefully so that voice leading between the two chords and the first chord of the copy is correct; (2) Copies must follow the voice leading of the model; (3) The root of each chord must be diatonic to the key; (4) Write complete seventh chords, especially when they appear in inversion (the only exception is D2 with interlocking sevenths) (Laitz, Ch. 24, p. 556).

# Key Properties
1. Model-copy principle governs all sequences
2. Diatonic roots ensure the sequence remains grounded in the key
3. Complete seventh chords maintain proper voice leading
4. Interlocking sevenths in D2 are the one exception to completeness
