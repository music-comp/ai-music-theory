# Quick Definition
A melodic interval is formed when two pitches sound one after the other (successive); a harmonic interval is formed when two pitches sound simultaneously.

# Core Definition
When one pitch follows another in time, the interval is called a **melodic interval** (or **linear interval**), and we can specify direction (ascending or descending). When two pitches are sounded simultaneously, the interval is called a **harmonic interval** -- we cannot specify contour for harmonic intervals because they sound together.

# Prerequisites
- **Interval** -- melodic and harmonic are the two temporal settings for intervals

# Key Properties
1. Melodic intervals have direction (ascending or descending)
2. Harmonic intervals have no direction (simultaneous)
3. The same interval quality applies whether melodic or harmonic
4. Melodic intervals form melodies; harmonic intervals form chords and counterpoint
