# Quick Definition
Voice leading refers to how individual voices move when connecting chords; the primary rule is to move voices the shortest possible distance while maintaining independence.

# Core Definition
**Voice leading** refers to the way individual voices move from one chord to the next. Voice-leading rules derive from principles of two-voice counterpoint. The overriding principle is: **move the voices the shortest possible distance when changing chords**. This encompasses three techniques: (1) smoothness -- stepwise motion and common-tone retention; (2) register -- each voice maintains its own comfortable tessitura; (3) counterpoint -- contrary/oblique motion preferred, parallel thirds/sixths acceptable, parallel perfect intervals forbidden (Laitz, Ch. 8, pp. 191-197).

# Key Properties
1. Move voices as little as possible (steps and common tones)
2. Resolve tendency tones: ^7 up to ^1 (in outer voices), ^4 down to ^3
3. Retain common tones between chords
4. Maintain voice independence through contrary/oblique motion
5. Never move in parallel perfect fifths, octaves, or unisons
6. Keep adjacent upper voices within an octave of each other
7. Avoid voice crossing and voice overlapping

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Write outer voices first (bass and soprano)
2. Move upper voices in contrary motion to bass
3. Begin in close position
4. Retain common tones; move remaining voices by step
5. Check for parallel perfect intervals

## To Recognize:
1. Identify any parallel P5s, P8s, or unisons between voice pairs
2. Check for voice crossing or overlapping
3. Verify tendency tone resolution
4. Look for leaps larger than a fourth (possible symptom of error)

# Context & Application
Voice leading governs all chord-to-chord connections in tonal music. Good voice leading creates smooth, independent melodic lines within a harmonic framework. These principles apply whether writing chorales, orchestral music, or popular songs.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Leaps of fifths or fourths in two voices simultaneously
  **Correction**: Try switching the pitches between the two voices

- **Error**: Moving all voices in the same direction
  **Correction**: Use contrary motion to the bass; maintain common tones
