# Quick Definition
A phrygian cadence is a special half cadence in minor where iv6 moves to V, with the bass descending by half step from ^6 to ^5 -- creating a powerful, tension-filled arrival on the dominant.

# Core Definition
The **phrygian cadence** (PHRY) is a special type of half cadence in minor in which iv6 moves to V, with the bass descending by half step from ^6 to ^5 and the soprano usually ascending from ^4 to ^5. The falling half step in the bass (the natural half step between ^6 and ^5 in minor) creates intense pathos. Baroque composers frequently used it to close slow inner movements, leaving the listener with a strong expectation of tonic in the following movement. Other voices typically double ^1 to avoid voice-leading errors (Laitz, Ch. 12, pp. 284-285).

# Key Properties
1. iv6-V in minor only
2. Bass: ^6 descends by half step to ^5
3. Soprano: ^4 usually ascends to ^5
4. Other voices double ^1
5. Creates powerful tension and expectation
6. Baroque convention: closes slow inner movements
