# Quick Definition
Chromatic third relations are tonicizations or modulations to keys lying a chromatic third away from the tonic, typically bVI or bIII, achieved through modal mixture pivot chords.

# Core Definition
The major tonic has access to three sets of third relations: diatonic (iii, vi), raised chromatic (III, VI), and lowered chromatic from mixture (bIII, bVI). bVI and bIII are the most commonly tonicized chromatic harmonies. Chromatic third tonicizations function identically to their diatonic counterparts: upper thirds lead to 5 through arpeggiation, lower thirds lead to 4 through arpeggiation (Laitz, Ch. 27, p. 631; Ch. 28, pp. 637-638).

# Prerequisites
- **Modal Mixture** -- provides the chromaticism
- **Harmonic Mixture** -- creates bVI and bIII

# Key Properties
1. Three sets of third relations available: diatonic, raised chromatic, lowered chromatic
2. bVI and bIII are the most commonly tonicized
3. VI and III (raised chromatic, without flat) are less common
4. Minor tonic's options are limited (usually only diatonic III and VI)
5. Function identically to diatonic counterparts in larger harmonic progressions

# Context & Application
Chromatic third relations became a hallmark of nineteenth-century music, with Schubert as a primary practitioner. They often serve dramatic or expressive purposes and can create large-scale tonal plans involving chromatic key areas.
