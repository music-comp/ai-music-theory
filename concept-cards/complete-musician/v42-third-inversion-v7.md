# Quick Definition
V4/2 places the chordal seventh (^4) in the bass; it is the least stable V7 inversion and typically resolves to I6 through a passing or neighboring motion, often including a voice exchange.

# Core Definition
**V4/2** (third inversion of V7) places the chordal seventh (^4) in the bass. Given the prominent placement of the dissonant seventh, V4/2 is the least stable of the inverted dominants. It typically participates in tonic expansion from I to I6, often including a voice exchange. The seventh in the bass must resolve down by step to ^3 (the bass of I6). Composers often prepare the seventh as a passing tone. V4/2 can also upper-neighbor I6. At cadences, placing the seventh in the bass creates an **evaded cadence** because the required resolution to I6 prevents strong closure (Laitz, Ch. 11, pp. 250-252).
