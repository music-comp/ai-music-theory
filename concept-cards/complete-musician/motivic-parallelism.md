# Quick Definition
The recurrence of a motivic pattern at multiple structural levels -- the same contour or interval pattern appearing both on the musical surface and in the deeper structure of a passage.

# Core Definition
Motivic parallelism occurs when a motive operates simultaneously or successively at different structural levels -- for example, the same ascending-third contour appears as a surface motive and also as the large-scale trajectory of the soprano over several measures. This multi-level presence creates deep organic coherence, linking foreground events to background structure. (Laitz, Ch. 16, pp. 380-384)

# Prerequisites
- **Motive** — Must identify the surface motive
- **Two-level analysis** — Must distinguish surface from structural events

# Key Properties
1. The same pattern appears at multiple hierarchical levels
2. Creates organic unity between surface and deep structure
3. Often discovered through reductive analysis
4. A defining feature of the masterworks of tonal music

# Context & Application
Motivic parallelism is a hallmark of sophisticated tonal composition. Schenker and other analysts have shown how the great composers embed motivic shapes at every level of structure, from the immediate surface to the large-scale tonal plan.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 380): A descending-third motive on the surface is mirrored by a large-scale soprano descent of a third over an entire phrase.
