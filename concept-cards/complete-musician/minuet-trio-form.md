# Quick Definition
Minuet-trio form is a large compound ternary structure in which a minuet (A) is followed by a lighter trio (B), after which a da capo marking indicates the minuet is repeated, with nested binary forms within each section.

# Core Definition
The Baroque minuet was a type of dance that remained popular in the Classical period, becoming a standard inner movement in symphonies and chamber pieces. The Classical minuet is followed without pause by a companion piece of lighter texture called a trio, after which a da capo marking indicates the minuet is to be repeated. The result is a large ternary form called minuet-trio form. Haydn and Beethoven often transformed the minuet into a more spirited scherzo. As with many composite forms, the minuet-trio is often a compound ternary with nested binary forms (Laitz, Ch. 31).

# Key Properties
1. Large ternary structure: Minuet (A) -- Trio (B) -- Minuet da capo (A)
2. Trio is typically lighter in texture than the minuet
3. Each section usually contains a nested rounded binary form
4. Da capo creates the return of the minuet
5. Trio often in a contrasting key (parallel major/minor or related key)

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 716): Beethoven, Piano Sonata no. 1 in F minor, op. 2, no. 1, Menuetto-Trio -- Full sectional ternary with minuet in F minor and trio in F major (parallel major), both in rounded continuous binary form.
