# Quick Definition
A hemiola is a metrical disturbance in which the rhythmic grouping temporarily implies a shift between duple and triple organization (e.g., two measures of 3/4 sounding like three measures of 2/4, or vice versa).

# Core Definition
A **hemiola** is a rhythmic device that regroups beats to create a temporary shift between duple and triple patterns. The most common hemiola occurs when two measures of triple meter (3/4) are regrouped into three groups of two, creating a momentary duple feel. The opposite also occurs: three groups of two can be regrouped into two groups of three. The ratio is always 3:2.

# Prerequisites
- **Meter** -- hemiola disrupts the prevailing meter
- **Simple Meter** and **Compound Meter** -- hemiola plays between duple and triple

# Key Properties
1. Always involves a 3:2 ratio
2. Most common: two bars of 3/4 heard as three bars of 2/4
3. Does not change the time signature -- the disruption is temporary
4. Creates a sense of broadening or deceleration, often at cadences
5. Common at phrase endings in Baroque and Classical music

# Context & Application
Hemiola is a powerful rhythmic device used across centuries of Western music. It is particularly common in Baroque dance movements (minuets, courantes) and in Brahms's music.

# Relationships
## Related
- **Syncopation** -- both are metrical disturbances, but syncopation displaces individual accents while hemiola regroups entire beat patterns
