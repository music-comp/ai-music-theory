# Quick Definition
A comprehensive summary of how all seven diatonic harmonies participate in the phrase model through the three basic root motions (descending 5th, ascending 2nd, descending 3rd), showing every chord's functional roles and voice-leading connections.

# Core Definition
The synthesis of diatonic harmonic relationships summarizes how each of the seven diatonic triads (I, ii, iii, IV, V, vi, vii^o) participates in the phrase model. All harmonic motion can be reduced to three root motions: descending fifth, ascending second, and descending third. Each chord may serve multiple functional roles depending on context. The hierarchy: I and V are tonal pillars; ii and IV are primary pre-dominants; vi bridges T and PD or substitutes for T; iii participates in arpeggiations and descending fifths; vii^o is a contrapuntal dominant substitute. (Laitz, Ch. 19, pp. 454-460)

# Key Properties
1. Three root motions govern all diatonic progressions
2. Every chord has multiple potential functions depending on context
3. I and V are tonal pillars
4. ii, IV are primary PDs; vi can be PD or T extension; iii extends T
5. vii^o is a contrapuntal chord, not a structural dominant
6. This synthesis is prerequisite knowledge for analyzing larger forms (sonata, etc.)

# Context & Application
This synthesis provides the comprehensive framework needed before studying larger forms (binary, ternary, sonata). Understanding how every diatonic chord functions and how the three root motions generate all progressions is essential preparation.
