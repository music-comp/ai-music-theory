# Quick Definition
A contrapuntal technique in which a motive stated in one voice is immediately taken up by another voice, usually at a different pitch level.

# Core Definition
Imitation is the restatement of a motive in a different voice shortly after its initial presentation, typically at a different pitch level (often at the octave, fifth, or fourth). When imitation is strict and sustained, it becomes a canon. Imitation is one of the most important methods of distributing motivic material across voices and creating contrapuntal texture. (Laitz, Ch. 16, pp. 376-377)

# Prerequisites
- **Motive** — The melodic material being imitated
- **Contrapuntal motions** — Understanding voice relationships

# Key Properties
1. A motive in one voice is restated by another voice after a time delay
2. The imitation may be at the unison, octave, fifth, fourth, or any interval
3. Strict imitation at a consistent interval creates canon
4. Free imitation may modify intervals to stay within the key

# Context & Application
Imitation is fundamental to fugue, canon, and all contrapuntal genres. It also appears extensively in homophonic textures as a means of distributing motives across voices.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon

## Related
