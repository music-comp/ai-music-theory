# Quick Definition
A series of phrases punctuated by weak cadences, none of which is strong enough to close the passage -- lacking the antecedent-consequent relationship that defines a period.

# Core Definition
A phrase group (or phrase chain) is a succession of phrases that do not form a true period because no final cadence is strong enough to provide closure to the preceding phrases. Unlike an asymmetrical period, where the final cadence creates a clear point of arrival, a phrase group simply strings phrases together without a hierarchical cadential relationship. (Laitz, Ch. 21, p. 485)

# Key Properties
1. Multiple phrases in succession
2. No single strong cadence provides closure
3. Lacks the antecedent-consequent relationship
4. Not a period

# Relationships
## Contrasts With
- **Period** — Periods have a clear cadential hierarchy; phrase groups do not
- **Asymmetrical period** — Asymmetrical periods have a strong final cadence
