# Quick Definition
The submediant functioning directly as the pre-dominant chord, preceding V without an intervening ii or IV, creating the bass motion ^6-^5.

# Core Definition
Occasionally, vi immediately precedes V and functions as the pre-dominant. The vi-V progression produces the bass motive ^6-^5, which has motivic significance in many works. The strong outer-voice counterpoint 10-5-10 underpins this progression and makes it attractive to composers. (Laitz, Ch. 18, pp. 430-431)

# Prerequisites
- **Submediant** — vi is the chord in question
- **Pre-dominant function** — vi takes on this role directly

# Key Properties
1. vi moves directly to V without intervening ii or IV
2. Bass motion ^6-^5 has motivic potential
3. Outer-voice counterpoint: 10-5-10
4. Relatively uncommon but effective

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 430): Mahler, Symphony no. 2 ("Resurrection"), "Urlicht" -- vi functions as PD with bass ^6-^5.

**Example 2** (p. 430): Chopin, Scherzo no. 2 in B minor, op. 31 -- same vi-V progression in the same key, 70 years earlier than Mahler.
