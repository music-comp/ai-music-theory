# Quick Definition
A metrical figuration occupies one full metrical unit (one beat); because it lasts a full beat, it is usually harmonized (made consonant), yet remains subordinate to the controlling harmony.

# Core Definition
A **metrical figuration** is any tone of figuration whose duration occupies one complete metrical unit (one beat). For a tone of figuration to occupy an entire metrical unit, it must typically be rendered consonant by harmonization. Thus, metrical figurations are usually harmonized, creating subordinate chords. A **submetrical figuration** occupies less than one beat and need not be harmonized. A **supermetrical figuration** extends beyond one beat and is always harmonized (Laitz, Ch. 9, pp. 213-214).

# Prerequisites
- **Passing tone** -- metrical figurations are often harmonized passing tones
- **Harmonic rhythm** -- metrical figuration relates to the rate of harmonic change

# Key Properties
1. Occupies exactly one metrical unit (beat)
2. Usually harmonized (made consonant by supporting chord)
3. Creates subordinate harmonies within a controlling harmony
4. Contrasts with submetrical (<1 beat, often unharmonized) and supermetrical (>1 beat, always harmonized)

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 213): In Example 9.6A, D5 in m. 1 occupies one full beat and is harmonized by V -- a metrical passing tone within a controlling tonic harmony.
