# Quick Definition
A chromatic passing tone fills the space between two diatonic pitches a major second apart with a half step, creating a miniature leading tone that intensifies melodic direction.

# Core Definition
**Chromatic passing tones** (CPTs) fill the smaller intervallic space between diatonic stepwise pitches. Most often, the diatonic pitches are separated by a major second, creating half-step motions. Less often, CPTs fill a third. Ascending CPTs are more common than descending ones because they create miniature leading tones, intensifying melodic direction. CPTs can be unaccented or accented. The continuum from unaccented diatonic PT to accented chromatic PT represents increasing aural prominence (Laitz, Ch. 13, pp. 306-308).

# Prerequisites
- **Passing tone** -- CPT is its chromatic variant

# Key Properties
1. Fills space between diatonic pitches (usually M2 apart) with half steps
2. Ascending CPTs more common (create leading-tone effect)
3. Can be accented or unaccented
4. Increases melodic intensity beyond diatonic PT
5. Performance markings: Mozart uses two-note slurs to indicate emphasis

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 307-308): In Mozart's Piano Sonata K. 570, an accented CPT (E-natural) on beat 2 of m. 1 fills the space between Eb and F, creating a striking dissonance emphasized by a two-note slur.
