# Quick Definition
A borrowed division (tuplet) uses the beat division from the opposite meter type: triplets borrow ternary division in simple meter, duplets borrow binary division in compound meter.

# Core Definition
A **borrowed division** occurs when a beat is divided according to the opposite meter type. In simple meter, a **triplet** divides the beat into three equal parts (borrowing from compound meter). In compound meter, a **duplet** divides the dotted beat into two equal parts (borrowing from simple meter). Borrowed divisions are indicated by a number (usually 3 for triplets) placed above or below the note group, often with a bracket.

# Prerequisites
- **Beat Division** -- borrowed divisions alter the normal beat division
- **Simple Meter** and **Compound Meter** -- the "loan" crosses between these types

# Key Properties
1. Triplets in simple meter: three notes in the space of two
2. Duplets in compound meter: two notes in the space of three
3. Notated with a number (3, 2, etc.) and often a bracket
4. Other tuplets (quintuplets, sextuplets, etc.) are also possible
5. Borrowed divisions add rhythmic variety and expressiveness
