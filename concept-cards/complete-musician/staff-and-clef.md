# Quick Definition
A staff is a set of horizontal lines and spaces that, in conjunction with a clef, represents specific pitches; the most common clefs are treble (G) and bass (F), often combined into a grand staff.

# Core Definition
A **staff** (plural: **staves**) contains horizontal lines and spaces, each of which, in conjunction with a **clef**, represents a specific pitch. The **treble clef** (or G clef) indicates the location of G above middle C; the **bass clef** (or F clef) indicates the location of F below middle C. **C clefs** indicate middle C and may appear on different lines (alto clef, tenor clef). The **grand staff** combines treble and bass clefs, connected by a brace. **Ledger lines** extend a staff to accommodate pitches beyond its range. The **octave sign** (8va, 8vb) indicates performance one octave higher or lower than written.

# Prerequisites
- No formal prerequisites

# Key Properties
1. The treble clef encircles the G line; the bass clef's two dots bracket the F line
2. Only two C clefs remain common: alto (viola) and tenor (trombone, bassoon, cello)
3. Ledger lines extend the staff for pitches beyond its five-line range
4. The octave sign (8va above = one octave higher; 8vb below = one octave lower)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Identify the clef at the beginning of the staff
2. Use the clef to determine the reference pitch
3. Count lines and spaces from the reference to identify pitches

# Context & Application
Fluent reading of both treble and bass clefs is essential. The grand staff is standard for keyboard instruments and vocal scores.
