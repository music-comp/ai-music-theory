# Quick Definition
The dominant seventh chord (V7) is a major-minor seventh chord (Mm7) built on ^5, the most common seventh chord in tonal music, containing the tritone between ^7 and ^4 that drives resolution to the tonic.

# Core Definition
The **major-minor seventh chord** (Mm7) is the most common seventh chord in tonal music. It is called the **dominant seventh chord** because it most regularly appears on the dominant scale degree (^5). V7 = ^5-^7-^2-^4. It contains the crucial tritone between ^7 (leading tone) and ^4, whose resolution drives the V7-I motion. The seventh (^4) must always resolve down by step to ^3. The tritone resolves by contrary motion: d5 contracts to a third, A4 expands to a sixth.

# Key Properties
1. Built on ^5 in both major and minor keys
2. Structure: major triad + minor seventh = Mm7 (^5-^7-^2-^4)
3. Contains the tritone between ^7 and ^4
4. ^7 resolves up to ^1; ^4 (the chordal seventh) must resolve down to ^3
5. Complete V7 resolves to incomplete I (tripled root); incomplete V7 resolves to complete I
6. ^7 in an inner voice may fall to ^5 to create a complete I chord
7. V7 at authentic cadences; V triad (not V7) at half cadences
8. Common from the early seventeenth century through blues, jazz, and all tonal idioms
9. Figured bass: 8-7 shows the passing seventh's voice leading

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Build a major triad on ^5
2. Add a minor seventh above the root (^4)
3. Resolve: ^7 up, ^4 down, ^2 to ^1 or ^3, ^5 to ^1

## To Recognize:
1. Identify a major-minor seventh chord built on the fifth scale degree
2. Listen for the characteristic tension demanding resolution

# Context & Application
The V7 is the harmonic engine of tonal music. Its resolution to I (or i) is the most fundamental harmonic progression. Understanding V7 and its resolution is prerequisite to all further harmonic study.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 140): In C major, V7 = G-B-D-F; the tritone B-F resolves to C-E.

# Relationships
## Related
- **Tendency Tones** -- all four members of V7 are tendency tones
- **Tritone** -- the V7 contains the key-defining tritone

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Using V7 at a half cadence.
  **Correction**: Use the dominant triad at half cadences; V7 is for authentic cadences.

- **Error**: Resolving the seventh (^4) upward.
  **Correction**: ^4 must always descend by step to ^3.
