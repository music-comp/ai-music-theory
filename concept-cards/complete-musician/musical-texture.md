# Quick Definition
Musical texture refers to the number of voices, their spacing, and how melody is projected; the three basic types are monophonic (single line), polyphonic (multiple independent melodies), and homophonic (melody with accompaniment).

# Core Definition
**Texture** is a general term referring to many elements of music, including register and timbre of instrumental combinations. In particular, texture refers to music's density (number of voices and their spacing). Three basic categories exist, each distinguished by how the melody is projected: **monophonic** (single line, no accompaniment), **polyphonic** or **contrapuntal** (two or more independent melodies), and **homophonic** (melody with accompaniment). Homophonic texture is the most widespread in common-practice music.

# Prerequisites
- **Counterpoint** -- polyphonic texture involves counterpoint
- **Triads** -- homophonic texture distributes chords in various ways

# Key Properties
1. Monophonic: single line, even if doubled at the octave by multiple instruments
2. Polyphonic (contrapuntal): multiple independent melodies with no clear distinction between melody and accompaniment
3. Homophonic: a clear melody accompanied by other voices; the most varied and common type
4. Texture can change within a piece
5. **Harmonic rhythm** -- the rate of harmonic change -- varies by texture and style

# Context & Application
Recognizing texture is essential for harmonic analysis. In homophonic textures, chord members may be distributed across registers and instruments; in polyphonic textures, harmonies are implied by the convergence of independent lines.
