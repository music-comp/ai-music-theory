# Quick Definition
A character piece is a short, expressive work often written for solo piano, common in the nineteenth century, frequently cast in ternary form and featuring descriptive or evocative titles.

# Core Definition
The character piece was a new addition to the nineteenth century, replacing earlier forms like the da capo as the favored vehicle for ternary-form structures. Examples include the bagatelles of Beethoven, the impromptus and Moment musicales of Schubert, and the ballades, nocturnes, polonaises, and mazurkas of Chopin. Composers influenced by literature wrote entire cycles with descriptive titles, such as Schumann's Papillons and Carnival. Character pieces continued throughout the nineteenth century in the works of Liszt, Brahms, Scriabin, and Grieg (Laitz, Ch. 31).

# Key Properties
1. Short, expressive work, usually for solo piano
2. Often cast in ternary form (sometimes with hybrid elements)
3. Frequently bears descriptive or evocative titles
4. Nineteenth-century genre replacing Baroque da capo forms
5. Often features motivic transformation linking sections

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 719): Chopin, Mazurka in A minor, op. 17, no. 4 -- A character piece in ternary form with nested aba form in the A section, featuring motivic connections between sections.

**Example 2** (p. 703): Grieg, "Hjemve" ("Homesickness"), op. 57, no. 6 -- A character piece in full sectional ternary form.
