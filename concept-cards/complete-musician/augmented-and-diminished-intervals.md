# Quick Definition
Augmented intervals are a half step larger than major or perfect; diminished intervals are a half step smaller than minor or perfect. Both are dissonant.

# Core Definition
Increasing the size of a major or perfect interval by a half step (by raising the upper note or lowering the lower note) produces an **augmented interval** (A or +). Decreasing the size of a minor or perfect interval by a half step produces a **diminished interval** (d or deg). Major and minor intervals can never become perfect, and vice versa. The complete quality spectrum: diminished < minor < major < augmented (for 2nds, 3rds, 6ths, 7ths) and diminished < perfect < augmented (for unisons, 4ths, 5ths, octaves).

# Key Properties
1. Augmented = P or M + half step; Diminished = P or m - half step
2. All augmented and diminished intervals are dissonant
3. Common diatonic examples: A4 (^4 to ^7 in major), d5 (^7 to ^4 in major), A2 (^6 to ^7 in harmonic minor)
4. The size can be changed by altering either the upper or lower note
5. Abbreviations: A (or +) for augmented, d (or deg) for diminished

# Construction / Recognition
## To Transform:
1. To augment M or P: raise upper note or lower lower note by a half step
2. To diminish m or P: lower upper note or raise lower note by a half step
3. Major can become augmented (not perfect); minor can become diminished (not perfect)

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 77): Eb up to A: fifth, but A is not in Eb major (Ab is), so the interval is a half step larger than P5 = A5.
**Example 2** (p. 77): F# up to Ab: third, but Ab is smaller than a minor third from F#, so d3.
