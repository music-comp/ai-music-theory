# Quick Definition
viio7 is a fully diminished seventh chord (^7-^2-^4-^6) that shares three pitches with V7 and functions as its substitute for tonic expansion; it is more dissonant (two tritones, no common tones with I) and occurs primarily in minor.

# Core Definition
The **leading-tone seventh chord** (viio7) is a fully diminished seventh chord built on ^7 in minor: ^7-^2-^4-^6. It shares three pitches with V7 (^7, ^2, ^4) and adds ^6, which V7 lacks. It is more dissonant than V7 because it contains two tritones (^7-^4 and ^2-^6) plus a diminished seventh (^7-^6), and shares no common tones with tonic. Its inversions function identically to their V7 counterparts: viio7 = V6/5 (neighbor), viio6/5 = V4/3 (passing), viio4/3 = V4/2 (passing/neighbor to I6). viio4/2 is rare, usually neighboring root-position V. The fully diminished seventh appears more often in minor because it occurs naturally within the minor scale (Laitz, Ch. 11, pp. 257-265).

# Prerequisites
- **V7** -- viio7 is its substitute, sharing three pitches
- **viio6** -- viio7 extends viio6 by adding ^6

# Key Properties
1. Pitches: ^7-^2-^4-^6 (fully diminished seventh chord)
2. Two tritones: ^7-^4 and ^2-^6
3. No common tones with tonic (unlike V7 which shares ^5)
4. All four pitches are tendency tones
5. Primarily in minor keys
6. Voice leading: ^6 prepared by step, resolves down to ^5; ^4 down to ^3; ^7 up to ^1; ^2 up to ^3 (or down to ^1)
7. Resolution often produces doubled ^3 in tonic chord

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **viio6** -- adds ^6 to create the seventh chord
- **V7** -- shares ^7, ^2, ^4; substitutes ^6 for ^5

## Contrasts With
- **Half-diminished seventh chord** -- viio7 (in major) has minor seventh, less dissonant
