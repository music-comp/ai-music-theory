# Quick Definition
The chromatic scale is the collection of all 12 pitch classes, each separated by a half step, serving as the complete repository of available pitches in Western music.

# Core Definition
The **chromatic system** encompasses all 12 possible pitch classes; arranged in order by half steps, they form the **chromatic scale**. The term derives from the Latin *scala* (ladder/staircase). The chromatic scale is more a collection of possible pitches than a scale used as the basis of a composition. By convention, sharps and naturals are used ascending, flats and naturals descending.

# Prerequisites
- **Half Step and Whole Step** -- the chromatic scale moves entirely by half steps
- **Accidentals** -- needed to name all 12 pitch classes

# Key Properties
1. Contains all 12 pitch classes, each a half step apart
2. Pitches within a diatonic scale are called diatonic; those outside are called chromatic (from Latin *chroma*, "color")
3. Convention: sharps ascending, flats descending
4. Two kinds of half steps: diatonic half steps (two different letter names, e.g., G#-A) and chromatic half steps (same letter name, e.g., A-Ab)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Begin on any pitch
2. Ascend by half steps through all 12 pitch classes
3. Use sharps for ascending motion, flats for descending

# Context & Application
The chromatic scale is the master collection from which diatonic scales draw their seven pitch classes. As compositions incorporate more non-diatonic pitches, the music becomes increasingly chromatic.
