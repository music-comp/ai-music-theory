# Quick Definition
Tonality is the system of organizing pitches around a central pitch (the tonic), creating a hierarchy of stability and instability that drives musical motion.

# Core Definition
Tonality refers to the hierarchical organization of pitches around a single pitch class called the tonic. Western music written during the Baroque, Classical, and Romantic periods (c. 1650-c. 1900), called **tonal music** or music of the **common-practice period**, has a gravitational center around which all pitches orbit. This phenomenon is called tonality, and the gravitational center is called the tonic.

# Prerequisites
- No formal prerequisites; this is the most foundational concept in the text.

# Key Properties
1. All pitches in a tonal composition relate hierarchically to a single central pitch (the tonic)
2. Tonality creates expectations of tension and resolution that give music directed motion
3. The common-practice period spans c. 1650-1900, but tonality persists in film scores, popular music, folk music, and jazz

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Listen for a pitch that feels like "home" or a point of rest
2. Identify which pitch other pitches seem to gravitate toward
3. Check for a consistent key signature and diatonic pitch collection

# Context & Application
Tonality is the foundational organizing principle of Western art music and most popular music. It governs melodies, harmonies, and large-scale formal structures as composers move away from and return to the tonic.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 3): Schubert's "Gute Nacht" from Winterreise demonstrates tonality by establishing D as the tonic, with all pitches gravitating toward D as a point of stability.
**Example 2** (p. 28-30): Bach's Violin Partita no. 3, Prelude, shows how E functions as the tonic through metric accent, frequency of appearance, and its role as a point of arrival.

# Relationships
## Enables
- **Key** -- tonality is the system; a key is a specific instance within it
- **Tonic** -- the gravitational center of a tonal composition
- **Tonal Hierarchy** -- the ranking of pitch importance that tonality creates

## Related
- **Scale Degrees** -- each pitch's function within the tonal hierarchy
- **Mode** -- major or minor quality within the tonal system

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing tonality (the system) with key (a specific tonic and mode).
  **Correction**: Tonality is the broader organizational system; a piece "in D minor" has a key of D minor operating within the tonal system.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming tonality requires a key signature.
  **Clarification**: Tonality is an aural phenomenon of pitch hierarchy; key signatures are notational conventions that reflect it.
