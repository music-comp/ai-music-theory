# Quick Definition
Six common modifications of the basic suspension pattern: embellished suspensions, change of bass, restruck suspensions, double/triple suspensions, retardations (upward resolution), and suspension chains.

# Core Definition
Six common modifications of the basic suspension pattern include: (1) **Embellished suspensions**: submetrical figurations embellish the dissonant note. (2) **Suspension with change of bass**: bass leaps to another consonance at the point of resolution, often producing a harmonic change. (3) **Restruck suspensions**: the tie is omitted, and the suspended note is resounded at the point of dissonance. (4) **Double and triple suspensions**: two or three simultaneous dissonances, each resolving correctly. (5) **Retardations** (7-8): upward-resolving suspensions, relatively rare. (6) **Suspension chains**: patterned series of interlocking suspensions, derived from series of parallel 6/3 chords with alternating suspensions disguising the parallels (Laitz, Ch. 13, pp. 318-320).

# Key Properties
1. Embellished: submetrical ornaments during the dissonant stage
2. Change of bass: bass moves at point of resolution (figured bass shows it: e.g., 7-3)
3. Restruck: tie omitted, suspended note re-attacked
4. Double/triple: multiple simultaneous suspensions
5. Retardation: only upward type used is 7-8
6. Chains: interlocking series, typically 7-6, derived from parallel 6/3 chords

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 319-320): Haydn's Piano Sonata Hob. XVI/22 demonstrates a 7-6 suspension chain derived from parallel six-three chords, with outer voices in parallel tenths and suspensions in the alto disguising parallel fifths.
