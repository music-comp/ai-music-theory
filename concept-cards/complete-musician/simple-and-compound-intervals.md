# Quick Definition
Simple intervals span an octave or less; compound intervals span more than an octave and are often reduced to their simple equivalents for labeling.

# Core Definition
Intervals no larger than the octave are called **simple intervals**. Intervals larger than the octave are called **compound intervals**. Compound intervals up to a twelfth are named directly (ninth, tenth, eleventh, twelfth); larger ones are labeled as "compound" plus their simple equivalent (e.g., "compound sixth" or "octave plus a sixth").

# Prerequisites
- **Interval** -- simple/compound is a classification of interval size
- **Octave** -- the boundary between simple and compound

# Key Properties
1. Simple: unison through octave (1 through 8)
2. Compound: ninth through twelfth are named; larger intervals use "compound" prefix
3. Compound intervals retain the quality of their simple equivalent (a compound M3 = M10)
4. For analysis, compound intervals are often reduced to their simple forms
