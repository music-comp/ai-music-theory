# Quick Definition
The two component phrases of a period: the antecedent ends with a weaker cadence (creating an open question), and the consequent ends with a stronger cadence (providing the answer and closure).

# Core Definition
The **antecedent** phrase ends with a weaker cadence (HC, IAC, or another inconclusive ending) that creates expectation and demands continuation. The **consequent** phrase ends with a stronger cadence (PAC) that fulfills the expectation and provides closure. The relationship is analogous to a question and answer. The antecedent may begin similarly (parallel) or differently (contrasting) from the consequent. (Laitz, Ch. 20, pp. 464-466)

# Key Properties
1. Antecedent: weaker cadence (HC or IAC)
2. Consequent: stronger cadence (PAC)
3. Together they form a period
4. Melodic opening can be parallel or contrasting

# Relationships
## Related
- **Period** — Antecedent and consequent are the components of a period
- **Harmonic interruption** — In interrupted periods, the antecedent's HC creates the interruption
