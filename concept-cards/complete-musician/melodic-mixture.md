# Quick Definition
Melodic mixture is a type of modal mixture in which a chromatic alteration changes the quality of a chord without altering its root, most commonly involving b6 to create ii°, ii-half-dim-7, or iv.

# Core Definition
Melodic mixture is chromatic alteration that changes the quality of a chord but does not alter its root. The most common scale degree involved is b6, which transforms the most common pre-dominants: ii becomes ii° (diminished triad), ii7 becomes ii-half-dim-7, and IV becomes iv (minor triad). The resulting chords are labeled as if in a minor key (Laitz, Ch. 27, pp. 618-619).

# Key Properties
1. Root of the chord remains unchanged
2. Quality changes (major to minor, minor to diminished)
3. b6 is the most commonly altered scale degree
4. Four reasons b6 is most common: (a) least disruptive to key; (b) creates half-step to dominant; (c) colors all PD harmonies; (d) only non-tonic scale degree supported by harmony with 1 in bass

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 619): Schumann, "Ich grolle nicht" -- b6 creates ii°7 on the word "heart" (Herz), shattering the major mode expressively.
