# Quick Definition
Sectional ternary form is a ternary form in which either the A section or the B section (but not both) closes away from its tonic chord, and this closure is integral to the tonal motion of the section.

# Core Definition
When either A or B closes away from its respective tonic key and this tonal departure is integral to the section's design, the form is a sectional ternary. Two configurations are possible: A||BA (A closes in its tonic, B closes away) -- this is the common form; or AB||A (A closes away, B closes in its tonic) -- less common. The key distinction from full sectional ternary is that one section's closure moves to a different key (Laitz, Ch. 31).

# Prerequisites
- **Ternary Form** -- understanding the basic ABA structure

# Key Properties
1. One section (usually B) closes in a key other than its own tonic
2. The closure away from tonic is integral to the section's tonal motion
3. The other section closes in its own tonic

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Identify the three main sections
2. Check each section's closing key
3. Determine if exactly one section closes away from its tonic
4. Verify that the closure is integral (not just a brief transition)

# Context & Application
Sectional ternary is the most common of the three ternary subtypes. It provides a middle ground between full tonal independence and continuous tonal dependency.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 707): Brahms, Romanze in F, op. 118, no. 5 -- The B section closes in D major, making it a sectional ternary form (AB||A).

**Example 2** (p. 714): Bach, "Ich will Dir mein Herze schenken," St. Matthew Passion -- B part closes in B minor (v of vi), making it a sectional ternary.
