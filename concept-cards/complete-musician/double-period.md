# Quick Definition
A structure of four phrases (typically 16 measures) in which the antecedent and consequent segments each subdivide into two phrases at a smaller level, with cadences in mm. 8 and 16 stronger than those in mm. 4 and 12.

# Core Definition
A double period is a structure in which the antecedent (typically mm. 1-8) and consequent (mm. 9-16) each contain two internal phrases. The large-level cadences in mm. 8 and 16 are stronger than the smaller-level cadences in mm. 4 and 12. The cadence in m. 16 (PAC) answers the cadence in m. 8 (HC), creating the large-level antecedent-consequent relationship. The internal phrases in mm. 1-4 and 5-8 (and 9-12 and 13-16) create smaller-level relationships that are not full antecedent-consequent pairs. Common cadence patterns: IAC-HC-IAC-PAC or HC-HC-HC-PAC. (Laitz, Ch. 21, pp. 483-485)

# Prerequisites
- **Period** — Must understand single-period structure

# Key Properties
1. Four phrases (typically 16 measures)
2. Two levels of antecedent-consequent relationship
3. Large-level: mm. 1-8 = antecedent, mm. 9-16 = consequent
4. Internal cadences weaker than large-level cadences
5. m. 8 cadence is inconclusive; m. 16 cadence provides closure
6. Often parallel at the large level (A-A')

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Identify four cadences across 16 measures
2. Check if cadences in mm. 8 and 16 are stronger than mm. 4 and 12
3. Check if m. 9 parallels m. 1 (parallel double period)
4. Verify that the PAC in m. 16 answers the HC in m. 8

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 484): Beethoven, Piano Sonata in Ab major, op. 26 -- parallel interrupted double period with cadences HC-HC-HC-PAC.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: A double period is simply two periods back to back
  **Clarification**: The four phrases form a single hierarchical structure where m. 16's PAC answers m. 8's HC
