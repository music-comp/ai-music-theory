# Quick Definition
Meter is the grouping of beats into recurring patterns of strong and weak beats, creating the underlying temporal framework of music.

# Core Definition
**Meter** refers to the grouping of both strong and weak beats into recurring patterns. Meters are classified by the number of beats per measure (duple, triple, quadruple) and by the division of the beat (simple or compound). In **simple meters**, the beat divides into two equal parts; in **compound meters**, the beat divides into three equal parts.

# Prerequisites
- **Pulse and Beat** -- meter organizes beats into patterns

# Key Properties
1. Beats per measure: duple (2), triple (3), quadruple (4)
2. Beat division: simple (2 parts) or compound (3 parts)
3. The first beat of each measure (the downbeat) receives the strongest accent
4. Measures are delineated by bar lines
5. Meter provides the framework against which rhythm operates

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Determine where the strongest beats fall (the downbeats)
2. Count how many beats occur between downbeats (duple, triple, quadruple)
3. Determine whether the beat naturally divides into two (simple) or three (compound)

# Context & Application
Meter is the temporal counterpart of tonality -- just as tonality creates a hierarchy of pitches, meter creates a hierarchy of beats. Composers create interest by both conforming to and disrupting the metric framework.
