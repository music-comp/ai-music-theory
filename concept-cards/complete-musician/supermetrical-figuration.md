# Quick Definition
A supermetrical figuration extends beyond a single metrical unit (spanning multiple beats or measures); it is always made consonant by supporting bass notes and represents large-scale melodic motion.

# Core Definition
**Supermetrical figurations** are embellishing figures whose durations extend beyond the value of a single beat, spanning multiple beats or even measures. They are always made consonant by supporting bass notes, as seen in the harmonization of large-scale passing or neighbor figures with the appropriate bass. For example, a supermetrical passing figure D-E-F spanning eight measures is supported by the bass progression D-A-D, creating i-V-i. Locating the entrance and resolution of the seventh in V7 often helps trace deeper-level supermetrical passing or neighbor motion (Laitz, Ch. 9, pp. 224-226).

# Prerequisites
- **Metrical figuration** -- the one-beat counterpart

# Key Properties
1. Extends beyond one beat (spanning multiple beats or measures)
2. Always harmonized (made consonant by supporting bass)
3. Represents large-scale structural melodic motion
4. Often visible only through reduction
5. Locating V7 seventh entrance and resolution helps trace them

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 224-225): In Beethoven's "Tempest" Sonata, the supermetrical passing motion D5-E5-F5 spans the entire eight-measure phrase, with each pitch supported by tonic or dominant harmony.
