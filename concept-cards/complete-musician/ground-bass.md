# Quick Definition
A ground bass is a repeating bass pattern that forms the foundation for continuous variations, with changes in register, texture, and motivic design occurring above it.

# Core Definition
A ground bass is a simple repeating bass pattern that provides the backbone for continuous variations. Over the ground bass, composers create changes in register, texture, and motivic design. The ground bass is one of three types of ostinato used in continuous variations; the distinction between ground bass, chaconne, and passacaglia is often artificial, as composers frequently combine repeating bass lines with repeating harmonic patterns (Laitz, Ch. 26, pp. 598-599).

# Prerequisites
- **Continuous Variations** -- the form in which ground bass operates

# Key Properties
1. Repeating bass line pattern
2. Upper voices vary while the bass remains constant
3. Often combined with a repeating harmonic pattern

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 599): Purcell, Ground in G -- eight-measure ground bass harmonized with a consistent harmonic pattern across variations.
