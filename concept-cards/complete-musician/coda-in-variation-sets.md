# Quick Definition
A coda in a variation set extends the final variation beyond the theme's length, often summarizing earlier material and touching on the subdominant to create a plagal effect signaling closure.

# Core Definition
Composers may abandon the theme's proportions in the final variation by adding a coda. The coda typically presents the opening gesture of the theme as a closing gesture, summarizes events from earlier variations, and may tonicize IV to create a plagal effect that signals the close. The coda provides a fitting end to the cumulative intensity of the variation set (Laitz, Ch. 26, p. 607).

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 607): Beethoven, "Nel cor piu non mi sento" -- a 24-measure coda (compared to the 20-measure theme) presents the opening scale degrees 5-3-2-1 as a closing gesture tossed imitatively between hands.
