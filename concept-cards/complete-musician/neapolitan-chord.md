# Quick Definition
The Neapolitan chord (bII) is a major triad built on the lowered second scale degree, functioning as a powerful chromatic pre-dominant that pulls strongly toward V, most commonly appearing in first inversion (bII6).

# Core Definition
The Neapolitan is a major triad built on b2 (e.g., in A minor: Bb-D-F). Named after Baroque composers from Naples who frequently used the sonority, it usually occurs in first inversion as the Neapolitan sixth chord (bII6), with scale-degree 4 in the bass. It functions as a pre-dominant that pulls strongly toward V. It occurs more often in minor-mode pieces; in major, it arises as a product of modal mixture requiring both b2 and b6. When doubling, prefer the bass (scale-degree 4); never double b2 (Laitz, Ch. 29, pp. 661-662).

# Key Properties
1. Major triad on b2
2. Usually appears in first inversion (bII6) with scale-degree 4 in bass
3. Functions as pre-dominant leading to V
4. More common in minor; product of mixture in major
5. Double the bass (scale-degree 4); do not double b2
6. The diminished third between b2 and leading tone 7 is acceptable

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Build a major triad on the pitch a half step above the tonic
2. Place it in first inversion (scale-degree 4 in bass)
3. In major keys, lower both scale-degree 2 and scale-degree 6

## To Recognize:
1. A major triad in first inversion with a chromatically lowered root
2. The bass is scale-degree 4, resolving to V
3. The distinctive sound of the lowered root creating the Neapolitan color

# Context & Application
The Neapolitan appears most commonly at cadences but can also be used within embedded phrase models (EPMs). Two common ways to fill the diminished third between b2 and the leading tone: a cadential 6/4 chord or an applied diminished seventh chord (vii°7/V).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Doubling b2 in bII6
  **Correction**: Double the bass (scale-degree 4) instead; b2 is a tendency tone
- **Error**: Creating an augmented second between scale-degree 3 and b2 in major
  **Correction**: Use soprano line 1-b2, or approach b2 from above

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing bII6 with a first-inversion IV chord
  **Clarification**: Check the root: bII6 has b2 as its root with scale-degree 4 in the bass; IV6 has 4 as its root
