# Quick Definition
A compound ternary form is a ternary structure (ABA) in which the larger sections themselves divide into smaller forms, typically rounded binary or simple binary.

# Core Definition
Compound ternary forms contain nested smaller forms within their larger ABA sections. Each larger section (A, B, A') may internally be organized as a rounded binary, simple binary, or other small form. This creates multiple levels of formal structure. The minuet-trio form is a prime example: the minuet (A) and trio (B) are each typically in rounded binary form, and the da capo return of the minuet creates the outer ternary structure (Laitz, Ch. 31).

# Prerequisites
- **Ternary Form** -- the outer form
- **Binary Form** -- the typical nested form within each section

# Key Properties
1. Multiple levels of formal structure
2. Larger sections divide into smaller forms (binary, rounded binary, etc.)
3. Nesting creates a hierarchy of repetition and contrast
4. Each nested form maintains its own tonal and thematic plan

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Identify the large-scale ABA structure
2. Within each large section, look for repeat signs, double bars, or tonal plans suggesting smaller forms
3. Label both the nested forms and the outer ternary structure

# Context & Application
Compound ternary forms are extremely common in Classical and Romantic music. They appear in minuet-trio movements, da capo arias, and character pieces. The nesting of smaller forms creates satisfying formal hierarchy.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 710): Grieg, "Hjemve" -- Both A and A' contain rounded binary forms nested within the larger full sectional ternary.

**Example 2** (p. 710): Haydn, Divertimento in G major, Hob. XVI.11 -- Both A and B contain rounded binary forms.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: "Compound" ternary vs. "compound" meter or intervals
  **Clarification**: These are unrelated uses of "compound." In form, "compound" means nested structures; in meter, it refers to triple subdivision of the beat.
