# Quick Definition
The chromatic step-descent bass is a fully chromaticized descending bass line from scale-degree 1 to 5, made possible in major keys through modal mixture importing the two diatonic forms of b6 and b7 from minor.

# Core Definition
In minor, the two diatonic forms of 6 and 7 permit a fully chromatic descent from 1 to 5 (e.g., 1-7-b7-6-b6-5). Modal mixture imports this progression into major keys. Two common settings exist: a direct descent to V, and an indirect descent to PD on scale-degree 4 followed by the dominant. The indirect version often features a chromatic voice exchange where the outer-voice pitches swap between chords (Laitz, Ch. 27, pp. 625-626).

# Prerequisites
- **Modal Mixture** -- provides the chromaticism for major-key versions

# Key Properties
1. Fully chromatic bass descent from 1 to 5
2. Uses both diatonic forms of 6 and 7 (or their borrowed equivalents)
3. Can be direct (to V) or indirect (to PD, then V)
4. Chromatic voice exchange often participates in the indirect form
5. A passing minor v6 may appear in the indirect version

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 625): Geminiani -- fully chromatic descending bass in minor using applied chord (V42/IV) leading to major IV6.
