# Quick Definition
An arpeggiating (waltz) six-four chord emerges from figurated bass patterns where the bass arpeggiates the chord; the six-four is merely part of a root-position harmony and need not be separately labeled.

# Core Definition
The **arpeggiating six-four chord** (also called **waltz six-four**) emerges from figurated textures and accompaniment patterns, common in marches, waltzes, and folk tunes. The six-four chord appears when the bass arpeggiates through chord members, and the six-four is simply the completion of a root-position harmony. These chords are consonant and merely part of the arpeggiation. When analyzing, it suffices to draw a dash after the initial roman numeral rather than labeling each arpeggiating six-four separately (Laitz, Ch. 14, pp. 329-330).

# Prerequisites
- **Chordal leap and arpeggiation** -- the bass motion creating the chord

# Key Properties
1. Emerges from figurated/accompanimental bass patterns
2. Consonant -- fills out a root-position harmony
3. Common in waltzes, marches, folk tunes
4. Need not be separately labeled (dash after roman numeral)
5. The low bass note (root) is heard as controlling the entire pattern

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 330): Schubert's Waltz in A minor: beats 2 and 3 arpeggiate the harmony, creating apparent six-four chords that simply complete the root-position chord established on beat 1.
