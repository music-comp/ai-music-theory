# Quick Definition
Tones of figuration is the preferred umbrella term for all melodic embellishments, both consonant (chordal leaps, arpeggiations) and dissonant (passing tones, neighbor tones).

# Core Definition
Because melodic embellishments include both consonant types (chordal leaps, arpeggiations) and dissonant types (passing tones, neighbor tones), Laitz uses the term **tones of figuration** rather than "nonharmonic tone" to refer to all melodic embellishments generically. The prefix "consonant" or "dissonant" may be added depending on function within the specific musical context (Ch. 7, p. 164).

# Prerequisites
- **Consonance and dissonance** -- determines which tones are consonant vs. dissonant
- **Nonchord tones** -- the dissonant subset of tones of figuration

# Key Properties
1. Umbrella term encompassing all melodic embellishments
2. Includes consonant types: chordal leaps (CL), arpeggiations (ARP)
3. Includes dissonant types: passing tones (PT), neighbor tones (N), and others
4. Their status (consonant or dissonant) depends on harmonic context
5. Later chapters add accented types: suspensions, appoggiaturas, accented passing tones

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Begin with a structural melody (chord tones on strong beats)
2. Add embellishments: PTs between chord tones, Ns around chord tones, CLs within the harmony

## To Recognize:
1. Identify the underlying harmony
2. Classify each pitch as chord tone or non-chord tone
3. Label non-chord tones by their melodic behavior
4. Label consonant embellishments (CLs, ARPs) accordingly

# Context & Application
Tones of figuration are what make melodies interesting. They flesh out the basic harmonic structure with melodic variety. Understanding them is essential for both analysis and composition.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 165): In Vivaldi's Sonata in C minor, the oboe melody contains both consonant tones of figuration (chordal skips within C minor) and dissonant ones (neighbor F4, passing D).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Using "nonharmonic tone" for consonant embellishments like chordal leaps
  **Correction**: Use "tones of figuration" as the generic term; chordal leaps are members of the harmony

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Tones of figuration are always dissonant
  **Clarification**: They include both consonant (chordal leaps) and dissonant (passing tones, neighbors) types
