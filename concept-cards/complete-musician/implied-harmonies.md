# Quick Definition
Harmonies that must be inferred from incomplete chord members in compound-melody textures, where even after identifying all voice-leading strands the chords remain incomplete.

# Core Definition
In analyzing works using compound melody, discerning the harmonic progression is often only a matter of locating all the voices to find full triads and seventh chords. But sometimes, even when all voice-leading strands are considered, the harmonies remain incomplete, and missing chord members must be inferred from context -- particularly from knowledge of what usually occurs in tonic expansions and other standard progressions. (Laitz, Ch. 15, p. 360)

# Prerequisites
- **Compound melody** — Must be able to extract implied voices from a single line
- **Contrapuntal expansion** — Knowledge of standard expansion patterns guides inference of missing members

# Key Properties
1. Harmonies are determined by implication rather than literal presence of all chord tones
2. Context (standard progressions, typical tonic expansions) guides the identification of missing members
3. Seventh chords with missing thirds are common
4. Opening sonorities may be ambiguous until subsequent context clarifies the key

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Extract all voice-leading strands from the compound melody
2. Verticalize to identify complete and incomplete chords
3. For incomplete chords, apply knowledge of standard progressions to infer missing members
4. Verify that inferred harmonies create logical voice leading with surrounding chords

# Context & Application
Implied harmonies are particularly common in Romantic-period music, where textures may be sparse and harmonically ambiguous. Understanding implied harmonies is crucial for performers of solo and chamber music, where the performer must project harmonic meaning from incomplete textures.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 360): Schubert, Piano Sonata no. 16 in A minor, op. 42 -- right-hand compound melody implies two voices, but many chords are incomplete. A chord with B3 in the bass and E4/D5 in the treble implies V4/3 with a missing third (G#).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Labeling every vertical sonority literally without considering context
  **Correction**: Use knowledge of standard progressions and voice-leading patterns to infer missing chord members

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: If a chord tone is not literally present, the harmony is indeterminate
  **Clarification**: Context (key, surrounding chords, standard progressions) reliably identifies the implied harmony
