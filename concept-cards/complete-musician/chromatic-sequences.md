# Quick Definition
Chromatic sequences are sequential patterns whose main chords contain altered scale degrees, maintaining consistent chord quality and exact intervallic distances between repetitions, unlike diatonic sequences which conform to the asymmetry of the diatonic scale.

# Core Definition
Chromatic sequences differ from diatonic sequences in that their main chords contain altered (chromatic) scale degrees, and they maintain strictly both chord quality and intervallic distance between repetitions. The designation "chromatic" refers to the alteration of main structural chords, not merely the presence of accidentals (applied-chord sequences have accidentals but are diatonic). Because chromatic sequences divide the octave into same-sized intervals, they avoid the shifting whole steps and half steps that create goal-directed motion in diatonic sequences. Three main chromatic sequence types correspond to diatonic counterparts: D2 (-P4/+m3), D2 (-P5/+P4), and A2 (-M3/+P4) (Laitz, Ch. 36).

# Key Properties
1. Main chords contain altered (chromatic) scale degrees
2. Chord quality maintained exactly across repetitions
3. Intervallic distance maintained exactly across repetitions
4. Divide the octave into same-sized intervals
5. Create temporary tonal ambiguity
6. More common in major mode than minor
7. Label includes specific interval motions (e.g., -P4/+m3) to distinguish from diatonic

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Establish a two-chord model with specific quality and interval
2. Replicate the model exactly (same quality, same intervals)
3. Break off the sequence at the pre-dominant for tonal reentry
4. Use enharmonic notation instead of double flats or double sharps

## To Recognize:
1. Look for uniform chord quality across all repetitions
2. Check if the intervallic distance between repetitions is constant
3. Identify altered scale degrees in the main chords

# Context & Application
Chromatic sequences became increasingly important in the nineteenth century as they provided a means of traversing chromatically related tonal areas symmetrically. They create temporary tonal ambiguity at the surface while prolonging an underlying harmony at a deeper level.

# Examples

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Applied-chord sequences vs. chromatic sequences
  **Clarification**: Applied-chord sequences contain accidentals but their main structural chords are diatonic. Chromatic sequences have chromatically altered main chords.
