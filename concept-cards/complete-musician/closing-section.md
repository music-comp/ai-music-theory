# Quick Definition
The closing section (Cl) follows the STA's conclusive cadence and reinforces the contrasting key through multiple cadential figures, often containing new thematic material.

# Core Definition
The closing section follows the appearance of contrasting thematic material in the STA and a conclusive cadence in the new key. Its purpose is to reinforce the new key through multiple cadential figures that may be cast in two or more subsections and may even contain new thematic material. The closing section is often longer than the STA itself, which may occupy eight or fewer measures. A double bar or repeat sign usually marks the end of the exposition (Laitz, Ch. 33).

# Prerequisites
- **Sonata Form Exposition** -- the closing section is the final part of the exposition

# Key Properties
1. Follows a PAC in the contrasting key
2. Reinforces the new key through cadential repetition
3. May contain multiple subsections
4. May introduce new thematic material
5. Often longer than the STA itself

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 757): Beethoven, op. 49, no. 1 -- Cl at m. 29 in III.

**Example 2** (p. 771): Haydn, Sonata in C -- Cl (mm. 46-62) in V, featuring a descending fifth arpeggiation figure.
