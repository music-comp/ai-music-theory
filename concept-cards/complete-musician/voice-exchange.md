# Quick Definition
A voice exchange occurs when two voices swap pitches, often creating an X pattern in notation; it commonly occurs when revoicing repeated harmonies or during tonic expansions with first-inversion chords.

# Core Definition
A **voice exchange** occurs when two voices exchange (swap) their pitches, typically marked by diagonal lines forming an X in the notation. This often happens when a chord is revoiced (e.g., shifting from open to close position within the same harmony) or during tonic expansions involving root-position and first-inversion chords (e.g., I to I6, where soprano ^3 moves to ^1 as bass ^1 moves to ^3). Multiple-pitch swappings between voices are called **part exchanges** (Laitz, Ch. 8, p. 197; Ch. 10, p. 228; Ch. 11, p. 252).

# Prerequisites
- **Spacing and voicing** -- voice exchanges often result from revoicing

# Key Properties
1. Two voices swap their pitches
2. Creates an X pattern in notation
3. Common in tonic expansions (I-I6 with soprano descending, bass ascending)
4. All upper voices may leap during a voice exchange
5. Part exchange: multiple-pitch swapping (Ch. 11)

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 228): In Brahms's "Vom verwundeten Knaben," voice exchanges between bass and soprano create stepwise contrary motion within the tonic expansion.
