# Quick Definition
Inverting an interval moves the lower pitch up an octave (or the upper pitch down); the generic sizes of inversionally related intervals sum to 9, and quality reverses (M becomes m, A becomes d) except P which stays P.

# Core Definition
When the lower pitch of a simple interval is moved up one octave (or the upper pitch moved down), the interval is **inverted**. The generic sizes of inversionally related intervals always sum to 9 (e.g., 3 + 6 = 9). The half steps always sum to 12. Quality rules for inversion: perfect remains perfect; major becomes minor (and vice versa); augmented becomes diminished (and vice versa).

# Key Properties
1. Generic sum rule: inversionally related intervals sum to 9
2. Half-step sum: inversionally related intervals sum to 12 half steps
3. Quality inversions: P stays P; M becomes m; m becomes M; A becomes d; d becomes A
4. The inversional pair A4/d5 is unique: both contain 6 half steps (the tritone)
5. Inversionally related pairs share a similar sound quality and stability level

# Construction / Recognition
## To Invert:
1. Subtract the generic interval from 9 (e.g., 3rd inverts to 6th)
2. Swap quality: P stays P, M becomes m, A becomes d

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 79): A m2 (1 half step) inverts to a M7 (11 half steps). 1+11=12. 2+7=9.
**Example 2** (p. 80): A P4 inverts to a P5. Both remain perfect.
