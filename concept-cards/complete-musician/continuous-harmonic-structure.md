# Quick Definition
A period type where the antecedent ends on a harmony other than V or I (e.g., ii, IV, vi), creating forward-moving harmonic continuity rather than the "restart" effect of interruption.

# Core Definition
In a continuous harmonic structure, the antecedent phrase ends on a non-tonic, non-dominant harmony (such as ii, IV, or vi). The consequent continues the harmonic motion forward to reach PAC. Unlike interrupted periods (where V creates a restart), continuous periods create seamless harmonic flow from antecedent through consequent. (Laitz, Ch. 20, pp. 470-471)

# Key Properties
1. Antecedent ends on a non-tonic, non-dominant harmony
2. Forward-moving harmonic continuity
3. No "restart" effect
4. Creates seamless connection between phrases
