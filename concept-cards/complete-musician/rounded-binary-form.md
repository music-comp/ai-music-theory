# Quick Definition
Rounded binary form features the return of all or part of the opening material in the tonic after a digression in the second section, creating a cyclical three-part thematic design within a two-part tonal structure.

# Core Definition
When all or part of the opening material of a binary form returns in the second section after the digression, the thematic design is called rounded. The name derives from the cyclical effect created by the return of the opening material. The second section begins with unstable, fragmented material (digression) leading to a half cadence (interruption), after which the opening material is restated in the tonic (recapitulation). This creates a formal tension between a three-part melodic design (A-digression-A') and a two-part tonal structure (Laitz, Ch. 26, pp. 589-591).

- **Binary Form** -- the overarching concept

# Key Properties
1. Opening material returns in the second section after a digression
2. The structure resembles a parallel interrupted period
3. Three-part thematic design: A - digression - A'
4. Two-part tonal structure based on interruption and restart
5. An interruption (structural HC) divides the second section

# Context & Application
Rounded continuous binary form is the most common type of binary form, used in Classical minuets, scherzos, and variation themes. It directly relates to sonata form, which evolved from this pattern.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 589): Haydn, Sonatina no. 4, Scherzo -- rounded sectional binary form with a 16-measure second section containing digression and recapitulation.

**Example 2** (p. 590): Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 284, theme -- rounded continuous binary form with a digression (mm. 9-12) and recapitulation of the tune.
