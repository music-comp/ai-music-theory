# Quick Definition
Single melodic lines in sequential passages that imply multiple voices, creating an implied texture of triads and seventh chords through registral separation and stepwise voice-leading of the implied strands.

# Core Definition
Compound melody is common in sequential passages. A single melodic line can split into two voices in separate registers, each descending in stepwise motion, combining to create implied seventh chords (e.g., alternating triads and 6/5 chords in a D2 pattern). The technique works because (1) the two implied voices occupy separate registers, (2) each maintains independence through stepwise motion, and (3) they combine to make good counterpoint. (Laitz, Ch. 23, pp. 524-525)

# Key Properties
1. Single line implies two or more voices in sequential patterns
2. Implied voices in separate registers
3. Each voice descends (or ascends) by step
4. Combined, they create seventh-chord sequences

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 524): Handel, Sonata for Flute and Continuo in A minor -- treble voice splits into two implied voices creating D2 (-5/+4) with alternating triads and 6/5 chords.
