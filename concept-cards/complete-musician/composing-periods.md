# Quick Definition
The compositional process of creating a period: write an antecedent phrase with a weak cadence, then write a consequent phrase (parallel or contrasting opening) that closes with a stronger cadence, balancing repetition with variety.

# Core Definition
Composing periods requires balancing repetition (unity) with variety (contrast). Steps: (1) Write an antecedent phrase ending with HC or IAC. (2) Decide whether the consequent will be parallel or contrasting. (3) Write a consequent that diverges from the antecedent to achieve a PAC. (4) Ensure the overall harmonic and melodic structure creates a satisfying question-answer relationship. Periods can incorporate sentence structure, subphrases, and sequences. (Laitz, Ch. 20, pp. 473-475)

# Key Properties
1. Balance repetition and variety
2. Antecedent: weak cadence
3. Consequent: stronger cadence (PAC)
4. Parallel or contrasting opening
5. Can incorporate sentence structure and sequences
