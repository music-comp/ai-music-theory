# Quick Definition
The harmonic structure of binary form is sectional when the first section closes on the tonic (both sections tonally closed), and continuous when the first section closes away from the tonic (typically modulating to V in major or III in minor).

# Core Definition
When the cadence at the end of the first section ends on the tonic, the harmonic structure is sectional, since both sections are tonally closed. When the first section closes away from the tonic and the following section continues away from the tonic, the harmonic structure is continuous. This terminology mirrors period terminology: sectional periods close on tonic, continuous periods do not. Most binary forms are continuous, following common modulatory schemes: major keys modulate to V; minor keys modulate to III or v (Laitz, Ch. 26, pp. 587-589).
