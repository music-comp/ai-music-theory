# Quick Definition
The German Lied is a vocal genre for solo voice and piano that became the primary laboratory for chromatic experimentation in the nineteenth century, with Schubert as its central figure.

# Core Definition
The German Lied (art song) emerged in the late eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries as a genre for solo voice with piano accompaniment. It became the primary vehicle for exploring chromatic harmony, including modal mixture, chromatic modulation, and text-painting. The two main formal types are strophic (same music for each poetic stanza) and through-composed (new music for each stanza). Schubert, Schumann, Brahms, and Wolf were the genre's greatest practitioners. The Lied's intimate scale made it ideal for harmonic experimentation (Laitz, Ch. 28, pp. 649-654).

# Key Properties
1. Solo voice with piano accompaniment
2. Text-music relationship is paramount
3. Two main forms: strophic and through-composed
4. Modified strophic: strophic with harmonic/melodic alterations for dramatic stanzas
5. Piano accompaniment often serves illustrative or symbolic function
6. Chromatic harmony used for text-painting and dramatic expression

# Context & Application
The Lied demonstrates how chromatic harmonies (mixture, Neapolitan, augmented sixth) serve dramatic purposes. Modal mixture frequently underscores emotional contrasts in the text, while chromatic modulations create narrative arc.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 650): Schubert, "Lachen und Weinen" -- demonstrates modal mixture underscoring the contrast between "laughter" (major) and "tears" (minor).
