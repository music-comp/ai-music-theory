# Quick Definition
Motivic patterns that are embedded within the musical texture and not immediately apparent on the surface -- revealed only through analytical reduction by connecting structurally prominent pitches.

# Core Definition
Hidden motivic repetitions are instances where a motive's contour or interval pattern is embedded within the structural pitches of a passage, not apparent from the note-to-note surface. They are discovered by connecting metrically accented notes, registral high/low points, or other structurally prominent pitches to reveal the motivic shape. Hidden repetitions create subliminal coherence that listeners perceive without necessarily being able to identify consciously. (Laitz, Ch. 16, pp. 384-386)

# Prerequisites
- **Motivic parallelism** — Understanding multi-level motivic presence

# Key Properties
1. Not apparent from note-to-note surface reading
2. Revealed through reduction (connecting accented notes, extremes, etc.)
3. Create subliminal coherence
4. Often involve the same contour or intervals as the surface motive

# Context & Application
Hidden repetitions are a hallmark of sophisticated compositional craft. They reward analytical and aural study by revealing connections that enrich the listener's understanding.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 384): Structural pitches on downbeats trace the same descending-third pattern as the surface motive, though the intervening notes disguise the connection.
