# Quick Definition
A suspension is the most important accented tone of figuration: a consonant pitch is held (tied) past a chord change, creating a strong-beat dissonance that resolves down by step on a weak beat.

# Core Definition
A **suspension** is the most important type of accented tone of figuration, found consistently from the fifteenth century to today's popular music. It consists of a two-pitch figure governed by a three-stage process: (1) **Preparation** (P): a weak-beat consonance (chord tone); (2) **Suspension** (S): the prepared pitch is sustained (by tie or dot) into a strong-beat chord change, where it becomes dissonant; (3) **Resolution** (R): the dissonant suspension falls by step on a weak beat to a chord tone. The duration of the suspended note should be at least as long as the preparation and resolution (Laitz, Ch. 13, pp. 310-316).

# Prerequisites
- **Accented vs. unaccented dissonance** -- suspensions are accented dissonances
- **Voice leading** -- suspensions are voice-leading events

# Key Properties
1. Three stages: Preparation (weak beat, consonant) -> Suspension (strong beat, dissonant) -> Resolution (weak beat, consonant)
2. Suspension pitch is tied or sustained from preparation
3. Resolution is ALWAYS by step DOWN (except retardation: 7-8)
4. Upper-voice types: 9-8, 7-6, 4-3
5. Bass type: 2-3 (numbers increase because bass descends)
6. Do not double the resolution pitch in another voice (except 9-8)
7. Suspension figure always involves a descending stepwise motion that is temporarily postponed

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Find a voice moving down by step to the next chord tone
2. Sustain (tie) the higher pitch past the chord change
3. The sustained pitch is now dissonant against the new chord
4. Resolve by step down on the following weak beat

## To Recognize:
1. Look for tied notes crossing a bar line or beat boundary
2. The tied pitch should be dissonant against the new chord
3. It should resolve down by step to a chord tone

# Context & Application
Suspensions create some of the most expressive moments in tonal music. They work best with specific chord types: 7-6 with 6/3 chords, 4-3 and 9-8 with root-position chords, 2-3 with the progression I-V6.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Resolving the suspension upward
  **Correction**: Suspensions always resolve DOWN by step (except the rare 7-8 retardation)

- **Error**: Doubling the resolution pitch in another voice
  **Correction**: Never double the resolution pitch (except 9-8, where distance mitigates the effect)
