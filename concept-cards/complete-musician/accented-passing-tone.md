# Quick Definition
An accented passing tone (APT) fills a melodic third like a regular passing tone but occurs ON the beat rather than between beats, creating a more striking dissonance.

# Core Definition
An **accented passing tone** (APT) fills a melodic third between two chord tones, just like an unaccented passing tone, but occurs on a metrically accented beat rather than between beats. The consonance occurs on the weak beat (offbeat), reversing the normal pattern. APTs most often occur in descending lines and usually create 7-6 or 4-3 contrapuntal motion against the bass (Laitz, Ch. 13, pp. 305-306).

# Prerequisites
- **Passing tone** -- APT is its accented variant
- **Accented vs. unaccented dissonance** -- APT belongs to the accented category

# Key Properties
1. Fills a melodic third between two chord tones (same as PT)
2. Occurs ON the beat (accented)
3. Consonance occurs on the offbeat (reversed hierarchy)
4. Most common in descending lines
5. Usually creates 7-6 or 4-3 motion against bass

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 306): In Schumann's "Am leuchtenden Sommermorgen," A at the downbeat of m. 4 forms a dissonant fourth against E harmony before resolving to the chordal third.
