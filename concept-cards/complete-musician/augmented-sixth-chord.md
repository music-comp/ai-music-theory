# Quick Definition
An augmented sixth chord is a chromatic pre-dominant harmony built on b6 in the bass, containing the characteristic interval of an augmented sixth between the bass (b6) and an upper voice (#4), both of which resolve outward by half step to 5.

# Core Definition
The augmented sixth chord derives from the Phrygian cadence (iv6-V) by chromatically raising scale-degree 4 to #4, creating the strongly directed interval of an augmented sixth above the bass. The bass b6 falls by half step to 5, while #4 ascends by half step to 5, creating powerful contrary-motion voice leading toward the dominant. The chord functions primarily as a pre-dominant, occurs more frequently in minor-mode pieces (where b6 is diatonic), and comes in three varieties: Italian (It6), German (Ger65), and French (Fr43). The remaining inner voices double scale-degree 1 and move in contrary motion (Laitz, Ch. 30, pp. 678-680).

# Prerequisites
- **Pre-Dominant Function** -- the augmented sixth serves this function
- **Phrygian Cadence** -- the diatonic origin of the augmented sixth

# Key Properties
1. Built on b6 in the bass
2. Contains #4 in an upper voice (usually soprano)
3. Both voices resolve outward by half step to 5
4. Three varieties: Italian, German, French
5. Derives from the Phrygian cadence (iv6-V)
6. More linear drive toward the dominant than any other PD harmony
7. In major, b6 must be chromatically lowered

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Place b6 in the bass
2. Place #4 in an upper voice
3. Double scale-degree 1 (the third above the bass)
4. Choose the fourth pitch: doubled 1 (Italian), b3/5th above bass (German), 2/aug. 4th above bass (French)

## To Recognize:
1. The distinctive augmented sixth interval resolving outward
2. Half-step motion in both bass and soprano converging on 5
3. French is easiest to identify (two tritones, major-second clash in inner voices)
4. Italian sounds thinnest (three pitch classes); German sounds like V7

# Context & Application
Augmented sixth chords contain more linear drive toward the dominant than any other PD harmony. Their appearance after a pivot chord is particularly helpful in securing a new key during modulations. They frequently combine with other pre-dominants (iv, ii) to expand the PD function.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 678): Schubert, Waltz in G minor -- the augmented sixth chord replaces the expected Phrygian cadence with a chromatic variant.

**Example 2** (p. 678): Handel, "Since by Man Came Death," Messiah -- demonstrates the same augmented sixth sonority in a Baroque context.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Resolving the augmented sixth inward rather than outward
  **Correction**: b6 descends to 5; #4 ascends to 5 (contrary motion outward)

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing the three types of augmented sixth chords
  **Clarification**: All share b6 in bass, #4 in upper voice, and doubled 1. The difference is the fourth pitch: It6 doubles 1; Ger65 has b3; Fr43 has 2
