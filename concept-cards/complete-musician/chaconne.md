# Quick Definition
A chaconne is a continuous variation form built on a repeating harmonic pattern, with variations occurring above the recurring chord progression.

# Core Definition
A chaconne is a continuous variation form based on a repeating harmonic pattern (as opposed to the ground bass's repeating bass line, or the passacaglia's combination of both). In practice, all three terms are often used interchangeably, as composers frequently use repeating harmonies to accompany a repeating bass and vice versa (Laitz, Ch. 26, p. 598).

# Prerequisites
- **Continuous Variations** -- the form in which the chaconne operates

# Key Properties
1. Repeating harmonic pattern (chord progression)
2. Ostinato may begin in midmeasure to maintain continuity
3. Variations overlap, with each ending dovetailed with the next
4. Upper-voice melodic material varies while harmony repeats

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 600): Bach, Chaconne from Partita no. 2 in D minor -- based on a repeating series of harmonies with the ostinato beginning in midmeasure.
