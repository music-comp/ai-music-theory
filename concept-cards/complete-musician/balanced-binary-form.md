# Quick Definition
A balanced binary form is one in which the closing material of the first section recurs, transposed to the tonic, at the end of the second section, providing tonal and melodic closure.

# Core Definition
Simple and rounded binary forms can also be melodically balanced if the closing material in the first section is restated as the closing material in the second section. The term refers more to the harmonic underpinning than to the repeated melody, because in most binary forms the first section closes in a nontonic key. The restatement occurs in the tonic to provide convincing tonal closure (Laitz, Ch. 26, p. 591).

# Prerequisites
- **Binary Form** -- the overarching concept

# Key Properties
1. Closing material from section 1 returns at the end of section 2
2. The restatement is transposed to the tonic
3. Provides both melodic and harmonic closure
4. Can combine with either simple or rounded thematic design
5. The harmonic balance (closing in tonic) is the primary feature

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 592): Handel, Gavotte in G major -- balanced continuous binary form with 13-measure digression compensated by restated closing material from the first section.
