# Quick Definition
Voice leading for the Neapolitan involves b2 descending to the leading tone (7), creating a diminished third that can be filled by a cadential 6/4 or an applied vii°7/V.

# Core Definition
bII6-V progressions typically have b2-7 in the soprano. The diminished third between b2 and the leading tone is perfectly acceptable, though it can be filled with passing tones. Two common harmonizations of the passing tone (scale-degree 1): a cadential 6/4 chord or an applied diminished seventh chord (vii°7/V). The bass (scale-degree 4) descends to scale-degree 5 (V). In major keys, avoid the augmented second between scale-degree 3 and b2 by using the soprano motion 1-b2 (Laitz, Ch. 29, pp. 662-663).

# Key Properties
1. Soprano typically moves b2-7 (diminished third, acceptable)
2. The diminished third can be filled by cadential 6/4 or vii°7/V
3. Bass moves 4-5 (to the dominant)
4. Double scale-degree 4 (the bass); optionally double scale-degree 6
5. Avoid augmented second between 3 and b2 in major mode

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 663): Two common harmonizations of the passing tone between bII6 and V: cadential 6/4 and vii°7/V.
