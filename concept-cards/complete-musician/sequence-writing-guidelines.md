# Quick Definition
Three essential guidelines for writing sequences: (1) correct voice leading in the first three chords, (2) start with bass and soprano using contrary step motion, (3) include at least one imperfect consonance in the outer voices.

# Core Definition
Three guidelines for writing sequences: **Guideline 1**: Make sure the voice leading in the first three chords (model + first chord of copy) is correct, because any error will be replicated in every copy. **Guideline 2**: Start with bass and soprano; aim for contrary step motion. **Guideline 3**: Include at least one imperfect consonance (3, 6, or 10) between the outer voices in the model. Within sequences, normally forbidden voice-leading situations (parallel 5ths, doubled leading tones, root-position diminished triads, dissonant leaps) are acceptable because of the overall repetitive pattern. (Laitz, Ch. 22, pp. 509-510)

# Key Properties
1. First three chords must have correct voice leading
2. Outer voices in contrary motion
3. At least one imperfect consonance in outer voices
4. Normally forbidden situations acceptable within sequences
