# Quick Definition
A false recapitulation occurs when the FTA theme appears in the "wrong" key during the development, deceiving the listener into thinking the recapitulation has arrived; the real recapitulation in the tonic follows soon after.

# Core Definition
The false recapitulation is a harmonic anomaly in which the theme from the FTA appears in the wrong key during the development section. It deceives the listener into expecting the recapitulation, but the real recapitulation in the tonic usually follows soon after. False recapitulations are actually part of the development, not the recapitulation (Laitz, Ch. 33).

# Prerequisites
- **Sonata Form Development** -- false recapitulations occur within the development
- **Sonata Form Recapitulation** -- the listener expects the real recapitulation

# Key Properties
1. FTA theme appears in a wrong key
2. Occurs within the development section
3. Creates a surprise when the real recapitulation follows
4. Part of the development, not the recapitulation

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 762): Haydn, String Quartet in B minor, op. 33, no. 1 -- Apparent retransition leads to V of F# minor instead of V of B minor; A major enters boldly with the FTA theme. The real recapitulation arrives at m. 59 via an augmented sixth chord.
