# Quick Definition
An incomplete neighbor steps away from a chord tone but does not return to the same pitch, resolving instead to a different chord tone by step.

# Core Definition
An **incomplete neighbor** (IN) is a variant of the neighbor tone in which the three-note figure is not completed. The pitch neighbors a chord tone but does not return to the original pitch. It either approaches by leap and leaves by step, or approaches by step and leaves by leap. In "God Save the King," G4 neighbors F4 but does not return to F, making it an incomplete neighbor (Laitz, Ch. 7, p. 170).

# Prerequisites
- **Neighbor tone** -- the complete form from which this derives

# Key Properties
1. Moves by step in one direction but not the other (leap + step or step + leap)
2. Does not complete the neighbor-tone figure by returning to the starting pitch
3. Resolves to a different chord tone by step

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Identify a pitch that is a step away from a chord tone
2. Verify that the figure does not return to the original chord tone
3. Check that the pitch resolves by step to a (different) chord tone

# Context & Application
Incomplete neighbors appear frequently in melodies where the line needs to change direction or move to a new pitch level. They are related to appoggiaturas when they occur in accented positions.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 170): In "God Save the King," the G4 in m. 1 is an incomplete neighbor to F4 -- it steps up from F but does not return, instead leading eventually to A4.

## Contrasts With

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing incomplete neighbors with passing tones
  **Correction**: A passing tone fills a third between two chord tones; an incomplete neighbor neighbors a chord tone but departs by leap or arrives by leap
