# Quick Definition
The medial caesura is the pause that very often occurs between the end of the transition and the beginning of the STA, marking the approximate midpoint of the exposition.

# Core Definition
The medial caesura (MC) is a structural articulation point that marks the approximate midpoint of the exposition. It occurs between the end of the transition and the beginning of the STA. When the transition ends on the new dominant (rather than the new tonic), the medial caesura makes the arrival of the STA theme more dramatic. It provides a moment of breath that separates the two tonal areas of the exposition (Laitz, Ch. 33).

# Key Properties
1. Occurs between the end of the transition and the start of the STA
2. Marks the approximate midpoint of the exposition
3. Often a brief pause or rest
4. May be on the new dominant or new tonic

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 755): In Beethoven's op. 49, no. 1, the transition ends with a HC in III, creating a medial caesura before the STA enters.
