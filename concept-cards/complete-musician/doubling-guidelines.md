# Quick Definition
In four-voice writing, double the root of root-position chords; prefer doubling the fifth to the third; never double the leading tone or other tendency tones.

# Core Definition
When writing for four voices with triads (three pitches), one member must be doubled. In general, double the root as the most stable member. If voice leading requires it, double the fifth; doubling the third is a last resort. **Never double tendency tones** (^7, chordal dissonances, chromatically altered tones) because (1) they are already aurally prominent, and (2) correctly resolving both instances produces parallel octaves (Laitz, Ch. 8, pp. 195-196).

# Prerequisites
- **SATB texture** -- four voices require doubling in triads
- **Tendency tones** -- which tones must not be doubled

# Key Properties
1. Root doubling preferred (most stable)
2. Fifth doubling acceptable for smoother voice leading
3. Third doubling as last resort
4. Never double ^7 (leading tone) -- absolute rule
5. Never double chordal dissonances or chromatically altered tones
6. The fifth may be omitted for smooth voice leading (root and third required)
