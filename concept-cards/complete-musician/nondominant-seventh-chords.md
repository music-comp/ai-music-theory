# Quick Definition
Seventh chords that do not have dominant (major-minor) or leading-tone (diminished-diminished) quality -- primarily ii7 and IV7 and their inversions, which function as pre-dominants.

# Core Definition
Nondominant seventh chords are seventh chords whose quality is not major-minor (dominant) or diminished-diminished (leading tone). They include ii7, ii half-dim 7, IV7, and iv7 in major and minor keys. These chords provide color and contrast and are generally easy to implement. The seventh must be **prepared** (by common tone or step from above) and **resolved** (step down). ii6/5 is the most common type and inversion. (Laitz, Ch. 17, pp. 406-410)

# Prerequisites
- **Pre-dominant function** — These chords serve the PD function
- **Seventh chords** — Must understand seventh chord structure and voice leading

# Key Properties
1. The seventh must be prepared (common-tone preparation preferred) and resolved (step down)
2. Chords in inversion should be complete
3. Root-position seventh chords can omit the fifth and double the root to avoid parallels
4. ii6/5 is the most common form
5. IV7 must be handled carefully to avoid parallels with V (often resolved to V7 or cadential 6/4)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Prepare the seventh by common tone or step from above in the same voice
2. Resolve the seventh down by step to the next chord
3. For ii6/5, ensure the chord is complete
4. For root-position ii7, the fifth may be omitted and root doubled

## To Recognize:
1. Look for non-dominant-quality seventh chords in PD position
2. Check that the seventh is prepared and resolved
3. Distinguish from suspensions that may create apparent nondominant sevenths

# Context & Application
Nondominant seventh chords enrich the pre-dominant function with greater tension and color. The progression ii7-V7-I creates a powerful chain of seventh-chord resolutions. Composers from Chopin to Mahler exploited the tension of unprepared nondominant sevenths for expressive effect.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 406): Chopin, Nocturne in B major, op. 62, no. 1 -- ii7-V7-I progression where the ii7 enters abruptly and V7 sounds more like a resolution of ii7 than a dissonance.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Failing to prepare the seventh of a nondominant seventh chord
  **Correction**: Always prepare by common tone or step from above

- **Error**: Confusing a suspension on ii6/5 with IV7 (e.g., ^3 suspended over ii creates apparent IV7)
  **Correction**: Check whether the "seventh" resolves as a suspension or functions as a chord tone
