# Quick Definition
An interval is the distance between two pitches (a dyad), measured by a generic number (letter-name count) and a specific quality (exact size in half steps).

# Core Definition
A pair of pitches is called a **dyad**, and the distance between the pitches in a dyad is the **interval**. Intervals are the basic building blocks of tonal music. They are identified by two components: a **generic name** (the numerical distance based on letter-name count) and a **specific quality** (the precise size based on the number of half steps). Together, these produce the interval's **specific size** (e.g., M3, P5, m7).

# Prerequisites
- **Pitch and Pitch Class** -- intervals measure the distance between pitches
- **Half Step and Whole Step** -- intervals are measured in half steps

# Key Properties
1. Intervals are measured by counting both the lower and upper pitch in the letter-name span
2. Contour and accidentals do not change an interval's generic size
3. Intervals can be ascending or descending
4. Every interval has both a generic (numerical) and specific (quality) component

# Context & Application
Intervals are the atomic units of melody and harmony. Understanding intervals is essential for analyzing chords, writing counterpoint, and dictation.
