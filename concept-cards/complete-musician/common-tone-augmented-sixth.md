# Quick Definition
A common-tone augmented sixth chord (c.t.+6) is an augmented sixth sonority that extends the tonic rather than leading to the dominant, with the bass sustaining a common tone (the tonic root) instead of resolving by half step.

# Core Definition
The common-tone augmented sixth chord contains an augmented sixth interval (like a regular Ger-6/5) but sustains the tonic root as a common tone in the bass rather than having flat-6 in the bass resolving to 5. Because the augmented sixth chord returns to the tonic, it demonstrates a common-tone prolongational function rather than a dominant-directed function. The bass may also skip down a major third to what would be the usual bass of an augmented sixth chord before returning to tonic. Nineteenth-century music contains a variety of such chromatic third-related vacillations (Laitz, Ch. 35).

# Key Properties
1. Contains an augmented sixth interval (like Ger-6/5)
2. Bass sustains the tonic root as common tone
3. Extends tonic rather than leading to dominant
4. Creates neighbor-like chromatic motion above and below the fifth of the tonic
5. Labeled c.t.+6

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 823): Schubert, "Am Meer," D. 957 -- c.t.+6 on beat 2 of m. 1 creates neighbor figures above and below the fifth of the tonic, extending I over a sustained C bass.

**Example 2** (p. 825): Wolf, "Man sagt mir," Italienisches Liederbuch -- c.t.+6 in m. 1 creates a neighbor figure over A minor tonic.
