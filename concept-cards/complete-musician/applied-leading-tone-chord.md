# Quick Definition
An applied leading-tone chord is a diminished triad or diminished seventh chord (vii° or vii°7) that functions as a dominant substitute, tonicizing a diatonic chord other than the tonic.

# Core Definition
Applied leading-tone chords (vii°6, vii°7, and their inversions) are dominant substitutes that tonicize diatonic scale degrees. They function identically to how vii° relates to I in the home key, but applied to other harmonies. The introduction of applied leading-tone chords allows the use of fully diminished seventh chords (vii°7) to tonicize both major and minor triads (Laitz, Ch. 24, p. 545).

# Prerequisites
- **Applied Chord** -- general concept of secondary function
- **Diminished Seventh Chord** -- the chord type used for applied leading-tone chords

# Key Properties
1. Can be diminished triad (vii°), half-diminished seventh, or fully diminished seventh
2. The varied types and inversions allow extended tonicization of a single harmony
3. The root of the applied leading-tone chord is a half step below the root of the target chord

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Identify the target chord
2. Build a diminished triad or seventh chord on the pitch a half step below the target's root

## To Recognize:
1. A diminished-quality chord with chromaticism resolving up by half step to a diatonic triad
2. Multiple applied leading-tone chords in succession extending a single tonicization

# Context & Application
Applied leading-tone chords participate in contrapuntal expansions of non-tonic harmonies, just as vii°6 and vii°7 expand the tonic. Their various inversions enable extended tonicizations lasting several beats or measures while the target harmony retains its underlying function.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 545): In C major, applied chords vii°7/ii, vii°6/ii, and vii°3/ii tonicize ii for nearly two full measures, yet ii's larger pre-dominant function remains unchanged.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Doubling the root of the applied leading-tone chord
  **Correction**: The root is a tendency tone (temporary leading tone) and must not be doubled

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing vii°7/X with a diatonic diminished seventh
  **Clarification**: Check whether the chord resolves to a non-tonic harmony; if so, it is applied
