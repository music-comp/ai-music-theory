# Quick Definition
Unaccented tones of figuration occur on metrically weak beats (PTs, Ns, CLs); accented tones of figuration occur on metrically strong beats (APTs, ANs, suspensions, appoggiaturas), creating more striking, emotive dissonances.

# Core Definition
Tones of figuration are grouped into two categories based on rhythmic placement. **Unaccented tones of figuration** occur on metrically weak beats and include chordal leaps, passing tones, and neighbor tones. **Accented tones of figuration** occur on metrically strong beats and include accented passing tones, accented neighbor tones, suspensions, appoggiaturas, and pedals. Accented dissonances are more striking and emotive because consonance and metrical accent do not align, reversing the usual hierarchy (Laitz, Ch. 13, pp. 302-305).

# Prerequisites
- **Tones of figuration** -- the broader category being subdivided

# Key Properties
1. Unaccented: on weak beats, lighter, more buoyant
2. Accented: on strong beats, more striking, more dramatic
3. Accented dissonances reverse the normal alignment of consonance and metric stress
4. Baroque and Classical performers were expected to add tones of figuration in performance
