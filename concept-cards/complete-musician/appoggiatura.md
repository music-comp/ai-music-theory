# Quick Definition
An appoggiatura enters by leap and resolves by step on an accented beat; it is essentially an accented incomplete neighbor -- dissonant, unprepared, and dramatically prominent.

# Core Definition
The **appoggiatura** (APP) is a striking type of figuration that enters by leap, is dissonant and accented, and resolves by step to a chord tone (usually in the direction opposite the leap to balance the contour). It is related to the accented incomplete neighbor (AIN) and the two terms are often used interchangeably. Unlike the suspension, which is prepared by a consonant tie, the appoggiatura is unprepared -- it enters abruptly, making it particularly dramatic. The most common intervallic structures are 4-3 and 9-8 (Laitz, Ch. 13, pp. 310-311).

# Prerequisites
- **Incomplete neighbor** -- the appoggiatura is its accented form

# Key Properties
1. Enters by leap (unprepared)
2. Dissonant and accented (on the beat)
3. Resolves by step (usually opposite direction of leap)
4. More dramatic than suspension (no preparation)
5. Most common: 4-3 and 9-8
