# Quick Definition
Figured bass is a shorthand notation system that indicates the intervals above a bass note, revealing chord structure and voice movement: 5/3 (root position), 6/3 or 6 (first inversion), 6/4 (second inversion).

# Core Definition
**Figured bass** (or **thoroughbass**) is a shorthand notation that describes the generic intervals above bass notes. Used by composers from 1600-1800, it indicates chordal construction and melodic movement of voices. Numbers are listed from largest to smallest. The bass is harmonically the most important voice. Figured bass symbols are combined with roman numerals for complete harmonic analysis.

# Key Properties
1. Root position: 5/3 (usually omitted -- absence of figures = root position)
2. First inversion: 6/3 (abbreviated to just 6)
3. Second inversion: 6/4 (always written in full)
4. Dashes (--) indicate melodic motion of a single voice (e.g., 5--6)
5. Accidentals next to figures alter the specified pitch; accidental alone = altered third above bass
6. Chromaticism in the bass cannot be shown in figured bass
7. Figures do not specify voicing or octave placement
8. Chromatic alterations in figured bass apply only to the pitch directly above, not through the measure
9. Symbols: #, b, natural modify the figured pitch; /, + raise by a half step
10. Compound intervals are reduced (a tenth is shown as a third)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Analyze (from score to figures):
1. Identify the bass note
2. Count intervals from bass to upper voices
3. List from largest to smallest

## To Realize (from figures to score):
1. Read the bass note
2. Build intervals above it according to the figures
3. Observe the prevailing key signature
