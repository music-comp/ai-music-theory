# Quick Definition
Seventh chords built on ii and IV that serve the pre-dominant function, with ii6/5 being the most common form. The seventh must be prepared (by common tone or step) and resolved (step down).

# Core Definition
Pre-dominant seventh chords -- ii7 (and ii half-dim 7 in minor) and IV7 (iv7 in minor) -- add a seventh to the standard PD triads. The most common form is ii6/5 (first inversion). Voice-leading rules: (1) prepare the seventh by common tone (preferred) or step from above; (2) resolve the seventh down by step; (3) inversions should be complete; root position may omit the fifth. IV7 moving to V risks parallel fifths; solutions include using V7 instead, the cadential 6/4, or IV6/5-V6/5. (Laitz, Ch. 17, pp. 406-410)

# Key Properties
1. ii6/5 is the most common nondominant seventh chord
2. The seventh must be prepared and resolved
3. IV7-V risks parallel fifths; use IV7-V7, IV7-cad 6/4, or IV6/5-V6/5
4. Root-position ii7 may omit the fifth and double the root
