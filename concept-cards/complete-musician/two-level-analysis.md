# Quick Definition
An analytical approach using two levels: the first level labels each chord with a roman numeral; the second level groups chords into functional areas (T, PD, D, T), revealing the phrase model's deep structure.

# Core Definition
Two-level analysis distinguishes between chord-by-chord labeling (first level) and functional grouping (second level). At the **first level**, every chord receives a roman numeral. At the **second level**, chords are grouped under their functional role -- T (tonic), PD (pre-dominant), D (dominant), T (tonic) -- revealing the phrase model. EPMs, passing tonics, back-relating dominants, and voice-leading chords are subordinated at the second level. (Laitz, Ch. 17, pp. 411-413)

# Key Properties
1. First level: roman numerals for every chord
2. Second level: functional grouping (T-PD-D-T)
3. Subordinate chords (EPMs, passing tonics, BRDs) visible at second level
4. Second level reveals deep structure
