# Quick Definition
A V chord that prolongs the preceding tonic without resolving forward to a following tonic -- it "relates back" to the tonic rather than progressing forward. Shown only in second-level analysis under T.

# Core Definition
A back-relating dominant (BRD) is a V chord that prolongs the preceding tonic without resolving forward. When V appears to move to ii (a seeming retrogression from D to PD), the BRD interpretation shows that V looks backward to the preceding tonic rather than forward to the following pre-dominant. At the first level, the analysis shows I-V-ii; at the second level, V falls under T (marked as BRD), and ii begins a new PD function. The half cadence is a common and important type of BRD, since the music following a half cadence restarts rather than continuing. (Laitz, Ch. 19, pp. 451-454)

# Prerequisites
- **Phrase model** — Must understand functional progression T-PD-D-T
- **Two-level analysis** — BRDs are visible only at the second level

# Key Properties
1. V relates backward to T, not forward to the next chord
2. No functional connection between BRD and following chord
3. Resolves the analytical problem of apparent retrogression (V-ii)
4. Shown under T at second level
5. Half cadences are a type of BRD

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Find V followed by a chord other than I (typically ii or IV)
2. Ask whether V resolves the preceding tonic or progresses forward
3. If V closes off the tonic area without progressing, it is a BRD
4. Verify in second-level analysis: V falls under T, following chord begins PD

# Context & Application
BRDs are common at the boundary between tonic expansion and pre-dominant function. Recognizing them prevents the analytical error of labeling natural progressions as retrogressions.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 452): Bach, Prelude in Eb Major, WTC Book 2 -- V in m. 2 prolongs the preceding tonic; the overall progression is I-ii7-V7-I, with each harmony supporting soprano descent Bb4-Ab4-G4.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Phrase model** — BRD resolves a common analytical problem within the T-PD-D-T model

## Related
- **Half cadence** — Essentially a BRD, since the music afterward restarts
- **Retrogression** — BRD avoids the retrogression label for V-ii

## Contrasts With
- **Dominant function** — A functional dominant progresses forward to tonic; a BRD relates backward

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Labeling V-ii as a retrogression
  **Correction**: The V is a BRD that prolongs T; the ii begins a new PD function

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: A BRD is a structural dominant
  **Clarification**: A BRD does not participate in a cadence or progress forward to tonic; it is part of the tonic prolongation
