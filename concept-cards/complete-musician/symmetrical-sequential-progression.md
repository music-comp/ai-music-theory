# Quick Definition
Symmetrical sequential progressions partition the octave into equal intervals through chromatic sequences, with the D3 (-P4/+m2) dividing the octave into major thirds as the final type of equal octave division.

# Core Definition
Chromatic sequences create equal divisions of the octave: descending minor seconds (D2 -P5/+A4), descending major seconds (D2 -P4/+m3 and D2 -P5/+P4), ascending minor seconds (A2 -M3/+P4), minor thirds (omnibus), and major thirds (D3 -P4/+m2). The D3 (-P4/+m2) partitions the octave into major thirds with descending bass and whole-step soprano descent. These symmetrical progressions all create temporary tonal ambiguity, which composers extended into autonomous symmetrical progressions independent of sequential motion (Laitz, Ch. 36).

# Key Properties
1. Each chromatic sequence type divides the octave by a specific interval
2. Semitone: 12 divisions; whole tone: 6; minor third: 4; major third: 3; tritone: 2
3. All create temporary tonal ambiguity
4. Led to autonomous symmetrical progressions in later nineteenth-century music
