# Quick Definition
Tonicization creates a fleeting impression of a new key within a phrase, while modulation establishes a new key through strong cadences and sustained presence of the new tonal area.

# Core Definition
Tonicizations usually occur within phrases. They do not disrupt the feeling of the home key, lack strong cadences in new keys, and are fleeting. Modulations include a strong cadence in the new key, and the new key continues after the cadence, giving the feeling that a new key has usurped the home key. The distinction is often a matter of personal interpretation, especially in borderline cases (Laitz, Ch. 25, p. 568).

# Prerequisites
- **Tonicization** -- one side of the comparison
- **Modulation** -- the other side

# Key Properties
1. Tonicization: within a phrase, no strong cadence, fleeting, home key uninterrupted
2. Modulation: includes cadence, new key continues, home key displaced temporarily
3. The boundary is often subjective and context-dependent
4. Repeat signs can affect interpretation (first hearing may sound like tonicization; second time through, like modulation)

# Context & Application
Determining whether a passage tonicizes or modulates requires considering the strength of cadences, the duration of the new tonal area, and whether the home key returns immediately. The distinction has analytical significance for understanding the form of a work.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 568): Beethoven, Violin Sonata op. 12 no. 2 -- extended tonicizations of ii and IV without modulation; the entire passage is in A major.

**Example 2** (p. 569): Haydn, Piano Sonata in E minor -- convincing PAC in G major (III) suggests modulation, confirmed when G major continues after the double bar.

# Relationships
## Contrasts
- **Tonicization** vs. **Modulation** -- the key distinction is cadential confirmation

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming every chromatic passage is a modulation
  **Clarification**: Without a strong cadence and continued presence in the new key, a passage is merely a tonicization
