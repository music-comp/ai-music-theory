# Quick Definition
Suspensions are labeled by the interval of dissonance and resolution reckoned against the bass: upper-voice types are 9-8, 7-6, and 4-3; the bass suspension is 2-3 (with companion 5-6 in upper voice).

# Core Definition
Suspensions are labeled using figured bass numbers that show the melodic motion of the suspension and resolution stages, measured against the bass. The three common upper-voice suspensions are **9-8**, **7-6**, and **4-3**. Compound intervals are reduced to simple form (11-10 = 4-3), except by convention, 9-8 retains its compound label. The **2-3** suspension is the bass suspension, where the bass descends; the numbers increase because upper voices remain stationary while the bass moves. A dash connecting the two numbers distinguishes suspensions from chord inversions. Each type works best with specific chords: 7-6 with 6/3 chords; 4-3 and 9-8 with root-position chords; 2-3 with I-V6 (Laitz, Ch. 13, pp. 315-316).

# Key Properties
1. 9-8: consonant ninth resolves to octave (root-position chords)
2. 7-6: dissonant seventh resolves to sixth (first-inversion chords)
3. 4-3: dissonant fourth resolves to third (root-position chords)
4. 2-3: bass suspension, bass descends; upper voices create 5-6 motion
5. 2-1 is possible but rare (dissonance too close to resolution)
6. Dash notation (7-6) distinguishes from chord inversions (7 alone = seventh chord)
