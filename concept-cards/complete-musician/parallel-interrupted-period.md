# Quick Definition
A period in which both phrases begin with similar melodic material (parallel) and the antecedent ends with a half cadence on V (interrupted), creating the most common and recognizable period type. Abbreviated PIP.

# Core Definition
A parallel interrupted period (PIP) has (1) **parallel** melodic openings -- the consequent begins similarly to the antecedent, and (2) **interrupted** harmonic structure -- the antecedent ends on V (HC), interrupting the tonal motion, while the consequent continues to a PAC. The interrupted structure creates the sensation that the music "rewinds" after the HC and tries again, this time reaching full closure. The PIP is the most common period type. (Laitz, Ch. 20, pp. 466-468)

# Key Properties
1. Parallel melodic openings
2. Antecedent ends with HC (on V)
3. Consequent ends with PAC
4. Most common period type
5. The interruption creates a "restart" effect

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 466): Mozart, Piano Sonata in B-flat major, K. 333 -- PIP with HC in m. 4 and PAC in m. 8.
