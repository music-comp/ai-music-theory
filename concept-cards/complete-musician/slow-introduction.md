# Quick Definition
A slow introduction precedes the exposition in some sonata-form movements, beginning on the tonic (often weakly established), wandering through foreign harmonic territory, and closing on a half cadence that functions as a hugely extended upbeat to the allegro FTA.

# Core Definition
Some movements cast in sonata form contain slow introductions that touch on foreign harmonic territory and chromatic key areas and incorporate modal mixture. This is particularly common in large works such as symphonies. Slow introductions usually begin on the tonic (although I is not well established) and eventually move to and close on a half cadence. The introduction functions as a hugely extended upbeat that resolves to the tonic "downbeat" at the allegro FTA (Laitz, Ch. 33).

# Key Properties
1. Slow tempo preceding the allegro exposition
2. Begins on the tonic (weakly established)
3. Wanders through foreign harmonic territory
4. Uses chromaticism and modal mixture
5. Closes on a half cadence
6. Functions as an extended upbeat to the FTA

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 761): Beethoven, Symphony no. 1 in C -- 12-measure introduction that thwarts tonic clarity through applied chords and deceptive motions before V7 resolves to I at the allegro.
