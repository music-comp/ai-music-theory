# Quick Definition
Voice-leading rules for applied chords follow the same principles as for V7, with special attention to the temporary leading tone and chordal seventh as tendency tones.

# Core Definition
The voice-leading rules for V7 apply directly to applied dominant chords: (1) Do not double the leading tone or the seventh of the chord; (2) the leading tone must resolve upward when in an outer voice; (3) the seventh of the chord always resolves downward. Chromatic preparation--preceding a chromatic tone with its diatonic version in the same voice--is always preferred because it softens the aural effect (Laitz, Ch. 24, p. 542).

# Prerequisites
- **Applied Chord** -- the chords to which these rules apply
- **Voice Leading** -- fundamental part-writing principles

# Key Properties
1. Do not double the temporary leading tone or chordal seventh
2. Temporary leading tone resolves upward (especially in outer voices)
3. Chordal seventh resolves downward
4. Chromatic preparation is preferred: precede the chromatic tone with its diatonic version in the same voice
5. Root-position applied dominants can be complete or incomplete

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Apply standard V7 voice-leading rules
2. Prepare chromaticism in the same voice where possible
3. Avoid cross relations between outer voices

## To Recognize:
1. Check that tendency tones (temporary leading tone and seventh) resolve correctly
2. Verify no improper doublings

# Context & Application
When chromatic preparation is impossible, cross relations may occur. These are acceptable between inner voices or between one outer voice and an inner voice, but should be avoided between bass and soprano. A diminished fourth leap in the bass (e.g., to the temporary leading tone) is acceptable and expressive, especially in Baroque text-painting.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 542): In C major, V7/vi resolves with G# ascending to A (temporary leading tone) and the soprano line G-G#-A showing prepared chromaticism.

**Example 2** (p. 543): A diminished fourth leap in the bass to the temporary leading tone is acceptable and often accompanies words of sorrow in Baroque music.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Creating outer-voice cross relations
  **Correction**: Keep chromatic motion within a single voice; place unavoidable cross relations between inner voices

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking all cross relations are forbidden
  **Clarification**: Cross relations are acceptable between inner voices and are sometimes unavoidable; only outer-voice cross relations should be avoided
