# Quick Definition
The omnibus is an extended chromatic contrary-motion progression that prolongs a single harmony (typically the dominant) through an entire octave, with root-position dominant seventh chords appearing every third sonority, partitioning the octave into minor thirds.

# Core Definition
The omnibus extends chromatic contrary motion through an entire octave (ascending or descending) while prolonging a single underlying harmony. Different root-position dominant seventh chords appear on every third sonority, sometimes spelled enharmonically as Ger-6/5 chords. At each dominant seventh chord, an enharmonic voice exchange allows the contrary motion to begin anew with a different pair of voices. The bass ascent partitions the octave into minor thirds. First described by Viennese music theorists around 1800, the omnibus was used by Beethoven, Schubert, Chopin, and Liszt. In practice, composers generally used only portions of the complete omnibus (Laitz, Ch. 36).

# Prerequisites
- **Chromatic Contrary Motion** -- the technique being extended
- **Dissonant Prolongation** -- the omnibus is the maximal dissonant prolongation

# Key Properties
1. Traverses an entire octave through chromatic contrary motion
2. Prolongs a single harmony
3. Root-position V7 chords appear every third sonority
4. Bass ascent partitions octave into minor thirds
5. Enharmonic voice exchanges allow continuation
6. Different voice pairs take turns with the contrary motion
7. In practice, only portions are used

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The omnibus is a sequence
  **Clarification**: The omnibus is not a sequence. It is a prolongation of a single harmony through chromatic contrary motion. The V7 chords within the omnibus are all part of the same prolongation, not independent harmonic events.
