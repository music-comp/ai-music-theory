# Quick Definition
Tonal transposition restates a motive using the diatonic intervals of the prevailing key (interval quality may change); real transposition restates a motive preserving exact interval sizes (potentially leaving the key).

# Core Definition
**Tonal transposition** moves a motive to a new pitch level while maintaining membership in the prevailing diatonic scale. Because the pattern of whole and half steps varies within a scale, the quality of intervals may change (e.g., a major 2nd may become a minor 2nd). **Real transposition** moves a motive to a new pitch level while preserving the exact size and quality of every interval, which may require accidentals and effectively move outside the prevailing key. (Laitz, Ch. 16, p. 370)

# Prerequisites
- **Diatonic and chromatic scales** — Understanding scale-step patterns is essential to the distinction

# Key Properties
1. Tonal transposition: stays in key, interval quality may change
2. Real transposition: preserves intervals exactly, may leave key
3. Both preserve the motive's contour (direction of intervals)
4. Tonal transposition is more common in diatonic contexts; real transposition in chromatic or modulatory contexts

# Context & Application
The distinction is crucial in fugal writing (tonal vs. real answer), sequential passages, and motivic development. Tonal transposition creates smoother integration within a key; real transposition creates stronger connections between motivic statements but may introduce chromaticism.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 370): A motive ^1-^2-^3 (M2-M2) transposed tonally to ^2-^3-^4 (M2-m2) -- the last interval changes quality. The same motive transposed really to ^2-^3-#^4 (M2-M2) -- all intervals preserved, requiring an accidental.

- **Confusion**: Tonal transposition and real transposition sound the same
