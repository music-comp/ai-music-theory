# Quick Definition
An ascending sequence with root motion of descending third and ascending fourth, creating an overall step ascent. The first-inversion variant (ascending 5-6 sequence) features a sustained bass common tone and 5-6 motion in the outer voices. Perhaps the oldest of all sequences.

# Core Definition
The A2 (-3/+4) sequence ascends by step overall, with root motion of descending third (-3) and ascending fourth (+4). The descending third prepares the springboard motion up a fourth to begin the next copy. In its first-inversion variant -- the **ascending 5-6 sequence** -- the bass is sustained as a common tone throughout each model and copy, and parallels are averted by 5-6 contrapuntal motion. This is perhaps the oldest of all sequences, found in early 16th-century music (Josquin). (Laitz, Ch. 22, pp. 506-508)

# Key Properties
1. Root motion: -3/+4
2. Overall ascent by step
3. Propulsion comes from the ascending-fourth springboard
4. First-inversion variant = ascending 5-6 sequence
5. Bass sustained as common tone in 6/3 variant
6. Oldest known sequence type (Josquin)
7. Leads to PD
