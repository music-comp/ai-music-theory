# Quick Definition
A coda occurs after the recapitulation to confirm the closing key, often incorporating FTA or STA material over a pedal point; a codetta serves a similar function at the end of the exposition.

# Core Definition
Codas occur after the recapitulation and are optional ("coda" means "tail" or "appendage"). They confirm the closing key and often incorporate material from the FTA or STA, frequently stated over a pedal point for strong cadential effect. Codas often emphasize the subdominant, creating a large plagal motion that extends the prevailing key. Codettas serve the same function at the end of the exposition but are typically shorter and end away from the tonic key (Laitz, Ch. 33).

# Prerequisites
- **Sonata Form Recapitulation** -- the coda follows the recapitulation

# Key Properties
1. Optional section after the recapitulation
2. Confirms the closing key
3. Often uses FTA or STA material
4. Frequently features pedal point
5. May emphasize the subdominant (plagal motion)
6. Codetta: shorter version at the end of the exposition
