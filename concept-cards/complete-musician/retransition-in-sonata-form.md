# Quick Definition
The retransition (RTr) is the final area of the development section, where the dominant is secured and prolonged to prepare the return of the tonic at the recapitulation.

# Core Definition
The retransition is the final area of the development, where the dominant prepares the return of the tonic in the recapitulation. In major-mode sonata forms, the dominant is often secured much earlier (in the STA) and implicitly prolonged through the development; the retransition explicitly restates and expands the dominant at the end of the development, moving to the interruption that precedes the recapitulation (Laitz, Ch. 33).

# Prerequisites
- **Sonata Form Development** -- the retransition is the final part of the development

# Key Properties
1. Final area of the development
2. Secures and prolongs the dominant
3. Prepares the return of tonic at the recapitulation
4. Often features a dominant pedal point
5. Creates expectation for the recapitulation's arrival

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 758): Beethoven, op. 49, no. 1 -- RTr secures V before recapitulation at m. 64.

**Example 2** (p. 773): Haydn, Sonata in C -- Pedal on E (V/vi) leads through circle of fifths to the dominant, beginning the retransition at m. 94.
