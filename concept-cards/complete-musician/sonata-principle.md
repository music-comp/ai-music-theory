# Quick Definition
The sonata principle requires that material presented in the contrasting key (STA) during the exposition must return in the tonic key during the recapitulation.

# Core Definition
The sonata principle is the fundamental tonal requirement of sonata form, derived from balanced-binary form characteristics. Material (STA, usually with a new theme) presented at the end of the first section (exposition) in a contrasting key returns at the end of the piece (recapitulation) in the tonic key. This tonal resolution is what gives sonata form its large-scale harmonic coherence and dramatic trajectory (Laitz, Ch. 33).

# Prerequisites
- **Sonata Form** -- the formal context
- **Balanced Binary Form** -- the structural origin of the principle

# Key Properties
1. STA material must return in the tonic during recapitulation
2. Derived from balanced-binary characteristics
3. Creates the large-scale tonal resolution of the form
4. The principle applies regardless of whether themes are the same or different

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 753): In Beethoven's op. 49, no. 1, the STA theme first appears in III (Bb major) and returns in i (G minor) in the recapitulation.
