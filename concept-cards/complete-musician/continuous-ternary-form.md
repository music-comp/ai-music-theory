# Quick Definition
Continuous ternary form (ABA) is a ternary form in which both the A and B sections close away from their respective tonic chords.

# Core Definition
When both A and B close in keys other than their respective tonics, the form is a continuous ternary form. This is the most tonally interconnected of the ternary subtypes and can be difficult to distinguish from rounded binary form. The determining factor is the degree of independence of the B section from the A section: if there is little thematic/motivic connection and a clear change of key, ternary is indicated; if B is developmental and motivically tied to A, rounded binary is more appropriate (Laitz, Ch. 31).

# Prerequisites
- **Ternary Form** -- understanding basic ABA structure

# Key Properties
1. Both A and B close away from their respective tonic chords
2. Despite tonal openness, sections remain relatively independent
3. B section introduces genuinely contrasting material

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Identify the three main sections
2. Verify that both A and B close in keys other than their tonics
3. Assess B's independence from A -- key criterion for distinguishing from rounded binary

# Context & Application
Continuous ternary is the rarest and most ambiguous ternary subtype. Analysis often requires interpretive judgment about whether the form is rounded binary or continuous ternary.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 704): Laitz notes that it may not be easy to distinguish continuous ternary from rounded binary; the analyst must interpret based on how they hear the work and support their answer.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Continuous ternary vs. rounded binary
  **Clarification**: If B is genuinely independent with its own thematic material and little motivic connection to A, it is continuous ternary. If B is developmental and connected to A, it is rounded binary.
