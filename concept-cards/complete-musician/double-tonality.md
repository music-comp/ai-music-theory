# Quick Definition
Double tonality occurs when two keys vie for supremacy simultaneously throughout a piece, with the ambiguity so acute that the analyst may conclude the piece is controlled by both keys rather than one.

# Core Definition
Sometimes two keys vie for supremacy simultaneously throughout a piece. The ambiguity can be so acute that the analyst may interpret the piece as being controlled not by a single key but by the two keys juxtaposed throughout. Such pieces display double tonality. Most of the time, double-tonal pieces are better interpreted as being in a single key: the key in which the piece closes. Examples include songs by Schubert, scenes and acts of operas by Wagner, and orchestral works by Liszt, Mahler, and Strauss (Laitz, Ch. 34).

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 807): Brahms, "Es hing der Reif" -- C major and A minor compete throughout; interior cadences in C major, but the song closes in A minor.
