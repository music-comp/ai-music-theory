# Quick Definition
A passing six-four chord connects a root-position chord to its first inversion (or vice versa) through stepwise bass motion that fills the interval of a third, functioning identically to a passing viio6.

# Core Definition
A **passing six-four chord** (P 6/4) derives its name from the bass passing motion that fills the interval of a third. It connects a root-position chord with its first inversion through a stepwise bass line (e.g., I to I6 via bass ^1-^2-^3, where the passing six-four occurs on ^2). It functions identically to the passing viio6 chord. Like all unaccented six-four chords, it occurs on metrically weak beats (or weak measures in quick tempos, or weak parts of beats in slow tempos). It may extend any harmony, not just tonic (Laitz, Ch. 14, pp. 327-329).

# Prerequisites
- **Passing tone** -- the bass motion creating the chord
- **First-inversion triads** -- the chord being connected to root position

# Key Properties
1. Bass passes by step through a third (^1-^2-^3 or ^3-^2-^1)
2. Connects root position to first inversion of the same harmony
3. Functions like passing viio6
4. Occurs on weak beats/measures
5. Can extend any harmony (tonic, subdominant, etc.)

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 327-328): In Beethoven's "Tempest" Sonata, the passing six-four connects i to i6 within the tonic expansion (bass A-B-C), with restruck suspensions obscuring the passing chord.
