# Quick Definition
IV functions as pre-dominant because I-IV proceeds by descending fifth (the strongest root motion) and the bass ^4-^5 creates smooth stepwise motion to V; in minor, iv6-V produces the characteristic phrygian cadence.

# Core Definition
The **subdominant** (IV in major, iv in minor) frequently serves as pre-dominant because: (1) I-IV proceeds by descending fifth, the most convincing root motion in tonal music, and (2) the bass ascent from ^4 to ^5 creates smooth stepwise motion to V. When the bass approaches V from below (^4 to ^5), upper voices usually descend in contrary motion. IV6-V, with the bass falling from ^6 to ^5, is also common, especially in minor where the powerful falling half step creates the **phrygian cadence** (PHRY). IV can also prepare the seventh of V7 when ^4 is in the soprano (Laitz, Ch. 12, pp. 283-285).

# Prerequisites
- **Pre-dominant function** -- IV is one of two main PD chords

# Key Properties
1. Root position: bass ^4 rises to ^5 (smooth stepwise to V)
2. First inversion (IV6): bass ^6 falls to ^5
3. Phrygian cadence: iv6-V in minor (bass ^6-^5, soprano ^4-^5)
4. I-IV = descending fifth root motion (strongest)
5. Upper voices move contrary to bass when ^4 rises to ^5
6. ^4 in soprano can prepare the seventh of V7

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 285): Bach's Cantata no. 5 demonstrates the phrygian cadence (iv6-V) with its characteristic pathos, used by Baroque composers to close slow inner movements.
