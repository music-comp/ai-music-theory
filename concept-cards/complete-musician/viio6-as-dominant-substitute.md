# Quick Definition
viio6 is called a dominant substitute because it shares three pitches with V7 (^7, ^2, ^4); it functions as a passing chord between I and I6 with ^2 in the bass.

# Core Definition
The **viio6** chord is a first-inversion diminished triad built on the leading tone. It is called a **dominant substitute** because it shares three pitches with V7 (^7, ^2, and ^4). It is the ideal passing chord because: (1) its diminished sonority is inherently unstable, (2) it shares notes with V/V7, and (3) being in first inversion makes it naturally subordinate. Root-position viio is avoided due to the exposed tritone between bass and upper voice. Voice leading: never double ^7; try to double ^2 (the third of the chord); resolve the tritone when it involves the bass (Laitz, Ch. 10, pp. 233-236).

# Prerequisites

# Key Properties
1. Shares ^7, ^2, ^4 with V7 (missing only ^5)
2. Diminished sonority -- inherently unstable
3. First inversion -- subordinate function
4. Never double ^7
5. Double ^2 (the bass/third); ^4 acceptable
6. In minor: ^7 must be raised (figured bass shows accidental)
