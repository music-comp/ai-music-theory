# Quick Definition
A transition bridges from the tonic to a new key (A to B), while a retransition bridges from a contrasting key back to tonic (B to A). Both create continuity between self-contained sections.

# Core Definition
In ternary form, a transition is bridging material that leads from the tonic key to a new key (from A to B). A retransition (returning transition) leads from a contrasting key back to the tonic (from B to A). A ternary form may have any combination: both transition and retransition, only one, or neither. Retransitions occur much more frequently than transitions. These bridging passages make some of the most exciting music in a piece because their material is completely unpredictable (Laitz, Ch. 31).

# Key Properties
1. Transitions modulate from home key to a contrasting key
2. Retransitions return from contrasting key to home key
3. Content is unpredictable -- may be as simple as the dominant of the next key or extensively developmental
4. May foreshadow motives and harmonies of the upcoming section
5. Retransitions are more common than transitions

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Look for a conclusive cadence ending a section followed by modulatory material
2. Check for melodic fragmentation typical of transitional areas
3. Distinguish from continuous ternary: if modulation is integral to a section's motion, it is not a separate transition

# Context & Application
Transitions and retransitions appear in ternary, rondo, and sonata forms. In ternary form, they mediate between self-contained sections. In rondo form, retransitions return from episodes to refrains. In sonata form, transitions connect the FTA to the STA.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 707): Brahms, Romanze in F, op. 118, no. 5 -- A retransition (mm. 45-48) reprises the A section's slurred chords while retaining elements of B (key of D major, trills), bridging smoothly back to F major.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming every ternary form has a transition
  **Correction**: Many ternary forms proceed directly from one section to the next without bridging material

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Is the modulation part of the section or a separate transition?
  **Clarification**: If a section closes in another key but the closure is integral to the section's tonal motion, there is no separate transitional area. If there is a conclusive end followed by modulatory fragmentation, a transition is present.
