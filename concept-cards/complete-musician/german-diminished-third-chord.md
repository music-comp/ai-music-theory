# Quick Definition
The German diminished third chord (Ger7) is an inversion of the German augmented sixth in which #4 appears in the bass and b6 in an upper voice, creating a diminished third interval that contracts on resolution.

# Core Definition
When the pitches of the Ger65 are reordered so that #4 appears in the bass and b6 in an upper voice, the resulting chord is the German diminished third chord (Ger7), figured as "7" because a seventh appears above the bass. The voice leading is identical to the Ger65: b6 descends to 5 and #4 ascends to 5. But since the pitches are inverted, the voices contract on resolution instead of expanding. The Ger7 is enharmonically equivalent to V42 (Laitz, Ch. 30, pp. 689-690).

# Prerequisites

# Key Properties
1. Inversion of Ger65 with #4 in bass
2. Contains a diminished third interval (contracts on resolution)
3. Figured as "7" (Ger7)
4. Voice leading identical to Ger65 but voices contract instead of expand
5. Enharmonically equivalent to V42
6. Used in PD expansions through voice exchange with Ger65

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 690): Tchaikovsky, Sleeping Beauty, scene 1 -- Ger7 elaborates V as its neighbor, then is respelled as V42 of bII.
