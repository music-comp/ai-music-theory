# Quick Definition
A relatively independent part of a phrase, marked by a pause (caesura) and/or by the repetition and variation of short melodic gestures, that does not contain its own structural cadence.

# Core Definition
A subphrase is a subdivision of a phrase that possesses some degree of independence -- marked by a caesura, by motivic repetition, or by both -- but does not contain a structural cadence. Subphrases are components of a single phrase with one overall harmonic progression and one cadence. The presence of a caesura does not guarantee subphrases; a continuous harmonic progression with a caesura is still one undivided phrase. Conversely, subphrases can exist without caesuras if motivic structure clearly delineates them. (Laitz, Ch. 17, pp. 418-419)

# Prerequisites
- **Phrase model** — Must understand what constitutes a complete phrase

# Key Properties
1. Part of a larger phrase, not an independent formal unit
2. Can be marked by caesura, motivic repetition, or both
3. Does not contain a structural cadence
4. The parent phrase has only one overall harmonic progression and one cadence

# Context & Application
Subphrases are essential for analyzing how phrases are internally organized. They provide the basis for understanding composite phrases and sentence structures.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 418): Haydn, Symphony no. 100, "Military" -- an eight-measure phrase divides into three subphrases: two two-measure units ending with caesuras, plus a four-measure unit ending with a half cadence.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Phrase model** — Subphrases are internal divisions of phrases

## Enables
- **Composite phrase** — Phrases with three or more subphrases
- **Sentence structure** — The A-A'-B structure is delineated by subphrases

## Contrasts With
- **Period** — A period combines multiple phrases; subphrases are within a single phrase

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: A caesura always indicates a subphrase boundary
  **Clarification**: If the harmonic progression is continuous across the caesura, there may be no subphrase division
