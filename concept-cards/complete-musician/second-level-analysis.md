# Quick Definition
Second-level analysis goes beyond labeling every chord with roman numerals to show which harmonies are structural (controlling a passage) and which are subordinate (embellishing a structural harmony).

# Core Definition
**Second-level analysis** (also called **interpretive analysis**) is a multi-level analytical technique that distinguishes structural harmonies from ornamental ones. After labeling every chord with roman numerals (first-level/descriptive analysis), the analyst adds a second level showing the controlling harmony for each span. Structural chords retain their roman numerals; subordinate chords are labeled by their melodic function (PT, N, CL). Bracketed groups of chord symbols indicate larger harmonic units. Depending on the number of musical events, more than two levels may be required (Laitz, Ch. 9, pp. 214-215).

# Prerequisites
- **Embellishment and reduction** -- second-level analysis is the analytical notation of reduction
- **Hierarchy in tonal music** -- the principle being represented

# Key Properties
1. First level: descriptive -- roman numerals for every chord
2. Second level: interpretive -- identifies controlling harmonies
3. Subordinate chords labeled by melodic function (PT, N, CL)
4. Brackets group subordinate chords under their controlling harmony
5. Multiple levels may be needed for complex passages

# Context & Application
Second-level analysis is the standard analytical method used throughout the text. It reveals compositional processes invisible to surface-level labeling and carries performance implications, since understanding harmonic hierarchy informs phrasing and dynamic shaping.
