# Quick Definition
Embellishment adds tones of figuration to a basic harmonic structure to create a musical surface; reduction strips away embellishments to reveal the underlying two-voice contrapuntal framework.

# Core Definition
**Embellishment** is the process of adding tones of figuration (passing tones, neighbors, chordal leaps) and textural variety to a basic harmonic and contrapuntal structure. **Reduction** is the reverse process: simplifying a florid musical surface to reveal the underlying two-voice contrapuntal framework and essential harmonic progression. These complementary processes demonstrate that tonal music is hierarchical -- ornamental events decorate but do not obscure the structural framework (Laitz, Ch. 9, pp. 207-210).

# Prerequisites
- **Tones of figuration** -- the embellishments being added or removed
- **Hierarchy in tonal music** -- the principle that embellishment/reduction reveals

# Key Properties
1. Embellishment adds ornamental pitches to a skeleton
2. Reduction removes ornamental pitches to reveal the skeleton
3. Multiple rounds of embellishment can generate actual compositions
4. The outer-voice counterpoint controls the flow of events
5. Harmony provides the standard for measuring consonance vs. dissonance

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 207-210): Starting from a single tonic supporting ^1, Laitz adds neighbor and passing tones, then harmonizes them with V, then adds further figuration to create contrasting musical surfaces -- all sharing the same underlying I-V-I structure.

**Example 2** (p. 210): Vivaldi's "Spring" is shown as the embellishment of a simple tonic-to-dominant progression with recurring eighth-note figures and passing tones.
