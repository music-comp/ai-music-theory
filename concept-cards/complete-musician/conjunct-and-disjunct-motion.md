# Quick Definition
Conjunct motion (steps) moves between adjacent scale degrees; disjunct motion includes skips (thirds) and leaps (fourths or larger), with leaps generally followed by a change of direction.

# Core Definition
**Conjunct motion** (or stepwise motion) moves by seconds between adjacent scale degrees. **Disjunct motion** involves skips (jumps of a third) or leaps (jumps of a fourth or more). Good melodic writing uses primarily conjunct motion with occasional disjunct motion. The **law of recovery** requires that leaps be followed by a change of direction to fill in the musical space.

# Prerequisites
- **Interval** -- motion types are defined by interval size

# Key Properties
1. Steps (conjunct): seconds -- the most common melodic motion
2. Skips: thirds -- moderately disjunct
3. Leaps: fourths or larger -- strongly disjunct
4. Law of recovery: change direction after a leap, preferably by step
5. Two consecutive skips of a third are acceptable if followed by direction change
