# Quick Definition
Scale degrees are the numbered positions (^1 through ^7) of pitches within a diatonic scale, each with a functional name reflecting its relationship to the tonic.

# Core Definition
Each of the seven members of a diatonic scale can be numbered in ascending order beginning with the tonic. Scale degree numbers are distinguished by a caret (^) placed above the number. Scale degrees are also identified by functional names reflecting their relationship to the tonic.

# Prerequisites
- **Diatonic Scale** -- scale degrees exist within a diatonic context
- **Tonic** -- all degree names reference the tonic

# Key Properties
1. ^1 = **Tonic** -- the gravitational center
2. ^2 = **Supertonic** -- lies immediately above the tonic
3. ^3 = **Mediant** -- midway between tonic and dominant
4. ^4 = **Subdominant** -- lies a fifth below the tonic (or a fourth above)
5. ^5 = **Dominant** -- five steps above the tonic; the secondary tonal pillar
6. ^6 = **Submediant** -- lies three steps below the tonic (mirrors the mediant)
7. ^7 = **Leading tone** -- lies one step below the tonic, to which it usually leads
8. In minor, lowered ^7 is called the **subtonic** (it does not lead to the tonic but rather descends toward ^5)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Determine the key and tonic
2. Number each scale pitch ascending from ^1 to ^7
3. Apply the functional name based on position

# Context & Application
Scale degree names reveal hierarchical function. The tonic, dominant, and mediant are the most important in order of prominence. Understanding scale degrees is essential for harmonic analysis, chord construction, and melodic composition.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing the subdominant name's origin.
  **Correction**: The subdominant (^4) is named because it lies a fifth *below* the tonic, just as the dominant lies a fifth above. It does not mean "under the dominant."
