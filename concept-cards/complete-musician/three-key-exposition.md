# Quick Definition
A three-key exposition is a sonata exposition in which the STA moves first to a diatonic third-related key and then to the dominant, bisecting the traditional I-V fifth motion into two thirds.

# Core Definition
The three-key exposition is found in major-mode works in which the STA moves to a diatonic third-related key, bisecting the traditional fifth motion from I to V. The motivation may have been to create more dramatic tonal motion comparable to the minor-mode i-III-V progression. Some three-key expositions progress I-iii-V (e.g., Bruckner's Sixth Symphony in A major: I-iii-V). More radical examples postpone the structural dominant until the retransition, remaining in the chromatic mediant for the entire exposition (e.g., Beethoven's "Waldstein" Sonata: I-III throughout the exposition) (Laitz, Ch. 33).

# Key Properties
1. Three keys in the exposition instead of two
2. Third-related key bisects the I-V motion
3. May progress I-iii-V or I-III (with V postponed to retransition)
4. Creates more dramatic tonal trajectory than standard I-V
5. Particularly associated with Beethoven's "middle period" works

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 764): Beethoven, Piano Sonata op. 31, no. 1 -- Moves from I (G major) to III (B major), then to iii (B minor).

**Example 2** (p. 765): Beethoven, "Waldstein" Sonata, op. 53 -- Moves from I (C major) to III (E major) throughout the exposition; V secured only at the retransition.
