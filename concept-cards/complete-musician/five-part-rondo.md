# Quick Definition
The five-part rondo (A1-B-A2-C-A3) contains three refrains alternating with two contrasting episodes, often with transitions, retransitions, and a coda.

# Core Definition
The five-part rondo extends ternary form by adding a second contrasting episode (C) and a third refrain. The typical key scheme in major is: A1 (I) - B (V) - A2 (I) - C (i or vi or IV) - A3 (I). Episodes often end with retransitions leading back to the refrain in the home key. A coda may follow A3, providing extra cadential material to confirm the home key (Laitz, Ch. 32).

# Prerequisites
- **Rondo Form** -- the overarching form type

# Key Properties
1. Structure: A1-B-A2-C-A3 (plus optional coda)
2. Three refrains in the home key
3. Two contrasting episodes in different keys
4. Often includes retransitions at the ends of episodes
5. May include nested binary forms (compound rondo)

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 733): Clementi Piano Sonatina -- Five-part rondo: A1 (I) - B (V) - A2 (I) - C (i) - A3 (I) - Coda, with retransitions after B and C.

**Example 2** (p. 731): Couperin, "Air tendre" -- Early rondeau with A1 (i) - B (III) - A2 (i) - C (v) - A3 (i).
