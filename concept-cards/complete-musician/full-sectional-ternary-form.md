# Quick Definition
Full sectional ternary form (A||B||A||) is a ternary form in which every section closes in its respective tonic key.

# Core Definition
When each of the three sections of a ternary form closes in its respective tonic -- the A section in the home key, the B section in its contrasting key, and the returning A in the home key -- the form is called a full sectional ternary form. This represents the most independent, self-contained type of ternary organization (Laitz, Ch. 31).

# Prerequisites
- **Ternary Form** -- understanding the basic ABA structure and tonal plan

# Key Properties
1. A section closes in the home key
2. B section closes in its own contrasting key
3. Returning A (or A') closes in the home key
4. Maximum tonal independence between sections

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Identify all three sections (A, B, A or A')
2. Verify that A closes with a cadence in the tonic
3. Verify that B closes with a cadence in the contrasting key
4. Verify that the returning section closes in the tonic

# Context & Application
Full sectional ternary is the most clear-cut type of ternary form. It appears in character pieces, minuet-trio movements, and da capo arias where maximum contrast and independence between sections is desired.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 703): Grieg, "Hjemve," op. 57, no. 6 -- A full sectional ternary with each section closing in its own tonic.

**Example 2** (p. 716): Beethoven, Piano Sonata no. 1, Menuetto-Trio -- A full sectional ternary form with both minuet and trio as rounded continuous binary forms.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming that any ternary with clear section boundaries is full sectional
  **Correction**: Check the cadential key of each section; all must close in their respective tonics
