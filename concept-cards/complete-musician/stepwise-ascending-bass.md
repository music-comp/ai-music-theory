# Quick Definition
With the addition of pre-dominants, the bass can ascend stepwise from ^1 to ^5 (I-viio6/V4/3-I6-IV or ii6-V), creating one of the most common and powerful bass patterns in tonal music.

# Core Definition
With the addition of pre-dominant chords, a stepwise ascending bass line from ^1 to ^5 becomes possible: I (^1) - passing chord (^2) - I6 (^3) - PD (^4) - V (^5). This bass pattern appears in thousands of phrases from the early Baroque to today's popular music. It has an especially powerful effect in major, where the bass of I6 (^3) lies only a half step from the pre-dominant on ^4 (Laitz, Ch. 12, pp. 289-290).

# Prerequisites
- **Pre-dominant function** -- provides the ^4 step
- **Contrapuntal expansion** -- provides the ^2 and ^3 steps

# Key Properties
1. Bass: ^1-^2-^3-^4-^5
2. Harmonization: I - viio6 (or V4/3) - I6 - IV (or ii6) - V
3. Extremely common pattern across four centuries
4. Especially powerful in major (half step ^3 to ^4)
5. Combines tonic expansion (^1-^2-^3) with pre-dominant to dominant (^4-^5)
