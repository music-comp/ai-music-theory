# Quick Definition
Unequal fifths (P5 to d5) are permissible when moving from I to V7, as long as the d5 resolves to a third; the reverse (d5 to P5) is generally not permitted.

# Core Definition
**Unequal fifths** occur when a perfect fifth moves to a diminished fifth (or vice versa) between two voices. P5 to d5 (as in I moving to V7) is permissible as long as the diminished fifth resolves to a third. However, d5 to P5 is generally not permitted, since it contradicts the natural tendency of the diminished fifth to resolve inward to a third (Laitz, Ch. 9, p. 222).

# Prerequisites
- **Parallel fifths and octaves** -- unequal fifths are the exception
- **V7** -- the context where unequal fifths most commonly arise

# Key Properties
1. P5 to d5: PERMITTED (I to V7)
2. d5 to P5: generally NOT permitted
3. The d5 must resolve to a third
4. Different from parallel P5s (which are always forbidden)
