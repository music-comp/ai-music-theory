# Quick Definition
Elaboration and reduction are complementary processes applied to instrumental performance: reduction simplifies a score to its four-voice skeleton, which is then arpeggiated; elaboration builds from a figured bass or progression outward through arpeggiation and added tones of figuration.

# Core Definition
**Elaboration** and **reduction** are complementary processes for transferring analytical knowledge to instrumental practice. **Reduction** involves: (1) determining the key; (2) identifying harmonic function and harmonic rhythm from accented bass notes; (3) finding the soprano line and outer-voice counterpoint; (4) adding inner voices to create a four-voice structure; (5) arpeggiating the result for melody instruments. **Elaboration** involves: (1) realizing a figured bass or progression; (2) adding soprano and inner voices; (3) arpeggiating in strict voice-leading; (4) adding changes in contour and tones of figuration. These processes reveal how the same harmonic paradigms underlie vastly different surface textures (Laitz, Ch. 11, pp. 268-275).

# Key Properties
1. Reduction: florid score -> four-voice skeleton -> arpeggiation
2. Elaboration: figured bass -> four-voice realization -> arpeggiation -> figuration
3. Melody instruments arpeggiate the homophonic chords
4. Flexibility needed: omit figurations, normalize registers, infer missing pitches
5. Can slow harmonic rhythm by extending each chord's duration
