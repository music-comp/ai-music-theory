# Quick Definition
A modulation is a longer tonicization that occupies an entire section of a piece, includes a strong cadence in the new key, and gives the feeling that the new key has usurped the home key.

# Core Definition
Modulations include a strong cadence in the new key, and the new key continues after the cadence. They give the feeling that a new key has usurped the home key, at least for the moment. Although it is difficult to draw a firm line between tonicizations and modulations, tonicizations usually occur within phrases and are fleeting, while modulations establish new tonal areas confirmed by cadences (Laitz, Ch. 25, p. 568).

# Prerequisites
- **Tonicization** -- modulation extends the concept of tonicization

# Key Properties
1. Includes a strong cadence (usually PAC) in the new key
2. The new key continues after the cadence
3. The listener feels the new key has replaced the home key
4. Very few compositions end in a different key from the one in which they begin
5. Modulations participate in a single overall harmonic motion at the deepest level

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Establish the original key
2. Place the pivot chord about halfway through the modulating phrase
3. Use at least a few chords in each key
4. Do not crash into a PAC immediately after the pivot
5. Close with a strong cadence (PAC with cadential 6/4) in the new key

## To Recognize:
1. Listen for persistent new chromatic pitches through the end of a phrase
2. Note changes of mode between opening and closing of a section
3. Compare the closing tonic pitch with the opening tonic

# Context & Application
Modulations in tonal music move from a home key to closely related keys. Major keys tend to modulate to V, vi, and iii; minor keys to III, v, and VI. Despite modulations, the prevailing tonic of the entire work is never displaced.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 569): Haydn, Piano Sonata in E minor -- the second phrase closes with a convincing PAC in G major (III), and the music continues in G major, indicating a modulation.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Using the dominant of the new key as a pivot chord
  **Correction**: Avoid this; it produces a harsh-sounding modulation

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking a modulation permanently changes the key
  **Clarification**: Modulations are subordinate to the overall tonic; the home key returns
