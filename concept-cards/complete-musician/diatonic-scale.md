# Quick Definition
A diatonic scale is a seven-note scale using each letter name once, mixing half and whole steps in a specific pattern, and serving as the foundation of a musical key.

# Core Definition
A **diatonic** scale (meaning "through the tones") is built from the ordered succession of seven different letter names, each represented once and only once. It contains five whole steps and two half steps in a specific pattern. A composition based on such a scale is said to be in a **key**. Pitches belonging to the scale are **diatonic**; pitches lying outside are **chromatic** (from Latin *chroma*, "color").

# Prerequisites
- **Half Step and Whole Step** -- diatonic scales are defined by their H/W pattern
- **Chromatic Scale** -- the diatonic scale is a subset of the chromatic twelve

# Key Properties
1. Contains exactly seven pitch classes, each letter name used once
2. Five whole steps and two half steps per octave
3. Can begin on any of the 12 pitch classes
4. The specific placement of H and W steps determines the mode (major or minor)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Choose a starting pitch (the tonic)
2. Write each letter name in order
3. Apply the appropriate H/W pattern for the desired mode

## To Recognize:
1. Confirm seven distinct letter names
2. Verify five whole steps and two half steps per octave

# Context & Application
Diatonic scales form the pitch material for the vast majority of common-practice music. Chromaticism adds "color" to the diatonic foundation.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 7): Example 1.7 shows the E-major diatonic scale as unfilled notes within the chromatic scale, with five whole steps and two half steps (between G#/A and D#/E).

# Relationships
## Contrasts With
- **Chromatic Scale** -- all twelve pitches vs. the diatonic seven

## Enables
- **Major Scale** -- one specific diatonic pattern (W-W-H-W-W-W-H)
- **Minor Scales** -- another family of diatonic patterns
- **Key** -- a composition based on a diatonic scale

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: "Diatonic" means "white keys only."
  **Clarification**: Diatonic means "belonging to the prevailing scale." In A major, C#, F#, and G# are diatonic.
