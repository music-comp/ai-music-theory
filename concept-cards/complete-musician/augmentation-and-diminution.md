# Quick Definition
Augmentation proportionally lengthens a motive's rhythmic values (e.g., doubling durations); diminution proportionally shortens them (e.g., halving durations). Both preserve the pitch contour.

# Core Definition
**Augmentation** is the proportional lengthening of a motive's rhythmic values while preserving its pitch content and contour. **Diminution** is the proportional shortening of rhythmic values. These transformations change the temporal scale of a motive without altering its melodic identity, and they are often used to create contrast between statements at different structural levels or to create stretto effects. (Laitz, Ch. 16, p. 375)

# Prerequisites
- **Motive** — The original motive must be recognizable for the transformation to be perceived

# Key Properties
1. Augmentation: typically doubles or triples rhythmic values
2. Diminution: typically halves rhythmic values
3. Pitch contour and intervals are preserved
4. Augmentation can create a sense of broadening or grandeur
5. Diminution can create urgency or excitement

# Context & Application
Augmentation is common at climactic or concluding moments; diminution is common in development sections and in creating stretto effects in fugues.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 375): A motive in quarter notes is augmented to half notes, creating a broader, more expansive statement.
