# Quick Definition
A passing chord harmonizes a passing tone in the bass, typically connecting a root-position chord with its first inversion (e.g., I-viio6-I6, where viio6 passes between ^1 and ^3).

# Core Definition
A **passing chord** results from harmonizing a bass passing tone that connects a root-position chord with its first inversion. The most common example is viio6 passing between I and I6 (bass ^1-^2-^3). Like neighboring chords, passing chords usually fall on metrically weak beats and are subordinate to the structural harmony they embellish. The viio6 is preferred over root-position ii for this function because it is unstable (diminished sonority), shares notes with V and V7, and is in first inversion (Laitz, Ch. 10, pp. 233-235).

# Prerequisites
- **Passing tone** -- the bass motion creating the chord
- **First-inversion triads** -- the passing chord is typically in first inversion

# Key Properties
1. Harmonizes a passing-tone motion in the bass
2. Usually connects root position with first inversion of the same harmony
3. Falls on metrically weak beats
4. viio6 is the primary passing chord for tonic expansion
5. IV6 can serve as passing chord for dominant expansion (V-IV6-V6)
