# Quick Definition
The da capo aria is the most important ternary structure in the Baroque period, a vocal form punctuated by instrumental ritornellos in which the ABA structure is realized by a da capo marking.

# Core Definition
The da capo aria is arguably the most important ternary structure in the Baroque period. One or more instrumental interludes called ritornellos usually punctuate formal parts and sections of text. The A section typically remains in the tonic key, while the B section is more transitory and sequential, similar to a binary form digression. The da capo marking creates the return of A (Laitz, Ch. 31).

# Key Properties
1. Vocal form with instrumental accompaniment
2. Punctuated by ritornellos (instrumental interludes)
3. A section usually stays in the tonic
4. B section is transitory, sequential, and modulatory
5. ABA structure realized by da capo marking

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 712): Bach, "Ich will Dir mein Herze schenken," St. Matthew Passion -- A da capo aria with ritornello punctuation, A section in I (G major), B section moving through vi and v of vi.
