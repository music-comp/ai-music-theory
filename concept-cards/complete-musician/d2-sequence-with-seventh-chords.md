# Quick Definition
D2 (-5/+4) sequences enhanced with diatonic seventh chords, in two varieties: alternating sevenths (every other chord has a seventh) and interlocking sevenths (every chord has a seventh, with preparations and resolutions interlocking between voices).

# Core Definition
Sevenths can be added to D2 (-5/+4) sequences in two ways: (1) **Alternating sevenths** -- every other chord receives a seventh. Each seventh is prepared by common tone and resolved down by step. Once started, the resolution pitch simultaneously serves as the next preparation pitch. (2) **Interlocking sevenths** -- every chord contains a seventh. The soprano and alto have interlocking sevenths: as one voice resolves its seventh, the other prepares its seventh. In interlocking sequences, every other chord is incomplete (omitted fifth, doubled root). The one rule: prepare and resolve each chordal seventh. (Laitz, Ch. 23, pp. 518-522)

# Key Properties
1. Alternating: seventh on every other chord; all chords can be complete
2. Interlocking: seventh on every chord; every other chord incomplete
3. Both types: seventh prepared (common tone) and resolved (step down)
4. Adds harmonic richness and color (four different seventh-chord qualities: mm, MM, dm, Mm)
