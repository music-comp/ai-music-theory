# Quick Definition
The bVI chord is a major triad built on the lowered sixth scale degree, a product of harmonic mixture that functions as a submediant with pre-dominant tendencies and a dramatic half-step relationship to V.

# Core Definition
bVI arises from harmonic mixture by lowering the root of the submediant chord and also lowering b3 to produce a consonant major triad (rather than the dissonant augmented triad that would result from lowering only the root). Despite its chromatic inflection, bVI continues to function as a submediant: it participates in descending arpeggiations, descending-fifth motions, precedes V as PD, and substitutes for tonic in deceptive motions (V-bVI). Because bVI lies a half step above V, composers exploit this proximity for dramatic upper-neighbor effects (Laitz, Ch. 27, pp. 620-621).

# Prerequisites
- **Harmonic Mixture** -- the type of mixture that produces bVI

# Key Properties
1. Major triad on b6 (requires both b6 and b3)
2. Functions as submediant despite chromaticism
3. Half-step proximity to V creates dramatic neighbor effects
4. Participates in deceptive motions (V-bVI)
5. Three scale degrees shared with Ger65 (enabling conversion)

# Context & Application
bVI is one of the most versatile mixture chords. It can be converted to a Ger65 by adding #4 (replacing one pitch), creating a smooth path to the dominant. This conversion is particularly useful when bVI needs to lead directly to V.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 621): bVI participates in descending arpeggiations (I-bVI-iv-V), descending fifths, and deceptive motions in C major.
