# Quick Definition
A formal/harmonic principle in which a phrase's harmonic motion from I to V is "interrupted" (stopped on V), requiring a restart and second attempt that completes the journey I-V-I.

# Core Definition
Harmonic interruption occurs when the tonic-to-dominant motion stops on V (half cadence), creating an open-ended phrase that demands continuation. The music "restarts" from tonic and completes the full I...V-I motion. Interruption is the harmonic foundation of the most common period types (PIP, CIP). The two-pass structure I...V || I...V-I (where || marks the interruption) generates the antecedent-consequent relationship. (Laitz, Ch. 20, pp. 466-468)

# Key Properties
1. Phrase stops on V (HC) without resolving to I
2. Music restarts from tonic
3. Second pass completes the I...V-I motion
4. Creates the question-answer effect of periods
5. The interrupted V is essentially a back-relating dominant
