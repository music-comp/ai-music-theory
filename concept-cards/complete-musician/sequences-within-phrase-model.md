# Quick Definition
Sequences generally occur after a tonic expansion and before the PD-D-T cadential area, extending the phrase model. The three basic components of a phrase containing a sequence are: tonic expansion, sequence, and cadence.

# Core Definition
Sequences fit within phrases as extensions of the tonic area, typically occurring between the tonic expansion and the cadential PD-D-T. A phrase containing a sequence has three basic components: (1) tonic expansion, (2) sequence (prolongational or transitional), (3) cadence. Fitting a sequence requires consideration of harmonic rhythm, balance, and proportion. Solutions include using faster rhythmic values, using only part of the sequence, increasing phrase length, or overlapping the tonic expansion with the sequence's beginning. (Laitz, Ch. 23, pp. 514-517)

# Key Properties
1. Three components: tonic expansion, sequence, cadence
2. Sequence usually occurs between tonic area and PD-D-T
3. Harmonic rhythm, balance, and proportion must be considered
4. Sequence can overlap with tonic expansion or PD

# Context & Application
Understanding how to fit sequences within the phrase model is essential for both analysis and composition. Many longer phrases (6, 8, or more measures) result from inserted sequences.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 516): A four-measure phrase with tonic expansion (m. 1), D2 sequence overlapping tonic and PD (m. 2), and cadence (mm. 3-4).
