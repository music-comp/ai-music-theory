# Quick Definition
Chordal dissonance occurs when a dissonant interval (the seventh) is incorporated as a member of a chord; like melodic dissonance, it creates tension requiring resolution -- the seventh must resolve down by step.

# Core Definition
**Chordal dissonance** occurs when a dissonant interval (specifically a seventh) becomes a structural member of a chord rather than a passing embellishment. Just as dissonant intervals resolve to consonant ones, dissonant chords are active sonorities that seek resolution. The seventh of V7 must resolve down by step. The approach to the seventh (its **preparation**) can occur by step (preferred), common tone, or leap (avoid), but the departure (its **resolution**) must always be by descending step (Laitz, Ch. 9, pp. 217-219).

# Prerequisites
- **Consonance and dissonance** -- chordal dissonance extends intervallic dissonance
- **V7** -- the primary example of chordal dissonance

# Key Properties
1. The seventh is a dissonant member of the chord
2. Preparation: by step (preferred) or common tone
3. Resolution: always by step DOWN
4. Creates tension and heightens expectation
5. V to V7 is not reversible (seventh intensifies)
