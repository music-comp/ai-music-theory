# Quick Definition
A passacaglia is a continuous variation form based on both a repeating bass line and a repeating harmonic pattern, combining features of the ground bass and chaconne.

# Core Definition
A passacaglia combines a repeating bass pattern (ground bass) with a repeating harmonic pattern (chaconne). In practice, the distinctions among ground bass, chaconne, and passacaglia are often blurred, as composers regularly use both devices together (Laitz, Ch. 26, p. 598).

# Prerequisites
- **Continuous Variations** -- the form in which the passacaglia operates

# Key Properties
1. Combines repeating bass line with repeating harmonic pattern
2. Distinction from chaconne and ground bass is often artificial
3. Found across multiple style periods

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 601): Brahms, Symphony no. 4, Allegro energico e passionato -- combines ground and chaconne in the finale, with both harmonic and bass patterns providing structure.
