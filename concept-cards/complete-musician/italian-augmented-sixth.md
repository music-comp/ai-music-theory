# Quick Definition
The Italian augmented sixth chord (It6) is the simplest variety, containing only three distinct pitch classes: b6 in the bass, #4 in an upper voice, and doubled scale-degree 1.

# Core Definition
The Italian augmented sixth chord (labeled It6) is the simplest type, containing b6 in the bass, #4 in an upper voice (often soprano), and scale-degree 1 doubled. Since it has only three distinct pitch classes, always double the third above the bass (scale-degree 1). The It6 moves directly to V without passing through a cadential 6/4. It is enharmonically equivalent to an incomplete V7 (without fifth) (Laitz, Ch. 30, p. 681).

# Key Properties
1. Three distinct pitch classes: b6, 1 (doubled), #4
2. Simplest of the three varieties
3. Moves directly to V (no cadential 6/4 needed)
4. Sounds thinnest due to only three pitch classes
5. Enharmonically equivalent to incomplete V7

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Place b6 in the bass
2. Place #4 in an upper voice
3. Double scale-degree 1

## To Recognize:
1. The augmented sixth interval with only three distinct pitches
2. Direct resolution to V (no passing 6/4)
3. Thinner sound than German or French
