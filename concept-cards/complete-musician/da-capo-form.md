# Quick Definition
Da capo form is a ternary structure (ABA) realized by a marking at the end of the score instructing the performer to return to the beginning and play to the fermata sign, creating the final balancing A section.

# Core Definition
Da capo form creates an ABA structure through the performance instruction "Da Capo (al fine)" -- literally "from the head to the fermata sign." The return to the beginning creates the final A section; the da capo instruction saves space and ink. Although da capo form is the primary form of the Baroque aria, it can be found in almost all genres, style periods, and instrumental combinations of the common-practice era (Laitz, Ch. 31).

# Prerequisites
- **Ternary Form** -- da capo form is a specific realization of ternary structure

# Key Properties
1. ABA structure created through performance instruction, not written-out repetition
2. "Da Capo" marking directs return to the beginning
3. "Fine" or fermata marks the end of the repeated A section
4. Often features compound ternary structure with nested binary forms

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Look for "Da Capo" or "D.C." marking at the end of the score
2. Look for "Fine" or fermata marking within the score
3. The material between the beginning and Fine constitutes A
4. The material between A and the D.C. marking constitutes B

# Context & Application
Da capo form is the primary form of the Baroque aria and is found in instrumental works across all common-practice periods. It fell out of favor in the nineteenth century, replaced by through-composed and hybrid forms.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 710): Haydn, Divertimento in G major, Hob. XVI.11, Presto -- A da capo form with nested rounded binary forms in both A and B sections, creating a compound ternary.

**Example 2** (p. 712): Bach, "Ich will Dir mein Herze schenken," St. Matthew Passion -- A da capo aria with ritornello punctuation and sectional ternary structure.
