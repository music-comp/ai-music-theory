# Quick Definition
The French augmented sixth chord (Fr43) contains an augmented fourth above the bass (scale-degree 2), creating two tritones and a distinctive major-second clash in the inner voices.

# Core Definition
The French augmented sixth chord (Fr43) contains scale-degree 2 (an augmented fourth above the bass) instead of the doubled scale-degree 1 or b3 found in the Italian and German varieties. The figured bass is 4/3. Its two tritones create an exotic, distinctive sound with a major-second clash in the inner pitches. Scale-degree 2 is held as a common tone with V, where it becomes the chordal fifth. The French augmented sixth resolves directly to V (no cadential 6/4 needed) (Laitz, Ch. 30, p. 681).

# Key Properties
1. Four distinct pitch classes: b6, 1, 2, #4
2. Contains two tritones (b6-2 and 1-#4)
3. Easiest to identify aurally due to distinctive sound
4. Scale-degree 2 is a common tone with V
5. Resolves directly to V (no cadential 6/4 needed)
6. The figured bass is 4/3

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Place b6 in the bass
2. Add scale-degree 1, 2, and #4

## To Recognize:
1. Two tritones creating an exotic sound
2. Major-second clash between inner voices
3. Direct resolution to V with 2 as common tone
