# Quick Definition
The chromatic A2 (-M3/+P4) sequence ascends by half step with all-major structural chords, derived from the diatonic ascending 5-6 motion, with each second chord transformed into an applied dominant.

# Core Definition
Like the diatonic A2 (-3/+4) sequence, the chromatic forms derive from the contrapuntal 5-6 motion. In the fully chromatic variant A2 (-M3/+P4), the structural first chord of every repetition is major, and the sequence ascends by half step. The bass is sustained through each two-chord repetition, with every second chord transformed into an applied 6/5 chord (shown by enharmonic respelling of the bass as a leading tone). A variant sustains two common tones, creating augmented triads on the second chord. The sequence commonly appears with root-position applied chords in popular music (Laitz, Ch. 36).

# Key Properties
1. Derived from 5-6 contrapuntal motion
2. All structural chords are major
3. Ascends by half step (minor second)
4. Second chord of each pair is an applied dominant
5. Bass sustained through each two-chord pair
6. Variant creates augmented triads
