# Quick Definition
Accidentals are symbols that modify pitch: sharp (#) raises a half step, flat (b) lowers a half step, natural cancels a previous accidental, double sharp (x) raises a whole step, and double flat (bb) lowers a whole step.

# Core Definition
**Accidentals** (or **chromatic alterations**) are five symbols that modify pitch: the **sharp** (#) raises a pitch one half step; the **flat** (b) lowers a pitch one half step; the **natural** cancels a previous accidental; the **double sharp** (x) raises a pitch two half steps; the **double flat** (bb) lowers a pitch two half steps. An accidental applies only in the octave register in which it occurs and remains in effect until the end of the measure.

# Prerequisites
- **Half Step and Whole Step** -- accidentals raise or lower by these increments

# Key Properties
1. Seven natural pitch names (A-G) plus accidentals name all twelve keys within each octave
2. Accidentals carry through the measure for that specific pitch and octave
3. Accidentals do not carry across bar lines (unlike key signature accidentals)
4. A composer must restate an accidental in subsequent measures to continue it

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Sharp (#): raises one half step
2. Flat (b): lowers one half step
3. Natural: cancels any previous accidental or key signature alteration
4. Double sharp (x): raises two half steps total
5. Double flat (bb): lowers two half steps total

# Context & Application
Accidentals create chromaticism, indicate leading tones in minor keys, and specify non-diatonic pitches. Correct choice of accidental (vs. enharmonic equivalent) communicates harmonic function.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Forgetting that accidentals carry through the measure.
  **Correction**: An F# on beat 1 makes all subsequent Fs in that measure and octave also sharp unless a natural sign cancels it.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing accidentals with key signature alterations.
  **Clarification**: Key signature accidentals persist until the key changes; written accidentals apply only within the current measure.
