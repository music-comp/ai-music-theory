# Quick Definition
The recapitulation restates the exposition's material with the crucial change that both the FTA and STA now appear in the tonic key, fulfilling the sonata principle.

# Core Definition
The recapitulation repeats many events of the exposition but contains crucial changes: the most important is that the STA and closing section return in the tonic, fulfilling the sonata principle. Transitions are often altered since they no longer need to modulate. Composers may also compress thematic material from the FTA, introduce tonicizations using modal mixture, or reverse the order of themes (Laitz, Ch. 33).

# Prerequisites
- **Sonata Form Exposition** -- the material being restated
- **Sonata Principle** -- the requirement that STA returns in tonic

# Key Properties
1. FTA, STA, and Cl all in the tonic key
2. Transition often altered (no longer modulatory)
3. May compress or reorder material
4. May introduce modal mixture
5. Fulfills the sonata principle

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 773): Haydn, Sonata in C -- Recapitulation begins at m. 104 with FTA an octave lower; m. 111 introduces modal mixture (parallel minor); transition compressed to 15 measures.
