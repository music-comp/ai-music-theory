# Quick Definition
Keyboard style places three upper voices (soprano, alto, tenor) in the right hand within one octave and the bass in the left hand; chorale style places soprano and alto in treble clef, tenor and bass in bass clef.

# Core Definition
**Keyboard style** is a four-voice texture with three notes (soprano, alto, tenor) played in the right hand within the space of one octave, and one note (bass) played in the left hand. **Chorale style** distributes soprano and alto in the treble clef, tenor and bass in the bass clef. Both use the standard four voices: soprano (highest), alto, tenor, and bass (lowest). These are the primary formats for writing and analyzing harmony.

# Prerequisites
- **Triads** -- these styles present triads in four voices
- **Voicing and Doubling** -- both styles require a doubled note in triads

# Key Properties
1. Four voices: soprano, alto, tenor, bass (SATB)
2. Keyboard style: right hand = 3 voices within one octave; left hand = bass
3. Chorale style: treble clef = soprano + alto; bass clef = tenor + bass
4. Both emphasize outer voices (soprano and bass)
