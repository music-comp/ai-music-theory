# Quick Definition
A progression in which the bass roots arpeggiate a triad (e.g., ^1-^6-^4 or ^1-^3-^5), creating a bridge between harmonic functions through third-related root motion.

# Core Definition
A harmonic arpeggiation occurs when the roots of successive chords trace out a triad. The descending version ^1-^6-^4 (I-vi-IV) and the ascending version ^1-^3-^5 (I-iii-V) are the most common. A distinction exists between a **harmonic arpeggiation** (roots actually spell a triad) and a **melodic bass arpeggiation** (the bass line arpeggiates a triad, which may differ from chord roots due to inversions). For example, I-vi-ii6 creates a melodic bass arpeggiation ^1-^6-^4 where the ii6 root is ^2 not ^4. (Laitz, Ch. 18, pp. 425-428)

# Prerequisites
- **Descending-thirds progression** — The most common harmonic arpeggiation

# Key Properties
1. Bass roots trace a triadic outline
2. Can be ascending or descending
3. Harmonic arpeggiation = roots spell a triad
4. Melodic bass arpeggiation = bass notes spell a triad (roots may differ due to inversions)
