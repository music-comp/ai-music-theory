# Quick Definition
Pre-dominant chords (IV and ii) are independent harmonic sonorities that precede and intensify the motion to the dominant, providing the connective tissue between tonic and dominant.

# Core Definition
**Pre-dominant** chords are the third and final harmonic function in tonal music, following tonic and dominant. Unlike contrapuntal chords (which are subordinate embellishments), pre-dominant chords are independent sonorities that provide the connective tissue between the tonal skeleton of tonic and dominant. The most important pre-dominant chords are **IV** (subdominant) and **ii** (supertonic). They usually precede the dominant. The T-PD-D-T order is **not reversible** -- pre-dominants must precede, not follow, the dominant. At the second level of analysis, label pre-dominants as PD (Laitz, Ch. 12, pp. 281-283).

# Prerequisites
- **Tonic and dominant** -- PD connects these two functions

# Key Properties
1. Third harmonic function (after tonic and dominant)
2. Usually IV or ii (ii6 in minor: iio6)
3. Precedes the dominant (T-PD-D-T is irreversible)
4. Independent sonority (not a contrapuntal embellishment)
5. Intensifies the drive toward the dominant
6. In second-level analysis, labeled PD

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Locate the dominant (structural V at a cadence)
2. Look for IV or ii immediately preceding it
3. Verify the chord provides genuine motion toward V (not just contrapuntal embellishment)

# Context & Application
Pre-dominants add a new dimension to harmonic tension. While I-V-I is sufficient for coherent music, inserting PD between T and D creates richer, more satisfying progressions.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 282): Meyerbeer uses IV between I and V; Foster's "Beautiful Dreamer" uses ii6; Verdi's La forza del destino overture uses ii -- all intensifying the drive to dominant.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Placing PD after the dominant (D-PD)
  **Correction**: T-PD-D-T is not reversible; PD must precede D
