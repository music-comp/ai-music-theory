# Quick Definition
Laitz's analysis of Scriabin's Prelude, op. 39, no. 2 in D major demonstrates how a symmetrical opening sonority (F#-A#-C-E) functions in multiple keys through altered dominant, French sixth, and FrV4/3 interpretations, and how the deep structure maintains a traditional I-IV-bVII-bII-V-I framework beneath extreme surface chromaticism.

# Core Definition
This 1903 prelude, midway between Scriabin's tonal and atonal periods, postpones an authentic cadence in D major until its final two measures. The opening sonority F#-A#-C-E is symmetrical (a rectangle on the chromatic clock), with all intervals belonging to one whole-tone track. It can function as: altered V7 in B, French sixth in Bb, FrV4/3 in F, and analogous functions a tritone away. The form features literal transposition of mm. 1-12 down a major second in mm. 13-24, followed by a four-measure cadence. The underlying progression descends by step (D-G-C | F-Bb | Eb-A-D), revealing a traditional diatonic plan (I-PT-bVII | bVI | bII-V-I) beneath the chromatic surface. The bass notes in mm. 1-13 project the opening sonority (F#-A#-C-E), demonstrating motivic parallelism at a dissonant, structural level (Laitz, Ch. 37).
