# Quick Definition
The tritone is the interval of three whole steps (six half steps), occurring as either an augmented fourth (A4) or a diminished fifth (d5) -- the only inversional pair with the same number of half steps.

# Core Definition
The **augmented fourth** (A4) contains three whole steps, hence the name **tritone**. Its inversion, the **diminished fifth** (d5), also contains six half steps, making it the only inversional pair whose number of half steps remains the same after inversion. The tritone is highly dissonant and historically unstable, playing a central role in harmonic resolution.

# Prerequisites
- **Augmented and Diminished Intervals** -- the tritone is an A4 or d5
- **Interval Inversion** -- the tritone is its own inversional equivalent in half steps

# Key Properties
1. Exactly six half steps (three whole steps)
2. A4 and d5 both contain six half steps but have different generic sizes
3. In major: A4 occurs between ^4 and ^7; d5 between ^7 and ^4
4. Highly dissonant -- creates strong pull toward resolution
5. Divides the octave exactly in half

# Context & Application
The tritone is central to dominant function in tonal music. The resolution of the tritone between ^4 and ^7 (resolving to ^3 and ^1) is the fundamental driver of V-I motion.
