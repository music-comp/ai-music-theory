# Quick Definition
In a continuous variation set, the theme is relatively short and gives the effect of being incomplete, permitting each variation to flow seamlessly into the next, often through overlapping cadences.

# Core Definition
In a continuous variation set, the theme is relatively short (usually a phrase) and gives the effect of being incomplete to permit each variation to flow seamlessly into the next. This is often accomplished by an overlapping process whereby the ending tonic of one variation simultaneously acts as the beginning of the next. Continuous variations are usually constructed through a repeated idea called an ostinato -- either a ground bass, a chaconne (repeating harmonic pattern), or a passacaglia (repeating bass and harmonic pattern) (Laitz, Ch. 26, pp. 598-601).

# Prerequisites
- **Variation Set** -- the overarching concept

# Key Properties
1. Theme is short, often a single phrase
2. Variations flow seamlessly into each other (no clear breaks)
3. Overlapping cadences create continuity
4. Built on ostinato patterns (ground bass, chaconne, or passacaglia)
5. Changes in register, texture, and motivic design occur over the ostinato

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 598): Monteverdi, "Lamento della ninfa" -- repeating bass pattern forms the foundation for vocal variations.

**Example 2** (p. 600): Bach, Chaconne from Partita no. 2 in D minor -- repeating harmonic ostinato beginning in midmeasure, with overlapping variations.
