# Quick Definition
The IV-ii complex is a voice-leading technique where IV shifts to ii6 (via 5-6 motion above the bass) to avoid parallel fifths when moving to V; both chords are grouped under a single PD label.

# Core Definition
The **IV-ii complex** is a voice-leading device that extends the pre-dominant function. When moving from IV to V with parallel outer-voice motion, parallel fifths are likely. The solution is to shift the upper note of the fifth by step (creating a 5-6 contrapuntal motion above the bass), which produces ii6. This gives the impression of two chords working seamlessly to extend PD, but the motion is motivated by voice leading rather than functional chord change. The goal-oriented motion IV-ii(6) is common; the weaker ii-IV is not. In second-level analysis, IV and ii6 are grouped under a single PD bracket (Laitz, Ch. 12, pp. 291-293).

# Prerequisites
- **Subdominant** and **supertonic** -- the two chords in the complex

# Key Properties
1. IV shifts to ii6 via 5-6 voice-leading motion
2. Avoids parallel fifths between IV and V
3. Both grouped under single PD label in second-level analysis
4. IV-ii(6) is directional; ii-IV is weak and uncommon
5. Also called "helping chords" or "voice-leading chords"

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 293): Mozart's "Haffner" Symphony: IV moves to V using the 5-6 shift to create ii6, avoiding the parallel fifths that would result from direct IV-V motion.
