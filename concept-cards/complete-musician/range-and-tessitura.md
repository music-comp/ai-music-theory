# Quick Definition
Range is the total span of pitches a voice can produce (~a twelfth); tessitura is the comfortable optimal range (~a sixth or seventh for upper voices); ambitus is the specific range of a particular melody.

# Core Definition
**Range** covers the total span of pitches a voice can sing (roughly a twelfth). **Tessitura** is the more comfortable, narrower register within which a voice spends most of its time; for upper voices, approximately a sixth or seventh. **Ambitus** refers to the specific range of a given melody, as opposed to the instrument's total range. When writing for four voices, observe tessitura spans. The bass's register is usually an octave below the alto, and the tenor an octave below the soprano (Laitz, Ch. 8, pp. 192-193).

# Prerequisites
- **SATB texture** -- the voices whose ranges matter

# Key Properties
1. Write for a voice's midrange (tessitura), not extremes
2. Restrict upper-voice tessitura to about a sixth or seventh
3. Bass has the largest range, reflecting its dual melodic/harmonic role
4. Soprano leaps are fewer and smaller than bass leaps
5. Alto and tenor lines contain even fewer skips than soprano
