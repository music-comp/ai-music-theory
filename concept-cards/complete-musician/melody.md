# Quick Definition
A melody is a succession of pitches forming a coherent, singable musical line, typically moving by step with occasional leaps, shaped as an arch with a single climax, and ending with a stepwise approach to the tonic.

# Core Definition
A **melody** is a single-line succession of pitches that forms a coherent musical statement. Well-crafted tonal melodies share these characteristics: they are mostly diatonic and move primarily by steps (conjunct motion) with occasional skips and leaps (disjunct motion). They begin on a member of the tonic triad (^1, ^3, or ^5) and end with a stepwise approach to ^1 (the **melodic cadence**: ^2-^1 or ^7-^1). They have a logical shape, usually an arch with a single **melodic climax** near the middle.

# Prerequisites
- **Interval** -- melodies are sequences of intervals
- **Diatonic Scale** -- melodies are primarily diatonic

# Key Properties
1. Begin on ^1, ^3, or ^5; end with ^2-^1 or ^7-^1
2. Move primarily by step; leaps followed by change of direction (law of recovery)
3. Range limited to about a tenth; tessitura about a sixth
4. Consonant leaps only; avoid dissonant leaps (e.g., A2 in harmonic minor)
5. Single melodic climax, usually near the middle, approached and left by step
6. Leading tone (^7) should resolve up to ^1 (except in descending ^1-^7-^6-^5)
7. Avoid excessive repetition and sequences

# Construction / Recognition
## To Write:
1. Begin on ^1, ^3, or ^5
2. Move mostly by step with occasional consonant skips/leaps
3. Follow leaps with change of direction
4. Create an arch shape with one climax
5. End with ^2-^1 or ^7-^1
