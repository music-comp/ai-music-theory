# Quick Definition
A triad is a chord of three distinct pitches stacked in thirds, identified by its root (the lowest pitch when stacked), third, and fifth.

# Core Definition
A **chord** is the combination of three or more different pitches, the basic unit of harmonic organization. Chords comprising three distinct pitches stacked in thirds are called **triads**. When stacked in thirds, the chord is in **root position**: the lowest pitch is the **root**, the note a third above is the **third**, and the note a fifth above the root is the **fifth**. Chords of four pitches stacked in thirds are called **seventh chords** (Chapter 6).

# Prerequisites
- **Consonant and Dissonant Intervals** -- triads are classified by their intervallic content
- **Specific Intervals** -- thirds and fifths define triad quality

# Key Properties
1. Three distinct pitch classes stacked in thirds
2. Identified by root, third, and fifth
3. Four types: major (M3+m3), minor (m3+M3), diminished (m3+m3), augmented (M3+M3)
4. Major and minor triads are consonant (spanning a P5); diminished and augmented are dissonant
5. Only major, minor, and diminished triads are used as independent harmonies in common-practice music
6. The augmented triad results from contrapuntal convergence, not independent harmonic function

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Choose a root
2. Add a third above (M3 for major/augmented; m3 for minor/diminished)
3. Add a fifth above the root (P5 for major/minor; d5 for diminished; A5 for augmented)

## To Recognize:
1. Stack the pitches in thirds
2. Identify the root (lowest pitch when stacked)
3. Determine the quality of the third and fifth above the root
