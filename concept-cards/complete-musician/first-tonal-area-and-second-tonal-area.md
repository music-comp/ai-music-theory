# Quick Definition
The first tonal area (FTA) presents material in the tonic key; the second tonal area (STA) presents material in a contrasting key (usually V in major, III in minor).

# Core Definition
In the exposition, the FTA presents material in the tonic key while the STA presents material -- usually with a new theme -- in a contrasting key. In major-mode works, the STA is typically in V; in minor-mode works, in III. The FTA and STA may contain similar or contrasting thematic material and may contain multiple themes. Themes are labeled with numbers indicating tonal area and letters indicating order: 1a, 1b (FTA themes); 2a, 2b (STA themes). In the recapitulation, both FTA and STA return in the tonic (Laitz, Ch. 33).

# Prerequisites
- **Sonata Form** -- the formal context

# Key Properties
1. FTA in tonic key; STA in contrasting key
2. Typical key for STA: V (major), III (minor)
3. May contain one or multiple themes each
4. Theme labeling: 1a, 1b (FTA); 2a, 2b (STA)
5. In recapitulation, STA returns in tonic

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. FTA begins at the start of the exposition in the tonic
2. A transition connects FTA to STA
3. STA begins when the new key is established (often after a medial caesura)
4. Look for the key, not just the theme -- FTA and STA are defined by tonality

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 754): Beethoven, op. 49, no. 1 -- FTA in G minor (i), STA in Bb major (III) starting at m. 16.

**Example 2** (p. 766): Haydn, Sonata in C major -- FTA (mm. 1-19) in I, STA (mm. 36-45) in V.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Defining FTA and STA by theme alone
  **Correction**: They are defined by key. A monothematic sonata has different tonal areas but the same thematic material.
