# Quick Definition
Second-species (2:1) counterpoint places two notes in the contrapuntal voice against each note of the cantus firmus, introducing oblique motion and the controlled use of dissonance through unaccented passing tones.

# Core Definition
In **second-species counterpoint** (2:1), two pitches are written against each CF note, creating a metric hierarchy of strong beats (where both voices move) and weak beats (where only the counterpoint moves). Strong beats must be consonant. Weak beats may be consonant or dissonant, but the only permitted dissonance is the **unaccented passing tone** -- a stepwise note filling the space of a third between two consonant strong beats.

# Key Properties
### Rules:
1. Strong beats must be consonant
2. Avoid parallel perfect intervals between successive strong beats and between weak-to-strong beats
3. Avoid direct motion to perfect intervals from weak to strong beats
4. The only dissonance permitted is the weak-beat passing tone (fills a melodic third by step)
5. Begin on ^1 (below CF) or ^1, ^3, ^5 (above CF); may begin with a half rest
6. In minor, use lowered ^6/^7 within; raise ^7 at penultimate measure

### Guidelines:
1. Incorporate as many dissonant passing tones as possible
2. Use chordal skips to balance passing tones
3. Place leaps within, rather than between, measures

# Context & Application
Second species introduces the crucial concept of controlled dissonance. The passing tone is the first type of nonchord tone studied and is fundamental to all tonal music.
