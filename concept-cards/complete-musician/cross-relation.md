# Quick Definition
A cross relation occurs when a chromatic tone is prepared not in the same voice but in a different voice, producing a harsh aural effect due to the juxtaposition of diatonic and chromatic forms of a pitch across voices.

# Core Definition
When a chromatic tone is prepared by another voice instead of the same voice, the result is a cross relation. This produces a harsh aural effect due to awkward leaps. Cross relations should be avoided between the aurally prominent bass and soprano; instead, they should be placed between inner voices or between one outer voice and an inner voice (Laitz, Ch. 24, p. 542).

# Prerequisites
- **Voice Leading for Applied Chords** -- context in which cross relations arise

# Key Properties
1. Results from chromatic and diatonic forms of a pitch appearing simultaneously or consecutively in different voices
2. Most problematic between bass and soprano
3. Acceptable between inner voices
4. Sometimes unavoidable, especially with applied chords in inversion

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Look for the diatonic form of a pitch in one voice and its chromatic alteration in another
2. Check whether the two forms appear in adjacent chords

# Context & Application
Cross relations are most commonly encountered when writing applied chords, where a temporary leading tone in one voice conflicts with the diatonic form of that pitch in another voice. In chromaticized bass descents and chromatic voice exchanges, intervening passing chords can diminish the harshness of unavoidable cross relations.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 542): In C major, a progression from I to V6/vi places G# in the tenor while G-natural was in the soprano, creating a cross relation.

# Relationships
## Related
- **Chromatic Voice Exchange** -- a special type of cross relation where chromatic tones are exchanged between voices with intervening passing chords

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Placing cross relations between bass and soprano
  **Correction**: Keep cross relations between inner voices or between an inner voice and one outer voice
