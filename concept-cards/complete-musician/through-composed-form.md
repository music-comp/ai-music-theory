# Quick Definition
Through-composed form is a song structure in which new music is composed for each stanza of text, allowing the music to follow the dramatic arc of the poetry without repetition.

# Core Definition
Through-composed form (German: durchkomponiert) uses new music for each stanza, enabling the composer to respond directly to the changing narrative and emotional content of the text. This form sacrifices the unity of strophic repetition for maximum dramatic flexibility and text-painting. Schubert's ballads and dramatic songs frequently employ through-composed form (Laitz, Ch. 28, p. 649).

# Prerequisites
- **German Lied** -- the genre context

# Key Properties
1. New music for each stanza
2. Maximum dramatic flexibility
3. Follows the narrative arc of the poetry
4. No melodic or harmonic repetition between stanzas
5. Allows extensive use of chromatic modulation and text-painting

# Relationships
## Contrasts With
