# Quick Definition
The half-diminished seventh chord (viio7) is the leading-tone seventh in major keys; it has a minor seventh between ^7 and ^6, making it less dissonant and less common than the fully diminished viio7.

# Core Definition
The **half-diminished seventh chord** (viio7) is the leading-tone seventh chord in major keys (e.g., B-D-F-A in C major). The minor seventh between ^7 and ^6 is much less dissonant than the diminished seventh in the fully diminished chord. Because of its less goal-oriented tendencies, viio7 occurs much less often than viio7 in minor. It appears most commonly in root position as a neighboring chord to expand tonic. Composers often import the fully diminished seventh from minor into major-mode works by borrowing b^6 from the parallel minor (Laitz, Ch. 11, pp. 264-265).

# Prerequisites
- **viio7** -- the fully diminished counterpart in minor

# Key Properties
1. Built on ^7 in major: ^7-^2-^4-^6 (natural ^6, not lowered)
2. Contains a minor seventh (less dissonant than diminished seventh)
3. Less common than fully diminished viio7
4. Usually in root position
5. Follow same voice-leading rules as viio7
6. Composers often prefer borrowing fully diminished viio7 from parallel minor
