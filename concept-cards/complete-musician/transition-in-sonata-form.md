# Quick Definition
The transition (Tr) in sonata form is a passage that modulates from the FTA to the STA, ending on the new tonic or the new dominant. It may be dependent (reusing FTA material) or independent (using new material).

# Core Definition
Two types of transitions exist: a dependent transition (DTr) begins with a restatement of the initial theme from the FTA; an independent transition (ITr) uses new thematic material. Both modulate to the STA and end either on the new tonic or the new dominant. In the recapitulation, the transition is often altered since it now must remain in (or return to) the tonic rather than modulate. In major-key sonatas, accidentals typically mark the beginning of the transition; in minor-key sonatas, the removal of raised leading tones signals the move to III (Laitz, Ch. 33).

# Prerequisites
- **Sonata Form Exposition** -- the transition operates within the exposition

# Key Properties
1. Modulates from FTA key to STA key
2. Dependent (DTr): reuses FTA thematic material
3. Independent (ITr): introduces new material
4. Ends on the new tonic or new dominant
5. Often altered in the recapitulation
6. Accidentals in major signal the transition; removal of accidentals in minor

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 755): Beethoven, op. 49, no. 1 -- DTr (mm. 9-15) begins identically to FTA but alters to modulate to III.

**Example 2** (p. 771): Haydn, Sonata in C -- ITr (mm. 20-35) introduces new material with stepwise ascents.
