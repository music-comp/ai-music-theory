# Quick Definition
First-inversion triads (6/3 chords) place the chordal third in the bass; they are less stable than root position, creating motion and allowing the bass to become melodic.

# Core Definition
**First-inversion triads** (notated with figured bass 6/3 or simply 6) place the chordal third in the bass. They are less stable than root-position triads because they lack the grounding of the root in the lowest voice, but this instability is an asset: it pushes music forward and renders the bass line more melodic. First-inversion triads are used primarily within phrases for expansion rather than at beginnings or endings, where root-position stability is preferred. Moving between root position and first inversion extends a single harmony (I to I6 or V to V6) through chordal leaps (Laitz, Ch. 10, pp. 226-229).

# Prerequisites
- **Triads** -- the chords being inverted
- **Contrapuntal expansion** -- first-inversion triads are tools for expansion

# Key Properties
1. Third of the chord in the bass
2. Less stable than root position -- creates forward motion
3. Used within phrases, not at beginnings or endings (usually)
4. I-I6 or V-V6 = chordal leap expansion (no harmonic change)
5. Voice leading: keep common tones, double the root (or ^1, ^4, ^5, ^2)
6. Never double ^7 (the bass of V6)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Identify the bass note and determine if it is the third of the chord
2. Check figured bass: 6 or 6/3 indicates first inversion

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 228-229): Brahms restricts harmonies to I, I6, V, V6 but the inclusion of first inversions transforms a static harmonization into a buoyant, directed passage.

# Relationships
## Enables
- **Neighboring chord** -- V6 neighbors I
- **Passing chord** -- viio6 passes between I and I6

## Contrasts With
- **Cadential six-four chord** -- second inversion functions very differently

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Doubling the bass of V6 (which is ^7, the leading tone)
  **Correction**: Never double the leading tone; double another chord member
