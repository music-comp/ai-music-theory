# Quick Definition
A period in which the consequent phrase closes in a different key from the antecedent, typically moving to the dominant key (V) or relative major (III).

# Core Definition
A progressive period has a consequent phrase that modulates, closing in a different key from the antecedent. The antecedent typically ends with a HC or IAC in the home key; the consequent modulates to a closely related key (usually V in major or III in minor) and closes with a PAC there. Progressive periods are common at the beginnings of binary forms. (Laitz, Ch. 20, pp. 472-473)

# Key Properties
1. Consequent modulates to a new key
2. Usually moves to V (major) or III (minor)
3. Common at openings of binary forms
4. Can be parallel or contrasting

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 472): Schubert, "Standchen" from Schwanengesang -- PPP where the second phrase closes in III.
