# Quick Definition
The expanded Neapolitan is a prolongation of bII through chordal leaps, tonicization (using V/bII = bVI), sequential treatment, or transposition of thematic material up a half step.

# Core Definition
Although the Neapolitan often occurs as a single bII6 chord, it can be prolonged within a phrase through several techniques: (1) chordal leap in the bass (bII6 to bII); (2) transposing a subphrase up a half step to bII; (3) tonicizing bII with its own dominant (V/bII, which is the bVI or VI chord); (4) extended tonicizations with multiple chords functioning in the temporary key of bII. No matter how extended, the Neapolitan almost always functions as a pre-dominant within the overall progression (Laitz, Ch. 29, pp. 667-670).

# Prerequisites
- **Neapolitan Chord** -- the chord being expanded

# Key Properties
1. Chordal leap: bII6 to root-position bII
2. Thematic transposition: subphrase moved up a half step to bII
3. Tonicization: V/bII (= bVI in minor, bVI in major) precedes bII
4. Extended tonicization: multiple chords in the temporary key of bII
5. Pre-dominant function is maintained regardless of expansion length
6. Can participate in A2 (-3/+4) sequences

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 668): Beethoven, String Quartet op. 59 no. 2 -- the opening tune is restated a half step higher, creating a root-position Neapolitan.

**Example 2** (p. 669): Chopin, Nocturne in B minor, op. 9 no. 1 -- bII tonicized through V65/bII, with enharmonic spelling (D major rather than Eb major).
