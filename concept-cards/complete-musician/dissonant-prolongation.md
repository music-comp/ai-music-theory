# Quick Definition
Dissonant prolongation extends a dissonant harmony (typically V7) through contrary motion in the outer voices, spanning intervals from a third to a tritone or more.

# Core Definition
It is possible to extend not only consonant triads but also dissonant harmonies -- most often a dominant seventh chord -- by contrary motion. The dissonant prolongation spans varying intervals: a third, a tritone, or even an entire octave (in the case of the omnibus). The inner voices move minimally or remain stationary while the outer voices diverge chromatically (Laitz, Ch. 36).

# Prerequisites

# Key Properties
1. Extends a dissonant harmony (usually V7)
2. Outer voices move in contrary motion
3. Inner voices remain stationary
4. Can span various intervals from a third to an octave
5. Creates chromatic passing chords between structural harmonies
