# Quick Definition
Because bVI and the German augmented sixth share three of four pitch classes, composers regularly convert bVI into a Ger65 to enable smooth voice leading to V, solving the parallel fifths that would occur from direct bVI-V motion.

# Core Definition
Leading bVI directly to V results in voice-leading problems (octaves and fifths). Since bVI and Ger65 share three of four scale degrees, composers regularly convert bVI into a German augmented sixth by substituting #4 for one of the shared pitches. This allows bVI's dramatic half-step bass motion (b6-5) to resolve properly to V. A tonicized bVI can also be destabilized by conversion to Ger65 (Laitz, Ch. 30, pp. 685-686).

- **Augmented Sixth Chord** -- the chord created by conversion

# Key Properties
1. bVI and Ger65 share three pitch classes
2. Only one pitch substitution needed (#4 for one existing pitch)
3. Solves parallel-fifths problem in bVI-V motion
4. Tonicized bVI can be converted to Ger65 for smooth return to V

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 686): A tonicized bVI (mm. 10-12) is converted to Ger65 (m. 13), which moves smoothly to V (m. 14).
