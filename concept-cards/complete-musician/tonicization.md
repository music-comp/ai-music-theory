# Quick Definition
Tonicization is the sensation of briefly experiencing a key other than the tonic, accomplished through the use of applied chords.

# Core Definition
Tonicization is the process by which a diatonic chord is momentarily treated as a tonic through the use of applied dominant or applied leading-tone chords. The essential feature is that it stretches out a diatonic harmony: the tonicized chord's functional role in the key (T, PD, or D) remains unchanged, but the area is expanded by the addition of applied chords. Tonicizations usually occur within phrases, do not disrupt the feeling of the home key, lack strong cadences in new keys, and are fleeting (Laitz, Ch. 24-25).

# Prerequisites
- **Applied Chord** -- the mechanism through which tonicization is achieved

# Key Properties
1. Brief -- typically lasts a few beats to a measure or two
2. Does not establish a new key with a cadence
3. The tonicized harmony retains its original function in the home key
4. Can involve a single applied chord or multiple applied chords (extended tonicization)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Select a target diatonic chord
2. Precede it with its applied dominant (V/X) or applied leading-tone chord (vii°/X)

## To Recognize:
1. Identify chromaticism that points toward a non-tonic chord
2. Verify the passage lacks a strong cadence in the temporary key
3. The home key reasserts itself quickly

# Context & Application
Tonicization adds harmonic color and intensifies motion to structural harmonies without the commitment of modulation. It is one of two tonal processes (along with modal mixture) accounting for chromaticism in tonal music.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 538): In D minor, the A-major chord (V/ii) creates a momentary tonicization of ii, expanding the pre-dominant area.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Applied Chord** -- tonicization is achieved through applied chords

## Enables
- **Extended Tonicization** -- tonicizations can be prolonged across several measures
- **Modulation** -- longer tonicizations may become modulations

## Contrasts With
- **Modulation** -- modulation includes a strong cadence in the new key and the new key continues after the cadence

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Labeling every chromaticism as a modulation
  **Correction**: Tonicizations are fleeting and lack cadential confirmation in the temporary key

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Conflating tonicization with modulation
  **Clarification**: Tonicization occurs within a phrase without disrupting the home key; modulation gives the feeling that a new key has usurped the home key
