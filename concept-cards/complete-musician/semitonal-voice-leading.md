# Quick Definition
Semitonal voice leading connects distantly related chords or tonal areas by moving two or more voices by a half step while usually keeping one common tone, creating tonal ambiguity through smooth chromatic connection.

# Core Definition
Nineteenth-century composers often connected distantly related chords or tonal areas with semitonal voice leading, which transforms one chord chromatically into the next. Two or more voices move by half step while usually keeping one common tone in another voice. This creates smooth connections between harmonies that are tonally distant, suspending tonal function so that the motion is more a chordal succession than a functional progression (Laitz, Ch. 34).

# Prerequisites
- **Tonal Ambiguity** -- the aesthetic context

# Key Properties
1. Two or more voices move by half step
2. Usually one common tone retained
3. Connects distantly related chords smoothly
4. Creates tonal ambiguity -- no sense of T, PD, or D
5. Motion is chordal succession rather than functional progression
6. Often combined with enharmonic reinterpretation

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 793): Mozart, "Dissonant" Quartet, K. 465, Adagio -- Sinuous chromatic voice leading clouds the tonic of C major in the slow introduction.

**Example 2** (p. 794): Root-related by major thirds: C major - Ab minor - E major - C major, connected by consistent half-step motion with common tones.
