# Quick Definition
Structural analysis uses specialized notation to show the relative importance of pitches: stems and beams for structural notes, noteheads only for subordinate notes, and slurs connecting embellishments to their structural tones.

# Core Definition
**Structural analysis** is a notational and analytical technique that reflects the relative importance of pitches. The most important notes receive stems and beams; less important notes consist only of noteheads. Slurs connect subordinate pitches to their structural tones (either embellishments returning to a chord tone, or members of a chord connecting to other members). This notation reveals underlying melodic trajectories that may be hidden beneath varied surfaces (Laitz, Ch. 7, pp. 168-170).

# Prerequisites
- **Hierarchy in tonal music** -- structural analysis reflects hierarchical relationships
- **Melodic fluency** -- reveals underlying stepwise lines

# Key Properties
1. Stemmed notes = structurally important pitches
2. Unstemmed noteheads = subordinate pitches
3. Slurs show dependency relationships
4. Beams connect structural pitches across time
5. Reveals similarities between superficially different melodies

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 168-170): Structural analyses of "Clementine" and "God Save the King" reveal that both share the same underlying structure despite different surfaces.
