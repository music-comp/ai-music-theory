# Quick Definition
Relative major and minor keys share the same key signature but have different tonics; the relative minor tonic lies three half steps below the relative major tonic.

# Core Definition
Major and minor keys with the same key signatures are called **relative** keys. The relative major key lies three half steps (skipping one letter name) above the tonic of the minor key. Conversely, the relative minor lies three half steps below the major tonic. Relative keys share all seven pitch classes (in the natural minor form) but have different tonal centers.

# Prerequisites
- **Key Signatures** -- relative keys share a key signature
- **Major Scale** -- the major key provides the reference
- **Minor Scales** -- the natural minor shares the same pitches as its relative major

# Key Properties
1. Relative keys share the same key signature and the same set of seven pitch classes
2. The relative minor tonic is ^6 of the relative major key
3. The relative major tonic is ^3 of the relative minor key
4. Key signatures differ by zero accidentals between relative keys

# Construction / Recognition
## To Find Relative Minor:
1. From the major tonic, descend three half steps (or ascend to ^6)
2. The resulting pitch is the relative minor tonic

## To Find Relative Major:
1. From the minor tonic, ascend three half steps (or ascend to ^3)
2. The resulting pitch is the relative major tonic

# Context & Application
Composers frequently move between relative keys because they share pitch content, making the transition smooth. Relative key relationships are among the most common in modulation.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 20): Beethoven, Piano Sonata in D major, op. 28, Andante (Example 1.19A) illustrates the relative relationship between D minor and F major.
**Example 2** (p. 20): Beethoven, Piano Sonata in D major, op. 10, no. 3 (Example 1.19B) shows movement from D major to B minor (relative minor).

# Relationships
## Contrasts With
- **Parallel Keys** -- share the same tonic but different key signatures (differ by three accidentals)

## Related
- **Circle of Fifths** -- relative keys occupy the same position on the circle

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing relative and parallel keys.
  **Correction**: Relative keys share a key signature (e.g., C major and A minor). Parallel keys share a tonic (e.g., C major and C minor).
