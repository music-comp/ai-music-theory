# Quick Definition
Homophonic texture features a clear melody accompanied by supporting voices, ranging from simple block chords (chorale texture) to elaborate broken-chord patterns (Alberti bass, arpeggiation).

# Core Definition
**Homophonic texture** is a cross between monophonic and polyphonic textures: there is usually a clear melody accompanied by additional voices. It is the most widespread texture type in common-practice music. Varieties include: **chorale texture** (block chords rhythmically aligned with the melody), **Alberti bass** (broken-chord accompaniment), arpeggiated patterns, and other figured accompaniments. In homophonic textures, single harmonies are often spread out over time, with chordal members distributed over one or more beats.

# Prerequisites
- **Musical Texture** -- homophonic is the most common category

# Key Properties
1. Clear distinction between melody (usually in the highest voice) and accompaniment
2. The most varied and widespread of the three texture types
3. Chorale texture: simplest form (rhythmically aligned block chords)
4. Alberti bass: broken-chord pattern common in Classical style (e.g., Mozart K. 545)
5. Chord members may be distributed across registers, instruments, and clefs
6. The rate of harmonic change is called **harmonic rhythm**

# Context & Application
Analyzing homophonic texture requires grouping chord members quickly across voices. The ability to scan music and identify patterns, repetitions, and harmonic outlines is crucial.

# Examples
