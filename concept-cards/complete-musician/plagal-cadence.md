# Quick Definition
A plagal cadence (IV-I, also called the "amen cadence") is much weaker than an authentic cadence; it usually follows an AC and extends the tonic through double upper-neighbor motion.

# Core Definition
The **plagal cadence** (PC) is a IV-I motion, often called the "amen cadence" because of its frequent appearance in church music. It is much weaker than the authentic cadence because: (1) IV-I has only descending-fourth root motion (vs. descending-fifth in V-I); (2) it lacks the melodic resolution of ^7 to ^1; (3) it typically has only a static common tone (^1) above. The plagal cadence is often a cadence in name only, since a strong authentic cadence usually precedes it. IV in this context extends the tonic through double upper-neighbor motion rather than functioning as a true pre-dominant (Laitz, Ch. 14, pp. 342-343).
