# Quick Definition
Syncopation occurs when rhythmic accents are placed on normally weak beats or between beats, contradicting the expected metric pattern and creating a sense of rhythmic tension.

# Core Definition
**Syncopation** is a metrical disturbance in which an accent is placed on a beat or beat subdivision that is normally weak, or in which emphasis falls between beats. The resulting contradiction between the expected metric pattern and the actual rhythmic accent creates a characteristic sense of tension, energy, and forward drive.

# Prerequisites
- **Meter** -- syncopation is defined relative to the metric framework
- **Accent Types** -- syncopation displaces expected accents

# Key Properties
1. Syncopation contradicts the prevailing meter without changing it
2. Created by ties across bar lines, accents on weak beats, or rests on strong beats
3. The listener's sense of the underlying meter is maintained despite the displacement
4. Syncopation is essential in jazz, ragtime, and many popular styles
