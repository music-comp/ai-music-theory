# Quick Definition
The digression is the portion of the second section of a binary form that features fragmented, unstable, and often sequential material, wandering from the preceding material before leading back to a recapitulation.

# Core Definition
In the second section of a rounded binary form, the digression begins after the double bar with fragmented and unstable material that wanders from the opening theme. It typically features sequential progressions, motivic development, and tonal instability. The digression leads to a half cadence (interruption), after which the opening material returns in the tonic (recapitulation). The digression is the precursor to the development section in sonata form (Laitz, Ch. 26, p. 589).

# Key Properties
1. Begins the second section after the double bar
2. Features fragmented, unstable material
3. Often sequential
4. Leads to a half cadence (interruption)
5. May develop motives from the opening material
6. Precursor to sonata-form development section
