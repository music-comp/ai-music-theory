# Quick Definition
A variation set begins with an initial theme from which a series of variations unfolds, balancing unity (retaining characteristic elements) with variety (altering figuration, harmony, texture, or mode).

# Core Definition
Variation sets usually begin with an initial idea, or theme, from which a series of variations unfolds. They may be independent pieces or members of larger multimovement works. The two common types are continuous variations and sectional variations. Composers balance unity with variety by retaining one or more characteristic elements while altering others. Form and general proportions tend to remain unchanged across variations (Laitz, Ch. 26, pp. 597-598).

# Prerequisites
- **Binary Form** -- variation themes are often in binary form

# Key Properties
1. Theme followed by series of variations
2. Balance between unity and variety is the central compositional challenge
3. Form and proportions of the theme generally preserved across variations
4. Two main strategies: rhythmic crescendo (ever-smaller note values) and grouping of variations
5. Can be continuous or sectional

# Context & Application
Variation sets are found across all instrumental combinations and historical periods. They test a composer's ability to sustain interest through repeated material while developing it in creative ways.
