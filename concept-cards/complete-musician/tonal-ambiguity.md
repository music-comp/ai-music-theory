# Quick Definition
Tonal ambiguity is the deliberate obscuring of the location of the tonic through techniques such as the plagal relation, semitonal voice leading, enharmonic reinterpretation, and mixture, becoming an important aesthetic in nineteenth-century music.

# Core Definition
Tonal ambiguity is the deliberate obscuring of the location of the tonic, an important aesthetic in nineteenth-century music. It arises from the rise of the plagal relation, semitonal voice leading, enharmonic reinterpretation of diminished seventh and augmented sixth chords, off-tonic beginnings, and double tonality. The listener loses tonal grounding because of conflicting tonal implications that confuse the three harmonic functions (T, PD, and D). Tonal ambiguity is not a flaw but a deliberate compositional goal (Laitz, Ch. 34).

# Context & Application
Tonal ambiguity is a central feature of nineteenth-century music, from early instances in Mozart and Beethoven to the extreme examples in Wagner, Liszt, and Scriabin. It reflects broader Romantic aesthetic values of mystery, emotion, and transcendence.
