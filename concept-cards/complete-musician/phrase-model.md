# Quick Definition
The phrase model T-PD-D-T describes the harmonic motion guiding a phrase from beginning to cadence: tonic is prolonged for most of the phrase, then pre-dominant and dominant lead to cadential tonic (or dominant at a HC).

# Core Definition
The **phrase model** is the harmonic motion T-PD-D-T (Tonic-Pre-Dominant-Dominant-Tonic) that guides a phrase from beginning to cadence. Four common four-measure models exist: (1) T-PD-D-T (PAC), (2) T--PD/D-T (PAC, compressed PD/D), (3) T--PD-D (HC), (4) T---PD/D (HC). In all models, tonic occupies significantly more time than PD or D combined. It is common for dominant to occupy two or three times more time than pre-dominant. The T-PD-D-T model generates unlimited compositional possibilities across all style periods (Laitz, Ch. 12, pp. 293-296).

# Prerequisites
- **Pre-dominant function** -- PD is part of the phrase model
- **Cadence types** -- the phrase model ends with a cadence

# Key Properties
1. T-PD-D-T is the standard four-function progression
2. Tonic prolongation occupies the majority of the phrase
3. PD and D occur together as a cadential gesture
4. Model 1-2: close with AC (T returns); Model 3-4: close with HC (end on D)
5. The T-PD-D-T order is not reversible
6. Four measures (or multiples) is common but not mandatory
7. Can occur within tonic prolongation at a smaller structural level

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 294): Haydn's String Quartet op. 50/6, Menuetto: a balanced four-measure phrase with harmonic rhythm shifting once per measure through T-PD-D-T.

**Example 2** (p. 295): Mozart's Piano Sonata K. 331: tonic extended for 3.3 measures, PD and D occupying barely one beat.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Placing PD after D
  **Correction**: T-PD-D-T is irreversible; PD must always precede D
