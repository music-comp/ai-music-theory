# Quick Definition
Laitz's analysis of Chopin's Prelude in A minor, op. 28, no. 2 demonstrates how a three-note intervallic cell (fourth + third, containing a second) controls melody, ostinato, bass, and deep structure across an entire piece that is harmonically ambiguous between E minor and A minor.

# Core Definition
The prelude begins in E minor but ends in A minor, raising questions of double tonality, progressive tonality, or off-tonic design. A three-note melodic cell (E-B-D in mm. 3-6: a falling fourth and rising third, containing a second) functions as the generative principle. The cell appears in the melody, is transposed down a second (D-A-B), appears in the left-hand ostinato (B-A#-B-G, invoking the "Dies irae"), controls the bass line (E-D-G), and at the deepest level structures the entire piece (E-F#-A). The cell's intervallic content -- a fourth, a third, and a second -- remains constant even as specific intervals and contours change. The large-scale key relationships (E minor/G major - B minor/D major - A minor) mirror the overall 24-prelude tonal plan (Laitz, Ch. 37).
