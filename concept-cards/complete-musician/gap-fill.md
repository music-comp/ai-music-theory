# Quick Definition
Gap-fill is a Gestalt principle: listeners expect a melodic leap to be followed by stepwise motion in the opposite direction that fills in the skipped interval.

# Core Definition
**Gap-fill** is a Gestalt principle predicated on the listener's expectation that a melodic leap will eventually be filled in by stepwise motion in the opposite direction. This principle of changing direction after a leap is an important means of creating melodic balance. It is a crucial component of successful composition (Laitz, Ch. 7, p. 174).

# Prerequisites
- **Melodic fluency** -- gap-fill is one mechanism creating fluent lines

# Key Properties
1. A melodic leap opens a "gap" in pitch space
2. Listeners expect stepwise motion in the opposite direction to fill that gap
3. The filling may be immediate or delayed
4. Creates melodic balance and contour variety

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 174): In Donizetti's Lucrezia Borgia, a chordal leap of a fourth (B to E) in m. 1 is filled in as the melody retraces itself by stepwise motion.

**Example 2** (p. 175): Chopin's nocturnes consistently open with leaps that are immediately filled by stepwise contrary motion.
