# Quick Definition
A period with contrasting melodic openings and a continuous harmonic structure where the antecedent ends on a non-tonic, non-dominant harmony (such as ii), and the consequent ends with PAC. Abbreviated CCP.

# Core Definition
A contrasting continuous period has (1) **contrasting** melodic openings and (2) **continuous** harmonic structure -- the antecedent ends on a harmony other than V or I (e.g., on ii, IV, or vi), and the consequent continues the harmonic motion to PAC. The continuous structure creates forward momentum rather than the "restart" feeling of interrupted periods. (Laitz, Ch. 20, pp. 470-471)

# Key Properties
1. Contrasting melodic openings
2. Antecedent ends on non-tonic, non-dominant harmony
3. Continuous harmonic motion from antecedent through consequent
4. Consequent ends with PAC

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 470): Beethoven, Piano Sonata no. 11 in B-flat major, op. 22 -- CCP with antecedent ending on ii.
