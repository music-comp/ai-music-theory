# Quick Definition
Symmetrically constructed harmonies are chords that partition the octave into identical intervals, such as the diminished seventh chord (minor thirds) and the augmented triad (major thirds), making their inversions aurally indistinguishable and enabling enharmonic modulation.

# Core Definition
Chords that partition the octave into identical intervals are symmetrically constructed harmonies. The diminished seventh chord divides the octave into four minor thirds; its inversions produce no new intervals (unlike the major triad, whose inversions produce six different intervals). The augmented triad divides the octave into three major thirds. Because of their symmetrical structure, these chords are inherently ambiguous and can function in multiple keys through enharmonic reinterpretation. There are only three distinct diminished seventh chords and four distinct augmented triads (Laitz, Ch. 34).

# Prerequisites
- **Diminished Seventh Chord** -- the most common symmetrical harmony

# Key Properties
1. Partition the octave into identical intervals
2. Inversions produce no new interval types
3. Inherently ambiguous -- no single "root" is aurally apparent
4. Enable enharmonic modulation to distant keys
5. Limited number of distinct chords (3 dim7 chords, 4 aug triads)

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 802): The diminished seventh B-D-F-Ab: in any inversion, only minor thirds (or their enharmonic equivalents) result. Four different spellings access four different keys.
