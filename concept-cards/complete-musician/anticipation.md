# Quick Definition
An anticipation is an unaccented nonchord tone that sounds a member of an upcoming chord prematurely, before the chord itself arrives -- the "mirror opposite" of the suspension.

# Core Definition
The **anticipation** (ANT) is an unaccented nonchord tone that appears before the chord to which it belongs actually sounds, creating a dissonance with the still-sounding chord. It is the "mirror opposite" of the suspension: whereas a suspension delays a chord tone's arrival, an anticipation accelerates it. Anticipations are most effective at cadences, where the final chord is strongly expected. Double anticipations (two voices anticipating simultaneously) are also common (Laitz, Ch. 13, p. 320).

# Prerequisites
- **Tones of figuration** -- anticipation is a specific type

# Key Properties
1. Unaccented (occurs on weak beat)
2. Premature entrance of a chord tone from the upcoming chord
3. Creates dissonance with the still-sounding harmony
4. Most effective at cadences
5. Mirror opposite of suspension

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 320): In Corelli's Violin Sonata op. 1, a double anticipation at a phrygian cadence: C# and A (members of V) enter prematurely while iv6 is still sounding.
