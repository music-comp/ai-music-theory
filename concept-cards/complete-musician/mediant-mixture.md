# Quick Definition
The bIII chord is a major triad built on the lowered third scale degree, a product of harmonic mixture that borrows b3 and b7 to create a consonant triad, functioning as a mediant bridge between tonic and pre-dominant.

# Core Definition
Like bVI, bIII arises from harmonic mixture by lowering the root (b3) and also borrowing b7 to create a consonant major triad. bIII functions as a mediant chord: it divides the fifth between I and V into two smaller thirds, serves as a bridge between T and PD, participates in descending-fifths motion, and can be preceded by its own dominant (V/bIII). It also substitutes for I6, though less commonly than diatonic iii (Laitz, Ch. 27, pp. 622-623).

# Prerequisites
- **Harmonic Mixture** -- the type of mixture producing bIII

# Key Properties
1. Major triad on b3 (requires both b3 and b7)
2. Divides the fifth I-V into two thirds (I-bIII-V)
3. Bridges tonic and pre-dominant functions
4. Can be preceded by V/bIII
5. Can lead to V43
