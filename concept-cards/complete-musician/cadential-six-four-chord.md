# Quick Definition
The cadential six-four chord is a root-position V whose chordal fifth and third are temporarily postponed by a dissonant sixth and fourth above the bass (^5); the 6/4 resolves to 5/3 as a double suspension or accented passing tone.

# Core Definition
The **cadential six-four chord** (cad 6/4) is the only type of accented six-four chord. It is NOT a second-inversion tonic chord; rather, it is a root-position dominant harmony (bass on ^5) whose chordal fifth and third are temporarily postponed by two nonchord tones: a dissonant sixth and fourth above the bass. These resolve by step down to the fifth and third (6/4 to 5/3). The cad 6/4 evolved from two dissonant events: the double suspension (preparation by common tone from I or IV) and the accented passing tone (preparation by step from ii(6)). It always occurs on metrically accented beats to highlight its dissonance. It can participate in both half cadences and authentic cadences, and may lead to V or V7 (Laitz, Ch. 14, pp. 330-336).

# Prerequisites
- **Suspension** -- cad 6/4 evolved from double suspensions
- **Accented passing tone** -- cad 6/4 evolved from APTs
- **Pre-dominant function** -- PD usually precedes cad 6/4

# Key Properties
1. Bass = ^5 (root of dominant)
2. Sixth and fourth above bass are nonchord tones that resolve to 5/3
3. Always on accented beats (beat 1 or 3 in 4/4; beat 1 or 2 in 3/4)
4. Resolution on weaker beat
5. Preceded by PD (ii, ii6, IV) or tonic (preparation by common tone)
6. NOT preceded by another dominant-function chord (weakens impact)
7. Double the bass (^5) in the chord
8. Can resolve to V (HC) or V-I (AC) or V7-I (AC)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Place ^5 in the bass on an accented beat
2. Add ^1 and ^3 above as the sixth and fourth (plus doubled ^5)
3. Resolve: ^3 to ^2 (sixth to fifth), ^1 to ^7 (fourth to third)
4. Precede with PD or I

## To Recognize:
1. Look for a chord on ^5 on an accented beat
2. The interval of a sixth and fourth above the bass
3. Resolution to 5/3 (or 7/5/3 for V7)

# Context & Application
The cadential six-four is one of the most powerful devices for intensifying cadences in Classical-period music. It draws attention through its accented dissonance and creates a dramatic "held breath" before the dominant resolves.

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 333-334): Mozart's Horn Concerto K. 447: first phrase closes with cad 6/4 at HC (m. 4), second phrase with cad 6/4 at PAC (m. 8).

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Suspension** -- evolved from double suspension
- **Accented passing tone** -- evolved from APT filling the gap ^2-^7 or ^4-^2

## Enables
- **Evaded cadence** -- adding ^4 in the bass (V4/2) instead of resolving to 5/3

## Contrasts With
- **Unaccented six-four chords** -- pedal, passing, arpeggiating are all unaccented

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Labeling cad 6/4 as a "I6/4" tonic chord
  **Correction**: It is a dominant chord with nonchord tones; the bass ^5 is the root of V

- **Error**: Placing cad 6/4 on a weak beat
  **Correction**: Cad 6/4 must occur on an accented beat
