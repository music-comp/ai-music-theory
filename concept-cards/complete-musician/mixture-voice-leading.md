# Quick Definition
Voice leading for mixture harmonies follows three guidelines: avoid doubling chromatically altered tones (except the root of bVI), prepare and resolve b6 by step, and continue using mixture once introduced until reaching the dominant.

# Core Definition
Three voice-leading guidelines for mixture: (1) Avoid doubling a chromatically altered tone unless it is the root of the chord (as in bVI). (2) Since b6 is either a neighbor (5-b6-5) or a descending passing tone (6-b6-5), prepare and resolve it by step; keep the chromatic line in a single voice to avoid cross relations. (3) Once modal mixture is introduced, continue its use until reaching the dominant function, because b3 and b6 possess powerful drives to scale degrees 2 and 5 that diatonic forms would disrupt (Laitz, Ch. 27, pp. 623-624).

# Key Properties
1. Do not double chromatically altered tones (exception: root of bVI)
2. b6 resolves by step (down to 5 or as neighbor back to 5)
3. Keep chromatic motion in one voice to avoid cross relations
4. Once mixture starts, continue until the dominant
5. Interrupting mixture with diatonic forms creates jarring cross relations
