# Quick Definition
A cadence in which either the dominant or the tonic (or both) are inverted, creating a weaker close than a root-position V-I authentic cadence.

# Core Definition
Contrapuntal cadences close phrases using inverted dominant and/or tonic harmonies. Composers use them to save the powerful root-position V-I until a more dramatic and final-sounding cadence is required. Both V and vii^o6 participate in contrapuntal authentic motions. For example, I-IV6-V6/5-I or I-vii^o6-I can close a phrase contrapuntally. (Laitz, Ch. 17, p. 414)

# Prerequisites
- **Cadence types** — Must understand structural (root-position) cadences
- **Embedded phrase model** — Contrapuntal cadences use similar harmonies to EPMs but at phrase endings

# Key Properties
1. Either V or I (or both) are inverted
2. Weaker than root-position authentic cadence
3. Used to defer powerful closure to a later point
4. vii^o6 can substitute for V in contrapuntal cadences
5. The four-measure phrase may still sound like a phrase despite the weak cadence

# Context & Application
Contrapuntal cadences are common at internal phrase endings in periods, where a strong cadence is reserved for the consequent phrase. They also appear in passages where the composer wants to maintain forward momentum.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 414): Schubert, "Die Krahe" from Winterreise -- vii^o6-i closes the first vocal phrase. The excerpt has phrase characteristics (melodic descent to ^1, clear beginning and end) but the weak cadence is debatable as true phrase closure.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **Cadence types** — Contrapuntal cadences are a weaker variant of authentic cadences

## Contrasts With
- **Cadential six-four chord** — The cadential 6/4 strengthens the cadence; contrapuntal cadences weaken it

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: A passage ending with a contrapuntal cadence cannot be a phrase
  **Clarification**: Whether a musical unit with a contrapuntal cadence constitutes a phrase is often a matter of interpretation
