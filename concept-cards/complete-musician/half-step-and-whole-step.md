# Quick Definition
A half step is the smallest interval in Western music (adjacent keys on the keyboard); a whole step equals two half steps (keys separated by one intervening key).

# Core Definition
The **half step** is the smallest standard interval in Western music, occurring between any two adjacent keys on the keyboard (e.g., E-F, B-C, C-C#). The **whole step** is defined as the distance of two half steps, occurring between keys separated by one intervening key (e.g., C-D, A-B). The octave is divided into 12 half steps. Scales are built from specific patterns of half and whole steps.

# Prerequisites
- **Pitch and Pitch Class** -- understanding pitch names and keyboard layout

# Key Properties
1. On the keyboard, half steps occur naturally between E-F and B-C (no black key between them)
2. All other adjacent white keys are a whole step apart (with a black key between)
3. Half steps and whole steps are the building blocks of all Western scales
4. Abbreviations H and W are used in scale construction

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. On a keyboard: adjacent keys (including black keys) = half step
2. Keys with one key between them = whole step
3. In notation: consider whether an intervening pitch exists

# Context & Application
Half and whole steps are the fundamental building blocks for constructing scales and understanding intervallic relationships.
