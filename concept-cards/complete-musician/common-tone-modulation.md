# Quick Definition
A common-tone modulation links two nondiatonic keys lying a third apart through a single shared pitch that is reinterpreted in the context of the new key, without a traditional pivot chord.

# Core Definition
In a chromatic common-tone modulation, two nondiatonic keys lying a third above or below one another are linked by a single pitch common to both keys. There is typically no hint of the upcoming modulation; the common tone is heard in retrospect as belonging to both keys (e.g., D is heard as 1 in D major and reinterpreted as 3 in Bb major). This technique lacks a traditional pivot chord -- the connection is purely through the shared pitch (Laitz, Ch. 28, pp. 647-648).

# Key Properties
1. A single pitch connects two non-diatonically related keys
2. No traditional pivot chord
3. Usually links keys a third apart
4. The common tone is reinterpreted in the new key
5. Often dramatic and unprepared

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 647): Schubert, String Quartet No. 3 -- D major juxtaposed with Bb major (bVI); the pitch D is common to both keys (1 in D, 3 in Bb).
