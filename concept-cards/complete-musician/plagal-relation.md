# Quick Definition
The plagal relation is a nineteenth-century technique in which harmonies such as iv and ii-half-dim-6/5 substitute for the dominant, moving directly to the tonic and weakening the dominant function.

# Core Definition
The free interchange of major and minor modes through modal mixture compressed 24 major and minor keys to 12 "major-minor" keys, allowing easy access to chromatically related keys. This weakened the dominant function. As a result, the final structural cadence of pieces incorporated harmonies such as iv and ii-half-dim-6/5 -- chords that substitute for the dominant -- moving directly to the tonic in a technique called the plagal relation. Paradoxically, this caused the dominant to become more important as a signpost for tonic location (Laitz, Ch. 34).

# Key Properties
1. Pre-dominant harmonies move directly to tonic, bypassing dominant
2. Weakens dominant function
3. Uses iv, ii-half-dim-6/5 as dominant substitutes
4. Contributes to tonal ambiguity
5. Paradoxically increases the signaling importance of the dominant
