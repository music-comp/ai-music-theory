# Quick Definition
Seventh chords have three inversions beyond root position, labeled by figured bass: root position (7), first inversion (6/5), second inversion (4/3), and third inversion (4/2 or just 2).

# Core Definition
Seventh chords often appear in inversion. With four different pitches, there are three inversions beyond root position: **first inversion** (third in bass, figured bass: 6/5), **second inversion** (fifth in bass, figured bass: 4/3), and **third inversion** (seventh in bass, figured bass: 4/2 or just 2). The numbers decrease systematically: 7, 6/5, 4/3, 2.

# Key Properties
1. Root position: 7 (root in bass)
2. First inversion: 6/5 (third in bass)
3. Second inversion: 4/3 (fifth in bass)
4. Third inversion: 4/2 or 2 (seventh in bass)
5. Memory aid: numbers decrease steadily: 7-6-5-4-3-2
6. To find the root: look for the seventh (or its inversion, the second); the root is the lower note of the seventh or the upper note of the second
7. Inverted seventh chords almost always use complete harmonies (no doubled pitches)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Determine Root:
1. Find the interval of a seventh or a second among the pitches
2. The lower note of the seventh (or upper note of the second) is the root
3. Stack in thirds from the root to confirm
