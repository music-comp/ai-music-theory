# Quick Definition
Five seventh chord types exist, named by triad quality + seventh quality: major-major (MM7), major-minor (Mm7/dominant 7th), minor-minor (mm7), diminished-minor (half-diminished), and diminished-diminished (fully diminished).

# Core Definition
Five important types of seventh chord, named by triad quality and seventh quality:
1. **Major-major seventh** (MM7): major triad + major seventh -- strident sound
2. **Major-minor seventh** (Mm7): major triad + minor seventh -- the most common; called the **dominant seventh**
3. **Minor-minor seventh** (mm7): minor triad + minor seventh -- soft, gentle sound
4. **Diminished-minor seventh** (dm7 or half-diminished, symbol: o-with-slash-7): diminished triad + minor seventh -- associated with anguish in Romantic music
5. **Diminished-diminished seventh** (dd7 or fully diminished, symbol: deg7): diminished triad + diminished seventh -- the most dissonant; all minor thirds and diminished fifths

# Key Properties
1. The Mm7 (dominant seventh) is by far the most common in tonal music
2. The MM7 is more strident than Mm7; common in jazz
3. The mm7 is the least dissonant seventh chord
4. The dd7 is the most dissonant; contains three minor thirds and two diminished fifths
5. The dd7 divides the octave into four equal parts (all minor thirds)
6. Roman numeral conventions: Mm and MM = uppercase; mm, dm, dd = lowercase; dd gets deg sign; dm gets slashed-deg sign
