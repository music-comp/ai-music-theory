# Quick Definition
A technique in which the end of one phrase or section shares motivic material with the beginning of the next, creating a seamless connection between formal units.

# Core Definition
Linkage occurs when the concluding material of one phrase overlaps with or directly generates the opening material of the next phrase. The shared motivic element acts as a hinge, connecting formal sections that might otherwise feel disconnected. This technique provides continuity and forward momentum across phrase boundaries. (Laitz, Ch. 16, p. 378)

# Prerequisites
- **Motive** — Must recognize the shared motivic material

# Key Properties
1. End of one section shares material with beginning of next
2. Creates continuity across phrase boundaries
3. Can involve exact or modified repetition of the shared material
4. Strengthens the listener's sense of organic flow

# Context & Application
Linkage is common in developmental passages and in transitions between formal sections. It prevents the music from feeling episodic or disjointed.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 378): The final gesture of phrase 1 becomes the opening gesture of phrase 2, creating a seamless connection.
