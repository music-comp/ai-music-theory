# Quick Definition
IV6 serves two contrapuntal functions: (1) it splits the large bass leap ^1-^3 into ^1-^6-^3 during tonic expansion by arpeggiation, and (2) it passes between V and V6 during dominant expansion.

# Core Definition
**IV6** has two distinct contrapuntal functions: (1) In **tonic expansion**, it splits the large bass leap from ^1 down to ^3 (I to I6) into a more graceful arpeggiation ^1-^6-^3, with the soprano typically ascending ^3-^4-^5. (2) In **dominant expansion**, IV6 passes between V and V6 (bass ^5-^6-^7), much as viio6 passes between I and I6. In minor keys, ^6 must be raised to avoid an augmented second between ^6 and ^7, resulting in a major IV6 chord. Any member may be doubled, with ^1 (the third above the bass) being common (Laitz, Ch. 10, pp. 236-238).

# Key Properties
1. Tonic expansion: bass ^1-^6-^3 (arpeggiation splitting I-I6)
2. Dominant expansion: bass ^5-^6-^7 (passing V-IV6-V6)
3. In minor: raise ^6 to avoid A2 between ^6 and ^7
4. Soprano often ^3-^4-^5 during tonic expansion (ascending)
5. Common doubling: ^1 (third above bass)
