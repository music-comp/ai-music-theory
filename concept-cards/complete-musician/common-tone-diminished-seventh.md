# Quick Definition
A common-tone diminished seventh chord (c.t. dim 7) is a contrapuntal diminished seventh chord that shares a common tone with the chord it resolves to, functioning as a neighbor or passing chord that prolongs I or V.

# Core Definition
Contrapuntal diminished seventh chords that sustain the root of the harmony they extend are called common-tone diminished seventh chords, labeled c.t. dim 7. They arise from lower neighbor and passing motions combined with 5-6 motion. Two functions exist for diminished seventh chords: tonicizing (vii-dim-7 or applied vii-dim-7) and contrapuntal (c.t. dim 7 or applied c.t. dim 7). Key distinction: common-tone diminished seventh chords share a common tone with the chord of resolution; vii-dim-7 chords have no common tones with their resolution. Beware of vii-dim-7/V to cad 6/4, where common tones are misleading suspensions (Laitz, Ch. 35).

# Prerequisites
- **Diminished Seventh Chord** -- the harmonic structure being used contrapuntally

# Key Properties
1. Shares a common tone with the chord of resolution
2. Functions as neighboring or passing chord
3. Prolongs I or V
4. Distinguished from applied vii-dim-7 by the presence of a common tone
5. Labeled c.t. dim 7

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Check if the diminished seventh chord shares a common tone with the next chord
2. If common tone exists: c.t. dim 7 (contrapuntal)
3. If no common tone: vii-dim-7 (tonicizing)
4. Exception: vii-dim-7/V to cad 6/4 shares tones that are suspensions, not true common tones

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 822): Schubert, Waltz in D major, D. 779 -- Lower neighbors and 5-6 motion create a c.t. dim 7 over a sustained tonic root D.
