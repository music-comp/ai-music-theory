# Quick Definition
The doubly augmented sixth chord (Swiss sixth) is a notational variant of the German augmented sixth used in major keys, where b3 is respelled as #2 to visually show the chromatic ascent to scale-degree 3 in the cadential 6/4.

# Core Definition
When a German augmented sixth occurs in the major mode and leads to a cadential 6/4 with a major sixth, composers often notate the chromatic ascent from b3 to natural 3 using the enharmonic equivalent #2, which visually leads more effectively upward. The resulting doubly augmented fourth above the bass creates an awkward-looking chord referred to as the doubly augmented sixth chord or Swiss augmented sixth. The sound is identical to the German augmented sixth (Laitz, Ch. 30, p. 682).

# Key Properties
1. Notational variant of the German augmented sixth in major keys
2. Uses #2 instead of b3 for visual clarity
3. Contains a doubly augmented fourth above the bass
4. Sounds identical to Ger65
5. Purely a notational convention
