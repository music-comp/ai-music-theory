# Quick Definition
Cadences are points of arrival at phrase endings; the two main types are authentic cadences (V-I) and half cadences (ending on V), with authentic cadences graded by strength into PAC and IAC.

# Core Definition
A **cadence** is a point of arrival usually occurring at the end of a phrase, where melody, harmony, counterpoint, rhythm, and meter all participate. The two structural cadence types are: (1) **Authentic cadences** (AC), closing V-I with both chords in root position, subdivided into **perfect authentic cadences** (PAC, soprano ^2-^1 or ^7-^8 with bass ^5-^1) and **imperfect authentic cadences** (IAC, soprano on ^3 or ^5); (2) **Half cadences** (HC), closing on a root-position dominant. A special IAC called a **contrapuntal cadence** occurs when the bass moves by step (^7-^1 or ^2-^1) rather than by the usual ^5-^1 leap (Laitz, Ch. 8, pp. 188-190).

# Prerequisites
- **Tonic and dominant as tonal pillars** -- cadences are defined by I-V relationships

# Key Properties
1. PAC: soprano ^2-^1 or ^7-^8, bass ^5-^1 (strongest closure)
2. IAC: soprano on ^3 or ^5, bass ^5-^1 (weaker closure)
3. Contrapuntal cadence: bass ^7-^1 or ^2-^1 (weakest authentic)
4. HC: phrase ends on root-position V (open, questioning effect)
5. In minor: ^7 must be raised for the leading tone; Picardy third optional at final tonic

# Construction / Recognition
## To Recognize:
1. Identify the final two harmonies and their bass motion
2. Check soprano scale degree at the point of arrival
3. V-I with soprano on ^1 = PAC; with ^3 or ^5 = IAC
4. Ending on V = HC

# Context & Application
Cadences define phrases and articulate musical form. PACs provide definitive closure; IACs provide partial closure; HCs create expectations for continuation. The choice of cadence type has immediate performance implications.

# Examples
**Example 1** (pp. 188-189): Mozart's Rondo K. 144 -- first phrase closes on V (HC in m. 4), second phrase closes V-I (PAC in m. 8).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing IAC and PAC based on the bass alone
  **Correction**: The soprano determines the distinction -- PAC requires ^2-^1 or ^7-^1 in the soprano

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: A half cadence is defined by what precedes V
  **Clarification**: Only the ending on root-position V matters; what precedes it does not change the HC classification
