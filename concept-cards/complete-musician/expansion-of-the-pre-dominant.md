# Quick Definition
Techniques for prolonging the pre-dominant function through passing chords, voice exchanges, and the IV-ii complex, just as tonic and dominant can be expanded contrapuntally.

# Core Definition
The pre-dominant can be expanded using the same contrapuntal techniques applied to tonic and dominant: (1) a passing chord between ii and ii6 (using I6 as passing chord); (2) a passing chord between IV and IV6 (using i6/4 as passing chord); (3) a passing chord moving from IV6 to ii6/5 (the IV-ii complex with passing 6/4); (4) restating tonic material up a step to expand ii. In all cases, the "tonic" chord within a PD expansion is subordinate -- a **passing tonic** -- not a structural tonic. (Laitz, Ch. 17, pp. 414-418)

# Prerequisites
- **Pre-dominant function** — Must understand PD as a harmonic function
- **Contrapuntal expansion** — Same techniques applied to tonic are now applied to PD

# Key Properties
1. I6 can be a passing chord between ii and ii6 (but ii6 cannot expand tonic)
2. A passing 6/4 chord often connects IV to ii within the PD expansion
3. Voice exchanges are common
4. The passing tonic within PD expansion does not function as structural tonic
5. Restating opening material up a step is a powerful way to expand ii

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Establish the PD with ii or IV
2. Connect inversions through passing chords (I6, i6/4)
3. Use voice exchanges for smooth bass-soprano counterpoint
4. Resolve the expanded PD to V

## To Recognize:
1. Look for I6 or i6/4 chords on weak beats within a PD context
2. Check for voice exchanges between ii and ii6
3. Identify material restated up a step from the tonic area

# Context & Application
PD expansion creates richer, longer phrases and balances the typically longer tonic expansion at the phrase's beginning. It is particularly important in longer phrases and periods.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 415): I6 passes between ii6 and ii in a voice exchange, with three voices moving by step and one holding a common tone.

**Example 2** (p. 416): Bach, St. Matthew Passion -- expanded iv with a passing i6/4 chord on a weak beat between iv6 and iv.

**Example 3** (p. 418): Mozart, Piano Sonata in D major, K. 576 -- opening tonic material restated up a step in mm. 5-6 creates an expanded ii.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon

## Related

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Interpreting the passing I6 within PD expansion as a return to structural tonic
  **Correction**: The I6 is a passing chord subordinate to the PD; it does not restore tonic function
