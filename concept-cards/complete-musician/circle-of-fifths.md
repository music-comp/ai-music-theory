# Quick Definition
The circle of fifths is a diagram arranging all major and minor keys by ascending fifths (clockwise, adding sharps) or descending fifths (counterclockwise, adding flats), showing systematic key relationships.

# Core Definition
The **circle of fifths** organizes keys so that each successive key clockwise is a perfect fifth higher (adding one sharp), and each successive key counterclockwise is a perfect fifth lower (adding one flat). Starting from C (no accidentals), moving clockwise yields G(1#), D(2#), A(3#), E(4#), B(5#), F#(6#), C#(7#). Moving counterclockwise yields F(1b), Bb(2b), Eb(3b), Ab(4b), Db(5b), Gb(6b), Cb(7b). Three enharmonic key pairs meet at the bottom: B/Cb, F#/Gb, C#/Db.

# Prerequisites
- **Key Signatures** -- the circle organizes keys by their signatures
- **Major Scale** -- keys on the circle are defined by major scales

# Key Properties
1. 15 keys listed (12 unique, 3 enharmonic pairs)
2. Adjacent keys on the circle are **closely related keys** (key signatures differ by one accidental)
3. Both major keys (uppercase) and relative minor keys (lowercase) appear on the circle
4. The order of sharps (F-C-G-D-A-E-B) follows the clockwise progression
5. The order of flats (B-E-A-D-G-C-F) follows the counterclockwise progression

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Place C at the top (no accidentals)
2. Move clockwise by fifths, adding one sharp each step
3. Move counterclockwise by fifths, adding one flat each step
4. Add relative minor keys three half steps below each major key

# Context & Application
The circle of fifths is essential for understanding key relationships, transposition, and modulation. Keys adjacent on the circle share most pitch content and are the most common modulation destinations.

## Builds Upon

## Enables
- **Closely Related Keys** -- adjacent keys on the circle
- **Transposition** -- the circle guides transposition by fifths
- **Relative Keys** -- visualized as paired positions on the circle

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The circle shows only major keys.
  **Clarification**: Each position represents both a major key and its relative minor, sharing the same key signature.
