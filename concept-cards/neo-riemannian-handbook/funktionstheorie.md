# Quick Definition

Riemann's systematic framework for analyzing harmony by classifying all chords as expressions of three fundamental functions — Tonic (T), Dominant (D), and Subdominant (S) — using a compact symbolic notation designed for efficient analytical reduction.

# Core Definition

**Funktionstheorie** is Riemann's comprehensive analytical system, first named in *Vereinfachte Harmonielehre* (1893), that reduces all harmonic events to manifestations of three primary functions. The system includes:

1. **Three function categories**: T (Tonic), D (Dominant), S (Subdominant)
2. **Modifier symbols**: p (parallel), l (Leittonwechsel), v (Variante), and figured-bass-style annotations for alterations
3. **Parenthetical notation**: Applied dominants shown as (D) or D/x

Grabner formulated the pedagogical goal: "It was particularly important... to explain the formula *T–S–D–T* as early as possible and to demonstrate that 1. all progressions used in music are but derivations from these fundamental sonorities and 2. it is possible to analyze with this plain formula the most complex modern compositions" (Ch. 1, p. 14).

The system was explicitly built on Fetis's concept of tonality: "Our theory of tonal functions of harmony is nothing other than the development of Fetis's concept of tonality" (Riemann, *Handbuch der Harmonielehre*, cited in Ch. 3, n. 8).

# Key Properties

1. All harmony reduces to three categories (T, D, S), not seven scale degrees
2. Symbolic notation allows economical analysis of chromatically complex music
3. The system was originally dualistic (pairing major and minor derivations)
4. Applied dominants (Zwischendominanten) extend functional analysis without invoking modulation
5. Function symbols can be combined with figured-bass-style annotations for specificity
6. The system presupposes a governing tonic throughout a passage

# Construction / Recognition

## To Analyze Using Funktionstheorie
1. Identify the governing tonic of the passage
2. Classify each chord as T, D, or S (or a representative thereof)
3. For subsidiary chords, add modifier: p (parallel), l (Leittonwechsel), v (Variante)
4. For applied dominants, use parenthetical notation: (D) resolving to the target
5. For alterations, add figured-bass style numbers
6. At points of true modulation, establish a new tonic

# Context & Application

Holtmeier traces how Funktionstheorie was transformed by successive generations of theorists:

**Schreyer (1905)**: Used function symbols alongside Riemann's Klangschluessel for analytical reduction of masterworks. His goal was "the *introduction to an understanding of the masterworks*" rather than Riemann's practical exercises (Ch. 1, p. 15).

**Grabner (1920s)**: Called functional recognition "the reduction of a complicated sonic structure to its simplest form" and aimed to begin composition teaching with analytical exercises (Ch. 1, p. 14).

**Louis/Thuille (1907)**: Absorbed function-theoretical principles while retaining Roman numerals, creating a synthesis that Riemann claimed was dependent on his work (Ch. 1, pp. 41-42).

The critical insight is that Funktionstheorie was not monolithic. Holtmeier shows that the "reform-pedagogical theory of functions" diverged significantly from Riemann's own practice, particularly in abandoning dualism while retaining the three-function framework and analytical reduction.

# Examples

**Example 1** (Ch. 1, p. 14): Grabner's analytical formula T-S-D-T applied to Romantic music: "all progressions used in music are but derivations from these fundamental sonorities."

**Example 2** (Ch. 1, p. 15): The economy of function symbols vs. Roman numerals — Riemann observed that Roman numeral analysis requires "also accidentals for the scale-degree figure," while function symbols remain compact.

**Example 3** (Ch. 1, p. 18): Schreyer's reduction of the transition to the finale of Beethoven's Fifth Symphony to a simple cadential combination, using function symbols to show that 50 measures of apparent harmonic activity reduce to T-D motion.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Using function symbols without establishing the governing tonic
  **Correction**: All function labels are relative to a tonic; the tonic must be identified first

- **Error**: Declaring modulation for every chromatic chord
  **Correction**: Use applied dominant notation (Zwischendominant) to maintain the governing tonic through chromatic passages

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Believing Funktionstheorie is a single, monolithic system
  **Clarification**: Holtmeier shows multiple variants: Riemann's own (dualistic), Schreyer's (reductive), Grabner's (pedagogical), Louis/Thuille's (synthetic). "The theory of functions" has no single canonical form.

- **Confusion**: Equating Funktionstheorie with neo-Riemannian theory
  **Clarification**: Neo-Riemannian theory largely *jettisons* functional thinking in favor of transformational relations. As Hyer notes, modern neo-Riemannian theory is "much less Riemannian than Fregean" (Ch. 3).
