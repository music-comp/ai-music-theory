# Quick Definition

The structural property of the consonant triad whereby it can be generated by multiple independent interval-class combinations (IC3, IC4, IC5), each suggesting a different set of transformational generators and thus a different group structure, creating a "source of discord" among competing neo-Riemannian systematizations.

# Core Definition

The **over-determined triad** is Engebretsen's central concept in Chapter 12, describing a fundamental structural property of the consonant triad: it is "over-determined" because its three constituent intervals (IC3, IC4, IC5) are not all independent as generators. Any two of these intervals suffice to generate the triad, but different choices of generating pair lead to different transformational systems with different privileged operations. The Quintschritt/Terzschritt system (using IC5 and IC4) emphasizes acoustic relationships; the PLR system (using IC3 and IC4, roughly) emphasizes voice-leading parsimony. The "discord" of Engebretsen's title refers to the conflict between these competing systematizations — a conflict that traces back to the triad's internal structure. Each system captures genuine properties of the triad, but no single choice of generators captures all of its properties equally. This is why the triad has been independently systematized in multiple, non-trivially different ways (Oettingen, Riemann, Hauptmann, Hostinsky, and the various neo-Riemannians).

# Key Properties

1. **Three interval classes**: The consonant triad contains IC3, IC4, and IC5
2. **Any two suffice**: Any pair of ICs generates the triad (and the full group of transformations on triads)
3. **Different generators yield different emphases**: IC5+IC4 (Riemann) emphasizes acoustic relations; IC3+IC4 (neo-Riemannian) emphasizes voice leading
4. **Source of discord**: Competing transformational systems arise from different generator choices
5. **Multiple systematizations**: Oettingen, Riemann, Hauptmann, Hostinsky each chose differently
6. **No neutral choice**: Every generator set privileges certain relationships over others

# Construction / Recognition

The over-determination is visible in the various group presentations for the same group (D12):

Using Q (IC5) and T (IC4): Emphasizes fifth and third relations; dominant motion is a single operation (Q); voice-leading smoothness is compound
Using P, L, R (IC3+IC4 based): Emphasizes voice-leading parsimony; dominant motion is compound (LR); voice-leading smoothness is basic
Using other IC combinations: IC3+IC5, IC3+IC4+IC5 — each produces a valid but different systematization

All generate isomorphic groups, but the "character" of each system (which operations are simple, which are compound) differs according to the generator choice.

# Context & Application

Engebretsen uses over-determination to explain why the neo-Riemannian research program has produced multiple competing systems that are "at once alike and unalike." The concept also explains why 19th-century theorists (Hauptmann, Oettingen, Riemann, Hostinsky) arrived at different but related systematizations: each was responding to a genuine property of the triad while emphasizing different intervals as fundamental. The concept connects to Cohn's observation about the Tonnetz's multiple rediscoveries (Ch. 11): different theorists discover different Tonnetze because different IC-pairings generate different spatial structures, all grounded in the same over-determined triad.

# Examples

Engebretsen's comparison (Ch. 12):

In **Riemann's system** (Q, T generators): The Quintschritt (dominant relation) is a single generator operation. The PLR-equivalent of L (Leittonwechsel) requires a compound: Q*T*Seitenwechsel.

In the **PLR system** (P, L, R generators): The Leittonwechsel is a single generator. The dominant relation requires a compound: LR.

Both systems are isomorphic (D12), but the "cost" of different musical relationships is distributed differently, reflecting the choice of generators.

Hostinsky (1879), uniquely, treated the Terzwechsel as a generator alongside the Terzschritt, producing a slightly different systematization that treats third relations and their mode changes as equally fundamental.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming one generator set is "correct" and others are wrong
  **Correction**: Over-determination means multiple valid generator sets exist; the choice depends on analytical goals, not correctness

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Over-determination means the triad is somehow defective
  **Clarification**: "Over-determined" is a structural observation (more generating resources than needed), not a criticism; it explains the richness of the triad as a musical object

- **Confusion**: All generator sets produce the same analytical results
  **Clarification**: While all produce isomorphic groups, they assign different "costs" (compound vs. simple) to different musical relationships, affecting which progressions appear analytically natural vs. complex
