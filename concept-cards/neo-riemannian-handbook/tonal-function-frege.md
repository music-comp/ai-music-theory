# Quick Definition

Brian Hyer's philosophical analysis of Riemann's concept of tonal function through the lens of Gottlob Frege's mathematical logic, demonstrating that "function" in Riemann refers to the relational *form* of harmonic meaning rather than the particular *content* of any chord. The analysis reveals why neo-Riemannian theory is "much less Riemannian than Fregean."

# Core Definition

Hyer's chapter "What Is a Function?" (Ch. 3) explores the philosophical depth of Riemann's term *Funktion* by comparing it with Frege's mathematical concept of function (Frege was Riemann's near-exact contemporary, 1848-1925 vs. 1849-1919, and both studied at Gottingen under Lotze).

The key insight from Frege: in the functional equation y = f(x), a function lies not in the variables (x, y) but in the *form* of the expression — the letter f. "The heart of the matter lies in the word 'correlation'" (Frege, cited in Ch. 3, p. 96). A function is neither a number nor a name but a *pattern of relationship*.

Applied to Riemann: tonal function is not a property of any particular chord (that would confuse "the function with its value for an argument" — Ch. 3, p. 96) but the *form* of the relationship between a chord and its tonic. When we say V is "dominant," we are not describing the chord G-B-D but the relational pattern: "the Klang of the upper fifth of the tonic." Different chords can instantiate the same function (different x values yielding the same f(x)).

Frege's distinction between *Sinn* (sense) and *Bedeutung* (meaning/reference) maps onto Riemann's theory: the *Sinn* of a chord is how it presents itself (e.g., "the triad on the fifth degree"); its *Bedeutung* is what it means functionally (dominant). The functional label is the *Bedeutung*.

Hyer concludes that neo-Riemannian theory ironically carries forward the Fregean dimension (abstract relational operations) while abandoning the Riemannian dimension (functional meaning within a tonal hierarchy): "What we have come to call neo-Riemannianism is, both ironically and poetically, much less Riemannian than Fregean" (Ch. 3, p. 131).

# Key Properties

1. Function = relational form, not particular content
2. Frege's distinction: function vs. argument / form vs. content / sign vs. thing signified
3. "Confusing the function with its value for an argument" is the central error Hyer identifies
4. Riemann's *Bedeutung* = functional meaning; not identical to Frege's *Bedeutung* = reference
5. Neo-Riemannian theory preserves the Fregean abstraction but drops the Riemannian semantics
6. Riemann's late psychologism diverges from Frege's anti-psychologism — "the crux of the problem"

# Construction / Recognition

## Frege-Riemann Parallel
- Frege: 2x^2 + x = the function f(x) = 2x^2 + x. The numbers 2, 4, 1 are arguments/values; f is the form.
- Riemann: T, D, S are functional forms. The particular chords C-E-G, G-B-D, F-A-C are arguments/values; the relational pattern to the tonic is the function.

## The Psychologism Divergence
- Frege: Purged psychology from logic; thoughts (*Gedanken*) are objective, mind-independent
- Riemann: Grounded musical logic in psychology (*Tonvorstellung*); meaning is a mental act
- This divergence is "the crux of the problem concerning the notion of a tonal function" (Ch. 3, p. 129)

# Context & Application

Hyer's analysis illuminates why the term "function" was so powerful for Riemann despite his near-total failure to define it: the term imports the entire machinery of mathematical-logical reasoning about abstract relationships. It also explains the productive ambiguity in neo-Riemannian theory, which operates with abstract transformational operations (like mathematical functions) while abandoning the tonal-hierarchical semantics that Riemann intended.

# Examples

**Example 1** (Ch. 3, p. 97): Frege's example: "2+5 and 3+4 are equal but not the same" — like saying "the sweet-smelling violet differs from *Viola odorata* because the names sound different." Different signs, same meaning. Analogously: different chords can have the same function.

**Example 2** (Ch. 3, p. 95): Riemann's dominant as functionally incomplete: "dominants are never completely consonant because they are always represented and judged from the tonic." The function determines the chord's incompleteness, not its acoustic properties.

**Example 3** (Ch. 3, p. 131): "What we have come to call neo-Riemannianism is, both ironically and poetically, much less Riemannian than Fregean" — neo-Riemannian operations are abstract relational forms without tonal-semantic content.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating "function" as merely a synonym for "chord label"
  **Correction**: Hyer shows function is about relational form, not labeling content. T is not "another name for I" but a different kind of theoretical claim.

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking Hyer claims Riemann read Frege or was influenced by him
  **Clarification**: The parallel is structural, not historical. Both worked in the same intellectual milieu (Gottingen, Lotze, neo-Kantianism), but Hyer's comparison is analytical, not genealogical.
