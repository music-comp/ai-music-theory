# Quintschritt and Terzschritt

## Quick Definition

The two fundamental mode-preserving harmonic steps in Riemann's system: Quintschritt moves by perfect fifth (the most acoustically privileged interval) and Terzschritt moves by major third, together generating all Schritte in the Harmonieschritte taxonomy.

## Formal Definition

### Quintschritt (Fifth Step)
The **Quintschritt** (Q) relates triads whose roots are a perfect fifth apart:
- From C major: C+ → G+ (schlichte Quintschritt, "plain")
- From E minor: °e → °a (in the direction of chord generation)

**Direction matters**:
- **Schlichte Quintschritt**: In direction of chord's overtone generation (up for major, down for minor)
- **Gegenquintschritt**: Opposite direction (down for major, up for minor)

### Terzschritt (Third Step)
The **Terzschritt** (T) relates triads whose roots are a major third apart:
- From C major: C+ → E+
- From E minor: °e → °c#

**Direction matters**:
- **Schlichte Terzschritt**: In direction of generation (up major third for major)
- **Gegenterzschritt**: Opposite direction (down major third for major, = up minor sixth)

## As Generators

### The Minimal Generator Set
Together with Seitenwechsel (⊕), Q and T generate all 24 operations:

| Combination | Result |
|-------------|--------|
| Q | Quintschritt |
| Q⁻¹ | Gegenquintschritt |
| T | Terzschritt |
| T⁻¹ | Gegenterzschritt |
| Q² | Ganztonschritt |
| QT | Leittonschritt |
| Q³T | Tritonusschritt |
| Q⊕ | Quintwechsel |
| T⊕ | Terzwechsel |

### Group Presentation
⟨Q, T, ⊕ | Q¹² = T⁴ = ⊕² = e, QT = TQ, (Q⊕)² = (T⊕)² = e⟩

Key features:
- Q has order 12 (circle of fifths)
- T has order 4 (diminished seventh roots)
- Q and T commute (order doesn't matter)

## Acoustic Justification

### Why These Intervals?
Riemann chose Q and T because:
- **Perfect fifth** (Q): Most consonant interval after octave (3:2 ratio)
- **Major third** (T): Next most consonant (5:4 ratio)
- Together they represent the prime-limit just intonation (2, 3, 5)

### The Tonnetz Axes
On the Tonnetz:
- **Horizontal axis**: Generated by Q (fifths)
- **Vertical axis**: Generated by T (major thirds)
- Minor thirds emerge as diagonal (difference T - Q in interval class)

## Derivation of All Schritte

### Simple Schritte (Length 1)
- Q: Quintschritt
- T: Terzschritt

### Compound Schritte (Length 2+)
| Schritt | Derivation | Word Length |
|---------|------------|-------------|
| Ganztonschritt | Q² | 2 |
| Kleinterzschritt | Q⁻¹T (or TQ⁻¹) | 2 |
| Leittonschritt | QT | 2 |
| Tritonusschritt | Q³T | 4 |

### Inverse Relations
- Gegenquintschritt = Q⁻¹ (= Q¹¹ in Z₁₂)
- Gegenterzschritt = T⁻¹ (= T³ in Z₄)

## Comparison to PLR

### Different Emphases
| Feature | Q and T | P, L, R |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Basis | Root intervals | Voice leading |
| Fifth relation | Direct (Q) | Compound (LR) |
| Third relation | Direct (T) | Via mode change |
| Privilege | Acoustic | Parsimonious |

### Equivalences
| Q-T Derivation | PLR Equivalent |
|----------------|----------------|
| Q | LR |
| T | RPR or LPL |
| ⊕ | P |
| T⊕ | R |
| QT⊕ | L |

## Analytical Applications

### Root Motion Analysis
Tracking Q and T reveals:
- Dominant motion (Q and Q⁻¹)
- Chromatic third relations (T and T⁻¹)
- Compound motion (Q², QT, etc.)

### Sequential Patterns
Common sequences involve:
- Repeated Q: Circle of fifths sequences
- Repeated T: Chromatic third chains
- Alternating: Various modulatory patterns

### Kopp's Preference
David Kopp favors Q and T (direct fifth/third relationships) over PLR compounds for analyzing 19th-century music where acoustic relations are primary.

## The "Plain" and "Contrary" Distinction

### Dualistic Origin
The schlicht/gegen distinction reflects dualism:
- Major chords "generate" upward (overtones)
- Minor chords "generate" downward (undertones, in Riemann's theory)
- "Plain" = with generation; "Contrary" = against

### Practical Effect
For C major (generates up):
- Schlichte Quintschritt: C+ → G+ (up fifth)
- Gegenquintschritt: C+ → F+ (down fifth)

For A minor (generates down):
- Schlichte Quintschritt: °a → °d (down fifth)
- Gegenquintschritt: °a → °e (up fifth)

## Common Confusions

- **Direction is contextual**: "Schlicht" means different physical directions for major vs. minor
- **Q ≠ dominant function**: Q describes root motion, not function (though related)
- **T ≠ mediant function**: Similarly, T is an interval relation, not a function
