# Quick Definition

The dualist analytical principle that harmonic structure is organized by processes of opposition, negation, and balance, with the tonic functioning as the mediating element between subdominant and dominant -- the element that resolves their opposition into unity.

# Core Definition

Klumpenhouwer proposes that "a hard dualist perspective might understand the dynamics and mechanics of harmonic practice to be centered on mediation, and its auxiliary concepts of opposition, negation and balance, as its organizing principle" (Ch. 6, p. 211). The tonic is not merely the "home" key but the element that **mediates** the opposition between subdominant and dominant.

This does not mean that every dualist analysis must find structures that "sum to null." Rather, "while the unfolding harmonic structure of an excerpt or piece may not be mediated by the tonic or tonic elements or structural sum to null, it is always mediated by *some* musical element(s)" (p. 211).

# Key Properties

1. Tonic emerges in perception when both subdominant and dominant are presented (serially or simultaneously)
2. The tonic mediates the opposition between S and D -- it relates to S as D relates to T
3. Mediation need not produce literal inversional cancellation ("summing to null")
4. Every harmonic structure has some mediating element, even if not the tonic
5. The determination of what mediation "means musically" depends on analytical perspective, not on the technology alone

# Construction / Recognition

## Identifying Mediation in Analysis
1. Identify opposing harmonic forces (typically D and S regions)
2. Locate the element that mediates between them (typically T)
3. Assess how the mediation is articulated: through serial presentation, simultaneous aggregation, or structural balance
4. Note: Mediation occurs at the level of methodology, not necessarily at the level of literal pitch operations

## The Dominant Seventh as Mediation
Riemann's analysis of the dominant seventh chord as an aggregate of two triads (dominant + subdominant representative) instantiates mediation: the simultaneity of dominant and subdominant elements creates the tonic as implied mediator (p. 212, n. 22).

# Context & Application

The mediation principle connects Klumpenhouwer's defense of dualism to Hauptmann's original dialectical framework (thesis-antithesis-synthesis). It provides a methodological bridge between abstract dualist theory and concrete analytical practice.

Klumpenhouwer distinguishes between what the technology reveals (inversional relationships, Schritt/Wechsel patterns) and what the methodology makes of those revelations (claims about mediation, opposition, musical meaning). "It is pure mystification to assert they are identical, that our thinking about a thing is identical to the thing" (p. 214, n. 21).

# Examples

**Tonic emergence** (n. 22): "Playing subdominant and dominant (in either order) in any key, we can easily imagine the appropriate tonic. A dualist explanation of the phenomenon claims that the tonic emerges in our imagination because it mediates the opposition between subdominant and dominant."

**Beethoven analysis** (pp. 211-213): The account of Ged was generated from the Ged' account by systematically replacing dualist terms (°a → c+, mixed minor → mixed major, subdominant → dominant). The mediation principle reveals how the opposing harmonic forces in each section are resolved through their complementary relationship.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Requiring that every dualist analysis produce exact inversional cancellation
  **Correction**: Mediation identifies processes of opposition and balance, not literal zero-summing

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Mediation is a property of the music itself
  **Clarification**: Klumpenhouwer distinguishes the analytical methodology (which identifies mediation) from the music (which is organized but not "identical" to our analytical account)
