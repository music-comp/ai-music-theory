# Quick Definition

The central epistemological tension identified by Engebretsen between Riemann's original conception of Harmonieschritte as key-dependent, functionally constrained classifications of chord relationships, and the neo-Riemannian reinterpretation of the same relationships as key-independent, unconstrained group transformations.

# Core Definition

Engebretsen (Ch. 12) identifies a fundamental **conflation** at the heart of neo-Riemannian theory: the reinterpretation of Riemann's Harmonieschritte from **functional relations** (descriptions of chord relationships within a key-based, hierarchical tonal framework) to **root-interval transformations** (autonomous operations on triads independent of key). In Riemann's system, the Quintschritt from C+ to G+ means "the tonic relates to its dominant"; in neo-Riemannian theory, the same operation means "transpose by seven semitones, preserving mode." The functional reading embeds each progression within a key and evaluates it by intelligibility and tonal coherence; the transformational reading treats it as a formal operation independent of context. Engebretsen emphasizes that neo-Riemannian theorists have generally acknowledged this conflation but argues it deserves more sustained critical attention, especially because Riemann himself recognized that removing functional constraints would produce a "chaos of possibilities."

# Prerequisites

- **Harmonieschritte**: The system that is subject to both functional and transformational interpretation
- **Schritt/Wechsel system**: The group-theoretic formalization of the transformational reading

# Key Properties

1. **Two readings of the same system**: Functional (key-dependent) and transformational (key-independent)
2. **Conflation acknowledged**: Neo-Riemannian theorists generally recognize the interpretive shift but proceed anyway
3. **Loss of hierarchy**: The transformational reading eliminates Riemann's intelligibility hierarchy
4. **Loss of key context**: The transformational reading operates without reference to a governing tonic
5. **Gain of group structure**: The transformational reading enables formal group-theoretic analysis
6. **Riemann's resistance**: Riemann explicitly subordinated Harmonieschritte to function theory in later work

# Construction / Recognition

Engebretsen's comparison (Ch. 12):

**Functional reading** (Riemann):
- C+ to G+ = "the Quintschritt connects tonic to dominant"
- Meaningful only within the key of C major
- Subject to intelligibility constraints
- Evaluated by Harmoniewirkung
- Function theory is "the true guide through the labyrinth of possible harmonic successions"

**Transformational reading** (neo-Riemannian):
- C+ to G+ = S7 (or LR): transpose root up 7 semitones, preserve mode
- Meaningful regardless of key context
- All operations structurally equivalent
- No Wirkung evaluation
- Group structure is the organizing principle

# Context & Application

This tension is the fundamental historiographical and methodological issue in neo-Riemannian theory. It matters for:
- Understanding what is genuinely "Riemannian" about neo-Riemannian theory
- Evaluating whether neo-Riemannian analyses are faithful to their historical sources
- Deciding when functional vs. transformational analysis is appropriate for a given passage
- Understanding why Riemann's "horror vision" became neo-Riemannian theory's normal operating mode

Kopp's observation (cited by Engebretsen): neo-Riemannian "functional" labels (P, R, L) are actually root-interval labels, making the system "a pure root-interval system" despite its claim to describe "functions."

# Examples

Engebretsen (Ch. 12) cites Klumpenhouwer's acknowledgment of the "ideational structure" imported when treating Schritte as transformations rather than classifications. Hyer's original neo-Riemannian work similarly acknowledged the interpretive shift.

Riemann's own late position (Handbuch, 4th edition, cited by Engebretsen): "The true guide through the labyrinth of possible harmonic successions is no longer the nomenclature of the Harmonieschritte but rather that of function" — explicitly subordinating the transformational-susceptible system to functional constraints.

Cohn (cited by Engebretsen): identified the "path/goal duality" as an open question — whether the same harmonic event is best understood as a point in a functional hierarchy or as a step on a transformational path.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming the functional and transformational readings are fully compatible
  **Correction**: Engebretsen shows they are in genuine tension; the transformational reading strips away constraints that were essential to Riemann's system

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Neo-Riemannian theory is what Riemann intended
  **Clarification**: Neo-Riemannian theory extracts and transforms elements from Riemann's work; it is not a faithful continuation of his project, which was always key-functional at its core
