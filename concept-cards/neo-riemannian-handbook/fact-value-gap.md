# Quick Definition

The disconnect between what neo-Riemannian analysis reveals (voice-leading coherence) and what originally draws analysts to the passages they study (the remarkable, disruptive sound), identified by Rings as a fundamental methodological tension.

# Core Definition

Rings identifies a paradox at the heart of neo-Riemannian analysis: "Surely a prime reason for the success of neo-Riemannian theory is that it allows analysts to dwell on the most remarkable sounding passages...But it is not the remarkable sound of those passages that is analyzed; it is their coherence" (Rings, p. 495). The analyst is drawn to a passage because it sounds extraordinary, but the analytical product demonstrates orderliness (voice-leading parsimony, regional membership). "The analysis values the passage for its coherence...but we may value the passage for its sound" (p. 495). This gap between what motivates the analysis and what the analysis produces Rings calls the "fact/value gap."

- **Analytical values and paradigms** — Understanding the ideological divergence between paradigms is necessary to appreciate the gap

# Key Properties

1. Neo-Riemannian analysis is attracted to remarkable-sounding passages
2. The analytical product reveals coherence, not remarkableness
3. The gap exists because the values that motivate analysis differ from what the analysis demonstrates
4. Both Riemannian and neo-Riemannian analyses ultimately reveal order, though they begin from different ends
5. The gap cannot be fully closed by formal means alone

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify the Fact-Value Gap

1. Ask: Why was this passage selected for analysis? (Usually: because it sounds extraordinary)
2. Ask: What does the analysis demonstrate? (Usually: voice-leading coherence)
3. Note the disconnect: the analysis does not capture the quality that motivated it
4. Consider what additional framework might bridge the gap (e.g., tonal intention, phenomenology)

# Context & Application

The fact-value gap explains why neo-Riemannian analyses can feel both illuminating and incomplete. The analyst's engagement with remarkable passages is genuine and valuable, but the formal tools available produce a different kind of insight than the one initially sought. Rings's tonal-intention framework (pp. 500-506) represents one attempt to close the gap, though he acknowledges it cannot be fully closed: "No formal model can capture all aspects of our musical experience" (p. 506).

# Examples

**Schubert Impromptu, mm. 78-82** (pp. 494-495): The analyst is drawn to this passage because "the tonal bottom seems to drop out" — a visceral, experiential response. The neo-Riemannian analysis reveals that the triads belong to a Weitzmann region with consistent voice-leading distances. The analysis tells us the passage is coherent, but that is not why we find it remarkable.

**Parsifal analyses** (p. 495): "Many analytical forays into *Parsifal* have begun not at the work's outset, but with the most chromatically distorted version of the Grail motive." The starting point is the most disruptive moment; the analysis reveals its systematic logic.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Dismissing neo-Riemannian analysis because of the gap
  **Correction**: The coherence revealed is genuinely informative; the gap simply means additional perspectives are needed

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the fact-value gap is unique to neo-Riemannian theory
  **Clarification**: Riemann's analysis also has a version of this gap — he demonstrates conformance but does not explain why the passages sound remarkable
