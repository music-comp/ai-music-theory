# Quick Definition

The compositional technique of extending the eight-measure periodic prototype through inserted material, repeated functions, or delayed cadential arrivals, creating dramatic or comic effect through the amplification of closural energy while maintaining the underlying periodic logic.

# Core Definition

Phrase expansion in Riemann's analytical framework refers to techniques by which composers extend beyond the eight-measure periodic norm while preserving its structural functions. Burnham identifies the key analytical distinction (from Riemann's *Grosse Kompositionslehre*): "The most important distinction is between *actually constitutive, developing passages* and *insertions*... the *essential musical action* and the *moments of idle lingering* inserted between the main phases of that action" (p. 450). This distinction creates "the most palpable bridge between background prototype and foreground realization" (p. 446). Types include: inserted repetitions (3a-4a, 5a-6a), multiple cadential arrivals (8, 8a, 8b), iterated antepenultimate (repeated "6" function), back-relating functions (9 echoing 8), and *Takttriole* (three measures for two).

# Prerequisites

- **Periodic function** — The numbered measure functions that expansion elaborates
- **Hierarchical metric organization** — The eight-measure prototype against which expansion is measured

# Key Properties

1. Expansion does not destroy the periodic structure — it elaborates within it
2. Expanded material receives alphabetic suffixes (3a, 4a, 8a, etc.)
3. The "8" function remains invariant even in massively expanded periods
4. The "6" function has special status: the antepenultimate can be iterated dramatically because it signals imminent resolution
5. Riemann sees Beethoven's maturation as "ever firmer positioning of the actual structure" through expansion
6. The stations of the prototype (especially 6 and 8) "carry the weight of ever larger spans of music"

# Construction / Recognition

## Types of Expansion

1. **Inserted repetitions**: Measures labeled 3a-4a, 5a-6a repeat earlier functions (often in different register)
2. **Multiple cadential arrivals**: 8, 8a, 8b, 8c — repeated closural functions
3. **Iterated antepenultimate**: Repeated "6" functions delay resolution, building energy
4. **Back-relating functions**: A "9" that echoes "8" rather than starting a new period
5. **Takttriole**: Three measures substituted for two between strong downbeats

## To Analyze Expanded Periods

1. Identify the "8" function (cadential goal)
2. Work backward to assign essential functions
3. Label repeated or inserted material with alphabetic suffixes
4. Note which functions are expanded and the effect this creates

# Context & Application

Burnham demonstrates that phrase expansion is the key to understanding Beethoven's formal innovations. The three Op. 31 sonatas show contrasting expansion strategies: Op. 31, No. 1 proliferates "8" functions for comic effect ("a door that slams shut, only to pop back open," p. 452); Op. 31, No. 3 delays the "8" through "telescoping elongation" (p. 453); Op. 31, No. 2 ("Tempest") iterates the "6" function for dramatic intensity, building up energy "like a capacitor" until the "7" leads to grounding "8" (p. 454).

**Historical claim**: Riemann argues that Beethoven's development shows "ever firmer positioning of the actual structure, around which all accessory material is arranged" (p. 451) — maturation involves more sophisticated expansion, not formal dissolution.

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 446, Ex. 16.4): Op. 31, No. 3, Period IV. 1-2, 3-4, (3a-4a), 5-6, (5a-6a), 7-8, (8a). Repeated pairs in higher register extend without advancing the harmonic argument; 8a in dropped register reverses the tendency.

**Example 2** (p. 451, Ex. 16.11): Op. 31, No. 1, Period III. Sixteen measures with five "8" functions. An "about-face" (*Umwenden*) at m. 36 retrospectively reinterprets an "8" as "7."

**Example 3** (pp. 451-452, Ex. 16.12): Op. 31, No. 1, Period IX (end of exposition). Nine "8" functions — the movement's "comic tic" of closural proliferation.

**Example 4** (pp. 452-453): Op. 31, No. 3, Period VI. 33 measures: "7-8, 7-8, 6-7-8, 5-6, 5-6, 5-6, 4-5-6-7-8, (6-7-8)" — a "telescoping elongation, always moving a step further from the 8."

**Example 5** (pp. 453-455, Ex. 16.14): Op. 31, No. 2 ("Tempest"), Period II. The "6" function "furiously churns away," "building up to a tremendous discharge of energy at measure 21."

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating expansion as violation of the periodic structure
  **Correction**: Expansion elaborates the structure; it does not break it

- **Error**: Assuming all expanded material is unimportant
  **Correction**: The "essential/accessory" distinction is analytical, not evaluative; expanded material creates the dramatic effect

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking multiple "8" functions mean the analysis is wrong
  **Clarification**: Multiple 8s are analytically revealing — they show compositional strategies around closure (comic proliferation, dramatic intensification)

- **Confusion**: Believing expansion techniques are uniform across composers and movements
  **Clarification**: Each work deploys expansion differently; the contrast between Op. 31 sonatas illustrates this variety
