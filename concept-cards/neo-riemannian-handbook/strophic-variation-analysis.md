# Quick Definition

An analytical method in which successive strophes of a modified strophic song are compared against a strophic norm to reveal text-motivated compositional choices in melody, harmony, and metric organization.

# Core Definition

Riemann's analyses of Brahms's late songs proceed by systematic comparison of successive strophes. Both "Immer leiser" and "Das Madchen spricht" are "explicated in terms of their deviations from a strophic norm" (Berry, p. 476). Riemann "not only compares successive strophes explicitly, but also represents their melodies and pivotal harmonies atop one another in graphic notation," a technique borrowed from his approach to Caccini's vocal music in the *Handbuch der Musikgeschichte* (Berry, p. 476). For literally strophic songs, the method reveals subtle alterations; for modified strophic songs, it opens "an interpretive space in which Brahms's compositional choices can be related productively to the text" (p. 476).

# Prerequisites

- **Text declamation** — Strophic comparison reveals how declamation varies between strophes to serve different texts
- **Normative analysis** — The strophic norm functions as the reference against which deviations are measured

# Key Properties

1. Successive strophes are graphically superimposed for comparison
2. Deviations from strict strophic repetition are attributed to text-expressive motivations
3. The method assumes a strophic norm even when the song is significantly varied
4. Harmonic, melodic, and metric deviations are tracked simultaneously
5. The approach works best for modified strophic songs (predominant in Brahms's late output)

# Construction / Recognition

## To Perform Strophic Variation Analysis

1. Identify the strophic structure of the song (number of strophes, their boundaries)
2. Align corresponding passages across strophes
3. Note where melody, harmony, or rhythm deviates between strophes
4. Attribute deviations to text-expressive motivations where possible
5. Identify the normative strophic pattern underlying the variations

# Context & Application

Brahms's output from the 1880s features a "preponderance of modified strophic songs," making this method particularly appropriate (Berry, p. 476). Riemann's approach provides "a fresh point of departure for further scholarship" on these songs. The method also has historical precedent in Riemann's analysis of Caccini, where increasingly varied monodies are compared across stanzas (p. 476).

# Examples

**"Immer leiser," Op. 105/2** (pp. 466-468): The two highly varied strophes share "only four measures of music" that return unchanged. The second stanza transfers "Ja, ich werde sterben mussen" to a higher register, "substituting a jealous emotion in place of poignant resignation" (p. 466). The second strophe's chromatic sequence consists of ascending minor third motions (E, G, Bb, Db) rather than the first strophe's descending pattern, avoiding "the mitigatingly explanatory resolutions" (p. 468).

**"Das Madchen spricht," Op. 107/3** (pp. 469-471): This nearly strophic song allows Riemann to demonstrate that "the analytic premise is inevitable and its implications trivial" for strictly strophic settings (Berry, p. 476), while subtle alterations in piano figuration still merit attention.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating all deviations as equally significant
  **Correction**: Distinguish between structurally meaningful variations (harmonic, melodic) and surface alterations (accompaniment figuration)

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Believing strophic variation analysis requires literally strophic songs
  **Clarification**: The method works best precisely with modified strophic songs, where the norm provides a framework for understanding creative departures
