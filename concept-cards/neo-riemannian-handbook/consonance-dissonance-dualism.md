# Consonance / Dissonance in Dualist Theory

## Quick Definition

In dualist theory, consonance is defined as the perception of tonal unity within the Klang (major or minor triad), while dissonance arises from the simultaneous occurrence of multiple Klange or from tones that mediate between independent prime tones.

## Formal Definition

**Dualist Definition of Consonance**:
Consonance is the combination of tones that leads to a **unitary conception** - a single Klang-representation. The major and minor triads are the only two fundamental consonances because they are "the two unique, fundamentally different [entities] in terms of which all other possible combinations are heard."

**Oettingen's Definition of Dissonance**:
"Dissonance is the simultaneous occurrence of two or more Klange (the word Klang to be understood not as an individual tone but in terms of Klang-representation)."

**Riemann's Refinement**:
Dissonance arises when:
1. Two or more incompatible Klang-representations compete
2. A tone requires mediation through an intervening prime (e.g., D as fifth of G, interpolating G between D and C)
3. Higher prime-numbered intervals (7, 9, 11, 13...) create non-unity

## The Critique of Acoustic Consonance

**Problems with Helmholtz's Theory**:
Riemann systematically dismantled acoustic explanations:

1. **"Clouded consonance" rejected**: Helmholtz claimed minor was major with a "clouded" third. But why doesn't lowering the fifth also merely "cloud" the chord? Why are the 7th and 9th partials dissonant despite being "natural"?

2. **Twin fundamentals rejected**: Helmholtz, Hostinsky, and Capellen proposed that the minor triad combines elements from multiple major triads (C-G from C major, Eb-G from Eb major). Riemann: This "contradicts the core idea of Rameau's system (consonance as a complex of tones all engendered by the selfsame son generateur)."

3. **Stumpf's fusion theory limited**: Stumpf ranked intervals by degree of "fusion" but could not explain why the augmented triad C-E-G# sounds dissonant when all its component intervals are consonant.

**The Unified Solution**:
Both major and minor consonance derive from the same principle: the simplest numerical ratios, applied to different domains (frequencies vs. wavelengths). "Emancipation from acoustical phenomena" makes both consonances equally valid.

## Theoretical Framework

**Why Only Two Consonances?**:
The concept of Klang is restricted to prime, third, and fifth because:
- The octave creates identity, not relationship
- The fifth of the fifth (ninth) introduces a mediating prime
- The third of the third (augmented fifth) likewise mediates
- Higher prime-number intervals (7, 11, 13) can be re-interpreted as members of the opposite series

**The Characteristic Dissonances**:
In Riemann's mature theory:
- **Major**: Dominant seventh (adds the under-seventh to V)
- **Minor**: Subdominant with "under-seventh" (adds the sixth to iv)

These are inversionally related, as Rehding illustrates: the dominant seventh in major mirrors the subdominant sixth in minor.

**Hierarchical Dissonances**:
Riemann distinguishes:
- **First-degree dissonance**: Tones one fifth-step away (dominant, subdominant)
- **Second-degree dissonance**: Tones two fifths away (the "impossible" major subdominant in minor)

## Musical Context

**The Minor Mode Problem**:
Helmholtz and the "monists" could not explain why:
- Minor sounds equally consonant as major (not "clouded" or "artificial")
- Minor is historically older than major (not derived from it)
- The minor triad has the same intervallic simplicity as major

**Riemann's Resolution**:
"A healthy, natural musical instinct cries out for the unconditional recognition of the consonant character of the minor chord, for unreserved and straightforward coordination with the major chord as one of the two important forms of consonance, alongside which there is not, and cannot be, a third option!"

**Practical Voice-Leading**:
Dissonance handling follows from Klang-representation:
- Dissonant tones are heard as "added" to underlying Klang
- Resolution means returning to pure Klang
- The "characteristic dissonances" identify function (D7 implies dominant function)

## Examples

### The Augmented Triad Paradox

C-E-G# contains only consonant intervals:
- C-E: major third (consonant)
- E-G#: major third (consonant)
- C-Ab: minor sixth (consonant)

Yet the simultaneity is dissonant because:
- The mind cannot assign a single Klang-representation
- E-G# implies E major while C-E implies C major or A minor
- The competing Klang-representations create dissonance

### The Diminished Triad

B-D-F in C major:
- Contains a diminished fifth (dissonant interval)
- Yet functions clearly as representing dominant (G7 without root)
- The mind supplies the missing G as the prime
- Dissonance is "elliptical" - the Klang is understood even when incomplete

## Common Confusions

1. **Consonance is not "pleasant"**: Dualist consonance means tonal unity, not aesthetic preference

2. **Dissonance is not intervallic**: Dissonance arises from competing Klang-representations, not from specific interval types

3. **The perfect fourth**: Dissonant against bass not because of interval quality but because it implies a different Klang-representation than the bass suggests

4. **"Natural" intervals**: The 7th partial (Bb above C) is "natural" acoustically but dissonant harmonically because it can be reinterpreted as belonging to the undertone series

## Historical Significance

Riemann's psychological definition of consonance:
- Freed theory from defending acoustic undertones
- Unified major and minor as equal consonances
- Anticipated 20th-century psychoacoustic approaches
- Established that musical hearing is interpretive, not merely receptive

The critique that even *major* consonance cannot be fully justified acoustically was revolutionary - it made the psychological turn necessary for both modes equally.
