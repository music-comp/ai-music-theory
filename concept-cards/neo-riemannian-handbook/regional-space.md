# Regional Space

## Quick Definition

The representation of diatonic keys as bounded regions on the Tonnetz, typically parallelograms containing the pitch classes of a scale, whose position and movement models tonal relationships and modulation.

## Formal Definition

**Regional space** describes how diatonic collections (keys) map onto the Tonnetz as geometric regions:

### Diatonic Encapsulation
A diatonic collection appears as a **parallelogram** on the Tonnetz:
- Contains 7 pitch classes of a major or minor scale
- Bounded by syntonic images (same letter name, different comma position)
- Two D naturals, for example, mark the left and right boundaries of C major

### Regional Properties
Each diatonic region:
- Encloses all scale degrees
- Contains 6 major and 6 minor triads (the diatonic chords)
- Has characteristic shape and orientation
- Shares edges with closely related regions

### Modulation as Sliding
Key change (modulation) appears as:
- Shifting the parallelogram to a new position
- The overlap between old and new regions indicates common pitch classes
- Close keys have maximal overlap; distant keys have minimal overlap

## Cohn's Integration of Regional and Triadic Space

### The Conflation Argument
Richard Cohn argues (contra Lerdahl) that:
- Pitch-class, chord, and key spaces need not be separate
- The Tonnetz naturally represents all three levels
- Regions emerge from the triadic structure

### How Regions Emerge
1. Plot pitch classes on IC4 × IC5 Tonnetz
2. Connect pitch classes into triadic triangles
3. Group adjacent triangles into diatonic parallelograms
4. Observe regional relationships through parallelogram positions

### Triadic vs. Regional Tonnetz
These are **geometric duals**:
- Triadic Tonnetz: Nodes = triads, edges = common-tone connections
- Regional Tonnetz: Nodes = regions, edges = shared triads

## Relationship to Key Distance

### Circle of Fifths Embedded
Moving the parallelogram:
- One step right (add sharp) = move to dominant key
- One step left (add flat) = move to subdominant key
- Vertical moves = relative/parallel relationships

### Measuring Key Distance
Regional distance can be measured by:
- Number of parallelogram edges crossed
- Number of pitch-class substitutions required
- Minimal path length on a regional graph

## Comparison to Other Models

### Lerdahl's Tonal Pitch Space
Fred Lerdahl proposed a **separated hierarchy**:
- Pitch-class space (chromatic)
- Chord space (triadic relationships)
- Regional space (key relationships)

Each level has its own structure and distance metric.

### Cohn's Counter-Argument
Cohn maintains that the Tonnetz already provides:
- Pitch-class relations through nodes
- Chord relations through triangles
- Regional relations through parallelograms

The separation is unnecessary; mutual implication is sufficient.

### Neo-Riemannian Perspective
For chromatic analysis:
- Regional determination may be suspended
- Triadic motion can occur without fixed tonic
- Regions become relevant only when tonal reference is established

## Analytical Applications

### Regional Indeterminacy
Some passages resist regional assignment:
- Hexatonic cycles (LP chains) span multiple regions
- Octatonic passages occupy region boundaries
- Sequential passages modulate through multiple regions

The Tonnetz allows tracking motion without forcing regional determination.

### Regional Reinterpretation
When a passage suddenly "makes sense" in a new key:
- The parallelogram snaps to a new position
- Retroactive reinterpretation is modeled geometrically
- Pivot chords appear at parallelogram overlaps

### Structural Modulation
Long-range key relations in sonata forms:
- Tonic region (exposition)
- Dominant region (secondary key)
- Development regions (various)
- Return to tonic (recapitulation)

Can be visualized as parallelogram trajectories.

## The Syntonic Seam

### Definition
The boundary where two syntonic versions of a pitch class meet (e.g., D and D, differing by syntonic comma in just intonation).

### Function
- Marks the edge of a diatonic region
- Crossing the seam indicates modulation
- In equal temperament, becomes a theoretical boundary

### Regional Bounding
Each region is "held in place" by its syntonic seams:
- Upper and lower limits on the third axis
- Left and right limits on the fifth axis

## Common Confusions

- **Region vs. key**: A region is a geometric area; a key is a functional organization with a tonic
- **Fixed vs. floating**: The parallelogram can be positioned anywhere; its location indicates the key
- **Necessary vs. optional**: Regional analysis is optional for chromatic passages that resist tonal determination
