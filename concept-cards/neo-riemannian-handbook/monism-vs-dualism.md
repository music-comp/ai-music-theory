# Monistic vs. Dualistic Reception

## Quick Definition

The fundamental divide in how Riemann's theory was received: dualists derived major and minor from opposite principles (overtones/undertones), while monists derived minor from major, rejecting the undertone series as the basis for minor.

## Formal Definition

**Harmonic Dualism**: The theory that major and minor triads are polar opposites derived from symmetrical acoustic principles:
- Major (Oberklang): Built upward from a generator via overtone ratios
- Minor (Unterklang): Built downward from a generator via undertone ratios

**Monism**: The rejection of undertone-based minor derivation in favor of:
- Minor as modification of major
- Minor derived from the overtone series only
- Minor's root remains the lowest tone (not the "generator")

The term "monism" was coined by **Georg Capellen** in his 1901 article "Die Unmoglichkeit und Uberflussigkeit der dualistischen Molltheorie Riemanns" ("The Impossibility and Redundancy of Riemann's Dualistic Theory of Minor").

## Historical Origin

### The Dualistic Tradition

1. **Zarlino (1558)**: First to show major/minor as polar opposites through harmonic vs. arithmetic string division
2. **Tartini (1754)**: Rediscovered Zarlino's duality; sought acoustic grounding
3. **Hauptmann (1853)**: "Epoch-making" philosophical derivation of dualism; the "originator of this idea" for modern theory
4. **Oettingen (1866)**: Developed "phonic overtones" (multiplication tones) to ground minor acoustically
5. **Riemann**: Systematized dualism for practical composition

### The Undertone Problem

Riemann initially tried to prove undertones exist physically:
- "Moonshine experiments" with tuning forks and resonance
- *Klirrtone* (rasping tones) showing undertones
- Sympathetic vibration phenomena

But acoustic science (d'Alembert's critique of Rameau) showed undertones are not independently produced by vibrating bodies as overtones are.

### Riemann's Retreat

Riemann eventually shifted to psychological justification:
> "What distinguishes major from minor comes down to the essence of major consonance being the simplest ratios in the increase in speed of vibration, that of minor consonance, by contrast, being the simplest ratios in the enlargement of the vibrating mass."

This "psychologizing retreat" satisfied neither dualists nor monists.

## Musical Context

### The Root Problem

Capellen's core critique: In dualistic theory, the "generator" of the minor triad (the tone from which the Unterklang is built) is *perceived* as the fifth, not the root:

- A minor (dualistic): Generator is E; Klangschluessel is °e
- A minor (monistic): Root is A; notation reflects this

This creates a disconnect between theoretical derivation and practical perception.

### Practical Consequences

**Dualistic notation** (Klangschluessel):
- C major = c+
- A minor = °e (E under-Klang)
- Requires learning two systems of chord identification

**Monistic notation**:
- C major = C or I
- A minor = a or vi
- Root position = bass note in normal hearing

### The Second Scale Degree

The "black hole" of strict dualistic function theory: In the major-minor polarity system, the chord built on ^2 (supertonic) has no clean functional derivation.

## Examples

### Dualistic Derivation

Major chord on C (c+):
```
Overtone series from C:
C - C - G - C - E - G...
Major triad: C-E-G (ratios 4:5:6)
```

Minor chord "on" E (°e) in dualistic theory:
```
Undertone series from E:
E - E - A - E - C - A...
Minor triad: A-C-E (ratios 1/4:1/5:1/6 from E)
```

### Monistic Derivation

Minor chord on A:
```
A minor = modification of A major
A-C-E derived from lowering third of A-C#-E
Root = A (not E)
```

### Reception History

**Orthodox Dualists**:
- Stephan Krehl (Leipzig)
- Sigfrid Karg-Elert
- Fritz Reuter, Paul Schenk

**Monistic Reformers**:
- Georg Capellen (critic)
- Rudolf Louis (synthesis)
- Ernst Kurth (psychological approach)
- Nearly all post-WWII German function theory

## Evolution/Variants

### Stages of the Debate

1. **1890s-1900s**: Capellen and others attack dualistic minor
2. **1900s-1910s**: Riemann's disciples mostly adopt monistic variants
3. **1920s-1930s**: Dualism persists only in "esoteric" circles
4. **Post-1945**: Function theory standardizes as monistic

### Kurth's Assessment

Ernst Kurth reframed dualism as a "theory of projection"—valuable as abstract analogy even without physical undertones:
> "From the perspective of an approach that is more independent of the physical foundations of music theory, dualism, as a two-sided projection, can gain a foundation that falls into the realm of psychology."

### Hidden Dualism in Modern Theory

Even "monistic" function theory retains dualistic traces:
- Parallels (Tp, Sp, Dp) positioned differently in major vs. minor
- Major: parallels lie *above* their functions (vi for I)
- Minor: parallels lie *below* their functions (III for i)

This polar derivation persists in Grabner, Maler, and standard German pedagogy.

### Neo-Riemannian Approach

Modern neo-Riemannian theory sidesteps the debate entirely:
- P, L, R operations are symmetrical transformations
- No acoustic derivation claimed
- Major and minor treated as transformationally equivalent
- Mathematical rather than physical justification

## Common Confusions

1. **Dualism = wrong**: Dualism is not "disproven" but is one theoretical framework among others; its elegance is acknowledged even by critics
2. **Riemann = dualist only**: Riemann's practical function symbols work monistically; dualism was for theoretical derivation
3. **Monism = modern**: Some aspects of dualistic thinking persist in modern function theory
4. **Neo-Riemannian = Riemannian**: Neo-Riemannian theory is arguably more "Fregean" (mathematical) than "Riemannian" (dualistic)
5. **The debate is settled**: While monism dominates pedagogy, dualistic insights inform transformational theory
