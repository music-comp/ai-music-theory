# Quick Definition

The fundamental divide in harmonic theory between monists, who derive minor from major (rejecting the undertone series), and dualists, who treat major and minor as equal, opposite entities generated by symmetric principles.

# Core Definition

**Harmonic monism** holds that the minor triad is derived from or subordinate to the major, typically explaining minor as a modification of the overtone-generated major triad. The term was coined by **Georg Capellen** in his 1901 article "Die Unmoglichkeit und Uberflussigkeit der dualistischen Molltheorie Riemanns" ("The Impossibility and Redundancy of Riemann's Dualistic Theory of Minor").

**Harmonic dualism** holds that major and minor triads are polar opposites, each independently generated by inverse acoustic/mathematical principles. Riemann's 1905 "Das Problem" (Ch. 5) is his definitive response to monist critics.

The debate centers on the status of the minor triad: Is it an independent, equal entity (dualism) or a derivative, subordinate one (monism)?

# Prerequisites

- **Harmonic dualism** -- Understanding the dualist position is needed to grasp the debate
- **Overtone series** -- The acoustic phenomenon both sides reference

# Key Properties

1. Monism derives minor from major (lowered third, "clouded" consonance)
2. Dualism derives major and minor independently from inverse principles
3. Monism keeps the root at the bass for both modes
4. Dualism assigns the prime of minor to the fifth (creating the minor root problem)
5. Riemann's function theory works largely monistically in practice despite dualist derivation

# Construction / Recognition

## Monist Approaches (as critiqued by Riemann)
- **Helmholtz**: Minor as major with a "clouded" third (p. 183)
- **Hostinsky/Capellen**: Minor triad as combination of elements from multiple major triads (twin fundamentals)
- **Stumpf**: Consonance as "fusion" -- ranked intervals by degree of blend

## Dualist Approaches
- **Hauptmann (1853)**: Dialectical -- major "has" intervals, minor "is had" by intervals
- **Oettingen (1866)**: Tonicity vs. phonicity; systematic dual notation
- **Riemann (1873-1919)**: Most extensive development; shift from acoustic to psychological basis

## Riemann's Critique of Monism (pp. 183-186)
1. "Clouded consonance" fails: Why doesn't lowering the fifth also merely "cloud" it?
2. Twin fundamentals contradict consonance as unity (Rameau's *son generateur*)
3. Stumpf's fusion cannot explain the augmented triad paradox

# Context & Application

Capellen demanded of Riemann: "How can a theory claim validity that cannot be justified by natural laws?" Riemann's response was to turn the critique against monism as well: even major consonance cannot be fully justified by acoustics. The move freed him to ground both modes psychologically.

In practice, the debate was largely settled by the mid-20th century in favor of monism for pedagogy, but neo-Riemannian theory revived dualist thinking in mathematical form. Even "monistic" function theory retains dualistic traces: parallels (Tp, Sp, Dp) are positioned differently in major vs. minor.

# Examples

**Capellen's core critique** (p. 183): In dualistic theory, the "generator" of A minor is E (°e), yet A is perceived as the root. This disconnect between theory and perception makes dualism "impossible and superfluous."

**Riemann's counter** (p. 190): "Any hint of minor being derived in some way from major must be avoided right from the start." He claims from teaching experience that students find the symmetrical construction intuitive.

**Belinfante's function argument** (p. 191): Function labels (T, S, D) should apply in reverse for minor if dualism is consistent. Riemann responds that Belinfante "defines the conditions for consistency with monist, not dualist terms of reference."

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming monism is "correct" and dualism is "wrong"
  **Correction**: Both are theoretical frameworks; dualism captures mathematical symmetries that monism misses, while monism better reflects practical perception

- **Error**: Treating the debate as purely historical
  **Correction**: The monism/dualism tension persists in neo-Riemannian theory's relationship to functional harmony

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Riemann's practical function symbols are monistic, therefore Riemann abandoned dualism
  **Clarification**: As Klumpenhouwer shows (Ch. 6), the function labels describe the same relationships through inversionally related Schritte -- the apparent monism is superficial

- **Confusion**: Neo-Riemannian theory resolved the debate
  **Clarification**: Neo-Riemannian theory sidesteps it by treating inversion as a mathematical operation without acoustic claims, but the underlying tension between harmonic and contrapuntal views of dualism persists (Tymoczko, Ch. 8)
