# Quick Definition

The analytical tension between interpreting harmonic progressions as operating within an extended diatonic framework (where chromatic events are understood as modifications of diatonic norms) versus a chromatic framework (where direct chromatic relationships are autonomous and not reducible to diatonic origins).

# Core Definition

Clark's analyses of Schubert's Lieder (Ch. 10) foreground the tension between **diatonic** and **chromatic** modes of hearing. In a diatonic hearing, chromatic third relations (e.g., C major to Ab major) are understood as modifications or extensions of diatonic relationships, ultimately referrable to a tonic. In a chromatic hearing, such progressions are understood as direct, autonomous relationships that operate outside the diatonic framework — the domain of neo-Riemannian analysis. Clark argues that Riemann's concept of Klangvertretung provides a bridge between these hearings: by tracking how individual tones shift their triadic identity (root, third, or fifth) across chord changes, one can observe the diatonic and chromatic dimensions simultaneously. The changing triadic-functional identities of sustained tones reveal whether a passage maintains diatonic coherence (tones retain identities consistent with a single key) or enters chromatic territory (tones take on identities that cannot be reconciled with any single diatonic collection).

# Prerequisites

- **Tonal imagination**: The active mental process of assigning triadic identities to tones
- **Klangvertretung**: The principle that mediates between diatonic and chromatic hearings

# Key Properties

1. **Not binary**: Passages exist on a continuum between purely diatonic and purely chromatic
2. **Tone-level diagnosis**: Tracking individual tone identities reveals which framework is operative
3. **Klangvertretung as mediator**: Tone representation bridges the two frameworks
4. **Analytical consequence**: Different frameworks yield different analytical results for the same passage
5. **Schubert as test case**: Schubert's music frequently inhabits the boundary between diatonic and chromatic frameworks

# Construction / Recognition

To determine whether a passage invites diatonic or chromatic hearing:
1. Track individual tones across chord changes using Klangvertretung (six possible triadic identities per tone)
2. If tone identities can be reconciled with a single diatonic collection, the passage supports diatonic hearing
3. If tone identities require membership in triads from different diatonic collections, chromatic hearing is indicated
4. Common tones that shift triadic identity without changing pitch are key diagnostic markers

# Context & Application

This concept is central to Clark's analytical argument in Chapter 10. She uses it to show that Schubert's chromatic third relations — a hallmark of his harmonic language — are enriched by attending to tone-level identity shifts rather than analyzing only at the chord level. The concept is relevant for any repertoire where diatonic and chromatic frameworks coexist or compete, including late Beethoven, Schubert, Schumann, Brahms, and Wagner.

# Examples

Clark's analysis of Schubert's "Liedesend" (Ch. 10) demonstrates how a single sustained pitch undergoes triadic-identity transformation across a chromatic modulation. The analysis shows that what appears to be a purely chromatic progression at the chord level maintains a thread of diatonic coherence at the tone level through common-tone retention and Klangvertretung shifts.

Clark also analyzes passages where the "changing triadic-functional identities of tones" in three Schubert Lieder offer "an enriched view of structural and chromatic third relations in the works" (Ch. 10, abstract).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating diatonic and chromatic hearings as mutually exclusive
  **Correction**: Clark shows they can coexist in the same passage, with different analytical layers revealing different frameworks operating simultaneously

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Chromatic hearing means abandoning tonal analysis
  **Clarification**: Neo-Riemannian chromatic analysis provides its own form of coherence through transformational logic; it is an alternative tonal framework, not an abandonment of tonal thinking
