# Quick Definition

The "effect of harmony" in which moments of harmonic change are perceived as metrically accented, serving as Riemann's primary criterion for determining expressed meter based on musical content rather than notation alone.

# Core Definition

Harmoniewirkung ("harmonic effect" or "effect of harmony") is Riemann's principal criterion for expressed meter. From the *Grundriss der Kompositionslehre*: "The more accented a note is, the more one expects it to have a change of harmony; in other words, the moments of time upon which new harmonies preferably enter are the strong points of the motive, group, and phrases" (cited by Caplin, p. 422). Under the expressed-meter perspective, "the listener can recognize the various weights of the tones from the harmonic content" without seeing the score (p. 422). Even-numbered "heavy" measures (*schwere Zeiten*) are "in general the carriers of Harmoniewirkungen" (Riemann, *Vademecum*, cited by Burnham, p. 442).

# Prerequisites

- **Expressed meter** — Harmoniewirkung is the primary criterion within the expressed-meter framework
- **Harmonic function** — Understanding T-S-D relationships is necessary to recognize harmonic change

# Key Properties

1. Harmonic change correlates with metrical accent — moments where harmony changes are "strong points"
2. Operates at multiple hierarchical levels: beat level, measure level, double-measure level
3. The T-S-D-T progression maps naturally onto the metric hierarchy: S on measure 2, D on measure 4 (or similar)
4. When harmonic change operates at a level *above* the harmonic rhythm, it creates "initial accents" — the onset of a new harmony at the measure level creates accent at the beat level
5. Harmoniewirkung is the *primary* expressed-meter criterion; other criteria serve as "assistants"
6. Riemann sometimes treats certain harmonic changes as "passing" or "ornamental" to preserve his a priori model

# Construction / Recognition

## To Apply Harmoniewirkung

1. Identify all points of harmonic change in the passage
2. These points indicate metrically strong positions
3. At the beat level: the beat on which harmony changes is accented
4. At the measure level: the measure in which harmony changes is strong
5. At the double-measure level: the measure pair where a new harmony enters is the strong pair
6. When Harmoniewirkung is ambiguous (static harmony), invoke other expressed-meter criteria

# Context & Application

Harmoniewirkung creates the critical bridge between Riemann's harmonic function theory and his metric theory. The principle that harmonic change creates accent means that the T-S-D-T functional progression directly generates metric structure. This is the connection Caplin identifies as most significant: "Riemann's metric criteria provide a theory of how these [accent and unaccent] structures arise — through the content of the music itself" (p. 422).

**Limitation**: When harmony remains static for multiple measures, Harmoniewirkung cannot determine accent, and Riemann must invoke "assistant" criteria (melodic imitation, durational accent). Riemann also inconsistently applies the principle, sometimes treating harmonic changes as "passing" when they would contradict his Auftakttheorie model.

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 422): T-S-D-T progression across four measures. Measures 2 and 4 are accented: the T-S change on measure 2 creates accent, and the D-T cadence on measure 4 creates the strongest accent.

**Example 2** (p. 430): Beethoven, Op. 31, No. 1, iii. Despite a T-T-D-D progression suggesting accent on measure 3 (where dominant enters), Riemann uses melodic imitation to justify accent on measure 2, preserving his model. This reveals tension between Harmoniewirkung and the a priori scheme.

**Example 3** (p. 423): At the double-measure level: T-D-D-T. Tonic continues into measure 5, showing Harmoniewirkung operating at a level above individual measures.

# Relationships

## Builds Upon
- **Expressed meter** — Harmoniewirkung is the primary expressed-meter criterion
- **Harmonic function** — T-S-D changes are the harmonic events that create Harmoniewirkung

## Enables
- **Auftakttheorie** — Harmoniewirkung on even-numbered measures supports end-accentuation
- **Periodic function** — The metric weight of each periodic function is partly determined by Harmoniewirkung

## Related
- **Schlusswirkung** — A specific type of Harmoniewirkung at cadential points
- **Durational accent** — An assistant criterion when Harmoniewirkung is unavailable

## Contrasts With
- **Motivic imitation in metrics** — Can contradict Harmoniewirkung when they indicate different accentual patterns

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating every harmonic change as creating accent
  **Correction**: Riemann considers some changes "passing" (e.g., a dominant between two tonics may not create Harmoniewirkung)

- **Error**: Applying Harmoniewirkung at only one hierarchical level
  **Correction**: The principle operates at beat, measure, and multi-measure levels simultaneously

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking "harmonic effect" means harmony is dynamically loud
  **Clarification**: Harmoniewirkung refers to the correlation between harmonic change and metric weight, not dynamic level

- **Confusion**: Believing Riemann always follows Harmoniewirkung consistently
  **Clarification**: Riemann sometimes overrides Harmoniewirkung when it conflicts with his Auftakttheorie model, treating inconvenient harmonic changes as "ornamental" or "passing"
