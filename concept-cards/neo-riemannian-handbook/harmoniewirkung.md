# Harmoniewirkung

## Quick Definition

The "effect of harmony" in which harmonic change creates metrical accent, serving as Riemann's primary criterion for determining which beats and measures are metrically strong based on musical content rather than notation alone.

## Formal Definition

**Harmoniewirkung** (German: "harmonic effect" or "effect of harmony") is Riemann's principal criterion for expressed meter, stating that moments of harmonic change are the "strong points of the motive, group, and phrases."

**Core Principle** (from *Grundriss der Kompositionslehre*):
> "The more accented a note is, the more one expects it to have a change of harmony; in other words, the moments of time upon which new harmonies preferably enter are the strong points."

**Two Perspectives**:
1. **Notated-meter view**: Structural units are identified as accented first; harmonies are then "placed" within this framework
2. **Expressed-meter view**: The listener can "recognize the various weights of the tones from the *harmonic content*" without seeing the score

**Application Across Levels**:
- At the **beat level**: Change of harmony on beat 2 makes beat 2 stronger than beat 1
- At the **measure level**: Change of harmony in measure 2 makes measure 2 stronger than measure 1
- At the **double-measure level**: The T-D-D-T progression places accents on measures 2 and 4

**Initial Accents**: When harmonic change operates at a level *above* the harmonic rhythm, it creates what Caplin calls "initial accents"—the onset of a new harmony at the measure level creates accent at the beat level.

## Musical Context

Harmoniewirkung connects Riemann's metric theory to his harmonic function theory:

- Even-numbered "heavy" measures (*schwere Zeiten*) are the typical carriers of harmonic effect
- Harmonic function (T, S, D) interacts with metric function (accent/unaccent)
- Performers can use harmonic content to determine correct metric interpretation

**Limitations Riemann Acknowledged**:
- Not every chord change marks a strong beat (see passing harmonies)
- When harmony remains static for many measures, other criteria must determine accent
- The progression T-T-D-T may not create accent on measure 3 if the D is considered "passing"

**Theoretical Tension**: Riemann sometimes ignores Harmoniewirkung when it conflicts with his *Auftakttheorie* model, treating certain harmonic changes as "passing" or "ornamental" to preserve the a priori scheme.

## Examples

### Basic

**T-S-D-T progression across four measures**:
```
Measure:    1    2    3    4
Harmony:    T    S    D    T
Accent:     U    A    U    A
```
Measures 2 and 4 are accented due to Harmoniewirkung. The T-S change on measure 2 creates accent; the D-T cadence on measure 4 creates the strongest accent.

**T-D-D-T at double-measure level**:
```
Measures:   1-2  3-4
Harmony:    T-D  D-T
Accent:     U    A
```
When tonic continues into measure 5, the entire four-measure unit shows Harmoniewirkung at the double-measure level.

### From Repertoire

**Beethoven, Op. 2, No. 1, ii**: The change of harmony creates initial accents in measures 2 and 4, with bar lines corresponding to these accents.

**Beethoven, Op. 14, No. 1, i**: Similar T-S-D-T pattern demonstrates "dependence of the choice of meter and time signature on the motion of the harmony."

**Beethoven, Op. 31, No. 1, iii**: Despite T-T-D-D progression suggesting accent in measure 3, Riemann uses melodic imitation to justify accent on measure 2, preserving his model.

## Common Confusions

- Not ALL harmonic changes create accent—some are "passing" in Riemann's view
- Harmoniewirkung operates at MULTIPLE hierarchical levels simultaneously
- The principle sometimes conflicts with other expressed-meter criteria (melodic imitation, etc.)
- Riemann's application is inconsistent—he overrides Harmoniewirkung when it conflicts with his model
- "Harmonic effect" doesn't mean harmony is *loud*—it means harmonic change correlates with metric accent
- A "passing dominant" between two tonics may NOT create Harmoniewirkung on the dominant
- The principle works best when harmonic rhythm and metric hierarchy align
