# Quick Definition

Riemann's approach to phrase structure in Brahms's late songs, which combines normative eight-measure periodic analysis with sensitivity to metric, harmonic, and text-driven deviations to reveal "the well-proportioned design and immanent logic in Brahms's works."

# Core Definition

In Berry's account, Riemann's phrase-rhythm analysis of Brahms proceeds from two complementary principles. First, music exhibits an underlying periodic regularity based on the eight-measure period. Second, Brahms's late songs contain "abstruseness of formal structure" whose "sublimely artful effect is beyond question" and whose "inner regularity" must be uncoverable through "detailed analysis" (Berry, p. 464). The analyses begin "from small-scale metric disruptions in the initial measures" and gradually build "integrated interpretations of meter, phrase structure, and salient harmonic detail that illuminate each song as a coherent whole" (Berry, p. 476). Both songs are shown to have opening phrases parsable as "consisting of eight downbeats, the first four in triple meter and the next four in duple" (p. 476).

# Prerequisites

- **Metric freedom in song** — Phrase rhythm analysis depends on identifying where notated and perceived meter diverge

- **Phrasing theory** — Riemann's theory of Auftaktigkeit and hierarchical phrase structure

# Key Properties

1. Analyses proceed from local metric details to global formal interpretation
2. Multiple converging criteria (harmony, melody, text) support phrase-rhythm readings
3. The eight-measure period serves as both norm and analytical goal
4. Metric organization may alternate between duple and triple within a single phrase
5. Chromatic sequences resist phrase-level articulation, creating elongated consequent phrases
6. Re-barring reveals "the structural simplicity" underlying Brahms's complex notation

# Construction / Recognition

## To Analyze Phrase Rhythm in Brahms

1. Identify periodic boundaries using cadential articulations
2. Count downbeats (not measures) to determine phrase length
3. Identify where meter shifts between duple and triple
4. Use harmonic motion to confirm or disconfirm perceived downbeats
5. Check text accent for supporting evidence
6. Map the resulting phrase structure against an eight-measure periodic model

# Context & Application

Berry notes that Riemann's commitment to the eight-measure period leads to "fascinating but highly abstract analyses" where passages are "reconceived in multiple meters without stretching the concept of meter beyond the limits of audibility" (p. 476). The approach is most convincing in portions focusing on "poetic motivations for harmonic and melodic deviations from strict strophic form" (p. 476). Berry also observes that Riemann's quasi-prolongational interpretations serve his metric arguments: harmonic stasis supports specific readings of phrase rhythm (p. 479).

# Examples

**"Immer leiser," Op. 105/2, first period** (pp. 465-466): Riemann parses the opening as an eight-downbeat structure with the first four beats in triple time and the next four in duple. The harmonic stasis across the first three notated measures supports hearing the passage as projecting fewer metrical accents than the barlines suggest.

**"Immer leiser," chromatic sequences** (pp. 467-468): The sequential passages based on successive minor-third motions resist cadential effect, preventing "segmentation into multiple consequents" and creating an elongated consequent that spans multiple metric regions.

**"Das Madchen spricht," Op. 107/3, prelude** (pp. 469-470): The ritornello is "deeply problematic" metrically, standing "independently between the sung stanzas." The antecedent adheres to one meter while the consequent shifts to another.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming Riemann's re-barring eliminates all metrical complexity
  **Correction**: Re-barring reveals different metric levels; complexity remains in the interaction between levels

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Believing Riemann's eight-measure norm is empirically validated
  **Clarification**: It is a theoretical postulate that enables productive analysis but can produce "highly abstract" results when applied rigidly (Berry, p. 476)
