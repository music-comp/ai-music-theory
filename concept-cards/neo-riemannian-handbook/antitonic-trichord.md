# Quick Definition

Harrison's term for the [026] trichord — the three-note core of the dominant seventh chord (root, third, and seventh, omitting the fifth) — which he argues is "the fundamental dominant discord" and the essential bearer of antitonic (non-tonic) functional energy.

# Core Definition

Following Rameau, Harrison argues that the [026] trichord (e.g., B-D#-F in the key of E) is "not only a minimally adequate representation of the dominant seventh chord, but also the fundamental dominant discord" (Harrison, p. 555). Rameau called these notes "dominant (the '2' in the set), major dissonance ('6'), and minor dissonance ('0')" and "mentioned not at all" the chordal fifth (p. 555). Because "all of the [026]s are interpreted as functional mixtures" of D and S components, Harrison substitutes "the adjective 'antitonic' for 'dominant' when describing them" (p. 556). The [026] trichord is the tonal counterpart to the tonic [037] concord: where [037] is consonant and functionally stable, [026] is dissonant and seeks discharge.

# Key Properties

1. Set class [026] — a tritone with an embedded major second
2. The chordal fifth is dispensable: it "does not affect the character and function of the chord"
3. All [026] trichords are functional mixtures of D and S — hence "antitonic" rather than "dominant"
4. Enjoys PLR-like cycle structure: (PL)/(LP) cycles on [026] are possible
5. In chromatic space, [026] is the least unparsimonious trichord after [037] — it has two parsimonious displacements
6. In whole-tone space, [026] becomes fully parsimonious
7. Nontonic chords are "by nature" seventh chords; even a dominant triad is a "feigning consonance"

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify Antitonic Trichords

1. Extract the root, third, and seventh of any dominant-type seventh chord (omit the fifth)
2. The resulting [026] trichord is the antitonic entity
3. Example: G7 (G-B-D-F) -> antitonic trichord G-B-F = [026]
4. In context, identify which of the three discharge types (Da, Db, Dc) connects to the following tonic

# Context & Application

The antitonic trichord concept enables Harrison to bridge neo-Riemannian set-class analysis with Riemannian function theory. By treating [026] as the functional counterpart to [037], he can analyze nonatonic cycles as alternations of tonic and antitonic entities, with PLR-type operations relating members within each class and discharge functions connecting between classes. The concept also opens connections to Kurth's "intensive alteration style" and to the "whole-tone dominant" structures discussed by Horace Alden Miller (1930) (pp. 561-562).

# Examples

**Nonatonic cycle** (p. 554): C+ - Eb7 - Ab+ - B7 - E+ - G7 - C+... The [026] subsets of the "seventh chords" alternate with [037] triads, creating the nonatonic cycle.

**Chromatic intensification** (pp. 560-561): When both raised and lowered fifths are present in an altered dominant, the [02468] pentachord results — an "agglomeration of [026] antitonic trichords" with "characteristic functional energy gain."

**Pitch-class band** (p. 555): Harrison replaces Santa's whole-tone scale bass line with a chromatic one, showing that the "new" notes create additional [026] trichords with the given trichord, intensifying antitonic energy without changing the fundamental functional attitude.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating [026] as merely an "incomplete" seventh chord
  **Correction**: Harrison (following Rameau) argues it is the fundamental discord — the fifth is extra, not the seventh

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Equating "antitonic" with "dominant"
  **Clarification**: "Antitonic" is broader — all three discharge types involve D and S mixtures, not pure dominant function. The term avoids implying a specific root motion (descending fifth).
