# Vorhang

## Quick Definition

A "curtain" or introductory passage in Riemann's periodic analysis that precedes the main period, often fulfilling late periodic functions (6-7-8) to set up the entrance of a proper eight-measure period beginning on function 1.

## Formal Definition

**Vorhang** (German: "curtain") is Riemann's term for introductory material that stands apart from yet connects to the first full period of a movement or theme. The *Vorhang* fulfills periodic functions but represents a kind of prelude or framing device.

**Characteristic Features**:
- Often occupies functions 6-7-8 or some subset
- Creates an "8" function before the main period's "1"
- May be repeated along with the following period
- Represents highly characteristic, attention-grabbing opening gestures

**Analytical Function**: The *Vorhang* allows Riemann to:
- Account for introductory figures that don't fit the eight-measure model
- Explain opening contrasts (e.g., slow introduction + fast theme)
- Incorporate material that establishes tonic before the "real" theme begins
- Handle openings where the first strong gesture seems cadential

**Relationship to Period**: The combination of *Vorhang* + full period creates an extended structure where the cadential functions (6-7-8) appear twice: once in the *Vorhang* and again at the period's conclusion.

## Musical Context

The *Vorhang* concept addresses the observation that many "modern themes" (Mannheim style onward) open with distinctive, contrasting material:

- The "union of a greater number of different motives into a greater unified configuration"
- Opening Allegro themes as prime sites for this new compositional approach
- Characteristic openings that announce the theme's individuality before its periodic structure unfolds

**Tonal Implications**: A *Vorhang* can establish the tonic in a way that the subsequent period can then question or destabilize:
- *Vorhang* cadences firmly in tonic
- Period I may move to dominant or other key
- The *Vorhang* grounds the listener before harmonic exploration begins

## Examples

### Basic

**Vorhang + Period I structure**:
```
Vorhang:    [6  7  8]  | Period I: [1  2  3  4  5  6  7  8]
            curtain      main theme
```
The "8" of the *Vorhang* provides an initial closure; the period then begins fresh with "1."

**Repeated pattern**:
```
Vorhang + Period I:  6-7-8 + 1-2-3-4-5-6-7-8
Vorhang + Period II: 6-7-8 + 1-2-3-4-5-6-7-8a-8b-8c
```
The *Vorhang* and period combination repeats, with Period II extended.

### From Repertoire

**Beethoven, Op. 31, No. 1, i**: The running two-measure figure at the opening serves as *Vorhang* (functions 6-7-8), contrasting with the blocky chordal theme that follows (Period I, functions 1-8). This *Vorhang* + Period combination repeats in Period II (transposed down a step to F major).

**Beethoven, Op. 31, No. 2 ("Tempest")**: The famous Largo/Allegro contrast at the opening is unified as a single period. The Largo functions as *Vorhang* (functions 3-4), leading into the Allegro (functions 5-8). The extreme contrast is "stitched together" analytically.

**Result in Op. 31, No. 1**: By the end of Period II, there have been seven "8" functions—the *Vorhang*'s peremptory tonic cadences multiply the closural functions, creating a distinctively "comic" proliferation of endings.

## Common Confusions

- *Vorhang* is NOT simply an introduction—it specifically fulfills periodic functions
- The term implies that something is being "revealed" or "unveiled" by the curtain
- A *Vorhang* can be substantial or brief, elaborate or simple
- Not all introductory passages qualify as *Vorhang*—they must integrate with the period structure
- The *Vorhang* concept allows Riemann to maintain his 8-measure model for diverse openings
- Multiple *Vorhange* in a movement contribute to the proliferation of "8" functions
