# Periodic Function

## Quick Definition

The numerical designation (1-8) that Riemann assigns to each measure within a period, indicating its metrical weight and position within the eight-measure prototype, which every actual period realizes, expands, contracts, or otherwise transforms.

## Formal Definition

**Periodic functions** are the numbered positions (1 through 8) that Riemann assigns to measures within his prototypical eight-measure period. Each function carries specific metrical weight and structural significance.

**The Eight Functions**:
| Function | Weight | Role | Harmonic Tendency |
|----------|--------|------|-------------------|
| 1 | Weak | Opening upbeat | Often tonic presentation |
| 2 | Strong | First accent | Harmonic change expected |
| 3 | Weak | Upbeat to 4 | Transitional |
| 4 | Strong | Half-phrase end | Often dominant or secondary |
| 5 | Weak | Second half begins | Upbeat to 6 |
| 6 | Strong | Penultimate accent | Often cadential preparation |
| 7 | Weak | Upbeat to 8 | Dominant expected |
| 8 | Strong | INVARIANT goal | Cadential resolution |

**Invariant Feature**: The "8" function is absolutely required—every period must contain this final, goal-oriented accent. The period structure is "left-branching" (everything branches from the final term).

**Non-Mechanical Application**: Riemann allows for:
- **Elisions**: "8=1" where cadence and new beginning coincide
- **Expansions**: Repeated functions (3a, 4a, etc.)
- **Multiple 8s**: Several cadential arrivals (8, 8a, 8b)
- **Back-relating functions**: "5" that relates back to "4" rather than forward to "6"
- **Vorhang** (curtain): Introductory material before the proper period begins

## Musical Context

Periodic functions allow Riemann to parse entire movements as realizations of his prototype:

- Every measure receives a function, even in extended developmental passages
- The functions create "logical necessity" in musical structure
- Deviations from the prototype reveal compositional strategies

**Analytical Utility** (per Burnham):
- Distinguishes "essential stations" from "inserted or appended" material
- Harmonic progression = "evolving musical argument" determining primary stations
- Harmonic stasis = "confirmation or filler" within the structure

**Performer's Guide**: Functions help performers understand phrase rhythm and communicate structure to listeners who cannot see the score.

## Examples

### Basic

**Standard T-S-D-T realization**:
```
Function:  1    2    3    4    5    6    7    8
Harmony:   T    S    D    T    T    S    D    T
           weak strong weak strong weak strong weak strong
```

**Period with elision**:
```
Function: ...6   7   8=1   2   3   4   5   6   7   8
          [old period ends] [new period begins]
          The cadential 8 simultaneously starts the new period as 1
```

### From Repertoire

**Beethoven, Op. 31, No. 1, opening**: The *Vorhang* (mm. 1-2) fulfills functions 6-7-8, then Period I begins fresh with functions 1-8. The combination is repeated in Period II, generating seven "8" functions by the end of Period II—a "comic tic" of the movement.

**Beethoven, Op. 31, No. 3, Period IV**: Contains 3-4, 3a-4a, 5-6, 5a-6a, 7-8, 8a. The repeated functions (3a-4a, 5a-6a) extend without disrupting the underlying prototype.

**Beethoven, Op. 31, No. 1, Period III**: Shows "about-face" (*Umwenden*) at m. 36, where an "8" function is retrospectively heard as "7." The 8-9 back-relating pairs become 4-5 pairs in the recapitulation, showing functional equivalence.

## Common Confusions

- Function numbers do NOT always equal measure numbers in the score
- Multiple "8" functions in one passage is analytically meaningful, not an error
- "Incomplete" periods are always versions of 5-8, never 1-4 (you can't lop off the trunk)
- Back-relating functions (9 relating to 8, or 5 relating to 4) are legitimate analytical moves
- Functions are assigned through a combination of a priori model AND expressed-meter criteria
- Riemann's ad hoc adjustments (like reinterpreting 4-5 as 5-6) reveal tensions in his system
- The functions mix "empirical and theoretical evidence" in Riemann's analyses
