# Tonvorstellung (Tone Imagination/Representation)

## Quick Definition

The mental representation or imagination of tones and their relationships, which for Riemann constitutes the true foundation of music theory rather than acoustic physics or sensory physiology.

## Formal Definition

**Tonvorstellung** (literally "tone-representation" or "tone-imagination") is Riemann's mature concept describing how musical tones exist in the mind:

1. **Not physical sound**: Beyond acoustics (*Physik*)
2. **Not sensation**: Beyond physiological stimulus (*Empfindung*)
3. **Mental representation**: Psychological/logical activity (*Vorstellung*)

Riemann's key distinction (from "Nature of Harmony"):
> "If listening to music is a *selecting-out from chordal material presented to the ear* according to simple principles..., then it is no longer a physical suffering (*ein physisches Erleiden*), but rather a *logical activity.* It is precisely a matter of *representing* (*Vorstellen*), a uniting, separating, comparing, relating-to-one-another of representations."

The term becomes central in Riemann's late work "Ideen zu einer 'Lehre von den Tonvorstellungen'" (1914).

## Historical Origin

### The Empfindung-Vorstellung Distinction

This distinction has deep roots in German philosophy:
- **Empfindung** (sensation): Raw sensory input, passive reception
- **Vorstellung** (representation): Active mental processing, conceptualization

### Helmholtz's Position

Helmholtz emphasized *Empfindung*:
- Sought to break down perception into elementary sensations
- Defamiliarization of musical elements
- Skeptical of *Vorstellung* as "merely practical truth"

Helmholtz divided hearing into:
1. **Material ear** (*leibliches Ohr*): Physiological sensation
2. **Mental ear** (*geistiges Ohr*): Psychological representation

### Riemann's Inversion

Riemann inverted Helmholtz's priorities:
- *Empfindung* = "physical suffering," passive
- *Vorstellung* = "logical activity," active
- True musical understanding lies in representation, not sensation

As Steege notes: "Where Helmholtz had been concerned to reveal the inner workings of tone in infinitesimal detail—almost for the sake of detail itself—Riemann aims to construct a kind of parallel universe to the raw sensory interventions of Helmholtzian listening."

### Late Development

In "Ideen zu einer 'Lehre von den Tonvorstellungen'" (1914), Riemann made *Tonvorstellung* foundational:
> "The alpha and omega of music as art lies not in music as it sounds but rather in the mental representation (*Vorstellung*) of tone relations, which lives in the musical imagination (*Tonphantasie*) of the creative artist prior to its inscription in musical notation and subsequent re-emergence in the imagination of the hearer."

## Musical Context

### Theory of Harmony as Psychology

Riemann's shift repositioned the entire discipline:
> "Through this most recent progress of scientific knowledge, the theory of harmony has developed from a theory of the mathematical ratios of musical intervals into a theory of tone representations and their concatenation."

Music theory's domain is no longer physics or physiology but *psychology*.

### What Tonvorstellung Captures

1. **Functional meaning**: Hearing G-B-D as "dominant in C major"
2. **Tonal hierarchy**: Mental prioritization of tonic
3. **Expected resolutions**: Anticipation of harmonic motion
4. **Klang representation**: Single tone implying complete triad

### Relation to Frege

Hyer draws parallels between Riemann's *Vorstellung* and Frege's logic:

| Frege | Riemann |
|-------|---------|
| Concept expression | Tonal function |
| Sense (*Sinn*) | Harmonic meaning |
| Reference (*Bedeutung*) | Chord as sonic event |
| Thought (*Gedanke*) | Musical proposition |

But Riemann differs from Frege: Where Frege purged psychology from logic, Riemann's late work "verges on full-blown psychologism."

### Noumena and Phenomena

Riemann (per Rehding): Tonal functions are "the harmonic noumena behind the sounding phenomena"—the underlying mental forms through which we perceive music.

## Examples

### Basic Application

Hearing a single tone E:
- **As Empfindung**: Frequency 329.63 Hz, timbre characteristics
- **As Vorstellung**: Third of C major, root of E major, fifth of A major/minor, etc.

The same physical stimulus generates different *Tonvorstellungen* depending on context.

### Compositional Communication

> "Both the registration of artistic creation in musical notation and the actual realization of the work in performance are but means of transplanting a musical experience from the imagination (*Phantasie*) of the composer into that of the musical listener."

Notation and sound are *media*; the true musical content is *Tonvorstellung*.

### Analytical Implication

Function symbols (T, D, S) represent not sounds but *Tonvorstellungen*:
- Each symbol is an "assertion of functional equivalence"
- Analysis documents the listener's "continual acts of judgment concerning the harmonic truth of the music"

## Evolution/Variants

### Helmholtz to Riemann

| Helmholtz | Riemann |
|-----------|---------|
| Sensation primary | Representation primary |
| Defamiliarization valued | Consolidation valued |
| Analysis into elements | Synthesis into wholes |
| Skeptical of representation | Celebrates representation |

### Riemann's Personal Evolution

1. **Early (1870s-80s)**: Acoustic/physical justification sought
2. **Middle**: Integration of psychology with acoustics
3. **Late (1910s)**: Full psychological foundation; "Lehre von den Tonvorstellungen"

### Translation Issues

*Vorstellung* can mean:
- Representation
- Imagination
- Presentation
- Mental image
- Idea

Wason and Marvin's standard translation "imagination of tone" captures creative/active dimension, but "representation" better fits philosophical context.

## Common Confusions

1. **Vorstellung vs. Empfindung**: Vorstellung is active/mental, Empfindung is passive/physical
2. **Imagination vs. Fantasy**: Tonvorstellung includes but exceeds "creative imagination"
3. **Psychological vs. Psychologistic**: Riemann's theory risks reducing logic to psychology (which Frege rejected)
4. **Inner vs. Outer music**: The "real" music for Riemann exists in the mind, not in sound
5. **Objectivity**: Despite being mental, Tonvorstellung is meant to be *objective*—shareable between minds
