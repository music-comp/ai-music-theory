# Quick Definition

The mental representation or imagination of tones and their relationships, which for Riemann constitutes the true foundation of music theory rather than acoustic physics or sensory physiology. Riemann's mature concept positing that musical hearing is active, selective, and logical rather than passive reception of sound.

# Core Definition

**Tonvorstellung** (literally "tone-representation" or "tone-imagination") is Riemann's concept describing how musical tones exist in the mind. The term becomes central in his late work "Ideen zu einer 'Lehre von den Tonvorstellungen'" (1914), but it appears already in his early *Musikalische Syntaxis* (1877), where Riemann writes:

"If listening to music is a *selecting-out from chordal material presented to the ear* according to simple principles, then it is no longer a physical suffering (*ein physisches Erleiden*), but rather a *logical activity.* It is precisely a matter of *representing* (*Vorstellen*), a uniting, separating, comparing, relating-to-one-another of representations... — *Tonvorstellungen*" (Ch. 2, n. 17, from *Musikalische Syntaxis*, viii).

In the 1914 "Ideen," Riemann made this foundational: "The alpha and omega of music as art lies not in music as it sounds but rather in the mental representation (*Vorstellung*) of tone relations, which lives in the musical imagination (*Tonphantasie*) of the creative artist prior to its inscription in musical notation and subsequent re-emergence in the imagination of the hearer" (Ch. 3, n. 138).

The concept marks Riemann's decisive shift from physical-acoustic to psychological foundations. As Steege translates from "Die Natur der Harmonik": "Through this most recent progress of scientific knowledge, the theory of harmony has developed from a theory of the mathematical ratios of musical intervals into a theory of tone representations and their concatenation" (Ch. 2, p. 83).

# Key Properties

1. Musical hearing is active and selective, not passive reception
2. The "real" music exists in the mind, not in sound — notation and performance are media for transmitting Tonvorstellungen between minds
3. Tonvorstellungen are objective (shareable between minds), not merely subjective
4. The mind seeks "greatest simplicity" and "highest possible economy" in organizing tone relationships
5. A single acoustic stimulus generates different Tonvorstellungen depending on musical context
6. The German *Vorstellung* ranges across "idea," "conception," "presentation," "representation," "imagination" — no single English translation captures it

# Construction / Recognition

## To Understand What Tonvorstellung Does
1. Distinguish the acoustic event (Empfindung) from the mental representation (Vorstellung)
2. Recognize that the listener actively organizes, compares, and relates tones
3. The "empty" representation of an isolated tone is enriched by hearing it as part of a Klang
4. The Klang-representation is further enriched by hearing it within a key (as T, D, or S)
5. Function symbols (T, D, S) represent not sounds but Tonvorstellungen

## Translation Issue
- Wason and Marvin's standard translation "imagination of tone" captures the creative/active dimension
- "Representation" better fits the philosophical context (neo-Kantian tradition)
- Steege (n. 36) notes that "in the present context, early on in Riemann's thinking, the concept of 'representation' seems to capture better Riemann's ideas"

# Context & Application

The Empfindung/Vorstellung distinction has deep roots in German philosophy (see empfindung-vorstellung card). Helmholtz divided hearing into the "material ear" (*leibliches Ohr*) and the "mental ear" (*geistiges Ohr*) (Ch. 2, n. 16). Riemann inverted Helmholtz's priorities: where Helmholtz sought to break perception down into elementary sensations, Riemann insisted that musical understanding lies in the active synthesis — the Vorstellung, not the Empfindung.

Hyer (Ch. 3) draws parallels between Riemann's Tonvorstellung and Frege's logic, noting both the similarities and a crucial divergence: where Frege purged psychology from logic (anti-psychologism), "Riemann's late work verges on full-blown psychologism" (Ch. 3, p. 129). Function symbols become "assertions of functional equivalence" documenting the listener's "continual acts of judgment concerning the harmonic truth of the music."

The practical consequence: consonance and dissonance are redefined as relational rather than acoustic. "We know today that there is no absolute consonance, that even a chord which is, according to physical and physiological formulations, the most undisturbed and euphonious can be a dissonance musically (for example, the six-four chord)" (Ch. 2, p. 83).

# Examples

**Example 1** (Ch. 2, p. 84): "As long as a tone is not determined according to the significance of its Klang, we have the simplest musical representation of tone; the conception of tone is empty and unsatisfying." The isolated tone is a bare Tonvorstellung; in context, it acquires rich meaning.

**Example 2** (Ch. 3, n. 138): "Both the registration of artistic creation in musical notation and the actual realization of the work in performance are but means of transplanting a musical experience from the imagination (*Phantasie*) of the composer into that of the musical listener." Notation and sound are media; the true musical content is Tonvorstellung.

**Example 3** (Ch. 2, p. 84): Hearing E in different contexts: as Empfindung, it is a frequency of 329.63 Hz. As Tonvorstellung, it might be the third of C major, the root of E major, the fifth of A major — different mental representations of the same physical stimulus.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Translating Tonvorstellung with a single consistent English word
  **Correction**: The German ranges across "imagination," "representation," "idea," "conception" — different translations suit different contexts

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking Tonvorstellung means "creative imagination" in the artistic sense
  **Clarification**: While it includes imagination, *Vorstellung* is a philosophical-psychological term for mental representation more broadly — how the mind conceives of tones

- **Confusion**: Believing Tonvorstellung makes harmonic theory subjective or relativistic
  **Clarification**: Riemann still believed in "correct" hearings; the mind seeks simplest, most economical interpretations. Tonvorstellungen are meant to be objective (shareable between minds).

- **Confusion**: Confusing Riemann's psychologism with Frege's anti-psychologism
  **Clarification**: Despite parallels in their logic, Riemann grounded truth in psychological acts while Frege insisted on separating logic from psychology. Hyer calls this "the crux of the problem" (Ch. 3, p. 129).
