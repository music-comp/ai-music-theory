# Quick Definition

Rehding's methodological concept that dualistic analysis requires adopting a specific listening perspective -- one that attends to subdominant-dominant symmetry and dialectical synthesis -- recognizing that this perspective is a chosen analytical framework rather than a claim about natural perception.

# Core Definition

"What does it mean, putting it somewhat fancifully, to 'hear with dualistic ears'?" (Ch. 7, p. 239). Rehding acknowledges that "the first and most powerful criticism of the dualists had always been that we do not hear music in this way." His response: the analysis assumes "contrariwise, that the dualists take their fundamental theoretical position seriously -- more so, perhaps, than they themselves dared to do at the time -- and draws out the final consequences."

The concept involves triangulation between theory, repertoire, and analytical position. "Does the Clarinet Trio need dualistic theory (to readjust its position in the chamber-music canon) more than the theory needs the piece (to valorize its claims)?" (p. 239). Theory and music mutually illuminate each other, but always through the analyst's chosen perspective.

# Key Properties

1. Dualistic hearing is a chosen analytical perspective, not a claim about natural perception
2. It attends to subdominant-dominant balance, not just dominant-tonic resolution
3. Moments uninteresting to monist hearing (e.g., subdominant passages in the coda) become significant
4. The perspective reveals formal structures "otherwise inaccessible" (p. 239)
5. Theory, repertoire, and analytical position must be "triangulated" -- none determines the others

# Construction / Recognition

## Adopting Dualistic Ears
1. Attend to both dominant-side AND subdominant-side harmonic motion
2. Track whether both sides of the tonic have been explored
3. Notice moments where subdominant function is fully articulated (characteristic dissonances)
4. Assess whether dialectical synthesis is achieved
5. Compare what this perspective reveals with what monist hearing reveals

## What Changes
- Monist hearing: Recap achieves synthesis by returning to tonic; coda is supplementary
- Dualist hearing: Coda achieves synthesis by completing subdominant exploration; the recap alone is insufficient

# Context & Application

Rehding frames the entire dualistic forms project as "utopian thinking" -- an exercise in "what could have been." This connects to broader questions about the relationship between theory and analysis: theory provides a lens, and different lenses reveal different structures.

The concept resonates with Klumpenhouwer's (Ch. 6) insistence that dualist analysis operates within a chosen "experiential world" -- one that is "regulated and organized along dualist lines" (p. 214). Both authors reject the idea that there is one correct way to hear tonal music.

# Examples

**The D minor moment in Brahms** (p. 236): "Of no particular interest in a conventional reading," the D minor with characteristic dissonance in the coda is "the moment of dualistic epiphany where everything falls into place." Different ears, different significance.

**Example 7.19** (p. 237): Rehding juxtaposes monistic and dualistic hearings: "the conventional version would pay more attention to the 'synthesis' of the diverse key areas... whereas for the dualists the downward move from A minor to D minor would represent the actual synthesis."

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating dualistic hearing as the "correct" way to hear
  **Correction**: It is one analytical perspective among others; Rehding explicitly frames it as hypothetical and utopian

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: If we don't naturally hear dualistically, the analysis is invalid
  **Clarification**: Rehding argues that analytical perspectives are chosen, not given; the value lies in what the perspective reveals, not in whether it matches naive listening
