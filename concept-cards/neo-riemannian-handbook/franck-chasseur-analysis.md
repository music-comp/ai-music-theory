# Quick Definition

Cook's sustained analysis of Franck's symphonic poem *Le chasseur maudit*, which compares contrapuntal, functional, and contextual-transformational approaches to demonstrate how a contextual model built on major-third pairs and the X/Y group captures what traditional analyses miss.

# Core Definition

Cook analyzes Franck's *Le chasseur maudit* ("The Accursed Huntsman," after Burger's ballade) as "an extended and elegant reflection on the potential and limitations of various analytical frameworks" (abstract, p. 513). The analysis proceeds through three approaches: (1) a contrapuntal (Schenkerian-inspired) reading that identifies F# as the crucial bass-line pivot; (2) a Riemannian functional reading that reveals parallel between local third pairs and global endpoints; and (3) a contextual transformational reading using the X/Y group that models the music's circumnavigation of triadic space (pp. 514-542). The program of the piece — a count damned for blasphemous hunting on the Sabbath — provides figurative language to animate the technical analysis.

# Key Properties

1. Three analytical approaches compared: contrapuntal, functional, contextual-transformational
2. Each approach has strengths and limitations; none is complete alone
3. The X/Y group models major-third-pair nodes with mode-switching and mode-preserving operations
4. The program (Burger's tale) serves as figurative language, not as analytical justification
5. The piece eventually completes a full circumnavigation of the triadic space
6. Large-scale major-third transposition structures the piece (G minor -> B minor -> Eb major/minor)
7. The riding music is diatonic and tonally stable; the warnings are chromatic and mobile

# Construction / Recognition

## To Follow Cook's Analytical Method

1. Begin with contrapuntal analysis: identify voice-leading framework and pivotal bass notes
2. Apply functional analysis: label harmonic functions and identify the structural problem (dual nature of triads)
3. Identify the salient contextual feature: major-third pairs as basic unit of motion
4. Construct the X/Y group from directed voice-leading sums
5. Trace the music's path through the transformational network
6. Use programmatic language to narrate the path
7. Acknowledge what the transformational model excludes

# Context & Application

The analysis demonstrates Cook's claim that chromatic music "often works in the seams between traditional diatonic hierarchies and a more fluid syntax of (only) locally intelligible chord-to-chord relations" (p. 532). The three approaches are not competing; the contrapuntal and functional analyses "are as much part of the analytical picture we paint...as the transformational analysis" (p. 531).

# Examples

**Mm. 129-140, the first warning** (pp. 524-531): The passage that drives the entire analysis. Cook shows how contrapuntal analysis identifies F# as pivot, functional analysis reveals third-pair parallels, and the X/Y group models the Y3 circumnavigation arrested by X4.

**Mm. 273-346, the final ride** (pp. 534-542): Three minor-third ascents (Y9) drive through the system. E-flat minor's appearance "looses the demons" and opens the way for complete circumnavigation. "The hunter is damned."

**The riding music vs. warnings** (p. 534): "The riding music is generally diatonic and tonally stable, while the good knight's pleas and the sudden quiet before damnation are chromatic and in nearly constant motion."

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Using the program to justify analytical claims
  **Correction**: Cook is explicit: "We do not assert that the music retells Burger's tale. Instead, we tell the story of the music *through* Burger's tale" (p. 533)

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the contextual analysis replaces the contrapuntal and functional ones
  **Clarification**: All three remain part of the analytical picture; the contextual model captures what the others miss, while they capture what it misses
