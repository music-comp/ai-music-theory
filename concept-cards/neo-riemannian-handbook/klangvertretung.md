# Klangvertretung (Tone Representation)

## Quick Definition

The principle that individual tones are understood not in isolation but as representatives of complete Klange (triads), and that subsidiary chords represent the principal harmonies (T, D, S) to which they relate.

## Formal Definition

**Klangvertretung** ("Klang representation" or "tone representation") is Riemann's concept that:

1. **Tone level**: Each tone can be understood as representing one of six possible Klange (three major, three minor) depending on whether it functions as root, third, or fifth
2. **Chord level**: Secondary chords (parallels, leading-tone changes) represent the principal functions they relate to
3. **Key level**: Related keys represent the principal key in larger formal contexts

Riemann's definition from the *Musik-Lexikon*:
> "Klangvertretung is the particular meaning that a tone or interval acquires depending on which Klang it is understood to represent."

**Formal Properties**:
- Any tone E can represent:
  - E over-Klang (E-G#-B) as root
  - A over-Klang (A-C#-E) as fifth
  - C over-Klang (C-E-G) as third
  - E under-Klang (A-C-E) as "root" (dualistic)
  - B under-Klang (E-G-B) as fifth
  - G# under-Klang (C#-E-G#) as third

## Historical Origin

### Development

The concept has precedents but was systematized by Riemann:

1. **Helmholtz**: First explicitly stated that "we conceive tones as representatives of Klange," though limited to major (over-Klange)
2. **Oettingen (1866)**: Extended Helmholtz's concept to include minor Klange, giving it "unparalleled breadth"
3. **Riemann**: Integrated Klangvertretung into comprehensive function theory

### Significance

As Steege's translation of Riemann states:
> "The principle of Klang representation no longer belongs within physics, nor within physiology, but rather in psychology. If experience shows that we are able to understand a tone as the representative of a minor chord just as easily as we can understand it as the representative of a major chord (without one Klang or the other actually sounding), then this is a scientific fact on which we can build."

This shift from acoustic to psychological grounding was crucial for Riemann's mature theory.

## Musical Context

### Three Levels of Representation

**1. Tone-to-Klang**:
A single melodic tone implies a complete harmonic context. Hearing a melody note as ^5 implies tonic Klang even when no chord sounds.

**2. Chord-to-Function**:
"Side-triads" (Nebendreiklange) represent principal functions:
- Tp (tonic parallel): A-C-E represents C-E-G in C major
- Dp (dominant parallel): E-G-B represents G-B-D
- Sp (subdominant parallel): D-F-A represents F-A-C

**3. Key-to-Key**:
In a C major composition, the key of G major represents the dominant function at a larger scale.

### Scheinkonsonanz (Apparent Consonance)

Representative chords create "apparent consonances" - they sound consonant but function as dissonances because they represent (and differ from) the principal Klang:

> "The consonant chord as such is not yet entirely able to conclude a piece of music; this consonant chord must rather be the tonic, if it is really to have a conclusive effect."

Even the dominant triad in C major "is not a perfect consonance" because our representation includes its relation to the tonic.

### Practical Applications

Klangvertretung enables:
1. **Extended tonality**: Chromatic chords understood within tonal framework
2. **Reductive analysis**: Complex passages reduced to fundamental Klange
3. **Harmonic economy**: All harmony reducible to T, D, S representations

## Examples

### Basic

In C major, the chord A-C-E (vi or Tp):
- **As Stellvertreter**: Represents tonic function (shares C and E with C major triad)
- **As Scheinkonsonanz**: Sounds consonant but is functionally "dissonant" in relation to true tonic
- **Derivation**: Fifth of tonic replaced by sixth (C→A)

### Analytical Application

Schreyer's analysis of Beethoven's Fifth Symphony transition:
- 50 measures reduced to single prolonged dominant Klang
- Intermediate harmonies are "parenthetical" representations
- Final reduction: T-D-T motion

### From Riemann's "Nature of Harmony"

> "If I think to myself of a G-major chord in the context of the key of C major, then I think of it as the Klang of the over-fifth of the C-major Klang; that is, the C-major chord itself enters the mental representation as the Klang by which the significance of the G-major chord is determined—the center of the mental representation lies outside it."

## Evolution/Variants

### Riemann vs. Successors

| Aspect | Riemann | Post-Riemann |
|--------|---------|--------------|
| Derivation | Strictly dualistic | Often monistic |
| Parallels | Polar (above in major, below in minor) | Sometimes diatonic only |
| Scheinkonsonanz | All representatives | Selective application |
| Notation | Klangschluessel | Function symbols only |

### Louis's Modification

Rudolf Louis replaced "apparent consonance" with "conceptual dissonance" (*Auffassungsdissonanz*):
- Apparent consonances: sound consonant, are structurally dissonant
- Conceptual dissonances: sound dissonant in context, look consonant structurally

This phenomenological inversion emphasized perception over theoretical derivation.

### Modern Applications

Neo-Riemannian theory largely abandons Klangvertretung's hierarchical dimension, treating all triads as equal nodes in transformational networks rather than representatives of privileged functions.

## Related Concepts

- **Prerequisites**: Klang, harmonic-function, consonance, dissonance
- **Leads to**: Parallelklang, Leittonwechselklang, Scheinkonsonanz, modulation
- **See also**: Stellvertreter, apparent-consonance, Tonvorstellung

## Common Confusions

1. **Representation vs. Substitution**: Klangvertretung implies the representative *is* the function in modified form, not merely replaces it
2. **Physical vs. Psychological**: Later Riemann grounded this in psychology, not acoustics
3. **Dualistic implication**: In strict Riemannian theory, parallels in major lie *above* (vi for I), in minor lie *below* (III for i) - this polar derivation persists even in "monistic" modern function theory
4. **Hierarchy**: Not all representations are equal; primary functions (T, D, S) remain conceptually central
