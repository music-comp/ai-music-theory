# Combinatorial Group Theory in Harmony

## Quick Definition

The mathematical framework treating harmonic relationships as elements of algebraic groups generated by fundamental operations, where all possible relationships arise as combinations ("words") of a few basic transformations.

## Formal Definition

**Combinatorial group theory** describes groups in terms of:
1. **Generators**: A set of basic elements from which all others derive
2. **Relators**: Equations constraining how generators combine
3. **Words**: Sequences of generators representing group elements
4. **Presentations**: Complete specifications ⟨generators | relators⟩

### Group Presentation Notation
⟨g₁, g₂, ... gₙ | r₁, r₂, ... rₘ⟩

where:
- g₁, g₂, ... are generators
- r₁, r₂, ... are relators (equations that equal identity)

### Example: PLR Group
⟨L, P, R | L², P², R², (LP)³, (PR)⁴, (LR)¹²⟩

This says:
- L, P, R are generators
- Each is self-inverse (squares to identity)
- LP has order 3 (hexatonic cycle)
- PR has order 4 (octatonic cycle)
- LR has order 12 (chromatic cycle)

## Historical Context

### Mid-19th Century Origins
Engebretsen traces a combinatorial tradition including:
- **Hauptmann** (1853): Dialectical derivation of relationships
- **Oettingen** (1866): Systematic interval combinations
- **Hostinsky** (1879): Comprehensive relationship catalogs

### Key Insight
All theorists sought to:
1. Identify directly intelligible relationships
2. Build complex relationships from simple ones
3. Classify all possibilities systematically

### Not Explicit Group Theory
These theorists predated formal group theory (emerging simultaneously in mathematics). They worked combinatorially without the modern algebraic vocabulary.

## Generators and Coherence

### The "Bearers of Coherence"
Generators were conceived as **directly intelligible** relationships:
- Quintschritt (fifth motion): Acoustically privileged
- Terzschritt (third motion): Consonant interval
- Seitenwechsel (mode change): Minimal transformation

Other relationships were "intelligible" only through **mediation**:
- Ganztonschritt (whole-tone) = two fifths
- Tritonusschritt = complex derivation

### Coherence Principle
Progressions are coherent when:
- Derivable from generators
- Shorter derivations = more coherent
- Generators ground the system's logic

## Word Length and Distance

### Defining Distance
"Word length" = number of generator symbols needed

Examples in ⟨Q, T, ⊕⟩ system:
- Quintschritt: Q (length 1)
- Terzschritt: T (length 1)
- Ganztonschritt: Q² (length 2)
- Tritonuswechsel: Q³T⊕ (length 5)

### Analytical Meaning
Shorter words = closer relationship
- Adjacent fifths are "close"
- Tritone relations are "distant"
- This formalizes intuitions about harmonic proximity

### Caveats
Word length depends on choice of generators:
- Different generators yield different distances
- PLR emphasizes voice-leading distance
- S/W emphasizes root-interval distance

## Spatial Interpretation

### Cayley Graphs
Groups can be visualized as graphs:
- Nodes = group elements
- Edges = generator applications
- Path length = word length

### Tonnetz as Cayley Graph
The Tonnetz approximates a Cayley graph for harmonic relationships:
- Generators Q and T create the lattice
- Wechsel operations flip between layers
- Paths represent transformational derivations

### Word Length = Path Length
Moving from one triad to another:
- Count generator edges traversed
- This equals the word length of the transformation
- Geometric distance reflects combinatorial distance

## Neo-Riemannian Revival

### What Cohn and Others Recovered
Neo-Riemannian theory made explicit:
- The group structure implicit in 19th-century theory
- Formal presentations with generators and relators
- Connections between combinatorial and geometric models

### What They Changed
- Emphasized voice-leading generators (P, L, R) over acoustic (Q, T)
- Adopted equal temperament (finite groups)
- Focused on cycles and symmetries

## Engebretsen's Contribution

### Documenting the Tradition
Nora Engebretsen's dissertation (*The Chaos of Possibilities*, 2002) traced:
- Pre-Riemannian combinatorial thinking
- Development of generator-based systems
- Connection to formal group theory

### The "Chaos of Possibilities"
Oettingen's phrase for the problem:
- Generators can combine infinitely
- All chords can connect to all others
- How to constrain the "chaos"?

Riemann's solution: Function theory as constraint.
Neo-Riemannian solution: Embrace structural logic.

## Common Confusions

- **Generators are not unique**: Different presentations describe the same group
- **Relators are not optional**: They constrain how generators interact
- **Historical theorists weren't group theorists**: They thought combinatorially without the formalism
