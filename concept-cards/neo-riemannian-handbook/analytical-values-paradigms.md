# Quick Definition

The divergent ideological assumptions underlying Riemannian and neo-Riemannian analysis: Riemann valued order, conformance to universal tonal laws, and containment of chromaticism; neo-Riemannians value crisis, disruption, tonal disunity, and voice-leading coherence independent of tonal center.

# Core Definition

Rings argues that the central differences between Riemannian and neo-Riemannian analysis are not primarily technical but ideological. Riemann's analysis demonstrates "relentless normativity," using theory as "a bastion against historical change" to show that even the most chromatic passages conform to universal tonal laws (Rings, p. 493). Neo-Riemannian analysis, by contrast, begins with "the most remarkable sounding passages" and "areas of tonal strain," valorizing disruption and seeking voice-leading coherence where tonal coherence seems absent (pp. 494-495). The choice between approaches reveals "what we value in music — and in musical analysis (the two are not necessarily the same)" (p. 495).

# Key Properties

1. Technical differences between paradigms mask deeper ethical and ideological divergences
2. The same passage can be analytically constructed to show either conformance or crisis
3. Riemann's values: order, universality, monotonality, pedagogical utility
4. Neo-Riemannian values: the remarkable, disruption, voice-leading parsimony, individual works
5. Both paradigms share "pan-Riemannian" values: drive toward systematization, logical rigor, elegant symmetry, detection of order
6. Historical contexts shape analytical values: Riemann feared tonal dissolution; neo-Riemannians emerged after atonality was institutionalized

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify Analytical Values in an Analysis

1. Note the starting point: Does the analysis begin with normative passages or exceptional ones?
2. Identify what counts as success: Is it showing conformance or revealing disruption?
3. Examine the tonic: Is it treated as controlling or as absent/attenuated?
4. Check the vocabulary: Does it emphasize "order" and "confirmation" or "crisis" and "disunity"?
5. Consider the historical position of the analyst

# Context & Application

Rings uses Schubert's Impromptu in G-flat, D. 899, no. 3, as his case study. Riemann analyzed this piece in *Musikalische Syntaxis* (1877), finding it "a formally quite lucidly organized composition" governed by tonic control. Neo-Riemannian analysis (inspired by Cohn) would instead begin with the extraordinary passage at mm. 78-82, exploring its voice-leading logic within a Weitzmann region. The contrast between these readings exposes the divergent values at work (pp. 488-498).

# Examples

**Riemann on Schubert Impromptu** (p. 492): The piece is a "masterpiece" in which the "controlling force of the main tonic" is paramount. The chromatically extreme passage at m. 80 receives only brief mention as one of several progressions that "consolidate the tonic" with "wholly excellent effect."

**Neo-Riemannian analysis of the same passage** (pp. 494-496): The passage at mm. 78-82 would be the "first point of analytical entry," with the G-minor chord analyzed within a Weitzmann region sharing common tones with an augmented triad. The analysis reveals voice-leading coherence but does not capture the sound of extremity.

**Riemann's Garden of Eden metaphor** (p. 493): Riemann's description of the harmonic system as a "splendid Garden of Eden" reveals his conviction that compositional possibilities exist within bounded, known terrain — the analyst's job is to map the paths, not transgress the boundaries.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating the choice between paradigms as purely technical
  **Correction**: The choice reflects what we value in music and what questions we ask of it

- **Error**: Assuming neo-Riemannian analysis captures the sound of disruption
  **Correction**: Rings notes the "fact/value gap" — the analysis reveals coherence, not the remarkable sound that motivated the analysis

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Believing one paradigm is simply "better" than the other
  **Clarification**: Both paradigms have strengths; Rings advocates recognizing their complementary values and working toward reconciliation

- **Confusion**: Thinking neo-Riemannian theory is purely disruptive
  **Clarification**: Neo-Riemannian analysis also seeks order — specifically voice-leading coherence — making it share the pan-Riemannian drive toward systematization
