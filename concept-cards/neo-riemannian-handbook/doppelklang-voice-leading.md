# Quick Definition

The application of parsimonious voice-leading principles to Doppelklänge (dissonant chords understood as triadic combinations), where transformational operations act on embedded triadic subsets to generate voice-leading networks among tetrachordal sonorities, including the DOUTH2 relation.

# Core Definition

Doppelklang voice leading extends neo-Riemannian transformational analysis from consonant triads to dissonant sonorities by treating Doppelklänge as containing embedded triadic subsets on which transformational operations can act. Gollin demonstrates that operations targeting specific triadic components within a tetrachord generate parsimonious voice-leading connections analogous to P, L, R operations on triads. The DOUTH2 relation (from Douthett and Steinbach) connects chords where two tones remain fixed and two move by parallel semitones, creating "parsimonious voice-leading networks" among tetrachordal sonorities (p. 396ff). This approach enables analysis of voice-leading parsimony in music featuring seventh chords, major-seventh chords, and other four-note sonorities — particularly early twentieth-century repertoire by Still and Ravel.

# Construction / Recognition

## To Analyze Doppelklang Voice Leading

1. Identify the dissonant sonority and its set class (e.g., [0148])
2. Decompose into two embedded triads (the Doppelklang analysis)
3. Apply transformational operations to one or both embedded triads
4. Track which tones remain fixed (common tones) and which move (voice leading)
5. Map the resulting connections to form a voice-leading network
6. Check for DOUTH2 relations (two fixed, two moving by semitone)

# Context & Application

This approach bridges Riemann's nineteenth-century concept of Doppelklänge with twenty-first-century voice-leading geometry. It addresses a persistent limitation of neo-Riemannian theory — its restriction to consonant triads — by providing principled transformational operations for dissonant sonorities. The analytical applications focus on music where tetrachordal voice leading is audibly parsimonious: Impressionist harmony (Ravel), early jazz-influenced concert music (Still), and other transitional repertoires.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Applying triadic PLR operations directly to tetrachords
  **Correction**: Operations must be designed for the specific set class; PLR acts on embedded triadic subsets, not on the tetrachord as a whole

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking DOUTH2 is the same as semitone voice leading
  **Clarification**: DOUTH2 specifically requires TWO fixed tones and TWO tones moving by parallel semitones — it is more constrained than general semitone voice leading
