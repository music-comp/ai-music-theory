# Seitenwechsel (Mode Exchange)

## Quick Definition

The most fundamental Wechsel operation in Riemann's system, exchanging a triad's mode (major to minor or vice versa) while keeping the root/Hauptton stationary, corresponding to the Parallel (P) transformation in neo-Riemannian theory.

## Formal Definition

**Seitenwechsel** (German: "side exchange" or "lateral change") describes:
- The relationship between a Klang and its "contrary" (*gegentheilige*) form
- C major (c+) ↔ C minor (°c)
- E minor (°e) ↔ E major (e+)

### In Riemann's Notation
- Seitenwechselklang of c+ = °c
- Seitenwechselklang of °e = e+
- Symbol: ⊕ or W₀

### Voice-Leading Properties
- **Common tones**: Root and fifth (2 tones)
- **Moving voice**: Third (by semitone)
- C-E-G → C-Eb-G: only E→Eb moves

## Position in the System

### Most Fundamental Wechsel
The Seitenwechsel is:
- The simplest mode-changing operation
- A generator for the complete Schritt/Wechsel group
- The only Wechsel with 0 root motion (W₀)

### Derivation of Other Wechsel
All other Wechsel derive from Seitenwechsel combined with Schritte:
- Quintwechsel = Quintschritt + Seitenwechsel (Q⊕)
- Terzwechsel = Terzschritt + Seitenwechsel (T⊕)
- Leittonwechsel = Quintschritt + Terzschritt + Seitenwechsel (QT⊕)

## Relationship to P Transformation

### Identity
The Seitenwechsel is exactly the **P (Parallel)** transformation:
- Both change mode without changing root
- Both preserve root and fifth as common tones
- Both move the third by semitone

### Why Two Names?
- **Seitenwechsel**: Riemann's term, emphasizes mode exchange
- **Parallel**: Neo-Riemannian term, emphasizes the parallel major/minor relationship

### Terminological Caution
Riemann used "Parallele" for a different operation (what we call "Relative"):
- Riemann's Parallele = modern R
- Modern Parallel = Riemann's Seitenwechsel

## Functional Implications

### Within Riemann's Function Theory
The Seitenwechselklang:
- Shares function with its modal partner
- C minor in C major = variant tonic
- F minor in C major = subdominant's Seitenwechselklang

### Role in Keys
- Major key includes minor subdominant (Seitenwechsel of IV)
- Minor key includes major dominant (Seitenwechsel of V)
- These are treated as functional equivalents

## Group-Theoretic Properties

### As Generator
In the presentation ⟨Q, T, ⊕⟩:
- ⊕ is one of three generators
- ⊕² = identity (self-inverse)
- Commutes with neither Q nor T

### Defining Relator
⊕² = e (Seitenwechsel applied twice returns to original)

### Combined with Schritte
| Combination | Result |
|-------------|--------|
| ⊕⊕ | Identity |
| Q⊕ | Quintwechsel |
| ⊕Q | Gegenquintwechsel |
| T⊕ | Terzwechsel |
| ⊕T | Gegenterzwechsel |

## Analytical Applications

### Mode Mixture
Passages alternating major and minor versions:
- C major → C minor → C major
- Each change = one Seitenwechsel
- Creates major/minor oscillation

### Chromatic Coloring
The minor subdominant in major:
- iv in major keys (F minor in C major)
- Reached via Seitenwechsel from IV
- Common in 19th-century practice

### The P Cycle
Repeated Seitenwechsel:
- C+ → c- → C+ → c- ...
- Period 2 (returns after 2 applications)
- Creates major/minor alternation

## Acoustic and Psychological Basis

### Acoustic Interpretation (Early Riemann)
- Major and minor triads share overtone series relationship
- Seitenwechsel preserves the most consonant interval (P5)
- Minimal acoustic disturbance

### Psychological Interpretation (Late Riemann)
- The "same" Klang heard in opposite mode
- Mental image of the triad flips
- Maintains sense of tonal center

## Common Confusions

- **Seitenwechsel vs. Parallele**: In Riemann's terminology, these are different operations
- **P in neo-Riemannian = Seitenwechsel**, not Riemann's "Parallele"
- **Mode vs. root**: Seitenwechsel changes mode; root stays the same
