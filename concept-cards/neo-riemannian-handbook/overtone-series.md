# Overtone Series / Harmonic Series

## Quick Definition

The natural series of frequencies produced when a vibrating body sounds, consisting of a fundamental pitch and its integer multiples, historically used to justify the consonance of the major triad.

## Formal Definition

The **overtone series** (German: *Obertonreihe*) consists of all the tones that can be produced by a single vibrating string or air column. When ordered by frequency, these form the series:

**Frequency ratios**: 1 : 2 : 3 : 4 : 5 : 6 : 7 : 8 ...

Starting from C2:
- 1 = C2 (fundamental)
- 2 = C3 (octave)
- 3 = G3 (perfect fifth above octave)
- 4 = C4 (two octaves)
- 5 = E4 (major third above two octaves)
- 6 = G4 (perfect fifth above two octaves)
- 7 = Bb4* (slightly flat - not in tempered tuning)
- 8 = C5 (three octaves)

*The 7th, 11th, 13th, and higher prime-numbered partials do not correspond to pitches in the diatonic system.

The first six partials outline a major triad, leading Rameau and others to claim the major chord as "natural" or acoustically generated.

## Historical Context

**Rameau's Use (1722)**:
Rameau introduced the concept of *son generateur* (generating tone), arguing that the fundamental "generates" the fifth and third above it. This became the foundation of his harmonic theory.

**Riemann's Critique**:
In "Das Problem des harmonischen Dualismus" (1905), Riemann dismantles the acoustic justification:

1. **"Generation" is incorrect**: "To speak of a 'generation' of higher tones by the deepest tone is a thoroughly amateurish way of putting it that does not at all correspond to the actual physical-mechanical processes."

2. **All partials sound simultaneously**: The fundamental and overtones all result from the initial excitation; none "generates" the others.

3. **Higher partials create problems**: Partials 7, 11, 13, etc. belong to the series but are not consonant. Riemann asks: if we justify C-E-G from partials 4:5:6, why not justify C-Bb-F#-Ab from 7:9:11:13?

4. **The diminished triad paradox**: The overtones of B-D-F (in C major) include F#, A, C#, D#, etc. - "What a chaos of disconcerting elements!" Yet we still hear B-D-F as representing the dominant.

## Musical Context

**Implications for Consonance**:
Riemann ultimately rejected overtones as the *cause* of consonance:
- The major triad's derivation from overtones is "not all that secure"
- The minor triad's derivation is "a downright impossibility"
- Helmholtz's "clouded consonance" explanation for minor is a "white lie"

**Klangvertretung (Klang-representation)**:
Even single tones are heard as representing complete triads. The overtones may *support* this hearing but do not *cause* it. Musical hearing is "active, selective" - the mind organizes sounds regardless of acoustics.

**Practical Influence**:
Overtones do affect:
- **Parallel fifths prohibition**: Real fifths in overtones can cause voices to merge
- **Voice leading**: Awareness of overtones influences voicing choices
- **Timbre**: Relative strengths of overtones determine instrument color

## Examples

### Capellen's Experiment (criticized by Riemann)

Georg Capellen proposed: silently hold A and C, then strike A-C-E. The upper pitches will ring "as a consonance" - proving minor requires a "double foundation."

Riemann's counter: Using the same logic, one could "prove" the consonance of any arbitrary combination whose frequencies happen to match natural ratios - including the highly dissonant chord built on partials 7:9:11:13.

### The Subdominant in Minor

In a two-voice passage in G minor with Eb-G (example in Riemann's text):
- The overtones of Eb and G include B, D, G#, etc.
- Yet we hear Eb-G as representing the subdominant G minor chord
- This proves "such processes of hearing can be explained by means of neither overtones nor combination tones"

## Common Confusions

1. **Overtones vs. harmonics**: Often used interchangeably, though "overtones" excludes the fundamental while "harmonics" includes it
2. **Natural vs. tempered tuning**: The overtone series gives just intonation ratios, not equal temperament
3. **Cause vs. correlation**: Riemann distinguishes between overtones *existing* and overtones *causing* our sense of consonance
4. **The 7th partial**: Often called the "natural seventh" but it's significantly flatter than the minor seventh in any scale system

## Theoretical Significance

The critique of overtone-based consonance theory was crucial for Riemann's shift from:
- **Acoustics** (physical, external, objective) to
- **Psychology** (mental, internal, subjective)

This "emancipation from acoustical phenomena" (Stumpf's contribution) opened the way for Riemann's theory of *Tonvorstellungen* and the psychological foundation of harmonic dualism.
