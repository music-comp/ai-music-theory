# Quick Definition

Riemann's model of meter as a multi-level structure extending from the beat through measure, half-phrase, phrase, and period levels, with consistent accent-unaccent alternation at each level, culminating in the eight-measure period as the fundamental unit of musical form.

# Core Definition

Riemann's hierarchical metric organization comprises four essential levels of structure, each exhibiting regular alternation of accented and unaccented events. Caplin explains that Riemann "brings to culmination" the nineteenth-century tendency to view higher-level rhythm as hypermetrical, "extending to higher structural levels the same principles of accent alternation that govern the individual measure" (p. 417). The prototypical eight-measure period serves as the fundamental unit at the highest level, with the invariant "8" function as its metrical goal. This model functions as a "notated meter" for hypermetric levels — at the beat and measure levels, notation provides metric symbols (time signatures, bar lines), but at higher levels, the periodic model substitutes (p. 418).

# Key Properties

1. Four structural levels: beat, measure, two-measure (half-phrase), four-measure (phrase)
2. Consistent accent-unaccent alternation at every level
3. All levels are end-accented (per Auftakttheorie)
4. The eight-measure period is the fundamental formal unit
5. Measure "8" is invariant — every period must conclude with this function
6. The model is a priori, not derived from individual pieces
7. Deviations from the model (expansions, contractions) are measured against the 8-measure norm

# Construction / Recognition

## The Prototypical Eight-Measure Period

```
Level:       Measures: 1  2  3  4  5  6  7  8
Beat:                  w  s  w  s  w  s  w  s  (within each measure)
Measure:               U  A  U  A  U  A  U  A  (odd=weak, even=strong)
2-measure:             U--A  U--A  U--A  U--A
4-measure:             U----A      U----A
8-measure:             U-----------------------A (goal)
```

## To Apply the Model

1. Identify the eight-measure period boundaries
2. Assign functions 1-8 to measures based on the model
3. For expanded periods, label inserted material with alphabetic suffixes (3a, 4a, etc.)
4. The "8" function must be present in every period
5. Incomplete periods are versions of 5-8, never 1-4

# Context & Application

The hierarchical model provides the framework for all of Riemann's analytical work on phrase rhythm. As Burnham demonstrates in Chapter 16, the model enables analysts to parse entire Beethoven movements as realizations of the prototype, with "the most palpable bridge between background prototype and foreground realization" being the distinction between essential and accessory material (p. 446). The model's utility lies in revealing how deviations from the norm create specific compositional effects.

**Connection to harmonic theory**: Even-numbered "heavy" measures (*schwere Zeiten*) typically carry Harmoniewirkung (harmonic change), connecting the metric hierarchy directly to harmonic function.

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 421, Ex. 15.1): Riemann's prototypical construction showing the upbeat/downbeat impulse at several levels: "the last four measures answer the first four; the two-measure pairs 3-4 and 7-8 answer the two-measure pairs 1-2 and 5-6; each even measure answers each odd measure."

**Example 2** (Burnham, p. 442, Ex. 16.1): Beethoven, Op. 31, No. 1, Rondo theme. An "unproblematic" realization where "each even-numbered downbeat is a harmonic resolution."

**Example 3** (Burnham, p. 446, Ex. 16.4): Op. 31, No. 3, Period IV. Expanded to include 3a-4a and 5a-6a repetitions in higher register plus 8a in dropped register. The repeated pairs "extend the fundamental period without furthering the harmonic argument."

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Expecting all music to be exactly 8 measures long
  **Correction**: The model is a prototype; actual music realizes, expands, contracts, or transforms it

- **Error**: Assuming Riemann's numbered functions correspond to actual measure numbers
  **Correction**: Function numbers may differ from score measure numbers due to insertions, elisions, and reinterpretations

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the hierarchy is empirically derived from individual pieces
  **Clarification**: It is an a priori scheme applied to all music; expressed-meter criteria may confirm it, but the model exists independently

- **Confusion**: Believing expansions and contractions violate the model
  **Clarification**: They are measured *against* the model — the model provides the norm from which deviations derive their expressive significance
