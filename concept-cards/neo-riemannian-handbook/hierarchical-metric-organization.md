# Hierarchical Metric Organization

## Quick Definition

Riemann's model of meter as a multi-level structure extending from the beat through measure, half-phrase, phrase, and period levels, with consistent accent-unaccent alternation at each level, culminating in the eight-measure period as the fundamental unit of musical form.

## Formal Definition

**Hierarchical metric organization** in Riemann's mature theory comprises four essential levels of structure, each exhibiting regular alternation of accented and unaccented events:

**The Four Levels**:
1. **Beat level**: Individual beats within measures (strong/weak)
2. **Measure level**: Measures within two-measure groups (odd=weak, even=strong)
3. **Two-measure (half-phrase) level**: Two-measure units within four-measure groups
4. **Four-measure (phrase) level**: Four-measure units within eight-measure periods

**Prototypical Eight-Measure Period**:
```
Level:      Measures: 1  2  3  4  5  6  7  8
Beat:                 w  s  w  s  w  s  w  s  (within each measure)
Measure:              U  A  U  A  U  A  U  A
2-measure:            U--A  U--A  U--A  U--A
4-measure:            U----A      U----A
8-measure:            U----------A (goal)
```

**Key Features**:
- Each level contains regularly alternating accents and unaccents
- Every rhythmic grouping is end-accented (per *Auftakttheorie*)
- The model functions as a "notated meter" for hypermetric levels where notation lacks symbols
- Measure "8" is invariant—every period must conclude with this function

**Historical Significance**: Riemann brings to culmination the nineteenth-century tendency to view higher-level rhythm as hypermetrical, providing consistent principles across all structural levels.

## Musical Context

The hierarchical model serves multiple analytical purposes:

- Provides a framework for understanding phrase rhythm in classical music
- Extends traditional metric concepts (downbeat/upbeat) to larger formal spans
- Creates criteria for analyzing phrase expansion, contraction, and irregularity
- Allows comparison of actual music against the idealized prototype

**Analytical Application**:
- Deviations from the model (5-measure phrases, elisions, expansions) are measured against the 8-measure norm
- Periods can be labeled with their functions (1-2-3-4-5-6-7-8)
- Inserted material receives "a" designations (e.g., 3a, 4a for repetitions)

**Relation to Expressed Meter**: The hierarchical model functions as a "notated meter" at higher levels, but Riemann's expressed-meter criteria can confirm or challenge its application.

## Examples

### Basic

**Simple realization of the prototype**:
```
Beethoven, Op. 31, No. 1, Rondo theme:

Measure:  1   2   3   4   5   6   7   8
Function: U   A   U   A   U   A   U   A
Harmony:  T   D   T   D   T   D   T   T

Each even-numbered measure brings harmonic resolution.
Answering relationships audible at three levels.
```

**Expanded period with insertions**:
```
Measure:  1  2  3  4  3a 4a  5  6  5a 6a  7  8  8a
The repeated 3-4 and 5-6 extend without disturbing the underlying prototype.
```

### From Repertoire

**Beethoven, Op. 10, No. 1, i**: Riemann's analysis shows how the model accommodates elisions and extensions. At m. 22, an "8=1" elision projects both cadential function and the beginning of a new period simultaneously.

**Beethoven, Op. 31, No. 3**: Extended Period VI (33 measures) maintains the underlying 8-measure structure through multiple insertions and repetitions, with added measures receiving alphabetic designations (6a, 7a, 8a, etc.).

**Beethoven, Op. 31, No. 2 ("Tempest")**: The hierarchical model reveals how Beethoven "harnesses and amplifies the closural energy" through iteration of the 6-function before resolution to 8.

## Common Confusions

- The model is an a priori SCHEME, not derived from individual pieces
- "8" being the accented goal doesn't mean all music must be 8 measures long
- Expansions and contractions are measured AGAINST the model, not as violations
- The hierarchy assumes CONSISTENT end-accentuation at every level
- This model conflicts with views where phrase beginnings are metrically strong
- Riemann's numbered functions (1-8) do NOT always align with measure numbers in the score
- The model provides both notated meter (a priori) and expressed meter (criteria) perspectives
