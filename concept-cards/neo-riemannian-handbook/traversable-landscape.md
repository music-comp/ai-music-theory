# Traversable Landscape vs. Matrix

## Quick Definition

The conceptual transformation of the Tonnetz from Oettingen's static multiplication table for calculating acoustic frequencies into Riemann's dynamic space where pathways between tones carry meaning independent of absolute pitch values.

## Formal Definition

This concept describes a fundamental **epistemological shift** in how the Tonnetz was understood:

### The Matrix Conception (Oettingen)
The Tonnetz as **static multiplication table**:
- Entries are results of calculations (5^m × 3^n)
- Spatial position records exponent values
- Internal relations subordinated to external organization
- Each entry is a unique "address" in prime-factor space
- Relations between entries mediated by row/column indices

### The Traversable Landscape (Riemann's Evolution)
The Tonnetz as **dynamic navigation space**:
- Entries are points on paths
- Spatial position reflects derivational meaning
- Internal relations are the primary content
- Paths define tone identity ("same" note via different paths = different meaning)
- Relations directly expressed through geometry

## Gollin's Five-Stage Historical Narrative

### Stage 1: Oettingen's Table (1866)
- Literal frequency calculations
- Just intonation assumed
- Entries represent unique acoustic events
- *Striche* (comma marks) indicate fine intonational distinctions

### Stage 2: Riemann's Adoption (1873-1877)
- Inherited acoustic function from Oettingen
- Used to demonstrate comma distinctions
- *Striche* still indicate tuning differences
- But Riemann begins noting geometric patterns (triangular triads)

### Stage 3: Drobisch's Notation (1880s)
- Adoption of Q and T as path symbols
- Shift from ratio-based to step-based thinking
- Paths become primary over destinations
- Roots of combinatorial interpretation emerge

### Stage 4: Path Combinatorics (1890s-1900s)
- *Musik-Lexikon* (1894): Introduction of *Verwandtschaftsgrad*
- Distance measured in path steps, not frequency ratios
- The table becomes a map of relationships
- Tone identity tied to derivational pathway

### Stage 5: Psychological Foundation (1914-1915)
- "Ideen zu einer 'Lehre von den Tonvorstellungen'"
- Complete shift to mental representation
- Enharmonic equivalence embraced as "indispensable"
- *Striche* now indicate path derivation, not intonation
- The Tonnetz mediates phenomenal (sound) and noumenal (meaning)

## Theoretical Implications

### From Acoustic to Psychological
| Aspect | Matrix (Acoustic) | Landscape (Psychological) |
|--------|-------------------|---------------------------|
| Basis | Physical ratios | Mental representations |
| Entries | Frequency values | Pathway intersections |
| *Striche* | Comma distinctions | Path derivations |
| Space type | Absolute | Relative |
| Enharmonics | Distinct | Equivalent |
| Purpose | Calculate | Navigate |

### From Representation to Navigation
- **Matrix**: The table *represents* acoustic facts
- **Landscape**: The space *enables* compositional/analytical navigation

### From External to Internal Organization
- **Matrix**: Exponents in margins determine internal content
- **Landscape**: Internal paths determine meaning; margins are incidental

## Consequences for Analysis

### Pre-Transformation Analysis
Using the table as matrix:
- Each chord has fixed acoustic identity
- Progressions evaluated by frequency relationships
- Just intonation errors are compositional mistakes
- Enharmonic passages are problematic

### Post-Transformation Analysis
Using the table as landscape:
- Chords derive meaning from arrival paths
- Progressions evaluated by transformational logic
- Equal temperament is assumed
- Enharmonic reinterpretation is a compositional resource

## The Walking Metaphor

Riemann increasingly used spatial-movement language:
- Describing harmonic motion as "steps" (*Schritte*)
- Measuring "degrees of relationship" (*Verwandtschaftsgrad*)
- Conceptualizing modulation as "wandering" through tonal space
- Viewing the composer as navigating a landscape

This metaphor shift enabled:
- Transformational thinking (Lewin)
- Graph-theoretic models (Douthett, Steinbach)
- Geometric voice-leading theory (Tymoczko)

## Common Confusions

- **Not a replacement**: Neo-Riemannian theory uses the landscape model, but the matrix model remains valid for different purposes (historical study, microtonal theory)
- **Riemann didn't abandon acoustics**: He retained acoustic grounding but subordinated it to psychological function
- **Both views preserve structure**: The same geometric relationships obtain; only their interpretation differs
