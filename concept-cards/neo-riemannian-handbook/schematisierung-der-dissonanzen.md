# Quick Definition

Riemann's systematic taxonomy of dissonant chords from his *Skizze einer neuen Methode der Harmonielehre* (1880), which classifies dissonances into categories including Doppelklänge (double sounds), based on their derivation from consonant triadic components.

# Core Definition

The *Schematisierung der Dissonanzen* ("systematization of dissonances") is a section of Riemann's *Skizze* (1880) that provides a comprehensive classification of dissonant chords. As Gollin notes, this is "the less examined aspect of Riemann's *Skizze*" (p. 382). The system classifies dissonant formations according to how they relate to consonant triads, with the Doppelklänge category being the most theoretically significant. Riemann's approach reflects his core principle that all harmonic phenomena can be understood through reference to consonant triadic foundations. Gollin provides a complete translation of this section in the chapter's appendix.

# Prerequisites

- **Klang** — The consonant triad as the fundamental harmonic unit, from which dissonances are derived
- **Harmonieschritte** — The system of step-relations used to classify relationships between component triads

# Key Properties

1. Provides a systematic taxonomy of all dissonant chord types based on triadic derivation
2. The Doppelklänge (double sounds) category classifies dissonant chords as combinations of two consonant triads
3. Classification uses the same Harmonieschritte that organize harmonic progression
4. The system is dualistic, accounting for both major-generated and minor-generated dissonances
5. Though "short-lived" in Riemann's own development, it reveals deep connections between his treatment of dissonance and his theory of harmonic relations

# Construction / Recognition

1. Begin with Riemann's inventory of consonant triads (Klänge)
2. Identify possible combinations of two triads sharing common tones
3. Classify the resulting dissonant aggregates by the Harmonieschritt between their components
4. Organize the complete taxonomy according to the system of Schritte and Wechsel

# Context & Application

The *Schematisierung* appears in Riemann's early theoretical work and represents his attempt to bring dissonance under the same systematic control as consonance. While Riemann later moved away from this specific classification, the underlying principle — that dissonance is comprehensible through consonant triadic reference — remained central to his harmonic thinking. Gollin's chapter revives and extends this framework for modern analytical purposes.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming the Schematisierung represents Riemann's mature theory of dissonance
  **Correction**: It appears in the early *Skizze* (1880) and was not extensively developed in Riemann's later works

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the Schematisierung is merely a list of chord types
  **Clarification**: It is a systematic taxonomy organized by the same transformational relations (Harmonieschritte) that govern harmonic progression, revealing deep structural parallels between chord classification and harmonic motion
