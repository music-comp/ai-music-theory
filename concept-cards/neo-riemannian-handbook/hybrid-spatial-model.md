# Quick Definition

Cohn's proposed integration of the Riemannian Tonnetz tradition and Lerdahl's hierarchical Tonal Pitch Space into a single analytical framework that allows communication between tempered and unbounded spaces and between tone, chord, and key levels, demonstrated through analyses of Schumann, Wagner, and Chopin.

# Core Definition

In the concluding sections of Chapter 11, Cohn proposes a **hybrid spatial model** as a "pragmatic solution" that draws on both the Riemannian Tonnetz tradition and Lerdahl's hierarchical Tonal Pitch Space. Rather than choosing one framework over the other, the hybrid model allows the analyst to move between them as the music demands: using the Tonnetz for regionally indeterminate passages where triadic motion operates outside key-based frameworks, and using TPS-like regional concepts when a key is clearly established. The model enables "communication, reinterpretation, and relocation between tempered and unbounded spaces of tones, key spaces, and diatonic spaces or regions of triads, through the graphic metaphors of the Tonnetz and its allied spatial models" (Ch. 11, abstract).

# Prerequisites

- **Tonnetz**: The spatial model for triadic-level analysis
- **Tonal Pitch Space**: Lerdahl's hierarchical model for key-level analysis
- **Regional space**: The concept of keys as regions that can be mapped onto or derived from the Tonnetz

# Key Properties

1. **Framework switching**: The analyst can move between Tonnetz and TPS depending on the passage
2. **Regional flexibility**: Some passages analyzed with regions (TPS-like), others without (Tonnetz)
3. **Multiple levels**: Tone, chord, and key levels are addressed, sometimes separately, sometimes unitarily
4. **Pragmatic**: Driven by analytical need rather than theoretical consistency
5. **Analytical demonstrations**: Illustrated through analyses of Schumann, Wagner, and Chopin

# Construction / Recognition

The hybrid model operates by:
1. Begin with the Tonnetz as the default spatial framework
2. When a passage establishes a clear key, activate regional encapsulation (parallelogram on the Tonnetz or TPS regional analysis)
3. When a passage is regionally indeterminate, track triadic motion on the Tonnetz without regional commitment
4. Transition between modes as the music transitions between regionally determinate and indeterminate sections
5. Use geometric duality to move between pitch-class and triadic perspectives as needed

# Context & Application

The hybrid model represents Cohn's constructive response to the debate between Tonnetz and TPS advocates. Rather than declaring one model superior, he shows that both capture genuine properties of tonal music and that a competent analyst should be able to use both. The model is particularly useful for late Romantic music where regionally determinate and indeterminate passages alternate, often within a single piece or even a single phrase.

# Examples

Cohn (Ch. 11) illustrates the hybrid model with analyses of:
- **Schumann**: Passages where regional analysis and triadic motion analysis complement each other
- **Wagner** (Parsifal, Faith Proclamation): The hexatonic cycling is analyzed on the Tonnetz; entry and exit from the cycling are analyzed with regional awareness
- **Chopin**: Passages where the music oscillates between clear tonal reference and chromatic drift

In the Parsifal analysis, the LP cycling within hexatonic space is best tracked on the Tonnetz (no key assignment needed), while the framing cadential passages that establish and reestablish tonal reference are best analyzed with regional concepts.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating the hybrid model as a fully formalized system
  **Correction**: Cohn describes it as a "pragmatic solution," not a rigorous formalization; it is an analytical approach rather than a theory

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: The hybrid model means TPS and Tonnetz are equivalent
  **Clarification**: They are complementary but not equivalent; the hybrid model recognizes that each is suited to different analytical contexts
