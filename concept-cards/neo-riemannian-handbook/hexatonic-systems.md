# Quick Definition

Four non-overlapping collections of six triads, each generated by the LP (or PL) compound transformation, that partition the 24 major and minor triads and organize maximally smooth voice-leading relationships in chromatic music.

# Core Definition

**Hexatonic systems** are cyclic structures created by alternating L and P transformations on consonant triads. Each system contains 3 major and 3 minor triads, all sharing membership in a single hexatonic collection (set class [014589]). The LP compound has order 6, meaning it returns to the starting triad after 6 applications, generating a cyclic group Z6. Four such systems partition all 24 consonant triads without overlap. On the toroidal Tonnetz, hexatonic systems appear as cylindrical "tubes." Cohn identifies the hexatonic pole relationship (triads three LP steps apart within a system, sharing no common tones) as producing maximum voice-leading distance within maximum pitch-class proximity — a relationship he connects to the musical "uncanny" (Ch. 11). The connection to Carl Friedrich Weitzmann's 1853 identification of the augmented triad as a nexus connecting six consonant triads through single-semitone voice leading provides historical depth.

# Prerequisites

- **PLR transformations**: L and P are the specific transformations that generate hexatonic cycles
- **Tonnetz**: The spatial framework on which hexatonic systems appear as distinct regions

# Key Properties

1. **LP generation**: Each system is an orbit under the LP subgroup (cyclic group Z6)
2. **Partition**: The four systems partition all 24 consonant triads completely and without overlap
3. **Hexatonic collection**: Each system's pitch classes form set class [014589]
4. **Maximally smooth**: Each step in the cycle moves only one voice by one semitone (DVLS = 1)
5. **Hexatonic poles**: Triads three LP steps apart share no common tones, creating maximum internal distance
6. **Augmented triad nexus**: Each system contains one augmented triad [048] at its center, connecting all six consonant triads

# Construction / Recognition

The four hexatonic systems:
- **Northern**: C+, C-, Ab+, Ab-, E+, E- (augmented triad: C-E-G#)
- **Eastern**: G+, G-, Eb+, Eb-, B+, B- (augmented triad: G-B-D#)
- **Southern**: D+, D-, Bb+, Bb-, F#+, F#- (augmented triad: D-F#-A#)
- **Western**: A+, A-, F+, F-, C#+, C#- (augmented triad: A-C#-E#)

To generate any system: start from a major triad, apply L, then P, then L, then P, then L, then P, arriving back at the start.

# Context & Application

Hexatonic systems are particularly relevant for analyzing late Romantic chromatic music:
- Wagner's *Parsifal* (Cohn's Faith Proclamation analysis in Ch. 11)
- Liszt's late piano works
- Brahms's chromatic passages
- Any music featuring chains of chromatic third relations

On the toroidal Tonnetz, hexatonic systems appear as tubes; movement between systems requires R (or another non-LP operation), transitioning through octatonic space.

# Examples

Cohn's analysis of the Faith Proclamation in Wagner's Parsifal (Act I Prelude) demonstrates LP cycling through hexatonic space, with departure through octatonic connections (R operations) serving as bridges between hexatonic systems (Ch. 11).

Weitzmann (1853) independently identified the same structures by observing that each augmented triad connects to three major and three minor triads through single-semitone voice leading, producing an IC3 x IC4 Tonnetz equivalent (Ch. 11).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Confusing hexatonic system (6 triads) with hexatonic collection (6 pitch classes)
  **Correction**: The system is a set of triads; the collection is the pitch-class set [014589] formed by their union

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: LP and PL produce different cycles
  **Clarification**: LP and PL cycle through the same 6 triads (just starting from different points in the cycle)

- **Confusion**: Hexatonic poles are transformationally simple
  **Clarification**: Despite sharing a hexatonic collection and being separated by zero common tones, hexatonic poles require 3 LP operations to connect — they are maximally distant within the system
