# Hexatonic Systems / Weitzmann Regions

## Quick Definition

Four non-overlapping collections of six triads, each generated by the LP (or PL) compound transformation, that partition the 24 major and minor triads and organize voice-leading relationships in chromatic music.

## Formal Definition

**Hexatonic systems** are cyclic structures created by alternating L and P transformations:

### Structure
Each hexatonic system contains:
- 3 major triads
- 3 minor triads
- All 6 triads share membership in a single hexatonic collection [014589]

### The Four Systems
Starting from any major triad and applying LP repeatedly:

| System | Triads (+ = major, - = minor) |
|--------|-------------------------------|
| Northern | C+, E-, E+, G#-, G#+, C- |
| Eastern | G+, B-, B+, D#-, D#+, G- |
| Southern | D+, F#-, F#+, A#-, A#+, D- |
| Western | A+, C#-, C#+, F-, F+, A- |

### Hexatonic Poles
Within each system, the two triads **three LP steps apart** share no common tones:
- C major and G# minor are **hexatonic poles**
- E major and C minor are hexatonic poles
- These relationships create maximum voice-leading distance within the system

## Weitzmann Regions

Carl Friedrich Weitzmann (1853) identified the **augmented triad** as a nexus connecting three major and three minor triads through single-semitone voice leading.

### The Augmented Triad Connection
Each hexatonic system contains one augmented triad [048] at its center:
- Northern: C-E-G# augmented
- Each consonant triad in the system is one semitone away from this augmented triad

### Weitzmann's Table (1853)
Weitzmann represented the 12 pitch classes as a cross product of:
- Augmented triads (IC4 cycles): C-E-G#, D-F#-A#, D#-G-B, E#-A-C#
- Diminished seventh chords (IC3 cycles)

This creates an IC3 × IC4 Tonnetz equivalent.

## Analytical Applications

### Late Romantic Repertoire
Hexatonic systems are particularly relevant for analyzing:
- Wagner's *Tristan und Isolde* and *Parsifal*
- Liszt's late piano works
- Brahms's chromatic passages
- Any music featuring third-related progressions

### Example: Parsifal's Faith Proclamation
Cohn's analysis (Chapter 11) demonstrates:
- Movement through hexatonic space via LP chains
- Relationship between hexatonic structure and formal design
- Interaction with diatonic regions

### Hexatonic Poles and the "Uncanny"
Cohn's research on hexatonic poles ("Uncanny Resemblances," 2004):
- Hexatonic pole relationships often accompany uncanny or supernatural text
- Maximum voice-leading distance within maximum pitch-class proximity
- Creates a distinctive "same but different" quality

## Mathematical Properties

### Cycle Properties
- LP (or PL) has **order 6** (returns to starting triad after 6 applications)
- LP generates the cyclic group Z₆
- The 4 hexatonic systems partition the 24 triads

### Relation to Group Structure
Within the full PLR group:
- Hexatonic systems are orbits under the LP subgroup
- Each system is closed under LP transformations
- Moving between systems requires R or other transformations

### Voice-Leading Geometry
Hexatonic systems represent:
- "Tubes" in Tymoczko's voice-leading space
- Maximally smooth cycles through triadic space
- Paths minimizing total voice-leading motion

## Relationship to Octatonic Systems

| Feature | Hexatonic | Octatonic |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| Generator | LP | PR |
| Triads per system | 6 | 8 |
| Number of systems | 4 | 3 |
| Underlying scale | [014589] | [0134679T] |
| Characteristic interval | Minor third chains | Major third chains |

Both partition the 24 triads but with different cyclic structures.

## Common Confusions

- **Hexatonic system vs. hexatonic collection**: The system contains 6 triads; the collection is the 6-note scale [014589]
- **Weitzmann regions vs. hexatonic systems**: Essentially the same concept viewed through different historical lenses
- **LP vs. PL**: These are equivalent operations (both cycle through the same 6 triads)
- **Poles are not transformationally simple**: Despite sharing all pitch classes, hexatonic poles require 3 LP operations to connect
