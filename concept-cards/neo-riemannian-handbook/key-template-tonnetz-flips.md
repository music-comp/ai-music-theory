# Quick Definition

The operation of flipping the diatonic key template along its longer edges on the Tonnetz, which produces exactly the four chromatic mediant keys, demonstrating that chromatic mediant relations are structurally intrinsic to the tonal system rather than extrinsic products of voice-leading operations.

# Core Definition

Kopp demonstrates that projecting the diatonic set of a key onto Riemann's classic Tonnetz (from "Ideen zu einer 'Lehre von den Tonvorstellungen'") and performing 180-degree flips of this template along its longer edges produces the keys of the four chromatic mediants. Just as neo-Riemannian triadic flips (L, P, R) change the orientation of the triadic triangle on the Tonnetz and always produce a change of mode, key-template flips produce chromatic mediant keys with their characteristic common-tone distributions. "They are exactly the keys of the four chromatic mediants, in close and direct relation to the tonic key" (p. 413). The diagrams also reveal "the even distribution of shared (common) and nonshared tones in these relationships, either three-to-four or four-to-three" (pp. 413-414).

# Prerequisites

- **Tonnetz** — The geometric pitch-space on which the key template is projected
- **Chromatic mediant** — The four mediant types that result from the flips

# Key Properties

1. Flipping the template having D at the lower right yields the upper mediants
2. Flipping the template having D at the upper left yields the lower mediants
3. Upward flips yield the sharp mediants (Tonnetz tends sharpward toward the top)
4. Downward flips yield the flat mediants
5. Each flip reveals 3-to-4 or 4-to-3 shared/nonshared tone distribution between keys
6. Flipping on short edges produces nondiatonic cyclic sets (incomplete octatonic or hexatonic with redundancy)

# Construction / Recognition

1. Project the seven-note diatonic set of the tonic key onto the Tonnetz as a connected template
2. Identify the two longer edges of the template
3. Perform a 180-degree flip along one longer edge
4. The resulting template position defines a chromatic mediant key
5. Repeat for the other longer edge and with the alternative D-position template to generate all four chromatic mediants

# Context & Application

Kopp presents this as a "postscript" that validates "the essential and unmediated harmonic nature of the chromatic mediants within the tonal system" (pp. 413-414). The key-template flip is analogous to, but operates at a different level than, the neo-Riemannian triadic flips. Where triadic flips generate mode changes at the chord level, key-template flips generate chromatic mediant relationships at the key level. This provides structural evidence that chromatic mediants are not compound products of multiple voice-leading operations but direct, first-order relationships.

**Critical contrast**: "This is in contrast to the neo-Riemannian hexatonic model, which generates chromatic mediants as the compound products of two different successive diatonic voice-leading operations at the chordal level" (p. 414).

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 413, Ex. 14.9): Four diagrams showing key-template flips from C major:

- Lower left: Lower Flat Mediant (Ab major)
- Upper right: Upper Sharp Mediant (E major)
- Lower right: Lower Sharp Mediant (A major)

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Confusing key-template flips with triadic PLR flips
  **Correction**: Key-template flips operate on the seven-note diatonic set, not on individual triads; they produce key relationships, not chord transformations

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the Tonnetz only represents chord-level relationships
  **Clarification**: Kopp shows the Tonnetz can also represent key-level relationships through the projection and manipulation of diatonic set templates
