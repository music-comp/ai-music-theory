# Quick Definition

The foundational epistemological distinction in German philosophy and psychology between passive sensory reception (Empfindung) and active mental representation (Vorstellung), which Riemann used to reframe harmonic theory from acoustic science to music psychology.

# Core Definition

The **Empfindung/Vorstellung** distinction divides perception into two domains:

- **Empfindung** (sensation): Raw sensory input, passive reception of physical stimuli — what Helmholtz called the "material ear" (*leibliches Ohr*)
- **Vorstellung** (representation/imagination): Active mental processing, conceptualization, organization of sensory data into meaningful patterns — what Helmholtz called the "mental ear" (*geistiges Ohr*)

Helmholtz articulated this in his 1857 lecture "On the Physiological Causes of Harmony in Music": "We must here distinguish two things, namely first the *sensation* (*Empfindung*) in the auditory nerve, as it develops without the interference of mental activity, and the *representation* (*Vorstellung*), which we form as a consequence of this sensation. We must therefore, as it were, distinguish the material ear of the body and the mental ear of the faculty of representation" (Ch. 2, n. 16).

Riemann inverted Helmholtz's priorities. Where Helmholtz valued the analytical decomposition of sensation, Riemann insisted that musical understanding lies in the synthetic, organizing Vorstellung: "If listening to music is a *selecting-out from chordal material presented to the ear*... then it is no longer a physical suffering (*ein physisches Erleiden*), but rather a *logical activity*" (Ch. 2, n. 17, *Musikalische Syntaxis*, viii).

# Prerequisites

- **klang** — The Klang is the primary object of Vorstellung; the distinction explains how Klange exist in the mind vs. in sound

# Key Properties

1. Empfindung is passive, physical, and elementary; Vorstellung is active, mental, and synthetic
2. Helmholtz prioritized Empfindung (breaking perception into elementary components)
3. Riemann prioritized Vorstellung (synthesizing perception into meaningful wholes)
4. The distinction enabled Riemann to declare music theory a branch of psychology rather than physics
5. Helmholtz's *Lehre von den Tonempfindungen* (1863) grounded theory in sensation; Riemann proposed a *Lehre von den Tonvorstellungen* to replace it
6. The philosophical roots extend to Kant and neo-Kantianism, particularly through Lotze and Drobisch

# Construction / Recognition

## To Recognize the Distinction in Practice
1. **Empfindung question**: What physical sound waves reach the ear? What overtones are present?
2. **Vorstellung question**: What does the listener understand? What Klang does the tone represent? What function does the chord serve?
3. The gap between these two levels is the space where Riemann's theory operates

## Key Philosophical Sources
- **Helmholtz**: The material ear / mental ear distinction
- **Lotze**: Critique of Helmholtz for lacking a positive account of consonance (Ch. 2, n. 19)
- **Drobisch**: "Every act of thinking is in general a gathering of a multitude and manifold into a unity" (Ch. 2, n. 18)

# Context & Application

Steege's chapter situates Riemann's essay "Die Natur der Harmonik" (1882) within the broader intellectual context of 19th-century German science. The essay was written "at the moment when a budding psychological perspective was beginning to supersede Riemann's earlier acoustical and physiological perspective" (Ch. 2, abstract).

The progression from physics to physiology to psychology mirrors a broader disciplinary shift: "the physics-physiology-psychology series in fact amounts to a cogent summary of the gradual transference of experimental technique from one set of 'objects,' to the next, to the last" (Ch. 2, n. 12).

Riemann's argument proceeds: Helmholtz "sought to explain from the nature of sounding bodies concepts that can only be explained from the nature of the perceiving mind" (Ch. 2, p. 83). The "mistake" was epistemological — looking for musical meaning in the wrong domain. Consonance and dissonance "are musical concepts, but not definite forms of sound waves."

# Examples

**Example 1** (Ch. 2, pp. 83-84): The six-four chord: "according to physical and physiological formulations, the most undisturbed and euphonious" chord — yet "can be a dissonance musically." This proves Empfindung (acoustic smoothness) does not determine Vorstellung (functional meaning).

**Example 2** (Ch. 2, p. 84): An isolated tone E. As Empfindung: a frequency. As Vorstellung: the third of C major, root of E major, fifth of A major — rich with tonal meaning depending on context.

**Example 3** (Ch. 2, n. 16): Helmholtz's original formulation asking "What does the ear do — does it resolve [compound tones] or grasp them as a whole?" The answer depends on whether we are asking about the "material ear" (Empfindung) or the "mental ear" (Vorstellung).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating the distinction as binary (either acoustic or psychological)
  **Correction**: Riemann acknowledged that acoustics and physiology are "supplementary sciences" — not irrelevant, but not foundational

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking Riemann rejected empirical science
  **Clarification**: Riemann valued empirical knowledge but argued it belonged to a different domain. "The impossibility of physics and physiology being used to achieve a grounding of musical concepts could only be seen once psychology could enter in its own right" (Ch. 2, p. 83).

- **Confusion**: Equating Vorstellung with subjective opinion
  **Clarification**: For Riemann, Vorstellungen are governed by laws of logic and can be right or wrong. Musical hearing seeks "greatest simplicity" and "economy" — there are better and worse representations.
