# Quick Definition

The perceptual and aesthetic effect produced by specific harmonic progressions in Riemann's Systematik der Harmonieschritte, where certain relationships have "cadential force," others produce "good effect," and still others are "scarcely comprehensible" — providing an evaluative criterion distinct from the structural intelligibility hierarchy.

# Core Definition

In the Systematik der Harmonieschritte (Skizze, section 38), Riemann evaluates each of his 25 named relationships not only by structural intelligibility but by their musical **Wirkung** (effect). This is distinct from the intelligibility hierarchy (which addresses whether a progression is "directly comprehensible" vs. "requiring mediation") and concerns instead the aesthetic-functional quality of each progression when it occurs in musical context. Engebretsen's translated appendix (Ch. 12) preserves Riemann's detailed evaluative language: Quintschritte and Terzschritte are "excellent" for direct confrontation with the tonic; Leittonschritte have "closing force" despite distant harmonic relationship; Ganztonschritte and Tritonusschritte "reach out a great distance" and require mediating chords; and chromatic Halbtonschritte produce effects "like a sudden flash of lightning." This evaluative dimension reveals that the Harmonieschritte system was intended as compositional guidance, not merely theoretical classification.

# Key Properties

1. **Cadential force** (Schlusswirkung): Strongest effect — the ability to create closure when approaching the tonic
2. **Direct confrontation**: Some progressions "prove very well-suited for direct confrontation with the tonic"
3. **Mediation requirement**: Weaker progressions require an intermediate chord to produce good effect
4. **Chromatic shift effect**: Riemann describes certain chromatic progressions as "magical," producing effects "like a sudden flash of lightning"
5. **Voice-leading enhancement**: Adding sevenths can improve the Wirkung of otherwise obscure progressions
6. **Tonal disturbance**: More distant Schritte "disturb a coherent sense of tonality"

# Construction / Recognition

Riemann's evaluative categories from the Systematik (Ch. 12 appendix):

**Strong Wirkung**: Quintschritte ("most important Schritte after the Seitenwechsel"), Terzschritte ("well-suited for direct confrontation"), Leittonschritte ("actually quite excellent... have closing force")

**Moderate Wirkung**: Kleinterzschritte (effective with some voice-leading refinement), Gegenleittonwechsel ("of good effect, particularly if the sevenths of both Klaenge are introduced")

**Weak/modulatory Wirkung**: Ganztonschritte ("not directly intelligible"), Tritonusschritte ("reaches out a great distance")

**Special Wirkung**: Chromatic shifts ("no empty folly; rather with them the masters achieve the most beautiful effects")

# Context & Application

This evaluative dimension of the Harmonieschritte is what neo-Riemannian theory systematically strips away. Where Riemann provided nuanced aesthetic-functional guidance about each progression's musical quality, neo-Riemannian theory treats all 24 transformations as structurally equivalent. Engebretsen identifies this as a key site of "discord" between Riemann's original system and its neo-Riemannian appropriation. Riemann's warning is explicit: "A coherent sense of tonality is disturbed by such Schritte... The digression from the tonic's closer relations must not appear unmotivated. These sorts of Harmonieschritte are therefore actually more a means of modulation than part of tonal harmony."

Note: This concept is distinct from "Harmoniewirkung" as used by Caplin in Ch. 15, where the term refers specifically to harmonic change as a criterion for metric accent.

# Examples

From the Systematik translation (Ch. 12 appendix):

On the **Leittonschritte**: "The Leitklaenge are suitable, actually quite excellent, for direct confrontation with the tonic and have closing force to it in much the same way as the Quintklaenge and Terzklaenge. This strange fact can be explained only as follows: that the Leittonschritte of all the voices, indeed the 'leading relationship' of the entire chord is regarded in a similar way as the Leittonschritt from the third of the schlicht Quintklang to the tonic."

On **chromatic Halbtonschritte**: "the sudden introduction of the a-major chord instead of the ab-major chord has a magical effect, like a sudden flash of lightning — all at once we are transported out of the depths of the flat keys into the bright heights of the sharp keys. The occurrence is then similar to when in a chord the fifth, the root or the third is altered; the chromatic Secundschritt is an alteration of the entire chord."

On **Tritonusschritte**: "the Schritt reaches out a great distance and will therefore lead to its skipped-over Klaenge."

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Confusing this concept with Caplin's Ch. 15 "Harmoniewirkung" (metric accent from harmonic change)
  **Correction**: In Ch. 12 context, the term refers to the aesthetic-functional effect of specific harmonic progressions; in Ch. 15, it refers to the metric accent created by harmonic change

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Wirkung is an objective, measurable property
  **Clarification**: Riemann acknowledged subjective elements in his assessments; Wirkung is an evaluative judgment informed by structural properties but not reducible to them
