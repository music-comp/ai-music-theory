# Quick Definition

The central pitch of Riemann's symmetrical pentatonic scale, borrowed from Greek tetrachordal theory and functioning as a "proto-tonic" around which the other scale degrees are organized symmetrically. Riemann uses the symbol ☉ to designate it.

# Core Definition

The **mese** (from Greek *mese*, "middle") is Riemann's term for the conceptual center of a pentatonic scale, around which the other four tones are arranged symmetrically — two above and two below. Riemann borrowed the term from Greek theory but applied it tendentiously: "many of the theoretical implications of the Greek term do not match Riemann's agenda" (Ch. 4, p. 148). He "quietly employs the symbol ☉ instead — presumably because many of the theoretical implications of the Greek term do not match" his usage.

In Riemann's system, the *mese* is not necessarily the lowest or highest tone but the *central* one, functioning as a proto-tonic reference point. Using the pentatonic scale A-C-D-F-G with *mese* D, the degrees are labeled: IV-II-☉-2-4 (Roman numerals descending, Arabic ascending from center).

Riemann's justification for assigning tonic-like status to the central pitch is "flimsy at best" (Ch. 4, p. 149): he admitted the only support was that in his reconstruction of the oldest Greek enharmonic scale, this pitch would have been designated *mese*. He demonstrated it with the Chinese melody "Tsi Tschong," where most phrase endings occur on the *mese* rather than the lowest scale degree.

# Key Properties

1. Occupies the central position in the chain of fifths from which the scale derives
2. Not necessarily the lowest or final tone — a departure from conventional finalis theory
3. Surrounded by the *pyknon* (cluster of two whole-tone steps)
4. Functions as a proto-tonic reference point for melodic organization
5. Symmetrical extension: Roman numerals below, Arabic numerals above
6. The concept drops out of Riemann's subsequent analysis after its initial introduction

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify the Mese
1. Derive the pentatonic scale from a chain of five consecutive fifths
2. The middle pitch of the five-fifth chain is the *mese*
3. Example: fifths F-C-G-D-A → *mese* = G (the middle of the chain)
4. The scale D-E-G-A-C has *mese* G, with D-E below and A-C above

# Context & Application

The *mese* concept is strategically important to Riemann despite its weak justification, because it provides the symmetrical center required for his dualistic approach to melodic theory. It also enables his later explanation of the leading tone: the *mese* must be the tonal center so that the cadential gesture of upper and lower neighbor tones around it can be identified as the prototype for the leading-tone cadence when *pien* tones are added (Ch. 4, pp. 156-157).

# Examples

**Example 1** (Ch. 4, ex. 4.2): "Tsi Tschong" — Chinese melody where "most phrases end on D (the *mese*)" despite the melody ending on G (the lower fifth).

**Example 2** (Ch. 4, ex. 4.1): Three pentatonic scales with their *mese* values: Scale on A (☉=A), Scale on D (☉=D), Scale on G (☉=G).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Equating the mese with the finalis or lowest note
  **Correction**: The mese is the *central* pitch of the chain of fifths, which may or may not be the final or lowest note

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking Riemann's mese corresponds to the Greek mese in standard tetrachordal theory
  **Clarification**: Riemann's usage is tendentious — "the conclusion that Riemann's use of these Greek terms is tendentious is difficult to deny" (Ch. 4, p. 148)
