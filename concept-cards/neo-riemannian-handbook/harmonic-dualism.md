# Quick Definition

Harmonic dualism is the theoretical framework asserting that major and minor triads are equal, opposite, and symmetrically constructed entities generated by inverse principles, rather than minor being derived from or subordinate to major.

# Core Definition

**Harmonic dualism** is a music-theoretical perspective characterized by two ideas: (1) **equality** -- major and minor triads arise as two distinct but equal expressions of a single structuring principle; and (2) **inversion** -- major and minor triads appear as mirror or inverted expressions of one another. In Klumpenhouwer's formulation (Ch. 6), "soft dualism" exhibits only equality without explicit inversion, while "hard dualism" exhibits both characteristics, viewing the minor triad as structurally inverted from the major.

Riemann's 1905 article "Das Problem des harmonischen Dualismus" (translated by Bent in Ch. 5) represents his final defense: "The two types of relationship... the series of simple multiples with respect to frequencies (over-series), and the series of simple multiples with respect to string-lengths (under-series). These series do not require verification by way of acoustical phenomena" (p. 189).

# Key Properties

1. Major and minor triads are treated as equally fundamental consonances
2. The major triad is constructed upward from its root (frequency ratios 4:5:6)
3. The minor triad is constructed downward from its fifth/prime (wavelength ratios 4:5:6, yielding 10:12:15 in frequency)
4. Both modes reduce to the same simple number series applied to different domains (frequencies vs. wavelengths)
5. Function labels (T, S, D) work identically in both modes, though the underlying transformations (Schritte) differ

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify a Dualist Framework
1. Check whether major and minor are treated as equal and independent (not minor derived from major)
2. Check whether the minor triad is conceptualized as built downward from its fifth
3. Check whether inversional relationships between major and minor are treated as structurally significant

## Historical Phases of Dualism
1. **Acoustic phase (1873-1890s)**: Riemann argued undertones exist physically
2. **Psychological phase (1905-1919)**: Riemann abandoned acoustics for mental representation (Tonvorstellungen)
3. **Mathematical phase (1980s-present)**: Neo-Riemannian theory formalizes dualism as group-theoretic operations

# Context & Application

Harmonic dualism originated with Zarlino (1558), who first articulated the logical opposition between arithmetic and harmonic divisions of the fifth. It was developed by Hauptmann (1853) through dialectical philosophy, systematized by Oettingen (1866) through dual notation, and most extensively elaborated by Riemann (1873-1919).

The central challenge for dualism has always been the "minor root problem": if the fifth is the generative prime of the minor triad, why does the bass (under-fifth) function as the practical root? Riemann's response evolved from acoustic justification to psychological explanation.

In contemporary neo-Riemannian theory, dualism provides the foundation for PLR transformations, where inversion is treated as a fundamental operation alongside transposition.

# Examples

**Riemann's mature formulation** (p. 189): "The principle of major is increase in speed of vibration, the principle of minor is growth in wave-lengths." Both use the ratio series 4:5:6, but applied to different physical domains.

**C major vs. C minor in dualist construction**:
- C major (c+): C (prime) - E (third) - G (fifth), built upward from C
- C minor (°g): G (prime) - Eb (under-third) - C (under-fifth), built downward from G

**Klumpenhouwer's defense** (Ch. 6): The critique that Riemann's function labels contradict dualism is refuted by noting that in C major, T to D = Quintschritt (Q), while in C minor, T to D = Gegenquintschritt (-Q) -- the same transformation applied inversionally.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Attempting to hear undertones acoustically as proof of dualism
  **Correction**: Riemann explicitly abandoned acoustic justification in 1905; dualism is a mathematical/psychological framework

- **Error**: Placing the dualist "prime" (fifth) in the bass of minor chords
  **Correction**: Riemann acknowledged that practical voice leading places the under-fifth in the bass; the prime and the bass are distinct roles

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Believing dualism requires the physical existence of undertones
  **Clarification**: Riemann's mature theory explicitly declares "emancipation from acoustical phenomena" -- both major and minor consonance are psychological, not acoustic

- **Confusion**: Thinking function labels (T, S, D) contradict dualism
  **Clarification**: As Klumpenhouwer demonstrates, the labels describe the same functional relationships but through inversionally related Schritte (Quintschritt vs. Gegenquintschritt)

- **Confusion**: Equating soft and hard dualism
  **Clarification**: Soft dualism asserts equality of major and minor without requiring explicit inversional structure; hard dualism requires both equality and inversion
