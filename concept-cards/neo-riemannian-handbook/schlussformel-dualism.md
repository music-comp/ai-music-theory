# Quick Definition

Riemann's concept of the cadential closing formula (*Schlussformel*), in which tonality is confirmed through a specific sequence of functional harmonies -- typically S-D-T in major, with the dualist mirror being the inversionally corresponding sequence in minor.

# Core Definition

The **Schlussformel** (closing formula) represents the minimal harmonic sequence needed to establish a tonic unambiguously. In Riemann's theory, this is not simply V-I but requires the full functional cycle S-D-T (or T-S-D-T), because the tonic's centrality between subdominant and dominant must be demonstrated (Ch. 7, pp. 222-224).

Rehding connects this to the triad of triads: "The supreme demand for all form, including musical form, is unity" which "can only fully unfold in the context of opposition, as contrast and contradiction" (Riemann, cited p. 222). The cadential formula enacts this dialectic at the phrase level.

# Prerequisites

- **Harmonic dualism** -- The Schlussformel operates within dualist functional theory
- **Characteristic dissonance** -- Dissonances help identify the functional roles within the formula

# Key Properties

1. The full cadential formula requires S, D, and T (not just D-T)
2. In major: S-D-T establishes tonic by presenting both opposing functions
3. The dualist mirror in minor: inversionally corresponding functional sequence
4. Riemann's "forms" are classified by how many times the Schlussformel is articulated
5. Riemann did not extend formal analysis beyond eight-measure periods in practice

# Construction / Recognition

## Standard Schlussformel in Major
1. Subdominant function (S): Establishes the "left" side
2. Dominant function (D): Establishes the "right" side
3. Tonic resolution (T): Confirmed as center mediating both

## Riemann's Formal Classification
- First form: Simple A-B-A (one Schlussformel)
- Second form: Larger A-B-A (minuet/trio)
- Third form: Elaborate A-B-A (rondo)
- Fourth form: Sonata (multiple Schlussformeln at different levels)

# Context & Application

Rehding notes that Riemann was "not interested in pursuing the formal element beyond the eight-measure period" (*Grosse Kompositionslehre*, 1: 424). His formal classifications derive from how the Schlussformel scales up through repetition and elaboration, but he never connected this systematically to large-scale tonal planning.

The gap between Riemann's phrase-level Schlussformel theory and large-scale form is what Rehding's "dualistic forms" chapter seeks to bridge: if the phrase-level cadence requires S-D-T, does the movement-level form require analogous S-side and D-side exploration?

# Examples

**Riemann's formal principle** (cited p. 222): "Form involves the resolution of a contrast or conflict." The Schlussformel enacts this: S creates opposition, D intensifies it, T resolves it.

**Brahms Trio, coda** (pp. 237-239): The subdominant D minor with characteristic dissonance followed by the tonic A minor enacts the Schlussformel at the movement level, completing the formal cycle that the exposition began.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Equating the Schlussformel with V-I
  **Correction**: The full formula requires S-D-T; V-I alone does not demonstrate the tonic's centrality

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Riemann had a fully developed theory of form based on the Schlussformel
  **Clarification**: Riemann classified forms by Schlussformel count but never extended this to movement-level tonal planning
