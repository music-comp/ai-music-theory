# Quick Definition

The "cadential effect" (German: *Schlusswirkung*) whereby points of harmonic and formal closure create metrical accent, serving as one of Riemann's key criteria for expressed meter and directly supporting his end-accentuation theory (Auftakttheorie).

# Core Definition

Schlusswirkung ("effect of cadence" or "cadential effect") is Riemann's principle that cadential arrivals — points where musical phrases reach closure — create metrical accent. From the *Elementar-Schulbuch der Harmonielehre*: "The harmonies that enter on the beginning of the fourth and eighth measures make *Schlusswirkungen* or *Schluss*-like effects, according to the extent to which the chords are suitable for the cadence" (cited by Caplin, p. 431). The concept directly supports Auftakttheorie by associating accent with endings rather than beginnings. In the eight-measure period, the invariant "8" function is the most powerful Schlusswirkung, and measure "4" provides a secondary closural accent.

# Prerequisites

- **Expressed meter** — Schlusswirkung operates within the expressed-meter framework as one of its criteria
- **Harmonic function** — Recognition of cadential harmonies (especially tonic resolution) is required to identify Schlusswirkung

# Key Properties

1. Cadential arrivals create metrical accent at the point of closure
2. The tonic is most suitable for creating Schlusswirkung (consonant, stable)
3. Other harmonies (including dominant) can create "a kind of Schlusswirkung" at half-cadence points
4. Schlusswirkung directly supports Auftakttheorie by associating accent with endings
5. The "8" function in the periodic model is the invariant bearer of Schlusswirkung
6. Traditional cadence involves multiple factors (harmonic, melodic, rhythmic, metric) — using Schlusswirkung as expressed meter requires identifying cadential effect independent of predetermined metric position

# Construction / Recognition

## Analytical Method

1. Identify Schlusswirkungen (cadential arrivals) in the passage
2. These points are metrically strong (accented)
3. Count backward from cadential points, assigning alternating accents and unaccents
4. The first sounding measure may thus be heard as metrically weak (upbeat)

## Circular Risk

Defining cadence independently of meter is essential but difficult — if cadence requires metrical placement, it cannot serve as an independent criterion for determining meter. This circularity is a theoretical problem Caplin identifies in Riemann's system.

# Context & Application

Schlusswirkung is central to Riemann's end-oriented metric theory and provides the most direct link between his metric and harmonic frameworks. In Burnham's analytical application (Ch. 16), the concept illuminates Beethoven's phrase rhythm: the repeated "8" functions in Op. 31 sonatas show how Schlusswirkung can be multiplied for comic or dramatic effect. The "6" function (antepenultimate) can delay Schlusswirkung by building up energy before the cadential discharge.

**Historical significance**: Riemann's foregrounding of cadential effect as a metric determinant represents a significant theoretical move, connecting formal structure (phrase endings) directly to metric perception.

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 431): The T-D cadence at measure 8 of a period creates the strongest Schlusswirkung. The tonic resolution is the goal of the entire period structure.

**Example 2** (p. 431): Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 283, ii. Riemann argues all Schlusswirkungen fall "in the middle of the measure," leading him to conclude Mozart "left out the bar lines of the accented measures."

**Example 3** (Burnham, pp. 453-454): Beethoven, Op. 31, No. 2 ("Tempest"), Period II. The "6" function (cadential 6/4) iterates repeatedly, "building up to a tremendous discharge of energy" at the eventual "8." The delay of Schlusswirkung creates dramatic effect — the "6" is like a capacitor building current until "7" leads to grounding "8."

**Example 4** (Burnham, pp. 451-452): Beethoven, Op. 31, No. 1, exposition. Nine "8" functions — the movement's "comic tic" of closural proliferation. The phrase feels like "a door that slams shut, only to pop back open."

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating every dominant-tonic progression as Schlusswirkung
  **Correction**: Schlusswirkung requires formal closure, not merely harmonic resolution; a mid-phrase V-I may not constitute Schlusswirkung

- **Error**: Assuming Schlusswirkung only occurs at perfect authentic cadences
  **Correction**: Half cadences, deceptive cadences, and other closural moments can create "a kind of Schlusswirkung"

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking cadential effect can determine meter without circularity
  **Clarification**: If cadence is partly defined by metric placement, using it to determine meter is circular. Caplin identifies this as an unresolved tension in Riemann's system

- **Confusion**: Believing Schlusswirkung supports "initial accentuation" (beginnings are strong)
  **Clarification**: Schlusswirkung specifically supports end-accentuation — it is the *ending* that receives accent, not the beginning
