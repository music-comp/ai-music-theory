# Schlusswirkung

## Quick Definition

The "cadential effect" whereby points of harmonic and formal closure create metrical accent, serving as one of Riemann's key criteria for determining metric weight based on the structural function of endings.

## Formal Definition

**Schlusswirkung** (German: "effect of cadence" or "cadential effect") is Riemann's principle that cadential arrivals—points where musical phrases reach closure—create metrical accent. This concept directly supports his *Auftakttheorie* by associating accent with endings rather than beginnings.

**Definition** (from *Elementar-Schulbuch der Harmonielehre*):
> "The harmonies that enter on the beginning of the fourth and eighth measures make *Schlusswirkungen* or *Schluss*-like effects, according to the extent to which the chords are suitable for the cadence."

**Components of Cadence**:
Traditional cadence involves multiple factors: harmonic, melodic, rhythmic, *and* metric. To use *Schlusswirkung* as expressed meter requires:
1. Identifying cadential effect INDEPENDENT of predetermined metric position
2. Then using that cadence to determine where accents fall

**Chord Suitability**:
- The tonic is most suitable for creating *Schlusswirkung*
- Other harmonies (including dominant) can create "a kind of *Schlusswirkung*" if they fall on appropriate metrical positions
- Consonant chords are required (dissonant dominant 7ths less suitable)

**Theoretical Problem**: Defining cadence independently of meter risks circularity—if cadence requires metrical placement, it cannot serve as an independent criterion for determining meter.

## Musical Context

Schlusswirkung is central to Riemann's end-oriented metric theory:

- Supports the *Auftakttheorie*: musical motion is directed toward goals (cadences)
- The "8" function in the eight-measure period is invariant and cadential
- Performers can determine correct barring by locating cadential points

**Relationship to Formal Structure**:
- Phrase endings correlate with metric accents
- Period structure reflects the privileged status of measure 8
- "Answer" follows "statement" at every level, with answer being accented

**Analytical Method**:
1. Identify *Schlusswirkungen* (cadential arrivals)
2. Count backward, assigning alternating accents and unaccents
3. First sounding measure may thus be heard as metrically weak (upbeat)

## Examples

### Basic

**Half cadence creating Schlusswirkung**:
```
Measure:  1    2    3    4
                    [Half Cadence]
          U    A    U    A
```
The dominant arrival in measure 4 creates *Schlusswirkung*, making it metrically strong.

**Authentic cadence at measure 8**:
```
Measures: 5    6    7    8
Harmony:  ...  ...  D    T
          U    A    U    A
                        [PAC]
```
The tonic resolution is the goal of the entire period structure.

### From Repertoire

**Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 283, ii**: Riemann argues that "all of the *Schlusswirkungen* fall in the middle of the measure," leading him to conclude Mozart misplaced his bar lines. The cadential points determine the "true" metric organization.

**Beethoven, Op. 31, No. 1**: The exposition features multiple "8" functions (cadential arrivals), each creating *Schlusswirkung*. The proliferation of cadential moments becomes a compositional characteristic.

**Beethoven, Op. 31, No. 2 ("Tempest")**: Period II reaches a climactic "6" function that iterates repeatedly, building toward a tremendous discharge at the eventual "8." The delay of *Schlusswirkung* creates dramatic effect.

## Common Confusions

- *Schlusswirkung* is NOT just any dominant-tonic progression—it requires formal closure
- Using cadence to determine meter risks circularity if cadence is defined metrically
- Not all theorists accept that endings create accent—initial accentuation is an alternative
- *Schlusswirkung* operates at multiple hierarchical levels (phrase, period, section)
- A half cadence can create *Schlusswirkung* even though it doesn't resolve to tonic
- The concept works best when formal and metric structures align naturally
- Riemann's claim that ENDINGS are accented contradicts intuition that BEGINNINGS receive emphasis
