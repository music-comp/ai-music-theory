# Quick Definition

Riemann's systematic theory of musical phrase structure, positing that all well-formed phrases follow hierarchical metric patterns based on regular groupings of 2, 4, and 8 measures with prescribed patterns of accentuation, forming a core component of his comprehensive music-theoretical system alongside harmonic function theory.

# Core Definition

Riemann's **Phrasierungslehre** (phrasing theory) proposed that musical phrasing follows strict hierarchical metric patterns. As Holtmeier notes in Chapter 1, "it was particularly on the practical disciplines of harmony and phrasing that [Riemann] intended to leave his mark." The theory posited metric regularity (well-formed phrases follow powers of 2), hierarchical structure (smaller units nest within larger ones), prescribed accentuation, and "logical" organization paralleling harmonic function.

In Riemann's late "Ideen zu einer 'Lehre von den Tonvorstellungen'" (1914), he explicitly listed "the harmonic concepts Tonic, Dominant, and Subdominant and the rhythmic concepts of heavy and light durations" as the fundamental elements of musical logic, integrating phrasing directly with harmonic function.

# Prerequisites

- **Harmonic function**: Phrasing theory is integrated with function theory; the T-S-D-T progression serves as a phrase archetype.
- **Auftakttheorie**: Riemann's end-accented metric model underlies his approach to phrase structure.

# Key Properties

1. **Metric regularity**: Well-formed phrases follow powers of 2 (2, 4, 8, 16 measures)
2. **Hierarchical nesting**: Smaller units nest within larger ones at multiple levels
3. **Prescribed accentuation**: Specific patterns of strong and weak positions at each level
4. **Integration with harmony**: Phrase structure reflects harmonic logic (T-S-D-T as phrase archetype)
5. **Pedagogical orientation**: Intended for performers and analysts as systematic principles for articulation

# Construction / Recognition

Riemann's ideal phrase model places metric weight at the end of each grouping level:
- 8-measure period: antecedent (mm. 1-4) + consequent (mm. 5-8)
- 16-measure sentence: two 8-measure periods, each subdivided into 4-bar phrases and 2-bar groups
- Riemann produced analytical editions of complete repertoires with phrasing annotations showing phrase boundaries, metric weight, and hierarchical grouping at multiple levels.

# Context & Application

During Riemann's lifetime (1849-1919), phrasing theory "occupied a central position in music-theoretical discourse" and was part of his attempt to systematize practical musicianship, connected to pedagogical reform movements. Beginning in the 1910s, however, the theory gradually lost its central position, was taken up by only a few successors, and "disappeared completely from 'modern' theories of function."

# Examples

**Criticism and decline**: Emil Ergo, one of Riemann's "faithful disciples," publicly criticized the phrasing theory and "made suggestions for improvement." In response, "Riemann retreated into resentful silence" -- an example of Riemann's hypersensitivity to criticism even from allies (Ch. 1, pp. 5-6).

**Modern descendants**: Later metric theories (Cooper/Meyer, Lerdahl/Jackendoff) share concerns with hierarchical organization, strong/weak patterning, and grouping structure, but differ in allowing greater flexibility (not locked to powers of 2), separating meter from grouping, and adopting less prescriptive approaches.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming phrasing theory was merely descriptive.
  **Correction**: It was systematic and prescriptive, proposing specific rules for how phrases should be structured and heard.

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Believing harmonic function theory was always Riemann's most important contribution.
  **Clarification**: During Riemann's lifetime, phrasing was as significant as harmony in his system; only function theory survived him with influence intact.

- **Confusion**: Thinking phrasing theory left no trace in modern theory.
  **Clarification**: Some Riemannian phrasing ideas persist in form analysis (period, sentence) even where his specific theory is forgotten.
