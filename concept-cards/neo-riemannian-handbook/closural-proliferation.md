# Quick Definition

The compositional strategy of accumulating multiple "8" functions (cadential arrivals) within a movement or exposition, creating comic or dramatic effects through the sheer repetition of closural gestures, as tracked by Riemann's periodic analysis of Beethoven's Op. 31 sonatas.

# Core Definition

Closural proliferation refers to the accumulation of multiple "8" functions (cadential goals) within a period or across a series of periods, a phenomenon Burnham traces through Riemann's analyses of the Op. 31 sonatas. In Op. 31, No. 1, the Vorhang's peremptory tonic cadence generates the first 8 function, followed by cadences in the dominant, subdominant of the subdominant, subdominant, and threefold tonic cadence — creating a "steady proliferation of 8 functions" that becomes "a comic tic of the movement" (pp. 448-449). By the end of the exposition, Burnham counts "a total of nine 8 functions" (p. 452). The phenomenon is not merely numerical: "Tracking Riemann's 8 function reveals a previously neglected comic tendency of this exposition: an over-the-top proliferation of closural figures" (p. 452).

# Key Properties

1. Multiple "8" functions accumulate within periods and across expositions
2. Each "8" represents a genuine cadential arrival (Schlusswirkung), not merely a notational artifact
3. The effect can be comic (Op. 31, No. 1: "a door that slams shut, only to pop back open") or dramatic (Op. 31, No. 2: storm that "keeps blowing through proffered 8s")
4. Vorhang measures contribute to closural proliferation by adding pre-period 8 functions
5. The proliferation connects Riemann's metric analysis to larger questions about expressive character
6. Each sonata in Op. 31 handles closural proliferation differently, revealing distinct compositional strategies

1. Track all "8" functions in Riemann's analysis of a movement

# Context & Application

Burnham uses closural proliferation as a lens to compare the three Op. 31 sonatas:
- **Op. 31, No. 1**: Comic proliferation — "the movement lives on the comic premise of the peremptory downbeat" (p. 452)
- **Op. 31, No. 3**: Delayed cadence through telescoping elongation — "delaying the definitive 8 for as long as possible" (p. 453)
- **Op. 31, No. 2 ("Tempest")**: Dramatic proliferation — an "implacable process that keeps blowing through proffered 8s like a storm that will not move off" (p. 455)

This tracking of 8 functions demonstrates the analytical payoff of Riemann's periodic method: it reveals compositional strategies that would be invisible without systematic function assignment.

# Examples

**Example 1** (pp. 447-449): Op. 31, No. 1, Periods I-II. Seven "8" functions by the end of Period II, generated by the Vorhang + Period combination repeated twice.

**Example 2** (pp. 451-452, Ex. 16.12): Op. 31, No. 1, Period IX (end of exposition). Nine "8" functions. "For a cartoonish sight-gag version of this effect, imagine the tonic arrival in measure 98 as a door that slams shut, only to pop back open on the following root-position dominant, slam shut again on the ensuing tonic, and so on."

**Example 3** (pp. 455-456, Ex. 16.15): Op. 31, No. 2 ("Tempest"), Periods IV-V. From the arrival of the second theme: "8, 7-8, 7-8, 7-8, 7-8, 7-8, 8, 8, 7-8." The prolonged dominant makes this "less about comic repetition... and more about an implacable process."

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating multiple "8" functions as analytical errors or over-counting
  **Correction**: Each "8" represents a genuine perceived cadential arrival; the multiplicity is analytically meaningful

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking closural proliferation has only one expressive character
  **Clarification**: The same phenomenon (multiple 8 functions) can be comic (Op. 31/1), dramatic (Op. 31/2), or tonally exploratory (Op. 31/3)
