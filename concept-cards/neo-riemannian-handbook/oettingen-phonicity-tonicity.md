# Quick Definition

Arthur von Oettingen's dual concepts for generating major and minor triads: **tonicity** identifies tones sharing a common fundamental (generating major), while **phonicity** identifies tones that project a common upper partial (generating minor) -- providing a logical if not acoustic mirror for the overtone-based construction.

# Core Definition

In Oettingen's *Harmoniesystem in dualer Entwickelung* (1866), the major triad is unified by **tonicity**: C, E, and G are the 4th, 5th, and 6th partials of the common fundamental C2 -- they share a "tonic fundamental." The minor triad is unified by **phonicity**: C, Eb, and G all project G as a common overtone (the "phonic overtone") -- they are fundamentals to a shared upper partial.

Riemann criticized Oettingen's derivation of phonicity as "pseudo-logic" -- "a vicious circle" (Ch. 5, p. 186) because it derives the minor triad by first positing its component tones, then finding their common overtone, rather than generating the triad from a single principle.

# Prerequisites

- **Harmonic dualism** -- Phonicity/tonicity are attempts to ground dualism acoustically
- **Overtone series** -- Tonicity is based directly on the overtone series; phonicity mirrors it

# Key Properties

1. Tonicity: Relationship between tones that share a common fundamental (acoustic reality)
2. Phonicity: Relationship between tones that project a common upper partial (logical construct)
3. Tonicity and phonicity are logically reciprocal but phenomenologically asymmetric
4. Phonicity requires tones to BE in pure intervallic relationships (demanding just intonation)
5. Riemann judged phonicity as circular reasoning

# Construction / Recognition

## Tonic Generation (Major Triad)
1. Start with a fundamental tone (e.g., C2)
2. Its overtones include C, E, G (partials 4, 5, 6)
3. These tones are "tonically unified" by sharing the fundamental C

## Phonic Generation (Minor Triad)
1. Take tones C4, Eb4, G4
2. Each projects overtones; look for a common upper partial
3. G6 appears as an overtone of all three (G4 at octave, Eb4 at major 10th, C4 at 12th)
4. These tones are "phonically unified" by projecting the common overtone G

## The Circularity Problem
- To find the phonic overtone, you must first have the minor triad's tones
- But the purpose was to generate those tones from a single principle
- This is the "vicious circle" Riemann identified

# Context & Application

Oettingen's system was historically significant as the most systematic pre-Riemannian formalization of harmonic dualism. His *Harmoniesystem* (1866) established the table of tone relations that became the prototype for Riemann's Tonnetz, and introduced systematic dual notation.

Oettingen was a strict advocate of just intonation -- the phonic principle requires pure tuning to guarantee that the overtones actually coincide. This led him to view Beethoven's enharmonic modulations as "compositional errors."

# Examples

**Major triad (tonic)**: C-E-G are partials 4:5:6 of fundamental C -- tonically unified.

**Minor triad (phonic)**: C-Eb-G all project G as common overtone -- phonically unified. But as Riemann noted, this gives the minor triad three or four "fundamentals" (C, Eb, G as independent generators), contradicting the idea of consonance as unity under a single *son generateur*.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Treating phonicity as acoustically equivalent to tonicity
  **Correction**: Tonicity arises naturally from a single fundamental's overtones; phonicity requires pre-selecting tones and finding their coincidence point

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Phonicity and undertones are the same concept
  **Clarification**: Oettingen derived minor through common upper partials (phonicity), not through a descending series from a single tone (undertones). Riemann initially preferred the undertone approach and later criticized Oettingen's method
