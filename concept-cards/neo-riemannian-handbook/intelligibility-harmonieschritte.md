# Quick Definition

Riemann's graduated ranking of harmonic progressions by their directness of comprehension, distinguishing 16 relationships that are immediately understood from 9 that require mental mediation through intermediate "skipped-over" chords.

# Core Definition

**Intelligibility** (*Verstaendlichkeit*) in Riemann's Harmonieschritte system refers to the degree to which a harmonic progression is directly comprehensible to the listener. Riemann divided his 25 Harmonieschritte into **directly intelligible** relationships (16) and those **requiring mediation** (9). Directly intelligible progressions are understood without reference to intermediate chords and "have cadential force in retrograde" (i.e., they can serve as approaches to the tonic). Progressions requiring mediation are understood through reference to a "skipped-over" Klang (*uebersprungener Klang*) and tend to create expectations that this mediating chord will follow. Engebretsen (Ch. 12) emphasizes that this hierarchy was Riemann's mechanism for constraining the combinatorial "chaos of possibilities" that his complete system would otherwise permit. Where Riemann carefully ranked and constrained these relationships, neo-Riemannian theory abandons the hierarchy entirely, treating all 24 operations as structurally equivalent.

# Prerequisites

- **Harmonieschritte**: The complete taxonomy within which intelligibility is assessed

# Key Properties

1. **16 directly intelligible**: Understood without mediation; have cadential force in retrograde
2. **9 requiring mediation**: Understood through a skipped-over Klang; better suited for modulation
3. **Criteria for directness**: Acoustic proximity of roots, common-tone count, diatonic membership, derivational simplicity
4. **Imperfect correlation**: Intelligibility does not perfectly align with any single metric (word length, common tones, etc.)
5. **Subjective element**: Riemann acknowledged his classifications included subjective judgment
6. **Constraining function**: The hierarchy limits acceptable progressions within a key

# Construction / Recognition

Directly intelligible (16):
- All Seitenwechsel (1)
- Quintschritte: schlicht, gegen, Quintwechsel (3 of 4; Gegenquintwechsel requires mediation)
- Terzschritte: all 4
- Kleinterzschritte: all 4
- Halbtonschritte: Leittonschritt, Gegenleittonschritt, Leittonwechsel (3 of 4; Gegenleittonwechsel is ambiguous)

Requiring mediation (9):
- Gegenquintwechsel (1)
- All 4 Ganztonschritte (understood as Doppelquintschritte — double fifths)
- Schlichte Tritonusschritt, Gegentritonusschritt, Tritonuswechsel (3)
- Gegenleittonwechsel (1)

Riemann's explanation for mediation: "The root interval is dissonant and therefore only indirectly understandable. Comprehension is aided by a mediating Klang closely related to both Klange" (Systematik, Ch. 12 appendix).

# Context & Application

The intelligibility hierarchy represents Riemann's primary mechanism for maintaining tonal coherence within his combinatorial system. Without such constraints, the 25 Harmonieschritte would permit "any chord following any other" — what Engebretsen characterizes as Riemann's "horror vision." In later editions of the *Handbuch*, Riemann further subordinated Harmonieschritte to function theory, writing that "the true guide through the labyrinth of possible harmonic successions is no longer the nomenclature of the Harmonieschritte but rather that of function."

Engebretsen observes that neo-Riemannian theory represents the realization of precisely this horror vision: all transformations are treated as equal, and coherence comes from transformational group structure rather than acoustic intelligibility.

# Examples

From the Systematik translation (Ch. 12 appendix):

Riemann on the **Ganztonschritte** (requiring mediation): "The Ganztonschritte, whose harmonic relationships, as Doppelquintschritte, are not directly intelligible because the root interval is dissonant and therefore only indirectly understandable."

Riemann on the **Leittonschritte** (directly intelligible despite distant root relationship): "Practice proves the opposite [of expected unintelligibility]. The Leitklaenge are suitable, actually quite excellent, for direct confrontation with the tonic and have closing force." He explains this as the effect of semitone proximity overriding harmonic-distance expectations.

Riemann on the Halbtonschritte as chromatic shifts: "The chromatic shifts are no empty folly; rather with them the masters achieve the most beautiful effects. For example, the sudden introduction of the a-major chord instead of the ab-major chord has a magical effect, like a sudden flash of lightning."

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming intelligibility is the same as compositional frequency
  **Correction**: "Requiring mediation" does not mean "never used"; Riemann acknowledges that these progressions appear in great music, but are understood through their mediating context

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Intelligibility is purely acoustic
  **Clarification**: Later Riemann grounded intelligibility in psychology, not acoustics; the hierarchy reflects how the mind processes harmonic connections, not physical sound properties

- **Confusion**: Neo-Riemannian theory proves the hierarchy wrong
  **Clarification**: Neo-Riemannian theory simply sets aside the hierarchy as an analytical constraint; the question of whether some progressions are more "immediately comprehensible" than others remains open
