# Quick Definition

Riemann's 1882 lecture-essay tracing the development of harmonic theory from ancient string ratios through Helmholtz's acoustics to his own psychological foundation, representing the pivotal moment when Riemann began shifting from acoustic to psychological justification of harmony.

# Core Definition

**"Die Natur der Harmonik"** ("The Nature of Harmony") was a lecture delivered at the Hamburg Conservatory on February 4, 1882, and published in Waldersee's *Sammlung musikalischer Vorträge* (vol. 4, 1882, pp. 159-190). Steege's chapter provides a full English translation with extensive commentary.

The essay traces a compact intellectual history of harmonic theory in five stages:
1. Ancient mathematical ratios (Pythagoras, Arabs, medieval theorists)
2. Zarlino's recognition of the third and the "double form" of chords
3. Rameau's fundamental bass and the overtone series
4. Helmholtz's physiological acoustics and its limitations
5. The psychological turn: Klangvertretung and Tonvorstellung

The essay's trajectory enacts Riemann's central argument: "The mistake Helmholtz made is now easy to recognize; he sought to explain from the nature of sounding bodies concepts that can only be explained from the nature of the perceiving mind" (Ch. 2, p. 83). The essay culminates in the introduction of *Tonvorstellung* as the foundation for a new theory of harmony.

Steege observes that the essay was "written at the moment when a budding psychological perspective was beginning to supersede Riemann's earlier acoustical and physiological perspective" (Ch. 2, abstract).

# Key Properties

1. Historical survey moving from physics → physiology → psychology
2. Introduces Klangvertretung as the psychological alternative to acoustic grounding
3. First appearance of the term Tonvorstellung in Riemann's theoretical vocabulary
4. Demonstrates that consonance/dissonance are psychological, not physical concepts
5. Proposes three hierarchical levels of tonal relations: tone-to-Klang, Klang-to-key, key-to-composition
6. Written for a general musical audience at the Hamburg Conservatory

# Construction / Recognition

## Structure of the Essay
1. Ancient theory: octave, fifth, third as fundamental intervals (Pythagoras → Hauptmann)
2. Zarlino and Tartini: discovery of the major/minor "double form"
3. Rameau: the *corps sonore* and overtone series
4. Helmholtz: physiological acoustics, beats, and the critique of minor
5. Oettingen and Hauptmann: harmonic dualism recovered philosophically
6. Riemann's synthesis: Klangvertretung as psychology, not physics

# Context & Application

Steege situates the essay within the broader 19th-century shift from physics to psychology as the grounding discipline for music theory. The essay's rhetoric "could hardly be more modern, even modernist" (Ch. 2, p. 55), with Riemann encouraging readers "to *attempt* something new, to venture, rather than, as hitherto, to *seek* something new."

Key intellectual interlocutors:
- **Helmholtz**: The primary target of Riemann's critique; his *Lehre von den Tonempfindungen* (1863) is the work being superseded
- **Oettingen**: Extended Klangvertretung to minor, giving it "unparalleled breadth"
- **Hauptmann**: Revived harmonic dualism through philosophical speculation
- **Lotze**: "Found the Achilles heel of the Helmholtzian system" by demanding principled differentiation of consonance/dissonance

The essay is also significant for Riemann's history of theory: the treatment of the "syntonic comma" (Didymus comma, 80:81), Hauptmann's letter-nomenclature for tones, and the progression from fifth-based to third-based tone relations.

# Examples

**Example 1** (Ch. 2, pp. 80-82): Hauptmann's recognition that "there are three directly intelligible intervals: the octave, the fifth, and the (major) third" — the founding insight that all intervals are "products and powers of the numbers 2, 3, and 5."

**Example 2** (Ch. 2, p. 83): The six-four chord paradox: physically "the most undisturbed and euphonious" chord, yet musically a dissonance. This single example proves the acoustic foundation is insufficient.

**Example 3** (Ch. 2, pp. 84-85): The three levels of tonal relations: tone within Klang, Klang within key, key within composition. "In a piece in C major, the G-major key plays the same role or a similar role to that played in a brief C-major cadence by the G-major chord."

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Reading "Die Natur der Harmonik" as a complete rejection of acoustics
  **Correction**: Riemann acknowledged acoustics and physiology as "supplementary sciences" — important but not foundational for music theory

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the essay represents Riemann's final position
  **Clarification**: The 1882 essay is transitional; the full psychological theory develops in "Ideen zu einer 'Lehre von den Tonvorstellungen'" (1914), over 30 years later

- **Confusion**: Conflating Steege's commentary with Riemann's text
  **Clarification**: Ch. 2 contains both Steege's scholarly introduction and his translation of Riemann's essay. The translated text runs approximately from p. 76 to p. 86.
