# Quick Definition

Riemann's insistence that the Harmonieschritte system must be constrained by key-based functional context to prevent a "chaos of possibilities" where any chord can follow any other, a concern that Engebretsen argues neo-Riemannian theory has deliberately realized.

# Core Definition

Engebretsen (Ch. 12) devotes her third section to Riemann's recognition that the Harmonieschritte system, if left unconstrained, would "portend the sort of harmonic practice embraced by neo-Riemannians." Riemann was explicitly aware that his complete taxonomy of 25 triadic relationships could, in principle, license "any chord following any other" — what Oettingen had called the "chaos of possibilities." Riemann's response was to impose **tonal coherence constraints**: intelligibility hierarchies (directly intelligible vs. mediated), functional subordination (all progressions evaluated relative to a tonic), and key-based limitations (diatonic relationships privileged over chromatic ones). In the *Musikalische Syntaxis* (1877), Riemann characterized the unconstrained combinatorial possibilities as a "horror vision"; in the *Handbuch* (4th edition, 1906), he subordinated the Harmonieschritte to function theory entirely: "the true guide through the labyrinth of possible harmonic successions is no longer the nomenclature of the Harmonieschritte but rather that of function."

# Prerequisites

- **Harmonieschritte**: The system requiring constraint

# Key Properties

1. **Key-based limitation**: Acceptable progressions are determined by their relationship to a governing tonic
2. **Intelligibility hierarchy**: Directly intelligible progressions are privileged over mediated ones
3. **Functional subordination**: In later Riemann, function theory supersedes Harmonieschritte as the organizing principle
4. **Diatonic priority**: Diatonic relationships are more "natural" than chromatic ones
5. **Modulation control**: More distant Schritte are "means of modulation" rather than "part of tonal harmony"
6. **Explicit recognition**: Riemann was consciously aware of what would happen without these constraints

# Construction / Recognition

Riemann's constraining mechanisms (reconstructed from Engebretsen, Ch. 12):

1. **Diatonic system**: Initial presentation limits to relationships between primary and secondary triads within a key
2. **Chromatic extension**: The Systematik extends to all 25 relationships but with evaluative constraints
3. **Intelligibility ranking**: 16 directly intelligible vs. 9 requiring mediation
4. **Cadential force test**: Progressions with cadential force are privileged
5. **Function theory override**: In later writings, function labels (T, S, D) supersede Schritt labels
6. **Cautionary advice**: "The digression from the tonic's closer relations must not appear unmotivated"

# Context & Application

The tonal coherence constraint is what neo-Riemannian theory explicitly removes. Where Riemann constrained the system to maintain key-based coherence, neo-Riemannian theory embraces the unconstrained group and finds coherence in transformational logic (group structure, cycle structure, voice-leading parsimony) rather than tonal-functional logic. Engebretsen observes that this means neo-Riemannian theory realizes precisely the scenario Riemann sought to prevent — making it both an heir to and a departure from Riemannian theory.

The distinction between **harmonic coherence** (the ability to connect chords through some logic) and **tonal coherence** (the ability to connect chords through a key-based hierarchy) is drawn sharply by Kopp, as cited by Engebretsen. Neo-Riemannian theory achieves harmonic coherence without tonal coherence.

# Examples

Riemann in *Musikalische Syntaxis* (1877, cited by Engebretsen Ch. 12): characterized the unrestricted combinatorial possibilities as a "horror vision" of theoretical chaos.

Riemann in the *Handbuch* (4th edition, 1906, cited by Engebretsen): "the true guide through the labyrinth of possible harmonic successions is no longer the nomenclature of the Harmonieschritte but rather that of function."

Riemann on distant Schritte (Systematik appendix, Ch. 12): "A coherent sense of tonality is disturbed by such Schritte, as it is scarcely possible to return directly to the tonic. In general, the introduction of more distant Schritte must be handled very cautiously and judiciously."

Riemann in "Degeneration und Regeneration in der Musik" (1908, cited by Engebretsen): explicitly addressed the concern that unconstrained harmonic freedom leads to "degeneration."

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming Riemann was unaware of the transformational potential of his system
  **Correction**: Engebretsen demonstrates that Riemann explicitly recognized and actively resisted the unconstrained possibilities

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Neo-Riemannian theory "fixes" a problem in Riemann's system
  **Clarification**: Neo-Riemannian theory does not fix Riemann's system; it intentionally removes constraints that Riemann considered essential, achieving a different kind of coherence (group-theoretic rather than tonal-functional)
