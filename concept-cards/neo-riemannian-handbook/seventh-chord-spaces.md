# Quick Definition

Extensions of neo-Riemannian voice-leading spaces from triads to seventh chords and other tetrachords, enabling transformational analysis of parsimonious voice-leading among four-note sonorities through Tonnetz-like geometric representations.

# Core Definition

Seventh chord spaces are geometric and transformational frameworks that extend the parsimonious voice-leading approach of neo-Riemannian triadic theory to tetrachordal sonorities. Gollin notes that he and others "have explored Tonnetz-like spaces for seventh chords" including "transformation groups on [0258], [0358], etc." and "voice-leading parsimony in tetrachordal contexts" (p. 382ff). These spaces treat seventh chords as objects in a voice-leading geometry where minimal voice-leading connections (analogous to the P, L, R operations on triads) define the structure of the space. The Doppelklänge concept provides the theoretical foundation: since seventh chords contain embedded triadic components, transformations can target these embedded triads.

# Prerequisites

# Key Properties

1. Seventh chord spaces are analogous to the Tonnetz but operate on four-note sonorities
2. Connections between chords are defined by parsimonious voice-leading (minimal semitone motion)
3. Different set classes ([0258] dominant seventh, [0358] minor seventh, [0148] major seventh, etc.) generate different space topologies
4. The DOUTH2 relation connects chords where two tones remain fixed and two move by parallel semitones
5. Cyclic structures analogous to hexatonic and octatonic systems emerge in tetrachordal spaces
6. Three-dimensional Tonnetze can represent the additional pitch-class degree of freedom

# Construction / Recognition

1. Select a set class of tetrachords (e.g., [0258] for dominant sevenths)
2. Identify all voice-leading connections where one or two voices move by semitone
3. Map these connections as edges in a geometric space
4. Identify the resulting cyclic or symmetric structures
5. Use transformational labels (analogous to PLR) to name the operations

# Context & Application

The extension to seventh chords addresses a limitation of classical neo-Riemannian theory, which focuses almost exclusively on consonant triads. Since much tonal music — especially nineteenth-century chromatic music — makes extensive use of seventh chords, these spaces fill an important analytical gap. Gollin's work, along with contributions by Adrian Childs and others, establishes the theoretical framework for this extension.

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 382ff): The [0148] tetrachord (e.g., C-E-G-B, a major seventh chord) participates in parsimonious voice-leading networks where two tones remain fixed and two move by semitone (DOUTH2 relation), analogous to PLR operations on triads.

**Example 2** (p. 396ff): In Ravel's Forlane from *Le tombeau de Couperin*, recurring [0148] tetrachords are connected by DOUTH2 relations, creating a parsimonious voice-leading network among dissonant sonorities.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming PLR operations apply directly to seventh chords
  **Correction**: Seventh chords require their own operation set; PLR applies to embedded triadic subsets

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking seventh-chord spaces are simply the Tonnetz with extra dimensions
  **Clarification**: While the geometric principle is analogous, seventh-chord spaces have fundamentally different topologies due to the additional pitch-class dimension
