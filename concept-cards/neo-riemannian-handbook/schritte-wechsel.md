# Quick Definition

The fundamental distinction in Riemann's Harmonieschritte system: Schritte (steps) connect triads of the same mode (major-to-major or minor-to-minor), while Wechsel (exchanges) connect triads of opposite mode (major-to-minor or minor-to-major).

# Core Definition

**Schritte** (singular: *Schritt*, "step") and **Wechsel** (singular: *Wechsel*, "exchange") constitute the fundamental binary classification of all triadic relationships in Riemann's system. A **Schritt** preserves the mode of the triad (major to major, or minor to minor) and is named by the root interval; a **Wechsel** reverses mode (major to minor, or minor to major) and is named by the root interval plus "Wechsel." The most basic Wechsel is the **Seitenwechsel** ("side exchange"), which changes mode on the same root and serves as one of the three generators of the complete system. Engebretsen (Ch. 12) notes that this distinction, combined with dualistic directionality (schlicht/gegen), yields a taxonomy where each root interval produces up to four relationships: schlicht Schritt, gegen Schritt, schlicht Wechsel, and gegen Wechsel. The Seitenwechsel is unique in having only one form (since it has zero root interval).

# Prerequisites

- **Klang**: The concept of consonant triads as harmonic objects with definite mode (major or minor)
- **Harmonic dualism**: The framework that gives directionality to the schlicht/gegen distinction

# Key Properties

1. **Mode criterion**: Schritte preserve mode; Wechsel reverse mode
2. **Root interval naming**: Both are named by the interval between roots
3. **12 Schritte, 12 Wechsel**: Including identity, 24 total (the complete group)
4. **Seitenwechsel as generator**: The fundamental mode-exchange operation
5. **Schlicht/gegen subdivision**: Each root interval yields up to 4 relationships via the mode and direction distinctions
6. **Inversional consistency**: The same Schritt or Wechsel name applies to inversionally related progressions in major and minor

# Construction / Recognition

The four-way classification for any root interval:

| | Same mode (Schritt) | Opposite mode (Wechsel) |
|---|---|---|
| **Schlicht** (with generation) | Schlichte [interval]schritt | [interval]wechsel |
| **Gegen** (against generation) | Gegen[interval]schritt | Gegen[interval]wechsel |

Example for the fifth interval:
- Schlichte Quintschritt: C+ to G+ (up fifth, same mode)
- Gegenquintschritt: C+ to F+ (down fifth, same mode)
- Quintwechsel: C+ to g- (up fifth, mode change)
- Gegenquintwechsel: C+ to f- (down fifth, mode change)

# Context & Application

The Schritt/Wechsel distinction maps onto the modern distinction between transposition-type and inversion-type operations: Schritte are analogous to transpositions (preserving set-class type), while Wechsel are analogous to inversions (changing set-class type, from a dualistic perspective). Klumpenhouwer (1994) revived this terminology as a corrective to the PLR system, which he argued inconsistently mixed dualist (Wechsel-type) and monist (transposition-type) operations. The PLR equivalences are: P = W0 (Seitenwechsel), L = W3 (Leittonwechsel), R = W4 (Terzwechsel).

# Examples

From Engebretsen's Ch. 12 discussion:

Schritte (mode-preserving):
- Quintschritt Q: C+ to G+ (both major)
- Terzschritt T: C+ to E+ (both major)
- Ganztonschritt Q^2: C+ to D+ (both major)

Wechsel (mode-reversing):
- Seitenwechsel: C+ to c- (same root, mode change)
- Terzwechsel: C+ to e- (major third + mode change = R transformation)
- Leittonwechsel: C+ to b- (leading-tone relation + mode change = L transformation)

In Riemann's dualistic framework, the same Quintschritt applies to both C+ to G+ (major: up a fifth) and to the dualistic minor equivalent (e- to a-: down a fifth in the direction of minor generation).

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Confusing the root interval of a Wechsel with the voice-leading interval
  **Correction**: Wechsel are named by the interval between roots (in a dualistic sense), not by the semitone motion in the moving voice

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: P, L, and R correspond to Schritte
  **Clarification**: P, L, and R are all Wechsel (mode-reversing); the PLR group contains both Schritte and Wechsel, but the three generators happen to be Wechsel

- **Confusion**: Schritte and Wechsel are neo-Riemannian inventions
  **Clarification**: They originate with Riemann in the 1880 Skizze; Klumpenhouwer (1994) revived them for neo-Riemannian use
