# Quick Definition

Harrison's approach to harmonic analysis that endows musical objects with "attitudes, orientations, and moods" of T, D, and S, restoring the sensuous dimension of hearing that neo-Riemannian theory's energetic focus tends to evacuate.

# Core Definition

Drawing on Ernst Kurth, Harrison argues that harmony exhibits two dimensions: sensuousness and energy. Neo-Riemannian theory models the energetic dimension well but evacuates the sensuous. Sensuousness "involves objects that have mass and substance and that, moreover, ask to be savored, appreciated, and even caressed by one's own voice" (Harrison, p. 553). Objects acquire "energetic properties of T, D, and S, which I have defined elsewhere as attitudes, orientations, and moods" (p. 553). By "endowing objects with attitudes and moods," Harrison "thereby allowed them tendencies and urges — which is to say, their own motive power. This move in effect makes musical motion the product of sensuous actors, placing the origins of such motion somehow within these actors" (p. 553).

# Prerequisites

- **Harmonic function** — T, D, S are the functional attitudes that constitute sensuousness
- **Objects-transformations dichotomy** — Sensuousness addresses the "evacuation of objects" problem

# Key Properties

1. Sensuous hearing involves objects with mass, substance, and quality
2. T, D, S function as "attitudes, orientations, and moods" rather than chord labels or operations
3. Endowing objects with attitudes collapses the being/doing distinction
4. Musical motion becomes "the product of sensuous actors" with internal motive power
5. T especially has "presence, substance, quality, and intrinsic power"
6. D and S require a concept of T; they are similarly constituted as substantial presences
7. The approach follows Kurth's recognition of sensuousness as counterweight to energy

# Construction / Recognition

## To Apply Sensuous-Functional Hearing

1. Listen for the "gravitational" pull of tonics — where chords feel like stable destinations
2. Identify the T, D, S attitudes of sounding harmonies (not just their chord labels)
3. Note where functional attitudes create tendency and direction
4. Recognize where sensuous hearing breaks down (absolute progressions, modal chromaticism)
5. Switch to transformational analysis where sensuous-functional analysis loses traction

# Context & Application

Harrison's sensuous-functional analysis is the counterweight to purely transformational approaches. It is most appropriate where tonal gravity is felt — where chords have recognizable functional character and discharge/charge patterns are audible. It becomes strained in absolute progressions where each chord proposes itself as a local tonic, creating what Harrison calls "too much tonality" rather than none (p. 573).

# Examples

**Nonatonic system, linking analysis** (pp. 558-559): Taking E+ as T, other triads in the (PL) cycle are "conceived as functional mixtures of various D and S components, as well as modal flavorings of T. Movement among and between the [037] elements is thus always in reference to the governing T."

**Nonatonic system, accumulative analysis** (pp. 558-559): Each numbered chord receives a "tonicizing discharge from the first" (retrospective) or the "tonicized first charges the second" (prospective). In Liszt's Eb piano concerto, the accumulation of subdominant-function tonics discharges through a final plagal cadence.

**Vaughan Williams, *Tallis Fantasia*** (pp. 567-568): In the Tallis theme, "the G-major chords can sound like dominants of a key on C yet are clearly acting as tonics of a mode on G" — the "motive power of the chords here is screwed up." Sensuous-functional analysis reveals the confusion; transformational analysis addresses what to do when function fails.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Applying sensuous-functional analysis to passages with no clear tonal center
  **Correction**: Harrison is clear that such passages require transformational analysis instead

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking "sensuous" means purely subjective or impressionistic
  **Clarification**: Harrison's functional attitudes are theoretically grounded in Riemann's T, D, S and Rameau's fundamental bass; they are systematic, not merely evocative
