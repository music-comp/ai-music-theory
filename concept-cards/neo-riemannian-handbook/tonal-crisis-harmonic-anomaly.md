# Tonal Crisis and Harmonic Anomaly

## Quick Definition

Passages where the tonal framework is stretched or torn, creating a "harmonic surplus" that overflows familiar tonal categories---the very passages that neo-Riemannian analysis typically takes as starting points but Riemann sought to contain.

## Formal Definition

**Tonal crisis** describes moments when:
- The "tonal bottom seems to drop out of the music"
- Harmonic events overflow the bounds of familiar tonal categories
- It takes cognitive effort to maintain tonal hearing
- Notation itself may fracture (key signature changes, enharmonic respellings)

These passages exhibit what Rings calls **harmonic surplus**---energy exceeding what can be contained by traditional functional labeling.

## The Paradigm Case: Schubert Impromptu mm. 78-82

In just over two measures:
- Progression from tonic Gb major through B minor to G minor
- "Menacing bass trill on C" announces the coming chord
- The G-minor chord (enharmonic minor Neapolitan) creates shock even when anticipated

**Signs of crisis**:
- Notation fractures: six-flat signature cancelled, then reinstated
- The ffz augmented sixth "effortfully hauls the music back"
- Correctly spelled (Ab minor), the chord would require 14 flats

## Riemann's Response: Containment

For Riemann, this passage serves the "final consolidation of the primary key" with "wholly excellent effect":
- Just one of several progressions to confirm tonic
- No mention of disruption or trauma
- The chord gets only passing mention despite its striking sound

His functional label requires **three modifications** to basic S:
1. Major S to minor (degree sign)
2. Leading-tone change of that (angle bracket)
3. Variant of *that* (v)

This straining of the system betrays "an anxiety, an attempt to contain the harmonic extravagance."

## Neo-Riemannian Response: Valorization

Neo-Riemannian analysts begin with such passages:
- The chromatic extreme serves as "first point of analytical entry"
- Voice-leading patterns are sought where tonal strain appears
- Enharmonic equivalence sidesteps notational complexity
- The passage is valued for its disruption of tonal norms

## The "Extratonal Surplus"

Rings describes the G-minor chord as emanating a "surplus of harmonic energy":

> "We can indeed hold the Gb tonic in our ears throughout the passage---thus retaining the minor-Neapolitan hearing---but it takes some effort to do so."

This effort registers in:
- The length of the intentional path back to tonic (PLPS)
- The fragility of maintaining cognitive contact with the tonal center
- The accumulation of flatward "shading" along the path

## Notational Pressure

Following Daniel Harrison, Rings notes how flatward motion "exerts pressure on the notation":
- Schubert's six-flat signature is already at "flatward extremity"
- Further motion forces enharmonic "snaps"
- Visual fissures on the page register tonal disruption

## What Makes a Crisis?

Criteria for identifying tonal crisis:
1. **Harmonic distance**: Chord very remote from tonic
2. **Cognitive strain**: Effort required to maintain tonal hearing
3. **Notational disturbance**: Enharmonic respellings, signature changes
4. **Affective intensity**: The passage "sounds" extraordinary
5. **Analytical resistance**: Traditional labels become awkward

## Analytical Values Revealed

How analysts respond to crisis reveals what they value:

| Riemann | Neo-Riemannian |
|---------|---------------|
| Contains within functional system | Explores extratonal logic |
| Demonstrates tonal law | Reveals voice-leading coherence |
| Sees confirmation | Sees disruption |
| Values the ordinary | Values the extraordinary |

## The Crisis as Starting Point

For neo-Riemannian analysis:
> "One thinks here of the many analytical forays into Parsifal that have begun not at the work's outset, but with the most chromatically distorted version of the Grail motive, very near the end of act III."

The analytical method moves outward from crisis to broader interpretation.

## Restoring Extremity

Rings's phenomenological approach seeks to restore the sense of crisis:
- Intentional paths show the "benighted realms" of the tonal space
- Qualitative intensity comes from distance traveled
- The fragility of the path reflects danger of losing contact with tonic

## Related Concepts

- **Prerequisite**: Functional analysis, voice-leading efficiency
- **Leads to**: Analytical values, phenomenological approach
- **See also**: Garden of Eden metaphor, monotonality principle

## Critical Insight

The tonal crisis represents the **limit case** for both paradigms:
- For Riemann: The boundary that must be defended
- For neo-Riemannians: The phenomenon that justifies their methods
- For Rings: The place where phenomenology is most needed
