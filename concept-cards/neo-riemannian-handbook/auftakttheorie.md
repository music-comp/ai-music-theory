# Auftakttheorie

## Quick Definition

Riemann's "theory of upbeat" in which every rhythmic grouping at every level of metrical organization is understood to be end-accented, with unaccented events functioning as upbeats directed toward accented goals.

## Formal Definition

**Auftakttheorie** (German: "upbeat theory") is Riemann's fundamental principle that all musical meter is end-accented. Unlike traditional views where accent marks the *beginning* of a structural unit (the measure), Riemann holds that in all cases and at every level of metrical organization, the accented event is an *end*, a goal of musical motion.

**Core Principles**:
- Musical motion is directed toward accented goals, not away from starting points
- Unaccents function as "upbeats" (Auftakte) at every hierarchical level
- The eighth measure of a period is the metrical goal; measures 1-7 are effectively "upbeat"
- Listeners engage in "active hearing" (*aktives Horen*), anticipating resolutions rather than passively receiving sounds

**Hierarchical Application**: The upbeat-to-downbeat impulse operates at multiple levels:
- Beat level: weak beats lead to strong beats
- Measure level: odd measures (1, 3, 5, 7) lead to even measures (2, 4, 6, 8)
- Two-measure level: measures 1-2 lead to 3-4; measures 5-6 lead to 7-8
- Four-measure level: measures 1-4 lead to 5-8
- Eight-measure level: the entire period culminates in measure 8

**Model Structure**:
```
Measures:   1   2   3   4   5   6   7   8
Function:   U   A   U   A   U   A   U   A   (U=unaccent, A=accent)
```

## Musical Context

Auftakttheorie reflects Riemann's broader aesthetic philosophy:

- **Active Hearing**: We do not passively relate an unaccent to a preceding accent; we actively direct attention to the coming accent
- **End-Orientation**: Musical structure is fundamentally goal-directed
- **Invariant Principle**: The final "8" function is invariant—every period must have this terminal accent

**Analytical Implications**:
- A period can never end on measures 1-4 (incomplete periods are always some version of 5-8)
- Local upbeats are more dispensable than local downbeats
- There can be downbeats without upbeats, but never upbeats without downbeats
- First measures are NEVER metrically strong in Riemann's mature theory

**Critique**: The theory conflicts with the principle of "initial accentuation" (Hauptmann) where beginnings receive accent. Most modern theorists find the two principles incompatible—one must choose.

## Examples

### Basic

**Eight-measure period structure**:
```
1-2   3-4   5-6   7-8
 U     A     U     A    (2-measure level)
  1-4         5-8
   U           A        (4-measure level)
       1-8
        A               (8-measure level, goal)
```

Every level shows the same upbeat-to-downbeat pattern.

**Metric answering relationships**:
- 2 "answers" 1
- 3-4 "answers" 1-2
- 5-8 "answers" 1-4
- Even measures carry *Harmoniewirkung* (harmonic effect)

### From Repertoire

**Beethoven, Op. 31, No. 1, Rondo theme**: Riemann's analysis shows each even-numbered downbeat as a harmonic resolution, perfectly conforming to the Auftakt model. The answering relationships are audible at three different levels.

**Beethoven, Op. 31, No. 3, Second theme**: The "8=8a" designation (two consecutive 8-functions) allows Riemann to preserve the model when surface events seem to contradict it.

## Common Confusions

- Auftakttheorie is NOT simply about anacrusis/pickup notes—it applies to all hierarchical levels
- The theory does NOT claim all music literally begins with an upbeat note; rather, first *measures* function as upbeats to second measures
- "End-accented" doesn't mean final notes are loudest—it means they are metrical goals
- This theory conflicts with initial accentuation theories—both cannot be simultaneously correct
- Riemann's "even = strong, odd = weak" is opposite to much common intuition
- The model is an a priori scheme, not derived from individual pieces (though expressed meter criteria may confirm it)
- Riemann's rebarrings (moving bar lines) are attempts to align notation with Auftakttheorie
