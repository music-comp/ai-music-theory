# Quick Definition

A structural feature of Romantic chromatic harmony in which triads related by major third form pairs or cycles that serve as basic units of harmonic motion, particularly prominent in the music of Franck and other late-Romantic composers.

# Core Definition

In Cook's analysis of Franck, major-third pairs (triads whose roots lie a major third apart) serve as the fundamental organizational unit. The 24 consonant triads group into major-third pairs: for example, {G minor, B minor} and {G minor, Eb minor}. Any given triad's two major-third partners themselves make a major-third pair: {B minor, Eb minor}. Thus nodes in Cook's transformational network contain "not two but three triads apiece" (Cook, p. 528). These major-third groupings reflect the chromatic organization of Franck's music, where root motion by major third is a primary structural feature alongside the traditional fifth relationships (pp. 524-530).

# Key Properties

1. Three triads of the same quality whose roots are separated by major thirds form a cycle (e.g., G-, B-, Eb-)
2. Major-third pairs serve as nodes in Cook's transformational network
3. Root motion by major third divides the octave into three equal parts
4. Triads within a major-third cycle share membership in the same directed voice-leading sum class
5. Major-third relations are a hallmark of Franck's harmonic style and late-Romantic practice generally

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify Major-Third Cycles

1. Identify triads whose roots are related by major third (4 semitones)
2. Group these into three-member cycles (e.g., C/E/Ab or Db/F/A)
3. Check whether the music moves between members of such a cycle
4. Note whether mode changes accompany the major-third root motion
5. In Franck: look for passages where major-third pairs structure the large-scale harmonic plan

# Context & Application

Major-third cycles are a defining feature of late-Romantic harmonic practice, appearing prominently in Wagner, Liszt, and Franck. In *Le chasseur maudit*, the initial riding music in G minor returns in B minor (major-third transposition), and the transitional music returns transposed up another major third. These large-scale major-third pairs create what Cook calls a "large-scale major-third pair with measures 76-139" for the section at mm. 140-202 (p. 534).

# Examples

**Franck, *Le chasseur*, large-scale structure** (pp. 534-535): G-minor riding music (mm. 76-128) returns in B minor (mm. 140-191). The chromatic transition also returns, transposed up a major third (mm. 192-203). This creates a symmetrical major-third structure spanning 127 measures.

**Network nodes** (p. 528): In Cook's transformational network, the "home" node contains {G-, B-, Eb-}. Other nodes contain: {F#-, Bb-, D-}, {E-, Ab-, C-}, and {F-, A-, Db-}. Movement between nodes is modeled by X and Y transformations.

**mm. 328-333** (p. 537): Major-third root motion supports the motivic 5-6 figure: C-minor/Ab-minor through D-minor/Bb-minor to E-minor/C-minor. Transposition of each major-third pair by whole tone adds another layer of chromatic organization.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming all major-third relations are equivalent
  **Correction**: The mode of the triads and the direction of motion matter — ascending vs. descending major third, same-mode vs. mode-changing

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Equating major-third cycles with chromatic mediant relations
  **Clarification**: Chromatic mediant is a broader category; major-third cycles specifically involve root motion by major third (4 semitones), one type of mediant relation
