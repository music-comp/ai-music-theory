# Quick Definition

A *Takttriole* ("measure triplet") is Riemann's ad hoc device for expanding the normative two-measure space between strong downbeats to a three-measure space, typically deployed between functions 6 and 8 as an extended wind-up to the cadential goal.

# Core Definition

The *Takttriole* (German: "measure triplet" or "bar triplet") is an expansion device in Riemann's periodic analysis that substitutes three measures for the normative two between strong downbeats. Burnham identifies it as an "ad hoc device that expands the normative two-bar space between functional downbeats to a three-bar space" (p. 453, n. 8). The device appears to be deployed exclusively between functions 6 and 8, "where it functions as a kind of extended wind-up to the cadential 8" (p. 453, n. 8). Burnham mentions it as an alternative analytical possibility for passages in Op. 31, No. 3 and Op. 31, No. 1 that Riemann chose not to use, suggesting it was available in his analytical toolkit but used sparingly.

# Key Properties

1. Substitutes three measures for two between strong downbeats
2. Appears to be used only between functions 6 and 8 (before the cadence)
3. Functions as an "extended wind-up" to the cadential arrival
4. An ad hoc device within an otherwise binary metric system
5. Riemann deploys it sparingly, preferring other expansion techniques when possible
6. Strong two-bar patterning in a theme may discourage Takttriole insertion

# Construction / Recognition

## Standard Two-Measure Unit
```
Function:  6    7    8
Measures:  [m]  [m]  [m]
           A    U    A
```

## With Takttriole
```
Function:  6    [Triole]    8
Measures:  [m]  [m]  [m]   [m]
           A    --------    A
```
Three measures occupy the space normally covered by two.

# Context & Application

The Takttriole reveals the tension in Riemann's system between its rigid binary structure and the realities of musical practice. When a passage does not divide neatly into two-measure units, the Takttriole provides an escape valve. Burnham notes cases where Riemann *could* have used a Takttriole but chose not to, suggesting he preferred to maintain the prevailing two-bar patterning when it was strong enough to resist disruption (p. 453, nn. 8-9).

# Examples

**Example 1** (p. 453, n. 8): Op. 31, No. 3, Second theme. Burnham notes that a Takttriole extending from 6 to 8 is an analytical possibility that Riemann did not use here, perhaps because "the prevailing two-bar patterning of this theme feels too strong to Riemann to break with the insertion of a Triole."

**Example 2** (p. 453): Op. 31, No. 3, Period VI. Burnham refers to a *Triole* (insertion of three measures for two between strong downbeats) in the epilogue material at the end of the exposition, described as running "from 6 to 8 via what Riemann calls a Triole."

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Applying Takttriole freely between any two functions
  **Correction**: Riemann appears to use it only between 6 and 8, not between other function pairs

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking the Takttriole means triple meter
  **Clarification**: It refers to three-measure groupings within an otherwise binary hypermetric system, not to beat-level triple meter
