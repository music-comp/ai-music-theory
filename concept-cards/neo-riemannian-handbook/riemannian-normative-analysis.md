# Quick Definition

Riemann's analytical method of measuring actual musical structures against his idealized eight-measure periodic prototype, where deviations from the norm reveal compositional individuality, logical necessity, and artistic development rather than structural deficiency.

# Core Definition

Riemannian normative analysis is the methodological approach underlying Riemann's metric and formal theory, in which actual music is measured against the eight-measure periodic prototype. Burnham identifies the core claim: Riemann views "naturally given categories that have come into existence historically" as making artistic creation "a logically necessary imperative" (Riemann, foreword to Beethoven analyses, cited by Burnham, p. 459). The analytical method assigns periodic functions to every measure, distinguishes essential from accessory material, and tracks deviations from the prototype to reveal compositional strategy. Riemann's stated goal is to "rescue musical art from the arbitrary and secure its place as a product of nature revealed through history" (p. 459).

The method yields significant analytical payoffs: "Riemann's eight-measure periodic designations mix empirical and theoretical evidence" (p. 445), and "puzzling out the motivations for his functional designations forces one to ask different kinds of questions of these themes" (p. 445). Burnham concludes that "the mechanics of Riemann's prototypical period stir our own analytical preoccupations about phrase rhythm in the classical style" (p. 459).

# Prerequisites

- **Periodic function** — The numbered functions (1-8) that the method assigns
- **Hierarchical metric organization** — The prototypical eight-measure period serving as the norm

# Key Properties

1. All music is parsed into realizations of the eight-measure periodic prototype
2. Deviations from the norm are not failures but sources of expressive individuality
3. The distinction between essential and accessory material is the key analytical operation
4. Every measure receives a function assignment, even in highly expanded periods
5. The method reveals "logical necessity" in musical structure
6. Riemann sees Beethoven's maturation as "ever firmer positioning of the actual structure" — increasing sophistication in handling the norm
7. The method applies both harmonic function (T, S, D) and metric function (1-8) simultaneously

# Construction / Recognition

## Riemann's Analytical Procedure

1. Identify periodic boundaries (cadential goals as "8" functions)
2. Assign periodic functions (1-8) to all measures
3. Distinguish essential stations (harmonic argument) from accessory material (insertions)
4. Note deviations from the prototype and their expressive effects
5. Track patterns across periods and movements

# Context & Application

Burnham characterizes Riemann's method as revealing a "second nature" of sonata form — the naturally given categories that historical development has made second nature to composers and listeners. The method excels at "profiling the many ways that Beethoven harnesses and amplifies the closural energy of a period, and — by extension — the closural energy of the classical style" (pp. 457-458). While the method can appear "inconsistent" and "ad hoc" in individual applications, its cumulative effect is to reveal compositional strategies invisible to other analytical approaches.

**Riemann's highest stakes**: "The continued operation of artistic fantasy in both productive and receptive modes with naturally given categories... is a fact of our spiritual life whose significance cannot be overestimated. These categories rid artistic creation of every vestige of caprice and make it into a logically necessary imperative" (Riemann, quoted by Burnham, p. 459).

# Examples

**Example 1** (pp. 447-458): The complete analysis of Op. 31 openings demonstrates the method in action. Each sonata's characteristic opening is parsed into periods, functions are assigned, and the resulting patterns reveal distinct compositional strategies (comic proliferation in No. 1, dramatic intensification in No. 2, elongated delay in No. 3).

**Example 2** (p. 451): Riemann's chronological arrangement of the Beethoven sonata analyses reveals "the imposing growth and strengthening of artistic powers" — normative analysis applied diachronically to trace compositional development.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Dismissing the method because individual function assignments seem ad hoc
  **Correction**: The cumulative analytical insights (closural proliferation, dramatic expansion strategies) justify the method even when individual assignments are debatable

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking normative analysis claims all music should be 8 measures
  **Clarification**: The 8-measure prototype is a conceptual norm, not a prescription; deviations are the analytically interesting content

- **Confusion**: Believing the method is purely objective
  **Clarification**: Burnham notes it "mixes empirical and theoretical evidence" and involves interpretive choices that reveal tensions in Riemann's system
