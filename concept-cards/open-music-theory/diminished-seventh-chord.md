# Diminished Seventh Chord

## Quick Definition

A fully diminished seventh chord (diminished triad plus diminished seventh) built most commonly on the leading tone (^7) in minor keys, notated as vii°⁷, consisting entirely of stacked minor thirds creating a symmetrical structure with maximum harmonic tension—containing three tendency tones that demand resolution (the leading tone, the diminished fifth, and the diminished seventh), functioning as a rootless dominant ninth chord (V⁹ without root) with intense drive toward tonic, capable of resolving to major or minor chords, and exploited in later periods for its enharmonic ambiguity allowing modulation to distant keys.

## Formal Definition

**Diminished seventh chord** (°7 or dim7) is a four-note chord consisting of:
- **Root**: Usually leading tone (^7)
- **Third**: Supertonic (^2) - minor third above root
- **Fifth**: Subdominant (^4) - diminished fifth above root
- **Seventh**: Submediant (^6 or ♭^6) - diminished seventh above root

**Structure**: Diminished triad + diminished seventh (°7)
- All intervals are minor thirds (symmetrical)
- Creates maximum tension and instability

**In C minor**: B°⁷ = B-D-F-A♭ (all minor thirds)
**In C major** (less common): B°⁷ = B-D-F-A♭ (requires ♭^6)

**Function**: Dominant (substitute for or intensification of V⁷)

## Chord Structure and Symmetry

**Symmetrical structure**:
```
Root:    B
+m3:     D
+m3:     F
+m3:     A♭
(+m3 returns to B an octave higher)

Four notes, all separated by minor thirds
Divides octave into four equal parts
```

**Enharmonic equivalence**:
```
B°⁷ = B-D-F-A♭
    = D°⁷ = D-F-A♭-C♭(B)
    = F°⁷ = F-A♭-B-D
    = A♭°7 = A♭-B-D-F

Same chord, different spellings
Only 3 unique diminished seventh chords (due to symmetry)
```

## Voice Leading and Resolution

**Standard resolution to I**:
```
vii°⁷: B-D-F-A♭   →   I: C-E-G-C
S:     A♭ → G          (♭^6 → ^5, dim7 down)
A:     F  → E          (^4 → ^3, dim5 down)
T:     D  → C          (^2 → ^1, down)
B:     B  → C          (^7 → ^1, leading tone UP)

Three voices resolve down by half step
Leading tone (B) resolves up to tonic
Maximum voice leading efficiency
```

**Tendency tones** (all three resolve):
1. **Leading tone (^7)**: Resolves UP to ^1
2. **Diminished fifth (^4)**: Resolves DOWN to ^3
3. **Diminished seventh (♭^6)**: Resolves DOWN to ^5

**Resolution to i or I**: Can resolve to minor or major tonic

## vii°⁷ vs. V⁷

**Relationship**:
```
V⁷:     G-B-D-F     (in C minor)
vii°⁷:  B-D-F-A♭    (leading tone diminished seventh)

vii°⁷ = V⁹ without root
Contains same upper structure as V⁹
Functions as dominant substitute
```

**vii°⁷ advantages**:
- More linear voice leading (all voices move by step or half-step)
- Intensified tension (three tendency tones)
- Avoids bass leap (compared to V-I)
- Creates chromatic bass motion (^7→^1)

**V⁷ vs. vii°⁷**:
- V⁷: Bass leaps (^5→^1), more stable root
- vii°⁷: Bass steps (^7→^1), more linear

## Inversions of vii°⁷

**vii°⁷** (root position):
- Bass: ^7 (B in C minor)
- Figured bass: °7 or 7
- Leading tone in bass resolves up

**vii°⁶₅** (first inversion):
- Bass: ^2 (D in C minor)
- Figured bass: ⁶₅
- Common, bass moves down to ^1

**vii°⁴₃** (second inversion):
- Bass: ^4 (F in C minor)
- Figured bass: ⁴₃
- Bass moves down to ^3

**vii°⁴₂ or vii°²** (third inversion):
- Bass: ♭^6 (A♭ in C minor)
- Figured bass: ⁴₂ or ²
- Bass moves down to ^5

**All inversions commonly used**, each with smooth bass resolution

## Enharmonic Modulation

**Symmetry enables reinterpretation**:
```
In C minor: B°⁷ = B-D-F-A♭ (vii°⁷, resolves to Cm)

Reinterpreted:
- As D°⁷ in E♭ minor (vii°⁷/E♭m)
- As F°⁷ in G♭ minor (vii°⁷/G♭m)
- As A♭°7 in B♭♭ minor (enharmonic)

Same sound, different function
Enables modulation to distant keys
```

**Romantic era exploitation**: Composers use enharmonic reinterpretation for sudden modulations

## Musical Context

Diminished seventh chords serve specific purposes:
- **Maximum tension**: Most unstable diatonic chord
- **Dominant function**: Substitute for or intensification of V⁷
- **Linear voice leading**: Smooth stepwise resolutions
- **Enharmonic ambiguity**: Enables distant modulations (Romantic period)
- **Three tendency tones**: Leading tone, dim5, dim7 all resolve
- **Dramatic effect**: Creates urgency and heightened emotion
- **Common in minor keys**: More idiomatic than in major
- **Symmetrical structure**: Mathematically elegant division of octave

vii°⁷ is one of the most distinctive and expressive chords in tonal music.

## Examples

### Basic

**vii°⁷-i in C minor**:
```
vii°⁷: B-D-F-A♭   (fully diminished seventh)
i:     C-E♭-G-C   (tonic minor)

B → C   (^7 → ^1, leading tone up)
A♭ → G  (♭^6 → ^5, dim7 down)
F → E♭  (^4 → ^3, dim5 down)
D → C   (^2 → ^1, down)

All voices move by half step or whole step
Smooth, intense resolution
```

**vii°⁶₅-i** (first inversion):
```
vii°⁶₅: D-F-A♭-B   (^2 in bass)
i:      C-E♭-G-C

D → C (bass, step down)
Smooth resolution to tonic
```

**In C major** (borrowed ♭^6):
```
vii°⁷: B-D-F-A♭    (requires A♭, borrowed from parallel minor)
I:     C-E-G-C     (major tonic)

Less common in major (needs chromatic alteration)
More typical of minor keys
```

**Enharmonic reinterpretation**:
```
B°⁷ in C minor → resolves to Cm

Same chord respelled:
D°⁷ in E♭ minor → resolves to E♭m

Same sound, different key
Enables modulation
```

**All inversions**:
```
vii°⁷:  B-D-F-A♭  → i
vii°⁶₅: D-F-A♭-B  → i
vii°⁴₃: F-A♭-B-D  → i⁶
vii°²:  A♭-B-D-F  → i⁶

Each resolves smoothly
```

### From Repertoire

**Mozart, Symphony No. 40, g minor**: vii°⁷ appears frequently, dramatic dominant substitute, intense voice leading in minor key.

**Beethoven, "Pathétique" Sonata**: Diminished sevenths create dramatic tension, particularly in development sections.

**Bach, Well-Tempered Clavier, minor key preludes/fugues**: vii°⁷ and inversions common, demonstrates Baroque use in minor keys.

**Romantic composers (Chopin, Schumann, Wagner)**: Exploit enharmonic ambiguity, use for distant modulations, dramatic harmonic shifts.

**Wagner, "Tristan und Isolde"**: Famous enharmonic reinterpretations of diminished sevenths, enables chromatic modulation.

## Common Confusions

- Diminished seventh chord = vii°⁷ (fully diminished seventh)
- Structure: diminished triad + diminished seventh (all minor thirds)
- In C minor: B°⁷ = B-D-F-A♭
- Function: Dominant (substitute for V⁷)
- Three tendency tones: ^7 (up), ^4 (down), ♭^6 (down)
- Symmetrical: divides octave into four equal parts (minor thirds)
- Only 3 unique diminished seventh chords (due to enharmonic equivalence)
- vii°⁷ = V⁹ without root (same upper structure)
- More common in minor keys (native ♭^6)
- In major, requires borrowed ♭^6 from parallel minor
- All inversions commonly used (⁶₅, ⁴₃, ²)
- Enharmonic reinterpretation enables modulation (Romantic device)
- Leading tone in bass (root position) resolves UP to tonic
- Maximum tension and instability
- Different from half-diminished seventh (ø7 or m7♭5)
- Fully diminished = diminished triad + diminished seventh
- Half-diminished = diminished triad + minor seventh
- "°7" symbol denotes fully diminished seventh
- Resolves to major or minor tonic (i or I)
- Creates smooth voice leading (all stepwise or half-step motion)
