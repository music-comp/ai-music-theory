# Quick Definition
A short tune that forms the melodic basis of a fugue, recurring throughout, typically analyzable as three parts: Kopfmotiv (opening gesture), Fortspinnung (prolongation), and cadence.

# Core Definition
The **fugue subject** is a short tune that forms the melodic basis of a fugue, recurring throughout the work. The source describes its structure as often analyzable in three parts, like many tonal phrases charting a course "from a distinctive opening to a generic cadence": (1) *Kopfmotiv* ("head motive") -- an opening gesture; (2) *Fortspinnung* -- prolongation that may include sequence or motor rhythm; (3) Cadence. Subject character types include toccata style (motor-rhythm feel, often chromatic, irregular rhythmic placement) and ricercar (slow-moving, antiquated feel, extensive use of suspensions) (Ch. 9, "Subject").

# Prerequisites
- **Counterpoint** -- The subject must work contrapuntally with other voices

# Key Properties
1. Short, memorable melodic line forming the fugue's thematic basis
2. Three-part structure: Kopfmotiv, Fortspinnung, Cadence
3. Toccata style: motor-rhythm, chromatic, irregular
4. Ricercar style: slow, antiquated, suspension-heavy
5. Recurs throughout the fugue in various voices and keys
6. Must be compatible with countersubject and invertible counterpoint

# Construction / Recognition
## To Analyze a Subject:
1. Identify the opening gesture (Kopfmotiv)
2. Locate the prolongation section (Fortspinnung)
3. Find the cadential conclusion
4. Determine character type (toccata or ricercar)

# Context & Application
The subject determines the character, affect, and contrapuntal possibilities of the entire fugue. Its structure and character guide decisions about the answer type (real or tonal), countersubject design, and overall fugal process.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Writing a subject that is too long or complex for effective contrapuntal combination
  **Correction**: Subjects should be short enough to work when combined with countersubject and free counterpoint

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The subject is always in the tonic key
  **Clarification**: While the initial statement is in the tonic, the subject may modulate at its end to the dominant
