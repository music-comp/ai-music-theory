# Compound Meter

## Quick Definition

A meter in which each beat divides naturally into three equal parts (and subdivides into six), rather than two.

## Formal Definition

**Compound meter** is a metrical organization where each beat divides into three equal parts and further subdivides into six. This contrasts with simple meter, where beats divide into two and subdivide into four.

Key characteristics:
- Each beat is represented by a dotted note value
- If the division unit is eighth note (bottom number = 8), the beat is a dotted quarter
- If the division unit is quarter note (bottom number = 4), the beat is a dotted half
- If the division unit is sixteenth note (bottom number = 16), the beat is a dotted eighth

Time signature interpretation in compound meter:
- **Top number**: Number of divisions per measure (always a multiple of 3: 6, 9, 12, etc.)
- **Bottom number**: Note value that gets one division
- **Number of beats**: Top number ÷ 3

Types based on beats per measure:
- **Compound duple**: 2 beats (time signatures like 6/8, 6/4, 6/16)
- **Compound triple**: 3 beats (9/8, 9/4, 9/16)
- **Compound quadruple**: 4 beats (12/8, 12/4, 12/16)

## Musical Context

Compound meters create a lilting, flowing character distinct from simple meters. The division into three gives a "long-short-short" feel within each beat, creating:
- Dance-like rhythms (jigs, tarantellas)
- Flowing, lyrical melodies
- Pastoral or gentle character

Common compound meters:
- 6/8: The most common compound meter, used in hymns, folk songs, many classical pieces
- 9/8: Compound triple, creates a broader, more expansive feel
- 12/8: Compound quadruple, often used in blues and slow ballads

## Examples

### Basic

6/8 time:
- 6 eighth notes per measure
- 6 ÷ 3 = 2 beats per measure (compound duple)
- Dotted quarter note gets the beat
- Count: "1 la li 2 la li"

9/8 time:
- 9 eighth notes per measure
- 9 ÷ 3 = 3 beats per measure (compound triple)
- Dotted quarter note gets the beat

### From Repertoire

6/8: "When Johnny Comes Marching Home," Irish jigs, Brahms's Lullaby
9/8: "Jesu, Joy of Man's Desiring" (Bach), second movement of Haydn's Sonata No. 42
12/8: "The House of the Rising Sun," slow blues ballads, "Exogenesis Symphony Part III" (Muse)

## Common Confusions

- 6/8 is NOT six beats per measure; it's TWO beats (each dividing into three)
- 6/8 ≠ 3/4: Both have six eighth notes per measure, but 6/8 groups them as 2 beats of 3, while 3/4 groups them as 3 beats of 2
- In compound meter, the beat is always a dotted note (dotted quarter in 6/8, dotted half in 6/4)
- The bottom number shows the division unit, not the beat unit
- Compound meters divide into THREE, simple meters divide into TWO—this is the essential difference
