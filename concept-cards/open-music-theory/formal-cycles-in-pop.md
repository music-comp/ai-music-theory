## Quick Definition

The recurring grouping of sections in verse-chorus form, typically progressing Verse-Prechorus-Chorus-Postchorus, that repeats throughout a song.

## Formal Definition

A cycle in verse-chorus form is a complete unit of formal sections that recurs through the song. Sections within a cycle always progress in a fixed order: Verse (V) - Prechorus (P) - Chorus (C) - Postchorus (Z), though not all sections need be present. The cycle represents one complete "rotation" through the song's core formal pattern.

## Musical Context

Understanding cycles helps identify the large-scale structure of verse-chorus songs. A typical song contains 2-3 cycles, often with modifications in later cycles (added sections, omitted sections, or extended repetitions of the chorus).

### Cycle Characteristics

- Sections always appear in V-P-C-Z order (when present)
- First cycle often establishes the pattern
- Later cycles may add or omit sections
- Final cycle frequently features multiple chorus repetitions
- Bridge (B) typically replaces V and/or P in one cycle

### Common Cycle Patterns

- V-C (minimal)
- V-P-C (common)
- V-P-C-Z (full)
- B-C (bridge cycle, often final)

## Examples

### Basic Example

Three-cycle song:
1. V - P - C
2. V - P - C - Z
3. B - C - C (with fadeout)

### From Repertoire

- "Livin' on a Prayer" by Bon Jovi:
  - Cycle 1: V (0:47) - P (1:18) - C (1:34)
  - Cycle 2: V (1:54) - P - C - Z (2:56)
  - Cycle 3 (abbreviated): begins 3:00, multiple C repetitions with fadeout

## Common Confusions

- **Cycle vs. section**: A cycle is a grouping of multiple sections; sections are the individual formal units
- **Fixed order**: Within a cycle, sections always progress V-P-C-Z; if something appears "out of order," it likely starts a new cycle
- **Bridge placement**: Bridges replace verse/prechorus within their cycle, not added between cycles
