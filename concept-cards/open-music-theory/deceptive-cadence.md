# Deceptive Cadence

## Quick Definition

A cadence where the dominant (V) resolves to a chord other than the expected tonic (I), most commonly to the submediant (vi) instead, creating harmonic surprise by subverting the listener's expectation for authentic cadence resolution—prolonging the phrase and demanding continuation toward an eventual authentic cadence, functioning as a "false" or "interrupted" arrival that extends harmonic tension rather than releasing it.

## Formal Definition

**Deceptive Cadence** (DC) is a cadence where dominant (V or V⁷) resolves to a substitute for tonic, avoiding the expected I chord and delaying closure.

**Most common form**: V → vi (dominant to submediant)

**Essential characteristic**: V moves to chord OTHER than I
- Creates surprise (subverts expectation)
- Delays closure (demands continuation)
- "Deceptive" because it "deceives" expectation for V-I

**Other substitutions** (less common):
- V → IV (rare, "retrogression")
- V → iii (rare)
- V → ♭VI (in minor, dramatic)

**Effect**: Surprise, delay, extension—requires eventual authentic cadence

## V-vi Deceptive Cadence

**Standard pattern**: V → vi (major keys) or V → VI (minor keys)

**Voice leading** (V-vi):
```
Soprano: ^7 → ^1 (leading tone resolves UP to tonic scale degree)
Alto:    ^5 → ^5 (common tone, stays same)  
Tenor:   ^2 → ^3 (stepwise motion)
Bass:    ^5 → ^6 (deceptive motion, skips over ^1 to ^6)

Leading tone STILL resolves to ^1 (in soprano/alto)
Bass avoids ^1, goes to ^6 instead
```

**Why vi?**
- Shares two notes with I (^1 and ^3)
- Sounds "close" to I but not quite
- ^1 still appears in vi chord (but not in bass)
- Leading tone can still resolve up to ^1

## Function in Phrase Structure

**Extends phrase beyond expected ending**:
```
Expected:
...IV - V - I [PAC, phrase ends]

Actual:
...IV - V - vi [DC, continues] - IV - V - I [PAC, actual ending]

Deceptive cadence prolongs phrase, delays closure
```

**Creates formal extension**:
- Phrase seems ready to end (V chord)
- Instead continues (vi)
- Requires additional music to reach I
- Adds weight and interest to eventual resolution

## Deceptive Cadence vs. Related Concepts

**vs. Authentic Cadence**:
- AC: V → I (expected resolution)
- DC: V → vi (unexpected substitute)

**vs. Half Cadence**:
- HC: Ends ON V (no resolution)
- DC: Resolves FROM V (but not to I)

**vs. Interrupted Cadence**:
- Same thing (alternate term for deceptive cadence)

## Musical Context

Deceptive cadences serve specific purposes:
- **Phrase extension**: Prolong phrases beyond expected ending
- **Harmonic surprise**: Create momentary shock or interest
- **Delay closure**: Build anticipation for eventual PAC
- **Emotional expression**: Suggest longing, incompleteness, uncertainty
- **Formal expansion**: Add length and weight to sections
- **Avoid premature closure**: Keep music moving when ending would be too soon

Common in Classical, Romantic, and popular music.

## Historical Context

**Classical era** (Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven):
- V-vi deceptive cadences common
- Used for phrase extension and surprise
- Often followed quickly by authentic cadence

**Romantic era**:
- Increased use for expressive effect
- Sometimes chains of deceptive cadences
- Delays resolution for emotional impact

**Popular music**:
- V-vi extremely common
- "Deceptive" effect familiar to modern ears
- Often used at end of verses before chorus

## Examples

### Basic

**Simple deceptive cadence** (C major):
```
Expected:  G  - C
           V  - I [would be PAC]

Actual:    G  - Am
           V  - vi [DC, surprise!]

Listener expects C, hears Am instead
```

**Voice leading** (V-vi in C major):
```
V:     G-B-D-F (V⁷)
vi:    A-C-E-A

Soprano: F → E (^4 → ^3)
Alto:    D → C (^2 → ^1, or B → C if ^7 → ^1)
Tenor:   B → A (^7 → ^6, or stays G)
Bass:    G → A (^5 → ^6, DECEPTIVE MOTION)

Leading tone (B, ^7) still resolves up to C (^1) in inner voice
```

**Phrase extension via deceptive cadence**:
```
mm. 1-4:  I - vi - IV - V [expect PAC...]
m. 5:     vi [DC, surprise!]
mm. 5-8:  vi - IV - V - I [PAC, actual ending]

Deceptive adds 4 bars to phrase
```

**In minor** (A minor):
```
V  - VI
E  - F

Dominant resolves to ♭VI (flattened submediant)
Dramatic effect in minor keys
```

**Multiple deceptions** (chain):
```
...V - vi - IV - V - vi - IV - V - I

Two deceptive cadences before final PAC
Extends harmonic tension significantly
```

### From Repertoire

**Beethoven, Piano Sonata Op. 13 ("Pathétique"), I**: Multiple deceptive cadences create dramatic tension, delaying resolution and extending phrases.

**Mozart, Symphony No. 40, I**: Deceptive cadence (V-vi) extends phrase at crucial moment, adds emotional weight.

**Bach, Chorales**: Frequent use of V-vi to extend phrases, especially at penultimate phrase endings.

**Haydn, String Quartets**: Witty use of deceptive cadences for surprise and humor, subverting listener expectations.

**Beatles, "Yesterday"**: Modern example, verse ends with deceptive cadence before continuing to chorus.

## Common Confusions

- Deceptive cadence = V moves to vi (or other substitute, not I)
- "Deceptive" because deceives expectation for V-I resolution
- Most common: V-vi (major) or V-VI (minor)
- Requires continuation to eventual authentic cadence (can't end on DC)
- Leading tone still resolves up to ^1 (in inner voice, not bass)
- Bass motion ^5-^6 creates the deception (avoids ^1)
- Not same as half cadence (HC ends ON V; DC resolves FROM V)
- Not same as imperfect authentic cadence (IAC is V-I; DC is V-vi)
- "Interrupted cadence" = alternate term for deceptive cadence (same thing)
- vi substitutes for I (shares ^1 and ^3 with tonic)
- V-IV also called deceptive (but rare, "retrogressive")
- Deceptive prolongs phrase (extends beyond expected ending)
- Can chain multiple deceptives (V-vi, V-vi, V-I)
- Not wrong or bad (deliberate compositional choice for extension)
- Creates surprise only if listener expects V-I (depends on context)
- ♭VI in minor (V-♭VI) particularly dramatic deceptive resolution
- Deceptive cadence extends harmonic rhythm (delays tonic arrival)
