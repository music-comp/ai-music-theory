# Cadenza Semplice

## Quick Definition

A four-stage cadential galant schema with soprano pattern do-re-re-do (^1-^2-^2-^1) over bass mi-fa-sol-do (^3-^4-^5-^1), creating a complete authentic cadence through the progression I6-ii6/5-V-I, representing the standard galant authentic cadence without the cadential six-four expansion of Cadenza Composta.

## Formal Definition

The **Cadenza Semplice** (Italian: "simple cadence") is the basic four-stage authentic cadence schema in galant music, providing a complete and satisfying close through pre-dominant, dominant, and tonic harmonies.

**Four-stage structure**:

| Stage | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|-------|---|---|---|---|
| Meter | W | S | W | S |
| Melody | do (^1) | re (^2) | re (^2) | do (^1) |
| Bass | mi (^3) | fa (^4) | sol (^5) | do (^1) |
| Figures | 6 | 6/5 | 5 | 5 |
| Roman numerals | I6 | ii6/5 | V | I |

**Key characteristics**:
- **Cadential function**: Provides authentic cadence closure
- **Four stages**: Complete progression without cadential six-four
- **Pre-dominant → Dominant → Tonic**: Standard cadential progression
- **Soprano repetition**: ^2 repeated at stages 2-3 (characteristic feature)
- **Bass ascent**: ^3-^4-^5 stepwise to dominant, leap ^5-^1 to tonic
- **Metric pattern**: W-S-W-S (weak-strong-weak-strong)
- **"Simple"**: No cadential six-four (unlike Cadenza Composta)

**Harmonic progression**:
- Stage 1: I6 (tonic in first inversion)
- Stage 2: ii6/5 (supertonic seventh in first inversion, pre-dominant)
- Stage 3: V (dominant in root position)
- Stage 4: I (tonic in root position, final arrival)

Full progression: I6 - ii6/5 - V - I (classic pre-dominant - dominant - tonic)

**Soprano melody**:
- Ascends ^1→^2 (stages 1-2)
- Sustains ^2 (stage 3)
- Descends ^2→^1 (stages 3-4)
- Neighbor-tone-like motion around tonic (^1-^2-^2-^1)
- Leading tone to tonic resolution occurs in inner voice or bass

**Bass line**:
- Stepwise ascent: ^3-^4-^5 (first three stages)
- Leap down fifth: ^5-^1 (stages 3-4, typical authentic cadence bass)
- Creates strong root motion: IV→V→I (if analyzed from root positions)

**Function**:
- **Authentic cadence**: Provides definitive tonic arrival
- **Formal closure**: Marks ends of phrases, sections, periods
- **Standard formula**: Most common galant cadence type
- **Pedagogical model**: Teaches basic authentic cadence structure

**Relation to other cadences**:
- **Cadenza Composta**: Adds cadential six-four between ii and V (five stages)
- **Cadenza Semplice**: Direct ii-V motion (four stages)
- "Semplice" (simple) distinguishes from "Composta" (compound/expanded)

**Variations**:
- Pre-dominant chord can be IV instead of ii6/5 (less common)
- Dominant may include seventh (V7) for stronger pull to tonic
- First inversion tonic (I6) standard but root position possible
- Metric placement can vary while maintaining harmonic progression

## Musical Context

The Cadenza Semplice serves essential purposes in galant music:
- **Standard cadence**: Most common way to close phrases
- **Formal articulation**: Marks structural boundaries
- **Harmonic clarity**: Unambiguous authentic cadence
- **Voice-leading model**: Teaches standard cadential approach
- **Baseline schema**: Foundation from which other cadences elaborate

The schema's ubiquity in galant music makes it the default cadential formula, with other cadences representing expansions or variations.

## Examples

### Basic

**Cadenza Semplice in C major**:
```
Stage 1 (W): Soprano C5 (do/^1), Bass E4 (mi/^3), I6
Stage 2 (S): Soprano D5 (re/^2), Bass F4 (fa/^4), ii6/5
Stage 3 (W): Soprano D5 (re/^2), Bass G4 (sol/^5), V
Stage 4 (S): Soprano C5 (do/^1), Bass C4 (do/^1), I

Soprano: C-D-D-C (^1-^2-^2-^1)
Bass: E-F-G-C (^3-^4-^5-^1)
Harmonic: I6 - ii6/5 - V - I
```

**Soprano ^2 repetition** (characteristic):
```
Stage 2: D5 (re/^2) over F4 (ii6/5)
Stage 3: D5 (re/^2) over G4 (V)

^2 sustained/repeated over changing harmony
Then resolves down to ^1 at final stage
```

**Bass line** (stepwise ascent + leap):
```
Bass: ^3 → ^4 → ^5 → ^1
      E  →  F  →  G  →  C (in C major)
      \_______/   leap down fifth

Stepwise approach to dominant, leap to tonic (authentic cadence)
```

**Cadenza Semplice vs. Cadenza Composta**:
```
Semplice (4 stages):  I6 - ii6/5 - V - I
Composta (5 stages):  I6 - ii6/5 - [Cad. 6/4] V7 - I
                                    ↑
                           Added elaboration

Semplice is direct, Composta expands with cadential six-four
```

**Harmonic skeleton**:
```
Pre-dominant (ii6/5) → Dominant (V) → Tonic (I)

Standard authentic cadence formula
Semplice = galant realization of this progression
```

### From Repertoire

**Mozart Piano Sonatas**: Cadenza Semplice appears at phrase endings throughout, demonstrating standard Classical-era cadence.

**Haydn String Quartets**: Uses Cadenza Semplice for routine phrase closures, reserving Cadenza Composta for stronger emphases.

**Mozart Symphonies**: Cadenza Semplice in transition sections and less emphatic cadence points.

**Clementi Sonatinas**: Pedagogical works feature clear Cadenza Semplice examples for teaching basic cadence.

**Galant keyboard sonatas** (J.C. Bach, Galuppi): Cadenza Semplice as workhorse cadence throughout movements.

## Common Confusions

- "Semplice" (simple) doesn't mean easy or basic—just not expanded with 6/4
- Cadenza Semplice still four stages (not "simpler" than four)
- Soprano ^2 repeated at stages 2-3 (characteristic feature, not error)
- I6 at stage 1 standard (first inversion), though root position possible
- Pre-dominant typically ii6/5 but IV also possible
- Cadenza Semplice vs. Composta: Semplice lacks cadential six-four
- Four-stage structure, not three (don't collapse ii and V into single stage)
- Bass ^5 to ^1 leap creates authentic cadence (not deceptive or half)
- Works in both major and minor modes with appropriate adjustments
- "Cadenza" here = cadence schema (not performer's improvised cadenza)
- Metric placement matters less than harmonic progression order
- Most common galant cadence type (ubiquitous in repertoire)
