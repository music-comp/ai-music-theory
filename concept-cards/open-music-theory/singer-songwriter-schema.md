## Quick Definition

The chord progression vi-IV-I-V (or I-V-vi-IV), common in 1990s singer/songwriter music, characterized by plagal approach to the major tonic and inherent tonal ambiguity.

## Formal Definition

The singer/songwriter schema is a four-chord cyclical progression using I, IV, V, and vi, most commonly ordered as vi-IV-I-V or its rotation I-V-vi-IV. In C major: Am-F-C-G or C-G-Am-F. The schema exhibits tonal ambiguity because either the major chord (I) or its relative minor (vi) can function as tonic, making the progression analyzable as i-VI-III-VII in minor.

## Musical Context

This schema became prevalent in the mid-1990s singer/songwriter movement but has roots in earlier music. Its tonal ambiguity serves songs exploring themes of uncertainty or emotional complexity.

### Tonal Ambiguity

Without a clear authentic cadence:
- IV-I creates plagal motion (weak resolution)
- VII-i creates stepwise motion (no leading tone)

Listeners may perceive either the major or minor chord as tonic based on:
- Surrounding progressions
- Which chord begins/ends phrases
- Melodic emphasis

## Examples

### Basic Example

In C major/A minor:
- As major: vi-IV-I-V (Am-F-C-G)
- As minor: i-VI-III-VII
- Rotation: I-V-vi-IV (C-G-Am-F)

### From Repertoire

- "Despacito" by Luis Fonsi & Daddy Yankee (2017) - Bm-G-D-A; ambiguous between D major and B minor
- "What About Love" by Heart (1982) - exploits major/minor ambiguity, moving between D minor verses and F major chorus
- "Alejandro" by Lady Gaga (2009) - chorus uses IV-I-V-vi rotation, creating deceptive cadence effect
- Various 1990s songs by Sarah McLachlan, Jewel, Joan Osborne

## Common Confusions

- **Major vs. minor interpretation**: The same chords can sound like major (I-based) or minor (vi-based) depending on context; both analyses may be valid
- **Singer/songwriter vs. doo-wop**: Singer/songwriter approaches I through IV (plagal); doo-wop approaches I through V (authentic)
- **Axis progression**: Sometimes called the "axis progression" - same schema, different name
