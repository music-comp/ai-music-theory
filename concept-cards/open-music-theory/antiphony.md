# Quick Definition
Antiphony is the passing of material between different instrumental groups (e.g., strings to winds), creating timbral variety and articulating formal structure. It ranges from whole blocks of material exchanged between sections (common in Baroque orchestral scores) to more subtle timbral alternations.

# Core Definition
Antiphony distributes material between groups, creating timbral variety and formal articulation. In Grieg's "In the Hall of the Mountain King," the tune repeats with only the timbral change from strings to winds as variation. Antiphony can operate at multiple scales: large blocks between full sections, or fine-grained exchanges within subsections.

# Key Properties
1. Material passed between instrumental groups
2. Creates timbral variety
3. Articulates formal structure
4. Can be large-scale (block exchange) or fine-grained

# Context & Application
Antiphony is a favored method for distributing material and creating variety. It is related to but distinct from dovetailing, which shares a continuous line rather than exchanging complete blocks.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Grieg, "In the Hall of the Mountain King"): Strings-to-winds antiphony at the repeat of the tune.
**Example 2** (Baroque concerto grosso): Systematic alternation between concertino and ripieno groups.

# Relationships
## Related
- **dovetailing** -- A complementary technique that shares a continuous line
- **orchestral-blend** -- Antiphony contrasts with blended writing

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Antiphony and dovetailing are the same
  **Clarification**: Antiphony exchanges complete blocks; dovetailing shares a continuous line
