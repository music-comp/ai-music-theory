# Quick Definition
A compositional form based on a bass line that repeats continually throughout a piece, with variety created through re-harmonization, imitation, and textural change in parts above the bass.

# Core Definition
**Ground bass** compositions are based on a bass line that repeats throughout the piece, usually exactly or nearly so. The source distinguishes three levels of specificity: **ostinato** (any repeating pattern in any voice), **ground bass** (a specific type of ostinato in the bass), and **chaconne/passacaglia** (sub-genres of Baroque ground bass in triple meter). "Every Chaconne or Passacaglia is also a ground bass, and every ground bass is also an ostinato, but the reverse is not true" (Ch. 10). Variety is created through re-harmonization of the bass to suggest tonicizations, lively imitation between upper parts, and textural/rhythmic variation.

# Prerequisites
- **Counterpoint** -- Upper parts are composed contrapuntally above the bass
- **Imitation** -- A key technique for creating variety

# Key Properties
1. Bass line repeats continually throughout the piece
2. Variety through re-harmonization, imitation, texture, rhythm
3. Re-harmonization can suggest tonicizations of other keys
4. Avoiding the main cadence at iteration boundaries varies phrase rhythm
5. Hierarchy: ostinato > ground bass > chaconne/passacaglia
6. Extremely popular in Baroque era (Purcell) and modern popular music

# Construction / Recognition
## To Compose:
1. Begin with a simple diatonic harmonization as a prototype
2. Create alternative harmonizations including tonicizations
3. Write melodic parts with imitative relationships between upper voices
4. Combine ideas into an overall piece balancing textural and harmonic interest
## To Recognize:
1. Listen for a repeating bass line pattern
2. Note how upper parts change while the bass stays constant

# Context & Application
Ground bass is "an extremely and enduringly popular form" used extensively in Baroque music (Purcell was "a particularly keen and expert protagonist"), popular music, and many styles in between. The source provides detailed analysis of Purcell's Sonata in G Minor (Z 807) showing how he varies harmony, uses imitation, and changes rhythmic values across iterations.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Using the same harmonization for every iteration of the ground
  **Correction**: Re-harmonize the bass to create variety and avoid harmonic monotony

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Ground bass, ostinato, chaconne, and passacaglia are synonymous
  **Clarification**: They form a hierarchy from general to specific; not every ostinato is a ground bass
