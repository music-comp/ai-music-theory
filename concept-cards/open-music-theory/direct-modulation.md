# Quick Definition

A direct modulation is an abrupt key change where the composer goes directly to the new key without using a pivot chord, typically occurring at phrase boundaries. Also called "phrase modulation."

# Core Definition

**Direct modulations** (also called "phrase modulations") are abrupt: the composer goes directly to the new key (OMT, Ch. IV.17). They tend to occur at phrase boundaries. To recognize a direct modulation in analysis, simply label the new key and continue with harmonic analysis.

# Prerequisites

- **Modulation** — Direct modulation is one of two basic modulation types

# Key Properties

1. Abrupt key change (no pivot chord)
2. Typically at phrase boundaries
3. Also called "phrase modulation"
4. Analysis: simply label the new key and continue

# Examples

**Example 1** (OMT Ch. IV.17, Example 1): Direct modulation in Joseph Bologne, String Quartet No. 4, I, mm. 15-29.

# Relationships

## Builds Upon
- **Modulation** — Direct modulation is the abrupt modulation type

## Contrasts With
- **Pivot chord** — Pivot chord modulation is subtle; direct modulation is abrupt

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking all modulations must have pivot chords
  **Clarification**: Direct modulations are common and perfectly valid, especially at phrase boundaries
