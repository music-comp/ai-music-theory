# Quick Definition

The smallest interval in Western music, equal to the distance between any key and the very next key (white or black) on a piano.

# Core Definition

A **half step** (semitone) is the smallest interval in Western musical notation. On the piano keyboard, a half step above any key is the key to its immediate right; a half step below is the key to its immediate left. Most white keys have a black key between them, but two pairs of white keys are natural half steps with no black key in between: E-F and B-C. These groupings of black keys into sets of two and three make navigation easier.

# Prerequisites

- **Pitch**: the concept being measured
- **Piano keyboard**: the physical layout that illustrates half steps

# Key Properties

- Smallest interval in the Western tuning system
- On piano: distance from any key to the very next adjacent key
- Natural half steps (no black key between): E-F and B-C
- In equal temperament: frequency ratio of approximately 2^(1/12):1
- In pitch-class integer notation: 1 semitone

# Construction / Recognition

On a keyboard, a half step is always the distance to the nearest neighboring key. Aurally, half steps sound close, tense, and unstable.

# Context & Application

Half steps are the building blocks of all scales and intervals in Western music. The pattern of half and whole steps defines scale quality (major, minor, chromatic, etc.). Leading tones resolve to the tonic by half step, creating the fundamental tension-resolution dynamic of tonal music.

# Examples

- C to C#/Db = half step (white to black key)
- E to F = half step (white to white, natural half step)
- B to C = half step (white to white, natural half step)
- G# to A = half step

# Common Errors

- Assuming all adjacent white keys are a whole step apart (E-F and B-C are half steps)

# Common Confusions

- Half step vs. whole step: a whole step equals TWO half steps
- On the keyboard, E-F and B-C look like they might be whole steps (both white keys), but they are half steps
