# Quick Definition

Modulation is a longer-term change of tonic (key change), contrasting with the temporary nature of tonicization. It can be introduced abruptly (direct modulation) or subtly (pivot chord modulation), and is confirmed by a cadence in the new key.

# Core Definition

**Modulation** involves a longer-term change of tonic, in contrast to the temporary nature of tonicization (OMT, Ch. IV.17). Two basic ways to introduce a new key:
1. **Direct modulation** (abrupt): The composer goes directly to the new key, often at phrase boundaries
2. **Pivot chord modulation** (subtle): A single chord diatonic in both keys serves as the transition point

Two pieces of advice for identifying modulations:
1. Cadences establish keys — identify cadences first to find where new keys occur
2. Consistent accidentals indicate potential modulation

Tonicization and modulation exist on a spectrum: modulations are longer and stronger (confirmed by cadence, continued in new key); tonicizations are shorter and weaker (no cadence, returns to home key).

# Prerequisites

- **Tonicization** — Modulation is the longer, stronger end of the tonicization spectrum
- **Cadence** — Cadences confirm new keys in modulation

# Key Properties

1. Longer-term key change (not temporary like tonicization)
2. Confirmed by cadence in the new key
3. Two types: direct (abrupt) and pivot chord (subtle)
4. Consistent accidentals signal potential modulation
5. Exists on a spectrum with tonicization (stronger/longer = modulation; weaker/shorter = tonicization)

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify Modulation:
1. Look for consistent accidentals
2. Find cadences that establish the new key
3. If cadence in new key + continued music in new key = modulation
4. If cadence in new key but returns immediately = weaker modulation

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: Transitions in sonata form; development sections; second themes
- **Common modulation targets**: In major: I to V, I to vi; in minor: i to III, i to v

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Labeling every tonicization as a modulation
  **Correction**: Modulation requires cadential confirmation and sustained presence in the new key

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking modulation and tonicization are binary categories
  **Clarification**: They exist on a spectrum; "extended tonicization" is the gray area between them
