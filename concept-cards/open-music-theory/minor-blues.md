# Minor Blues

## Quick Definition

A blues variation where the i and iv chords are minor seventh quality rather than dominant seventh, typically with a ii-V-I replacing the V-IV-I of the standard blues.

## Formal Definition

The **minor blues** adapts the 12-bar blues schema to a minor key context:

**Chord quality changes**:
- i chord: minor seventh (instead of dominant seventh)
- iv chord: minor seventh (instead of dominant seventh)
- V chord: remains dominant seventh

**Structural change in phrase 3**:
- Standard blues: V7 - IV7 - I7 - I7 (plagal motion)
- Minor blues: ii half-dim7 - V7 - i7 - i7 (ii-V-i motion)

The V-IV-I motion of standard blues sounds anticlimactic in minor (major V to minor iv), so the ii-V-i progression provides a more satisfying cadential motion.

**Basic minor blues structure**:
| i7 | i7 | i7 | i7 |
| iv7 | iv7 | i7 | i7 |
| ii half-dim7 | V7 | i7 | i7 |

## Musical Context

Minor blues provides a darker, more brooding character than major blues. The form became especially popular in jazz, where the ii-V-i motion in the final phrase connects blues harmony with jazz harmonic practice.

The minor blues allows for the combination of blues expressiveness with the harmonic sophistication of jazz ii-V progressions.

## Examples

### Basic

In C minor:
| Cm7 | Cm7 | Cm7 | Cm7 |
| Fm7 | Fm7 | Cm7 | Cm7 |
| Dm7b5 | G7 | Cm7 | Cm7 |

### From Repertoire

"The Thrill is Gone" (B.B. King, 1970): In minor, with bVI-V replacing ii-V in the final phrase.

"Mr. P.C." (John Coltrane): A jazz minor blues that became a standard.

"Equinox" (John Coltrane): Another well-known minor blues composition.

## Related Concepts

- **Prerequisite**: 12-bar-blues, ii-v-i-progression
- **Leads to**: jazz-blues
- **See also**: plagal-cadence, double-variations

## Common Confusions

- Only the i and iv change to minor; the V STAYS dominant
- The ii-V-i in phrase 3 is not optional; it replaces the V-IV-I that doesn't work well in minor
- Minor blues is still blues, not simply "jazz in minor"
- The minor seventh quality on i is different from dominant seventh; different sound and function
- Blue notes and the blues scale are still used over minor blues changes
