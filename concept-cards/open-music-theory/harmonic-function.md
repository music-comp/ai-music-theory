# Harmonic Function

## Quick Definition

The role a chord plays in tonal music based on its tendency toward stability or motion, categorized into three primary functions: tonic (stable, at rest, goal), predominant (prepares dominant, moves away from tonic), and dominant (unstable, pulls toward tonic)—creating the fundamental harmonic cycle T-PD-D-T that governs tonal progressions and defines the sense of departure, tension, and resolution in Western common-practice music.

## Formal Definition

**Harmonic Function** is the system of chord relationships in tonal music where chords are grouped by their role in creating and resolving harmonic tension, independent of their specific scale-degree identity.

**Three primary functions**:

**1. Tonic (T)**:
- **Chords**: I (primary), vi, iii (weak tonic substitutes)
- **Character**: Stable, at rest, resolved
- **Function**: Goal of harmonic motion, point of arrival
- **Feel**: "Home," conclusive, stable

**2. Predominant (PD)** (also called "Subdominant"):
- **Chords**: IV, ii (primary), vi (dual function)
- **Character**: Moves away from tonic, prepares dominant
- **Function**: Bridge between tonic and dominant
- **Feel**: Departure, preparation, "moving away from home"

**3. Dominant (D)**:
- **Chords**: V, vii° (primary)
- **Character**: Unstable, tense, pulls strongly to tonic
- **Function**: Creates tension demanding resolution to tonic
- **Feel**: Tension, urgency, "need to resolve"

## Functional Cycle

**Basic harmonic cycle**: T → PD → D → T
```
I  →  IV  →  V  →  I
(Tonic → Predominant → Dominant → Tonic)
```

**Expanded cycle**:
```
I → vi → ii → V → I
T    T    PD   D   T
```

**Functional rules**:
- **T can move to**: PD, D, or remain T
- **PD typically moves to**: D (occasionally back to T)
- **D typically moves to**: T (strong pull to resolve)
- **Avoid**: D → PD (backwards motion, weakens function)

## Chord Function by Scale Degree

**Major keys**:
```
I     ii    iii   IV    V     vi    vii°
T     PD    T     PD    D     T/PD  D
```

**Minor keys**:
```
i     ii°   III   iv    V     VI    vii°
T     PD    T     PD    D     PD    D
```

**Dual-function chords**:
- **vi**: Can function as tonic substitute OR predominant
  - After I: tends toward tonic function (I - vi)
  - Before ii or IV: tends toward predominant (vi - ii - V)
- **iii**: Weak tonic substitute, relatively rare

## Functional Progressions

**Strong functional progressions** (follow T-PD-D-T cycle):
```
I - IV - V - I        (T - PD - D - T, classic)
I - ii - V - I        (T - PD - D - T, common)
I - vi - IV - V - I   (T - T - PD - D - T)
```

**Weaker progressions** (skip functions):
```
I - V - I             (T - D - T, lacks PD preparation)
I - IV - I            (T - PD - T, lacks D tension)
```

**Retrogression** (backwards, weakens function):
```
I - V - IV - I        (avoid D → PD if possible)
```

## Functional Substitution

**Tonic substitutes**:
- vi for I (deceptive cadence: V - vi instead of V - I)
- iii for I (rare, weak substitution)

**Predominant substitutes**:
- ii for IV (or vice versa, both equally strong PD)
- vi as PD (when moving toward ii or V)

**Dominant substitutes**:
- vii° for V (shares leading tone and tendency)
- iii in minor (rare, weak dominant function)

## Musical Context

Harmonic function serves critical purposes:
- **Tonal motion**: Creates directed harmonic movement
- **Tension/resolution**: Establishes and resolves harmonic tension
- **Phrase structure**: Articulates formal divisions through cadences
- **Compositional logic**: Provides principles for chord selection
- **Analytical understanding**: Reveals why progressions work (or don't)
- **Pedagogical framework**: Simplifies complex chord relationships

Understanding function is essential for composition, analysis, and improvisation.

## Examples

### Basic

**Classic T-PD-D-T progression**:
```
C   -  F   -  G   -  C
I      IV     V      I
T      PD     D      T

Departure (IV), tension (V), resolution (I)
```

**With predominant substitute**:
```
C   -  Dm  -  G   -  C
I      ii     V      I
T      PD     D      T

ii substitutes for IV as predominant
```

**Extended functional progression**:
```
C   -  Am  -  Dm  -  G   -  C
I      vi     ii     V      I
T      T      PD     D      T

vi extends tonic, ii prepares dominant
```

**Weak progression (skips predominant)**:
```
C   -  G   -  C
I      V      I
T      D      T

Functional but less sophisticated (no PD preparation)
```

**Functional substitution (deceptive cadence)**:
```
C   -  F   -  G   -  Am  -  F   -  G   -  C
I      IV     V      vi     IV     V      I
T      PD     D      T      PD     D      T

vi substitutes for I (deceptive), requires continuation
```

### From Repertoire

**Bach, Chorale harmonizations**: Demonstrate classic T-PD-D-T cycles with ii and IV as predominants, V as dominant, I as tonic goal.

**Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 545, I**: Opening I-V-I shows basic T-D-T, later progressions include I-IV-V-I demonstrating full functional cycle.

**Beethoven, "Ode to Joy"**: Primarily uses I-IV-V-I functional progression throughout, clear pedagogical example of harmonic function.

**Bach, Well-Tempered Clavier, Prelude in C Major**: Functional progression I - ii⁶₅ - V⁷ - I demonstrates T-PD-D-T with inversions.

## Common Confusions

- Harmonic function = role in creating tension/resolution (not just chord identity)
- Three functions: Tonic (stable), Predominant (prepares), Dominant (tension)
- T-PD-D-T is the basic cycle (tonic → predominant → dominant → tonic)
- vi has dual function: tonic substitute or predominant (context-dependent)
- Predominant = subdominant function (both terms used)
- D → PD is retrogression (backwards motion, generally avoided)
- I-V-I functional (T-D-T) but lacks predominant preparation
- ii and IV both predominant (interchangeable, both strong PD)
- vii° functions as dominant (shares dominant function with V)
- Function independent of inversion (I⁶ still tonic function)
- Deceptive cadence: V-vi (D-T, vi substitutes for I)
- Not every vi-ii-V uses vi as PD (depends on context and voicing)
- Functional progression ≠ only allowed progression (just strongest)
- iii weak tonic, rarely used in practice
- Predominant prepares dominant (creates expectation for D → T resolution)
