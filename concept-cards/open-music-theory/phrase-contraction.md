# Quick Definition
The process of making a phrase shorter than expected, often creating a sense of abruptness at the cadence -- less common than expansion.

# Core Definition
A **contraction** occurs when "a phrase is shorter than we might expect it to be." Contractions are less frequent than expansions. The effect is typically an abrupt cadence: "As you listen, notice how the cadence feels abrupt, a feeling that usually accompanies contraction" (Ch. 4, "Contraction").

# Prerequisites
- **Phrase** -- Must understand expected phrase lengths

# Key Properties
1. Phrase shorter than expected
2. Less common than expansion
3. Creates abrupt feeling at cadence
4. Always occurs within a phrase (no external contractions)
5. Expected length inferred from archetype or context

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 4, Example 12): Elmas, Piano Concerto No. 1, III -- 4-measure c.b.i. followed by only 3-measure continuation.
