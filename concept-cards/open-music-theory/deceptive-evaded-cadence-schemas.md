# Deceptive and Evaded Cadence Schemas

## Quick Definition

Two closely related four-stage pre-cadential galant schemas that avoid expected tonic arrival: the Deceptive Cadence resolves V to vi (submediant) instead of I, while the Evaded Cadence resolves V to I6 (first inversion tonic) instead of root-position I, both creating incomplete closure that invites continuation rather than strong ending.

## Formal Definition

**Deceptive Cadence** and **Evaded Cadence** are pre-cadential schemas that set up the expectation of an authentic cadence but avoid the expected root-position tonic resolution, creating harmonic surprise and formal continuation.

## Deceptive Cadence Schema

| Stage | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|-------|---|---|---|---|
| Meter | W | S | W | S |
| Melody | do (^1) | re (^2) | re (^2) | do (^1) |
| Bass | mi (^3) | fa (^4) | sol (^5) | la (^6) |
| Figures | 6 | 6/5 | 5 | 5 |
| Roman numerals | I | ii | V | vi |

**Key characteristics**:
- **"Deceptive" resolution**: V→vi instead of expected V→I
- **Submediant arrival**: Bass moves to ^6 (not ^1)
- **Soprano still resolves**: ^2→^1 as expected in authentic cadence
- **Harmonic surprise**: Unexpected chord at moment of expected arrival
- **Continuation function**: Invites further music (not conclusive ending)

**Deceptive quality**:
- First three stages identical to Cadenza Semplice
- Stage 4 substitutes vi for I (bass ^6 instead of ^1)
- Soprano ^1 still arrives (partial satisfaction)
- Bass ^6 creates "wrong" chord (vi instead of I)
- Listener expects I, hears vi—hence "deceptive"

## Evaded Cadence Schema

| Stage | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|-------|---|---|---|---|
| Meter | W | S | W | S |
| Melody | do (^1) | re (^2) | re (^2) | do (^1) |
| Bass | mi (^3) | fa (^4) | sol (^5) | mi (^3) |
| Figures | 6 | 6/5 | 5 | 6 |
| Roman numerals | I | ii | V | I6 |

**Key characteristics**:
- **"Evaded" resolution**: V→I6 instead of expected V→I
- **Tonic in first inversion**: Correct chord (I) but wrong inversion
- **Weaker arrival**: I6 less stable than root-position I
- **Soprano resolves**: ^2→^1 as expected
- **Bass evades**: ^5→^3 instead of ^5→^1 (no root in bass)

**Evaded quality**:
- First three stages identical to Cadenza Semplice
- Stage 4 uses I6 instead of I (bass ^3 instead of ^1)
- Tonic chord arrives (correct harmony)
- But first inversion weakens closure (incomplete bass resolution)
- "Evaded" because full authentic cadence avoided

## Comparison

**Similarities**:
- Both avoid root-position tonic at stage 4
- Both create incomplete closure
- Both invite continuation
- Both begin identically to Cadenza Semplice (stages 1-3)
- Both function as pre-cadential (not conclusively cadential)

**Differences**:
- **Deceptive**: Wrong chord (vi), correct soprano resolution
- **Evaded**: Correct chord (I6), incomplete bass resolution
- **Deceptive**: More surprising (different chord entirely)
- **Evaded**: Less surprising (same chord, different inversion)

**Functional roles**:
- **Deceptive Cadence**: Creates harmonic surprise, requires additional cadence for closure
- **Evaded Cadence**: Softens arrival, permits gentle continuation or repetition
- **Both**: Prolong formal process, delay strong authentic cadence

## Musical Context

These schemas serve specific purposes in galant music:
- **Formal extension**: Avoid premature closure, allow phrase expansion
- **Harmonic variety**: Provide alternatives to predictable authentic cadence
- **Rhetorical effect**: Create surprise (deceptive) or gentleness (evaded)
- **Continuation invitation**: Weak closures prompt further music
- **Pedagogical models**: Teach alternatives to authentic cadence

Composers use these schemas when strong closure is inappropriate or when formal extension is desired.

## Examples

### Basic

**Deceptive Cadence in C major**:
```
Stage 1 (W): Soprano C5 (do/^1), Bass E4 (mi/^3), I
Stage 2 (S): Soprano D5 (re/^2), Bass F4 (fa/^4), ii6/5
Stage 3 (W): Soprano D5 (re/^2), Bass G4 (sol/^5), V
Stage 4 (S): Soprano C5 (do/^1), Bass A4 (la/^6), vi

Expected: Bass G→C (^5→^1, authentic cadence)
Actual: Bass G→A (^5→^6, deceptive cadence)
Soprano resolves as expected (D→C), but harmony is vi, not I
```

**Evaded Cadence in C major**:
```
Stage 1 (W): Soprano C5 (do/^1), Bass E4 (mi/^3), I
Stage 2 (S): Soprano D5 (re/^2), Bass F4 (fa/^4), ii6/5
Stage 3 (W): Soprano D5 (re/^2), Bass G4 (sol/^5), V
Stage 4 (S): Soprano C5 (do/^1), Bass E4 (mi/^3), I6

Expected: Bass G→C (^5→^1, authentic cadence)
Actual: Bass G→E (^5→^3, evaded cadence)
Tonic chord (I) arrives, but in first inversion (I6), weakening closure
```

**Comparison to Cadenza Semplice**:
```
Semplice:   I6 - ii6/5 - V - I   (strong authentic cadence)
Deceptive:  I6 - ii6/5 - V - vi  (substitutes vi for I)
Evaded:     I6 - ii6/5 - V - I6  (substitutes I6 for I)

All three identical through stage 3; differ only at resolution
```

**Functional continuation**:
```
[Theme] → [Deceptive Cadence: V→vi] → [Continues...] → [Authentic Cadence: V→I]
                                       ↑
                          Deceptive avoids closure, requires authentic to follow

Deceptive cadence typically followed by authentic cadence for true closure
```

### From Repertoire

**Mozart Piano Sonatas**: Frequent use of both schemas to extend phrases and avoid premature closure.

**Haydn String Quartets**: Deceptive cadences create formal surprise; evaded cadences soften phrase endings for continuation.

**Beethoven Sonatas**: Uses deceptive cadences for dramatic effect, particularly in development sections.

**Mozart Symphonies**: Evaded cadences in transitions where strong closure is inappropriate.

**Galant arias** (opera seria): Deceptive cadences extend vocal lines, delaying final cadence for dramatic effect.

## Common Confusions

- Deceptive ≠ wrong or mistake—it's deliberate compositional choice
- Deceptive vs. Evaded: vi vs. I6 (different chord vs. different inversion)
- Both schemas avoid strong closure (neither provides authentic cadence)
- Deceptive more surprising than Evaded (wrong chord vs. weak inversion)
- Soprano resolution identical in all three (Semplice, Deceptive, Evaded): ^2→^1
- Bass motion distinguishes them: ^5→^1 (authentic), ^5→^6 (deceptive), ^5→^3 (evaded)
- Deceptive cadence often followed by authentic cadence for closure
- Evaded cadence can lead to repetition, sequence, or authentic cadence
- "Deceptive" doesn't mean unsuccessful—it's rhetorically effective evasion
- Both are pre-cadential schemas (prepare for later authentic cadence)
- Work in both major and minor modes with appropriate adjustments
- Not every V-vi progression is Deceptive Cadence schema (need full four-stage pattern)
- vi at stage 4 can be substituted with IV in some deceptive cadences (other variant)
