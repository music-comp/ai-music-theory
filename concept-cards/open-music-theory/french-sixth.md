# French Sixth

## Quick Definition
The French sixth (Fr+6) is an augmented sixth chord containing four notes (^b6, ^1, ^2, and ^#4), distinguished by its inclusion of the supertonic (^2) which creates a whole-tone collection and gives the chord its distinctive "hollow" sound, resolving directly to the dominant with ^2 serving as a common tone.

## Formal Definition
**French sixth** (Fr+6) is an augmented sixth chord with:

**Structure**: Four notes
- ^b6 (lowered submediant) in bass
- ^1 (tonic) in middle voice
- ^2 (supertonic) in middle voice
- ^#4 (raised subdominant) in upper voice

**Interval content**:
- Augmented sixth (A6) between ^b6 and ^#4
- Major third (M3) between ^b6 and ^1
- Major second (M2) between ^1 and ^2
- Augmented fourth (A4) between ^1 and ^#4
- Major third (M3) between ^2 and ^#4

**Unique property**: All four notes form a whole-tone collection

**Function**: Chromatic predominant resolving to V

## Whole-Tone Structure

**The French sixth and whole-tone scale**:
```
Fr+6 in C: Ab-C-D-F#

Check: Ab - C - D - F# = all whole steps apart
       Ab to C = M3 (4 half steps = 2 whole steps)
       C to D = M2 (2 half steps = 1 whole step)
       D to F# = M3 (4 half steps = 2 whole steps)

All notes belong to same whole-tone collection
Creates characteristic "impressionistic" quality
```

**Symmetrical structure**:
```
Ab  -  C  -  D  -  F#
    M3    M2    M3
    
Tritones: Ab-D and C-F#
Two interlocking tritones create unique sonority
```

## Voice Leading

**Resolution pattern**:
```
Fr+6:  Ab-C-D-F#   ->   V:  G-B-D-G
       ^b6-^1-^2-^#4      ^5-^7-^2-^5

Voice leading:
Ab -> G  (^b6 descends by half step to ^5)
C  -> B  (^1 descends by half step to ^7)
D  -> D  (^2 remains as COMMON TONE to ^2)
F# -> G  (^#4 ascends by half step to ^5)
```

**Key feature**: ^2 is common tone with V
- D in Fr+6 = D in V (in C major/minor)
- Smooth voice leading advantage
- No parallel fifth problem (unlike German)

**Four-voice resolution**:
```
Soprano: F# -> G  (up by half step)
Alto:    D  -> D  (common tone)
Tenor:   C  -> B  (down by half step)
Bass:    Ab -> G  (down by half step)
```

## Spelling in Various Keys

**In C major/minor**: Ab-C-D-F#
**In G major/minor**: Eb-G-A-C#
**In D major/minor**: Bb-D-E-G#
**In A major/minor**: F-A-B-D#
**In E major/minor**: C-E-F#-A#
**In F major/minor**: Db-F-G-B

**Pattern**: Always ^b6-^1-^2-^#4 in any key

## Sound Quality

**Characteristic sound**:
- More dissonant than Italian or German
- "Hollow" or "open" quality from whole-tone structure
- Two interlocking tritones (^b6-^2 and ^1-^#4)
- Impressionistic foreshadowing
- Sounds like altered dominant seventh

**Comparison to V7 with altered bass**:
```
V7:   G-B-D-F  (dominant seventh in C)
Fr+6: Ab-C-D-F# (French sixth in C)

Similar function, different spelling
Fr+6 has ^b6 bass instead of ^5
Fr+6 has ^#4 instead of ^4
```

## Comparison with Other Augmented Sixths

| Chord | Notes | Structure | Common Tone with V |
|-------|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| It+6 | Ab-C-F# | M3 + A4 | None |
| **Fr+6** | Ab-C-D-F# | M3 + M2 + M3 | ^2 (D) |
| Ger+6 | Ab-C-Eb-F# | M3 + m3 + A2 | None |

**French is unique**: Whole-tone structure, common tone with V

## Musical Context

The French sixth serves specific purposes:
- **Distinctive color**: Whole-tone sonority unique among augmented sixths
- **Smooth resolution**: ^2 common tone creates elegant voice leading
- **No parallels problem**: Resolves directly without I64 (unlike German)
- **More dissonant**: Two tritones create maximum tension
- **Impressionistic precursor**: Whole-tone quality anticipates Debussy
- **Classical/Romantic**: Common from Mozart through Brahms
- **Dramatic predominant**: Intensifies cadential approach

## Examples

### Basic

**French sixth in C minor**:
```
Fr+6:  Ab-C-D-F#   V:  G-B-D-G
       |  |  |  |      |  |  |  |
Bass:  Ab    ->    G   (down by half step)
Tenor: C     ->    B   (down by half step)  
Alto:  D     ->    D   (COMMON TONE)
Sop:   F#    ->    G   (up by half step)

^2 (D) sustains through resolution
Smoothest voice leading of three types
```

**In progression**:
```
i  -  iv6  -  Fr+6  -  V  -  i
Cm    Fm/Ab   Ab-C-D-F#  G    Cm

Fr+6 replaces iv6 chromatically
D in Fr+6 becomes D in V (common tone)
```

### From Repertoire

**Mozart, Piano Concerto No. 20, K. 466, I**: French sixths in D minor, dramatic chromatic predominants.

**Beethoven, Piano Sonata Op. 13 "Pathetique"**: French sixth chords used for intense expression.

**Chopin, Ballade No. 1**: French sixths in chromatic passages, Romantic harmonic color.

**Brahms, Symphony No. 4, IV**: French sixth in passacaglia theme, creates distinctive tension.

## Common Confusions

- French sixth = four-note augmented sixth with ^2 (Fr+6)
- Spelling: ^b6-^1-^2-^#4 (in bass to soprano)
- In C: Ab-C-D-F# (includes supertonic D)
- **Four notes**: All four distinct (unlike Italian with doubled ^1)
- **Whole-tone collection**: All notes separated by whole steps
- **Common tone with V**: ^2 remains in both chords
- Resolves directly to V (no I64 needed)
- Two tritones: ^b6-^2 and ^1-^#4
- ^b6 descends by half step to ^5
- ^#4 ascends by half step to ^5
- ^1 descends by half step to ^7
- ^2 stays as common tone
- Named for supposed French origin (historical simplification)
- Function: Chromatic predominant (PD -> D)
- No root (unlike traditional chords)
- Most dissonant of three augmented sixth types
- "Hollow" sound from whole-tone structure
