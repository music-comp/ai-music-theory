# Exposition (Sonata Form)

## Quick Definition

The opening section of sonata form that presents the movement's main thematic material in a tonal conflict: the primary theme (P) in tonic, a transition (TR) that modulates, and the secondary theme (S) in a contrasting key (dominant in major, relative major in minor), followed by a closing zone (C), creating the fundamental tonal and thematic opposition that will be resolved in the recapitulation—typically repeated to establish the material firmly in listeners' ears.

## Formal Definition

**Exposition** (Latin: "setting forth") is the first main division of sonata form, functioning to present the principal themes and establish the tonal conflict that defines the movement's dramatic arc.

**Essential components**:

**1. Primary Theme Zone (P)**:
- **Key**: Tonic (I or i)
- **Function**: Establishes main theme and tonic key
- **Character**: Often assertive, energetic, or clearly defined
- **Structure**: Period, sentence, or hybrid theme type
- **Stability**: Tonally stable, cadences in tonic

**2. Transition (TR)**:
- **Key**: Modulates from I → V (major) or i → III (minor)
- **Function**: Creates momentum and energy toward new key
- **Character**: Unstable, sequential, goal-directed
- **Material**: Based on P or independent
- **Ending**: Medial Caesura (MC), often V:HC

**3. Secondary Theme Zone (S)**:
- **Key**: Dominant (V) in major keys, relative major (III) in minor keys
- **Function**: Presents contrasting theme in new key
- **Character**: Often more lyrical, relaxed, or feminine (traditional terminology)
- **Structure**: Usually period or sentence
- **Cadence**: PAC in new key (Essential Expositional Closure = EEC)

**4. Closing Zone (C)** (optional but common):
- **Key**: Remains in secondary key (V or III)
- **Function**: Confirms secondary key, provides codetta material
- **Character**: Cadential, conclusive, often energetic
- **Material**: Cadential formulas, new closing themes, or based on S/P
- **Ending**: Strong PAC in secondary key

**5. Repeat**: ||: Exposition :||
- Entire exposition typically repeated (especially Classical era)
- Repeat reinforces themes and tonal areas
- Performance practice varies (repeat sometimes omitted)

## Tonal Structure

**Major-key exposition**:
```
P          TR         S          C
I    →     →     →    V     →    V
Tonic      Modulation Dominant   Dominant

Tonal conflict: I vs. V established
```

**Minor-key exposition**:
```
P          TR         S          C
i    →     →     →    III   →    III
Tonic      Modulation Rel. Major Rel. Major

Tonal conflict: i vs. III established
(occasionally i → v, but III more common)
```

## Thematic Organization

**Two-theme exposition** (standard):
- P and S clearly differentiated
- Distinct thematic characters
- Clear formal boundaries

**Monothematic exposition** (Haydn):
- P and S use same basic material
- Tonal contrast primary (not thematic)
- Demonstrates S in new key, not just new theme

**Three-theme exposition** (less common):
- P, S1, and S2 in secondary key area
- Expanded secondary zone
- More lyrical/developmental space

## Formal Boundaries

**Medial Caesura (MC)**:
- Marks boundary between TR and S
- Creates "breath" or pause
- Common types: V:HC, I:HC
- Often includes fermata, rest, or clear articulation

**Essential Expositional Closure (EEC)**:
- First PAC in S zone
- Structural goal of exposition
- Confirms secondary key
- May be followed by additional S material or C zone

## Musical Context

The exposition serves critical functions:
- **Thematic presentation**: Introduces main musical ideas
- **Tonal conflict**: Establishes I vs. V (or i vs. III) opposition
- **Dramatic setup**: Creates tension requiring resolution
- **Listener orientation**: Provides material for development/recapitulation
- **Structural foundation**: Basis for entire movement

The exposition is the "argument" that development will explore and recapitulation will resolve.

## Examples

### Basic

**Simple Classical exposition** (32 bars):
```
P (mm. 1-8, C major, I):
  8-bar period in tonic
  Ends: PAC in I

TR (mm. 9-16):
  P motive fragmented and sequenced
  Modulates: I → V
  Ends: V:HC (Medial Caesura)

S (mm. 17-24, G major, V):
  Contrasting lyrical theme
  Ends: PAC in V (EEC = Essential Expositional Closure)

C (mm. 25-32, G major, V):
  Cadential material, scales, arpeggios
  Ends: Strong PAC in V

Exposition repeat: ||: mm. 1-32 :||
```

**Minor-key exposition**:
```
P (mm. 1-12, g minor, i):
  Primary theme, tonic minor
  PAC in i

TR (mm. 13-22):
  Modulates: i → III (Bb major)
  MC: III:HC

S (mm. 23-34, Bb major, III):
  Lyrical theme in relative major
  EEC: PAC in III

C (mm. 35-42, Bb major, III):
  Closing material in III
  Final PAC in III

Typical minor-key tonal plan (i → III)
```

**Monothematic exposition** (Haydn):
```
P (mm. 1-8, I):
  Main theme

TR (mm. 9-16):
  P motive developed, modulates to V

S (mm. 17-24, V):
  "P theme" presented in dominant key
  Same melodic material as P
  Tonal contrast, not thematic contrast

C (mm. 25-30, V):
  Cadential close in V

Haydn specialty: tonal drama primary
```

**Extended exposition with multiple S themes**:
```
P (mm. 1-20, I): Extended primary zone

TR (mm. 21-35): Modulatory transition

S1 (mm. 36-50, V): First secondary theme, EEC

S2 (mm. 51-65, V): Second secondary theme (lyrical variant)

C (mm. 66-80, V): Extended closing zone

Expanded secondary area (Romantic tendency)
```

### From Repertoire

**Mozart, Symphony No. 40, I, mm. 1-100**: Classic exposition with clear P (g minor), energetic TR, lyrical S (Bb major, III), codetta; demonstrates perfect Classical proportions and clarity.

**Beethoven, Symphony No. 5, I, mm. 1-124**: Monumental exposition with famous "fate motive" as P, dramatic TR, contrasting S horn theme in Eb (III), powerful closing zone.

**Haydn, Symphony No. 104, I (after introduction)**: Monothematic exposition, P material returns as S in dominant, demonstrates Haydn's preference for tonal over thematic contrast.

**Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 545, I, mm. 1-28**: Pedagogical example, transparent textures, clear formal divisions (P, TR, S, C), perfect for learning exposition structure.

**Beethoven, Piano Sonata Op. 2, No. 1, I**: Dramatic exposition with forceful P, energetic TR, lyrical S, extended C with virtuosic figuration.

## Common Confusions

- Exposition = first main section of sonata form (presents themes and tonal conflict)
- Typically repeated ||: Exposition :|| (whole section, not individual themes)
- P in tonic, S in dominant (major) or relative major (minor)
- Medial Caesura (MC) marks TR-S boundary (pause/articulation)
- Essential Expositional Closure (EEC) = first PAC in S zone
- Monothematic exposition uses same theme for P and S (Haydn specialty)
- Closing zone (C) remains in secondary key (confirms V or III)
- Tonal conflict established: I vs. V (will be resolved in recapitulation)
- Not all expositions have all elements (some omit C, some have brief TR)
- TR modulates in exposition (but not in recapitulation)
- Repeat signs traditional but sometimes omitted in performance
- Minor-key expositions usually i → III (not i → v)
- EEC is structural goal (first strong cadence in S zone)
- Development follows exposition (after repeat if taken)
- Exposition establishes material that development transforms
