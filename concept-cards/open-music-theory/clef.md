# Quick Definition

A symbol placed at the beginning of a staff that indicates which pitches are assigned to each line and space.

# Core Definition

A **clef** is a symbol written at the beginning of a staff that assigns specific pitch names to the lines and spaces of that staff. Without a clef, a staff can only indicate relative pitch (higher vs. lower); the clef provides absolute pitch identification. The four most commonly used clefs are treble (G clef), bass (F clef), alto (C clef on middle line), and tenor (C clef on second line from top). Different clefs make reading different pitch ranges easier by minimizing the need for ledger lines.

# Prerequisites

- **Staff**: the five-line grid on which notes are placed
- **Pitch**: the concept of highness/lowness that clefs help specify

# Key Properties

- Treble clef (G clef): wraps around the second line from bottom, designating it as G
- Bass clef (F clef): dot begins on the second line from top, designating it as F
- Alto clef (C clef): center indents around the middle line, designating it as C (middle C)
- Tenor clef (C clef): center indents around the second line from top, designating it as C
- The same pitch occupies different positions on the staff depending on the clef

# Construction / Recognition

Each clef has a distinctive visual shape. The treble clef has a sweeping curve; the bass clef has a backward-C with two dots; the alto and tenor clefs have two vertical lines with interlocking backward-Cs (sometimes drawn as a letter "K").

# Context & Application

- **Treble clef**: flute, violin, trumpet, soprano voice (higher instruments/voices)
- **Bass clef**: bassoon, cello, trombone, bass voice (lower instruments/voices)
- **Alto clef**: viola (mid-range instrument)
- **Tenor clef**: upper register passages for cello, bassoon, trombone

# Examples

- The pitch C4 (middle C) appears on the first ledger line below treble clef, first ledger line above bass clef, middle line in alto clef, and second line from top in tenor clef
- Treble clef lines (bottom to top): E, G, B, D, F ("Every Good Bird Does Fly")
- Bass clef lines (bottom to top): G, B, D, F, A ("Good Bikes Don't Fall Apart")

# Common Errors

- Omitting the clef from a staff (rendering pitch names ambiguous)
- Confusing alto and tenor clefs (both are C clefs but on different lines)

# Common Confusions

- A clef is required to specify absolute pitches; a staff without a clef cannot indicate specific pitch names
- The treble clef is also called "G clef," bass is "F clef," alto and tenor are both "C clefs" (but on different lines)
- The same staff position means different pitches in different clefs
