# Clef

## Quick Definition

A symbol placed at the beginning of a staff that indicates which pitches are assigned to each line and space.

## Formal Definition

A **clef** is a symbol written at the beginning of a staff that assigns specific pitch names to the lines and spaces of that staff. Without a clef, a staff can only indicate relative pitch (higher vs. lower); the clef provides absolute pitch information.

The four most common clefs in Western music are:

- **Treble clef** (G clef): wraps around the second line from bottom (G line)
- **Bass clef** (F clef): dots surround the second line from top (F line)
- **Alto clef** (C clef): center indents around the middle line (C line)
- **Tenor clef** (C clef): center indents around the second line from top (C line)

Different clefs make reading different pitch ranges easier by minimizing the need for ledger lines.

## Musical Context

Different instruments and voices use different clefs based on their typical range:

- **Treble clef**: flute, violin, trumpet, soprano voice (higher instruments)
- **Bass clef**: bassoon, cello, trombone, bass voice (lower instruments)
- **Alto clef**: viola (mid-range instrument)
- **Tenor clef**: upper register of cello, bassoon, trombone

The same pitch can be written in different clefs, appearing on different lines or spaces depending on which clef is used.

## Examples

### Basic

The pitch C4 (middle C) appears:

- On the first ledger line below the staff in treble clef
- On the first ledger line above the staff in bass clef
- On the middle line in alto clef
- On the second line from top in tenor clef

### From Repertoire

Piano music uses both treble and bass clefs (grand staff). Orchestral scores use multiple clefs appropriate to each instrument. String quartet scores use treble (violin), alto (viola), and bass (cello) clefs.

## Common Confusions

- A clef is required to specify absolute pitches; a staff without a clef cannot indicate specific pitch names
- The treble clef is also called "G clef" (it circles the G line), bass is "F clef" (dots mark F), alto and tenor are both "C clefs" (they mark different C lines)
- Reading music in an unfamiliar clef requires learning new associations between staff positions and pitch names
- The same notated position means different pitches in different clefs
