## Quick Definition

A refrain is a lyric-invariant passage within a larger section (too short to be its own section), while a chorus is a complete standalone section with primary lyrical content.

## Formal Definition

**Refrain**: A lyric-invariant (same words each time) passage contained within a section that is otherwise lyric-variant (different words). A refrain is typically a single phrase or less, appearing either at the end of a section (tail refrain) or the beginning (head refrain). Refrains are associated with strophic and AABA forms.

**Chorus**: A complete, standalone core section in verse-chorus form that is lyric-invariant and contains the primary lyrical material (title, hook). A chorus has heightened musical intensity compared to verses and typically spans multiple phrases.

## Musical Context

The distinction matters because it affects formal analysis:
- A song with refrains but no chorus is strophic or AABA
- A song with a chorus is verse-chorus form
- The terms should not be used interchangeably in formal analysis

### Refrain Types

- **Tail refrain**: Appears at the end of a strophe/verse
- **Head refrain**: Appears at the beginning of a strophe/verse

## Examples

### Basic Example

**Refrain**: "Blue suede shoes" at the end of each strophe in "Blue Suede Shoes"
**Chorus**: The entire "Livin' on a Prayer" section with "Woah, we're halfway there..."

### From Repertoire

Tail refrains:
- "Blue Suede Shoes" by Carl Perkins - title lyric at strophe endings
- "I Want to Hold Your Hand" by The Beatles - title lyric as tail refrain

Head refrain:
- "Cathedrals" by Jump, Little Children - "In the shadows of tall buildings..." opens each strophe

Chorus (not refrain):
- "Livin' on a Prayer" by Bon Jovi - entire repeated section is the chorus
- "Pride (In the Name of Love)" by U2 - chorus is a complete section with title lyrics

## Common Confusions

- **Colloquial vs. analytical usage**: In everyday speech, "chorus" and "refrain" are often interchanged; in music theory, they describe different formal units
- **Size matters**: Refrains are part of a section; choruses are complete sections
- **Form implications**: The presence of a true chorus (not just refrain) indicates verse-chorus form rather than strophic or AABA
