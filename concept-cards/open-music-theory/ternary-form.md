# Quick Definition
A three-section form (ABA) with an opening section, a contrasting middle section, and a return of the opening material, where A and B may each repeat independently but do not repeat together.

# Core Definition
**Ternary form** consists of three distinct sections with an ABA pattern: an opening A section, a contrasting B section, and a return of A (usually as A'). Each section may independently repeat (||:A:|| ||:B:|| ||:A':||), but A and B do not repeat together, nor do B and A. Ternary form is **compound** (or composite) if one or more sections comprises a complete musical form (often rounded binary); it is **simple** if sections do not contain complete forms. The B section provides contrast through key, mode, texture, time signature, rhythmic ideas, range, or instrumentation, while maintaining proportional length to A (Ch. 7).

# Prerequisites
- **Binary form** -- Ternary often contains binary forms as internal structures
- **Phrase** -- Each section comprises multiple phrases

# Key Properties
1. Three sections: A, B, A' (return)
2. Each section may repeat independently
3. Simple vs. compound: whether sections contain their own complete forms
4. B section provides contrast (key, mode, texture, etc.)
5. B may be stable (minuet/trio) or unstable (arias)
6. Common auxiliary sections: transitions, retransitions, prefixes, suffixes
7. Minuet & trio and scherzo & trio are common compound ternary genres

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Find three main sections
2. Verify the third section returns to A material in the home key
3. Check that A and B do not repeat together (distinguishes from rounded binary)
4. Determine if sections contain their own internal forms (compound vs. simple)

# Context & Application
Ternary form is used across many genres: minuet & trio, scherzo & trio, da capo arias, character pieces. The contrast between A and B creates a "departure and return" narrative. In compound ternary, each section may itself be a rounded binary form.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 7): Chopin, Polonaise in A major, Op. 40, No. 1 -- compound ternary with rounded binary sections.

**Example 2** (Ch. 7): Handel, "Waft Her Angels" from Jephtha -- ternary form in an aria.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing rounded binary with ternary form
  **Correction**: In ternary form, A and B are independent sections that repeat separately; in rounded binary, B and A' form a single reprise

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: ABC is ternary form
  **Clarification**: ABC is through-composed; ternary requires the return of A (ABA)
