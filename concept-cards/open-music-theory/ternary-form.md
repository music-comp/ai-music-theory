# Ternary Form

## Quick Definition

A musical form consisting of three distinct sections in an ABA pattern: an opening section, a contrasting middle section, and a return to the opening material.

## Formal Definition

**Ternary form** is a musical structure consisting of three sections with an **ABA** pattern: an opening **A** section, a contrasting **B** section, and a return to **A** (often labeled **A'** to indicate possible variation). Each section may repeat individually, but sections do not repeat together.

**Basic structure**:
- ||: A :|| ||: B :|| ||: A' :||
- A may repeat, B may repeat, A' may repeat
- But A and B do not repeat together, nor do B and A

**Two types**:
1. **Simple ternary**: Sections do not contain complete forms
2. **Compound ternary**: One or more sections comprise complete forms (often binary)
   - Common in minuet & trio, scherzo & trio
   - Often all three sections are complete forms (e.g., rounded binary)
   - 19th-century works sometimes simplify final A section

**Contrasting B section**:
- Provides contrast through: key, mode, texture, meter, rhythm, melody, range, instrumentation
- Length proportional to A section
- May be stable (minuet & trio) or unstable (arias)
- Stability relates to key relationships and phrase structure

**Harmonic characteristics**:
- Each section can be harmonically open (ending away from tonic) or closed (ending on tonic)
- Modulation possible within sections (rare in A, more common in B)
- B section often in different key (dominant, relative major/minor, parallel mode)

**Auxiliary sections**:
- Transitions, retransitions, prefixes, and suffixes possible
- Connect or frame the main ABA sections

## Musical Context

Ternary form serves important structural functions:

**Historical context**:
- Common in Baroque dance movements (da capo arias)
- Standard in Classical minuet & trio, scherzo & trio
- Prevalent in 19th-century character pieces
- Continues in 20th-century compositions

**Compositional uses**:
- Minuet and trio structure (compound ternary)
- Scherzo and trio movements
- Character pieces (nocturnes, mazurkas, waltzes)
- Da capo arias (ABA with written-out or improvised ornamentation on return)
- March form (march-trio-march)

**Relationship to other forms**:
- Compound ternary often contains binary forms within each section
- ABA vs. ABC: ABC is through-composed, not ternary
- Minuet-trio-minuet is compound ternary where each part is itself a form

**Expressive character**:
- A: Establishes main material
- B: Provides contrast, variety, development
- A': Return, resolution, completion

## Examples

### Basic

**Simple ternary structure**:
- A: 8 measures in tonic
- B: 8 measures in dominant with contrasting melody
- A': 8 measures, return to tonic with original material

**Compound ternary (minuet & trio)**:
- A (Minuet): ||: a :||: b a' :|| (rounded binary)
- B (Trio): ||: c :||: d c' :|| (rounded binary)
- A' (Minuet da capo): Abbreviated return or exact repeat

### From Repertoire

**Compound ternary**:
- **Beethoven, Piano Sonatas**: Minuet & trio movements
- **Mozart, Symphonies**: Menuetto & trio movements
- **Chopin, Mazurkas**: Many in ternary form
- **Brahms, Waltzes**: Often compound ternary

**Simple ternary**:
- **Chopin, Nocturnes**: Some use simple ternary
- **Schumann, Character pieces**: Album for the Young pieces
- **Mendelssohn, Songs Without Words**: Various examples

**Da capo arias (ternary)**:
- **Handel, Arias**: "Waft Her Angels" from *Jephtha*
- **Bach, Cantatas**: Many arias in da capo form
- **Mozart, Opera arias**: Da capo structure

## Common Confusions

- Ternary means three sections (A-B-A), not three phrases
- ABA is ternary; ABC is through-composed (not ternary)
- Repeats don't change the form—||: A :|| ||: B :|| ||: A :|| is still ternary
- Ternary vs. rounded binary: Both have returning A, but ternary has three separate sections; rounded binary has two reprises
- Compound ternary has complete forms within sections; simple ternary doesn't
- The return (A') may be varied or abbreviated—doesn't have to be exact
- Minuet-trio-minuet is compound ternary (each section is a complete form)
- B section provides contrast but should be proportional in length to A
- "Da capo" means return to the beginning (da capo al fine = play A again)
- Not every three-section piece is ternary—must have ABA pattern with contrast
- The three sections don't have to be equal in length
- Simple vs. compound refers to whether sections contain complete forms, not to complexity
