# Octatonic Scale

## Quick Definition

An eight-note symmetrical scale built by strictly alternating whole steps and half steps, dividing the octave into a pattern based on minor thirds--known in jazz as the "diminished scale" because it fits perfectly over diminished seventh chords, this collection became a favorite of Russian and Eastern European composers from Rimsky-Korsakov through Stravinsky, offering rich triadic possibilities (eight major/minor triads) while avoiding dominant-tonic function, creating an exotic, magical, or mysterious atmosphere.

## Formal Definition

**Octatonic scale** (octatonic collection, diminished scale) is an eight-note pitch collection with the following properties:

**Structure**:
- **Interval pattern**: 1-2-1-2-1-2-1-2 (half-whole) or 2-1-2-1-2-1-2-1 (whole-half)
- **Total**: 8 notes, 4 half steps + 4 whole steps = 12 semitones
- **Cardinality**: 8 pitch classes

**Symmetry**:
- **Rotational symmetry**: Pattern repeats every minor third
- **Transpositional equivalence**: Only THREE unique octatonic collections exist
- **Contains two interlocking diminished seventh chords**

**Naming conventions**:
- OCT0,1: Contains semitone C-C# (pitch classes 0 and 1)
- OCT1,2: Contains semitone C#-D (pitch classes 1 and 2)
- OCT2,3: Contains semitone D-Eb (pitch classes 2 and 3)

The subscript numbers indicate a characteristic half step in the collection.

## Construction

**Two rotations (modes) of the octatonic**:

**Half-whole (diminished scale in jazz)**:
```
Starting on C:
C - Db - Eb - E - F# - G - A - Bb - (C)
    H    W    H    W   H   W   H   W

Intervals: 1-2-1-2-1-2-1-2
```

**Whole-half (dominant diminished in jazz)**:
```
Starting on C:
C - D - Eb - F - F# - G# - A - B - (C)
   W   H    W   H    W    H   W  H

Intervals: 2-1-2-1-2-1-2-1
```

**The three unique collections**:
```
OCT0,1: C - C# - D# - E - F# - G - A - Bb
        Pitch classes: 0, 1, 3, 4, 6, 7, 9, 10

OCT1,2: C# - D - E - F - G - Ab - Bb - B
        Pitch classes: 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11

OCT2,3: D - Eb - F - F# - G# - A - B - C
        Pitch classes: 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9, 11, 0
```

## Internal Structure

**Two interlocking diminished seventh chords**:
```
OCT0,1 contains:
  - C°7 = C - Eb - Gb - Bbb (C - Eb - F# - A)
  - C#°7 = C# - E - G - Bb

These two dim7 chords, a semitone apart, combine to form OCT0,1
```

**Symmetry at the minor third**:
```
Transposing OCT0,1 by 3 semitones (m3):
C-C#-D#-E-F#-G-A-Bb becomes D#-E-F#-G-A-Bb-C-C#

Same pitch classes, different starting point
This is why only 3 unique collections exist (12/4 = 3)
```

## Harmonic Content

**Triadic content** (remarkably rich):
```
Within OCT0,1:

Major triads (8):     C, Eb, F#, A (roots form dim7)
Minor triads (8):     C#m, Em, Gm, Bbm (roots form other dim7)
Diminished triads:    C°, C#°, Eb°, E°, F#°, G°, A°, Bb°

No two triads are related by fifth!
Abundant third relations instead
```

**Seventh chord content**:
```
Available in each octatonic collection:
- 4 dominant seventh chords (e.g., C7, Eb7, F#7, A7)
- 4 minor seventh chords
- 4 half-diminished seventh chords
- 2 fully diminished seventh chords
- NO major seventh chords
```

**Key limitation**:
```
No root motion by perfect fifth possible
Therefore no functional dominant-tonic progressions
Root motion by third predominates
```

## Transpositional Equivalence

**Only three unique forms**:
```
Why three?
- Transposition by m3 (3 semitones) maps collection onto itself
- 12 semitones / 3 semitone period = 4 transpositions per collection
- But 12 total pitch classes / 8 notes per collection = 1.5
- Result: 3 distinct collections × 4 transposition equivalents each = 12 total positions

Or simply: 12 starting points / 4 equivalent positions = 3 unique collections
```

**Testing collection identity**:
```
Find any half step in the scale:
- C-Db present → OCT0,1
- C#-D present → OCT1,2
- D-Eb present → OCT2,3
```

## Musical Characteristics

**Tonal implications**:
- Rich in triadic harmonies (unlike whole-tone)
- Multiple key implications simultaneously
- No single tonic emerges naturally
- Creates "supernatural" or "magical" quality

**Distinctive features**:
- Tritone relationship between alternate scale degrees
- Each triad has another triad a tritone away
- Creates harmonic ambiguity with tonal vocabulary

**Common associations**:
- Russian nationalism (Rimsky-Korsakov, "Koschei's theme")
- Magic, sorcery, the supernatural
- Exotic or "oriental" topics
- Modernist harmonic language

## Musical Context

**Historical origins**: The octatonic scale appeared in Russian music by the mid-19th century, strongly associated with Rimsky-Korsakov, who used it for supernatural characters in his operas. It spread to his students and influenced Stravinsky, Bartok, and many 20th-century composers.

**Jazz usage**: Known as the "diminished scale," jazz musicians use:
- Half-whole over diminished 7th chords
- Whole-half (dominant diminished) over dominant 7th chords for altered extensions

**20th-century concert music**: Bartok, Stravinsky, and Messiaen used octatonic collections as organizing principles, sometimes in combination with other collections.

## Examples

### Basic

**OCT0,1 spelled from C (half-whole)**:
```
C - Db - Eb - E - F# - G - A - Bb - (C)
   1    2    1    2    1   2   1   2

Note the two diminished 7th chords:
C - Eb - F# - A (one dim7)
Db - E - G - Bb (= C# - E - G - Bb, other dim7)
```

**Available major triads in OCT0,1**:
```
C major:  C - E - G    (all in collection)
Eb major: Eb - G - Bb  (all in collection)
F# major: F# - A# - C# (A# = Bb, C# = Db, all in collection)
A major:  A - C# - E   (C# = Db, all in collection)

Roots spell a diminished 7th chord: C - Eb - F# - A
```

**Tritone relationships**:
```
In OCT0,1:
C major and F# major (tritone apart)
Eb major and A major (tritone apart)

Same with minor triads:
C#m and Gm (tritone apart)
Em and Bbm (tritone apart)
```

### From Repertoire

**Rimsky-Korsakov, _Kashchei the Immortal_**: The villain Kashchei is characterized by octatonic melodies and harmonies, creating an otherworldly, evil atmosphere.

**Stravinsky, _Firebird_**: Octatonic collection associated with magical/supernatural elements; diatonic collection with human characters.

**Stravinsky, _Petrushka_**: Combines octatonic elements with other collections.

**Bartok, "From the Island of Bali"**: Octatonic collection emerges from combining two hands playing different (0,1,6,7) tetrachords.

**Chopin, Ballade in G minor, m. 106ff**: Early Romantic use of octatonic over dominant harmony.

**Joan Tower, _Silver Ladders_**: Opening clearly articulates octatonic collection.

**Messiaen**: Uses octatonic as his "Mode 2" (mode of limited transposition).

## Common Confusions

- "Octatonic" means 8 notes (octa = 8), formed by alternating half and whole steps
- Only THREE unique octatonic collections exist (not 8 or 12)
- Two rotations exist: half-whole and whole-half (different starting interval)
- Jazz calls it "diminished scale"--same thing as octatonic
- Contains TWO interlocking diminished seventh chords
- Very different from whole-tone: octatonic has MANY triads, whole-tone has only augmented
- No major seventh chords possible, but dominant sevenths are available
- Roots of available major triads spell a diminished seventh chord
- Transposing by minor third gives the SAME collection
- The "magical" quality comes from triadic richness WITHOUT functional harmony
- NOT the same as the 8-note scales formed by adding one note to diatonic
