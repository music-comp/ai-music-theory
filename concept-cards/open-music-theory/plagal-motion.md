# Quick Definition

Plagal motion is the progression (IV)-I used to prolong tonic, most commonly after an authentic cadence or at the beginning of a phrase. The parentheses around IV indicate its prolongational (not predominant) function.

# Core Definition

**Plagal motion** is IV functioning not as a predominant but rather to prolong I (OMT, Ch. IV.10). In analysis, plagal (IV) is placed in parentheses to differentiate it from IV's more common use as a predominant. Plagal motion most commonly serves to prolong tonic in two places: (1) after an authentic cadence, or (2) at the beginning of a phrase in place of more common tonic prolongations involving dominant-function chords.

The source uses the broader term "plagal motion" rather than "plagal cadence" because in 18th-century classical music, (IV)-I more commonly occurs as a prolongation rather than as a true phrase ending. The term "plagal cadence" inaccurately implies that (IV)-I always ends a phrase.

# Prerequisites

- **Harmonic function** — Understanding that IV can function as either PD or as a tonic prolonger
- **Tonic prolongation** — Plagal motion is one method of prolonging tonic

# Key Properties

1. Progression: (IV)-I, with parentheses indicating prolongational function
2. Two typical locations: after an authentic cadence, or at a phrase beginning
3. Most common with both chords in root position (after AC)
4. (IV) to I6 possible at phrase beginnings but watch for parallels
5. The more standard prolongation at phrase beginnings uses inverted V7, not (IV)
6. fa-mi (^4-^3) at phrase beginning should default to V4/2-I6, not (IV)-I6

# Construction / Recognition

## Writing After an Authentic Cadence:
1. Both I and (IV) in root position
2. Make both complete (don't omit the fifth)
3. Double the bass in both chords
4. Move upper voices by step or common tone
5. Most common soprano: do (^1) sustained

## To Recognize:
1. Find IV-I where IV does NOT precede a cadential V
2. Check: does it prolong tonic (after a PAC or at phrase beginning)?
3. If yes: label as (IV)-I with parentheses

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: Post-cadential prolongation; phrase beginnings (less common)
- **Historical/stylistic notes**: Source illustrates with Handel, "Hallelujah Chorus" from Messiah (plagal motion after PAC). While true (IV)-I phrase endings do exist, they are less common than prolongational uses.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Not using parentheses around (IV) when it functions as prolongation
  **Correction**: Parentheses signal that IV is prolonging tonic, not functioning as a predominant

- **Error**: Defaulting to (IV)-I6 at phrase beginnings instead of V4/2-I6
  **Correction**: Dominant-function prolongation is far more common; fa-mi at phrase beginning usually = V4/2-I6

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Calling all (IV)-I progressions "plagal cadences"
  **Clarification**: The source prefers "plagal motion" because (IV)-I more commonly functions as a prolongation than as a true phrase-ending cadence
