## Quick Definition

A section between verse and chorus that builds energy and tension, preparing the listener for the chorus's arrival.

## Formal Definition

The prechorus is a core section in verse-chorus form characterized primarily by energy gain. It shares functional characteristics with the departure (d) phrase in srdc structure: fragmentation, acceleration of harmonic rhythm, movement away from tonic harmony, and harmonic openness. Historically, the prechorus evolved from the d section of srdc strophes as pop forms expanded.

## Musical Context

The prechorus creates anticipation for the chorus through:
- Increased rhythmic activity
- Rising melodic contour
- Harmonic tension (often dominant preparation)
- Textural buildup
- Dynamic crescendo

### Relationship to SRDC

The prechorus originated when srdc strophes expanded:
- **sr** became the verse section
- **d** became the prechorus section
- **c** became the chorus section

## Examples

### Basic Example

In a verse-chorus cycle:
V (on-tonic, stable) - P (off-tonic, building) - C (arrival, release)

## Common Confusions

- **Prechorus vs. climb**: A prechorus is a full section; a climb is a single phrase with prechorus function at the end of a verse or strophe
- **Prechorus vs. bridge**: Prechorus appears in every cycle before the chorus; bridge typically appears once in the song
- **Energy gain**: The defining characteristic - if a section between verse and chorus doesn't build energy, it may be a different formal unit
