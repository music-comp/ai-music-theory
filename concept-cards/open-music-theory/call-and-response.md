# Call and Response

## Quick Definition

A musical texture in which a melodic phrase (the call) is answered by another phrase (the response), common in blues where vocals alternate with instrumental fills.

## Formal Definition

**Call and response** is a performance practice in which music alternates between two contrasting elements:

**In blues context**:
- **Call**: The vocal, lyricized melody (typically in the first two bars of each four-bar phrase)
- **Response**: An instrumental fill (in the remaining two bars)

**Structural relationship to blues lyrics**:
The aab lyric structure of blues naturally creates space for call and response:
- Lyric line 1 (a) - call
- Instrumental response
- Lyric line 1 repeated (a) - call
- Instrumental response
- Contrasting lyric line (b) - call
- Instrumental response

**Origins**:
Call and response likely derives from the work songs of enslaved Africans and African Americans, where a leader would sing a line and workers would respond in unison.

## Musical Context

Call and response is fundamental to many African and African American musical traditions. In blues, the vocal phrases typically leave large gaps precisely to allow for instrumental responses - this is not empty space but an essential part of the musical structure.

This interaction between voice and instrument (or between different instruments in jazz) creates a conversational quality that defines much of the blues and jazz aesthetic.

## Examples

### Basic

In a 12-bar blues:
- Bars 1-2: Singer delivers first line (call)
- Bars 3-4: Instrument plays response (guitar, piano, harmonica)
- Bars 5-6: Singer repeats first line (call)
- Bars 7-8: Instrument responds
- Bars 9-10: Singer delivers contrasting line (call)
- Bars 11-12: Instrument responds (often with turnaround)

### From Repertoire

"Gulf Coast Blues" (Clarence Williams, recorded by Bessie Smith, 1923): Each lyric line is sung in the first two bars of its phrase, with Clarence Williams's piano responding in the remaining bars.

Call and response appears in gospel music, jazz (trading fours/eights), and many pop and rock songs influenced by these traditions.

## Common Confusions

- The "response" is NOT just filler; it's an essential part of the musical conversation
- Call and response is a TEXTURE/PRACTICE, not a form (it occurs within forms like 12-bar blues)
- Not all alternation between elements is call and response; there should be a conversational quality
- In jazz, call and response can occur between any instruments, not just voice and accompaniment
- The response often complements or comments on the call, not just imitates it
