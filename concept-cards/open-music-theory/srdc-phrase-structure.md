## Quick Definition

A four-phrase sentential structure in pop/rock music: Statement, Restatement/Response, Departure, Conclusion.

## Formal Definition

SRDC (coined by Walter Everett) describes a four-phrase sentential structure common in pop/rock sections. The pattern consists of:

1. **Statement (s)**: The basic musical idea in the first phrase
2. **Restatement/Response (r)**: Repetition or response to the statement
3. **Departure (d)**: Contrasting material, often featuring fragmentation, acceleration of harmonic rhythm, and movement away from tonic
4. **Conclusion (c)**: Resolution, either returning to the basic idea and tonic or presenting new material that provides melodic, rhythmic, and harmonic closure

## Musical Context

SRDC is the most common internal pattern for strophes in both strophic and AABA forms. The structure parallels classical sentence form (presentation-continuation-cadential) adapted to the four-phrase format typical of pop music sections.

The structure naturally divides into two halves:
- First half: **sr** (presentation function)
- Second half: **dc** (continuation and cadential function)

## Examples

### Basic Example

Using lowercase letters:
- **aaba** = statement returns as conclusion
- **aabc** = new material for conclusion

### From Repertoire

- "Dream Lover" by Bobby Darin - classic example of srdc structure:
  - s: "Every night I hope and pray..."
  - r: "Someone I can call my own..."
  - d: Contrasting melodic and harmonic material
  - c: Returns to establish closure

## Common Confusions

- **srdc vs. AABA form**: SRDC describes phrase structure within a single section; AABA describes the relationship between multiple sections in a song
- **Lowercase vs. uppercase letters**: Lowercase (srdc, aa'b) labels phrases within a section; uppercase (AABA, VCV) labels entire sections within a song
