# Quick Definition
An aggregate is a complete collection of all twelve pitch classes, typically formed within a specific span of music through a complete row statement, through combinatorial pairing of hexachords from different row forms, or through partially-ordered structures ensuring all twelve pitches sound.

# Core Definition
The aggregate is the fundamental structural unit of twelve-tone music -- the complete chromatic collection. It can be achieved through: (1) complete statement of a row; (2) combinatorial hexachords from different row forms; (3) partially-ordered structures. Aggregate completion serves as a structural goal, ensures pitch-class equality (all used before repetition), can articulate phrase structure, and provides the basis for contrapuntal combinations. Aggregate thinking extends beyond strict serialism to much post-tonal music (e.g., Crawford Seeger, Carter, Babbitt).

# Prerequisites

# Key Properties
1. Contains all 12 pitch classes
2. The fundamental twelve-tone structural unit
3. Multiple formation methods: row statement, combinatorial pairing, partial ordering
4. Aggregate completion can articulate phrases and sections
5. Extends beyond strict serialism

# Context & Application
Tracking aggregate completion is a key analytical activity in twelve-tone music. Combinatorial row-form pairings ensure aggregate completion vertically, and composers use aggregate boundaries to create formal articulations.

# Examples
**Example 1**: Single-voice aggregate -- a complete row statement: P0 plays all 12 pitch classes.
**Example 2**: Two-voice combinatorial aggregate -- P0 hexachord 1 = {0,1,2,3,4,5}, I6 hexachord 1 = {6,7,8,9,10,11}; together = complete aggregate.
**Example 3** (Webern, Symphonie Op. 21 mvt. 2, Variation 5): Four-note cells in different instruments create aggregates without strict row ordering.
