## Quick Definition

The classification of sections in pop music into core sections (verse, chorus, strophe, bridge) that carry main content and auxiliary sections (intro, outro, coda) that frame them.

## Formal Definition

Pop form sections are classified by their formal function:

### Core Sections
Sections that form the main musical and lyrical content:
- **Verse (V)**: Lyric-variant section advancing the narrative
- **Chorus (C)**: Lyric-invariant section with primary/title lyrics
- **Strophe (A)**: Self-contained section combining verse and chorus functions
- **Bridge (B)**: Contrasting section creating tension before return

### Auxiliary Sections
Sections that frame the core sections:
- **Introduction (I)**: Transitions from silence to the first core section
- **Outro (O)**: Transitions from song back to silence; based on previous material
- **Coda (X)**: Song-ending section presenting new material

## Musical Context

Section boundaries are typically marked by:
- Poetic structure (end of stanza or couplet)
- Clear rhythmic, harmonic, and melodic arrival
- Change in instrumentation or dynamics
- Return to previously heard material

### Important Terminology

- **Lyric-variant**: Different lyrics each time
- **Lyric-invariant**: Same lyrics each time
- **Music-variant**: Different music each time
- **Music-invariant**: Same music each time
- **On-tonic**: Begins with tonic harmony (I in root position)
- **Off-tonic**: Begins with non-tonic harmony
- **Harmonically closed**: Ends on tonic
- **Harmonically open**: Ends on non-tonic harmony
- **Turnaround**: Non-tonic chord (usually V) at phrase end leading back to tonic

## Examples

### Basic Example

A typical verse-chorus song structure:
- I (Intro) - V (Verse) - C (Chorus) - V - C - B (Bridge) - C - O (Outro)

### From Repertoire

- "Pride (In the Name of Love)" by U2 - clear verse-to-chorus boundary at 2:42 marked by drum fill, dynamic increase, added guitars, and background vocals
- "Muse's Resistance" - contains both a coda (4:05, new material) and an outro (4:54, returns to intro material as "bookend")

## Common Confusions

- **Chorus vs. refrain**: A chorus is a standalone section; a refrain is a lyric-invariant passage within a larger section
- **Outro vs. coda**: An outro uses previously heard material; a coda introduces new material
- **Strophe vs. verse**: Strophes are self-contained and harmonically closed; verses are typically part of a verse-chorus cycle
