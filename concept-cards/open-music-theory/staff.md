# Staff

## Quick Definition

A set of five horizontal, evenly-spaced lines on which music is written, showing the relative height of pitches.

## Formal Definition

A **staff** (plural: staves) is a fundamental element of Western musical notation consisting of five horizontal parallel lines with four spaces between them. Notes are placed on lines or in spaces to indicate their pitch, with higher positions representing higher frequencies.

In Western notation, the vertical axis (y-axis) represents pitch, while the horizontal axis (x-axis) represents time/rhythm.

## Musical Context

The staff is the foundation of written music in Western notation. Every instrumental and vocal part is written on a staff (or multiple staves). Without a staff, the precise pitch relationships between notes cannot be clearly indicated. The staff requires a clef to specify which pitches correspond to which lines and spaces.

## Examples

### Basic

A blank staff consists of five horizontal lines:

```
─────
─────
─────
─────
─────
```

Notes can be placed:

- On any of the five lines
- In any of the four spaces between lines
- Above or below the staff using ledger lines

### From Repertoire

Piano music uses two staves simultaneously (the grand staff), orchestral scores use many staves (one per instrument or section), and most solo instrumental music uses a single staff.

## Common Confusions

- The staff itself does not specify which pitches the lines and spaces represent—this requires a clef
- A staff always has exactly five lines (not more, not fewer); additional range is provided by ledger lines
- "Staff" is singular; "staves" is plural (not "staffs" in musical contexts)
