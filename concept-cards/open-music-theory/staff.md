# Quick Definition

A set of five horizontal, evenly-spaced lines on which music is written, showing the relative height of pitches.

# Core Definition

A **staff** (plural: staves) is a fundamental element of Western musical notation consisting of five horizontal parallel lines with four spaces between them. Notes are placed on lines or in spaces to indicate their pitch, with higher positions representing higher pitches and lower positions representing lower pitches. The vertical axis (y-axis) represents pitch, while the horizontal axis (x-axis) represents time/rhythm.

# Prerequisites

None -- the staff is one of the most foundational elements of Western notation.

# Key Properties

- Always exactly five lines (not more, not fewer)
- Four spaces between the five lines
- Notes can be placed on any of the five lines or in any of the four spaces
- Higher position on the staff = higher pitch
- A clef is required to assign specific pitch names to lines and spaces
- Can be extended with ledger lines for notes above or below

# Construction / Recognition

A staff is recognized as five parallel horizontal lines of equal spacing. It appears at the beginning of every line of music and always has a clef at its left edge.

# Context & Application

The staff is the foundation of written music in Western notation. Every instrumental and vocal part is written on a staff (or multiple staves). Piano music uses two staves simultaneously (the grand staff), orchestral scores use many staves (one per instrument or section), and most solo instrumental music uses a single staff.

# Examples

- A blank staff consists of five horizontal lines with equal spacing
- Notes placed on the third line from the bottom in treble clef represent the pitch B4

# Relationships

- **Leads to**: clef, note, ledger-lines, grand-staff
- **See also**: pitch, rhythm

# Common Errors

- Drawing more or fewer than five lines
- Uneven spacing between lines
- Omitting the clef (without which the staff cannot indicate specific pitches)

# Common Confusions

- The staff itself does not specify which pitches the lines and spaces represent -- this requires a clef
- "Staff" is singular; "staves" is the standard plural in musical contexts (not "staffs")
