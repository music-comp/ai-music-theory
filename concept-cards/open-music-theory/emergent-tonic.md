## Quick Definition

A technique where the tonic chord is withheld until a triumphant moment (typically the chorus), with other chords initially sounding like tonic before the true tonic is revealed.

## Formal Definition

An emergent tonic is a tonic chord that is deliberately withheld during the opening sections of a song, finally appearing at a climactic moment such as the chorus. Early sections may use the relative major/minor or other substitute harmonies that initially sound like tonic, creating the impression of one key before the true tonic "emerges" triumphantly. This technique often accompanies lyrical themes of transformation, triumph, and self-realization.

## Musical Context

Emergent tonics create a powerful narrative arc in harmony that parallels lyrical development. The listener experiences harmonic uncertainty or apparent stability in one key, followed by revelation and resolution when the true tonic finally sounds.

### Harmonic Strategies

- Opening in relative minor (vi sounds like i)
- Using IV-V oscillation to build tension
- Delaying authentic cadence until chorus
- Modal ambiguity in verses resolving in chorus

### Lyrical Associations

- Triumph and overcoming obstacles
- Self-discovery and identity
- Transformation
- Liberation

## Examples

### Basic Example

Verse: Am - G - Am - G (sounds like A minor)
Prechorus: F - G - F - G (IV-V building tension)
Chorus: C - G - Am - F (C major emerges as true tonic)

### From Repertoire

- "Let It Go" from Frozen (2013):
  - Verse in F aeolian (vi function)
  - Prechorus oscillates between Eb (IV) and Bb (V)
  - Chorus: Ab major emerges as true tonic, coinciding with Elsa's decision to embrace her identity
  - Harmonic emergence mirrors narrative transformation

- "Little Red Corvette" by Prince (1982) - appears to be in Bb minor until Db emerges as true tonic in the chorus

## Common Confusions

- **Emergent vs. modulation**: Emergent tonic involves reinterpreting earlier material, not simply moving to a new key
- **Emergent vs. delayed tonic**: The tonic doesn't just arrive late; other chords actively sound like tonic first
- **Narrative function**: Emergent tonics typically align with a lyrical turning point or emotional climax
