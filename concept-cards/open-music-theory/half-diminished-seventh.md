# Half-Diminished Seventh

## Quick Definition

A seventh chord consisting of a diminished triad plus a minor seventh above the root—notated with the symbol ø7 or m7♭5, occurring diatonically on the second scale degree in minor keys (iiø⁷) where it functions as the predominant seventh chord, with structure of stacked intervals minor third + minor third + major third creating the quality half-diminished (as opposed to fully diminished which is all minor thirds), resolving most commonly to V with characteristic voice leading where the chordal seventh resolves down by step, essential in minor-key harmony and jazz as the "minor seven flat-five" chord.

## Formal Definition

**Half-diminished seventh chord** (ø7 or m7♭5) is a four-note chord consisting of:
- **Root**: Variable (iiø⁷ in minor: ^2)
- **Third**: Minor third above root
- **Fifth**: Diminished fifth above root (♭5)
- **Seventh**: Minor seventh above root

**Structure**: Diminished triad + minor seventh
- **Intervals**: m3 + m3 + M3 (stacked)
- **Quality symbol**: ø7 (half-diminished) or m7♭5

**In A minor (iiø⁷)**: B-D-F-A (Bø7 or Bm7♭5)
- Root: B (^2)
- Minor third: D (^4)
- Diminished fifth: F (♭^6 in minor, ^6 natural)
- Minor seventh: A (^1)

**Function in minor**: Predominant (leads to V)

## Half-Diminished vs. Fully Diminished

**Half-diminished** (ø7):
```
Structure: dim triad + minor 7th
Intervals: m3 + m3 + M3
Example: B-D-F-A (Bø7)

Last interval is MAJOR third (F-A)
"Half" diminished
```

**Fully diminished** (°7):
```
Structure: dim triad + diminished 7th
Intervals: m3 + m3 + m3 (all minor thirds)
Example: B-D-F-A♭ (B°7)

All intervals minor thirds
"Fully" diminished
Symmetrical
```

**Key difference**: Seventh interval (minor vs. diminished)

## iiø⁷ in Minor Keys

**Diatonic seventh chord on ^2** (in natural/harmonic minor):

**In A minor**: Bø7 = B-D-F-A
- **Root**: B (^2, supertonic)
- **Third**: D (^4)
- **Fifth**: F (♭^6, or natural ^6 in minor)
- **Seventh**: A (^1)

**Function**: Predominant (most common predominant in minor)

**Progression**: iiø⁷ → V → i

## Voice Leading: iiø⁷ → V

**Standard resolution**:
```
iiø⁷:  B-D-F-A    V:  E-G#-B-(E)
S:     A  →  G#        (^1 → ^7, seventh resolves down)
A:     F  →  E         (♭^6 → ^5, down by half step)
T:     D  →  B or E    (^4 → ^2 or ^5)
B:     B  →  E         (^2 → ^5, root motion)

Seventh (A) resolves down by step to G#
Diminished fifth (F) resolves down to E
Common tone D-B possible
```

**Smooth voice leading**:
- Chordal seventh (^1) resolves DOWN to leading tone (^7)
- Diminished fifth (♭^6) resolves DOWN to dominant (^5)
- Root (^2) leaps to ^5 in bass
- Strong predominant-to-dominant motion

## Inversions of iiø⁷

**iiø⁷** (root position):
- Bass: ^2 (B in A minor)
- Figured bass: ø7 or ø7/5
- Most common

**iiø⁶₅** (first inversion):
- Bass: ^4 (D in A minor)
- Figured bass: ø⁶₅
- Common, smooth bass motion

**iiø⁴₃** (second inversion):
- Bass: ♭^6 (F in A minor)
- Figured bass: ø⁴₃
- Less common

**iiø⁴₂** or **iiø²** (third inversion):
- Bass: ^1 (A in A minor)
- Figured bass: ø⁴₂ or ø²
- Seventh in bass, resolves down

## Half-Diminished in Jazz

**"Minor seven flat-five"** (jazz terminology):
```
Symbol: m7♭5 or ø7
Example: Bm7♭5 = B-D-F-A

Very common in jazz harmony
ii-V-i in minor: iiø⁷-V⁷-i
"Half-diminished" = classical term
"m7♭5" = jazz term
```

**Jazz progressions**:
```
iiø⁷  -  V⁷   -  im⁷
Bø7     E7      Am7

Standard jazz minor ii-V-i
Ubiquitous in jazz standards
```

## Musical Context

Half-diminished seventh serves specific purposes:
- **Primary predominant in minor**: Most common PD seventh in minor keys
- **Diatonic in minor**: Built on ^2 of natural/harmonic minor
- **Smooth voice leading**: Seventh and dim5 both resolve down
- **Jazz harmony**: Essential chord type (m7♭5)
- **Classical minor**: Standard predominant function
- **Different from fully dim**: Less tension than °7
- **Functional clarity**: Unambiguous predominant-to-dominant motion

Essential for minor-key harmony in classical and jazz idioms.

## Examples

### Basic

**iiø⁷-V-i in A minor**:
```
iiø⁷:  B-D-F-A     (Bø7 or Bm7♭5)
V:     E-G#-B      (E major)
i:     A-C-E       (A minor)

B → E (^2 → ^5, bass leap)
A → G# (^1 → ^7, seventh down)
F → E (♭^6 → ^5, dim5 down)
D → B or E (^4 moves)

Standard minor-key predominant
```

**Voice leading detail**:
```
        iiø⁷     V       i
S:      A    →   G#  →   A   (1→7→1, seventh resolves)
A:      F    →   E       E   (♭6→5→5, or ♭6→5→3)
T:      D    →   B   →   C   (4→2→♭3)
B:      B    →   E   →   A   (2→5→1, roots)

Smooth resolution of tendency tones
```

**iiø⁶₅ - V** (first inversion):
```
iiø⁶₅: D-F-A-B    (first inversion, ^4 in bass)
V:     E-G#-B-E   (dominant)

D → E (bass, stepwise)
Smooth voice leading
```

**Jazz ii-V-i**:
```
iiø⁷  V⁷   im⁷
Bø7   E7   Am7

Standard jazz minor progression
iiø⁷ = Bm7♭5 (jazz notation)
```

**Longer progression**:
```
i  -  iv  -  iiø⁷  -  V  -  i
Am    Dm    Bø7      E    Am

iiø⁷ as predominant
Classic minor-key cadence
```

### From Repertoire

**Bach, Well-Tempered Clavier, minor-key fugues**: iiø⁷ as predominant, proper voice leading with seventh resolution.

**Mozart, Piano Concerto No. 20 in D minor**: iiø⁷-V-i progressions throughout, Classical minor-key syntax.

**Chopin, Preludes in minor keys**: Half-diminished sevenths as predominants, expressive minor-key harmony.

**Jazz standards** (e.g., "Autumn Leaves", "Blue Bossa"): iiø⁷-V⁷-i progressions in minor sections, ubiquitous jazz harmony.

**Beethoven, Piano Sonatas in minor**: iiø⁷ as standard predominant, particularly in cadential approaches.

## Common Confusions

- Half-diminished seventh = diminished triad + minor seventh (ø7 or m7♭5)
- Structure: m3 + m3 + M3 (last interval is MAJOR third)
- Diatonic on ^2 in minor keys (iiø⁷)
- In A minor: Bø7 = B-D-F-A (Bm7♭5 in jazz)
- Function: **Predominant** (leads to V)
- Different from fully diminished (°7): °7 has all minor thirds (m3+m3+m3)
- "Half" diminished = dim triad + MINOR seventh
- "Fully" diminished = dim triad + DIMINISHED seventh
- Symbol: ø7 (classical) or m7♭5 (jazz)
- Voice leading: seventh resolves down, dim5 resolves down
- Most common predominant seventh in minor keys
- Replaces ii⁷ from major (which would be ii°⁷ in minor, but iiø⁷ more common)
- Jazz "minor seven flat-five" = same chord
- ii-V-i in jazz: iiø⁷-V⁷-im⁷ (minor ii-V-i)
- All inversions usable (⁶₅, ⁴₃, ⁴₂/²)
- Not symmetric (unlike fully diminished °7)
- Classical and jazz both use extensively
- In A minor: iiø⁷ = Bø7 = B half-diminished seventh
- Quality symbol ø distinguishes from ° (fully diminished)
