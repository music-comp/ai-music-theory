# Elided Resolution

## Quick Definition

A voice-leading technique in which a dominant seventh chord resolves not to its expected tonic triad but to another dominant seventh chord built on that expected root, effectively "eliding" the expected chord with the subsequent harmony and creating a chain of interlocking seventh chords where the leading tone descends to become the new chordal seventh while the original chordal seventh descends to become the new leading tone.

## Formal Definition

**Elided resolution** occurs when:

**Standard resolution expectation**:
```
G7 → C (or Cm)
Expected: Dominant seventh resolves to tonic triad
```

**Elided resolution**:
```
G7 → C7
Instead of tonic triad, another dominant seventh on the expected root
The expected chord is "elided" (merged) with the following chord
```

**Voice exchange mechanism**:
- **Leading tone** (^7) of V7 resolves **down by half step** → becomes **new chordal seventh**
- **Chordal seventh** of V7 resolves **down by half step** → becomes **new leading tone**

## Voice Leading Pattern

**The leading-tone/chordal-seventh exchange**:
```
G7:     G  -  B  -  D  -  F
        ↓     ↓     ↓     ↓
C7:     C  -  E  -  G  -  Bb

Key exchange:
B (leading tone of G7) → Bb (seventh of C7)    [down by half step]
F (seventh of G7) → E (leading tone of C7)     [down by half step]

The leading tone becomes the seventh
The seventh becomes the leading tone
```

**Complete voice-leading analysis**:
```
G7:  G-B-D-F
     G → G or C (root motion)
     B → Bb (leading tone → seventh)
     D → C or E (fifth resolves)
     F → E (seventh → third/leading tone)
C7:  C-E-G-Bb
```

## Musical Function

**Why elided resolutions occur**:
- Creates smooth voice-leading in seventh-chord chains
- Enables sequences with consistent harmonic rhythm
- Produces chromatic sequences that traverse the octave
- Fundamental to jazz harmonic practice

**Result of elision**:
- We "mentally skip over" the expected resolution chord
- The resolution is implied but not literally present
- Each chord functions as V7 of the chord that follows
- Creates a chain of interlocking dominant sevenths

## Musical Context

Elided resolutions appear in various contexts:
- **Chromatic sequences**: Essential for descending-fifths chromatic sequences
- **Jazz harmony**: Foundation of jazz chord progressions
- **Extended dominant chains**: V7/V7/V7 etc.
- **Continuous modulation**: Romantic-era harmonic practice
- **Circle of fifths patterns**: When chromaticized with seventh chords
- **Harmonic ambiguity**: Creates tonal suspension

## Examples

### Basic

**Simple elided resolution**:
```
Expected:           Elided:
G7 → C              G7 → C7

G7:  G-B-D-F       G7:  G-B-D-F
     ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓            ↓ ↓  ↓ ↓
C:   C-E-G-C       C7:  C-Bb-G-E

"Expected" C is elided with following C7
We hear dominant seventh instead of triad
```

**Chain of elided resolutions**:
```
G7  →  C7  →  F7  →  Bb7  →  Eb7  →  ...
↓      ↓      ↓       ↓        ↓
Each resolves to next dominant seventh
Not to expected tonic triad

Voice exchange in each resolution:
Leading tone → becomes seventh (down by half step)
Seventh → becomes leading tone (down by half step)
```

**Voice exchange detail**:
```
G7 → C7:     B → Bb (leading tone → seventh)
             F → E  (seventh → leading tone)

C7 → F7:     E → Eb (leading tone → seventh)  
             Bb → A (seventh → leading tone)

F7 → Bb7:    A → Ab (leading tone → seventh)
             Eb → D (seventh → leading tone)

Pattern continues through the cycle
```

### From Repertoire

**Bach, Chromatic Fantasias**: Chains of dominant sevenths with elided resolutions creating harmonic sequences.

**Jazz standards ("Autumn Leaves", "All the Things You Are")**: ii-V-I progressions extended through elided resolutions, continuous dominant chains.

**Romantic opera (Wagner, Verdi)**: Elided resolutions maintain harmonic momentum and delay resolution.

**Chopin, Preludes and Ballades**: Chromatic sequences featuring elided resolution voice-leading.

## Common Confusions

- Elided resolution = resolution to another dominant seventh (not expected triad)
- "Elision" means the expected chord is skipped/merged with what follows
- The key is the **voice exchange**: leading tone ↔ chordal seventh
- Leading tone resolves DOWN to become new seventh (not up as expected)
- Chordal seventh resolves DOWN to become new leading tone
- This is NOT an error or avoided resolution—it's a technique
- Essential for proper voice-leading in chromatic sequences
- Each chord genuinely functions as V7 of the chord that follows
- Fundamental to jazz harmony (why jazz uses so many seventh chords)
- Creates smooth chromatic voice-leading between chords
- The "expected" chord is mentally present but physically absent
