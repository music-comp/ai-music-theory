# Altered Dominant Chord

## Quick Definition

A dominant chord (V or V7) with a chromatically altered fifth, either raised (augmented fifth, indicated by "+" in analysis) or lowered (diminished fifth, indicated by "o" in analysis), creating distinctive sonorities that provide alternative dominant colors and resolution possibilities, with the raised fifth resolving upward to the third of the target chord and the lowered fifth creating a sonority identical to the French augmented sixth chord.

## Formal Definition

**Altered dominant chords** feature a chromatic alteration to the fifth:

**Two types**:

1. **Dominant with augmented fifth (V+)**:
   - Fifth is raised by half step: ^5 → ^#5
   - Creates an augmented triad quality
   - Indicated by "+" beside Roman numeral
   - ^#5 resolves upward by step to ^3 of target chord

2. **Dominant with diminished fifth (V°5 or Vo)**:
   - Fifth is lowered by half step: ^5 → ^b5
   - Creates a diminished fifth above the root
   - Indicated by "o" beside Roman numeral
   - With added seventh: sounds identical to French augmented sixth

**Standard altered dominant in C**:
```
V+ (G augmented): G - B - D#
V°5 (G with dim5): G - B - Db
V°7 in second inversion: G - B - Db - F (= French +6)
```

## Resolution Patterns

**Augmented fifth dominant (V+)**:
```
V+:   G - B - D#    (G augmented)
      ↓   ↓   ↓
I:    C - C - E     

D# (^#5) resolves UP by step to E (^3)
B (leading tone) resolves up to C
G (root) resolves to C

Note: Does NOT resolve easily to MINOR tonic
(D# cannot resolve up to Eb in C minor)
```

**Diminished fifth dominant (V°5)**:
```
V°4/3: Db- G - B - F  (second inversion, Db in bass)
       ↓   ↓   ↓   ↓
V:     C - G - C - E

Db (^b5) resolves DOWN by step to C (^5 of new V)
Identical to French augmented sixth resolution!

V°4/3/V = Fr+6 (enharmonically identical)
```

## French Sixth Equivalence

**V°7 in second inversion = French augmented sixth**:
```
In C major, V°4/3/V:
Db - G - B - F

French augmented sixth on G:
Db - G - B - F (= Ab - C - D - F# respelled in G)

Identical sonority, identical resolution
The ^b5 in bass resolves down by step
```

## Musical Context

Altered dominant chords serve specific purposes:
- **Chromatic color**: Alternative dominant sonorities
- **Voice-leading interest**: Chromatic approach tones
- **Augmented fifth**: Creates upward pull to ^3
- **Diminished fifth**: French sixth connection
- **Enharmonic potential**: Augmented triad's symmetry
- **Modulation**: Augmented triad can resolve three ways
- **Post-Romantic harmony**: Expanded dominant vocabulary
- **Jazz harmony**: Foundation for altered dominant concept

## Examples

### Basic

**V+ in C major**:
```
V+:    G - B - D#    (G augmented triad)
       ↓   ↓   ↓
C:     C - C - E     (C major)

D# resolves UP to E (^#5 → ^3)
Creates chromatic approach to ^3
Only works with MAJOR tonic (not minor)
```

**Three resolutions of V+ (C augmented)**:
```
C+:    C - E - G#

Resolution 1 (as V+ in F):
C+ → F:    C-E-G# → F-F-A     (G# up to A)
           V+/F → F

Resolution 2 (as V+ in Ab, respelled):
Ab+ → Db:  Ab-C-E → Db-Db-F   (E=Fb up to F)
           V+/Db → Db

Resolution 3 (as V+ in Db, respelled):  
E+ → A:    E-G#-B# → A-A-C#   (B#=C up to C#)
           V+/A → A

Augmented triad is SYMMETRICAL
Can be respelled/reinterpreted THREE ways
Gateway to chromatic modulation (like dim7)
```

**V°7 as French sixth**:
```
In G major:
V°4/3/V (dominant of V):
Ab - D - F# - C  (G with diminished fifth, second inversion)

This IS the French augmented sixth:
Ab (^b6) - D (^2) - F# (^#4) - C (^1)

Same chord, same resolution to V:
Ab - D - F# - C  →  G - D - G - B (G major = V in C)
```

### From Repertoire

**Romantic Lieder (Schubert, Wolf)**: Altered dominants for expressive text-painting and harmonic color.

**Liszt, piano works**: Augmented triads exploited for their modulatory potential and symmetry.

**Wagner, Tristan und Isolde**: Altered dominants contribute to the expanded chromatic vocabulary.

**Jazz standards**: "Altered dominant" concept (V7#5, V7b5) derives from these classical alterations.

**Impressionism (Debussy)**: Augmented triads as coloristic harmony, not necessarily resolving functionally.

## Common Confusions

- Altered dominant = V with altered fifth (raised OR lowered)
- V+ (augmented fifth): ^#5 resolves UP by step to ^3
- V° (diminished fifth): ^b5 resolves DOWN by step
- "+" indicates augmented fifth, "o" indicates diminished fifth
- V+ does NOT resolve well to minor tonic (^#5 can't resolve to ^b3)
- V°7 in second inversion IS the French augmented sixth (identical!)
- Augmented triad is symmetrical (like diminished seventh)
- C+ = E+ = G#+ (enharmonically—same pitches, different spellings)
- This symmetry enables THREE possible resolutions/modulations
- Different from "altered scale" in jazz (though conceptually related)
- Altered dominants provide chromatic alternatives to standard V
