# Quick Definition
Altered dominant chords feature an augmented or diminished fifth (V+ or Vo). Extended dominant chords add upper extensions beyond the seventh: V9 (adds ninth), V11 (adds eleventh, replaces third), V13 (adds thirteenth, replaces fifth). In four-voice textures, extensions require omitting some chord tones — always keeping root and seventh.

# Core Definition
Altered dominants: V+ (raised fifth, becomes augmented triad) — raised fifth resolves upward by step to third of target chord. The augmented triad's symmetry enables multiple enharmonic interpretations and resolutions, but it does not resolve easily to minor triads since the raised fifth cannot descend. Vo (lowered fifth) — when a seventh is added, Vo7 sounds identical to a French augmented sixth chord. In second inversion (Vo4/3), the lowered fifth in bass resolves downward, making the equivalence with Fr+6 explicit. Extended dominants: V9 replaces doubled root with ninth (resolves down by step). V11 replaces third with eleventh (resolves by common tone, resembles IV over ^5 bass). V13 replaces fifth with thirteenth (resolves by leaping down a third to ^1). Typically found in root position only.

# Key Properties
1. V+ (augmented fifth): raised fifth resolves upward to third of target
2. Vo (diminished fifth): Vo7 = French augmented sixth chord enharmonically
3. V9: ninth replaces doubled root, resolves down by step
4. V11: eleventh replaces third, resolves by common tone; resembles IV/^5
5. V13: thirteenth replaces fifth, resolves by third-leap down to ^1
6. Extended chords always keep root and seventh
7. Typically root position only for extended dominants

# Context & Application
The equivalence of Vo4/3/V and Fr+6 is an important analytical insight. V+ is a pathway to distant chromatic modulations due to the augmented triad's enharmonic flexibility (three possible resolutions, one for each spelling of the root). Extended dominants (V9, V11, V13) are more common in Romantic-era and jazz harmony; in classical four-voice writing, choosing which tones to omit is a key compositional decision.

# Examples
**Example 1** (V+ resolutions): C augmented triad resolves to F, Ab, or Db depending on enharmonic interpretation.
**Example 2** (Vo7 = Fr+6): Vo4/3/V with lowered fifth in bass resolves identically to French augmented sixth.
