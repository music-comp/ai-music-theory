# Quick Definition
The standard ending formula in species counterpoint, where both voices approach the final unison or octave by contrary stepwise motion -- one voice moving re-do while the other moves ti-do.

# Core Definition
The **clausula vera** (Latin for "true close") is the required ending formula in species counterpoint. The final interval must be a perfect unison or octave on do (scale degree 1), approached by contrary stepwise motion. Two forms exist: (1) if the cantus ends re-do (2-1), the counterpoint ends ti-do (7-1); (2) if the cantus ends ti-do (7-1), the counterpoint ends re-do (2-1). The penultimate bar therefore forms either a third or a sixth between the two voices (Ch. 2, "Ending a first-species counterpoint"). The clausula vera is used across all five species with adaptations for each species' rhythmic profile.

# Prerequisites
- **First-species** -- Clausula vera is introduced as the ending formula for first species
- **Contrary motion** -- The clausula vera requires contrary stepwise motion to the final

# Key Properties
1. Final interval: perfect unison or octave (not fifth)
2. Both voices end on do (scale degree 1)
3. Approach by contrary stepwise motion
4. Two forms: re-do against ti-do, or ti-do against re-do
5. Penultimate interval is a third or sixth
6. Used in all five species of counterpoint
7. In fourth species, must use dissonant suspension (7-6 or 2-3) before final resolution
8. In second species, penultimate bar may be whole note or two half notes

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Determine the cantus firmus ending (re-do or ti-do)
2. Write the counterpoint's penultimate note as the complementary scale degree
3. End both voices on do, forming a unison or octave
4. Ensure contrary stepwise motion into the final interval
## To Recognize:
1. Look for contrary stepwise motion into the final unison or octave
2. Check that penultimate bar is a third or sixth
3. Verify both voices arrive on do (scale degree 1)

# Context & Application
The clausula vera is mandatory in all species counterpoint exercises and represents the strongest possible two-voice cadence. It ensures smooth, goal-directed motion toward the final with variety (contrary motion) and strong arrival (perfect consonance). Historically, it is the standard cadence in Renaissance polyphony and is the precursor to the authentic cadence in later tonal music.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Ending on a perfect fifth instead of unison or octave
  **Correction**: The final interval must be P1 or P8, never P5
- **Error**: Approaching the final by similar or parallel motion
  **Correction**: Both voices must move by step in contrary motion

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Any ending cadence is a clausula vera
  **Clarification**: Clausula vera specifically requires contrary stepwise motion into a unison or octave on do
- **Confusion**: The leading tone can resolve downward in a clausula vera
  **Clarification**: The leading tone (ti/scale degree 7) must resolve upward to tonic, even in minor modes
