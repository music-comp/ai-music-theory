# Clausula Vera

## Quick Definition

A traditional two-voice cadential formula where both voices approach the final unison or octave by contrary stepwise motion, with the penultimate interval being either a third or sixth, creating a strong sense of goal-oriented resolution in species counterpoint and early polyphony.

## Formal Definition

**Clausula vera** (Latin: "true clausula" or "true cadence") is the standard ending formula for species counterpoint exercises, characterized by specific melodic and harmonic requirements.

**Structure**:
- **Final interval**: Perfect unison or perfect octave on ^1 (do)
- **Penultimate interval**: Major or minor third, or major or minor sixth
- **Motion**: Both voices move by step in contrary motion into final interval
- **Scale degrees**: Specific combinations based on cantus firmus ending

**Two standard forms**:

1. **When cantus ends re-do (^2-^1)**:
   - Cantus: ^2 → ^1 (re → do), descending step
   - Counterpoint: ^7 → ^1 (ti → do), ascending step
   - Penultimate interval: third or sixth (depending on register)
   - Final interval: unison or octave

2. **When cantus ends ti-do (^7-^1)**:
   - Cantus: ^7 → ^1 (ti → do), ascending step
   - Counterpoint: ^2 → ^1 (re → do), descending step
   - Penultimate interval: third or sixth
   - Final interval: unison or octave

**Voice-leading principles**:
- Contrary stepwise motion creates strongest goal direction
- Leading tone (^7) resolves upward to tonic
- Supertonic (^2) resolves downward to tonic
- Both voices converge on tonic from different directions
- Creates sense of balance and completion

**Adaptations in different species**:
- **First species**: Penultimate bar = one note per voice
- **Second species**: Penultimate bar may be whole note or two half notes
- **Third species**: Penultimate bar approaches via quarter notes
- **Fourth species**: Must use dissonant suspension (7-6 or 2-3) before final resolution
- **Fifth species**: Combines features, often with embellishments

**Historical context**:
- Standard cadence in Renaissance polyphony
- Basis for authentic cadence in later tonal music
- "Clausula" = closing or cadence in medieval/Renaissance theory
- "Vera" = true/proper, distinguishing from other cadence types

## Musical Context

The clausula vera serves multiple purposes:
- **Pedagogical**: Teaches proper cadential voice leading
- **Harmonic**: Establishes tonic through convergent motion
- **Melodic**: Creates satisfying melodic conclusion in both voices
- **Perceptual**: Contrary motion and stepwise approach heightens sense of arrival

In species counterpoint, the clausula vera is mandatory—exercises must end with this formula to be considered correct.

## Examples

### Basic

**Clausula vera above cantus** (cantus ends re-do):
```
Penultimate measure:
Counterpoint: B4 (ti/^7)
Cantus:       D4 (re/^2)
Interval:     sixth

Final measure:
Counterpoint: C5 (do/^1) [step up]
Cantus:       C4 (do/^1) [step down]
Interval:     octave
Motion:       contrary stepwise ✓
```

**Clausula vera below cantus** (cantus ends re-do):
```
Penultimate:
Cantus:       D4 (re/^2)
Counterpoint: B3 (ti/^7)
Interval:     third

Final:
Cantus:       C4 (do/^1) [step down]
Counterpoint: C3 (do/^1) [step up]
Interval:     octave
Motion:       contrary stepwise ✓
```

**Clausula vera** (cantus ends ti-do):
```
Penultimate:
Counterpoint: D5 (re/^2)
Cantus:       B3 (ti/^7)
Interval:     tenth (compound third)

Final:
Counterpoint: C5 (do/^1) [step down]
Cantus:       C4 (do/^1) [step up]
Interval:     octave
Motion:       contrary stepwise ✓
```

**Fourth species clausula vera** (with suspension):
```
Penultimate measure:
Counterpoint: C5 (preparation) tied to → C5 (7-6 suspension over D4) → B4 (resolution)
Cantus:       ------- C4 ------------------ D4 --------------------

Final measure:
Counterpoint: C5 (do/^1)
Cantus:       C4 (do/^1)
The suspension must occur before final resolution
```

**Invalid - similar motion** (not clausula vera):
```
Penultimate:
CP: B4 (ti/^7)
CF: G3 (sol/^5)

Final:
CP: C5 (do/^1) [step up]
CF: C4 (do/^1) [leap up]
Error: Not contrary motion—both ascend
```

### From Repertoire

All of Fux's exercises in _Gradus ad Parnassum_ end with clausula vera. Renaissance polyphony (Palestrina, Lassus) uses clausula vera consistently at major cadences. Bach's contrapuntal exercises and inventions employ clausula vera principles. The formula persists as "authentic cadence" voice-leading in Classical-era music.

## Common Confusions

- Clausula vera ≠ any ending—it's a specific voice-leading pattern
- Both voices must move by step (no leaps in either voice)
- Must be contrary motion (not parallel, similar, or oblique)
- Final interval must be perfect unison or octave, not perfect fifth
- Penultimate interval = third or sixth, not fourth, fifth, or second
- Cantus can end re-do or ti-do, but counterpoint adjusts accordingly
- In fourth species, the suspension precedes the final resolution
- "Vera" implies "true/proper"—other cadences exist but this is the standard for species
- Leading tone must resolve upward (^7 → ^1), even in minor modes
- Not the same as "imperfect authentic cadence" (later harmonic concept)
