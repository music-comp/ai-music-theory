# Quick Definition
Twelve-tone analysis involves identifying row forms in a work, understanding their relationships, and connecting technical details to broader musical meaning -- including questions of form, timbre, historical context, and aesthetic intent. A list of row forms is necessary but not sufficient; it is comparable to listing keys in a tonal work.

# Core Definition
Analyzing twelve-tone music requires both technical identification of row forms and broader interpretive engagement. Technical tasks include: identifying the row, constructing the matrix, tracing row forms through the score, noting combinatorial relationships, and tracking aggregate completion. Interpretive tasks include: understanding how row structure relates to form, considering the meaning of genre titles (why a "Symphony" or "Concerto"?), examining timbre and texture, evaluating the audibility of serial structures, and placing the work in historical context. Webern's Op. 21 (Symphonie) and Op. 24 (Konzert) demonstrate how serial technique engages with classical forms, symmetry, and Klangfarbenmelodie.

# Prerequisites
- Row matrix and row operations (technical tools for analysis)

# Key Properties
1. Technical: row identification, matrix construction, form tracing
2. Interpretive: formal analysis, timbral analysis, historical context
3. Row-form listing is necessary but not sufficient for analysis
4. Symmetry properties often have formal significance
5. Timbre (Klangfarbenmelodie) articulates row structure
6. Genre titles (Symphony, Concerto) carry meaning

# Context & Application
Webern's Symphonie Op. 21 uses a retrograde-equivalent row with palindromic structures and double canons, raising questions about audible symmetry and symphonic form. His Konzert Op. 24 features a four-trichord derived row with possible concerto-like double exposition.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Webern, Op. 21): 24 distinct forms (due to retrograde equivalence), double canon in contrary motion, palindromic second movement.
**Example 2** (Webern, Op. 24): 12 distinct forms, trichordal cells related by P/R/I/RI, possible double-exposition reading.
