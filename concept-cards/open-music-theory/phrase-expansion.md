# Quick Definition
Techniques for making a phrase last longer than expected, either internally (repetition, stretching, one-more-time technique, alternative path) or externally (prefixes before the beginning, suffixes after the cadence).

# Core Definition
**Expansion** makes a phrase longer than expected; **contraction** makes it shorter. Internal expansions occur within the phrase (after beginning, before cadence) and include: **repetition** (extra length from repeated material), **stretching** (lengthening a harmony or melody), **one-more-time technique** (Schmalfeldt 1989: cadence attempted, evaded, retried), and **alternative path** (unexpected new material causing deviation -- either a **detour** that returns to previous material or a **reroute** that achieves cadence without returning). External expansions add length outside the phrase: **prefixes** (before beginning, e.g., introductions) and **suffixes** (after cadence: post-cadential extensions, codettas, codas) (Ch. 4).

# Prerequisites
- **Phrase/sentence/period** -- Must understand expected phrase structure to recognize expansion

# Key Properties
1. Internal expansions: repetition, stretching, one-more-time, alternative path
2. One-more-time: evaded cadence → retry with same or different material
3. Alternative path: detour (returns) or reroute (doesn't return)
4. External prefixes: introductions (large) or accompaniment lead-ins (small)
5. External suffixes: post-cadential extensions, codettas (repeated cadential material), codas (independent concluding sections)
6. Contraction: phrase shorter than expected (less common than expansion)
7. Multiple techniques often combine in a single expanded phrase

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify Expansion:
1. Determine the expected length (from archetype or earlier statement)
2. Locate where extra material occurs
3. Classify: internal (repetition, stretching, one-more-time, alternative path) or external (prefix, suffix)
## One-More-Time vs. Alternative Path:
- One-more-time: passage HAS tried to cadence and failed; backs up to retry
- Alternative path: passage has NOT yet tried to cadence; goes off course

# Context & Application
Expansion is one of the most important tools for creating drama and variety in tonal music. The one-more-time technique (coined by Janet Schmalfeldt) is particularly common at climactic moments. Suffixes range from tiny post-cadential extensions to massive codas (e.g., Shostakovich's Festive Overture).

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 4, Example 4): Mendelssohn, Op. 8/5 "Pilgerspruch" -- one-more-time technique with stretched retry.

**Example 2** (Ch. 4, Example 5): Mendelssohn, Hebrides Overture -- alternative path (detour) with repeated continuation going off track.

**Example 3** (Ch. 4, Example 12): Elmas, Piano Concerto No. 1, III -- contraction where 4-measure c.b.i. is followed by only 3-measure continuation.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing the one-more-time technique with alternative path
  **Correction**: OMT = passage has tried and failed to cadence; alternative path = passage hasn't yet tried

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: All repetitions create expansion
  **Clarification**: Predictable repetitions (e.g., b.i. repetition in a presentation) are expected, not expansions
