# Quick Definition
Olivier Messiaen's modes of limited transposition are scales that cannot be transposed in 12 unique ways because their internal symmetry causes transpositions to repeat the same pitch collection. Mode 1 is the whole-tone scale (2 transpositions), Mode 2 is the octatonic (3 transpositions), and Modes 3-7 have various limited transposition counts.

# Core Definition
Messiaen identified seven modes with this property. They are characterized by repeating interval patterns within the octave: Mode 1 (whole-tone, pattern: 2-2-2-2-2-2, 2 transpositions), Mode 2 (octatonic, 1-2-1-2-1-2-1-2, 3 transpositions), Mode 3 (2-1-1-2-1-1-2-1-1, 4 transpositions), and so on. Mode 1 is a subset of Modes 3 and 6. The limited transposition property creates a kind of built-in symmetry that appealed to Messiaen's aesthetic goals.

# Prerequisites
- Octatonic and whole-tone collections (the first two modes)

# Key Properties
1. Seven modes identified by Messiaen
2. Each has a repeating interval pattern within the octave
3. Cannot be transposed in 12 unique ways
4. Mode 1 = whole-tone (2 transpositions)
5. Mode 2 = octatonic (3 transpositions)
6. Internal symmetry is the defining feature
7. Acoustic scale does NOT have this property

# Context & Application
Central to Messiaen's compositional language and used by many subsequent composers. The symmetry of these modes connects to broader 20th-century interest in symmetric structures.

# Examples
**Example 1**: Mode 2 (octatonic): 1-2-1-2-1-2-1-2 = alternating half and whole steps. Only 3 distinct transpositions.
**Example 2**: Mode 3: 2-1-1-2-1-1-2-1-1 = 9 notes. Only 4 distinct transpositions.
