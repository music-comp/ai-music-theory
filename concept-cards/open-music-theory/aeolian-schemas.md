## Quick Definition

Chord progressions featuring flat-VI and flat-VII with a minor tonic, including the subtonic shuttle, aeolian shuttle, aeolian cadence, and aeolian lament.

## Formal Definition

Aeolian schemas employ the natural minor scale's characteristic flat^6 (le) and flat^7 (te) scale degrees prominently in harmonic progressions. Unlike minor-key progressions that use the raised leading tone (ti), aeolian schemas maintain the lowered ^7, creating distinctive flat-VI and flat-VII chords with subdominant and dominant function respectively.

## Musical Context

Aeolian can be thought of as natural minor used as a mode rather than a key - prominently featuring flat^6 and flat^7 rather than raised ^6 and ^7. The resulting progressions avoid the V-i authentic cadence in favor of flat-VII-i or flat-VI-flat-VII-i motion.

### Key Schemas

**Subtonic Shuttle (i-flat-VII)**
- Minor tonic version of the mixolydian subtonic shuttle
- Simple alternation between tonic and subtonic

**Aeolian Shuttle (i-flat-VII-flat-VI-flat-VII)**
- Shuttle between i and flat-VI with passing flat-VII
- Circular, non-goal-oriented

**Aeolian Cadence (flat-VI-flat-VII-i)**
- Goal-oriented cadential motion to tonic
- Often uses Picardy third (all major chords: flat-VI-flat-VII-I)
- Associated with triumph, heroism, success

**Lament (i-flat-VII-flat-VI-v)**
- Descending bass through minor tetrachord
- Aeolian flavor from flat-VII and minor v (no leading tone)

## Examples

### Basic Example

In A aeolian:
- Subtonic shuttle: Am - G (i-flat-VII)
- Aeolian shuttle: Am - G - F - G (i-flat-VII-flat-VI-flat-VII)
- Aeolian cadence: F - G - Am (flat-VI-flat-VII-i)

### From Repertoire

- "Somebody That I Used to Know" by Gotye (2011) - subtonic shuttle in verses, aeolian shuttle in chorus
- Fellowship Theme from Lord of the Rings (Howard Shore) - aeolian cadence with Picardy third at phrase endings
- Super Mario Bros. level-complete fanfare - aeolian cadence (the "Mario cadence")
- "I Don't Like You" by Eva Simons (2012) - lament with flat-VII and minor v

## Common Confusions

- **Aeolian vs. minor**: Minor keys typically use raised ^7 (leading tone) for V-i cadences; aeolian maintains flat^7 throughout
- **Aeolian shuttle vs. cadence**: The shuttle is circular/looping; the cadence is goal-oriented, ending on tonic
- **Aeolian cadence associations**: The flat-VI-flat-VII-I progression (with Picardy third) has strong cultural associations with heroism and triumph in film and video game music
