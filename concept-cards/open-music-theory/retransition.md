# Quick Definition
A connective auxiliary section that prepares the return of the main section (B→A), often driving toward the home key's dominant and prolonging it, creating anticipation for the return.

# Core Definition
A **retransition** connects two sections "where the upcoming section is the initiation of a large-scale return (i.e., between B and A, not between A and B)." It prepares the return of the main section, "often drives toward attaining the dominant chord of the home key and will often prolong the dominant once attained." Like transitions, retransitions come in large and small varieties. They may end with a clear half cadence (possibly followed by a suffix), or they may elide with the returning section (Ch. 5).

# Prerequisites
- **Formal sections** -- Understanding the auxiliary section taxonomy

# Key Properties
1. Connects B to A (not A to B; that's a transition)
2. Prepares return of the main section
3. Drives toward home key dominant
4. Often prolongs the dominant once attained
5. Common in ternary form, rondo, and sonata form (development→recapitulation)
6. Large or small varieties

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for passage between a contrasting section and the return of A
2. Check for dominant preparation in the home key
3. Note building anticipation for the return

# Context & Application
Retransitions are particularly common in rondo form, where they "dramatize the return of the refrain." In sonata form, the retransition connects the development to the recapitulation.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 5): Chopin Polonaise Op. 40/2 -- small retransition between B and A (2:35-2:39).

**Example 2** (Ch. 5): Mozart K. 333, I -- large retransition between development and recapitulation (4:29-4:45).

# Relationships
## Contrasts With

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: A retransition is just a transition in reverse
  **Clarification**: They have different functions -- retransition specifically prepares a RETURN
