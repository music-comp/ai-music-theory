# Meyer-Family Opening Schemas

## Quick Definition

A group of related four-stage opening galant schemas (Sol-Fa-Mi, Do-Re-Mi, Jupiter, Pastorella, Aprile) that share the Meyer's structural principle of starting on tonic, moving away for two stages, and returning to tonic, differing primarily in melodic patterns and harmonic details while serving the same opening gambit function.

## Formal Definition

The **Meyer-family schemas** are opening gambits that follow the Meyer prototype's basic harmonic trajectory (I → non-tonic → non-tonic → I) but with varied melodic patterns. All share the structural principle of tonic frame with contrasting middle stages.

**Common characteristics**:
- Four-stage structure
- Begin on tonic chord (I)
- Move away from tonic for two middle stages
- Return to tonic at end (often I or I6)
- Serve as opening gambits (begin themes/phrases)
- Question-answer pairing possible (first two stages = question, last two = answer)

## Individual Schemas

### 1. Sol-Fa-Mi

| Stage | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|-------|---|---|---|---|
| Meter | W | S | W | S |
| Melody | sol (^5) | fa (^4) | fa (^4) | mi (^3) |
| Bass | do (^1) | re (^2) | ti (^7) | do (^1) |
| Figures | 5 | 5 | 6/5 | 5 |
| Roman numerals | I | ii | V | I |

**Characteristics**:
- Opens on ^5 (dominant scale degree in melody)
- Descending melodic pattern: ^5-^4-^4-^3
- Pre-dominant (ii) at stage 2 (distinguishes from Meyer's V)
- Fa-mi closing (^4-^3) characteristic of many schemas

**Variant** (harmonic alteration):
- Stage 2 can be vii° instead of ii
- Both options maintain predominant→dominant→tonic flow

### 2. Do-Re-Mi

**Three-stage version**:

| Stage | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|-------|---|---|---|
| Meter | S | W | S |
| Melody | do (^1) | re (^2) | mi (^3) |
| Bass | do (^1) | ti (^7) | do (^1) |
| Figures | 5 | 6 | 5 |
| Roman numerals | I | V | I |

**Four-stage version** (expanded):
Doubles the central stage, creating four stages total.

**Characteristics**:
- Ascending melodic pattern: ^1-^2-^3
- Simple, stepwise ascent to mediant
- Can be divided into Do-Re (question) + Re-Mi (answer)
- Shortest of the opening gambits (often three stages)
- Extremely clear I-V-I structure

### 3. Jupiter

| Stage | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|-------|---|---|---|---|
| Meter | S | W | S | W |
| Melody | do (^1) | re (^2) | fa (^4) | mi (^3) |
| Bass | do (^1) | ti (^7) | sol (^5) | do (^1) |
| Figures | 5 | 6 | 5 | 5 |
| Roman numerals | I | V | V | I |

**Characteristics**:
- Opens on ^1 (tonic in melody)
- Ascending then descending: ^1-^2-^4-^3
- Skips ^3 in ascent, reaches ^4, descends to ^3
- Two dominant stages (stages 2-3 both V)
- Similar harmonic structure to Meyer (I-V-V-I)

### 4. Pastorella

| Stage | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|-------|---|---|---|---|
| Meter | S | W | S | W |
| Melody | mi (^3) | re (^2) | fa (^4) | mi (^3) |
| Bass | do (^1) | sol (^5) | sol (^5) | do (^1) |
| Figures | 5 | 5 | 7 | 5 |
| Roman numerals | I | V | V7 | I |

**Characteristics**:
- Opens and closes on ^3 (mediant in melody)
- Neighbor-tone-like motion: ^3-^2-^4-^3 (around ^3)
- Two dominant stages with seventh added at stage 3
- Bass ^5 sustained through middle stages
- "Pastoral" character suggested by name

### 5. Aprile

| Stage | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|-------|---|---|---|---|
| Meter | S | W | S | W |
| Melody | do (^1) | ti (^7) | re (^2) | do (^1) |
| Bass | do (^1) | re (^2) | ti (^7) | do (^1) |
| Figures | 5 | 6/4/3 | 6/5 | 5 |
| Roman numerals | I | V | V | I |

**Characteristics**:
- Opens and closes on ^1 (tonic in melody)
- Descends to ^7, leaps up to ^2, descends to ^1
- Melodic arch around tonic
- Two dominant stages (like Meyer)
- Bass pattern identical to Meyer

## Shared Principles

All Meyer-family schemas:
- **Tonic frame**: Begin and end on I (or I6)
- **Four stages typical**: Some (Do-Re-Mi) can be three
- **Middle contrast**: Two middle stages move away from tonic
- **Opening function**: Serve as opening gambits for themes
- **Melodic variety**: Different scale-degree patterns distinguish them
- **Harmonic similarity**: Most use I-V-V-I or I-ii-V-I progressions

**Choosing among them**:
- Character desired (pastoral, energetic, simple, etc.)
- Melodic contour preference (ascending, descending, arching)
- Harmonic color (pre-dominant vs. dominant emphasis)
- Voice-leading considerations in context

## Musical Context

These schemas serve identical formal functions:
- **Opening gambits**: Establish key and begin themes
- **Thematic identity**: Provide melodic/harmonic framework
- **Interchangeable**: Composers could substitute one for another
- **Pedagogical models**: Teach various approaches to opening phrases
- **Improvisational vocabulary**: Patterns for creating openings

The variety among Meyer-family schemas shows galant composers' flexibility in realizing the basic I-(non-tonic)-(non-tonic)-I structure with different melodic characters.

## Examples

### Basic Comparison

**All schemas in C major** (first two stages):

```
Meyer:      do-ti over do-re (I-V)
Sol-Fa-Mi:  sol-fa over do-re (I-ii)
Do-Re-Mi:   do-re over do-ti (I-V)
Jupiter:    do-re over do-ti (I-V)
Pastorella: mi-re over do-sol (I-V)
Aprile:     do-ti over do-re (I-V)
```

All start on I, but melodic patterns differ significantly.

**Functional equivalence**:
Any of these schemas could open a galant theme; choice depends on desired character and melodic shape.

### From Repertoire

**Mozart Piano Sonatas**: Uses various Meyer-family schemas as opening material, selecting based on character desired.

**Haydn String Quartets**: Employs Do-Re-Mi for simple, direct openings; Sol-Fa-Mi or Pastorella for more complex character.

**Galant keyboard sonatas** (Rutini, Galuppi, Paradisi): Mixture of opening schemas demonstrates variety within common framework.

**Mozart Symphonies**: Different movements use different opening schemas, showcasing range of possibilities.

## Common Confusions

- All Meyer-family schemas are opening gambits (not continuation or cadential)
- Same harmonic function, different melodic patterns (interchangeable in theory)
- Meyer is prototype; others are variants with distinct identities
- Do-Re-Mi can be three stages (exception to four-stage norm)
- Sol-Fa-Mi uses ii (pre-dominant), not V at stage 2 (unlike Meyer)
- Pastorella opens/closes on ^3, not ^1 (unique among opening gambits)
- Not every I-V-V-I progression is one of these schemas (melodic pattern matters)
- "Family" is analytical grouping (not historical term composers used)
- Jupiter named after Mozart's "Jupiter" Symphony (modern analytical naming)
- Aprile and Meyer very similar (nearly identical harmonically, slight melodic difference)
- Schemas can be combined or substituted (flexible compositional tools)
