# Quick Definition

An appoggiatura is an embellishing tone approached by leap and left by step in the opposite direction, typically occurring on a stronger part of the beat and most commonly resolving downward.

# Core Definition

**Appoggiaturas** (APP) are *approached by leap and left by step* in the opposite direction (OMT, Ch. IV.4). They belong to Category 2 (embellishing tones involving a leap). The appoggiatura typically occurs on a stronger part of the beat than its surrounding notes. It is more common for appoggiaturas to resolve downward than upward.

# Prerequisites

- **Embellishing tone** — Appoggiatura is one specific type of embellishing tone

# Key Properties

1. Approached by LEAP
2. Left by STEP in the opposite direction
3. Typically on a STRONGER part of the beat
4. More commonly resolves downward than upward
5. Creates an accented dissonance (strong-beat non-chord tone)

# Construction / Recognition

## To Identify:
1. Find a non-chord tone on a relatively strong beat
2. Check: was it approached by LEAP?
3. Check: is it left by STEP in the opposite direction?
4. If yes: appoggiatura

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: Melodic lines in all voices; common in expressive passages
- **Common applications**: Creating accented dissonance for dramatic effect
- **Historical/stylistic notes**: The source illustrates with Joseph Boulogne, String Quartet No. 4, I, mm. 5-9.

# Relationships

## Builds Upon
- **Embellishing tone** — Appoggiatura is a leap-involving embellishing tone type

## Related
- **Escape tone** — Also involves a leap, but approached by step and left by leap

## Contrasts With
- **Escape tone** — Escape tone: step then leap; appoggiatura: leap then step
- **Neighbor tone** — Neighbor approached by step, not leap

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Confusing appoggiatura with accented passing tone
  **Correction**: Appoggiatura is approached by LEAP; a passing tone (accented or not) is approached by STEP

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking appoggiaturas must always be on the downbeat
  **Clarification**: They occur on a "stronger part of the beat" relative to surrounding notes, which may be any metrically strong position
