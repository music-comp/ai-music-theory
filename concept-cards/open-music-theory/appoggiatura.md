# Appoggiatura

## Quick Definition

A non-chord tone approached by leap (typically from a consonance) that creates a dissonance on a strong beat, then resolves by step (usually downward) to a consonant chord tone—distinguished from other non-chord tones by its accented metric placement and approach by leap rather than step, creating expressive emphasis and emotional weight, with the Italian name meaning "to lean upon," reflecting how the dissonance leans into its resolution, common in Classical and Romantic melodic writing for heightened expression.

## Formal Definition

**Appoggiatura** (APP or A) is a non-chord tone that:

**Characteristics**:
- Approached by **leap** (third or larger)
- Occurs on **strong beat** (metrically accented)
- Creates **dissonance** against harmony
- Resolves by **step** (usually downward)
- **Not prepared** as consonance
- Resolves to consonant chord tone

**Pattern**:
```
Chord 1: [chord tone] → LEAP → APP (dissonant, strong beat)
                                  ↓
Chord 2:                          step down to consonance
```

**Etymology**: Italian "appoggiare" = "to lean upon"

**Function**: Creates accented dissonance, expressive emphasis, emotional weight

## Appoggiatura Patterns

**Standard appoggiatura** (leap up, step down):
```
     I              V
     C → leap → F (APP, dissonant)
                ↓
                E (resolves down by step)

F approached by leap (4th)
F dissonant against V chord (expecting D)
F resolves down to E
```

**Downward appoggiatura** (leap down, step up/down):
```
Leap down → APP → step to resolution

Less common than upward leap
```

**Long appoggiatura**:
```
APP lasts for substantial duration
May be longer than resolution note
Creates strong expressive effect
```

**Short appoggiatura**:
```
APP brief, quickly resolves
Ornamental character
Less structural weight
```

## Voice Leading

**Metric placement** (crucial):
```
Beat:    1    2    3    4
         APP  res
         ↑    ↑
         strong weak

Appoggiatura on strong beat (accented)
Resolution on weaker beat
Creates tension-resolution on metric level
```

**Approach and resolution**:
```
Previous chord tone → LEAP (3rd, 4th, 5th, etc.)
                      ↓
                    APP (dissonant, accented)
                      ↓
                    STEP down (usually) to consonance

Leap in, step out (opposite of escape tone)
```

**Resolution direction**:
- **Downward resolution**: Most common (step down)
- **Upward resolution**: Possible but less typical
- **Stepwise motion**: Required for resolution

## Appoggiatura vs. Other Non-Chord Tones

**vs. Suspension**:
- **Appoggiatura**: Approached by **leap**, not prepared
- **Suspension**: **Held over** from previous chord (prepared as consonance)
- Both: Accented, resolve by step down

**vs. Neighbor tone**:
- **Appoggiatura**: Approached by **leap**, resolves by step
- **Neighbor**: Approached and left by **step** (opposite directions)

**vs. Escape tone**:
- **Appoggiatura**: Leap in, **step out**
- **Escape**: **Step in**, leap out

**vs. Passing tone**:
- **Appoggiatura**: **Accented**, leap approach
- **Passing**: Unaccented, stepwise throughout

## Musical Context

Appoggiaturas serve specific purposes:
- **Expressive emphasis**: Creates emotional weight through accented dissonance
- **Melodic climax**: Often appears at melodic high points
- **Intensification**: Heightens tension before resolution
- **Classical/Romantic device**: Particularly common in expressive melodic writing
- **"Leaning" effect**: Dissonance leans into resolution
- **Vocal writing**: Natural for expressive text setting
- **Structural accent**: Emphasizes important melodic moments

Appoggiaturas are powerful expressive devices in tonal music.

## Examples

### Basic

**Simple appoggiatura** (C major):
```
     I                   V
     C → E (leap 3rd) → F → E
                        ↑   ↓
                       APP  res

F approached by leap (E→F)
F dissonant against V (expects D or G or B)
F resolves down to E by step
```

**Cadential appoggiatura**:
```
     V                    I
     D → F (leap 3rd) → E
         ↑              ↓
        APP             res

F = appoggiatura (^4 over I chord)
Delays arrival of ^3 (E)
Creates expressive cadence
```

**Long appoggiatura** (expressive):
```
Beat:  1    2    3    4  |  1
       APP       res
       
APP lasts three beats
Resolution brief
Strong emotional effect
```

**In melodic context**:
```
mm. 3-4:  I         V
Melody:   C - E - F - E
          ↑   ↑   ↑   ↑
          CT  CT  APP res

Leap to F (appoggiatura)
Resolves to E (chord tone)
```

**Upward resolution** (less common):
```
     V           I
     B → G → A
         ↑   ↓
        APP  res up

G approached by leap down
G resolves UP to A
Unusual but valid
```

### From Repertoire

**Mozart, Opera arias**: Abundant appoggiaturas in vocal lines, expressive dissonances at climactic moments, often written out or indicated.

**Beethoven, Slow movements**: Appoggiaturas create expressive weight, particularly in lyrical themes (e.g., "Pathétique" Adagio).

**Chopin, Nocturnes**: Frequent appoggiaturas in melodic embellishment, often as part of elaborate ornamentation.

**Classical period melody**: Appoggiaturas nearly ubiquitous in expressive singing-style melodies, structural and ornamental types.

**Handel, Arias**: Long appoggiaturas create emotional expression, particularly on important text syllables in da capo arias.

## Common Confusions

- Appoggiatura = approached by **leap**, resolves by **step**
- Occurs on **strong beat** (accented, unlike most non-chord tones)
- Creates **dissonance** against harmony
- Usually resolves **down** by step
- **Not prepared** as consonance (unlike suspension)
- "Leap in, step out" (opposite of escape tone which is "step in, leap out")
- Etymology: "appoggiare" = "to lean upon" (Italian)
- Different from suspension (suspension held over/prepared, appoggiatura leaped to)
- Different from neighbor (neighbor approached by step)
- Different from passing tone (passing unaccented)
- Can be **long** (substantial duration) or **short** (brief)
- Creates expressive emphasis through accented dissonance
- Very common in Classical vocal writing (Mozart, Haydn)
- Resolution usually downward, but upward possible
- "APP" or "A" abbreviation in analysis
- Structural importance (not mere ornament in many contexts)
- Can be written out (full note value) or notated as grace note
- Grace note appoggiatura = small note before main note (performance practice)
- Written-out appoggiatura = full rhythmic value
- Essential for expressive melodic writing
