# Quick Definition
In species counterpoint, imperfect consonances are major and minor thirds and sixths -- less stable than perfect consonances but preferred for most of an exercise because they promote forward motion and variety.

# Core Definition
**Imperfect consonances** in counterpoint are major and minor thirds (M3, m3) and major and minor sixths (M6, m6). They are consonant but less stable than perfect consonances. The source states they should be preferred to perfect consonances for all intervals other than the first and last, "in order to heighten the sense of arrival at the end and to promote a sense of motion toward that arrival" (Ch. 2, First-Species Counterpoint).

# Key Properties
1. Four intervals: major third, minor third, major sixth, minor sixth
2. Consonant but less stable than perfect consonances
3. Preferred for all intervals except first and last in species counterpoint
4. No more than three of the same imperfect consonance type in a row (e.g., three consecutive thirds)
5. Parallel imperfect consonances are permitted (unlike parallel perfect consonances)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Determine the interval between voices
2. If it is a M3, m3, M6, or m6, it is an imperfect consonance

# Context & Application
Imperfect consonances provide the primary harmonic color of contrapuntal writing. Their mild instability creates the forward motion and variety that counterpoint requires. The restriction against more than three consecutive imperfect consonances of the same type ensures that even this preferred interval category contributes to variety rather than monotony.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Using more than three consecutive imperfect consonances of the same type
  **Correction**: Vary the types of consonances to maintain variety

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: "Imperfect" means these consonances are flawed
  **Clarification**: "Imperfect" refers to their lesser stability compared to perfect consonances; they are fully consonant and in fact preferred
