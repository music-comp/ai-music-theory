# Quick Definition
In species counterpoint, perfect consonances are perfect unisons, perfect fifths, and perfect octaves -- the most stable harmonic intervals, reserved primarily for beginnings and endings.

# Core Definition
**Perfect consonances** in counterpoint are the intervals of the perfect unison, perfect fifth, and perfect octave. These are distinguished from imperfect consonances (thirds and sixths) by their greater stability. In species counterpoint, perfect consonances are required at the beginning and end of exercises but should be used sparingly in the middle to promote variety and forward motion (Ch. 1, Example 1).

# Prerequisites
- **Interval** -- Must understand interval classification to identify perfect consonances

# Key Properties
1. Three intervals: perfect unison (P1), perfect fifth (P5), perfect octave (P8)
2. Most stable harmonic intervals in tonal music
3. Required at beginning and end of species exercises
4. Parallel perfect consonances (P5-P5, P8-P8) are strictly forbidden
5. Direct/hidden fifths and octaves (similar motion into a perfect consonance) should also be avoided
6. Unisons permitted only at first and last intervals in species counterpoint

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Check if the interval between two voices is a P1, P5, or P8
2. Compound versions (P12 = compound P5) count the same as their simple form

# Context & Application
Perfect consonances serve as structural pillars in counterpoint: they mark beginnings and endings with stability. However, overuse in the middle of an exercise reduces variety and weakens the sense of arrival at cadences. The prohibition against parallel fifths and octaves is one of the most fundamental rules in Western tonal music, extending well beyond species counterpoint into common-practice harmony.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Using two perfect consonances of the same size in a row (e.g., P5-P5)
  **Correction**: Never move from one perfect consonance to the same type; this creates parallel fifths or octaves

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The perfect fourth is a perfect consonance in counterpoint
  **Clarification**: In two-voice species counterpoint, the P4 is treated as dissonant when it involves the lowest voice
