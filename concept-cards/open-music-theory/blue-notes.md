# Blue Notes

## Quick Definition

Pitches that fall between the major and minor versions of a scale degree, particularly the third and seventh, creating the characteristic tension of blues melody.

## Formal Definition

**Blue notes** are pitches that are neither the major nor minor version of a scale degree, but somewhere in between. The most characteristic blue notes involve:

- **The third**: Between me (flat^3) and mi (^3)
- **The seventh**: Between te (flat^7) and ti (^7)

**Origin of the clash**:
When blues scale pitches (with flat^3 and flat^7) are sung or played over major chords (with ^3 and ^7), the resulting dissonance creates tension. In vocal and non-fixed-pitch instrument performance, these notes are often "bent" to fall between the two fixed pitches.

**Notation approaches**:
- In chord symbols: major chord with #9 extension (e.g., C7#9) captures the clash of major and minor third
- In melody: written as flat^3 or flat^7, with the understanding that performers may inflect

Blue notes cannot be accurately represented in equal-tempered notation; they exist in a microtonal space between fixed pitches.

## Musical Context

Blue notes give blues music its distinctive emotional quality - a sound of yearning, tension, and expressiveness that cannot be fully captured in standard notation. This is one reason why blues is often taught through listening and imitation rather than solely through written music.

The clash between melodic blue notes and harmonic chord tones is not "wrong" but is a defining feature of the style.

## Examples

### Basic

Over a C7 chord (C-E-G-Bb):
- Singing Eb against the E in the chord creates blue note tension
- The pitch may be bent or slurred somewhere between Eb and E
- In jazz notation, this clash might be shown as C7(#9), where #9 = D#/Eb

### From Repertoire

"Gulf Coast Blues" (Bessie Smith, 1923): The vocal line demonstrates blue notes, particularly the expressive bending of the third and seventh scale degrees against the accompanying harmonies.

Blue notes are heard in virtually all authentic blues performances, as well as in jazz and rock music influenced by blues.

## Common Confusions

- Blue notes are NOT simply flat^3 and flat^7; they exist BETWEEN major and minor
- Standard notation cannot accurately represent blue notes; it's an approximation
- Blue notes are intentional, not mistakes or "out of tune" singing
- The #9 chord extension in jazz is one way to capture the blue note clash harmonically
- Blue notes require context; the same pitch might not be a blue note in different music
