# Roman Numeral Analysis

## Quick Definition

An analytical system using Roman numerals to identify chords by their scale degree, quality, and inversion within a key.

## Formal Definition

**Roman numeral analysis** is an analytical method for labeling chords within the context of a key signature. Each Roman numeral conveys three types of information:

1. **Scale degree of the root** (the numeral itself: I, ii, iii, IV, V, vi, vii)
2. **Chord quality** (uppercase = major, lowercase = minor, ° = diminished, + = augmented, ∅ = half-diminished)
3. **Inversion** (figured bass symbols after the numeral: 6, 6/4, 7, 6/5, 4/3, 4/2)

In major keys (triads):
- I, IV, V = major (uppercase)
- ii, iii, vi = minor (lowercase)
- vii° = diminished

In minor keys (triads):
- i, iv, v (or V) = minor (lowercase, except V when raised)
- III, VI, VII (or vii°) = major (uppercase, except vii° when raised)
- ii° = diminished

Seventh chords add a superscript 7, with special symbols for half-diminished (∅7) and fully diminished (°7).

## Musical Context

Roman numeral analysis reveals:
- **Harmonic function**: I = tonic, V = dominant, IV = subdominant
- **Chord progressions**: Common patterns like I-IV-V-I, ii-V-I
- **Key relationships**: Same numerals in different keys show functional equivalence
- **Structural organization**: How harmony creates form and direction

Benefits over chord symbols:
- Shows function relative to key (V-I progression works in any key)
- Reveals patterns across different keys
- Standard in classical music analysis
- Essential for understanding harmonic syntax

Roman numerals are typically written below the bass staff in scores.

## Examples

### Basic

In C major:
- C major chord = I (root position tonic)
- D minor chord = ii (supertonic)
- G major chord = V (dominant)
- A minor chord, first inversion = vi6
- G dominant seventh = V7
- B diminished = vii°

In A minor:
- A minor chord = i (tonic)
- B diminished = ii°
- E major chord = V (dominant with raised leading tone)
- F major chord = VI
- G♯ diminished seventh = vii°7

### From Repertoire

The progression I-V-vi-IV (C-G-Am-F in C major) appears in countless pop songs. Bach chorales demonstrate Classical-period harmonic syntax (I-IV-V-I, ii-V-I). Jazz ii-V-I progressions (Dm7-G7-Cmaj7 in C major) are fundamental to the idiom.

## Common Confusions

- Roman numerals show FUNCTION in a key; chord symbols show absolute pitch content
- Uppercase vs. lowercase matters: I ≠ i (major vs. minor)
- The numeral indicates scale degree, not the note name (V in C major = G, but V in D major = A)
- Seventh chords: I7 = major seventh, V7 = dominant seventh (both written with "7" but different qualities)
- ii° vs. vii°: both are diminished but on different scale degrees (with different functions)
- Figures after Roman numerals show inversion, not interval quality
