# Quick Definition

A neighbor 6/4 chord features a static bass over which two upper voices perform upper-neighbor motion, typically prolonging I or V. The chords on both sides are always in root position.

# Core Definition

The **neighbor 6/4** (n. 6/4) consists of a static bass over top of which two voices have upper-neighbor motion (OMT, Ch. IV.9). Sometimes called "pedal 6/4" because of the static pedal in the bass. It most commonly prolongs I or V. The chords on both sides of the n. 6/4 are always in root position.

# Prerequisites

- **Cadential six-four** — Understanding 6/4 chord types requires knowing cad. 6/4 first

# Key Properties

1. Static bass (three identical notes: do-do-do or sol-sol-sol)
2. Two upper voices perform upper-neighbor motion
3. Most commonly prolongs I or V
4. Surrounding chords are always in ROOT POSITION
5. Bass is doubled in the 6/4 chord
6. Soprano may have either the neighbor motion or the static line

# Construction / Recognition

## To Write:
1. Write the bass: three identical notes (^1-^1-^1 or ^5-^5-^5)
2. Spell n. 6/4: sixth and fourth above middle bass note; one voice doubles bass
3. Write soprano: choose upper-neighbor line or static line
4. Fill inner voices with minimal motion

## To Recognize:
1. Find a 6/4 chord
2. Check: is the bass static?
3. Check: do two upper voices make neighbor motion?
4. If yes: neighbor 6/4

# Relationships

## Builds Upon
- **Tonic prolongation** — N. 6/4 is a prolongation technique for I or V

## Contrasts With
- **Cadential six-four** — Cad. 6/4: sol in bass with 6-5, 4-3 resolution; n. 6/4: static bass with neighbor motion
- **Passing six-four** — Pass. 6/4: stepwise bass; n. 6/4: static bass

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Confusing neighbor 6/4 with cadential 6/4
  **Clarification**: N. 6/4 has a static bass and the 6/4 chord returns to the original chord; cad. 6/4 has sol in the bass and resolves to V
