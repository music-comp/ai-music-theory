# Quick Definition
A six-note collection made entirely of whole steps (all steps equal to 2 semitones). Only two unique whole-tone collections exist: WT0 (even-numbered pitch classes: 0,2,4,6,8,10) and WT1 (odd-numbered: 1,3,5,7,9,11). The uniform step size creates rotational ambiguity and an "unsettled" quality.

# Core Definition
The whole-tone collection is perfectly symmetric -- all six steps are whole tones. This uniform spacing means there is no inherent tonic, creating a floating, directionless quality commonly exploited in film/TV for dream sequences. Only two transpositions produce distinct collections before they cycle back. Abbreviations WT0 and WT1 distinguish them (subscript indicates whether C or C-sharp is included).

# Prerequisites
None specific

# Key Properties
1. Six notes, all steps exactly 2 semitones
2. Only 2 distinct collections (WT0 and WT1)
3. Rotationally ambiguous -- no inherent tonic
4. No perfect fifths, no leading tones
5. "Dreamy" or "floating" quality from uniform spacing
6. Associated with Debussy and Impressionism

# Context & Application
The whole-tone collection is closely associated with Impressionism (Debussy's "Voiles"). Its lack of any differentiated interval creates a sense of harmonic stasis.

# Examples
**Example 1**: WT0 = {0,2,4,6,8,10} = C-D-E-F#-G#-A#.
**Example 2**: WT1 = {1,3,5,7,9,11} = C#-D#-F-G-A-B.
