# Hexatonic Collection

## Quick Definition

The hexatonic collection is a six-note symmetrical scale built by alternating half steps and minor thirds (1-3-1-3-1-3 in semitones). It can also be generated by combining two augmented triads a semitone apart. There are only four unique hexatonic collections. It contains triads but, like octatonic, lacks dominant-tonic function.

## Formal Definition

**Hexatonic collection**: A six-note pitch collection with the following properties:

**Structure**:
- **Interval pattern**: 1-3-1-3-1-3 (semitone, minor 3rd, alternating)
- **Cardinality**: 6 pitch classes
- **Contains two augmented triads** a semitone apart
- **Set class**: (014589), Forte number 6-20

**Only four unique collections** (due to major-3rd symmetry):
```
HEX0,1: C - C# - E - F - G# - A
        {0, 1, 4, 5, 8, 9}

HEX1,2: C# - D - F - F# - A - Bb
        {1, 2, 5, 6, 9, 10}

HEX2,3: D - Eb - F# - G - Bb - B
        {2, 3, 6, 7, 10, 11}

HEX3,4: Eb - E - G - Ab - B - C
        {3, 4, 7, 8, 11, 0}
```

**Naming convention**: The subscript numbers indicate a characteristic semitone (like octatonic).

**Symmetry**: Transposition by major 3rd (T4) maps the collection onto itself. Therefore: 12 transpositions / 3 (period) = 4 unique forms.

**Alternative generation**: Two augmented triads a semitone apart:
- HEX0,1 = C aug (C-E-G#) + Db aug (Db-F-A) = C aug + C# aug

## Musical Context

The hexatonic collection occupies a middle ground between:
- Whole-tone (all augmented triads, no semitones)
- Octatonic (many triads, alternating half and whole steps)

**Harmonic content** (per collection):
- 3 major triads
- 3 minor triads
- 2 augmented triads
- No diminished triads, no dominant 7ths

**Characteristics**:
- More triadic than whole-tone
- Less triadic than octatonic
- Contains semitones (unlike whole-tone)
- Creates smooth voice-leading possibilities between triads

**Neo-Riemannian theory**: The hexatonic collection has become important in neo-Riemannian analysis, where it represents a cycle of parsimonious (smooth) voice-leading transformations between triads.

## Examples

### Basic

**HEX0,1 spelled from C**:
```
C - C# - E - F - G# - A - (C)
   1    3   1   3    1   3

Two augmented triads:
  C - E - G# = C augmented
  C# - F - A = C# augmented (= Db augmented)
```

**Available triads in HEX0,1**:
```
Major triads: C, E, Ab (roots form augmented triad)
Minor triads: C#m, Fm, Am (roots form other aug triad)

Note: Major triad roots form one augmented triad;
minor triad roots form the other!
```

**Hexatonic cycle** (voice-leading perspective):
```
In HEX0,1, triads can transform with single voice motion:

C major -> C# minor -> E major -> F minor -> Ab major -> A minor -> C major
   (P)       (L)        (P)        (L)        (P)         (L)

P = Parallel (change third quality)
L = Leading-tone exchange

Each step moves only one voice by semitone!
```

**Comparing the four collections**:
```
HEX0,1: {0, 1, 4, 5, 8, 9}  - contains C, E, Ab major
HEX1,2: {1, 2, 5, 6, 9, 10} - contains Db, F, A major
HEX2,3: {2, 3, 6, 7, 10, 11} - contains D, F#, Bb major
HEX3,4: {3, 4, 7, 8, 11, 0} - contains Eb, G, B major
```

### From Repertoire

**Liszt, late works**: Liszt's experimental late pieces often employ hexatonic collections, exploiting smooth voice-leading between triads.

**Wagner, Tristan und Isolde**: Some analysts find hexatonic relationships in the chromatic harmony.

**Bartok**: Uses hexatonic alongside octatonic and other collections.

**Film music**: The "ethereal" quality of hexatonic (triads without tonal function) appears in many film scores.

**Neo-Riemannian analyses**: Works by Brahms, Schubert, and others reveal hexatonic cycles in seemingly chromatic passages.

## Common Confusions

- **Only FOUR unique hexatonic collections exist**: Not six or twelve
- **"Hexatonic" specifically means this 1-3-1-3-1-3 pattern**: Other six-note scales exist but aren't called hexatonic
- **Contains semitones, unlike whole-tone**: Both have 6 notes, but different structures
- **Two augmented triads a semitone apart**: Not a tritone, not a minor third apart
- **No diminished triads or dominant 7ths**: Despite having both major and minor triads
- **Also called "magic hexachord" in some sources**: Due to its voice-leading properties
- **Transposition by M3 gives the SAME collection**: This is why only 4 unique forms exist
