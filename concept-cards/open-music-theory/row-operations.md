# Quick Definition
The four fundamental row operations are Prime (P, original order), Retrograde (R, reversed order), Inversion (I, reversed interval directions), and Retrograde Inversion (RI, both reversed). Each can be transposed to 12 pitch levels, labeled with subscript numbers (e.g., P0, I5, R10, RI3).

# Core Definition
Transposition (T) moves all pitches up or down by a specified number of semitones. Inversion (I) reverses interval directions: rising becomes falling. Retrograde (R) reverses pitch order. Retrograde Inversion (RI) combines retrograde and inversion. The "prime" form (P) is typically the most salient row at the beginning of a work. Row forms are labeled: P forms by their starting pitch class, I forms by their starting pitch class, R forms by their ending pitch class (matching the start of the corresponding P), and RI forms by their ending pitch class (matching the start of the corresponding I). This ensures P and R with the same subscript are exact retrogrades. Two rows sharing the same ordered pitch-class intervals are the same form type.

# Key Properties
1. Four operations: P (prime), R (retrograde), I (inversion), RI (retrograde inversion)
2. Each transposable to 12 levels: P0-P11, I0-I11, R0-R11, RI0-RI11
3. Maximum 48 distinct forms (4 types x 12 transpositions)
4. P preserves intervallic content and arrangement
5. R preserves intervallic content but reverses arrangement
6. I preserves interval sizes but reverses directions
7. RI reverses both order and interval directions
8. Pn and Rn are exact retrogrades of each other

# Context & Application
Row operations provide the basic transformational toolkit for twelve-tone composition. Composers use them to generate variety while maintaining structural coherence through preserved interval content. The choice of which row forms to use (and in what order) is a central compositional decision.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The I label always matches the inversion operation that produced it
  **Clarification**: Only if you begin with P0; otherwise the labels will differ
- **Confusion**: RI is always computed as "retrograde of inversion"
  **Clarification**: The order of operations matters, but the standard labeling follows specific conventions
- **Confusion**: Serial operations are unique to twelve-tone music
  **Clarification**: Transposition, inversion, and retrograde appear in tonal music (fugues, canons)
