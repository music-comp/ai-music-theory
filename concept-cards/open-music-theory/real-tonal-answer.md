# Real Answer and Tonal Answer

## Quick Definition

Two types of fugal answer that transpose the subject: a real answer is an exact transposition by perfect fifth or fourth, while a tonal answer adjusts certain intervals to maintain the tonic-dominant relationship and avoid modulation, with the choice depending on the subject's structure and prominent scale degrees.

## Formal Definition

In fugal writing, when a second voice enters after the subject is stated, it presents either a **real answer** or a **tonal answer**—both are transpositions of the subject, but they differ in how faithfully they preserve the subject's intervallic structure.

**Real Answer**:
- Exact transposition of subject by perfect fifth (up) or perfect fourth (down)
- All generic intervallic relationships preserved
- Interval qualities remain the same (major → major, minor → minor, etc.)
- Used when subject doesn't prominently feature tonic-dominant relationship at opening
- Simpler to construct (mechanical transposition)

**Tonal Answer**:
- Modified transposition that adjusts certain intervals
- Alters prominent dominant (^5) or tonic (^1) references
- Maintains tonal balance between subject and answer
- Used when subject needs modification to avoid unwanted modulation
- More common in Baroque fugues than real answers

**When to use tonal answer** (subject has these features):
1. **Starts on sol (^5)**: Tonal answer starts on do (^1), not re (^2)
   - Subject: ^5 → ... / Answer: ^1 → ...
   - Avoids over-emphasizing dominant at answer's start

2. **Prominent V-I suggestion at start**: Answer reverses to I-V suggestion
   - Subject: ^5-^1 (dominant → tonic) / Answer: ^1-^5 (tonic → dominant)
   - Maintains tonic-dominant balance across subject-answer pair

3. **Subject modulates**: Answer modulates back
   - Subject ends in dominant key / Answer ends in tonic key
   - If subject modulates to dominant, answer returns to tonic
   - Split subject into two phrases, adapt each phrase's key center

**Construction principles**:
- Make minimal adjustments
- Keep subject and answer as similar as possible
- Both must make melodic sense independently
- Alterations typically occur at beginning or modulation point
- Rest of answer often follows real transposition after initial adjustment

**Terminology**:
- **Real**: Exact transposition (all intervals preserved)
- **Tonal**: Adjusted to maintain tonal relationships (^5 ↔ ^1 swap)
- Both are valid fugal answers; choice depends on subject characteristics

## Musical Context

Real vs. tonal answer choice affects fugal exposition structure:
- Tonal answer more common in High Baroque (Bach, Handel)
- Real answer simpler but can destabilize tonal center if subject emphasizes ^5
- Tonal answer requires more compositional skill but creates better balance
- Countersubject must work with both subject AND answer
- Decision impacts harmonic plan of exposition

The distinction teaches composers to think about tonal relationships beyond mere transposition, considering how melodic gestures imply harmonic functions.

## Examples

### Basic

**Subject requiring tonal answer** (starts on ^5):
```
Subject (C major):  G-A-B-C-B-A-G
Scale degrees:      ^5-^6-^7-^1-^7-^6-^5

Real answer (would be):  D-E-F#-G-F#-E-D (starts on ^2 - unbalanced!)
Tonal answer (correct):  C-D-E-F-E-D-C (starts on ^1 ✓)
Scale degrees:           ^1-^2-^3-^4-^3-^2-^1

Adjustment: First note changed from D (^2) to C (^1)
Result: ^5 in subject becomes ^1 in answer
```

**Subject with V-I opening**:
```
Subject (C major):  G-F-E-F-G (^5-^4-^3-^4-^5, suggesting V context)
Real answer:        D-C-B-C-D (^2-^1-^7-^1-^2)
Tonal answer:       C-Bb-A-Bb-C (^1-^7-^6-^7-^1, suggesting I context)

The opening sol-fa (^5-^4) becomes do-te (^1-^7)
Reverses harmonic implication: V → I becomes I → V
```

**Subject that modulates**:
```
Subject (C major, phrase 1): C-D-E-F (stays in C)
Subject (phrase 2):          G-A-B-C (modulates to G major)

Tonal answer (phrase 1):     G-A-B-C (modulates to G - reverses subject's phrase 1)
Tonal answer (phrase 2):     C-D-E-F (returns to C - reverses subject's phrase 2)

Each phrase adapts to opposite key of corresponding subject phrase
```

**Subject NOT requiring tonal answer** (starts on ^1):
```
Subject (C major):  C-E-G-E-C-D-E
Scale degrees:      ^1-^3-^5-^3-^1-^2-^3

Real answer (works perfectly): G-B-D-B-G-A-B
Scale degrees:                 ^1-^3-^5-^3-^1-^2-^3 (in G major)

No tonal adjustment needed - subject doesn't emphasize ^5-^1 at opening
```

**Minimal adjustment principle**:
```
Subject:        ^5-^6-^7-^1-^2-^3-^4-^5
Tonal answer:   ^1-^2-^3-^4-^5-^6-^7-^1
                ↑         [exact transposition resumes after first note]
Only first note adjusted (^5 → ^1), rest follows real transposition
```

### From Repertoire

**Bach Fugue in C Minor (BWV 847), WTC Book I**:
- Subject starts ^1-^5-^6 (C-G-Ab), emphasizing ^1-^5
- Answer is tonal: starts ^5-^1-^2 (G-C-D), reversing the emphasis
- Demonstrates tonal answer adjusting opening ^1-^5 to ^5-^1

**Bach Fugue in C Major (BWV 846), WTC Book I**:
- Subject starts ^1-^2-^3-^4-^5 (C-D-E-F-G)
- Answer is real: G-A-B-C-D (exact transposition up P5)
- Subject doesn't prominently feature ^5 at start, so real answer works

**Handel Fugue from Messiah ("And with His Stripes")**:
- Subject emphasizes ^5-^1 relationship
- Tonal answer reverses to ^1-^5
- Typical Baroque practice of tonal adjustment

**Bach Fugue in D Major (BWV 850), WTC Book I**:
- Subject modulates from D to A (tonic to dominant)
- Answer modulates from A back to D (dominant to tonic)
- Demonstrates tonal answer handling modulating subject

## Common Confusions

- "Tonal" doesn't mean "in a key"—both real and tonal answers are tonal
- Tonal answer ≠ "wrong" transposition—it's a deliberate, sophisticated adjustment
- Not every fugue needs tonal answer—depends on subject's characteristics
- "Real" doesn't mean "better"—just means exact transposition
- Tonal answer adjustment usually at beginning, not throughout entire answer
- ^5 → ^1 swap is most common adjustment, but not the only possibility
- Countersubject must work with BOTH subject and answer (challenge when answer is tonal)
- Tonal answer ≠ inexact—it's precisely crafted for tonal balance
- Some subjects allow either real or tonal answer (composer's choice)
- Answer at fifth above (real) vs. answer adjusted for tonal balance (tonal) are different concepts
- Don't confuse with "real sequence" vs. "tonal sequence" (related but different context)
