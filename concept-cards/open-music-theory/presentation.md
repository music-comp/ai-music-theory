# Quick Definition
The first subphrase of a sentence, consisting of a basic idea and its repetition (often varied through transposition, embellishment, or re-harmonization), typically stable and beginning on tonic harmony.

# Core Definition
The **presentation** is the first subphrase of a sentence, composed of a **basic idea** (b.i.) and its **repetition**. The repetition may be exact or varied through transposition, melodic/rhythmic embellishment, change of harmonization, or change of interval quality/size. The key identifier is **contour**: if the two ideas share the same contour, the second is heard as a varied repetition. Presentations typically begin on tonic harmony and may prolong tonic (I-V-V-I), move to dominant (I--V--), or move to a predominant (Ch. 2, "More Detail: The Presentation").

# Prerequisites
- **Sentence** -- The presentation is the first half of a sentence
- **Basic idea** -- The unit that is stated and repeated

# Key Properties
1. First subphrase of a sentence
2. Basic idea + repetition (often varied)
3. Typically 4 measures (with 2-measure basic ideas) or 2 measures (1-measure b.i.)
4. Does NOT end with a cadence (distinguishes from antecedent)
5. Shared contour between b.i. and repetition
6. Harmonically stable, typically on tonic

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for two similar ideas sharing the same contour
2. Verify no cadence at the end (otherwise it's an antecedent)
3. Check that the passage begins on tonic harmony

# Context & Application
The presentation establishes the thematic material of a sentence through repetition. Its stability contrasts with the instability of the continuation that follows.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 2, Example 6): Louise Farrenc, Cello Sonata, II -- clear presentation with basic idea and varied repetition.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **sentence** -- Presentation is the first half of a sentence
## Related
- **continuation** -- The second subphrase that follows the presentation
## Contrasts With
- **antecedent** -- Antecedent has b.i. + c.i. and ends with cadence; presentation has b.i. + repetition and no cadence

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: A presentation must have exact repetition
  **Clarification**: The repetition is often varied (transposition, embellishment, re-harmonization) while sharing the same contour
