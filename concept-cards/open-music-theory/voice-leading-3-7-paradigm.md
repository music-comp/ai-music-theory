# Voice Leading (3-7 Paradigm)

## Quick Definition

A jazz voice-leading technique where two upper voices alternate between the third and seventh of successive chords, creating smooth connections through minimal motion.

## Formal Definition

The **3-7 voice leading paradigm** is a fundamental jazz voice-leading pattern for connecting chords:

**Two-voice texture** (plus bass on roots):
- One voice alternates: 3rd of chord 1 -> 7th of chord 2
- Other voice alternates: 7th of chord 1 -> 3rd of chord 2

This works smoothly because:
- The 3rd and 7th are the essential chord tones (define quality)
- When roots move by fifth, the 3rd of one chord is a step away from the 7th of the next
- This creates minimal voice movement (lazy voices!)

**Three-voice extension**:
Add a top voice connecting 9ths and 13ths:
- 9th of chord 1 -> 13th of chord 2 (or vice versa)

The only intervals appearing between the two main voices are perfect fourths and fifths.

## Musical Context

Smooth voice leading is essential in jazz. Rather than thinking of chords as isolated vertical entities, jazz musicians think horizontally, connecting one chord to the next with minimal motion. The 3-7 paradigm provides a reliable framework for this connection.

This approach allows for easy comping (accompaniment) while maintaining harmonic clarity, since the 3rd and 7th define each chord's quality and function.

## Examples

### Basic

ii-V-I in C major (Dm7 - G7 - Cmaj7):
- Voice 1: F (3rd of Dm7) -> F (7th of G7) -> E (3rd of Cmaj7)
- Voice 2: C (7th of Dm7) -> B (3rd of G7) -> B (7th of Cmaj7)

Each voice moves by step or stays on a common tone.

### From Repertoire

This paradigm applies to countless jazz standards. Any tune with ii-V-I progressions or circle-of-fifths motion benefits from 3-7 voice leading.

## Common Confusions

- The bass plays roots; the 3-7 paradigm applies to upper voices only
- This paradigm works for roots related by fifth or by second
- Adding the 5th as a third voice is less stylistic than adding extensions (9th, 13th)
- "Lazy" voice leading doesn't mean careless; it means efficient and smooth
- The paradigm is a starting point, not a rigid rule; context may require variations
