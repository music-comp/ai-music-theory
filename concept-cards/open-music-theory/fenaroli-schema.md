# Fenaroli Schema

## Quick Definition

A four-stage pre-cadential galant schema with alternating bass motion (ti-do-re-mi or ^7-^1-^2-^3) and descending soprano (fa-mi-ti-do or ^4-^3-^7-^1), creating a V-I-V-I pattern that delays final cadence while maintaining tonic-dominant alternation, named after the 18th-century Neapolitan pedagogue Fedele Fenaroli.

## Formal Definition

The **Fenaroli** (named after Fedele Fenaroli, 1730-1818, Neapolitan music theorist and composer) is a pre-cadential schema that prolongs the approach to a final cadence through alternating tonic and dominant harmonies with characteristic stepwise bass ascent.

**Four-stage structure**:

| Stage | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|-------|---|---|---|---|
| Meter | S | W | S | W |
| Melody | fa (^4) | mi (^3) | ti (^7) | do (^1) |
| Bass | ti (^7) | do (^1) | re (^2) | mi (^3) |
| Figures | 6/5 | 5 | 6/5 | 6 |
| Roman numerals | V | I | V6/5 | I6 |

**Key characteristics**:
- **Pre-cadential function**: Delays final cadence with V-I alternations
- **Ascending bass**: ^7-^1-^2-^3 (stepwise ascent through four stages)
- **Descending soprano**: ^4-^3-^7-^1 (falls to tonic by steps)
- **Alternating harmony**: V-I-V-I (back-and-forth between tonic and dominant)
- **Metric pattern**: Strong-weak-strong-weak (S-W-S-W)
- **Incomplete cadence**: Ends on I6 (first inversion), not root position

**Harmonic oscillation**:
- Stage 1: V (6/5 position)
- Stage 2: I (root position)
- Stage 3: V6/5 (first inversion with seventh)
- Stage 4: I6 (first inversion)
- Creates back-and-forth motion rather than directed progression
- Prolongs dominant-tonic tension before resolution

**Bass line character**:
- Stepwise ascent: ^7-^1-^2-^3
- Each step alternates harmonic function
- Smooth voice leading in bass
- Ascent contrasts with descending soprano (contrary motion)

**Soprano line character**:
- Descends: ^4-^3-^7-^1
- Moves through scale degrees to tonic
- Combines stepwise motion (^4-^3) with leap (^3 down to ^7) and step (^7-^1)
- Arrival on ^1 coincides with I6 (not strong root-position tonic)

**Function**:
- **Pre-cadential delay**: Postpones final strong cadence
- **Harmonic oscillation**: V-I alternation maintains tension
- **Contrary motion**: Bass ascends while soprano descends
- **Incomplete resolution**: Ends on I6, inviting continuation

**Relation to other schemas**:
- Fenaroli vs. Cadenza Semplice: Similar alternating V-I but different bass patterns
- Often followed by authentic cadence (V-I) to provide strong close
- Can lead to other schemas or cadential formulas

## Musical Context

The Fenaroli schema serves specific purposes in galant music:
- **Cadential approach**: Prepares for final cadence without arriving there
- **Harmonic variety**: Alternates tonic and dominant for color
- **Pedagogical importance**: Named after influential teacher (partimento tradition)
- **Voice-leading model**: Teaches contrary motion and alternating harmonies
- **Pre-cadential rhetoric**: Creates expectation of arrival while delaying it

The schema's association with Fenaroli connects it to the Neapolitan partimento tradition of the 18th century.

## Examples

### Basic

**Fenaroli in C major**:
```
Stage 1 (S): Soprano F4 (fa/^4), Bass B3 (ti/^7), V6/5
Stage 2 (W): Soprano E4 (mi/^3), Bass C4 (do/^1), I
Stage 3 (S): Soprano B3 (ti/^7), Bass D4 (re/^2), V6/5
Stage 4 (W): Soprano C4 (do/^1), Bass E4 (mi/^3), I6

Bass ascends: B-C-D-E (stepwise through four stages)
Soprano descends: F-E-B-C (falls to tonic)
Harmony alternates: V-I-V-I
```

**Fenaroli bass pattern** (stepwise ascent):
```
Bass: ^7 → ^1 → ^2 → ^3
      B  →  C  →  D  →  E (in C major)
      \_______________/
      stepwise ascending

Each step alternates between V and I function
```

**Fenaroli soprano pattern** (descent to tonic):
```
Soprano: ^4 → ^3 → ^7 → ^1
         F  →  E  →  B  →  C (in C major)
         step  leap    step

Descends to tonic through characteristic intervals
```

**Fenaroli as pre-cadential delay**:
```
[Thematic material] → [Fenaroli: V-I-V-I6] → [Authentic Cadence: V-I]
                       ↑                      ↑
                  Delays resolution      Provides strong close

Fenaroli postpones final arrival, building expectation
```

**Fenaroli followed by cadence**:
```
Fenaroli stage 4: I6 (first inversion, weak)
Next: V7 → I (authentic cadence, strong)

I6 invites continuation to stronger tonic resolution
```

### From Repertoire

**Galant keyboard sonatas** (Rutini, Galuppi, Paradisi): Fenaroli appears before final cadences, demonstrating pre-cadential function.

**Mozart Piano Sonatas**: Uses Fenaroli-like patterns in closing sections, alternating V-I before final cadence.

**Haydn String Quartets**: Fenaroli patterns in phrase endings, delaying strong cadential arrival.

**Neapolitan partimento manuscripts** (Fenaroli's own): Original pedagogical examples showing schema in partimento exercises.

**Cimarosa, Paisiello operas**: Fenaroli patterns in arias and ensemble numbers, characteristic of Neapolitan school.

## Common Confusions

- Fenaroli ≠ final cadence (pre-cadential, delays resolution)
- Ends on I6 (first inversion), not strong root-position tonic
- Fenaroli vs. Cadenza Semplice: similar alternating V-I, different bass patterns
- Bass ascends (^7-^1-^2-^3), soprano descends (contrary motion)
- Alternating V-I ≠ progression toward goal—oscillates back and forth
- Named after Fedele Fenaroli (18th-century pedagogue), not just descriptive term
- Four stages typical but principle (alternating V-I) matters more than exact length
- Often followed immediately by authentic cadence for strong close
- Fenaroli schema ≠ "Fenaroli's Rule of the Octave" (different concepts)
- Works in both major and minor modes with appropriate adjustments
- Pre-cadential function means it prepares but doesn't deliver final cadence
- I6 at end invites continuation (not conclusive like root-position tonic)
