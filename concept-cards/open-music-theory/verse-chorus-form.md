# Quick Definition
Verse-chorus form splits the primary lyrical/musical content between two core sections: the verse (lyric-variant, narrative-advancing) and the chorus (lyric-invariant, highest intensity, title lyrics). Sections progress in cycles: verse-prechorus-chorus-postchorus (not all required). Dominated pop/rock since the 1960s. Other possible sections: prechorus (P), bridge (B), postchorus (Z).

# Core Definition
Verse (V): lyric-variant, advances narrative, tends to begin on-tonic, increasingly harmonically open since 1960s. Prechorus (P): energy gain, fragmentation, harmonic openness — bears characteristics of the d phrase in srdc. Chorus (C): lyric-invariant, title lyrics/hook, heightened intensity, most often begins on-tonic. Postchorus (Z): short section following chorus that closes the cycle. Bridge (B): appears once, replaces verse/prechorus in final cycle; more free than in AABA form. Cycles: sections recur in prototypical order (V-P-C-Z); first cycle often V-P-C, second adds Z, final cycle may be abbreviated (B-C or just C repeated). Climb: a phrase with prechorus function contained within a verse (too short for its own section).

# Key Properties
1. Two core sections: verse (lyric-variant) and chorus (lyric-invariant, title lyrics)
2. Sections progress in cycles: V-(P)-(C)-(Z)
3. Prechorus: energy gain, harmonic openness, fragmentation
4. Chorus: highest intensity, most memorable music
5. Bridge: appears once in final cycle, replaces V/P
6. Postchorus: closes cycle after chorus
7. Dominated pop/rock since the 1960s
8. Climb: single prechorus-function phrase within verse/strophe

# Context & Application
"Livin' on a Prayer" (Bon Jovi): intro-V-P-C-interlude-V-P-C-Z-abbreviated cycle with repeated choruses and fadeout. This illustrates typical verse-chorus form with all five core section types. Based on research by Summach (2012), Spicer (2011), and others.

# Examples
**Example 1** ("Livin' on a Prayer"): V(0:47)-P(1:18)-C(1:34) | V(1:54)-P-C-Z(2:56) | abbreviated final cycle.
**Example 2** (Prototypical): Intro-V-P-C-V-P-C-B-C-C (with possible fadeout).

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Bridge in verse-chorus form works like bridge in AABA.
  **Clarification**: In verse-chorus, bridges are more flexible and don't need to build expectation for strophe return; they often just provide contrast before the final chorus.
