# Neapolitan Voice Leading

## Quick Definition

Neapolitan voice leading follows the standard altered-tone practice where chromatic notes continue moving in the direction of their alteration: lowered-^2 (ra) resolves downward to ^7 (ti), and lowered-^6 (le) resolves downward to ^5 (sol), with the bass (^4) typically moving up by step to ^5.

## Formal Definition

The voice leading of the Neapolitan sixth chord follows a consistent pattern based on the principle that **altered tones continue moving in the direction of their alteration**.

**Key resolutions**:
- **lowered-^2 → ^7**: The chromatically lowered supertonic descends to the leading tone
- **lowered-^6 → ^5**: The lowered submediant descends to the dominant degree
- **^4 → ^5**: The bass (chordal third) ascends to the dominant root

## Resolution Patterns

### Direct Resolution: ♭II6 → V

```
♭II6 (D♭/F in C):       V (G in C):

Soprano: D♭ (lowered-^2) → B (^7)     [descending]
Alto:    A♭ (lowered-^6) → G (^5)     [descending]
Tenor:   F (^4)          → G (^5)     [ascending, doubled]
Bass:    F (^4)          → G (^5)     [ascending]
```

**Voice motion summary**:
- Outer voices: Soprano descends, bass ascends (contrary motion)
- Inner voices: Converge on ^5

### Resolution with Cadential 6/4: ♭II6 → cad.6/4 → V

```
♭II6:    cad.6/4:    V:
D♭/F     C/G         G-B-D

S: D♭ →  C    →  B   (lowered-^2 → ^1 → ^7)
A: A♭ →  G    →  G   (lowered-^6 → ^5, common tone)
T: F  →  E    →  D   (^4 → ^3 → ^2)
B: F  →  G    →  G   (^4 → ^5, common tone)
```

**The cad.6/4 provides a passing tone (^1) between lowered-^2 and ^7**:
- lowered-^2 → ^1 → ^7 creates smooth stepwise descent
- Delays the resolution to leading tone

### Resolution with viio7/V: ♭II6 → viio7/V → V

```
♭II6:    viio7/V:    V:
D♭/F     F#o7        G

S: D♭ →  C or A  →  B   (various resolutions)
A: A♭ →  F# or A →  G   (various resolutions)
T: F  →  E♭ or C →  D   (various resolutions)
B: F  →  F#      →  G   (chromatic ascent)
```

**The applied viio7 intensifies the dominant approach**:
- Creates additional chromatic motion
- Adds tritone tension before resolution

## Doubling Guidelines

**What to double**:
- The bass note (^4) - safest choice
- Creates stability without emphasizing chromatic tones

**What to avoid doubling**:
- lowered-^2 (chromatic root) - resolution would create parallel octaves
- lowered-^6 (chromatic fifth) - similar voice-leading problems

## Common Voice-Leading Errors

**Avoid parallel fifths/octaves**:
```
INCORRECT:
♭II6:    V:
S: D♭ →  D   (parallel 5ths with bass)
B: F  →  G

CORRECT:
S: D♭ →  B   (lowered-^2 → ^7)
B: F  →  G   (^4 → ^5)
```

**Avoid augmented second in resolution**:
```
When resolving to V, be careful of:
D♭ → B (augmented second in inner voice)

Can be avoided by:
- Using cad.6/4 as intermediary
- Placing D♭ in soprano where the A2 is more acceptable melodically
```

## The Characteristic Resolution

The most distinctive voice leading of the Neapolitan is:

**lowered-^2 → ^7 (D♭ → B in C minor)**:
- A diminished third (enharmonically equivalent to major second)
- Sounds like a whole step down
- Highly characteristic of the chord's resolution
- Creates the "Neapolitan" color

## Musical Context

The voice leading of the Neapolitan creates:
- **Chromatic intensity**: Multiple chromatic tones resolving simultaneously
- **Smooth bass motion**: ^4 → ^5 provides stable foundation
- **Expressive descent**: Upper voices descending chromatically
- **Cadential weight**: Strong preparation of the dominant

## Examples

### Basic

**Standard four-voice resolution in C minor**:
```
        ♭II6     V       i
S:      D♭   →   B   →   C
A:      A♭   →   G   →   G  
T:      F    →   G   →   G
B:      F    →   G   →   C

All chromatic tones resolve correctly:
- D♭ → B (lowered-^2 → ^7)
- A♭ → G (lowered-^6 → ^5)
- Bass F → G (^4 → ^5)
```

**With cadential 6/4**:
```
        ♭II6     cad.6/4   V       i
S:      D♭   →   C     →   B   →   C
A:      A♭   →   G     →   G   →   G
T:      F    →   E     →   D   →   E♭
B:      F    →   G     →   G   →   C

D♭ passes through C on way to B
Smoother soprano line
```

### From Repertoire

**Chopin, Nocturne in F minor, Op. 55, No. 1**:
- Clear demonstration of standard ♭II6 voice leading
- Harmonic rhythm accommodates smooth resolution

**Classical-era examples (Mozart, Beethoven)**:
- Typically use cad.6/4 between ♭II6 and V
- Creates the characteristic lowered-^2 → ^1 → ^7 soprano line

## Common Confusions

- lowered-^2 resolves DOWN (not up to ^3)
- lowered-^6 resolves DOWN to ^5 (not to ^7)
- The bass (^4) goes UP to ^5 (contrary to the descending upper voices)
- With cad.6/4, lowered-^2 goes to ^1 FIRST, then to ^7
- Double the bass (^4), not chromatic tones
- The diminished third (D♭ → B) sounds like a whole step but is spelled as third
- Resolution to ^7 may create augmented second in inner voice (acceptable in context)
