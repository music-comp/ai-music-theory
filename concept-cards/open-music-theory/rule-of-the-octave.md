# Quick Definition
A standard harmonization of the bass scale (ascending and descending) providing one chord for each scale degree, serving as a framework for harmonizing bass lines in the partimento tradition.

# Core Definition
The **Rule of the Octave** is a standard harmonization of the bass scale in the galant/partimento tradition, based closely on Fenaroli's version (Naples, 1775). The source builds it in four steps: (1) parallel 6/3 chords only; (2) strategic 5/3 on tonic for closure; (3) 5/3 on dominant for hierarchy; (4) seventh chords before tonic and dominant. It can be practiced in three positions with top voice centered on tonic, mediant, or dominant (Ch. 13). The chapter also covers scale sequences: 5-6 patterns, 7-6 chains of suspensions, cycles of fifths, and 2-3 suspensions.

# Key Properties
1. One chord per scale degree, ascending and descending
2. Based on Fenaroli's version (Naples, 1775)
3. Built from parallel 6/3 chords through progressive enrichment
4. Strategic 5/3 on tonic and dominant creates harmonic hierarchy
5. Seventh chords precede important structural chords
6. Three positions (top voice on tonic, mediant, or dominant)
7. Related sequential harmonizations: 5-6, 7-6, cycle of fifths, 2-3

# Construction / Recognition
## To Build (four steps):
1. Parallel 6/3 chords on every scale degree
2. 5/3 on tonic (degrees 1 and 8)
3. 5/3 on dominant (degree 5)
4. Seventh chords before tonic and dominant chords

# Context & Application
The Rule of the Octave was a core pedagogical tool in 18th-century music education. Students memorized it in all keys and positions. It provides an efficient framework for harmonizing any bass line.
