# Quick Definition
The puff schema (I-iii-IV) uses the mediant triad as a stepping stone, typically at phrase openings (not as a loop). Named for "Puff, the Magic Dragon" (Peter, Paul and Mary, 1963). A common chromatic variant raises iii to III# (I-III#-IV), creating the chromatic voice-leading sol-si-la (^5-#^5-^6). III# can also function as V/vi, enabling deceptive motion when followed by IV instead of vi.

# Core Definition
The puff schema (I-iii-IV) is typically found at phrase beginnings, not as a repeating loop. What follows IV varies. In minor: i-III-iv (with variable chord quality on IV). The chromatic variant I-III#-IV raises the third of iii to make a major III# chord, producing the chromatic line sol-si-la. III# can be interpreted as V/vi: when followed by vi, it is an authentic resolution; when followed by IV, it creates deceptive motion (V-VI in the key of vi). "Creep" (Radiohead, 1993) combines puff with plagal mode mixture (I-III#-IV-iv). "Weekend Wars" (MGMT, 2007) exploits the play between deceptive (III#-IV) and authentic (III#-vi) resolutions.

# Key Properties
1. Core pattern: I-iii-IV (uses mediant as stepping stone)
2. Typically at phrase openings, not looped
3. Named for "Puff, the Magic Dragon"
4. Chromatic variant: I-III#-IV (raised third creates chromatic line)
5. III# = V/vi; can resolve authentically (to vi) or deceptively (to IV)
6. Minor version: i-III-iv (variable IV quality)
7. What follows IV varies (not fixed like four-chord schemas)

# Context & Application
"Puff, the Magic Dragon" (1963): puff schema at phrase beginnings, followed by I. "Let's Get It On" (Marvin Gaye): followed by V. "House of the Rising Sun" (Animals, 1964): minor puff with major IV progressing to VI. "Creep" (Radiohead, 1993): I-III#-IV-iv combining puff with plagal mode mixture.

# Examples
**Example 1** ("Puff, the Magic Dragon"): I-iii-IV-I at phrase openings.
**Example 2** ("Creep"): I-III#-IV-iv (G-B-C-Cm) with chromatic sol-si-la-le line.
