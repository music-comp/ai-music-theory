## Quick Definition

A chord progression built on I-iii-IV motion, typically used at phrase beginnings rather than as a repeating loop, named after "Puff, the Magic Dragon."

## Formal Definition

The puff schema is based on the progression I-iii-IV, featuring the relatively rare mediant (iii) chord. Unlike many pop schemas that loop cyclically, the puff schema typically appears at phrase openings as a building block within goal-oriented phrases. The chords following IV can vary, making this a partial schema that integrates with different phrase endings.

## Musical Context

The iii chord is uncommon in pop music, making the puff schema distinctive when it appears. The schema creates chromatic voice-leading opportunities, particularly when varied with a major III chord.

### Common Variations

- **I-III#-IV**: Major III replaces minor iii, creating chromatic line ^5-#^5-^6 (sol-si-la)
- **i-III-iv**: Minor-key version with major III
- **Chord quality flexibility**: IV can be major or minor in different contexts

## Examples

### Basic Example

In C major: C - Em - F (I-iii-IV)
Following chords vary: ...F - C or ...F - G - C, etc.

### From Repertoire

- "Puff, the Magic Dragon" by Peter, Paul and Mary (1963) - namesake song; puff schema begins most phrases
- "Let's Get It On" by Marvin Gaye - puff schema leads to V-I motion
- "House of the Rising Sun" by The Animals (1964) - minor key puff with major IV leading to VI
- "Creep" by Radiohead (1993) - I-III#-IV combined with plagal mode mixture (IV-iv-I)
- "Weekend Wars" by MGMT (2007) - plays with III# as V/vi, alternating authentic and deceptive resolutions

## Related Concepts

- Mediant chord
- Applied chords (V/vi)
- Plagal motion
- Deceptive resolution
- Chromatic voice leading

## Common Confusions

- **Puff vs. looping schemas**: The puff schema typically opens phrases rather than repeating as a chord loop
- **III# as V/vi**: When III# is followed by vi, it functions as an applied dominant; when followed by IV, it's a puff schema (deceptive resolution of V/vi)
- **iii vs. III#**: The diatonic iii is minor; the chromatic variation III# (raised third) is major and creates different voice-leading
