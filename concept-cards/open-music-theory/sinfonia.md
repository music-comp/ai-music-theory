# Sinfonia (Baroque Keyboard)

## Quick Definition

A three-voice Baroque keyboard composition (specifically J.S. Bach's 15 *Sinfonias*, BWV 787-801) employing continuous imitative counterpoint with all three voices equally important and active throughout, more complex than the two-voice *Inventions* through addition of a third independent contrapuntal line, serving pedagogical purposes for developing three-part contrapuntal technique, finger independence, and musical expression at the keyboard.

## Formal Definition

**Sinfonia** (in Bach's usage; Italian: "symphony" or "sounding together") refers to a keyboard composition in three-voice imitative counterpoint. While "sinfonia" had various meanings in Baroque music (orchestral introduction, opera overture), Bach's 15 keyboard *Sinfonias* (originally titled "Fantasias") represent a specific pedagogical genre.

**Key characteristics**:
- **Three voices**: Three independent contrapuntal lines throughout
- **Imitative texture**: All voices participate in presenting thematic material
- **Continuous development**: Through-composed, no strict sectional divisions
- **Equal importance**: All three voices melodically significant (not melody + accompaniment)
- **Motivic development**: Subjects presented, developed, combined systematically
- **Keyboard idiom**: Written specifically for harpsichord or clavichord
- **Pedagogical function**: Teaches three-part writing, contrapuntal thinking, keyboard technique
- **Binary or continuous form**: Some have two-part structure, others through-composed

**Relationship to Inventions**:
- **Inventions** (BWV 772-786): Two voices
- **Sinfonias** (BWV 787-801): Three voices
- Same pedagogical series, progressive difficulty
- Bach's original title: "Inventions" (2-voice) and "Fantasias" (3-voice)
- Later tradition: "Inventions" and "Sinfonias"

**Typical structure**:
1. **Exposition**: Subject presented in all three voices imitatively
2. **Development**: Motivic material developed, sequences, modulations
3. **Episodes**: Freer passages between subject entries
4. **Recapitulation**: Return to tonic with final subject entries (if binary form)
5. **Continuous**: Or through-composed without clear sectional divisions

## Contrapuntal Characteristics

**Three-voice texture**:
- Voice 1 (usually soprano): Melodic, thematic entries
- Voice 2 (usually alto/tenor): Independent countermelodies, thematic entries
- Voice 3 (usually bass): Harmonic foundation, but also thematically active

**All voices equal**: Unlike trio sonata texture (two melody voices + bass), sinfonia gives all three voices melodic/thematic significance

**Imitative entries**:
- Subject appears in different voices at different times
- Voices exchange material (invertible counterpoint common)
- Continuous interplay rather than strict fugal form

## Musical Context

Sinfonias serve specific purposes:
- **Pedagogical**: Teach three-part contrapuntal writing and thinking
- **Technical development**: Develop finger independence for three-voice keyboard texture
- **Musical expression**: Foster phrasing, articulation, dynamic shaping in polyphony
- **Compositional models**: Demonstrate three-voice imitative writing principles
- **Keyboard literature**: Essential repertoire for keyboard students and performers
- **Contrapuntal study**: Bridge between Inventions (2-voice) and fugues (3-4 voices)

Bach composed the Sinfonias as part of the *Clavier-Büchlein* for his son Wilhelm Friedemann.

## Examples

### Basic

**Typical opening (subject presentation)**:
```
Measure 1:
Voice 1 (soprano): C-D-E-F-G (subject begins)
Voice 2 (alto): [rest]
Voice 3 (bass): [rest]

Measure 3:
Voice 1: [continues with countermelody]
Voice 2: C-D-E-F-G (subject enters imitatively)
Voice 3: [rest]

Measure 5:
Voice 1: [continues]
Voice 2: [continues]
Voice 3: C-D-E-F-G (subject enters in bass)

All three voices participate in presenting subject imitatively
```

**Three-voice texture** (simultaneous activity):
```
Voice 1: Eighth-note scale passage (melodic activity)
Voice 2: Syncopated quarter notes (rhythmic contrast)
Voice 3: Sustained half notes (harmonic foundation)

All three voices independent and essential
No voice merely accompanying
```

**Invertible counterpoint** (voices exchange):
```
Measures 1-4:
Soprano: Subject
Alto: Countersubject A
Bass: Countersubject B

Measures 9-12:
Soprano: Countersubject B (exchanged from bass)
Alto: Subject (exchanged from soprano)
Bass: Countersubject A (exchanged from alto)

Voices trade material, demonstrating invertible counterpoint
```

**Sequential development**:
```
Sequence pattern repeated at descending intervals:
Measures 5-6: Motive at pitch level 1
Measures 7-8: Same motive at pitch level 2 (down a step)
Measures 9-10: Same motive at pitch level 3 (down another step)

Sequence typical in developmental sections
```

**Binary form structure** (some sinfonias):
```
Part A: Measures 1-16 (tonic to dominant, modulates, double bar)
Part B: Measures 17-32 (dominant returns to tonic)

Binary structure provides formal clarity
```

### From Repertoire

**J.S. Bach, Sinfonia No. 1 in C Major (BWV 787)**: Opening sinfonia in the set, clear three-voice texture, imitative entries, flowing eighth-note figuration throughout all voices.

**Bach, Sinfonia No. 4 in D Minor (BWV 790)**: Demonstrates sophisticated three-voice counterpoint, chromatic harmony, sequential development.

**Bach, Sinfonia No. 9 in F Minor (BWV 795)**: More complex, expressive character, demonstrates mature three-part writing with all voices thematically significant.

**Bach, Sinfonia No. 11 in G Minor (BWV 797)**: Continuous three-voice imitation, sophisticated motivic development, pedagogically valuable.

**Bach, Sinfonia No. 15 in B Minor (BWV 801)**: Concluding work in set, combines learned counterpoint with keyboard idiom, all three voices equally important.

## Common Confusions

- "Sinfonia" in Bach = three-voice keyboard piece (not orchestral symphony)
- Bach's original title: "Fantasias" (not "Sinfonias," later tradition renamed them)
- Sinfonia ≠ fugue (less strict formal structure, more continuous development)
- Three voices equally important (not melody + accompaniment)
- 15 Sinfonias complement 15 Inventions (one set, progressive pedagogy)
- All voices thematically active (not just soprano melody with inner voices)
- Pedagogical purpose (teach three-part writing, keyboard technique)
- Written for harpsichord/clavichord (not orchestra despite "sinfonia" name)
- Not same as opera sinfonia (orchestral introduction to opera/cantata)
- Not same as Italian overture/symphony (later classical symphony)
- "Sinfonia" = "sounding together" (three voices in counterpoint)
- More complex than Inventions due to third voice
- Continuous imitative texture throughout (no homophonic sections)
- Some have binary form, others through-composed
- Invertible counterpoint common (voices exchange material)
- Essential keyboard pedagogy repertoire (Czerny, Hanon level students progress to Bach)
- All 15 in different keys (pedagogical survey of major/minor keys)
