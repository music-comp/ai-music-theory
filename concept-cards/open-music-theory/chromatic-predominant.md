# Chromatic Predominant

## Quick Definition

A chromatic predominant is a chord that functions as a predominant (preparing the dominant) but contains chromatic alterations not found in the diatonic scale, including the Neapolitan sixth (♭II6), borrowed chords like iv and iio6 in major keys, and augmented sixth chords.

## Formal Definition

**Chromatic predominants** are chords that:
1. Function as predominants (lead to V or cad.6/4 → V)
2. Contain one or more chromatic (non-diatonic) notes
3. Provide more dramatic, intensified approaches to the dominant than diatonic predominants

**Categories**:
- **Neapolitan sixth** (♭II6): Major triad on lowered-^2
- **Borrowed predominants**: iv, iio6, ii∅7 in major keys (from parallel minor)
- **Augmented sixth chords**: Italian, French, German sixths (covered separately)

## Types of Chromatic Predominants

### Neapolitan Sixth (♭II6)

**Structure in C minor/major**:
- D♭-F-A♭ in first inversion (F in bass)
- Chromatic notes: lowered-^2 (D♭), lowered-^6 (A♭)
- Quality: Major

**Function**: Intensified predominant
**Resolution**: → V (or cad.6/4 → V)
**Character**: Dramatic, exotic, chromatic

### Borrowed Predominants (Modal Mixture)

**iv (minor subdominant in major)**:
- In C major: F-A♭-C
- Chromatic note: lowered-^6 (A♭)
- Quality: Minor
- Character: Poignant, dark

**iio6 (diminished supertonic in major)**:
- In C major: D-F-A♭ (first inversion)
- Chromatic note: lowered-^6 (A♭)
- Quality: Diminished
- Character: Tense, chromatic

**ii∅7 (half-diminished seventh in major)**:
- In C major: D-F-A♭-C
- Chromatic note: lowered-^6 (A♭)
- Quality: Half-diminished
- Character: Rich, chromatic

## Comparison of Chromatic Predominants

| Chord | Notes in C | Chromatic Content | Intensity |
|-------|------------|-------------------|-----------|
| iv | F-A♭-C | lowered-^6 | Moderate |
| iio6 | D-F-A♭ (F bass) | lowered-^6 | Moderate |
| ii∅7 | D-F-A♭-C | lowered-^6 | Moderate |
| ♭II6 | D♭-F-A♭ (F bass) | lowered-^2, lowered-^6 | High |

## Voice Leading Principles

All chromatic predominants follow the principle that **altered tones resolve in the direction of their alteration**:

**lowered-^6 → ^5**:
- A♭ → G in C major/minor
- Descending half step
- Common to all the above chords

**lowered-^2 → ^7** (Neapolitan only):
- D♭ → B in C
- Descending (diminished third / whole step)
- Most dramatic resolution

## Function in Cadential Progressions

**Standard pattern**:
```
... → Chromatic PD → V → I/i
      (♭II6, iv, etc.)
```

**With cadential 6/4**:
```
... → Chromatic PD → cad.6/4 → V → I/i
```

**Multiple predominants**:
```
... → iv → ♭II6 → V → i
      PD    PD    D   T
```

## Diatonic vs. Chromatic Predominants

| Aspect | Diatonic PD | Chromatic PD |
|--------|-------------|--------------|
| Notes | All in key | Contains alterations |
| Examples | ii, IV, ii7 | ♭II6, iv, ii∅7 |
| Intensity | Standard | Heightened |
| Color | Neutral | Dramatic |
| Context | General use | Expressive moments |

## Musical Context

Chromatic predominants are used for:
- **Dramatic cadences**: High emotional intensity
- **Expressive passages**: Romantic-era especially
- **Minor-key writing**: Natural home for ♭II6
- **Climactic moments**: Building tension before resolution
- **Color contrast**: Against diatonic passages

## Examples

### Basic

**Chromatic predominant comparison in C minor**:
```
Diatonic:    i  - iio6 - V  - i
             Cm - Do6  - G  - Cm

Chromatic:   i  - ♭II6 - V  - i
             Cm - D♭/F - G  - Cm
             (more dramatic)
```

**In C major (borrowed)**:
```
Diatonic:    I  - ii  - V  - I
             C  - Dm  - G  - C

Chromatic:   I  - iv  - V  - I
             C  - Fm  - G  - C
             (borrowed from parallel minor)
```

**Multiple chromatic predominants**:
```
i  - iv  - ♭II6 - V  - i
Cm - Fm  - D♭/F - G  - Cm

Two chromatic predominants intensify cadence
```

### From Repertoire

**Chopin, Nocturne in F minor**:
- Uses ♭II6 as dramatic predominant
- Creates characteristic Romantic intensity

**Brahms, symphonies and chamber music**:
- Extensive use of iv in major keys
- Borrowed predominants for expressive depth

**Beethoven, Piano Sonata "Pathetique"**:
- Neapolitan sixth at dramatic cadences
- Part of minor-key expressive vocabulary

## Common Confusions

- All chromatic predominants still FUNCTION as predominants (lead to V)
- Chromatic predominants are more INTENSE, not different in function
- The Neapolitan is the most chromatic of the common chromatic predominants
- Borrowed chords (iv, iio6, ii∅7) are also chromatic predominants when used in major keys
- Augmented sixth chords (not covered here) are another category of chromatic predominant
- The chromatic notes resolve in predictable ways (lowered-^6 → ^5, lowered-^2 → ^7)
- Chromatic predominants can be combined (iv → ♭II6 → V)
