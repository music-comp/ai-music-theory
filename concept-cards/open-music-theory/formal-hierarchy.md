# Quick Definition
Musical form is organized as a hierarchical grouping of units: pieces contain movements, movements contain sections, sections contain themes, themes contain phrases, phrases contain ideas, and ideas contain motives -- though in practice, levels may collapse or overlap.

# Core Definition
"One way to understand musical form is as a hierarchical grouping of units." The source presents the hierarchy: piece → movements → sections → themes → phrases → ideas → motives. However, "the relationships between the levels are a little more complicated in reality. For instance, sometimes two levels are collapsed into one: a single phrase may comprise an entire section of a work." The hierarchy is "a guide and not something that strictly defines formal levels" (Ch. 1, "Hierarchy").

# Key Properties
1. Seven levels: piece, movement, section, theme, phrase, idea, motive
2. Levels may collapse (e.g., a single phrase = an entire section)
3. Hierarchy is a guide, not a rigid structure
4. Square brackets: ideas and subphrases (below phrase level)
5. Arcs: phrases and above (must end with cadence)

# Context & Application
Understanding this hierarchy provides the framework for all formal analysis. Phrase-level form focuses on the lower levels (motives, ideas, subphrases, phrases).
