# Harmonic Rhythm

## Quick Definition

The rate and pattern of harmonic change in music—not the chord progression itself, but the rhythm created by when chords change, ranging from very slow harmonic rhythm (one chord per phrase or multiple measures) to very fast (chord changes on every beat or subdivision), with the interplay between harmonic rhythm and melodic/surface rhythm creating structural hierarchy, typically featuring acceleration toward cadences where faster harmonic rhythm intensifies arrival points, and serving as a crucial element of musical style, pacing, and formal articulation.

## Formal Definition

**Harmonic rhythm** is the rhythm created by the pattern of chord changes in a musical passage.

**Key characteristics**:
- **Rate of change**: How frequently harmonies change
- **Pattern of change**: Regular vs. irregular chord changes
- **Independent of melody**: Separate from melodic rhythm
- **Structural function**: Creates formal articulation
- **Cadential acceleration**: Often speeds up at cadences

**Terminology**:
- **Slow harmonic rhythm**: Infrequent chord changes (one chord per measure or longer)
- **Fast harmonic rhythm**: Frequent chord changes (multiple chords per measure)
- **Accelerating**: Harmonic rhythm speeds up
- **Decelerating**: Harmonic rhythm slows down

**Not the same as**: Chord progression (what chords), melodic rhythm (melody notes)

## Harmonic Rhythm Patterns

**Regular/Consistent harmonic rhythm**:
```
Measure: 1    2    3    4
Chord:   I    V    vi   IV

One chord per measure
Predictable, stable pattern
Common in popular music
```

**Irregular harmonic rhythm**:
```
Measure: 1         2    3    4
Chord:   I         IV   V    I
         (2 bars)  (1)  (1)  (1)

Varied durations
Less predictable
More sophisticated
```

**Cadential acceleration**:
```
Measures: 1-2     3-4     5      6      7      8
Chords:   I       IV      ii     V      V⁷     I
          (2m)    (2m)    (1m)   (1m)   (1m)   (1m)

Harmonic rhythm accelerates approaching cadence
Creates momentum and drive
```

**Sub-measure harmonic rhythm**:
```
Beat:  1    2    3    4
Chord: I    V    I    V

Chord changes every beat
Very fast harmonic rhythm
Creates intensity
```

## Harmonic Rhythm and Musical Structure

**Opening** (often slower):
```
Establishes key and stability
Longer chord durations
Relaxed pacing
```

**Middle/Development** (varied):
```
Can be faster or irregular
Creates motion and instability
Drives forward momentum
```

**Cadence** (typically accelerates):
```
Harmonic rhythm speeds up
More frequent chord changes
Intensifies arrival
```

**Post-cadential** (often decelerates):
```
Harmonic rhythm slows after cadence
Confirms arrival
Relaxes tension
```

## Harmonic Rhythm vs. Other Rhythms

**Harmonic rhythm** (chord changes):
```
Measure: 1         2         3         4
Chord:   I         I         V         I
HR:      [--------][--------][--------][--------]

One harmonic event per measure
```

**Melodic/Surface rhythm** (note events):
```
Measure: 1         2         3         4
Melody:  ♪♪♪♪ ♪♪♪♪ ♪♪♪♪ ♪♪♪♪
SR:      Many melodic events

Fast surface rhythm, slow harmonic rhythm
Creates tension between layers
```

**These can align or contrast**:
- **Aligned**: Chord changes on strong beats with melody
- **Contrasted**: Slow harmonic rhythm with fast melodic rhythm (common)
- **Syncopated**: Chord changes on weak beats

## Musical Context

Harmonic rhythm serves critical purposes:
- **Pacing control**: Regulates musical energy and momentum
- **Formal articulation**: Marks phrase boundaries and sections
- **Stylistic character**: Defines genre and period characteristics
- **Textural interest**: Creates layering with melodic rhythm
- **Cadential intensity**: Accelerates to strengthen arrivals
- **Structural hierarchy**: Slower changes = higher structural level
- **Expressive tool**: Affects tension, relaxation, drive

Harmonic rhythm is fundamental to musical architecture and expression.

## Examples

### Basic

**Slow harmonic rhythm**:
```
Measures: 1-4      5-8
Chords:   I        V
          (4 bars) (4 bars)

Very slow change
Creates stasis, modal quality
One harmony sustained
```

**Moderate harmonic rhythm**:
```
Measure: 1    2    3    4
Chord:   I    IV   V    I

One chord per measure
Standard, balanced pacing
Common in hymns
```

**Fast harmonic rhythm**:
```
Beat:    1    2    3    4  |  1    2    3    4
Chord:   I    vi   IV   V  |  I    vi   ii   V

Chord every beat
Creates urgency and drive
Common at cadences
```

**Cadential acceleration**:
```
mm. 1-2:   I  (2 measures, slow)
mm. 3-4:   IV (2 measures, slow)
m. 5:      ii (1 measure)
m. 6:      V  (1 measure)
m. 7-8:    I  (2 measures, slowing again)

Speeds up approaching cadence (mm. 5-6)
Slows after arrival (mm. 7-8)
```

**Syncopated harmonic rhythm**:
```
Beat:    1  &  2  &  3  &  4  &
         I     V     I     V

Chord changes on offbeats
Creates rhythmic interest
```

### From Repertoire

**Bach, Chorales**: Mostly regular harmonic rhythm (chord per beat or two), occasional acceleration at cadences, demonstrates clear phrasing.

**Mozart, Piano Sonatas**: Varied harmonic rhythm, slow at openings, accelerates toward cadences, creates Classical elegance and proportion.

**Beethoven, Symphonies**: Dramatic contrasts in harmonic rhythm, slow changes in lyrical sections vs. rapid changes in climactic passages.

**Popular music (generic)**: Often regular harmonic rhythm (one chord per measure or two), creates predictable, danceable quality.

**Jazz standards**: Can feature very fast harmonic rhythm (chord per beat or faster), creates complexity and forward drive.

**Romantic period (Chopin, Schumann)**: More varied and irregular harmonic rhythm than Classical period, expressive flexibility.

## Common Confusions

- Harmonic rhythm = **rhythm of chord changes** (not the chords themselves)
- Independent of melodic/surface rhythm (can contrast or align)
- Slow HR = infrequent changes (one chord per multiple measures)
- Fast HR = frequent changes (multiple chords per measure)
- Cadential acceleration = HR speeds up approaching cadence (very common)
- Not the same as chord progression (what chords, not when they change)
- Not the same as tempo (overall speed of music)
- Can be regular (predictable pattern) or irregular (varied durations)
- Creates structural hierarchy (slower changes = higher level)
- Affects pacing and energy (faster HR = more intense)
- Slower at beginnings, faster at cadences (typical pattern)
- Post-cadential deceleration common (confirms arrival)
- Can change within a piece (varies by section)
- Style-specific (Classical vs. popular vs. jazz have different norms)
- Syncopated HR = changes on weak beats/offbeats
- Interacts with melodic rhythm to create texture
- Essential for phrase definition and formal articulation
- "One harmony per measure" = moderate harmonic rhythm
- "Chord per beat" = fast harmonic rhythm
- Affects listener perception of motion and stability
