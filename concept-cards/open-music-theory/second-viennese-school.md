# Second Viennese School

## Quick Definition

The Second Viennese School refers to the group of composers centered around Arnold Schoenberg in early 20th-century Vienna, including his students Anton Webern and Alban Berg, who developed and systematized twelve-tone composition as a method for organizing atonal music—named in relation to the "First Viennese School" of Haydn, Mozart, and Beethoven, and representing a foundational moment in the development of serial and post-tonal music that influenced generations of subsequent composers.

## Formal Definition

**Second Viennese School**: Compositional school centered on Schoenberg, Webern, and Berg.

**Core members**:
- **Arnold Schoenberg** (1874-1951): Developer of twelve-tone method
- **Anton Webern** (1883-1945): Student, known for concentrated forms
- **Alban Berg** (1885-1935): Student, known for expressive synthesis

**Historical phases**:
```
1. Free atonality (c. 1908-1921)
   - Abandonment of tonality
   - Expressionist works
   - No systematic pitch organization

2. Twelve-tone period (c. 1921-onward)
   - Schoenberg's "method of composing with twelve tones"
   - Systematic pitch organization
   - Row-based composition
```

**"First Viennese School"** (for comparison):
- Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven
- Classical period Vienna
- Tonal, common-practice

## Musical Context

The Second Viennese School represents:
- **Historical turning point**: End of common-practice tonality
- **Methodological innovation**: Twelve-tone technique
- **Diverse approaches**: Three composers, three distinct styles
- **Lasting influence**: Foundation of post-war serialism

**Each composer's character**:
- **Schoenberg**: Tradition-conscious, saw self as heir to Brahms
- **Webern**: Concentrated, symmetrical, pointillistic
- **Berg**: Expressive, tonal references, dramatic

**Cultural context**:
- Vienna as cultural capital
- Expressionism in arts
- World War I disruption
- Rise of fascism (forced emigration)

## Examples

### Basic

**Three approaches to twelve-tone**:
```
SCHOENBERG:
- Developed the method
- Often used tonal forms (waltzes, sonatas)
- Saw himself in German tradition
- "Method of composing with twelve tones related only to one another"

WEBERN:
- Maximum concentration
- Symmetrical rows
- Klangfarbenmelodie
- Brief, aphoristic works
- 31 opus numbers, often under 5 minutes each

BERG:
- Synthesis with tonality
- Rows with tonal implications
- Dramatic, expressive
- Opera central to output (Wozzeck, Lulu)
```

**Chronological development**:
```
1899: Schoenberg, Verklärte Nacht (late Romantic)
1909: Schoenberg, Three Piano Pieces Op. 11 (free atonal)
1912: Schoenberg, Pierrot Lunaire (expressionist)
1921: Schoenberg develops twelve-tone method
1923: Schoenberg, Five Piano Pieces Op. 23 (first twelve-tone)
1924: Schoenberg, Suite for Piano Op. 25 (fully twelve-tone)
1925: Berg, Wozzeck premiered (atonal opera)
1928: Webern, Symphonie Op. 21 (concentrated serialism)
1935: Berg dies; Violin Concerto (tonal-serial synthesis)
1945: Webern dies
1951: Schoenberg dies
```

### From Repertoire

**Schoenberg, development of method**:
```
Schoenberg's famous statement:
"Method of composing with twelve tones
 which are related only with one another"

Also (possibly apocryphal):
"I have discovered something which will ensure
 the supremacy of German music for the next hundred years"

Shows:
- Self-conscious innovation
- Tradition-consciousness
- Nationalist undertones
```

**Webern's characteristics**:
```
Symphonie Op. 21:
- 2 movements, about 10 minutes total
- Symmetrical row
- Double canon
- Klangfarbenmelodie
- Palindromic structures

Konzert Op. 24:
- Only 12 distinct row forms
- Four (014) trichords
- Maximum concentration
- Sator Square interest
```

**Berg's synthesis**:
```
Violin Concerto (1935):
Row: G-Bb-D-F#-A-C-E-G#-B-C#-Eb-F
     Contains triads and whole-tone segment

Structure:
- Fourths and fifths (tonal)
- Triads embedded in row
- Quotes Bach chorale
- Combines twelve-tone with tonal allusions

"To the memory of an angel" (Manon Gropius)
Deeply expressive despite serial technique
```

**Influence and legacy**:
```
Direct students/followers:
- Hanns Eisler
- Roberto Gerhard
- Egon Wellesz

Post-war serialists influenced:
- Pierre Boulez
- Karlheinz Stockhausen
- Milton Babbitt
- Luigi Nono

The Second Viennese School's methods
became foundation of post-war avant-garde
```

## Related Concepts

- **Prerequisite**: tonality, atonality
- **Leads to**: twelve-tone-row, serialism, total-serialism

## Common Confusions

- Second Viennese School = Schoenberg, Webern, Berg (and circle)
- "First Viennese School" = Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven
- Started with free atonality (c. 1908), then twelve-tone (c. 1921)
- Three very different compositional personalities
- Schoenberg developed the method; students applied differently
- Not all their music is twelve-tone (early works, some later works)
- Berg often maintained tonal references within serial technique
- Webern influenced post-war serialists most directly
- School disrupted by Nazi regime (emigration, deaths)
- Twelve-tone was meant to ORGANIZE atonality, not create it
- The composers saw themselves in German Romantic tradition
- Not a unified "school" in strict sense (diverse approaches)
- Term "Second Viennese School" applied retrospectively
