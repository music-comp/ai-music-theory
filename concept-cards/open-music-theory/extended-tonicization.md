# Quick Definition

Extended tonicization is the gray area between brief tonicization and full modulation: a passage longer than a single applied chord but shorter than a cadentially confirmed key change. It is analyzed by placing the Roman numeral of the temporary key under a bracket spanning the entire passage.

# Core Definition

**Extended tonicization** is the gray area between clear tonicization and clear modulation on the spectrum (OMT, Ch. IV.17). These are passages longer than two chords (longer than V6/5/V to V) but not long enough to lead to a cadence establishing true modulation. They are analyzed by temporarily analyzing in a new key, shown by placing the Roman numeral of the new key underneath a bracket spanning the entire extended tonicization.

Analysts typically recognize extended tonicization when:
1. They find themselves writing x/ii, y/ii, z/ii repeatedly in a short space
2. Roman numerals stop making functional sense in the home key, but the upcoming cadence is still in the home key

Extended tonicizations can themselves serve as pivot areas between keys.

# Prerequisites

- **Tonicization** — Extended tonicization is the longer version of tonicization
- **Modulation** — Extended tonicization falls short of modulation

# Key Properties

1. Longer than standard tonicization (more than just one applied chord)
2. Shorter/weaker than modulation (no cadential confirmation)
3. Analyzed with bracket notation and Roman numeral of temporary key
4. Gray area on the tonicization-modulation spectrum
5. Can serve as a pivot area between keys

# Construction / Recognition

## Signs of Extended Tonicization:
1. Multiple applied chords to the same target (x/ii, y/ii, z/ii)
2. Roman numerals stop making sense in home key
3. No cadence in the temporary key
4. The home key returns (either directly or via modulation elsewhere)

## To Analyze:
1. Place bracket under the extended tonicization
2. Write the Roman numeral of the temporary key below the bracket
3. Analyze within the bracket using that temporary key

# Examples

**Example 1** (OMT Ch. IV.17, Example 8): Extended tonicization in Joseph Bologne, String Quartet Op. 4, II. Beginning in Eb major, the passage tonicizes F minor (ii) with a bracket, serving as a pivot area transitioning to C minor.
