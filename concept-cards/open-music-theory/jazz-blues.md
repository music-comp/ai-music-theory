# Jazz Blues

## Quick Definition

A variant of the 12-bar blues that incorporates jazz harmonic elements, particularly ii-V progressions and turnarounds, blending blues and jazz languages.

## Formal Definition

The **jazz blues** modifies the standard 12-bar blues by adding jazz harmonic devices:

**Key modifications**:
1. **Bar 8**: Instead of remaining on I7, uses an applied ii-V targeting the ii chord in bar 9
2. **Bars 9-10**: Replaces V-IV with ii-V (replaces plagal motion with authentic motion)
3. **Bars 11-12**: Adds a turnaround for harmonic momentum to repeat

**Typical jazz blues structure** (in C):
| C7 | F7 | C7 | C7 |
| F7 | F7 | C7 | Gm7 - C7 |
| Dm7 | G7 | C7 | Dm7 - G7 ||

**What's preserved from blues**:
- Dominant seventh quality on I and IV
- IV chord in bars 5-6 and (sometimes) bar 2
- Overall 12-bar, 3-phrase structure

**What's added from jazz**:
- ii-V progressions
- Turnarounds
- Applied chords

## Musical Context

Jazz blues represents the merger of two distinct African American musical traditions. While blues emphasizes plagal cadences and dominant-seventh-as-tonic, jazz emphasizes ii-V-I progressions. The jazz blues form allows musicians to play "the blues" while using jazz harmonic vocabulary.

This hybrid form is extremely common in jazz repertoire, providing a framework that feels like blues while allowing jazz harmonic sophistication.

### From Repertoire

"C Jam Blues" (Duke Ellington): As performed by the Lincoln Center Jazz Orchestra, demonstrates jazz blues harmony, though not every repetition includes all written chords.

"Billie's Bounce" (Charlie Parker): A bebop classic using jazz blues changes.

"Blues for Alice" (Charlie Parker): An elaborate jazz blues with extensive ii-V substitutions.

## Common Confusions

- Jazz blues is still recognizably BLUES, despite added jazz elements
- Not every chord in the form is played every chorus; performers may simplify or elaborate
- The ii-V in bars 9-10 replaces, not just precedes, the V-IV motion
- "Jazz blues" and "blues in jazz" are often used interchangeably
- Many further variations exist (Bird Blues, etc.); this is a basic jazz blues
