# Quick Definition
A period in which each phrase (antecedent and consequent) is itself a complete sentence or other form, creating a larger-scale question-answer structure where the component phrases have their own internal formal organization.

# Core Definition
A **compound period** occurs when "one form contains another kind of form." Since the sentence is a single phrase and the period comprises two phrases, "it's possible for a period to be made of two sentences." The antecedent (a sentence) ends with a weaker cadence (HC), and the consequent (also a sentence) ends with a stronger cadence (PAC), maintaining the weak-to-strong cadence pattern that defines periods (Ch. 2, "Compound Phrase-Level Forms").

# Prerequisites
- **Period** -- The outer form is a period
- **Sentence** -- The inner form of each phrase is typically a sentence

# Key Properties
1. Period whose phrases each contain their own complete form (usually sentences)
2. Antecedent sentence ends with weaker cadence (HC)
3. Consequent sentence ends with stronger cadence (PAC)
4. Maintains all period properties (question-answer, weak→strong cadences)
5. Typically 16 measures (two 8-measure sentences)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Find a period (two phrases, weak→strong cadence pattern)
2. Check if each phrase has its own internal formal structure (presentation+continuation)
3. If both phrases are sentences: compound period

# Context & Application
Compound periods are common in Classical music, especially in minuets, theme-and-variation themes, and opening themes. They create a satisfying balance of local structure (within each sentence) and large-scale structure (the period's question-answer).

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 2, Example 13): Christian Petzold, Minuet in G Major -- 16-measure compound period where both antecedent and consequent are 8-measure sentences.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: A compound period is just a long period
  **Clarification**: "Compound" specifically means the component phrases contain their own complete forms
