# Quick Definition
A four-stage opening galant schema with melody do-ti-fa-mi over bass do-re-ti-do, moving from tonic through dominant and back, typically used as the first phrase of an opening theme.

# Core Definition
The **Meyer** schema is an opening gambit named after Leonard Meyer. It has four stages: melody do(1)-ti(7)-fa(4)-mi(3) over bass do(1)-re(2)-ti(7)-do(1), with figured bass 5/3-4/3-6/5-5/3 and Roman numerals I-V-V-I. It divides into an opening half (I to V) and a closing half (V to I). At one stage per measure, the Meyer commonly appears in the presentation or antecedent of an opening theme; at two stages per measure, it may form a basic idea followed by a closing gesture such as the Prinner (Ch. 11-12).

# Key Properties
1. Four stages: do-ti-fa-mi (melody), do-re-ti-do (bass)
2. Meter: W-S-W-S
3. Harmonic movement: I-V-V-I (tonic to dominant and back)
4. Divides into two halves (opening: I-V; closing: V-I)
5. Opening gambit position
6. Similar structure to Pastorella, Jupiter, Aprile schemas

# Construction / Recognition
## To Realize:
1. Place scale degrees in specific key: melody 1-7-4-3, bass 1-2-7-1
2. Harmonize with figured bass: 5/3-4/3-6/5-5/3
3. Embellish to create musical character
## To Identify:
1. Look for do-ti-fa-mi melodic pattern at opening of a piece
2. Check bass for do-re-ti-do pattern

# Context & Application
The Meyer is one of the most common opening schemas in Galant music. Its I-V-V-I progression establishes the tonic quickly and creates a balanced question-answer structure within a single phrase.
