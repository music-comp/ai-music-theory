# Meyer Schema

## Quick Definition

A four-stage opening galant schema that moves from tonic to dominant and back to tonic (I-V-V-I), with a characteristic melodic pattern of do-ti-fa-mi (^1-^7-^4-^3) in the upper voice, commonly used to begin themes in 18th-century galant style music.

## Formal Definition

The **Meyer** (named after music theorist Leonard Meyer) is a prototypical opening schema in galant music, characterized by its four-stage structure, specific scale-degree patterns, and tonic-dominant-tonic harmonic trajectory.

**Four-stage structure**:

| Stage | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|-------|---|---|---|---|
| Meter | W | S | W | S |
| Melody | do (^1) | ti (^7) | fa (^4) | mi (^3) |
| Bass | do (^1) | re (^2) | ti (^7) | do (^1) |
| Figures | 5/3 | 6/4/3 | 6/5 | 5/3 |
| Roman numerals | I | V | V | I |

**Two-part division**:

Opening part (question):
| Stage | 1 | 2 |
|-------|---|---|
| Meter | W | S |
| Melody | do (^1) | ti (^7) |
| Bass | do (^1) | re (^2) |
| Figures | 5/3 | 6/4/3 |
| Roman numerals | I | V |

Closing part (answer):
| Stage | 1 | 2 |
|-------|---|---|
| Meter | W | S |
| Melody | fa (^4) | mi (^3) |
| Bass | ti (^7) | do (^1) |
| Figures | 6/5 | 5/3 |
| Roman numerals | V | I |

**Characteristics**:
- **Function**: Opening gambit (begins themes/phrases)
- **Harmonic trajectory**: I → V → V → I (tonic frame with dominant middle)
- **Melodic pattern**: ^1-^7-^4-^3 (soprano/melody)
- **Bass pattern**: ^1-^2-^7-^1
- **Metric placement**: Typically weak-strong-weak-strong (W-S-W-S)
- **Question-answer structure**: First half (I-V) poses question, second half (V-I) answers

**Timing variants**:
- **One stage per measure** (4 measures total): Common in presentation or antecedent phrases
- **Two stages per measure** (2 measures total): Forms a basic idea followed by closing gesture (often Prinner)
- Stage duration flexible depending on context

**Related schemas**:
Similar opening schemas include:
- **Pastorella**: Different melodic pattern (^3-^2-^4-^3) but similar I-V-V-I trajectory
- **Jupiter**: Different melodic contour but related harmonic structure
- **Aprile**: Similar approach with metric variations

All share:
- Start on tonic
- Move away for two stages
- Return to tonic at end
- Suitable for opening themes

## Musical Context

The Meyer schema serves specific functions in galant music:
- **Opening formula**: Establishes key and metric structure
- **Thematic material**: Provides melodic/harmonic framework for development
- **Pedagogical model**: Teaches I-V-I progression with characteristic voice leading
- **Improvisation basis**: 18th-century musicians learned schemas for improvisation
- **Partimento tradition**: Bass pattern used as teaching tool

The schema's balance of stability (tonic frame) and motion (dominant middle) makes it ideal for opening gestures.

## Examples

### Basic

**Meyer in C major** (one stage per measure):
```
M1 (W):  Soprano C5, Bass C4 (I, 5/3)
M2 (S):  Soprano B4, Bass D4 (V 6/4/3)
M3 (W):  Soprano F4, Bass B3 (V 6/5)
M4 (S):  Soprano E4, Bass C4 (I, 5/3)

Melodic pattern: C-B-F-E (do-ti-fa-mi)
Harmonic: I - V - V - I
```

**Meyer compressed** (two stages per measure):
```
M1: [C5, C4 (I)] - [B4, D4 (V)]
M2: [F4, B3 (V)] - [E4, C4 (I)]

Same melodic/harmonic pattern, faster presentation
Often followed by Prinner or other continuation schema
```

**Meyer opening** (question):
```
M1-2: do-ti over ^1-^2 bass (I-V)
Sets up expectation for continuation
```

**Meyer closing** (answer to opening):
```
M3-4: fa-mi over ^7-^1 bass (V-I)
Resolves the I-V tension created by opening
```

### From Repertoire

**Mozart Piano Sonata K. 545, I, mm. 1-4**: Opening theme uses Meyer-like pattern with characteristic ^1-^7-^4-^3 melodic contour and I-V-V-I progression.

**Haydn Symphony No. 104 "London," I**: Uses Meyer pattern in opening theme, demonstrating typical galant opening gambit.

**Mozart Piano Sonata K. 333, I, mm. 1-4**: Meyer schema with elegant embellishments, showing how composers elaborated basic prototypes.

**Galant keyboard sonatas** (Rutini, Galuppi, C.P.E. Bach): Frequently employ Meyer as opening formula, often followed by Prinner continuation.

Many Classical-era themes build on Meyer framework, though with personal variations and embellishments that go beyond the bare prototype.

## Common Confusions

- Meyer ≠ exact recipe—it's a prototype (composers vary it)
- Not every I-V-V-I progression is a Meyer (melodic pattern matters)
- Bass pattern (^1-^2-^7-^1) less fixed than melody
- Meyer can appear with predominant harmony at stage 2 (IV or ii instead of V)
- Stage duration varies (one stage = one measure OR two stages = one measure)
- Meyer vs. Pastorella/Jupiter: Different melodic patterns, similar harmonic trajectory
- "Opening gambit" doesn't mean only at piece's start—can begin sections/themes
- Schema ≠ exact musical quotation—framework for composition/improvisation
- Not limited to major mode (works in minor with adjustments)
- Don't confuse with "Meyer chord" or other unrelated terms
