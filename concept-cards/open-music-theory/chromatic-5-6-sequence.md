# Chromatic 5-6 Sequence

## Quick Definition

A chromatic sequence derived from the diatonic 5-6 pattern in which the interval between successive chord roots is made consistent (both size and quality), with secondary dominant chords creating an ascending or descending pattern that moves by major seconds on each strong beat, outlining the whole-tone scale and enabling modulation by half step in either direction.

## Formal Definition

**Chromatic 5-6 sequences** come in two forms:

**Ascending chromatic 5-6**:
```
Model: Major triad → dominant seventh (minor third down)
       Dominant seventh resolves up by perfect fourth to next major triad

Diatonic: C - Am7 - Dm - Bm7b5 - Em ...
          (root motion: m3 down, P4 up, m3 down, P4 up)

Chromatic: C - A7 - D - B7 - E - C#7 - F# ...
           All root motions consistent: M3 down, P4 up
           Strong beats: C - D - E - F# (whole-tone pattern)
```

**Descending chromatic 5-6** (Pachelbel variant):
```
Model: Major triad → chord (down by fourth)
       Second chord to next triad (down by minor third)

Diatonic (Pachelbel): I - V - vi - iii - IV - I ...
Chromatic: D - A/C# - F#m - C#/E# - Bbm - F/A - Dm ...
           Root motion: P4 down, m3 down (consistent)
           Strong beats: D - F# - Bb - Dm (or whole-tone)
```

## Voice Leading

**Ascending 5-6 chromatic sequence**:
```
C:    C-E-G
      ↓ ↓ ↓
A7:   A-C#-E-G  (V7/D)
      ↓  ↓  ↓ ↓
D:    D-F#-A-D
      ↓ ↓  ↓ ↓
B7:   B-D#-F#-A (V7/E)
      ↓  ↓  ↓  ↓
E:    E-G#-B-E
      ...

Each dominant seventh resolves to major triad P4 up
Each major triad followed by secondary dominant
Strong-beat roots: C - D - E - F# - G# - Bb - C (whole tones)
```

**Descending 5-6 chromatic sequence**:
```
Creates a bass line that descends chromatically
Inverted chords on weak beats facilitate this
Strong-beat roots outline whole-tone scale descending
D - C - Bb - Ab - Gb - E - D
```

## Harmonic Consequences

**Whole-tone division of the octave**:
- Strong beats outline whole-tone scale
- Completes cycle after six steps
- Creates harmonic ambiguity similar to whole-tone harmony
- Tonic sense suspended during sequence

**Modulation by half step**:
- Ascending version modulates DOWN by half step overall
- E.g., sequence in Bb → arrives at A major (down m2)
- Descending version modulates similarly
- Powerful tool for distant modulation

## Musical Context

The chromatic 5-6 sequences serve specific purposes:
- **Distant modulation**: Reach keys a half-step away
- **Harmonic ambiguity**: Suspend tonal center temporarily
- **Sequential development**: Extend melodic-harmonic patterns chromatically
- **Whole-tone color**: Creates distinctive "impressionistic" sound
- **Romantic harmony**: Common in 19th-century repertoire
- **Film music**: Creates sense of continuous harmonic motion

## Examples

### Basic

**Ascending chromatic 5-6 (starting in Bb)**:
```
Bb - G7 - C - A7 - D - B7 - E → resolves as V to A major

Bb → G7:  (down M3, G7 = V7/C)
G7 → C:   (up P4, resolution)
C → A7:   (down M3, A7 = V7/D)
A7 → D:   (up P4, resolution)
D → B7:   (down M3, B7 = V7/E)
B7 → E:   (up P4, resolution)
E → as V in A

Strong beats: Bb - C - D - E (whole-tone ascent)
Result: Modulated from Bb major to A major (down half step!)
```

**Descending chromatic 5-6 (Pachelbel-derived)**:
```
D:    D-F#-A      (I)
      ↓
A/C#: C#-E-A     (V6, or passing)
      ↓
F#m:  F#-A-C#   (chromatic alteration from original vi)
      ↓
C#/E#: E#-G#-C# (another passing chord)
      ...

With full chromatic treatment:
Bass descends chromatically: D - C# - C - B - Bb - A - Ab - G - F# - F - E - Eb - D

Creates smooth chromatic bass over descending sequence
Strong-beat harmonies outline whole-tone scale
```

### From Repertoire

**Mozart, Piano Sonatas**: Uses chromatic 5-6 sequences in development sections for modulation.

**Beethoven, Piano Sonatas**: Chromatic sequences with secondary dominants leading to distant keys.

**Schubert, Lieder**: Chromatic 5-6 patterns for text-painting and harmonic wandering.

**Brahms, chamber music**: Extended chromatic sequences creating harmonic complexity.

## Common Confusions

- Two types: ascending and descending chromatic 5-6
- "5-6" refers to the figured bass pattern (intervallic), not scale degrees
- Chromatic version has CONSISTENT root-motion intervals (both size and quality)
- Diatonic version has varying interval qualities to stay in key
- Strong beats outline the whole-tone scale (M2 apart)
- Ascending type: M3 down, P4 up pattern; modulates DOWN by half step
- Descending type: P4 down, m3 down; can create chromatic bass line
- Secondary dominants (V7/X) create the chromatic alterations
- Whole-tone ambiguity is a feature, not a bug
- Powerful modulation tool to distant keys
- Sequence can be exited at any point to establish new key
