# Descending-Fifths Chromatic Sequence

## Quick Definition

A chromatic sequence based on the descending-fifths pattern in which every chord is a dominant seventh resolving to another dominant seventh a perfect fifth below (or perfect fourth above), creating a chain of interlocking secondary dominants that outlines the whole-tone scale on successive strong beats and generates strong harmonic ambiguity through equal division of the octave.

## Formal Definition

**Descending-fifths chromatic sequence** transforms the diatonic pattern:

**Diatonic version**:
- Root progression descends by fifths within a key
- Interval quality varies (P5, A4, P4) to stay diatonic
- Example in G: G-C-F#-B-E-A-D-G

**Chromatic version**:
- Every chord is a dominant seventh (Mm7)
- Each chord acts as V7 of the chord that follows
- Root progression maintains **consistent perfect fifths**
- Strong-beat chord roots outline the **whole-tone scale**

**Pattern structure**:
```
Model: Dominant seventh → dominant seventh (fifth lower)
Each copy: Same pattern, consistent P5 root motion
Result: Interlocking chain of V7 chords
```

## Voice Leading

**Elided resolution in the sequence**:
```
G7 → C7 → F7 → Bb7 → Eb7 → Ab7 → Db7 → ...

Voice exchange at each step:
- Leading tone resolves DOWN → becomes new seventh
- Seventh resolves DOWN → becomes new leading tone

Cannot resolve both tritone members normally
Elision creates smooth voice-leading
```

**Whole-tone pattern on strong beats**:
```
Strong beats: G - F - Eb - Db - Cb(B) - Ab(Bbb) - ...
              (whole tone - whole tone - whole tone...)

Divides the octave into major seconds
Creates tonal ambiguity
```

## Harmonic Function

**Tonal implications**:
- Roman numeral analysis becomes tenuous
- Each chord could be "V7 of" the next
- No clear tonic established
- Creates a sense of floating tonality

**Modulation potential**:
- Can exit the sequence at any point
- Resolve any chord as a true dominant
- Facilitates modulation to distant keys

## Musical Context

The descending-fifths chromatic sequence serves multiple purposes:
- **Harmonic exploration**: Traverses chromatic space systematically
- **Distant modulation**: Reach remote keys smoothly
- **Dramatic development**: Build tension through extended chromaticism
- **Jazz foundation**: Basis for jazz ii-V chains and cycle of fifths patterns
- **Baroque sequences**: Chromatic variations on standard progressions
- **Romantic harmony**: Extended tonal ambiguity in 19th-century music

## Examples

### Basic

**Complete chromatic descending-fifths sequence**:
```
Starting from G7:

G7  →  C7  →  F7  →  Bb7  →  Eb7  →  Ab7  →  Db7  →  Gb7  →  Cb7  →  Fb7  →  Bbb7  →  Ebb7  →  (G7)
↓      ↓      ↓       ↓        ↓        ↓        ↓        ↓        ↓        ↓         ↓         ↓
P5     P5     P5      P5       P5       P5       P5       P5       P5       P5        P5        P5

All perfect fifths (or perfect fourths up)
Completes cycle through all 12 keys
```

**Strong-beat whole-tone pattern**:
```
Downbeats: G - F - Eb - Db - Cb - Bbb - ...
           (enharmonically: G - F - Eb - Db - B - A - G)

Whole-tone scale: G - F - Eb - Db - B - A - (G)
Divides octave into six equal parts
```

**Voice leading detail**:
```
G7:  G-B-D-F     (G dominant seventh)
     ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓
C7:  C-E-G-Bb    

B → Bb  (leading tone of G7 → seventh of C7)
F → E   (seventh of G7 → leading tone of C7)

This exchange continues through entire sequence
```

### From Repertoire

**Bach, Chromatic Fantasia and Fugue**: Extended chromatic sequences using interlocking dominant sevenths.

**Mozart and Beethoven development sections**: Chromatic sequences for harmonic exploration and modulation.

**Jazz standards**: "Tune Up," "How High the Moon" - extended dominant chains derived from this pattern.

**Romantic opera**: Wagner and Verdi use chromatic sequences for continuous harmonic motion.

## Common Confusions

- Different from diatonic descending-fifths sequence (quality varies in diatonic)
- In chromatic version: ALL intervals are perfect fifths (consistent quality)
- All chords are dominant sevenths (same chord quality throughout)
- Each chord functions as V7 of the next (interlocking secondary dominants)
- Strong beats outline whole-tone scale (equal division of octave)
- Uses elided resolutions (leading tone ↔ seventh exchange)
- Creates tonal ambiguity (no clear tonic)
- Roman numerals become impractical; lead-sheet symbols preferred
- Can exit sequence at any point to establish a key
- Foundation for jazz ii-V chains and cycle progressions
- Completes full cycle through all 12 keys (if continued)
