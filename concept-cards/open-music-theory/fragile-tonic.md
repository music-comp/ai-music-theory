# Quick Definition
Three techniques de-emphasize the tonic chord to create tonal ambiguity (per Mark Spicer, 2017). Fragile tonic: tonic exists but is weakened through inversion or other techniques (associated with vulnerability lyrics). Emergent tonic: tonic is withheld until a triumphant moment like the chorus (associated with overcoming/triumph). Absent tonic: tonic is implied melodically and by familiar progressions but never stated harmonically (most extreme).

# Core Definition
Fragile tonic: the tonic chord appears but is undermined — typically through inversion (first inversion instead of root position) or brief, unaccented appearances. Sufjan Stevens, "Oh God Where Are You Now": Gb tonic mostly in first inversion; root position appears only as incomplete passing chord. Emergent tonic: tonic is withheld through verses (which may emphasize vi or other chords) and emerges triumphantly at the chorus. "Let It Go" (Frozen): F aeolian verses, Eb mixolydian prechoruses, Ab major chorus where tonic finally emerges — paralleling Elsa's character arc. "Little Red Corvette" (Prince): Bb minor verses, Db emerges as tonic in chorus. Absent tonic: the tonic pitch is outlined in melody (descending ^3-^2-^1) and implied by familiar progressions (IV-ii-vi-V) but never sounds as a chord. "Last Friday Night" (Katy Perry): F# major implied but never stated. All three techniques can parallel lyrical themes: vulnerability (fragile), triumph (emergent), disconnect (absent).

# Key Properties
1. Fragile: tonic exists but weakened (inversion, brief appearances)
2. Emergent: tonic withheld until climactic moment (chorus)
3. Absent: tonic implied by melody/conventions but never stated harmonically
4. All create tonal ambiguity through de-emphasizing tonic chord
5. Often parallel lyrical themes (vulnerability, triumph, disconnect)
6. Based on Mark Spicer's research (2017)

# Context & Application
These techniques work partly because pop progressions use few chords in root position, making tonic ambiguity possible with simple means. The singer/songwriter schema is particularly prone to tonal ambiguity due to its lack of authentic cadence. Fragile tonics connect to piano-based composition; emergent tonics are especially effective in verse-chorus form.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Fragile): Sufjan Stevens, "Oh God Where Are You Now" — Gb tonic mostly in first inversion.
**Example 2** (Emergent): "Let It Go" — Ab major tonic withheld through verse/prechorus, emerges at chorus.
**Example 3** (Absent): Katy Perry, "Last Friday Night" — F# major outlined melodically (^3-^2-^1) but never sounded as chord.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: These songs have no tonic at all.
  **Clarification**: All three have a tonic — it is just weakened (fragile), delayed (emergent), or implied but unstated (absent). The tonic is still perceptible through melody and harmonic conventions.
