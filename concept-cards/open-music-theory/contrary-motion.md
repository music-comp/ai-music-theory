# Quick Definition
Contrary motion occurs when two parts move in opposite directions -- one ascending while the other descends -- and is the preferred type of motion in species counterpoint for promoting melodic independence and variety.

# Core Definition
**Contrary motion** is one of four types of two-part motion in species counterpoint, defined as occurring when "the two parts move in opposite directions (one up, the other down)" (Ch. 1, "Types of motion"). The source identifies it as "best for variety and preserving the independence of the lines, so it should be preferred where possible" (Ch. 2).

# Prerequisites
- **Interval** -- Understanding intervals is needed to analyze motion between voices

# Key Properties
1. Two voices move in opposite directions
2. Most preferred motion type in species counterpoint
3. Best preserves melodic independence of each line
4. Required for clausula vera (final cadential approach)
5. Best promotes variety in the exercise

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. When one voice moves up, move the other voice down (or vice versa)
## To Recognize:
1. Check if one voice ascends while the other descends between two successive intervals

# Context & Application
Contrary motion is fundamental to good counterpoint because it maximizes independence of the two voices. When voices move in opposite directions, listeners can more easily perceive them as separate melodic entities. The clausula vera requires contrary stepwise motion. In sixteenth-century contrapuntal style, contrary motion also predominates between parts.

## Contrasts With
- **parallel-motion** -- Both voices move same direction by same interval
- **similar-motion** -- Both voices move same direction by different intervals

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Moving into a perfect consonance by similar motion instead of contrary
  **Correction**: Always approach perfect consonances by contrary motion when possible

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Contrary motion and oblique motion are the same
  **Clarification**: In contrary motion both voices move (in opposite directions); in oblique motion one voice stays still
