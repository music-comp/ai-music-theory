# Quick Definition
Pandiatonicism uses the diatonic collection (e.g., the white notes) without making any specific pitch sound like a tonal center -- it is neither tonal nor modal, but simply diatonic. Stravinsky's ballet Petrushka contains characteristic pandiatonic passages.

# Core Definition
Pandiatonic music uses the diatonic collection freely, without establishing a tonic, dominant, or modal final. The pitches are diatonic, but no hierarchy among them is created. This distinguishes pandiatonicism from both tonal music (which establishes a key) and modal music (which establishes a final).

# Prerequisites

# Key Properties
1. Uses diatonic collection
2. No specific pitch center
3. Not tonal (no key) and not modal (no final)
4. Associated with Stravinsky and neoclassicism

# Context & Application
Pandiatonicism represents one 20th-century approach to the diatonic collection, treated as a pitch resource without hierarchical organization.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Stravinsky, Petrushka): Opening passages use diatonic pitches without establishing a tonal center.
