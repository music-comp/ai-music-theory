# Quick Definition

A scale whose third note is a half step lower than the corresponding major scale, existing in three forms: natural, harmonic, and melodic minor.

# Core Definition

A **minor scale's** third note is always a half step lower than the third note of the major scale with the same name. Three variations exist: **natural minor** (W-H-W-W-H-W-W), **harmonic minor** (W-H-W-W-H-3Hs-H, where 3Hs = three half steps), and **melodic minor** (ascending: W-H-W-W-W-W-H; descending: same as natural minor). These are like "flavors" -- a work is simply "in minor," not "in" a specific type. Minor key signatures are always based on the natural minor form. Natural minor has 3 lowered degrees (^3, ^6, ^7); harmonic has 2 (^3, ^6); melodic ascending has 1 (^3).

# Key Properties

- Natural minor: W-H-W-W-H-W-W (same ascending and descending)
- Harmonic minor: W-H-W-W-H-3Hs-H (same ascending and descending; raised ^7)
- Melodic minor: W-H-W-W-W-W-H ascending; W-W-H-W-W-H-W descending (= natural minor)
- Minor solfege: me (lowered-^3, "may"), le (lowered-^6, "lay"), te (lowered-^7, "tay")
- Leading tone (ti, ^7) creates closure absent in natural minor
- Subtonic: lowered-^7 (te, a whole step below tonic)
- Key signatures based on natural minor form

# Construction / Recognition

Start with the parallel major scale and lower ^3. For natural minor, also lower ^6 and ^7. For harmonic minor, lower only ^3 and ^6. For melodic minor ascending, lower only ^3.

# Context & Application

Minor keys are central to Western music. The three forms reflect common melodic and harmonic patterns: natural minor for descending lines, harmonic minor for dominant-tonic motion (raised leading tone), and melodic minor for smooth ascending lines.

# Examples

- C natural minor: C-D-Eb-F-G-Ab-Bb-C
- C harmonic minor: C-D-Eb-F-G-Ab-B-C
- C melodic minor: C-D-Eb-F-G-A-B-C (ascending); C-Bb-Ab-G-F-Eb-D-C (descending)

# Common Errors

- Forgetting that melodic minor has different ascending and descending forms
- Using harmonic minor's raised ^7 in contexts where natural ^7 is appropriate

# Common Confusions

- A piece is "in minor," not "in natural/harmonic/melodic minor" -- the three types describe scale patterns, not keys
- The relative minor of a major key is 3 half steps below the major key's tonic
