## Quick Definition

The chord progression IV-V-vi-I (or VI-VII-i-III in minor), common since 2010, named for its "step-step-skip" root motion pattern.

## Formal Definition

The hopscotch schema is a four-chord cyclical progression: IV-V-vi-I in major or VI-VII-i-III in minor. The name derives from its distinctive root motion pattern: step up (IV to V), step up (V to vi), skip down (vi to I). Like the singer/songwriter schema, it exhibits tonal ambiguity between the relative major and minor interpretations.

## Musical Context

The hopscotch schema has become especially common in pop music since approximately 2010. Its approach to the major tonic by skip (rather than by step or by fifth) distinguishes it from other four-chord schemas.

### Common Variation

Replacing VII with V in minor: VI-V-i-III
- Changes the chord quality (V uses ^7/ti; VII uses flat^7/te)
- Often keeps bass motion as step-step-skip by inverting the V chord
- Encourages a minor-mode interpretation

## Examples

### Basic Example

In C major: F-G-Am-C (IV-V-vi-I)
In A minor: F-G-Am-C (VI-VII-i-III)
Root motion: F(step)G(step)A(skip)C

## Common Confusions

- **Hopscotch vs. singer/songwriter**: Both are tonally ambiguous, but hopscotch approaches the major tonic by skip; singer/songwriter approaches by step (from IV)
- **Identifying by ear**: Listen to how the major tonic is approached - skip motion indicates hopscotch
- **VII vs. V substitution**: VI-V-i-III is a hopscotch variant, not a different schema; the substitution changes color but maintains function
