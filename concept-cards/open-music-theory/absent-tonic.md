## Quick Definition

A technique where the tonic chord never sounds in the accompaniment but is established through melody and familiar chord progressions that strongly imply its presence.

## Formal Definition

An absent tonic is a tonic that is implied through melodic contour and conventional harmonic progressions but never actually appears as a sounding chord in the accompaniment. The tonic is established through:
- Melodic emphasis on tonic pitch and related scale degrees
- Familiar chord progressions that normally include tonic
- Listener expectation based on stylistic conventions

This represents the most extreme manipulation of tonic presence, as the harmonic center exists only by implication.

## Musical Context

Absent tonics create a sense of perpetual anticipation or disconnection. The technique can reflect lyrical themes of absence, searching, incompleteness, or disconnection between different aspects of life.

### How Tonic Is Implied

- **Melodic outline**: Melody strongly emphasizes ^1, ^3, ^5 and descending ^3-^2-^1 patterns
- **Familiar progressions**: IV-ii-vi-V strongly implies I even without stating it
- **Harmonic function**: Subdominant and dominant chords create expectation for tonic resolution

### Lyrical Associations

- Disconnection
- Searching
- Perpetual motion without arrival
- Gap between desire and reality

## Examples

### Basic Example

Progression: F - Dm - Am - G (IV-ii-vi-V in C)
Melody: E-D-C (mi-re-do) over the progression
Result: C major is clearly implied but never sounds as a chord

### From Repertoire

- "Last Friday Night" by Katy Perry (2012):
  - Chord progression: IV-ii-vi-V (in F# major)
  - Tonic F# never sounds in the accompaniment
  - Melody outlines F# major clearly, with repeated ^3-^2-^1 descents
  - Familiar progression + melodic emphasis = implied tonic
  - Lyrical interpretation: disconnect between constant partying and real-world functioning

## Common Confusions

- **Absent vs. fragile tonic**: Absent tonic never sounds at all; fragile tonic sounds but is weakened
- **Absent vs. ambiguous tonic**: Absent tonic is clear but not stated; ambiguous tonic is unclear which chord is tonic
- **Melody's role**: In absent tonic songs, the melody does most of the work establishing tonality, reversing the typical melody-follows-harmony relationship
