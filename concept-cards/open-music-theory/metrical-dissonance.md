# Quick Definition
Metrical dissonance occurs when two or more different ways of hearing the meter are present simultaneously. There are two main types: displacement dissonance (same meter displaced in phase) and grouping dissonance (different grouping patterns, such as 3-against-4).

# Core Definition
The modern literature on metrical dissonance was galvanized by Krebs (1999), who cites Berlioz (1837) as the earliest explicit comparison. Displacement dissonance places two forms of the same meter against each other with different phase (e.g., the strong beat shifted by a quarter note). Grouping dissonance combines two meters with different internal organization (e.g., 6/8 against 3/4, or 3-against-4 patterns). The 3-against-4 pattern is probably the most ubiquitous form, originating in the Afro-Cuban tresillo rhythm. Metrical dissonance can change throughout a piece, creating tension-release arcs (as in Brahms's Lullaby, where the piano accompaniment progresses from double dissonance to no dissonance).

# Prerequisites
- Meter (understanding of beat, division, grouping)

# Key Properties
1. Two main types: displacement and grouping
2. Displacement: same period, different phase
3. Grouping: different meters simultaneously
4. 3-against-4 is most common grouping dissonance
5. Can create tension-release arcs within a piece
6. Originated with Krebs (1999), roots in Berlioz (1837)

# Context & Application
Metrical dissonance is a powerful tool for rhythmic complexity in all genres, from classical (Brahms, Beethoven) to popular music (Bill Withers, Calvin Harris). It can be analyzed systematically using Krebs's framework.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Bill Withers, "Lean on Me"): Displacement dissonance -- bass on beat 3, handclaps on off-beat eighth notes, drums on beats 2 and 4.
**Example 2** (Calvin Harris, "I'm Not Alone"): Grouping dissonance -- riff based on 3-grouping against 4/4 meter.
**Example 3** (Brahms, "Wiegenlied"): Piano accompaniment progresses from combined displacement + grouping dissonance to no dissonance, matching the lullaby's soothing function.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Metrical dissonance is always "bad" or disruptive
  **Clarification**: Like pitch dissonance, it is a musical resource that creates richness and interest
- **Confusion**: Syncopation and metrical dissonance are the same
  **Clarification**: Syncopation can be a form of displacement dissonance, but not all syncopation constitutes metrical dissonance
