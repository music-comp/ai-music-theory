# Quick Definition
The third species exercise, where the counterpoint moves in quarter notes against a whole-note cantus firmus (4:1 ratio), introducing neighbor-tone dissonance, double neighbors, and the nota cambiata, with a three-level metric hierarchy.

# Core Definition
In **third-species counterpoint**, the counterpoint line moves in quarter notes against a cantus firmus in whole notes, creating a 4:1 rhythmic ratio. This introduces a three-level metric hierarchy: strong beats (downbeats), moderately strong beats (beat 3), and weak beats (beats 2 and 4). New dissonance types include the **dissonant neighbor tone** (step away and back to a consonant tone), the **double neighbor** (both upper and lower neighbor tones together, e.g., C-D-B-C), and the **nota cambiata** -- a special five-note figure that uniquely includes a leap from a dissonance (Ch. 4).

# Key Properties
1. Counterpoint in quarter notes against whole-note cantus (4:1)
2. Three metric levels: strong (beat 1), medium (beat 3), weak (beats 2, 4)
3. Downbeats always consonant
4. Dissonance types: passing tone, neighbor tone, double neighbor, nota cambiata
5. Two consecutive dissonant passing tones allowed (P4-d5 or d5-P4)
6. Nota cambiata: five-note figure outlining a step, with leap from dissonance
7. Double neighbor: beats 1 and 4 same pitch, beats 2-3 are upper and lower neighbors
8. Leaps preferred within bars rather than across bar lines
9. If downbeat is P5, neither beat 3 nor 4 of previous bar can be a 5th
10. If downbeat is P8, neither beat 2, 3, nor 4 of previous bar can be an 8ve

# Construction / Recognition
## To Write Third Species:
1. Choose downbeat notes following first-species harmonic rules
2. Fill beats 2-4 with consonant and dissonant patterns
3. Use passing tones, neighbor tones, double neighbors as appropriate
4. Ensure all dissonances are preceded and followed by step (except nota cambiata)
5. End with clausula vera; final note is whole note on do
## To Identify Dissonance Types:
1. Passing tone: fills a third by step in one direction
2. Neighbor tone: steps away from and back to a consonant tone
3. Double neighbor: upper and lower neighbors together (C-D-B-C or C-B-D-C)
4. Nota cambiata: five-note figure, step down from consonance, leap to consonance, step-step

# Context & Application
Third species allows for more elaborate melodic patterns and introduces the neighbor-tone dissonance, which ornaments a consonant tone rather than connecting two different consonant tones. The nota cambiata is notable as the only figure in species counterpoint where a leap from a dissonance is permitted.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Leaping into or out of a dissonant neighbor tone
  **Correction**: Neighbor tones must be approached and left by step
- **Error**: Having parallel 5ths or 8ves between a weak beat and the following downbeat
  **Correction**: Motion from beat 4 to downbeat follows first-species rules

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Double neighbor is just two neighbor tones in sequence
  **Clarification**: The double neighbor is a specific four-note pattern where beats 1 and 4 are the same pitch, with both upper and lower neighbors in between
