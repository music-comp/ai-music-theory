# Circle of Fifths Progression

## Quick Definition

A harmonic progression where chord roots descend by perfect fifth (or ascend by perfect fourth)—following the circle of fifths backward through I-IV-vii°-iii-vi-ii-V-I, creating strong root motion with each chord functioning as a temporary dominant to the next, exploiting the powerful V-I relationship at every step, commonly appearing in complete or partial forms (especially the ii-V-I cadential segment), and serving as one of the most fundamental and ubiquitous chord progressions in tonal music from Baroque through jazz, valued for its forward momentum and smooth voice leading through descending fifths.

## Formal Definition

**Circle of fifths progression** is a harmonic progression featuring root motion by descending perfect fifth (P5↓) or ascending perfect fourth (P4↑).

**Complete sequence** (in C major):
```
I - IV - vii° - iii - vi - ii - V - I
C    F     B°     E    A   D   G   C

Each root descends by P5 (or ascends by P4)
```

**Key characteristics**:
- **Root motion**: Descending fifth (ascending fourth)
- **Strong progression**: Each chord acts like V to next chord
- **Voice leading**: Smooth, efficient connections
- **Ubiquitous**: Appears in all tonal styles
- **Partial sequences**: Often only 2-4 chords used

**Common segments**:
- **ii-V-I**: Most common (final three chords)
- **vi-ii-V-I**: Expanded cadential approach
- **iii-vi-ii-V-I**: Longer sequence

## Root Motion by Fifths

**Descending fifths** (ascending fourths):
```
C → F:  Down P5 (or up P4)
F → B:  Down P5
B → E:  Down P5
E → A:  Down P5
A → D:  Down P5
D → G:  Down P5
G → C:  Down P5 (return to tonic)

Complete circle returns to starting point
```

**Why strong?**:
- Each chord relates to next as **V relates to I**
- Exploits strongest root motion in tonal music
- Creates forward drive and momentum
- Inevitable pull toward resolution

## Common Segments

**ii-V-I** (most common):
```
ii  -  V  -  I
Dm    G    C   (in C major)

Standard cadential formula
Last three chords of circle
Universal in tonal music
```

**vi-ii-V-I**:
```
vi  -  ii  -  V  -  I
Am    Dm    G    C

Extended approach to cadence
Four chords, strong progression
Common in Classical and jazz
```

**IV-vii°-iii** (less common):
```
IV  -  vii°  -  iii
F      B°      Em

Middle portion of circle
Unusual but valid
```

**I-IV-vii°-iii-vi-ii-V-I** (complete):
```
Complete circle
All diatonic chords
Rare to use full sequence
```

## Voice Leading

**Smooth connections**:
```
    ii       V        I
    D-F-A    G-B-D    C-E-G

Common tones:
- D shared between ii and V
- G shared between V and I (becomes bass)

Stepwise motion:
- F → G → E  (stepwise or leap)
- A → B → C  (stepwise)

Efficient voice leading
```

**Root motion in bass**:
```
Bass: D → G → C
      ↓P5  ↓P5

Strong bass motion
Descending fifths
```

## Seventh Chords in Circle

**With seventh chords** (smoother):
```
ii⁷  -  V⁷  -  Imaj⁷
Dm⁷   G⁷    Cmaj⁷

Sevenths add forward drive
Especially common in jazz
```

**Voice leading** (with sevenths):
```
    ii⁷       V⁷       Imaj⁷
    D-F-A-C   G-B-D-F  C-E-G-B

Common tones:
- D (ii⁷) → D (V⁷) common tone
- G (Imaj⁷) in V⁷ bass

Seventh resolves:
- C (in ii⁷) → B (in V⁷) step down
- F (in V⁷) → E (in Imaj⁷) step down

Extremely smooth voice leading
```

## Circle Progression in Minor

**In minor keys**:
```
i - iv - VII - III - VI - ii° - V - i
Am   Dm   G     C    F    B°    E   Am

Adjusted for minor key harmony
ii° is diminished
VII and III major (common in minor)
```

**Still strong root motion** by descending fifths

## Musical Context

Circle of fifths progression serves critical purposes:
- **Strong root motion**: Most powerful bass progression
- **Forward momentum**: Drives toward cadence
- **Smooth voice leading**: Common tones and stepwise motion
- **Functional clarity**: Each chord pulls to next
- **Universal device**: Baroque, Classical, Romantic, jazz, popular
- **Cadential standard**: ii-V-I ubiquitous
- **Expandable**: Can use 2, 3, 4, or all chords
- **Pedagogical importance**: Demonstrates fundamental root motion

One of the most important progressions in tonal music.

## Examples

### Basic

**ii-V-I** (C major):
```
ii   V   I
Dm   G   C

Most common segment
Standard cadence
```

**vi-ii-V-I** (C major):
```
vi   ii   V   I
Am   Dm   G   C

Extended approach
Four descending fifths
```

**With seventh chords** (jazz style):
```
ii⁷  V⁷  Imaj⁷
Dm⁷  G⁷  Cmaj⁷

Jazz standard progression
Very smooth
```

**Longer sequence**:
```
iii  vi   ii   V   I
Em   Am   Dm   G   C

Five chords
Five descending fifths
Strong forward drive
```

**Complete circle** (rare):
```
I   IV   vii°  iii  vi   ii   V   I
C   F    B°    Em   Am   Dm   G   C

All diatonic chords
Complete cycle
```

### From Repertoire

**Bach, Preludes and Fugues**: Circle progressions common, especially ii-V-I and longer sequences, demonstrates Baroque harmonic practice.

**Mozart, Piano Sonatas**: vi-ii-V-I and ii-V-I progressions at cadences throughout, Classical standard.

**Beethoven, Symphonies**: Extended circle sequences in development sections, creates tonal drive and momentum.

**Jazz standards** (e.g., "Autumn Leaves", "Fly Me to the Moon"): Built on circle progressions, ii⁷-V⁷-I⁷ ubiquitous, with seventh chords throughout.

**Popular music**: ii-V-I common in verses and choruses, simplified circle segments provide strong progressions.

**Chopin, Preludes**: Circle progressions create harmonic flow, particularly in cadential approaches.

## Common Confusions

- Circle of fifths progression = chord roots descend by P5 (or ascend by P4)
- Root motion by fifths, NOT the circle of fifths diagram (though related)
- Complete sequence: I-IV-vii°-iii-vi-ii-V-I (all diatonic chords)
- Most common segment: **ii-V-I** (standard cadence)
- Extended segment: vi-ii-V-I (four chords)
- Each chord acts like V to the next (strong functional pull)
- Descending fifths in bass = ascending fourths (same interval)
- P5↓ = P4↑ (intervallic inversion)
- Very smooth voice leading (common tones, stepwise motion)
- Works in major and minor keys (adjusted for minor scale)
- With seventh chords: even smoother (ii⁷-V⁷-Imaj⁷)
- Jazz harmony built on circle progressions (especially with 7ths)
- Not always complete (partial sequences more common)
- Root motion strongest in tonal music (after P5 is P4)
- Creates forward momentum and drive
- "Backward through circle of fifths" (I→IV→VII→III→VI→II→V→I)
- Universal in all tonal styles (Baroque → contemporary)
- Can be expanded or contracted (use 2, 3, 4+ chords)
- ii-V-I perhaps most common progression in all tonal music
