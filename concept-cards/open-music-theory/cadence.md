# Quick Definition

A cadence is a melodic and harmonic pattern that creates a sense of goal or closure at the end of a phrase, functioning like a punctuation mark in music, with two primary types: conclusive (authentic cadences, V-I) and inconclusive (half cadences, ending on V).

# Core Definition

**Cadences** are melodic and harmonic patterns that create goals at phrase endings, analogous to punctuation marks in literary sentences (OMT, Ch. IV.1). They help determine phrase endings and establish a key. There are two primary categories:

1. **Conclusive**: Authentic cadences (V-I or V-i), which sound finished
2. **Inconclusive**: Half cadences (x-V), which sound open and unfinished

Cadences are special goal points where both the melody and harmony agree on a phrase ending. A harmonic pattern that merely resembles a cadence but does not mark the end of a phrase is not a true cadence.

# Prerequisites

- **Harmonic function** — Cadences are defined by functional progressions (dominant to tonic, or arrival on dominant)
- **Phrase model** — Cadences occupy the D and Te positions at the end of the phrase model

# Key Properties

1. Cadences mark phrase endings (melodic and harmonic agreement on closure)
2. Two primary categories: conclusive (authentic) and inconclusive (half)
3. Authentic cadences: V-I (or V-i), strongest sense of closure
4. Half cadences: x-V, any chord preceding V, sounds unfinished
5. Cadences help establish key
6. Not every V-I or x-V constitutes a cadence; the context must indicate a phrase ending

# Construction / Recognition

## Steps for Hearing Cadences (OMT Ch. IV.1):
1. Listen and mark potential points of rest, goal, or closure
2. Check the harmonies: x-V = potential HC; V-I = potential AC
3. For AC: check if PAC (root position, ^1 in soprano) or IAC
4. Listen for what happens after: a sense of beginning confirms a true cadence
5. If material from the middle of the previous phrase continues, the cadence may be subverted

## Rule of Thumb:
- Unless the tempo is quite slow, phrases are rarely two measures long
- Cadences are followed by a sense of beginning (repetition or new material)

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: Every tonal phrase ends with some form of cadence or cadence-avoidance
- **Common applications**: First step in harmonic analysis; identifying phrase structure; establishing key
- **Historical/stylistic notes**: Cadential patterns are consistent across common-practice music (Baroque through Romantic). The source recommends listening for cadences as a first step in analysis.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Marking every V-I as a cadence
  **Correction**: A cadence must mark a phrase ending; V-I within a tonic prolongation is not a cadence

- **Error**: Marking too many cadences (every two measures)
  **Correction**: Unless the tempo is quite slow, phrases shorter than four measures are uncommon

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Believing a deceptive motion (V-vi) is a type of cadence
  **Clarification**: The source treats V-vi as cadence avoidance ("deceptive motion"), not as a cadence, since the expected phrase ending does not occur

- **Confusion**: Thinking cadences are only about harmony
  **Clarification**: True cadences involve both melodic and harmonic agreement on a phrase ending
