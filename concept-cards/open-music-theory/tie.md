# Tie

## Quick Definition

A curved line connecting two or more notes of the same pitch to combine their durations into a single sustained sound.

## Formal Definition

A **tie** is a curved line that connects two or more notes of the same pitch, indicating that they should be performed as a single, continuous sound lasting for the combined duration of all tied notes. The first note is articulated (struck, plucked, or tongued), while subsequent tied-to notes are held without rearticulation.

Key characteristics:
- Connects notes of the SAME pitch only
- The total duration = sum of all tied note values
- Only the first note is attacked; tied-to notes are not rearticulated
- Can cross barlines and connect any number of notes
- Visually identical to a slur but functionally different

Ties are used when:
- A duration cannot be expressed with a single note or dotted note
- A note needs to sustain across a barline
- Syncopation requires a note to begin off the beat and sustain through the next beat

## Musical Context

Ties are essential for notating rhythms that don't fit standard note values or that span barlines. They enable:
- Syncopation (accents off the beat)
- Complex durations not achievable with dots alone
- Long sustained notes across multiple measures
- Unusual rhythmic groupings

Ties are particularly important in keyboard and wind music, where the attack-and-sustain nature of the instrument makes the tie's function clear. In vocal and string music, ties indicate not to re-bow or re-tongue.

## Examples

### Basic

Duration combinations:
- Half note tied to quarter note = 3 beats (same as dotted half)
- Quarter note tied to eighth note = 3 eighth notes (same as dotted quarter)
- Whole note tied to whole note = 8 beats (no single note value for this)

Syncopation:
- Eighth note (beat 2, second half) tied to quarter note (beat 3) = anticipation of beat 3

### From Repertoire

The opening of "The Star-Spangled Banner" uses a tie to create anticipation: the word "say" begins on a weak part of the beat and ties across to the next strong beat. Suspensions in classical music often feature ties sustaining notes across barlines while harmony changes beneath.

## Related Concepts

- **Prerequisite**: note, rhythm, pitch
- **Leads to**: syncopation, suspension, anticipation
- **See also**: dot, articulation

## Common Confusions

- Tie vs. slur: A tie connects notes of the SAME pitch (don't rearticulate); a slur connects notes of DIFFERENT pitches (play legato/smoothly)
- Ties add durations; they don't create a new type of note
- The curved line can go above or below the noteheads depending on stem direction and spacing
- You cannot tie different pitches—that would be a slur
- A tie across a barline is perfectly normal and necessary for many rhythms
