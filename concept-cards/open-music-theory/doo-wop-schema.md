# Quick Definition
Three main four-chord schemas use I, IV, V, vi in different orders. Doo-wop (I-vi-IV-V): tonic approached by authentic motion (V-I); common 1960s. Singer/songwriter (vi-IV-I-V or I-V-vi-IV): tonic approached by plagal motion (IV-I); tonally ambiguous between relative major/minor; common 1990s+. Hopscotch (IV-V-vi-I): tonic approached by skip; recent (2010s+). All can be rotated and varied with substitutions.

# Core Definition
All three use I, IV, V, and vi — the most common chords in pop music — in different orders. Doo-wop (I-vi-IV-V): named for 1950s-60s rock ballads; ii can substitute for IV; rotatable (IV-V-I-vi in "Viva la Vida"). Singer/songwriter (vi-IV-I-V or I-V-vi-IV): tonally ambiguous (could be i-VI-III-VII in relative minor); no authentic cadence makes tonic uncertain; associated with 1990s songwriters (McLachlan, Jewel, Osborne); rotation IV-I-V-vi creates deceptive variant. Hopscotch (IV-V-vi-I or VI-VII-i-III): "step-step-skip" root motion; also tonally ambiguous; common since 2010; replacing VII with V in minor (VI-V-i-III) encourages minor interpretation. Distinguish by ear: listen to how major tonic is approached — authentic (doo-wop), plagal (singer/songwriter), or skip (hopscotch).

# Key Properties
1. All use I, IV, V, vi — most common pop chords
2. Doo-wop: I-vi-IV-V (authentic approach to I)
3. Singer/songwriter: vi-IV-I-V (plagal approach to I; tonally ambiguous)
4. Hopscotch: IV-V-vi-I (skip approach to I; tonally ambiguous)
5. All rotatable (cyclical progressions can start on any chord)
6. ii can substitute for IV in doo-wop
7. V can replace VII in minor-mode hopscotch
8. Ear test: how is the major tonic approached?

# Context & Application
"Duke of Earl" (1962): classic doo-wop. "Despacito" (2017): singer/songwriter — Bmi-G-D-A ambiguous between D major and B minor. "Dancing with a Stranger" (Sam Smith, 2019): hopscotch. "Nightmare" (Halsey, 2019): hopscotch with V replacing VII in minor. The tonal ambiguity of singer/songwriter and hopscotch schemas is often exploited for songs that modulate between relative major and minor.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Doo-wop): I-vi-IV-V = C-Am-F-G in C major.
**Example 2** (Singer/songwriter): vi-IV-I-V = Am-F-C-G (or i-VI-III-VII = Am-F-C-G in A minor).
**Example 3** (Hopscotch): IV-V-vi-I = F-G-Am-C (step-step-skip root motion).

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: These schemas always start on their "first" chord.
  **Clarification**: All are cyclical and can be rotated to start on any chord. Identify by how the major tonic is approached, not by starting chord.
