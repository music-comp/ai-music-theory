# Quick Definition

The strong predominants IV and ii6 harmonize fa (^4) in the bass before a cadential dominant, strengthening phrase endings by creating the bass pattern fa-sol (^4-^5) at half cadences or fa-sol-do (^4-^5-^1) at authentic cadences.

# Core Definition

Most phrase endings are strengthened using a **strong predominant** (PD) that comes before the V chord (OMT, Ch. IV.3). The two strong predominants are **ii6** (more common) and **IV**, both harmonizing fa (^4) in the bass. Root-position ii is less common but usable in major keys; in minor, ii-o does not appear in root position.

When both IV and ii(6) are used before the dominant, ii always comes after IV because ii's root is a fifth from V's root (similar to V-I), making ii a stronger predominant.

# Prerequisites

- **Harmonic function** — Strong predominants occupy the PD slot in the functional cycle
- **Authentic cadence** — Strong PD typically precedes V at cadences
- **Half cadence** — Strong PD also precedes V at half cadences

# Key Properties

1. Two main strong predominants: ii6 (more common) and IV
2. Both harmonize fa (^4) in the bass
3. Create bass pattern fa-sol (^4-^5) at HC or fa-sol-do (^4-^5-^1) at AC
4. Parallel octaves and fifths are the most common error in the predominant area
5. Contrary motion in upper voices (moving down while bass moves up) helps avoid parallels
6. When both IV and ii6 are used, ii always follows IV (never before)
7. Root-position ii uncommon; ii-o in minor never appears root-position

# Construction / Recognition

## Writing with ii6:
1. Write the entire bass: fa-sol-do (^4-^5-^1) for AC, or fa-sol (^4-^5) for HC
2. Write soprano: start with V, place active note, resolve appropriately, approach from above (contrary motion) or by common tone
3. Fill in inner voices; in ii6 (first inversion), any note may be doubled for smoothest voice leading

## Writing with IV:
1. Same bass and soprano procedure as ii6
2. IV almost always has its bass doubled (root position)
3. Extra danger of parallels due to root motion by step; move upper voices contrary to bass

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: Phrase endings before cadences in common-practice music
- **Common applications**: ii6 is the default strong PD in most styles; IV is the alternative
- **Historical/stylistic notes**: The source uses examples from Maria Szymanowska (March no. 6) to illustrate strong predominants in practice.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Writing parallel fifths or octaves between PD and V
  **Correction**: Move upper voices in contrary motion to the bass (downward while bass rises fa-sol)

- **Error**: Moving all voices upward in parallel motion from IV to V
  **Correction**: Keep upper voices moving down while bass ascends (Example 5 in source shows this error)

- **Error**: Placing IV after ii when using both
  **Correction**: ii always comes after IV, never before, because ii has a stronger root relationship to V

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking ii6 and IV are interchangeable in all contexts
  **Clarification**: While both are strong PDs, ii6 has greater flexibility in doubling (first inversion) and ii has a stronger root relationship to V (fifth motion)

- **Confusion**: Using root-position ii-o in minor
  **Clarification**: Diminished triads do not normally appear in root position; use ii-o6 instead
