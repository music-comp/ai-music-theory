# Cadenza Composta

## Quick Definition

A five-stage cadential galant schema featuring a cadential six-four chord (I 6/4) between pre-dominant and dominant harmonies, with soprano pattern do-re-mi-re-do (^1-^2-^3-^2-^1) over bass mi-fa-sol-sol-do (^3-^4-^5-^5-^1), creating an expanded authentic cadence that strengthens closure through the characteristic cadential six-four.

## Formal Definition

The **Cadenza Composta** (Italian: "compound cadence" or "composite cadence") is an elaborated cadential schema that expands the basic authentic cadence (V-I) by incorporating a cadential six-four chord and pre-dominant harmony, creating a fuller and more emphatic cadential gesture.

**Five-stage structure**:

| Stage | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|-------|---|---|---|---|---|
| Meter | W | S | S | W | S |
| Melody | do (^1) | re (^2) | mi (^3) | re (^2) | do (^1) |
| Bass | mi (^3) | fa (^4) | sol (^5) | sol (^5) | do (^1) |
| Figures | 6 | 6/5 | 6/4 | 7 | 5 |
| Roman numerals | I6 | ii6/5 | I 6/4 (Cad.) | V7 | I |

**Key characteristics**:
- **Cadential function**: Provides strong authentic cadence closure
- **Five stages**: Expands basic V-I into fuller progression
- **Cadential six-four**: I 6/4 at stage 3 (characteristic feature)
- **Pre-dominant**: ii6/5 at stage 2 prepares cadential arrival
- **Soprano arch**: ^1-^2-^3-^2-^1 (ascends then descends back to tonic)
- **Bass ascent**: ^3-^4-^5 (stepwise to dominant), then leap ^5-^1
- **Metric pattern**: W-S-S-W-S (three strong beats frame the cadence)

**Harmonic progression**:
- Stage 1: I6 (tonic in first inversion)
- Stage 2: ii6/5 (pre-dominant, often supertonic seventh)
- Stage 3: I 6/4 (cadential six-four, functions as dominant embellishment)
- Stage 4: V7 (dominant seventh, resolution of cadential six-four)
- Stage 5: I (tonic, final arrival)

Full progression: I6 - ii6/5 - [Cad. 6/4] V7 - I

**Cadential six-four** (Stage 3):
- Labeled "Cad." or "Cad. 6/4"
- I 6/4 chord but functions as embellishing dominant
- ^3 and ^1 in upper voices resolve down by step (^3-^2, ^1-^7)
- Bass ^5 sustains (or repeated) through stages 3-4
- Dissonant fourth above bass resolves to third (6/4 → 7 or 5/3)

**Soprano melody**:
- Arch shape: ^1-^2-^3-^2-^1
- Ascends to ^3, descends back to ^1
- Smooth stepwise motion throughout
- ^3 typically resolves down to ^2 (as part of cadential six-four resolution)

**Bass line**:
- Stepwise ascent: ^3-^4-^5 (first three stages)
- Sustained or repeated ^5 at stage 4
- Leap down to ^1 at final stage (typical authentic cadence bass motion)

**Function**:
- **Strong cadence**: More elaborate than simple V-I
- **Formal closure**: Marks ends of phrases, sections, movements
- **Rhetorical emphasis**: Cadential six-four adds weight to arrival
- **Harmonic expansion**: Stretches cadence across five stages

**Relation to simpler cadences**:
- **Cadenza Semplice**: I6-ii6/5-V7-I (four stages, no cadential six-four)
- **Cadenza Composta**: Adds cadential six-four between ii and V
- "Composta" (compound) refers to additional elaboration via 6/4

## Musical Context

The Cadenza Composta serves essential purposes in galant music:
- **Formal articulation**: Marks major structural boundaries
- **Rhetorical weight**: Provides emphatic closure for important moments
- **Harmonic fullness**: Five-stage progression more expansive than basic cadence
- **Pedagogical model**: Teaches cadential six-four in context
- **Performance gesture**: Allows singer/player to emphasize arrival

The schema's incorporation of cadential six-four makes it characteristic of galant and Classical-era cadences.

## Examples

### Basic

**Cadenza Composta in C major**:
```
Stage 1 (W): Soprano C5 (do/^1), Bass E4 (mi/^3), I6
Stage 2 (S): Soprano D5 (re/^2), Bass F4 (fa/^4), ii6/5
Stage 3 (S): Soprano E5 (mi/^3), Bass G4 (sol/^5), I 6/4 (Cadential)
Stage 4 (W): Soprano D5 (re/^2), Bass G4 (sol/^5), V7
Stage 5 (S): Soprano C5 (do/^1), Bass C4 (do/^1), I

Soprano arch: C-D-E-D-C (^1-^2-^3-^2-^1)
Bass ascent: E-F-G, sustain G, leap to C
Harmonic: I6 - ii6/5 - [Cad. 6/4] - V7 - I
```

**Cadential six-four resolution** (stages 3-4):
```
Stage 3 (Cad. 6/4): E5 over G4 (sixth), C5 over G4 (fourth)
Stage 4 (V7):       D5 over G4 (fifth), B4 over G4 (third)

Upper voices resolve down by step: E→D (^3→^2), C→B (^1→^7)
Bass sustains G (^5)
Dissonant fourth resolves to consonant third
```

**Cadenza Composta vs. Cadenza Semplice**:
```
Semplice (4 stages):   I6 - ii6/5 - V7 - I
Composta (5 stages):   I6 - ii6/5 - [Cad. 6/4] V7 - I
                                      ↑
                              Added elaboration

Composta inserts cadential 6/4 between ii and V
```

**Bass line** (emphasizing stepwise ascent):
```
Bass: ^3 → ^4 → ^5 → ^5 → ^1
      E  →  F  →  G  →  G  →  C (in C major)
      \_______/   sustain  leap

Stepwise through pre-dominant to dominant, then leap to tonic
```

### From Repertoire

**Mozart Piano Sonatas**: Frequent use of Cadenza Composta at phrase endings, movement closes, demonstrating characteristic Classical-era cadence.

**Haydn String Quartets**: Cadenza Composta marks formal boundaries, particularly at ends of expositions and recapitulations.

**Mozart Operas** (arias and ensembles): Cadenza Composta provides emphatic close before fermatas or applause points.

**Clementi Sonatinas**: Pedagogical works feature clear Cadenza Composta examples, teaching cadential six-four usage.

**Beethoven Early Sonatas**: Uses Cadenza Composta for strong closures, sometimes with virtuosic embellishments.

## Common Confusions

- "Composta" (compound) means elaborated/expanded, not "composed"
- Cadential six-four (I 6/4) functions as dominant embellishment, not tonic
- Cadenza Composta vs. Semplice: Composta has cadential 6/4, Semplice doesn't
- Five stages, not four (don't conflate ii6/5 and Cad. 6/4)
- Soprano ^3 at stage 3 typically resolves down to ^2 (not up to ^4)
- Bass ^5 sustained or repeated from stage 3 to stage 4
- Metric placement: cadential six-four typically on strong beat (stage 3)
- I 6/4 labeled "Cadential" to distinguish from passing or neighboring six-four
- Not every I6-ii-V-I progression is Cadenza Composta (need specific five-stage pattern)
- Cadenza Composta = schema name (not performer's improvised cadenza)
- Works in both major and minor modes with appropriate adjustments
- Pre-dominant at stage 2 can be ii6/5, IV, or other pre-dominant chords (ii6/5 typical)
