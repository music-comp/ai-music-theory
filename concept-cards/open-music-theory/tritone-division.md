# Tritone Division

## Quick Definition

Division of the octave into two equal parts of six semitones each, creating the tritone (augmented fourth/diminished fifth)--the simplest non-trivial equal division, producing only a two-note sonority that occurs naturally between ^4 and ^7 of the major scale, and while a "chord progression" of just two tritone-related chords is relatively sparse as a compositional resource, tritone relationships became increasingly important in late Romantic and 20th-century harmony, from the dominant substitution principle in jazz to the "Petrushka chord" in Stravinsky to Bartok's "axis system."

## Formal Definition

**Tritone division** is the equal partition of the octave by augmented fourth/diminished fifth:

**Structure**:
- **Interval**: 6 semitones (augmented fourth/diminished fifth)
- **Division**: 12 semitones / 6 = 2 parts
- **Cardinality**: 2 pitch classes per collection
- **Unique tritone pairs**: 6

**Interval cycle**: C(6) -- traverses only 2 pitch classes before returning

**Set class**: (0,6) or [0,6] -- the tritone dyad

**Mathematical properties**:
- Period of symmetry: T(6)
- Number of distinct collections: 12/2 = 6 tritone pairs

## The Six Tritone Pairs

**All unique tritone dyads**:
```
{C, F#/Gb}    -- pitch classes 0 and 6
{C#/Db, G}    -- pitch classes 1 and 7
{D, G#/Ab}    -- pitch classes 2 and 8
{D#/Eb, A}    -- pitch classes 3 and 9
{E, A#/Bb}    -- pitch classes 4 and 10
{F, B}        -- pitch classes 5 and 11
```

**Together they partition all 12 pitch classes**:
```
6 pairs × 2 notes = 12 pitch classes total
Each pitch class belongs to exactly one tritone pair
```

## Tritone in Tonal Harmony

**The "diabolus in musica"**:
```
In C major scale: F and B form the tritone (^4 and ^7)

F ←--6 semitones--→ B

This tritone:
- Creates the dissonance in V7 (G-B-D-F)
- Resolves inward to E-C (^3 and ^1) or outward to Gb-Bb
- Drives dominant-tonic resolution
- Is the "engine" of functional harmony
```

**Tritone resolution**:
```
Standard resolution (in C major):
B → C (leading tone resolves up)
F → E (seventh resolves down)

Tritone (6 semitones) resolves to:
Major third (4 semitones) - inward
Minor sixth (8 semitones) - outward
```

## Tritone Substitution

**Jazz dominant substitution**:
```
Why can Db7 substitute for G7?

G7 = G - B - D - F (contains tritone B-F)
Db7 = Db - F - Ab - Cb(B) (contains tritone F-B)

Same tritone, different roots!
Roots are a tritone apart: G and Db

The resolution works because both contain ^7 and ^4 of C major:
G7: B-F → C-E (resolving to C major)
Db7: F-B → E-C (enharmonic, resolving to C major)
```

**Tritone-related dominants share tritone**:
```
For any dominant seventh:
The dom7 a tritone away contains the same tritone (inverted)

V7/C = G7 = {G, B, D, F} with tritone {B, F}
bII7/C = Db7 = {Db, F, Ab, B} with tritone {F, B}

This is the basis of tritone substitution
```

## Tritone Relations in 20th-Century Music

**Stravinsky's "Petrushka chord"**:
```
The famous bitonal sonority:
C major triad + F# major triad superimposed

C - E - G + F# - A# - C# = C - C# - E - F# - G - A# (B♭)

Triads a tritone apart create:
- Maximum harmonic distance
- Neither triad can "win" as tonic
- Emblematic of polytonality
```

**Bartok's axis system**:
```
Bartok organized keys by tritone relationships:

Tonic axis: C - F# (and their relative minors: A - Eb)
Dominant axis: G - C# (and E - Bb)
Subdominant axis: F - B (and D - Ab)

Each axis contains keys a tritone apart
Keys on same axis can substitute for each other
```

## Tritone as Building Block

**Relation to other symmetric collections**:
```
Tritone is subset of:
- Whole-tone scale (contains 3 tritones: C-F#, D-G#, E-A# in WT0)
- Octatonic scale (contains 4 tritones)
- Diminished 7th chord (contains 2 tritones: C-F# and Eb-A in C°7)

Tritone is generated by:
- Distance model 6:0 (just repeating tritones)
- Interval cycle C(6)
```

**Building larger collections from tritones**:
```
Two tritones a M2 apart = subset of whole-tone:
{C, F#} + {D, G#} = {C, D, F#, G#} (4 of 6 whole-tone notes)

Two tritones a m2 apart = (0167) Bartok cell:
{C, F#} + {C#, G} = {C, C#, F#, G} = set class (0167)

Four tritones (at m3 intervals) = octatonic:
{C, F#} + {D#, A} = first dim7
{C#, G} + {E, A#} = second dim7
All four = octatonic
```

## Musical Context

**Historical evolution**:
- **Medieval/Renaissance**: Tritone as "devil in music," avoided
- **Common practice**: Tritone essential to dominant function, but resolved
- **Late Romantic**: Tritone exploited for ambiguity (enharmonic reinterpretation)
- **20th century**: Tritone as structural interval (Bartok, Stravinsky)
- **Jazz**: Tritone substitution as standard technique

**Compositional uses of tritone division**:
1. **Bitonal superposition**: Stravinsky's Petrushka technique
2. **Axis relationships**: Bartok's key organization
3. **Dominant substitution**: Jazz and popular music
4. **Structural partition**: Dividing chromatic space equally
5. **Maximum harmonic distance**: Creating "far" relationships

## Examples

### Basic

**The tritone interval**:
```
C to F#: 6 semitones (augmented fourth)
C to Gb: 6 semitones (diminished fifth)
Same sound, different spelling (enharmonic)

C - C# - D - D# - E - F - F# 
   1    2    3    4   5    6

Halfway through the octave
```

**Tritone pairs related to each other**:
```
All tritone pairs:
{C, F#}, {C#, G}, {D, Ab}, {Eb, A}, {E, Bb}, {F, B}

Notice: C's partner F# is the root of the tritone pair that includes C#
The tritone pairs themselves are related by semitones (or fifths)
```

**Tritone substitution example**:
```
Original jazz turnaround:
Dm7 - G7 - Cmaj7

With tritone substitution:
Dm7 - Db7 - Cmaj7

Voice leading of Db7 to Cmaj7:
Db → C (half step down - smooth!)
F → E (half step down)
Ab → G (half step down)
Cb → B or C (to C for maj7)

Actually smoother than G7 to C!
```

### From Repertoire

**Stravinsky, _Petrushka_, "Petrushka's cell"**: C major and F# major superimposed create the famous bitonal chord representing the puppet's inner turmoil.

**Bartok, _Music for Strings, Percussion, and Celesta_**: Axis system organizes tonal centers by tritone relationships. Fugue subject in A, answered at tritone (Eb).

**Bartok, _Mikrokosmos_, "From the Island of Bali"**: Two (0167) sets (containing tritones) combine to create octatonic.

**Jazz standards**: "Cherokee," "How High the Moon," "All the Things You Are" and countless others use tritone substitution for chromatic bass lines.

**Beethoven, Sonata Op. 53 "Waldstein"**: C major to E major (secondary key), then to A-flat/G# for development--tritone-related keys feature prominently.

**Wagner, _Tristan und Isolde_**: A-Eb tritone axis important to harmonic structure.

## Common Confusions

- Tritone = augmented fourth = diminished fifth = 6 semitones (all the same interval)
- Only 6 distinct tritone PAIRS exist (not 12)
- The tritone is its own inversion (6 = 12 - 6)
- Tritone DIVISION gives 2 notes; other collections built FROM tritones have more
- Tritone is symmetrical at T(6) -- transposing by tritone gives the same pair
- The "diabolus" reputation comes from medieval theory, not acoustic facts
- Tritone substitution works because the TRITONE in the chord stays the same
- Bartok's axes group FOUR keys together (two tritone pairs: major and relative minor)
- The Petrushka chord is two TRIADS a tritone apart, not just a tritone dyad
- Whole-tone scale contains THREE tritones (C-F#, D-G#, E-A#)
