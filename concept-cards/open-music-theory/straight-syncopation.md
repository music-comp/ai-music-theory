## Quick Definition

Syncopation created by shifting a series of equal-duration notes early by half their value, causing notes to fall on offbeats.

## Formal Definition

Straight syncopation is a rhythmic technique in which a series of notes of equal duration are transformed by cutting the first note's duration in half and shifting all subsequent notes early by that same half-duration. This creates a pattern where notes consistently fall between beats rather than on them. The unit of syncopation (the amount of shift) is always half the duration of the original "straight" notes.

## Musical Context

Straight syncopation is fundamental to the rhythmic feel of pop and rock music. It can occur at different metrical levels:

- **Beat-level syncopation**: Quarter notes shifted by eighth notes
- **Division-level syncopation**: Eighth notes shifted by sixteenth notes

The technique creates rhythmic tension and forward momentum by placing emphasis on weak beats or offbeats.

## Examples

### Basic Example

Original rhythm: Four quarter notes on beats 1, 2, 3, 4
Syncopated: Eighth note on beat 1, then quarter notes on the "and" of 1, "and" of 2, "and" of 3

## Common Confusions

- **Straight syncopation vs. tresillo**: Straight syncopation shifts notes by exactly half their value (creating 2+2+2+2 patterns shifted), while tresillo divides time into nearly-equal groups (3+3+2)
- **Identifying the level**: Listen for which metrical level the offbeats occur on to determine if syncopation is at the beat or division level
