# Retardation

## Quick Definition

A non-chord tone identical to a suspension in preparation and dissonance but resolving **upward by step** instead of downward—featuring the same three-stage pattern of preparation (consonance on weak beat), suspension (dissonance on strong beat), and resolution (consonance on weaker beat), with the characteristic distinguishing feature being the ascending stepwise resolution (often ^7→^1, the leading tone to tonic), less common than the standard downward-resolving suspension, but appearing particularly in cadential contexts where the voice leading naturally rises to the tonic, creating an upward-pulling tension that resolves with melodic satisfaction.

## Formal Definition

**Retardation** is a non-chord tone occurring in three stages identical to suspension, except resolution moves **UP** by step:

**1. Preparation**:
- Consonant note in previous chord
- Appears on weak beat
- Same pitch as retardation

**2. Retardation proper**:
- Same note held over or re-articulated
- Becomes **dissonant** against new chord
- Occurs on **strong beat** (metrically accented)

**3. Resolution**:
- Moves **up by step** to consonant chord tone
- Occurs on **weaker beat**
- Resolves tension

**Key difference from suspension**: Resolution direction (UP vs. DOWN)

**Notation**: Often shown as **7-8** (in figured bass), where 7th resolves UP to 8ve

## Retardation vs. Suspension

**Suspension** (standard):
```
Preparation → Suspension → Resolution DOWN
C → C (dissonant) → B (down by step)

Most common
Resolves downward
```

**Retardation**:
```
Preparation → Retardation → Resolution UP
B → B (dissonant) → C (up by step)

Less common
Resolves upward
```

**Same in all other respects**:
- Three-stage pattern
- Strong beat dissonance
- Preparation required
- Stepwise resolution

## Common Retardation Patterns

**7-8 retardation** (most common):
```
Bass: C ---- C
Upper: B-C  (7th retards, resolves UP to 8ve)

Preparation: B (7th of chord, consonant)
Retardation: B (7th above bass, dissonant)
Resolution: C (8ve, consonant, UP by step)
```

**At final cadence**:
```
     V         I (with retardation)
S:   B  - C   (7-8 retardation)
A:   G  - E
T:   D  - C
B:   G  - C

B (^7, leading tone) retards
Resolves UP to C (^1, tonic)
Typical cadential context
```

**2-3 retardation** (in upper voice):
```
Bass: C ---- C
Upper: D-E  (2nd retards, resolves UP to 3rd)

Less common than 7-8
Still valid pattern
```

## Voice Leading

**Standard retardation voice leading**:
```
Measure: 1      2      |  3      4
Beat:    1  2   3  4   |  1  2   3  4
         [Prep] [Ret]  |  [Res]
         weak   STRONG |  weak

Chord 1: Consonance (preparation)
Chord 2: Dissonance on beat 1 (retardation)
         Resolution on beat 2 (upward step)
```

**Requirements** (same as suspension):
- **Preparation**: Must be consonant in previous chord
- **Metric placement**: Retardation on strong beat
- **Resolution**: Upward by step to consonance
- **Same voice**: Preparation, retardation, resolution in same voice

## Cadential Context

**Most common at final cadences**:
```
V → I with retardation in soprano:

     V         I
S:   B  - C    (^7 → ^1, retardation resolves UP)
A:   G  - E
T:   D  - C
B:   G  - C

Leading tone (B) retards on strong beat
Resolves up to tonic (C)
Delays final tonic arrival
```

**Why upward resolution?**: Leading tone naturally pulls to tonic (upward)

## Musical Context

Retardations serve specific purposes:
- **Upward-pulling tension**: Resolves with rising motion
- **Cadential delay**: Postpones arrival of expected tonic
- **Leading tone resolution**: ^7→^1 natural upward pull
- **Less common than suspension**: Downward resolution more typical
- **Strong beat accent**: Emphasizes dissonance
- **Three-stage pattern**: Preparation-retardation-resolution
- **Baroque and Classical**: Appears in cadential contexts

Retardations are the upward-resolving counterpart to suspensions.

## Examples

### Basic

**7-8 retardation** (C major, V-I):
```
     V         I
S:   B  - C    (7-8 retardation)
A:   G  - E
T:   D  - C
B:   G  - C

Beat: 1  2  3  4
      [P] [R] [Res]

B prepared on weak beat (7th of V)
B retards on strong beat (7th above C, dissonant)
B resolves UP to C (8ve above C, consonant)
```

**Voice leading detail**:
```
        V       I (retardation)  I
S:      B   →   B           →   C   (7→ret→8, up)
A:      G   →   G           →   E   
T:      D   →   D           →   C   
B:      G   →   C           →   C   

B retards over new tonic bass
Resolves upward to C
```

**2-3 retardation**:
```
Chord 1:  C-E-G    (I)
Chord 2:  C-E-G with D retarding (D is 2nd above C)

Upper:    D - E    (2-3 retardation)

D prepared, retards (dissonant 2nd), resolves UP to E (3rd)
Less common than 7-8
```

**At final cadence**:
```
Final progression: V - I (with retardation)
                   G   C

Soprano: B (^7) retards to C (^1)
Creates delayed arrival at tonic
Upward resolution characteristic
```

**Comparison to suspension**:
```
Suspension (downward):
V:  D-F-A-C    I:  C-E-G-C
S:  C  - B         (resolves DOWN)

Retardation (upward):
V:  G-B-D      I:  C-E-G-C
S:  B  - C         (resolves UP)

Direction of resolution is key difference
```

### From Repertoire

**Bach, Chorales**: Retardations at final cadences, ^7→^1 in soprano voice, creates upward-pulling tension before resolution.

**Handel, "Messiah"**: Retardations in cadential approaches, particularly 7-8 pattern in soprano, delays final tonic.

**Mozart, Masses and sacred works**: Final cadences with retardations, soprano leading tone retards before resolving to tonic.

**Baroque sacred music generally**: Retardations common in final cadences, especially in minor keys (^7→^1 particularly expressive).

**Classical period**: Less common than Baroque, but appears in cadential contexts, upward resolution for variety.

## Common Confusions

- Retardation = suspension that resolves **UP** instead of down
- Same three-stage pattern: preparation-retardation-resolution
- Preparation must be **consonant** in previous chord
- Retardation occurs on **strong beat** (metrically accented)
- Resolution moves **up by step** to consonance (weaker beat)
- Same voice throughout all three stages
- Most common: **7-8 retardation** (^7→^1, leading tone to tonic)
- Figured bass: 7-8 shows retardation (7th resolves UP to 8ve)
- Less common than regular suspension (downward resolution)
- Particularly common at **cadences** (V-I with ^7 retarding to ^1)
- Not same as anticipation (anticipation arrives early, no preparation)
- Not same as appoggiatura (appoggiatura approached by leap)
- Creates **accented** dissonance (strong beat emphasis)
- Upward resolution natural for leading tone (^7→^1)
- "Retardation" = delays resolution in upward direction
- Common in Baroque cadences (Bach, Handel)
- Resolution can be delayed (extended retardation)
- 2-3 retardation less common than 7-8
- Preparation required (unlike appoggiatura)
- Voice leading same as suspension except direction
