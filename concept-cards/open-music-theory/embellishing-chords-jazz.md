# Embellishing Chords (Jazz)

## Quick Definition

Additional chords inserted into a progression to decorate existing harmonies, including applied dominants, applied ii-Vs, and common-tone diminished seventh chords.

## Formal Definition

**Embellishing chords** are harmonies added to an existing chord progression to create interest and forward motion without changing the fundamental harmonic structure. Main types include:

**Applied V7**: Any chord can be embellished by preceding it with its dominant seventh:
- Original: Bbmaj7 | Gbmaj7
- Embellished: Bbmaj7 | Db7 | Gbmaj7 (Db7 is V7/Gb)

**Applied ii chord**: A dominant chord can be embellished by adding its ii chord before it, creating a ii-V:
- Original: Bbmaj7 | C7 | Cm7 | F7 | Bbmaj7
- Embellished: Bbmaj7 | Gm7 | C7 | Cm7 | F7 | Bbmaj7 (Gm7 is ii/C)

**Common-tone diminished seventh**: A voice-leading chord that embellishes through neighbor motion.

## Musical Context

Jazz performers often add their personal interpretation to standards by inserting embellishing chords. This creates harmonic variety between performances and allows musicians to demonstrate their harmonic sophistication.

Embellishing chords are not always written in lead sheets. They may be:
- Improvised by performers
- Built into the chord symbols
- Indicated as optional alternatives (in parentheses)

## Examples

### Basic

Original: Bbmaj7 | C7 | Cm7 | F7 | Bbmaj7

With applied ii: Bbmaj7 | Gm7 - C7 | Cm7 | F7 | Bbmaj7

With applied ii-V: The C7 chord is embellished by adding Gm7 before it, creating a temporary ii-V in F major.

### From Repertoire

"Mood Indigo" (Duke Ellington) is used as a demonstration in Open Music Theory, showing how the original chord progression can be decorated with applied chords and common-tone diminished seventh chords.

## Common Confusions

- Embellishing chords ADD to a progression; substitutions REPLACE chords
- The original harmony is still the structural foundation; embellishments decorate it
- Not all chords in a lead sheet are embellishments; context determines function
- Performers often embellish spontaneously; the same tune may sound different each time
- Applied ii-V embellishment cuts the original chord's duration to make room
