# Quick Definition
A four-stage continuation schema (described by Riepel) that tonicizes the subdominant and then the dominant through ascending sequential tonicizations, with melody do/te-la-re/do-ti and bass mi-fa-fi-sol.

# Core Definition
The **Monte** (described by Joseph Riepel) is a continuation schema with four stages: melody do/te(1/lowered 7)-la(6)-re/do(2/1)-ti(7) over bass mi(3)-fa(4)-fi(raised 4)-sol(5). Meter: W-S-W-S. Figured bass: 6/5-5-6/5-5. Roman numerals: V/IV-IV-V/V-V. Like the Fonte, it uses a modular sequential pattern, but ascending rather than descending. The modular half is V-I: melody sol/fa(5/4)-mi(3) over bass ti(7)-do(1) (Ch. 12).

# Key Properties
1. Four stages with two ascending sequential tonicizations (IV then V)
2. Modular: each half is a V-I progression, ascending by step
3. Bass rises chromatically: mi-fa-fi-sol (3-4-raised 4-5)
4. Meter: W-S-W-S
5. Continuation/process position
6. Often leads to a half cadence on V

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for ascending sequential V-I progressions
2. Check for chromatic bass motion (natural 4 to raised 4 to 5)

# Context & Application
The Monte is an energy-gaining schema that moves toward the dominant, often preparing a half cadence or transition. Its ascending motion contrasts with the Fonte's descent, and together they represent Riepel's "fountain" (Fonte) and "mountain" (Monte) metaphors.
