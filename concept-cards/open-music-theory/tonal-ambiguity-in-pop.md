## Quick Definition

The condition where a chord progression does not clearly establish a single tonic, allowing multiple chords to be heard as the tonal center.

## Formal Definition

Tonal ambiguity occurs when harmonic progressions lack sufficient information to definitively identify the tonic chord. This commonly arises in progressions without authentic cadences (V-I motion), where both plagal motion (IV-I) and stepwise motion (VII-i) provide weaker tonic confirmation. The singer/songwriter and hopscotch schemas are particularly prone to tonal ambiguity due to their cyclic nature and avoidance of authentic cadences.

## Musical Context

Tonal ambiguity can serve expressive purposes, reflecting lyrical themes of uncertainty, duality, or transformation. It also allows songs to move between relative major and minor keys without clear modulation.

### Factors Determining Perceived Tonic

When progressions are ambiguous, listeners may rely on:
- Which chord begins and ends phrases
- Melodic emphasis on particular pitches
- Surrounding harmonic context
- Bass emphasis
- Rhythmic/metric stress

### Common Ambiguous Situations

- **Relative major/minor**: vi-IV-I-V can sound like i-VI-III-VII
- **Hopscotch schema**: IV-V-vi-I can sound like VI-VII-i-III
- **Cyclic progressions**: No clear beginning or end point

## Examples

### Basic Example

The progression Bm-G-D-A could be:
- vi-IV-I-V in D major (tonic = D)
- i-VI-III-VII in B minor (tonic = Bm)

Both analyses are valid; the "correct" answer depends on musical context.

### From Repertoire

- "Despacito" by Luis Fonsi & Daddy Yankee (2017) - Bm-G-D-A; listeners may hear either D major or B minor as tonic
- "What About Love" by Heart (1982) - exploits ambiguity, seeming to modulate between D minor and F major
- "Let It Go" from Frozen - begins in apparent F aeolian, with Ab major emerging as the true tonic in the chorus

## Common Confusions

- **Ambiguity is not error**: Tonal ambiguity is often intentional and expressive, not a compositional mistake
- **Context determines tonic**: The "right" tonic in ambiguous progressions depends on surrounding music, not just the progression itself
- **Multiple valid analyses**: Both major and minor interpretations can be simultaneously valid for ambiguous progressions
