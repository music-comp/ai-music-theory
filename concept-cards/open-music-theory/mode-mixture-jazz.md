# Mode Mixture (Jazz)

## Quick Definition

Borrowing chords from the parallel minor key in a major-key context, particularly substituting lowered ^6 (le) for natural ^6 (la).

## Formal Definition

**Mode mixture** (also called modal interchange or borrowed chords) involves using chords from the parallel minor key in a major-key context, or vice versa. In jazz, the most common mixture involves substituting le (lowered ^6) for la (natural ^6):

**Common jazz mixture substitutions**:
- **ii7 -> ii half-dim7**: Dm7 becomes Dm7b5 (adds Ab instead of A)
- **V7 -> V7(b9)**: G7 becomes G7(b9) (adds Ab as the b9)

These substitutions share the same function as their diatonic counterparts but add color through the lowered sixth scale degree.

**Built-in mixture**: Some tunes have mixture chords written into the original composition (e.g., "All of You" by Cole Porter uses minor iv and ii half-dim7 in a major key).

**Improvised mixture**: Performers can substitute mixture chords to add variety to diatonic progressions.

## Musical Context

Mode mixture adds sentimentality and color to jazz harmony. The alternation between la and le (natural and lowered sixth degree) creates an expressive quality associated with jazz ballads and sophisticated harmony.

In jazz, mixture is both a compositional technique (built into tunes) and a performance technique (improvised substitutions).

## Examples

### Basic

Turnaround in C major:
- **Diatonic**: Cmaj7 - Am7 - Dm7 - G7 - Cmaj7
- **With mixture**: Cmaj7 - Am7 - Dm7b5 - G7(b9) - Cmaj7

The Dm7b5 and G7(b9) both contain Ab (le), borrowed from C minor.

### From Repertoire

"All of You" (Cole Porter): Built-in mixture with Fm (minor iv) and Dm7b5 (ii half-dim7) in a major key. The alternation between A natural and Ab creates the tune's characteristic sound.

"Satin Doll" (Duke Ellington): The ii7 chord can be performed as ii half-dim7 as an improvised mixture substitution.

## Common Confusions

- Mixture chords have the SAME FUNCTION as their diatonic equivalents
- Mixture is not modulation; the key doesn't change
- The most common mixture involves le (lowered ^6), not other borrowed notes
- Jazz mixture is similar to classical mixture but with seventh chords
- Mixture substitutions can be combined with other substitutions (tritone, applied chords)
