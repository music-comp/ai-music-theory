# Quick Definition
A special five-note figure in third species that uniquely includes a leap away from a dissonance, outlining a step progression from downbeat to downbeat, where the first, third, and fifth notes are consonant and the second is dissonant.

# Core Definition
The **nota cambiata** (changing tone) is "a five-note figure that outlines a step progression from downbeat to downbeat." It "highly unusually includes a leap from a dissonance." Two patterns exist: one filling in a step down and one filling in a step up. For it to be effective, "the first, third, and fifth notes must be consonant with the cantus. The second note will be dissonant and will leap to the third tone." The overall smoothness of the figure minimizes the leap's negative effect: "It is surrounded by stepwise motion, the overall progression is a single step, and the dissonant tone and the following downbeat are the same pitch" (Ch. 4).

# Prerequisites
- **Third species** -- The nota cambiata is introduced in third species

# Key Properties
1. Five-note figure spanning two bars
2. Outlines a single step from downbeat to downbeat
3. Second note is dissonant and LEAPS to the third note (unique in species counterpoint)
4. Notes 1, 3, 5 must be consonant with cantus
5. Two forms: filling a step down or a step up
6. Surrounded by stepwise motion to minimize the leap's effect

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for a five-note figure spanning two bars
2. Check if the second note is dissonant and leaps to the third
3. Verify notes 1, 3, 5 are consonant
4. Confirm overall progression outlines a single step

# Context & Application
The nota cambiata is the only figure in species counterpoint where a dissonance is left by leap. It appears in both species counterpoint and 16th-century contrapuntal style, where the source describes it as "generally do-ti-sol-la (8-7-5-6)."

- **Confusion**: Any leap from a dissonance is a nota cambiata
  **Clarification**: The nota cambiata is a specific five-note pattern; other leaps from dissonance are not permitted
