# Quick Definition
The Neapolitan sixth (bII6) is a chromatic predominant chord — a major triad built on the lowered second scale degree (ra/lowered-^2), typically in first inversion, functioning as a dramatic substitute for iio6 and resolving to V with characteristic voice leading where lowered-^2 descends to ^7.

# Core Definition
The Neapolitan sixth is essentially a chromatic version of iio6. Its structure: root = lowered-^2 (ra), third = ^4 (fa, typically in the bass as first inversion), fifth = lowered-^6 (le). Quality: major triad. In C major/minor: Db-F-Ab (Db major triad), with F in bass for bII6. Notation: N6, N, or bII6. The altered tone (lowered-^2) continues moving downward per standard altered-tone practice, ultimately resolving to ^7. When a cad.6/4 intervenes, the resolution path is ra-do-ti (lowered-^2 to ^1 to ^7).

# Key Properties
1. Major triad on lowered-^2, almost always in first inversion (^4 in bass)
2. Function: predominant (leads to V or cad.6/4 then V)
3. Chromatic version of iio6 — same function, more dramatic
4. lowered-^2 resolves DOWN to ^7 (or through ^1 to ^7 with cad.6/4)
5. lowered-^6 resolves DOWN to ^5
6. More common in minor keys than major keys
7. Named after Naples (Napoli), though used throughout Europe

# Context & Application
Common progressions: bII6-V, bII6-cad.6/4-V, bII6-viio7/V-V. The added viio7/V between bII6 and V intensifies the push toward the dominant. Root position bII is rare but possible for dramatic effect. The Neapolitan can be prolonged through extended tonicization or even serve as a temporary key area, as in Schubert's "Erlkonig" where bII is first tonicized and then used in the cadential progression bII6-viio7/V-V7-i.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: The "sixth" refers to scale degree 6.
  **Clarification**: The "sixth" refers to the inversion (first inversion = sixth above bass), not scale position.
- **Confusion**: bII6 is a minor chord because it has lowered notes.
  **Clarification**: bII6 is a MAJOR triad despite containing two lowered scale degrees.
- **Confusion**: Root position bII is the standard form.
  **Clarification**: First inversion (bII6, with ^4 in bass) is overwhelmingly standard; root position is rare.
