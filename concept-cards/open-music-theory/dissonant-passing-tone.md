# Quick Definition
The only dissonance type permitted in second species: a weak-beat note that fills in a melodic third by stepwise motion between two consonant downbeats, always approached and left by step in the same direction.

# Core Definition
A **dissonant passing tone** in species counterpoint is a weak-beat note that passes between two consonant downbeats by stepwise motion. "The melodic interval from downbeat to downbeat in the counterpoint will always be a third, and the passing tone will come in the middle in order to fill that third with passing motion." Since all dissonances in second species are passing tones, "you will not leap into or out of a dissonant tone, change directions on a dissonant tone, nor write a dissonance on a downbeat" (Ch. 3). In third species, consecutive dissonant passing tones are permitted (P4-d5 or d5-P4) as long as they don't fall on downbeats.

# Prerequisites
- **Second species** -- Dissonant passing tones are introduced in second species

# Key Properties
1. Only dissonance type in second species
2. Always on weak beats (never downbeats)
3. Fills a melodic third by step
4. Approached by step, left by step, same direction
5. Cannot leap into or out of dissonance
6. Cannot change direction on a dissonance
7. In third species: consecutive dissonant PTs possible (P4-d5 or d5-P4)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Use:
1. Identify a melodic third between two consonant downbeats
2. Place the passing tone on the weak beat between them
3. Ensure stepwise motion in one direction throughout
## To Identify:
1. Find a dissonant weak-beat note
2. Check: approached by step, left by step, same direction
3. Verify it fills a third between consonant beats

# Context & Application
The dissonant passing tone is the first dissonance type students encounter in species counterpoint. Its strict control -- weak beat only, stepwise, same direction -- teaches the principle that dissonance must be smoothly introduced and resolved.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Leaping to or from a dissonant passing tone
  **Correction**: Always approach and leave by step in the same direction

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Any dissonant note between two consonances is a passing tone
  **Clarification**: The passing tone specifically fills a third by step in one direction
