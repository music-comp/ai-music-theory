# Quick Definition
A four-note ornamental figure in third species where beats 1 and 4 are the same pitch, with both the upper and lower neighbor tones appearing on beats 2 and 3 (e.g., C-D-B-C or C-B-D-C), allowing a leap between two dissonances.

# Core Definition
The **double neighbor** occurs when "beats 1 and 4 in the counterpoint are the same tone, and beats 2 and 3 include the notes a step higher and a step lower than the original tone: for example, C-D-B-C or C-B-D-C." Both beats 2 and 3 may be dissonant, but "since both are embellishing the original tone by step, the leap between them does not significantly diminish the smoothness of the line." The direction between beats 3 and 4 should match the direction across the bar line, and that motion should be stepwise (Ch. 4).

# Prerequisites
- **Third species** -- Double neighbors are introduced in third species

# Key Properties
1. Beats 1 and 4 are the same pitch
2. Beat 2 and 3: upper and lower neighbors (either order)
3. Both beats 2 and 3 may be dissonant
4. Leap between beats 2 and 3 is permitted (unusual for species counterpoint)
5. Direction between beats 3-4 must match direction across bar line
6. Cross-bar motion must be stepwise
