## Quick Definition

Chord progressions featuring the major II# chord with a major tonic, particularly the lydian shuttle (I-II#) and lydian cadence (II#-IV-I).

## Formal Definition

Lydian schemas employ the raised ^4 (fi instead of fa) scale degree with a major tonic. This produces a major II chord (notated II# to indicate the raised third) where major mode would have minor ii. Lydian is rare in pop music as a sustained mode, but the II# chord frequently appears for brief lydian color within otherwise major songs.

## Musical Context

The II# chord can have either subdominant or dominant function depending on context. When II# moves to V, it should be analyzed as V/V (secondary dominant) rather than lydian. True lydian usage involves II# moving elsewhere, particularly to IV or I.

### Key Schemas

**Lydian Shuttle (I-II#)**
- Alternates between tonic and major supertonic
- Often neutralized later by IV or ii (returning to major)
- May use tonic pedal under II#

**Lydian Cadence (II#-IV-I)**
- Most popular progression using II#
- Immediately neutralizes fi with fa in the IV chord
- Features smooth chromatic voice leading: #^4-^4-^3 (fi-fa-mi)

## Examples

### Basic Example

In C lydian:
- Lydian shuttle: C - D (I-II#)
- Lydian cadence: D - F - C (II#-IV-I)
- Voice leading: F#-F-E in upper voice

### From Repertoire

- "Sara" by Fleetwood Mac (1979) - expanded lydian shuttle with tonic pedal and Imaj7; later neutralized by doo-wop schema
- "Forget You" by Cee Lo Green (2010) - clear lydian cadence (II#-IV-I) looped throughout; background vocals feature characteristic chromatic line
- "Starlight" by Taylor Swift (2012) - NOT lydian; the A-B chord alternation is IV-V in E major (doo-wop chorus confirms)

## Common Confusions

- **II# vs. V/V**: If II# goes to V, analyze it as V/V (applied dominant). True lydian has II# going elsewhere (to IV, I, etc.)
- **Lydian rarity**: Few pop songs are fully in lydian mode; most use II# for brief color before returning to standard major
- **Lydian shuttle vs. subtonic shuttle**: Both relate major chords by step, but lydian rises (I-II#) while subtonic/mixolydian descends (I-flat-VII). Context determines which applies.
