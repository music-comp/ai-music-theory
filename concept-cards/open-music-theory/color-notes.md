# Quick Definition
Color notes (also called "characteristic notes" by Persichetti) are the scale degrees that distinguish each diatonic mode from major or natural minor: mixolydian has lowered-7, lydian has raised-4, dorian has raised-6 (vs. minor), and phrygian has lowered-2 (vs. minor).

# Core Definition
Listeners often experience modes as sounding similar to major or minor but with certain inflected notes. These inflected notes are color notes. Modes are grouped by the quality of their third above the tonic: major-ish (mixolydian, lydian) or minor-ish (aeolian, dorian, phrygian). Each has one distinguishing color note. Composers who wish to create a modal sound will emphasize the tonic and feature the color note prominently. Color notes are also important in modal analysis: they help establish which mode is in operation.

# Key Properties
1. Mixolydian: lowered-7 (te) vs. major
2. Lydian: raised-4 (fi) vs. major
3. Dorian: raised-6 (la) vs. natural minor
4. Phrygian: lowered-2 (ra) vs. natural minor
5. Aeolian = natural minor (no distinct color note vs. minor)
6. Ionian = major (no distinct color note vs. major)

# Context & Application
Color notes are the primary aural cue for identifying modes. In analysis, finding the color note(s) narrows the modal identification.

# Examples
**Example 1**: In D dorian, the color note is B-natural (raised-6), distinguishing it from D natural minor (which has B-flat).
**Example 2**: In F lydian, the color note is B-natural (raised-4), distinguishing it from F major (which has B-flat).
