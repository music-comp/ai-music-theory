# Color Notes

## Quick Definition

Color notes are the distinctive scale degrees that differentiate each diatonic mode from major or natural minor. Lydian's color note is the raised 4th (fi); Mixolydian's is the lowered 7th (te); Dorian's is the raised 6th (la); and Phrygian's is the lowered 2nd (ra). These notes give each mode its characteristic "color" or sound.

## Formal Definition

**Color note** (also "characteristic note"): The scale degree that distinguishes a mode from the parallel major or minor scale.

**Color notes for each mode**:

| Mode | Color Note | Solfege | Compared to |
|------|-----------|---------|-------------|
| Lydian | raised 4 (^#4) | fi | Major |
| Mixolydian | lowered 7 (^b7) | te | Major |
| Dorian | raised 6 (^#6) | la | Natural minor |
| Phrygian | lowered 2 (^b2) | ra | Natural minor |
| Ionian | (none) | -- | = Major |
| Aeolian | (none) | -- | = Natural minor |

**Terminology**: Vincent Persichetti used the term "characteristic notes" for this concept. The term "color notes" emphasizes their role in giving each mode its distinctive sonic quality.

**Modal brightness**: Modes can be arranged from "brightest" to "darkest" based on their color notes:
- Lydian (raised 4) = brightest major mode
- Mixolydian (lowered 7) = darker major mode
- Dorian (raised 6) = brightest minor mode
- Phrygian (lowered 2) = darkest minor mode

## Musical Context

Color notes are crucial for:

1. **Identifying modes**: Without hearing the color note, a mode may sound like simple major or minor
2. **Establishing modal character**: Composers emphasize color notes to clarify the mode
3. **Creating modal "flavor"**: The color note is what makes a piece sound "modal" rather than tonal
4. **Analyzing ambiguous passages**: Determining which mode is in play often comes down to identifying the color note

**Listeners' expectations**: People accustomed to major/minor hear the color note as "altered." This creates the distinctive modal effect--a tension between expectation and actual scale content.

**Compositional technique**: Skilled modal composers balance:
- Frequent enough use of the color note to establish the mode
- Occasional withholding for variety or tension
- Strategic placement at cadences or structural points

## Examples

### Basic

**Hearing color notes** (all with C as tonic):

```
C Major (Ionian): C-D-E-F-G-A-B-C
  No color note--this IS the reference scale

C Lydian: C-D-E-F#-G-A-B-C
  Color note: F# (raised 4)
  Sound: "Bright" major, almost floating

C Mixolydian: C-D-E-F-G-A-Bb-C
  Color note: Bb (lowered 7)
  Sound: Major without leading tone pull

C Natural Minor (Aeolian): C-D-Eb-F-G-Ab-Bb-C
  No color note--this is the reference for minor modes

C Dorian: C-D-Eb-F-G-A-Bb-C
  Color note: A (raised 6)
  Sound: "Bright" minor, less melancholy

C Phrygian: C-Db-Eb-F-G-Ab-Bb-C
  Color note: Db (lowered 2)
  Sound: "Dark" minor, exotic/Spanish quality
```

**Color notes affecting harmony**:
```
In C Dorian (color note A natural):
  The iv chord becomes IV (Fm -> F major)
  The ii chord becomes ii (instead of ii dim.)
  This creates the characteristic Dorian sound

In C Lydian (color note F#):
  The IV chord becomes #ivdim7 or the V chord gains a #11
  Creates the "floating" Lydian quality
```

### From Repertoire

**"Scarborough Fair" (traditional)**: D Dorian. The color note A natural (instead of Ab) creates the characteristic minor-with-raised-6th sound that makes the tune immediately recognizable.

**"Norwegian Wood" (Beatles)**: E Mixolydian. The color note D natural (instead of D#) avoids the leading tone, creating the folk-like quality.

**Debussy, "The Engulfed Cathedral"**: Shifts between modes often hinge on the presence or absence of color notes--the raised 4th of Lydian vs. natural 4th of major.

**Vaughan Williams**: Frequently emphasizes Phrygian color (lowered 2), creating the archaic, modal sound associated with English pastoral music.

## Common Confusions

- **Color note is relative**: Dorian's "raised 6" is raised compared to Aeolian, not compared to all possible 6ths
- **Ionian and Aeolian have no color notes**: They ARE the reference scales (major and natural minor)
- **Color notes are not chromatic alterations**: They are diatonic to their modes, just different from major/minor
- **The mode isn't established until the color note is heard**: A melody using only 1-2-3-5-6 could be in either major or Lydian until the 4th is heard
- **Color notes need not be melodic**: They can appear in harmonies as well
- **Multiple color notes don't exist**: Each mode has exactly one distinguishing pitch from its major/minor counterpart
