# Quick Definition
A family of chromatic predominant chords characterized by the augmented sixth interval between le (lowered-^6 in bass) and fi (raised-^4 in an upper voice), which expands outward by half steps to an octave on the dominant. Three types: Italian (It+6: le-do-fi), French (Fr+6: le-do-re-fi), and German (Ger+6: le-do-me-fi).

# Core Definition
Augmented sixth chords are not root-based; they are identified by the characteristic augmented sixth interval between le and fi (lowered-^6 and raised-^4). Both notes are active tones that resolve to sol (^5) from a half step away. The three types differ in their additional pitches: It+6 has three notes (le-do-fi, typically doubling do); Fr+6 adds re (^2); Ger+6 adds me (lowered-^3). All function as predominants resolving to root-position V. The Ger+6 typically requires an intervening cad.6/4 to avoid parallel fifths between le-me and sol-re. In major keys, Ger+6 often respells me as ri (raised-^2) for clearer notation of ascending motion.

# Key Properties
1. Defining interval: augmented sixth (A6) between le (lowered-^6, bass) and fi (raised-^4, upper voice)
2. A6 expands outward to P8 on ^5 (characteristic resolution)
3. Italian (It+6): le-do-fi (3 notes, double do)
4. French (Fr+6): le-do-re-fi (4 notes, re as common tone with V)
5. German (Ger+6): le-do-me-fi (4 notes, requires cad.6/4 to avoid parallel fifths)
6. Not root-based chords — identified by interval content
7. Connection to lament-bass: replacing fa with fi in iv6 creates an augmented sixth

# Context & Application
Augmented sixth chords intensify cadential approaches, especially half and authentic cadences. They relate to the lament-bass progression (iv6 with raised fourth). The German diminished third chord (Ger-o3) is a 19th-century variant with fi in the bass instead of le, inverting the augmented sixth to a diminished third; it closely resembles viio7/V (differing only in le vs. la). Recognizing these chords: look for bass motion le-sol with fi in an upper voice. More common in minor keys but work in major.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Augmented sixth chords have roots like other chords.
  **Clarification**: They are identified by interval content (the A6), not by root. Stacking in thirds produces a confusing diminished third.
- **Confusion**: The three types are inversions of the same chord.
  **Clarification**: Italian, French, and German are three different chord types with different pitch content.
- **Confusion**: Ger+6 resolves directly to V.
  **Clarification**: Ger+6 typically requires an intervening cad.6/4 to avoid parallel fifths (le-me to sol-re).
