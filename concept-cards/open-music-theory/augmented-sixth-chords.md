# Augmented Sixth Chords

## Quick Definition

A family of chromatic predominant chords characterized by the interval of an augmented sixth between the bass (♭^6) and an upper voice (^#4), which expands outward by half steps to an octave on the dominant (^5)—comprising three types named after nationalities: Italian sixth (It⁺⁶ with three notes: ♭^6-^1-^#4), French sixth (Fr⁺⁶ with four notes adding ^2: ♭^6-^1-^2-^#4), and German sixth (Ger⁺⁶ with four notes adding ♭^3: ♭^6-^1-♭^3-^#4), all functioning as intensified predominants that resolve dramatically to V through characteristic chromatic voice leading, common in Classical and Romantic music for expressive cadential approaches.

## Formal Definition

**Augmented sixth chords** (⁺⁶) are chromatic predominant chords featuring:

**Defining interval**: Augmented sixth (A6) between ♭^6 (bass) and ^#4 (upper voice)

**Common tones**:
- **♭^6**: In bass (lowered submediant)
- **^1**: Tonic (usually present)
- **^#4**: Raised subdominant (creates A6 with bass)

**Three types**:
1. **Italian sixth** (It⁺⁶): ♭^6-^1-^#4 (three notes)
2. **French sixth** (Fr⁺⁶): ♭^6-^1-^2-^#4 (adds ^2)
3. **German sixth** (Ger⁺⁶): ♭^6-^1-♭^3-^#4 (adds ♭^3)

**Function**: Predominant, resolving to V (or I⁶₄-V)

## Resolution: Augmented Sixth Expands

**Characteristic resolution** (A6 → P8):
```
♭^6 → ^5 (descends by half step)
^#4 → ^5 (ascends by half step)

Augmented sixth EXPANDS to perfect octave on ^5 (dominant)
```

**Voice leading pattern**:
```
It⁺⁶:  A♭-C-F#   →   V:  G-B-D-G
       ↓  ↑  ↑         ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓
Bass:  A♭→ G          (♭^6 → ^5, down)
Upper: F#→ G          (^#4 → ^5, up)

Augmented sixth (A♭-F#) expands outward to octave (G-G)
```

## Italian Sixth (It⁺⁶)

**Structure**: ♭^6-^1-^#4 (three notes)

**In C major/minor**: A♭-C-F# 
- Bass: A♭ (♭^6)
- Middle: C (^1)
- Upper: F# (^#4)

**Resolution**:
```
It⁺⁶:  A♭-C-F#   →   V:  G-B-D-(G)
       ↓  ↓  ↑         ↓ ↓ ↓
       A♭→ G          (down by half step)
       C → B or D     (step up or common tone)
       F#→ G          (up by half step)

Often doubles ^1 (C) to fill out four voices
```

**Notation**: It⁺⁶ or It+6

## French Sixth (Fr⁺⁶)

**Structure**: ♭^6-^1-^2-^#4 (four notes)

**In C major/minor**: A♭-C-D-F#
- Bass: A♭ (♭^6)
- Chord tones: C (^1), D (^2), F# (^#4)

**Resolution**:
```
Fr⁺⁶:  A♭-C-D-F#  →  V:  G-B-D-G
       ↓  ↓ ↓  ↑        ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓
       A♭→ G           (♭^6 → ^5, down)
       C → B           (^1 → ^7, down)
       D → D           (^2 stays as common tone)
       F#→ G           (^#4 → ^5, up)

Four notes, complete chord
D common tone between Fr⁺⁶ and V
```

**Notation**: Fr⁺⁶ or Fr+6

**Sound**: Like dominant seventh with ♭^6 instead of ^5 in bass

## German Sixth (Ger⁺⁶)

**Structure**: ♭^6-^1-♭^3-^#4 (four notes)

**In C major/minor**: A♭-C-E♭-F#
- Bass: A♭ (♭^6)
- Chord tones: C (^1), E♭ (♭^3), F# (^#4)

**Resolution** (problem with parallel fifths!):
```
Ger⁺⁶: A♭-C-E♭-F#  →  V:  G-B-D-G
       ↓  ↓  ↓   ↑        ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓
       A♭→ G   →         (♭^6 → ^5, down)
       E♭→ D   →         (♭^3 → ^2, down)
       
BUT: A♭-E♭ (P5) → G-D (P5) = PARALLEL FIFTHS!
```

**Solution**: Interpolate I⁶₄
```
Ger⁺⁶: A♭-C-E♭-F#  →  I⁶₄: G-C-E  →  V: G-B-D
       ↓  ↓  ↓   ↑         ↓ ↓ ↓        ↓ ↓ ↓
       A♭→ G               (to I⁶₄ first)
       E♭→ E  → D          (E♭ to E, then D)
       F#→ G               (up by half step)

I⁶₄ interpolated to avoid parallels
Ger⁺⁶ → I⁶₄ → V standard progression
```

**Notation**: Ger⁺⁶ or Ger+6

**Sound**: Like dominant seventh (Mm7) with ♭^6 instead of ^5 in bass

## Comparison of Three Types

**All three in C major/minor**:

| Type | Spelling | Notes | Interval Structure | Resolution |
|------|----------|-------|-------------------|------------|
| **It⁺⁶** | A♭-C-F# | 3 notes (double ^1) | A6 + M3 | Direct to V |
| **Fr⁺⁶** | A♭-C-D-F# | 4 notes | A6 + M3 + M2 | Direct to V |
| **Ger⁺⁶** | A♭-C-E♭-F# | 4 notes | A6 + M3 + m3 | Via I⁶₄ to V |

**All feature**: ♭^6 in bass, A6 interval, ^#4 in upper voice

## Musical Context

Augmented sixth chords serve specific purposes:
- **Dramatic predominant**: Most chromatic, intense predominant
- **Expanding interval**: A6 expands outward to P8 (unique voice leading)
- **Cadential emphasis**: Intensifies approach to V
- **Classical/Romantic idiom**: Common from Mozart through late Romantic
- **Chromatic color**: Exotic, expressive sound
- **Three flavors**: Composers choose based on desired effect
- **German most common**: Ger⁺⁶ particularly frequent (with I⁶₄)
- **Voice leading drama**: Chromatic contrary motion

Essential chromatic predominants in common-practice harmony.

## Examples

### Basic

**Italian sixth in C minor**:
```
It⁺⁶:  A♭-C-F#    V:  G-B-D-G
       ↓  ↓  ↑       ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓
       A♭→ G       (♭^6 → ^5, down)
       C → B       (^1 → ^7)
       F#→ G       (^#4 → ^5, up)
       (C)→ D or G (doubled ^1)

Three-note chord (typically doubled ^1)
Simplest augmented sixth
```

**French sixth in C minor**:
```
Fr⁺⁶:  A♭-C-D-F#   V:  G-B-D-G
       ↓  ↓ ↓  ↑      ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓
       A♭→ G         (♭^6 → ^5)
       C → B         (^1 → ^7)
       D → D         (common tone)
       F#→ G         (^#4 → ^5)

Four notes, D common tone
"Dominant seventh sound"
```

**German sixth in C minor** (with I⁶₄):
```
Ger⁺⁶: A♭-C-E♭-F#  →  I⁶₄: G-C-E  →  V: G-B-D
       ↓  ↓  ↓   ↑        ↓ ↓ ↓        ↓ ↓ ↓
       A♭→ G              (to I⁶₄)
       E♭→ E  → D         (avoids parallels)
       F#→ G              (^#4 → ^5)

Must use I⁶₄ to avoid parallel fifths
Most common augmented sixth
```

**Progression with Ger⁺⁶**:
```
i  -  iv  -  Ger⁺⁶  -  I⁶₄  -  V  -  i
Cm    Fm    A♭/C/E♭/F#  Cm/G   G     Cm

German sixth in predominant position
Leads through I⁶₄ to V
```

### From Repertoire

**Mozart, Piano Sonatas in minor keys**: Augmented sixths (particularly German) at cadential points, dramatic predominant effect.

**Beethoven, "Pathétique" Sonata**: German sixth in C minor, characteristic chromatic predominant, leads through I⁶₄ to V.

**Chopin, Nocturnes and Preludes**: All three types of augmented sixths, particularly German sixth for dramatic cadences.

**Mozart, Symphony No. 40, G minor, I**: Augmented sixth chords in development and recapitulation, intensifies harmonic tension.

**Wagner, operas**: Extensive use of augmented sixths (especially German), chromatic harmony and expressive intensity.

**Classical period generally**: German sixth most common, Italian and French used selectively for variety.

## Common Confusions

- Augmented sixth chords = family of chromatic predominants with A6 interval
- Defining interval: **Augmented sixth** between ♭^6 (bass) and ^#4 (upper voice)
- A6 expands outward to P8 on ^5 (characteristic resolution)
- Three types: Italian (3 notes), French (4 notes), German (4 notes)
- **Italian** (It⁺⁶): ♭^6-^1-^#4 (simplest, often doubles ^1)
- **French** (Fr⁺⁶): ♭^6-^1-^2-^#4 (adds ^2, like altered V⁷)
- **German** (Ger⁺⁶): ♭^6-^1-♭^3-^#4 (adds ♭^3, most common)
- All resolve to V (or I⁶₄-V for German)
- Ger⁺⁶ uses I⁶₄ to avoid parallel fifths (A♭-E♭ → G-D)
- Function: **Predominant** (PD → D)
- ♭^6 descends to ^5 (half step down)
- ^#4 ascends to ^5 (half step up)
- Creates dramatic chromatic contrary motion
- More common in minor keys, but works in major
- "Sixth" refers to augmented sixth interval (not inversion)
- German sounds like V⁷ with ♭^6 bass
- French sounds like V⁷ with ♭^6 bass and added ^2
- Named after supposed national origins (Italian, French, German)
- Most chromatic predominants in common-practice harmony
- Classical/Romantic idiom (Mozart through Wagner)
- Not inversions of same chord (three different chord types)
