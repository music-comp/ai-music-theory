# Ostinato

## Quick Definition

A musical pattern that repeats persistently throughout a composition or section, typically in one voice or part.

## Formal Definition

An **ostinato** (Italian: "obstinate"; plural: **ostinati** or **ostinatos**) is a musical figure—melodic, rhythmic, or harmonic—that repeats continuously throughout a composition or a substantial section of it. The ostinato typically remains in a single voice while other parts vary.

**Characteristics**:
- **Repetition**: The pattern repeats many times (usually at least 3-4 iterations, often many more)
- **Persistence**: Continues throughout a section or entire piece
- **Any voice**: Can occur in bass, melody, inner voice, or rhythm
- **Any length**: Can be as short as one measure or as long as eight or more measures
- **Variation possible**: May undergo slight variation while remaining recognizable

**Types of ostinato**:
- **Melodic ostinato**: A repeating melodic figure
- **Rhythmic ostinato**: A repeating rhythmic pattern
- **Harmonic ostinato**: A repeating chord progression
- **Ground bass (basso ostinato)**: Ostinato specifically in the bass voice
- **Pedal point**: A sustained (or repeated) note, often in the bass

**Common locations**:
- **Bass voice**: Most common (ground bass, walking bass, bass riff)
- **Upper voice**: Melodic ostinato or riff
- **Inner voice**: Harmonic or rhythmic ostinato
- **Rhythmic**: Percussion ostinato (e.g., drum pattern)

## Musical Context

Ostinato serves crucial structural and expressive functions:

**Structural functions**:
- Provides unity and coherence through repetition
- Establishes a stable foundation for variation
- Creates rhythmic and harmonic continuity
- Defines the character and energy of a piece
- Generates momentum and forward drive

**Expressive functions**:
- Creates hypnotic or trance-like effect
- Suggests relentless motion or inevitability
- Can evoke obsession, persistence, or ritual
- Builds cumulative intensity
- Provides grounding stability

**Compositional uses**:
- Foundation for improvisation (jazz, blues, rock)
- Structural device for variations
- Rhythmic/harmonic anchor in complex textures
- Unifying element in sectional forms
- Dance and groove foundation (popular music)

**Historical/stylistic contexts**:
- **Baroque**: Ground bass, chaconne, passacaglia (Purcell, Bach)
- **Romantic**: Programmatic and dramatic effects (Ravel's *Boléro*)
- **20th century**: Minimalism (Reich, Glass), neo-Baroque, jazz
- **Popular music**: Ubiquitous—bass lines, riffs, drum patterns, harmonic loops
- **World music**: African drumming, Indian classical music (tanpura drone)

## Examples

### Basic

**Bass ostinato** (ground bass):
- Bass repeats: C–G–A♭–B♭ throughout piece
- Upper voices vary with each repetition

**Melodic ostinato**:
- Upper voice repeats short melodic figure
- Harmony and bass vary underneath

**Rhythmic ostinato**:
- Snare drum pattern repeats throughout
- Other parts vary around the fixed rhythm

**Harmonic ostinato**:
- Chord progression repeats: I–vi–IV–V (very common in popular music)

### From Repertoire

**Classical ostinato**:
- **Ravel, *Boléro***: Snare drum rhythmic ostinato throughout entire piece (178 repetitions), with melody repeated over it
- **Bach, "Crucifixus" from *Mass in B minor***: Descending chromatic ground bass (13 repetitions)
- **Purcell, "Dido's Lament"**: Chromatic descending ground bass
- **Orff, *Carmina Burana*, "O Fortuna"**: Powerful rhythmic and harmonic ostinati

**Jazz and popular music**:
- **Jazz walking bass**: Repeating bass pattern in many jazz standards
- **Blues**: 12-bar blues progression is a harmonic ostinato
- **Rock**: Bass riffs (e.g., "Money" by Pink Floyd, "Come Together" by The Beatles)
- **Hip-hop**: Looped samples function as ostinati
- **Electronic dance music (EDM)**: Repetitive bass and rhythm patterns
- **Minimalism**: Reich's *Music for 18 Musicians*, Glass's *Einstein on the Beach*

**World music**:
- **African drumming**: Polyrhythmic ostinati in multiple percussion parts
- **Indian classical music**: Tanpura provides drone ostinato
- **Flamenco**: Repeated rhythmic patterns (*palmas*, guitar *rasguado*)

## Common Confusions

- Ostinato vs. ground bass: Ground bass is a specific type of ostinato (in the bass); ostinato can be in any voice
- Ostinato vs. sequence: Sequence is a pattern repeated at different pitch levels; ostinato repeats at the same pitch level
- Ostinato vs. pedal point: Pedal point is a sustained (or repeated) single note; ostinato is a more complex pattern
- Ostinato vs. riff: "Riff" (in popular music) is essentially synonymous with ostinato, especially melodic/bass ostinati
- Ostinato can occur in any voice, not just the bass
- Ostinati can undergo slight variation while still being ostinati—absolute exactness is not required
- Ostinato doesn't have a fixed minimum number of repetitions, but typically repeats at least 3-4 times
- "Ostinato" is Italian for "obstinate," reflecting the persistent nature of the repetition
- Ostinato can be melodic, harmonic, or rhythmic (or a combination)
- Not all repetition is ostinato—ostinato specifically refers to persistent repetition throughout a section or piece
