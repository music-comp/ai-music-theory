# Quick Definition
An internal phrase expansion technique (coined by Janet Schmalfeldt, 1989) involving three steps: the music attempts a cadence, the cadence is evaded, and the music retries the cadence with the same or different material, creating a "backing up" effect.

# Core Definition
The **one-more-time technique** (Schmalfeldt 1989) involves three steps: "(1) the music tries to cadence, (2) the attempted cadence is evaded, and (3) the music retries the cadence." The retried cadence often uses the same material, "such that it feels like the music is 'backing up.'" This differs from an **alternative path**, where the passage has not yet tried to cadence and feels like it has gone off course. One-more-time = "passage has tried and failed"; alternative path = "passage has not yet tried" (Ch. 4).

# Prerequisites
- **Phrase expansion** -- The o.m.t. is an internal expansion technique

# Key Properties
1. Three steps: attempt, evade, retry
2. Feels like the music is "backing up"
3. Retried cadence may use same or different material
4. Creates internal expansion (adds measures)
5. Often combined with stretching in the retry

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 4, Example 4): Mendelssohn, Op. 8/5 -- cadence proposed in m. 10, evaded, retried with stretching.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: O.m.t. and alternative path are the same
  **Clarification**: O.m.t. = tried and failed to cadence; alternative path = hasn't tried yet
