# Lydian Mode

## Quick Definition

The brightest diatonic mode, with the interval pattern W-W-W-H-W-W-H, equivalent to a major scale with a raised fourth degree (^4).

## Formal Definition

**Lydian mode** is a seven-note diatonic mode with the ascending pattern of whole and half steps: W-W-W-H-W-W-H.

**Compared to major (Ionian)**:
- Same as major scale except ^4 is raised by one half step
- Solfège: do-re-mi-fi-sol-la-ti-do (where "fi" = raised ^4)

**Scale degrees** (compared to Ionian):
- ^1, ^2, ^3, ↑^4, ^5, ^6, ^7

**Characteristics**:
- Contains major third (mi/^3), making it a "bright" mode
- Augmented fourth above tonic (C to F♯ in C lydian)
- Perfect fifth above tonic
- Major seventh (leading tone)

**Position in modal brightness spectrum**: Brightest mode (leftmost in brightness continuum)

## Musical Context

Lydian's raised fourth creates a distinctive sound:
- Avoids the tritone between ^4 and ^7 found in major
- Creates tritone between tonic and ^4 instead (C-F♯)
- "Floating" or "dreamy" quality due to augmented fourth
- Common in film scores, jazz, and contemporary music
- Less common in traditional classical music than Ionian or Dorian

The raised ^4 is the defining characteristic distinguishing Lydian from Ionian (major).

## Examples

### Basic

**C Lydian**:
- Pattern: C-D-E-F♯-G-A-B-C
- W-W-W-H-W-W-H
- Raised ^4: F♯ (instead of F♮ in C major)

**G Lydian**:
- Pattern: G-A-B-C♯-D-E-F♯-G
- Raised ^4: C♯ (instead of C♮ in G major)

**Solfège**: do-re-mi-fi-sol-la-ti-do

### From Repertoire

"The Simpsons" theme uses Lydian mode. Joe Satriani's "Flying in a Blue Dream" prominently features Lydian. Film composer John Williams uses Lydian for ethereal, magical effects. Jazz musicians (Bill Evans, John Coltrane) explore Lydian for its bright, suspended quality.

## Common Confusions

- Lydian vs. major: Lydian has raised ^4 (F♯ in C), major has natural ^4 (F♮ in C)
- "Brightest" doesn't mean "loudest"—it refers to the sound character (major-like quality)
- Lydian is not a key—it's a mode (no functional harmony hierarchy like major/minor)
- All modes can start on any pitch (C Lydian, D Lydian, F♯ Lydian, etc.)
- Lydian ≠ Locrian (opposite ends of brightness spectrum)
- The raised ^4 creates augmented fourth from tonic, not perfect fourth
