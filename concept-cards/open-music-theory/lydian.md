# Quick Definition

The brightest diatonic mode, with the pattern W-W-W-H-W-W-H -- like major but with a raised ^4 (fi).

# Core Definition

The **lydian mode** has the ascending pattern W-W-W-H-W-W-H. It is the brightest of all seven modes. Compared to major (ionian), lydian has a raised ^4 (fi instead of fa). Solfege: do, re, mi, fi, sol, la, ti. The raised ^4 gives lydian a characteristically open, floating, or "dreamy" quality.

# Prerequisites

- **Mode**: the general concept
- **Major scale**: lydian is described relative to major

# Key Properties

- Pattern: W-W-W-H-W-W-H
- Like major with raised ^4
- Brightest mode of all seven
- Contains mi (^3) -- a bright mode
- Characteristic interval: augmented 4th above tonic (tritone between ^1 and raised ^4)

# Construction / Recognition

Start with the major scale and raise ^4 by a half step. The three consecutive whole steps at the beginning are recognizable.

# Context & Application

Lydian appears in film scores (the "Simpsons" theme uses lydian), jazz, and impressionist music. The raised ^4 creates an ethereal, uplifting quality.

# Examples

- C lydian: C-D-E-F#-G-A-B-C (note: F#, not F as in C major)
- F lydian: F-G-A-B-C-D-E-F (all white keys)

- Lydian is NOT the same as major -- the raised ^4 is the distinguishing feature
