# Converging Cadence Schema

## Quick Definition

A four-stage pre-cadential galant schema featuring the characteristic chromatic bass line fa-fi-sol (^4-♯^4-^5) with soprano mi-re-do-ti (^3-^2-^1-^7), creating a half cadence through contrary chromatic motion converging on the dominant, also known as the "fa-fi-sol half cadence."

## Formal Definition

The **Converging Cadence** (also called "fa-fi-sol half cadence") is a pre-cadential schema that creates a half cadence on the dominant through chromatic voice leading, with bass and soprano approaching the dominant from opposite directions.

**Four-stage structure**:

| Stage | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|-------|---|---|---|---|
| Meter | W | S | W | S |
| Melody | mi (^3) | re (^2) | do (^1) | ti (^7) |
| Bass | mi (^3) | fa (^4) | fi (♯^4) | sol (^5) |
| Figures | 6 | 6/5 | 6/5 | 5 |
| Roman numerals | I | ii (or IV) | V/V (or viio7/V) | V |

**Key characteristics**:
- **Half cadence function**: Arrives on V (dominant), not I (tonic)
- **Chromatic bass**: fa-fi-sol (^4-♯^4-^5), signature ascending pattern
- **Descending soprano**: mi-re-do-ti (^3-^2-^1-^7), stepwise descent
- **Contrary motion**: Bass ascends chromatically while soprano descends
- **"Converging"**: Both voices approach V from opposite directions
- **Chromatic leading tone**: ♯^4 (fi) acts as leading tone to ^5 (sol)
- **Metric pattern**: W-S-W-S (weak-strong-weak-strong)

**Harmonic progression**:
- Stage 1: I6 (tonic in first inversion)
- Stage 2: ii6/5 or IV (pre-dominant)
- Stage 3: V/V or viio7/V (secondary dominant/diminished seventh to V)
- Stage 4: V (dominant, half cadence goal)

**Bass line** (chromatic ascent):
- fa-fi-sol (^4-♯^4-^5)
- Chromatic half-step between stages 2-3 (F→F#)
- Creates leading-tone approach to dominant
- Intensifies arrival on V through chromaticism

**Soprano line** (stepwise descent):
- mi-re-do-ti (^3-^2-^1-^7)
- Descends through scale degrees to leading tone
- Contrary to ascending bass (converging motion)
- Arrives on ^7 (leading tone) over V

**"Converging" motion**:
- Bass ascends: fa→fi→sol (^4→♯^4→^5)
- Soprano descends: mi→re→do→ti (^3→^2→^1→^7)
- Both arrive at V from opposite directions
- Creates strong sense of directed motion toward dominant

**Function**:
- **Half cadence**: Ends on V (not I), creating open ending
- **Pre-dominant to dominant**: Chromatic approach intensifies arrival
- **Formal articulation**: Marks half cadence points in phrases
- **Chromatic color**: ♯^4 provides expressive chromaticism
- **Sectional boundary**: Often marks end of first section before new material

**Relation to Indugio**:
Both feature fa-fi-sol bass pattern:
- **Indugio**: Prolongs fa first (fa-fa-fa-fi-sol), then chromatic to sol
- **Converging Cadence**: Direct chromatic approach (fa-fi-sol) without prolongation
- Indugio delays; Converging Cadence proceeds directly to goal

## Musical Context

The Converging Cadence serves specific purposes in galant music:
- **Half cadence formula**: Standard way to create half cadence
- **Chromatic expressivity**: ♯^4 provides emotional intensity
- **Formal punctuation**: Marks ends of phrases or sections on V
- **Contrary motion model**: Teaches chromatic voice leading
- **Pre-retransition**: Often precedes return to tonic or main theme

The schema's chromatic bass and contrary motion make it a hallmark of galant half cadences.

## Examples

### Basic

**Converging Cadence in C major**:
```
Stage 1 (W): Soprano E4 (mi/^3), Bass E4 (mi/^3), I6
Stage 2 (S): Soprano D4 (re/^2), Bass F4 (fa/^4), ii6/5 or IV
Stage 3 (W): Soprano C4 (do/^1), Bass F#4 (fi/♯^4), V6/5/V or viio7/V
Stage 4 (S): Soprano B3 (ti/^7), Bass G4 (sol/^5), V

Bass ascends chromatically: E-F-F#-G (^3-^4-♯^4-^5, mi-fa-fi-sol)
Soprano descends: E-D-C-B (^3-^2-^1-^7, mi-re-do-ti)
Harmonic: I6 - ii6/5 - V/V - V (half cadence)
```

**Chromatic bass line** (fa-fi-sol):
```
Bass: ^4 → ♯^4 → ^5
      F  →  F# →  G (in C major)
      [chromatic half-step]

F# (fi) acts as chromatic leading tone to G (sol)
Signature pattern of Converging Cadence
```

**Contrary chromatic motion**:
```
Bass:    F  → F# → G  (ascending chromatically)
Soprano: D  → C  → B  (descending diatonically)

"Converging" on dominant (G) from opposite directions
Creates strong directed motion
```

**Half cadence** (stage 4):
```
Arrives on V (dominant) as half cadence
Leaves phrase open, requires continuation
Not conclusive like authentic cadence (V→I)
```

**Converging Cadence vs. Indugio**:
```
Indugio:            fa-fa-fa-fi-sol (prolongs subdominant)
Converging Cadence: (mi)-fa-fi-sol (direct chromatic approach)

Both use fa-fi-sol, but Indugio delays with repetition first
```

### From Repertoire

**Mozart Piano Sonatas**: Converging Cadence marks half cadences at ends of opening periods, preparing for contrasting sections.

**Haydn String Quartets**: Uses fa-fi-sol bass for emphatic half cadences, particularly before development sections.

**Beethoven Piano Sonatas**: Converging Cadence at structural half cadence points, often with dramatic dynamic emphasis.

**Mozart Symphonies**: Half cadences in exposition transitions employ Converging Cadence pattern.

**Galant keyboard sonatas** (J.C. Bach, C.P.E. Bach): Fa-fi-sol bass standard for half cadences throughout.

## Common Confusions

- "Converging" describes bass and soprano approaching V from opposite directions
- Half cadence (ends on V), not authentic cadence (would end on I)
- Fa-fi-sol bass is signature (chromatic ^4-♯^4-^5)
- ♯^4 (fi) is chromatic alteration (not diatonic ^4)
- Converging Cadence vs. Indugio: Converging direct, Indugio prolongs subdominant first
- Four stages typical, though principle (chromatic approach to V) essential
- Works in both major and minor modes (adjust soprano for minor)
- "Fa-fi-sol half cadence" = alternative name (describes bass pattern)
- Contrary motion not required but typical (soprano descends, bass ascends)
- Converging Cadence ends on V; continuation provides resolution to I
- Pre-cadential function (prepares but doesn't deliver final tonic)
- Stage 3 harmony (V/V or viio7/V) intensifies arrival on V at stage 4
