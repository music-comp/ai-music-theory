# Quick Definition
Seven specific patterns for consonant weak beats in second species: consonant passing tone, substitution, skipped passing tone, interval subdivision, change of register, delay of melodic progression, and consonant neighbor tone -- each defining a particular melodic and harmonic relationship between downbeats.

# Core Definition
In second species, weak beats may be consonant or dissonant. Seven consonant patterns are identified (terms from Salzer & Schachter's *Counterpoint in Composition*): (1) **consonant passing tone** -- fills a third by step, all consonant; (2) **substitution** -- leaps a fourth, steps back (outlines third); (3) **skipped passing tone** -- third + step same direction (outlines fourth); (4) **interval subdivision** -- two leaps dividing a fifth or sixth; (5) **change of register** -- large consonant leap then step opposite; (6) **delay of melodic progression** -- third leap then step back (outlines step); (7) **consonant neighbor tone** -- step away and back (Ch. 3, "Consonant weak beats").

# Key Properties
1. Seven distinct patterns for consonant weak beats
2. All three notes (downbeat, weak beat, next downbeat) must be consonant with cantus
3. Each pattern outlines a specific melodic interval between downbeats
4. Consonant passing tone: outlines a third
5. Substitution and delay: outline a third (different paths)
6. Skipped passing tone: outlines a fourth
7. Interval subdivision: outlines a fifth or sixth
8. Change of register: large leap for variety
9. Consonant neighbor: returns to same pitch

# Construction / Recognition
## To Use:
1. Choose downbeat notes following first-species rules
2. Select a consonant weak-beat pattern that connects them
3. Verify all notes are consonant with the cantus
## To Identify:
1. Determine the intervallic relationship (downbeat to downbeat)
2. Check the melodic path between downbeats against the seven pattern types

# Context & Application
These patterns serve as a guide for composing second-species counterpoint and evaluating it. The source notes: "Chances are high that if your weak beats do not fit into one of the following patterns, there is a problem with the counterpoint."
