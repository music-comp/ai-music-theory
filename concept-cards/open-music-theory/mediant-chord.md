# Quick Definition

The iii chord (III in minor) is a weak predominant that typically follows I, harmonizes a descending ti (^7), and moves through a strong predominant on the way to V. It is uncommon and is NOT a substitute for I6.

# Core Definition

The **iii chord** is used in one relatively specific situation: after a I chord, harmonizing a descending ti/te (^7/^b7) in an upper voice (OMT, Ch. IV.12). It commonly moves to a strong predominant, though it can move directly to V in a bass arpeggiation do-mi-sol (^1-^3-^5). It is usually in root position.

Critically, iii is NOT a substitute for I6 in Western classical music. The progression I-V4/3-iii would show V getting to iii, which is not stylistically normative. iii does not appear very often and should be used sparingly.

# Prerequisites

- **Harmonic function** — iii functions as a weak predominant
- **Tonic prolongation** — Understanding that mi (^3) in the bass is more commonly I6, not iii

# Key Properties

1. Weak predominant function
2. Follows I, harmonizing descending ti (^7) in an upper voice
3. Moves to strong predominant (IV, ii) or directly to V
4. Usually in root position
5. NOT a substitute for I6
6. Uncommon chord; use sparingly
7. In minor: III is major, using te (^b7) not ti (^#7); III to V creates problematic ^b7/^#7 juxtaposition

# Construction / Recognition

## To Write iii (OMT Ch. IV.12, Example 2):
1. Place after I with descending ti-la (^7-^6) in an upper voice
2. Move to a strong predominant (IV or ii)
3. In major: leading tone descends to la (^6) when iii moves to PD
4. In minor: III uses te (^b7); avoid III directly to V (^b7/^#7 clash)

# Relationships

## Related
- **Strong predominant** — iii typically leads to a strong PD on the way to V

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Using iii as a substitute for I6 in tonic prolongation
  **Correction**: iii is a weak predominant, not a tonic substitute; I-V4/3-iii is not stylistic

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking iii and I6 are interchangeable
  **Clarification**: iii has predominant function; I6 has tonic function. They are fundamentally different despite sharing the bass note mi (^3).
