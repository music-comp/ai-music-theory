# Ponte Schema

## Quick Definition

A three-stage galant schema featuring a static dominant pedal in the bass while the upper voice ascends (sol-ti-re or ^5-^7-^2), creating a bridge or transitional passage that prolongs dominant harmony before resolution or new material, commonly used to span structural gaps in 18th-century music.

## Formal Definition

The **Ponte** (Italian: "bridge"; named by Joseph Riepel in 18th century) is a transitional schema that creates a harmonic plateau on the dominant through bass pedal while upper voices provide melodic motion. The name directly describes its bridging function.

**Three-stage structure**:

| Stage | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|-------|---|---|---|
| Meter | S | W | S |
| Melody | sol (^5) | ti (^7) | re (^2) |
| Bass | sol (^5) | sol (^5) | sol (^5) |
| Figures | 5 | 7 | 7 |
| Roman numerals | V | V | V |

**Key characteristics**:
- **Static bass**: Dominant pedal (^5) sustained throughout all stages
- **Ascending melody**: Soprano rises sol-ti-re (^5-^7-^2), often by steps
- **Dominant prolongation**: All three stages prolong V harmony
- **Transitional function**: Bridges between sections or thematic areas
- **Metric pattern**: Strong-weak-strong (S-W-S)
- **No resolution**: Ponte typically doesn't resolve to tonic (leaves dominant open)

**Harmonic structure**:
- **Stage 1**: Root-position V (5/3)
- **Stage 2**: V7 or V with added seventh (5/7)
- **Stage 3**: V7 continues (5/7)
- Bass pedal eliminates root motion
- Upper voices provide all melodic interest and harmonic color

**Function**:
- **Bridge/transition**: Connects sections, themes, or formal units
- **Dominant prolongation**: Extends V harmony without resolving
- **Structural gap filler**: Spans time between important thematic statements
- **Suspenseful preparation**: Creates anticipation before new material
- **Modulatory pivot**: Can facilitate key changes by prolonging new dominant

**Contrast with other schemas**:
- **Fonte/Monte**: Sequential, modular, harmonically active
- **Ponte**: Static bass, non-sequential, single harmony prolonged
- **Prinner**: Descending bass, goal-directed to tonic
- **Ponte**: Stable bass, suspends resolution

**Typical contexts**:
- Between theme statements in ritornello forms
- Before recapitulation or return of main material
- Spanning formal boundaries
- Developmental sections requiring dominant prolongation
- Transitions modulating to new keys

**Relation to dominant pedal**:
- Ponte is specific galant application of dominant pedal technique
- Not every dominant pedal is a Ponte (need specific melodic pattern)
- Ponte formalizes pedal point as structural schema

## Musical Context

The Ponte serves multiple purposes in galant music:
- **Formal articulation**: Marks boundaries between sections
- **Transitional bridge**: Provides connective tissue between themes
- **Harmonic stasis**: Creates plateau contrasting with sequential motion
- **Suspense building**: Prolongs dominant tension before resolution or surprise
- **Time spanning**: Fills structural time without harmonic change

The static bass with ascending melody creates anticipatory tension characteristic of galant bridging passages.

## Examples

### Basic

**Ponte in C major** (dominant pedal):
```
Stage 1 (S): Soprano G4 (sol/^5), Bass G3 (sol/^5), V (5/3)
Stage 2 (W): Soprano B4 (ti/^7), Bass G3 (sol/^5), V7 (7)
Stage 3 (S): Soprano D5 (re/^2), Bass G3 (sol/^5), V7 (7)

Bass remains on G throughout (dominant pedal)
Soprano ascends: G-B-D (outlining G major triad = V in C)
Creates dominant prolongation spanning three stages
```

**Ponte as bridge** (formal function):
```
[End of theme A] → [Ponte: V-V-V on pedal] → [Beginning of theme B]
                     ↑
                 Bridges gap between themes
                 Prolongs dominant without resolving
```

**Ponte melodic ascent**:
```
Soprano rises through dominant harmony:
G (root) → B (third) → D (fifth, or ninth if extended)
Ascent creates sense of lift or anticipation
Static bass contrasts with melodic motion
```

**Ponte before recapitulation**:
```
[Development section ends] → [Ponte: dominant prolongation] → [Recapitulation begins]
                              ↑
                          Prepares return through dominant pedal
                          Creates structural articulation
```

**Extended Ponte** (more stages):
```
Can extend beyond three stages:
sol-ti-re-fa (^5-^7-^2-^4) over dominant pedal
Creates longer bridge for larger formal spans
Principle remains: static bass, ascending melody
```

### From Repertoire

**Mozart Piano Sonata K. 545, I, transition**: Ponte bridges first and second theme areas with dominant pedal and ascending melodic line.

**Haydn Symphony No. 104 "London," I**: Uses Ponte in ritornello to span time between orchestral statements.

**Mozart Piano Concerto K. 467, I**: Ponte connects solo and tutti sections, prolonging dominant before theme returns.

**Galant concertos** (J.C. Bach, C.P.E. Bach): Ponte frequently bridges solo and orchestral passages in ritornello form.

**Opera arias** (Pergolesi, Hasse): Ponte provides orchestral bridge during held vocal notes or between vocal phrases.

## Common Confusions

- Ponte ≠ any dominant pedal—specific ascending melodic pattern over V bass
- Static bass is defining feature (no root motion unlike Fonte/Monte)
- Ponte doesn't resolve to tonic (leaves dominant open for continuation)
- Not a cadential schema (bridges rather than concludes)
- "Ponte" (bridge) describes function—connects, doesn't close
- Three stages typical but can be extended (principle matters, not exact length)
- Ponte vs. standing on dominant: Ponte is specific galant schema with melodic pattern
- Ascending melody contrasts with static bass (motion vs. stasis)
- Can appear before or after cadences (transitional, not cadential function)
- Named by Riepel alongside Fonte and Monte (historical pedagogy)
- Ponte more about formal function than harmonic complexity
- Works in both major and minor modes (adjust ^5 as needed)
