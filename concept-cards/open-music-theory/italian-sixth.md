# Italian Sixth

## Quick Definition
The Italian sixth (It+6) is the simplest of the three augmented sixth chord types, containing only three notes (^b6, ^1, and ^#4) with the characteristic augmented sixth interval between the outer voices, functioning as a chromatic predominant that resolves directly to the dominant through dramatic contrary motion.

## Formal Definition
**Italian sixth** (It+6) is an augmented sixth chord with:

**Structure**: Three notes only
- ^b6 (lowered submediant) in bass
- ^1 (tonic) in middle voice
- ^#4 (raised subdominant) in upper voice

**Interval content**:
- Augmented sixth (A6) between ^b6 and ^#4
- Major third (M3) between ^b6 and ^1
- Augmented fourth (A4/tritone) between ^1 and ^#4

**Function**: Chromatic predominant resolving to V

## Voice Leading

**Resolution pattern** (A6 expands to P8):
```
It+6:  Ab-C-F#   ->   V:  G-B-D-G
       ^b6-^1-^#4      ^5-^7-^2-^5

Voice leading:
Ab -> G  (^b6 descends by half step to ^5)
C  -> B  (^1 descends by half step to ^7)
C  -> D  (doubled ^1 can ascend to ^2)
F# -> G  (^#4 ascends by half step to ^5)
```

**The defining motion**:
- ^b6 and ^#4 converge on ^5 from opposite directions
- Creates characteristic "wedge" or "pincer" voice leading
- Augmented sixth interval expands outward to octave

**Four-voice texture**:
```
In four voices, double ^1:
It+6: Ab-C-C-F#  ->  V: G-B-D-G
      ^b6-^1-^1-^#4
      
One C moves to B, the other to D
```

## Spelling in Various Keys

**In C major/minor**: Ab-C-F#
**In G major/minor**: Eb-G-C#
**In D major/minor**: Bb-D-G#
**In A major/minor**: F-A-D#
**In E major/minor**: C-E-A#
**In F major/minor**: Db-F-B

**Pattern**: Always ^b6-^1-^#4 in any key

## Comparison with Other Augmented Sixths

| Chord | Notes | Additional Tone | Resolution |
|-------|-------|-----------------|------------|
| **It+6** | Ab-C-F# | None (doubled ^1) | Direct to V |
| Fr+6 | Ab-C-D-F# | ^2 | Direct to V |
| Ger+6 | Ab-C-Eb-F# | ^b3 | Via I64 to V |

**Italian is the simplest**: No fourth note, requires doubling

## Musical Context

The Italian sixth serves specific purposes:
- **Simplest augmented sixth**: Only three distinct pitches
- **Direct resolution**: No need for I64 interpolation
- **Lighter texture**: Less dense than French or German
- **Historical origin**: Earliest form of augmented sixth usage
- **Baroque/Classical**: More common in earlier periods
- **Dramatic predominant**: Creates chromatic tension before V
- **Cadential emphasis**: Intensifies approach to half or authentic cadence

## Examples

### Basic

**Italian sixth in C minor**:
```
It+6:  Ab-C-C-F#   V:  G-B-D-G
       |  |  |  |      |  |  |  |
Bass:  Ab    ->    G   (down by half step)
Alto:  C     ->    B   (down by half step)  
Tenor: C     ->    D   (up by step)
Sop:   F#    ->    G   (up by half step)

Augmented sixth (Ab-F#) expands to octave (G-G)
```

**In progression**:
```
i  -  iv6  -  It+6  -  V  -  i
Cm    Fm/Ab   Ab-C-F#   G    Cm

It+6 replaces or follows iv6
Prepares half cadence
```

### From Repertoire

**Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 310, I**: Italian sixth chords in A minor, simpler texture in Classical style.

**Handel, Harpsichord Suites**: Italian sixths in Baroque cadential contexts, the earliest systematic use.

**Scarlatti, Keyboard Sonatas**: Italian sixths in dramatic cadences, Baroque virtuosic style.

**Early Classical period**: Italian sixth as the standard augmented sixth before French and German became common.

## Common Confusions

- Italian sixth = three-note augmented sixth chord (It+6)
- Spelling: ^b6-^1-^#4 (in bass-middle-top)
- In C: Ab-C-F# (simplest form)
- **Three notes only**: Requires doubling ^1 in four-voice texture
- Augmented sixth interval: Between ^b6 (bass) and ^#4 (soprano)
- Resolves directly to V (no I64 needed unlike German)
- ^b6 descends by half step to ^5
- ^#4 ascends by half step to ^5
- Creates "wedge" voice leading converging on ^5
- Simplest and historically earliest augmented sixth type
- Named for supposed Italian origin (all three nationalities are historical simplifications)
- Function: Chromatic predominant (PD -> D)
- No root (unlike traditional chords)
- Major third between ^b6 and ^1
- Augmented fourth/tritone between ^1 and ^#4
