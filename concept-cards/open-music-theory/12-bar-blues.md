# Quick Definition
The 12-bar blues is a three-phrase schema (4 bars each) where all chords are dominant seventh chords: phrase 1 on I, phrase 2 starts on IV then returns to I, phrase 3 goes V-IV-I. Unique features: dominant seventh on ALL chord functions (not just dominant), plagal cadences provide structural closure (not authentic), and major/minor thirds freely mix. It is a schema with countless variations.

# Core Definition
Blues harmony differs fundamentally from tonal and jazz harmony: dominant-quality sevenths can have any function (tonic, dominant, subdominant); plagal cadences (IV-I) provide closure instead of authentic cadences (V-I); major and minor thirds mix freely (sometimes written as #9 extension). Three phrases: (1) four bars of I, (2) two bars IV + two bars I, (3) one bar V + one bar IV + two bars I. The 16-bar blues adds a fourth phrase (usually two I phrases, then IV, then V). Minor blues: i and iv become minor sevenths, V stays dominant, third phrase often uses ii-V-I instead of V-IV-I. Jazz blues adds ii-V progressions and turnarounds to the basic schema, blending blues and jazz harmonic languages. Roots reach back to music of enslaved African Americans.

# Prerequisites
- Dominant seventh chord
- Plagal vs. authentic cadence

# Key Properties
1. Three 4-bar phrases: I | IV-I | V-IV-I
2. ALL chords dominant seventh (do not fit single key)
3. Plagal cadences (IV-I) provide structural closure
4. Major and minor thirds freely mixed (blue notes)
5. Schema with innumerable variations
6. 16-bar blues: four 4-bar phrases (I-I-IV-V)
7. Minor blues: i7 and iv7 (minor sevenths), V stays dominant
8. Jazz blues: adds ii-V progressions, replaces V-IV-I with ii-V-I

# Context & Application
"You Can't Do That" (Beatles): nearly basic 12-bar blues with V turnaround in final bar. "Hoochie Coochie Man" (Muddy Waters): 16-bar blues example. "The Thrill is Gone" (B.B. King): minor blues with bVI-V replacing ii-V. "C Jam Blues" (Ellington/LCJO): jazz blues with ii-V progressions. Common variations: IV in second bar, turnarounds in final bars, applied V/V before V.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Basic 12-bar): I7-I7-I7-I7 | IV7-IV7-I7-I7 | V7-IV7-I7-I7.
**Example 2** (Jazz blues): I7-IV7-I7-I7 | IV7-IV7-I7-VI7 | ii7-V7-I7-ii7-V7 (turnaround).
**Example 3** (Minor blues): i7-i7-i7-i7 | iv7-iv7-i7-i7 | ii-half-dim7-V7-i7-i7.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Blues uses standard tonal harmonic function.
  **Clarification**: Blues is distinct — dominant sevenths can be tonic or subdominant, and plagal cadences provide closure (not authentic).
- **Confusion**: Blues must follow the exact 12-bar pattern.
  **Clarification**: The blues is a schema; variations are the norm, not the exception.
