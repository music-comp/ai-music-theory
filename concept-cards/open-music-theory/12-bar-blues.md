# 12-Bar Blues

## Quick Definition

The most common blues form, consisting of three four-bar phrases with a I-IV-I-V-IV-I harmonic pattern where all chords are typically dominant sevenths.

## Formal Definition

The **12-bar blues** is a schema consisting of three four-bar phrases with the following basic harmonic structure:

**Phrase 1 (mm. 1-4)**: Tonic (I7)
- Four bars of I7

**Phrase 2 (mm. 5-8)**: Subdominant to Tonic
- Two bars of IV7
- Two bars of I7

**Phrase 3 (mm. 9-12)**: Dominant-Subdominant-Tonic (plagal motion)
- One bar of V7
- One bar of IV7
- Two bars of I7

**Key characteristics**:
- All chords are dominant seventh quality (not fitting a single key)
- Emphasis on plagal (IV-I) rather than authentic (V-I) cadences
- The V-IV-I motion in phrase 3 is distinctively blues
- Turnarounds commonly added in mm. 11-12

## Musical Context

The 12-bar blues is one of the most important forms in American popular music, with roots reaching back to the music of enslaved African Americans. The form underlies countless blues songs and influenced jazz, rock, and R&B.

The blues schema is extremely flexible; variations are the norm rather than the exception. The basic framework can be elaborated with ii-Vs, turnarounds, and other embellishments while remaining recognizably "a blues."

## Examples

### Basic

In C:
| C7 | C7 | C7 | C7 |
| F7 | F7 | C7 | C7 |
| G7 | F7 | C7 | C7 |

### From Repertoire

"You Can't Do That" (The Beatles, 1964): Nearly follows the basic form but adds V7 in the final bar as a turnaround.

"Runaway Blues" (Ma Rainey, 1928): Uses IV in the first phrase and embellishes V with applied V/V.

## Common Confusions

- All chords are DOMINANT seventh quality; this differs from tonal music norms
- The V-IV-I motion is NOT "wrong"; it's characteristic of blues (plagal emphasis)
- The form is a SCHEMA; endless variations exist while maintaining blues identity
- "12-bar blues" refers to harmonic structure, not necessarily 12 measures (may have pickup)
- Blues in "major" doesn't mean all pitches fit major scale; blue notes are common
