## Quick Definition

Chord progressions featuring the flat-VII chord with a major tonic, particularly the subtonic shuttle (I-flat-VII) and double plagal (flat-VII-IV-I).

## Formal Definition

Mixolydian schemas employ the characteristic flat^7 (te) scale degree in chord progressions with a major tonic. The flat-VII chord functions as a dominant substitute, providing an alternative to traditional V-I motion. The mode's color note (flat^7) appears in both the flat-VII chord and the minor v chord.

## Musical Context

Mixolydian's flat^7 creates a distinctly different dominant sound than the leading-tone-based V chord. The resulting progressions often sound more "earthy" or "rootsy" than standard major-key progressions.

### Key Schemas

**Subtonic Shuttle (I-flat-VII)**
- Basic alternation between tonic and subtonic
- Can also appear with minor tonic (aeolian version)

**Double Plagal (flat-VII-IV-I)**
- Chain of plagal relationships: flat-VII is IV of IV
- Discussed in detail in Blues-Based Schemas

## Examples

### Basic Example

In C mixolydian:
- Subtonic shuttle: C - Bb - C - Bb (I-flat-VII)
- Double plagal: Bb - F - C (flat-VII-IV-I)

## Common Confusions

- **Mixolydian vs. aeolian subtonic shuttle**: Same chord relationship (I/i - flat-VII), different tonic quality. Mixolydian has major tonic; aeolian has minor tonic
- **flat-VII function**: In mixolydian, flat-VII has dominant function despite lacking the leading tone
- **Mode vs. borrowed chord**: Using flat-VII once doesn't make a song mixolydian; consistent use of flat^7 harmonies indicates the mode
