# Quick Definition
The second subphrase of a sentence, characterized by instability and drive toward a cadence, typically featuring fragmentation, increased rhythmic activity, sequences, and/or increased harmonic rhythm.

# Core Definition
The **continuation** is the second subphrase of a sentence, feeling "less stable than the presentation." It is characterized by four traits: (1) **fragmentation** -- unit sizes shorter than the previously established basic idea; (2) **increased rhythmic activity** -- faster durations; (3) **sequences** -- units repeated and transposed; (4) **increased harmonic rhythm** -- chord changes more frequent. Not all traits need be present. Fragmentation usually signals continuation even in the absence of others, but not all continuations have fragmentation. Continuations always drive toward a cadence (PAC, HC, or IAC). The continuation is typically equal to or longer than the presentation, rarely shorter (Ch. 2, "More Detail: The Continuation").

# Prerequisites
- **Sentence** -- Continuation is the second half of a sentence
- **Presentation** -- The continuation follows the presentation

# Key Properties
1. Second subphrase of a sentence
2. Four characteristic traits: fragmentation, increased rhythmic activity, sequences, increased harmonic rhythm
3. Not all four traits required; any combination possible
4. Fragmentation: unit sizes shorter (not about melodic content)
5. Always drives toward a cadence
6. Equal to or longer than presentation; rarely shorter
7. Harmonically unstable at beginning

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Look for shorter unit sizes than the basic idea (fragmentation)
2. Check for faster rhythmic values, sequences, more frequent chord changes
3. Verify it drives toward a cadence
## Two forms:
- With fragmentation (most common)
- Without fragmentation (labeled "unit" to show it expresses continuation traits)

# Context & Application
The continuation creates the instability and forward drive that makes the sentence an effective formal structure. Its contrast with the stable presentation creates a clear beginning-middle-end trajectory within a single phrase.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- **presentation** -- Continuation follows the presentation
## Contrasts With
- **consequent** -- Consequent begins with b.i. and has stable opening; continuation begins unstably

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming fragmentation refers to melodic content derived from the basic idea
  **Correction**: "Fragmentation refers only to the length of the units" -- not their melodic content

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Every continuation must have all four traits
  **Clarification**: Even one trait can signal continuation
