# Passo Indietro and Comma Schemas

## Quick Definition

Two brief two-stage pre-cadential galant schemas: Passo Indietro ("stepping back") features ti-do (^7-^1) over fa-mi (^4-^3) creating a V4/2-I6 progression that delays strong cadence, while Comma presents fa-mi (^4-^3) over ti-do (^7-^1) creating a V6/5-I progression, both serving as cadential preparation or gentle closing gestures.

## Formal Definition

**Passo Indietro** and **Comma** are brief (two-stage) schemas that appear before or during cadential passages, providing pre-cadential preparation or gentle arrivals on tonic in first inversion.

## Passo Indietro ("Stepping Back")

| Stage | 1 | 2 |
|-------|---|---|
| Meter | S | W |
| Melody | ti (^7) | do (^1) |
| Bass | fa (^4) | mi (^3) |
| Figures | 6/4/2 | 6 |
| Roman numerals | V4/2 | I6 |

**Key characteristics**:
- **"Stepping back"**: Literally "passo indietro" in Italian
- **Two stages**: Shortest schema (just two stages)
- **Pre-cadential**: Often precedes significant cadence
- **V4/2 to I6**: Dominant seventh in third inversion to tonic first inversion
- **Descending bass**: fa-mi (^4-^3), stepwise descent
- **Rising soprano**: ti-do (^7-^1), leading tone resolution
- **Metric pattern**: Strong-weak (S-W)

**Harmonic progression**:
- Stage 1: V4/2 (dominant seventh, third inversion)
- Stage 2: I6 (tonic, first inversion)
- Creates V-I resolution but in inversions
- Weaker than V-I root position (hence "stepping back")

**Function**:
- **Pre-cadential delay**: "Steps back" before major cadence
- **Gentle arrival**: I6 less conclusive than root-position I
- **Dominant prolongation**: V4/2 extends dominant function
- **First two stages of Prinner**: Identical pattern, extended in Prinner

**Relation to Prinner**:
Passo Indietro = first two stages of Prinner:
- Prinner full: fa-mi-re-do (^4-^3-^2-^1) bass
- Passo Indietro: fa-mi (^4-^3) bass only
- Passo Indietro can lead to full Prinner continuation

## Comma

| Stage | 1 | 2 |
|-------|---|---|
| Meter | W | S |
| Melody | fa (^4) | mi (^3) |
| Bass | ti (^7) | do (^1) |
| Figures | 6/5 | 5 |
| Roman numerals | V | I |

**Key characteristics**:
- **"Comma"**: Brief pause or punctuation (like punctuation mark)
- **Two stages**: Brief cadential gesture
- **V6/5 to I**: Dominant to tonic (authentic cadence pattern)
- **Ascending bass**: ti-do (^7-^1), leading tone to tonic
- **Descending soprano**: fa-mi (^4-^3), stepwise descent
- **Metric pattern**: Weak-strong (W-S)

**Harmonic progression**:
- Stage 1: V6/5 (dominant seventh, first inversion)
- Stage 2: I (tonic, root position)
- Authentic cadence but with inverted dominant
- More conclusive than Passo Indietro (I not I6)

**Function**:
- **Gentle cadence**: Brief authentic cadence
- **Phrase punctuation**: Like comma in sentence (pause, not full stop)
- **Cadential formula**: Minimal cadential gesture
- **Weak-to-strong**: Metric pattern supports resolution

**Voice exchange**:
Notice that Comma and Passo Indietro have inverted soprano/bass:
- **Passo Indietro**: Soprano ti-do, Bass fa-mi
- **Comma**: Soprano fa-mi, Bass ti-do
- Voice exchange creates related but distinct schemas

## Comparison

**Similarities**:
- Both two-stage schemas (briefest galant schemas)
- Both involve ti-do and fa-mi scale degrees
- Both provide pre-cadential or cadential function
- Both feature contrary motion

**Differences**:
- **Passo Indietro**: V4/2→I6 (weaker, to first inversion)
- **Comma**: V6/5→I (stronger, to root position)
- **Passo Indietro**: "Steps back" (delays strong cadence)
- **Comma**: Provides gentle authentic cadence
- **Voice placement**: Inverted soprano/bass between them

## Musical Context

These schemas serve specific purposes:
- **Brief gestures**: Provide cadential function in minimal space
- **Pre-cadential preparation**: Lead to stronger cadences
- **Gentle closures**: Less emphatic than full cadences
- **Formal articulation**: Mark phrase boundaries lightly
- **Modularity**: Can be extended or combined with other schemas

Their brevity makes them flexible compositional tools for various contexts.

## Examples

### Basic

**Passo Indietro in C major**:
```
Stage 1 (S): Soprano B4 (ti/^7), Bass F4 (fa/^4), V4/2
Stage 2 (W): Soprano C5 (do/^1), Bass E4 (mi/^3), I6

Soprano rises: B→C (^7→^1, leading tone resolution)
Bass descends: F→E (^4→^3, fa→mi)
Harmonic: V4/2→I6 (weak cadential arrival)
"Steps back" to I6, not strong root-position I
```

**Comma in C major**:
```
Stage 1 (W): Soprano F4 (fa/^4), Bass B3 (ti/^7), V6/5
Stage 2 (S): Soprano E4 (mi/^3), Bass C4 (do/^1), I

Soprano descends: F→E (^4→^3, fa→mi)
Bass rises: B→C (^7→^1, leading tone resolution)
Harmonic: V6/5→I (authentic cadence)
Gentle cadence with inverted dominant
```

**Voice exchange** (Passo Indietro vs. Comma):
```
Passo Indietro:
  Soprano: ti→do (^7→^1)
  Bass:    fa→mi (^4→^3)

Comma:
  Soprano: fa→mi (^4→^3)
  Bass:    ti→do (^7→^1)

Same scale degrees, swapped between voices
```

**Passo Indietro leading to full cadence**:
```
[Passo Indietro: V4/2→I6] → [V→I authentic cadence]
                            ↑
                    "Steps back" then proceeds to strong cadence

I6 from Passo Indietro invites stronger root-position resolution
```

**Passo Indietro = beginning of Prinner**:
```
Passo Indietro: fa-mi (^4-^3) bass, then stops
Prinner full:   fa-mi-re-do (^4-^3-^2-^1) bass

Passo Indietro can lead to Prinner continuation
```

### From Repertoire

**Mozart Piano Sonatas**: Passo Indietro before final cadences, creating brief delay before strong closure.

**Haydn String Quartets**: Comma as gentle phrase ending, particularly in lyrical slow movements.

**Galant keyboard sonatas**: Both schemas appear frequently as brief cadential gestures.

**Mozart Operas**: Comma in recitatives for gentle punctuation between phrases.

**Clementi Sonatinas**: Clear pedagogical examples of both schemas in simple contexts.

## Common Confusions

- "Passo Indietro" (stepping back) describes function—delays strong cadence
- Passo Indietro ≠ backward motion (it's forward to I6, just weaker arrival)
- Passo Indietro = first two stages of Prinner (can continue to full Prinner)
- Comma = gentle cadence (like punctuation mark, not full stop)
- Voice exchange between Passo Indietro and Comma (soprano/bass swap)
- Passo Indietro to I6 (first inversion); Comma to I (root position)
- Both are two-stage schemas (briefest galant schemas)
- Neither provides strongest possible cadence (both use inversions or are brief)
- Passo Indietro often precedes stronger cadence (pre-cadential function)
- Comma can stand alone as gentle close or lead to stronger gestures
- Works in both major and minor modes with appropriate adjustments
- "Passo Indietro" Italian term (literally "step backward" or "stepping back")
- "Comma" refers to brevity and punctuation function (like written comma)
