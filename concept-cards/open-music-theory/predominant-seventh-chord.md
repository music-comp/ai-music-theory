# Quick Definition

Predominant seventh chords (ii7, IV7, vi7, iii7) add a seventh to predominant triads, intensifying their motion to the dominant. The chordal seventh is approached by step or common tone and resolves down by step. ii7 and its inversions are by far the most common.

# Core Definition

Adding a **chordal seventh** to predominant chords can intensify their motion to the dominant (OMT, Ch. IV.13). Two general guidelines for the treatment of the chordal seventh:
1. The chordal seventh is usually approached by step or common tone
2. The chordal seventh resolves down by step

**ii7** and its inversions are the most common. In order of commonality: ii6/5 (most common, substitutes for ii6), ii7 (near phrase endings), ii4/2 (expands tonic at beginnings), ii4/3 (uncommon, at phrase endings). IV7 and vi7 appear in root position; iii7 is very rare.

# Prerequisites

- **Strong predominant** — Predominant sevenths add a seventh to existing PD chords
- **Dominant seventh chord** — Same principle of adding a seventh for intensification

# Key Properties

1. ii7 most common predominant seventh; ii6/5 most common inversion
2. Chordal seventh approached by step or common tone
3. Chordal seventh resolves DOWN by step
4. ii7 to V7: one must be incomplete to avoid voice-leading problems
5. ii4/2 typically expands tonic: I-ii4/2-V6/5-I
6. IV7 and vi7 appear in root position; iii7 is very rare
7. vi7 only connects T to strong PD (not part of deceptive motion)

# Construction / Recognition

## To Write with ii6/5:
1. Follow typical writing procedures
2. Treat chordal seventh: approach by step/common tone, resolve down by step
3. ii6/5 often substitutes for ii6 near phrase endings

## To Write with ii7:
1. Precede with I6 (not I) to avoid parallels
2. When ii7 resolves to V7, one chord must be incomplete

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Not resolving the chordal seventh downward by step
  **Correction**: The seventh MUST resolve down by step in all predominant seventh chords

- **Error**: Using both ii7 and V7 as complete chords in succession
  **Correction**: One must be incomplete to avoid voice-leading problems

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking all predominant seventh inversions are equally common
  **Clarification**: ii6/5 is most common; ii4/3 is least common among ii7 inversions
