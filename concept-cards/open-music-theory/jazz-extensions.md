# Jazz Extensions

## Quick Definition

Notes added to a seventh chord by continuing to stack thirds, producing ninths, elevenths, and thirteenths.

## Formal Definition

**Extensions** in jazz harmony are tones added beyond the seventh of a chord by stacking additional thirds. The primary extensions are:

- **Ninth (9)**: An octave plus a second above the root
- **Eleventh (11)**: An octave plus a fourth above the root
- **Thirteenth (13)**: An octave plus a sixth above the root

Key principles:
- Extensions imply all lower extensions (C13 implies 7, 9, 11, and 13 are available)
- Default qualities: ninth and thirteenth are major; eleventh is perfect
- Alterations shown with sharp/flat: #11, b9, b13, etc.
- Alterations are written after "7" and often in parentheses: C7(#11)

Why compound intervals (9, 11, 13) instead of 2, 4, 6:
1. Presence of an extension implies all extensions below it
2. Extensions are typically voiced above other chord tones, sounding as compound intervals

## Musical Context

Extensions give jazz harmony its characteristic richness and color. While a C7 chord might work in any style, a C13(#11) chord is distinctively jazz. Extensions provide improvisers and accompanists with additional color tones beyond basic chord tones.

Not all implied extensions are necessarily played; performers choose which tones to include based on voicing considerations and desired sound.

## Examples

### Basic

- **C9** = C-E-G-Bb-D (major 9th, 13th implied but often omitted)
- **C11** = C-E-G-Bb-D-F (perfect 11th)
- **C13** = C-E-G-Bb-D-F-A (major 13th)
- **C7(#11)** = C-E-G-Bb with raised 11th (F#)
- **C7(b9)** = C-E-G-Bb-Db (lowered ninth)

### From Repertoire

"Giant Steps" by John Coltrane features complex harmony with various extensions. Jazz standards commonly use 9th, 11th, and 13th chords to create lush harmonies.

## Common Confusions

- C9 is NOT C add9; C9 implies the seventh, Cadd9 does not
- Extensions imply lower extensions but don't require playing all of them
- #11 means "raise the 11th," not necessarily F# (depends on root)
- The eleventh (F natural over C) often clashes with the major third; raising to #11 avoids this
- A 6 chord (C6) does NOT imply a seventh; it's a triad with added sixth
