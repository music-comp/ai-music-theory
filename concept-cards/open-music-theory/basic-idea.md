# Quick Definition
The initial thematic statement in a sentence's presentation or a period's antecedent/consequent, typically two measures long, which is repeated (in sentences) or followed by a contrasting idea (in periods).

# Core Definition
The **basic idea (b.i.)** is the opening unit in both sentences and periods. In a **sentence**, the basic idea appears in the presentation and is followed by its repetition (often varied). In a **period**, the basic idea opens both the antecedent and consequent, followed by contrasting ideas. Basic ideas are often two measures long, but one-measure or four-measure versions occur. The consequent's basic idea is usually similar or identical to the antecedent's. Common transformations of the basic idea include rhythmic/melodic embellishment, transposition, change of harmonization, and change of interval quality/size (Ch. 2).

# Prerequisites
- **Idea level** -- The basic idea operates at the idea level of formal hierarchy

# Key Properties
1. Opening thematic unit (typically 2 measures)
2. In sentences: stated and repeated in the presentation
3. In periods: opens both antecedent and consequent
4. May be varied through transposition, embellishment, re-harmonization
5. Shared contour between basic idea and its repetition suggests sentence
6. Basic idea followed by contrasting idea suggests antecedent or consequent

# Context & Application
The basic idea is the fundamental thematic building block in phrase-level analysis. Identifying it is the first step in determining whether a passage is a sentence, period, or hybrid form.
