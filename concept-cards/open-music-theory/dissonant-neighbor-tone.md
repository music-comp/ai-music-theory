# Quick Definition
A dissonance introduced in third species that ornaments a consonant tone by stepping away to a dissonant note and stepping back to the original consonance (e.g., 6-7-6 over the cantus), best placed on weak beats (2 or 4).

# Core Definition
The **dissonant neighbor tone** "ornaments a consonant tone by stepping away and stepping back to the original consonance (6-7-6 over the cantus, for example)." It is "melodically identical to the consonant neighbor tone of second species, with the difference being the harmonic dissonance." Best placed on weak beats (2 or 4) for greatest smoothness. Unlike the passing tone, which connects two different consonant tones, the neighbor returns to its starting pitch (Ch. 4).

# Prerequisites
- **Third species** -- Dissonant neighbor tones are introduced in third species

# Key Properties
1. Steps away from a consonant tone, then steps back
2. Returns to the original pitch
3. Best on weak beats (2 or 4)
4. Approached and left by step
5. Differs from passing tone: neighbor returns; passing tone continues
