# Fugal Link

## Quick Definition

A short interpolation between subject or answer entries in a fugal exposition, typically 1-4 measures long, using sequential treatment of a motive from the subject to bridge harmonic gaps, change register, vary phrase lengths, or dovetail entries, providing flexibility in voice entry spacing and harmonic progression.

## Formal Definition

A **fugal link** (or simply "link") is a brief connective passage that appears between subject/answer entries within the fugal exposition (and occasionally between middle entries). Links serve practical and musical purposes, facilitating smooth transitions between thematic statements.

**Key characteristics**:
- **Brief duration**: Typically 1-4 measures (shorter than episodes)
- **Location**: Between subject/answer entries in exposition
- **Motivic basis**: Usually derived from subject or countersubject
- **Sequential treatment**: Often features sequence of subject fragment
- **Optional**: Not every fugue has links; depends on compositional needs

**Contrast with episodes**:
- **Link**: Short (1-4 measures), within exposition, connects entries
- **Episode**: Longer (variable), after exposition, developmental function
- Links are transitional; episodes are developmental

**Functions of links**:

1. **Harmonic adjustment**:
   - End of one entry and beginning of next may not match harmonically
   - Link provides harmonic transition between keys/chords
   - Example: Subject ends on V, next entry begins on I; link bridges V→I

2. **Register change**:
   - Continually descending or ascending lines may exceed range
   - Link allows register reset (leap up/down octave)
   - Prevents voices from going too high or too low

3. **Phrase length variation**:
   - Subject of exactly 2 measures creates predictable hypermeter if entries follow every 2 measures
   - Link varies timing of entries (2 + 1 link + 2 = irregular grouping)
   - Creates more interesting hypermetric structure

4. **Dovetailing entries**:
   - Link develops Kopfmotiv sequentially
   - Next subject/answer enters seamlessly within link
   - Creates overlapping effect between link development and new entry
   - Smooth integration rather than clear separation

5. **Voice preparation**:
   - Allows voice to move into appropriate register for next entry
   - Sets up countersubject or free counterpoint
   - Smooths voice leading

**Motivic content**:
- Typically uses short motive from subject (often Kopfmotiv)
- Sequential repetition common
- May use countersubject material
- Maintains thematic unity with exposition

**Harmonic function**:
- Connects two harmonic areas
- May tonicize intermediate key briefly
- Creates smooth harmonic flow between entries
- Avoids abrupt harmonic shifts

**Metric placement**:
- Links usually occur where entries would be metrically comparable
- If subject enters on pickup, link maintains pickup for next entry
- Preserves metric consistency across exposition

## Musical Context

Links serve both practical and aesthetic purposes:
- **Exposition flexibility**: Not every voice can enter immediately after previous
- **Harmonic smoothness**: Links prevent jarring harmonic shifts
- **Register management**: Keeps voices in singable/playable ranges
- **Formal variety**: Prevents mechanical regularity of entries
- **Motivic development**: Early hint of developmental technique (fuller in episodes)

Understanding links helps distinguish between essential exposition structure (subject/answer entries) and connective material.

## Examples

### Basic

**Link for harmonic adjustment** (hypothetical):
```
Subject entry 1 ends: V in C major (dominant)
Link (2 measures): Sequential treatment of subject head, V→I progression
Subject entry 2 begins: I in C major (tonic)

Link bridges harmonic gap between V ending and I beginning
```

**Link for register change**:
```
Subject in bass: Descends from C3 to F2
Link (1 measure): Bass leaps up octave to F3
Subject in tenor: Begins on F3 (appropriate register)

Link resets register to keep bass in comfortable range
```

**Link for phrase variation**:
```
Without link: Subject (2 bars) → Subject (2 bars) → Subject (2 bars) = regular
With link: Subject (2 bars) → Link (1 bar) → Subject (2 bars) → Subject (2 bars) = varied

Irregular grouping (2+1+2) more interesting than regular (2+2+2)
```

**Dovetailing link** (subject overlaps link):
```
Link develops subject Kopfmotiv: C-D-E (sequence) A-B-C (sequence) F-G-A
New subject entry begins: F-G-A-Bb-C...

Subject F-G-A overlaps with link's F-G-A
Seamless integration of link and new entry
```

**Link vs. Episode**:
```
Link: 2 measures between entries 1 and 2 in exposition
Episode: 8 measures after all voices have entered

Link = brief connector
Episode = extended development
```

### From Repertoire

**Bach Fugue in C Minor (BWV 847), WTC Book I**:
- Link between second and third entries (mm. 5-6)
- Uses subject motive sequentially
- Provides harmonic connection and phrase variation

**Bach Fugue in D Major (BWV 850), WTC Book I**:
- Multiple links throughout exposition
- Links create irregular phrase groupings
- Sequential treatment of opening motive

**Bach Fugue in E-flat Major (BWV 852), WTC Book I**:
- Links between entries for register management
- Subject descends; links allow register reset
- Demonstrates practical function of links

**Handel Fugues from Messiah**:
- Links in choral fugues facilitate voice entry spacing
- Harmonic links smooth transitions between entries in different keys

**Bach Art of Fugue, various fugues**:
- Pedagogically clear links demonstrating multiple functions
- Shows how links maintain motivic unity while serving practical purposes

## Common Confusions

- Link ≠ episode (links shorter, in exposition; episodes longer, after exposition)
- Not every fugue has links (optional compositional device)
- Links use subject material (not unrelated music)
- Link vs. countersubject: link is transitional; countersubject is recurring accompaniment
- Links brief (1-4 measures typically), not extended passages
- "Link" is analytical term (composers didn't necessarily think "I'll insert a link here")
- Multiple links possible in one exposition (depends on harmonic/register needs)
- Links can appear between any entries, not just specific positions
- Link ≠ "filler"—serves specific compositional functions
- Some theorists use "codetta" instead of "link" (especially in Italian-influenced theory)
- Links in two-voice fugues still possible (not limited to three+ voices)
- Dovetailing via link different from stretto (stretto = overlapping complete subjects)
