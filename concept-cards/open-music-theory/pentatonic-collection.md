# Pentatonic Collection

## Quick Definition

The pentatonic collection is a five-note pitch collection built on the interval pattern major 2nd - major 2nd - minor 3rd - major 2nd - minor 3rd (2-2-3-2-3 in semitones). Corresponding to the black keys of the piano, it appears in music worldwide. Its lack of semitones makes it rotationally ambiguous--any of its five notes can easily function as a tonic.

## Formal Definition

**Pentatonic collection**: A five-note pitch collection with the following properties:

**Construction methods**:
1. **Major scale degrees**: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 (omitting 4 and 7)
2. **Stack of fifths**: C-G-D-A-E (five consecutive notes in the circle of fifths)
3. **Complement of diatonic**: The black keys are the pentatonic complement of C major's white keys
4. **Interval pattern**: 2-2-3-2-3 (whole step, whole step, minor 3rd, whole step, minor 3rd)

**Key property**: No semitones. The absence of half steps:
- Eliminates leading-tone tendency
- Makes all rotations/modes equally stable
- Creates a "floating" quality

**Five modes** (rotations):
- Mode 1: 2-2-3-2-3 (major pentatonic)
- Mode 2: 2-3-2-3-2
- Mode 3: 3-2-3-2-2
- Mode 4: 2-3-2-2-3
- Mode 5: 3-2-2-3-2 (minor pentatonic)

**Set class**: (02479), Forte number 5-35

## Musical Context

The pentatonic collection appears across virtually every musical culture:
- East Asian music (Chinese, Japanese, Korean)
- African music and African-American traditions
- Celtic and folk music worldwide
- Blues and rock (minor pentatonic)
- Impressionism and 20th-century classical

Its universality suggests something fundamental about the collection--perhaps its derivation from the simplest intervals (stacked fifths) or its consonant, ambiguity-free quality.

**In 20th-century concert music**, pentatonic offers:
- Exotic/folk associations
- Escape from major-minor duality
- Easy combination with other harmonic elements
- Clear melodic identity

**Harmonization**: Only two triads exist within the pentatonic (e.g., C major and A minor in C-D-E-G-A). Composers often harmonize pentatonic melodies with non-pentatonic chords, creating contrast.

## Examples

### Basic

**C major pentatonic**:
```
C - D - E - G - A - (C)
  2   2   3   2   3  (intervals in semitones)

Scale degrees: 1 - 2 - 3 - 5 - 6
Missing: 4 (F) and 7 (B)
```

**A minor pentatonic** (same notes, different starting point):
```
A - C - D - E - G - (A)
  3   2   2   3   2  (intervals in semitones)

This is the most common rotation in blues and rock
```

**Black keys = Gb pentatonic**:
```
Gb - Ab - Bb - Db - Eb - (Gb)
Same interval structure as white-key pentatonic
```

**Pentatonic as subset of modes**:
```
C pentatonic {C, D, E, G, A} is contained in:
- C Ionian (C major)
- C Lydian
- C Mixolydian
- G Ionian, G Lydian, G Mixolydian
- (and several others)

This subset relationship allows pentatonic melodies
to work over various modal harmonies.
```

### From Repertoire

**Debussy, "Pagodes"**: Extended pentatonic passages evoke East Asian music, particularly gamelan.

**Chen Yi, Percussion Concerto (first movement, 2:13)**: Pentatonic melody in strings emerges over atonal brass chords--showing how pentatonic can contrast with other 20th-century techniques.

**Ravel, "Laideronnette, Imperatrice des Pagodes"**: Like Debussy, uses pentatonic for "exotic" effect.

**Bartok**: Often uses pentatonic in connection with Hungarian and other folk music sources.

**Blues and Jazz**: The minor pentatonic with added "blue notes" (b5, sometimes b3) forms the basis of blues melody.

## Common Confusions

- **Pentatonic is not just "Eastern"**: It appears worldwide in virtually all musical cultures
- **Two common modes**: "Major pentatonic" (2-2-3-2-3) and "minor pentatonic" (3-2-2-3-2) are the most used, but all five rotations are valid
- **No semitones**: This is the key feature distinguishing pentatonic from diatonic
- **Not necessarily tonal**: Any note can function as tonic; the collection doesn't privilege one
- **"The black keys" is just one transposition**: There are 12 transpositions of pentatonic, like any collection
- **Only two triads fit**: C-E-G and A-C-E in C pentatonic; harmonization typically involves "outside" notes
- **Pentatonic =/= world music stereotype**: While used for exotic effect, pentatonic has legitimate structural uses in 20th-century composition
