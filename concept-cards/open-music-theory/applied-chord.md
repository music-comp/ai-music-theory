# Quick Definition

An applied chord is any chord that temporarily functions as the dominant or leading-tone chord of a diatonic chord other than the tonic, encompassing secondary dominants (V/x, V7/x) and secondary leading-tone chords (vii-o/x, vii-o7/x), notated with slash notation meaning "of."

# Core Definition

**Applied chord** (also called **secondary chord**) is an umbrella term for any chord creating tonicization (OMT, Ch. IV.15). Two types:

1. **Secondary dominants**: V/x, V7/x — major triad or dominant seventh functioning as dominant of x
2. **Secondary leading-tone chords**: vii-o/x, vii-o7/x — diminished triad or diminished seventh as leading-tone of x

Slash notation: chord before the slash = identity in the secondary key; chord after the slash = target chord being tonicized. The slash is read as "of."

Applied chords can be understood as altered diatonic chords: ii becomes II-sharp (= V/V); I + ^b7 = V7/IV; etc.

# Prerequisites

- **Tonicization** — Applied chords are the mechanism that creates tonicization

# Key Properties

1. Umbrella term covering secondary dominants AND secondary leading-tone chords
2. Notated with slash: V/V = "five of five"
3. Nearly always involve raised accidentals
4. All inversions valid (V6/5/V, vii-o4/3/ii, etc.)
5. Can be understood as chromatically altered diatonic chords
6. Quality determines the label: major/dominant seventh = V; diminished = vii-o

# Construction / Recognition

## To Spell an Applied Chord:
1. Identify the root of the target chord
2. Treat that root as temporary tonic
3. Build V7 or vii-o7 in that temporary key

## To Analyze:
1. Find chromaticism
2. Check chord quality: major/Mm7 = V; diminished = vii-o
3. Determine: in what key is this chord V or vii-o? That key's tonic = the slash target

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Confusing "applied chord" with "secondary dominant"
  **Correction**: "Applied chord" is the umbrella term; "secondary dominant" is one subtype (V/x only)

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Thinking applied chords are borrowed from another key
  **Clarification**: Applied chords are better understood as chromatically altered versions of diatonic chords, not as borrowed chords
