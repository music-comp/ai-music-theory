# Antecedent-Consequent

## Quick Definition

A paired phrase structure where the first phrase (antecedent) poses a musical "question" ending with a weaker cadence (typically half cadence), and the second phrase (consequent) provides a musical "answer" ending with a stronger cadence (typically authentic cadence), creating a complete musical thought through the relationship between opening tension and closing resolution—the fundamental building block of the classical period.

## Formal Definition

**Antecedent-Consequent** is a two-phrase structure that creates a complete musical unit through complementary phrases with contrasting cadential goals, forming the basis of the classical period.

**Antecedent phrase**:
- **Function**: Asks musical "question," creates expectation
- **Cadence**: Weaker cadence (Half Cadence most common, or Imperfect Authentic Cadence)
- **Length**: Typically 4 or 8 measures
- **Character**: Opens and creates forward momentum
- **Harmonic goal**: Dominant (V) or temporary pause

**Consequent phrase**:
- **Function**: Answers musical "question," provides resolution
- **Cadence**: Stronger cadence (Perfect Authentic Cadence most common)
- **Length**: Typically matches antecedent (4 or 8 measures)
- **Character**: Completes and resolves
- **Harmonic goal**: Tonic (I)

**Together they form**: **Period** (complete musical thought)

## Types of Periods

**Parallel period**:
- Antecedent and consequent begin with same or similar melodic material
- Differ primarily in their cadences
- Most common type in Classical music
```
Antecedent: [melodic idea a] → HC
Consequent: [melodic idea a] → PAC
```

**Contrasting period**:
- Consequent begins with different melodic material from antecedent
- Still paired by cadential relationship
- Less common but effective for variety
```
Antecedent: [melodic idea a] → HC
Consequent: [melodic idea b] → PAC
```

**Modulating period**:
- Antecedent modulates to new key (often dominant)
- Consequent cadences in that new key
```
Antecedent: I → [modulation] → V:PAC
Consequent: V → → → V:PAC
```

## Cadential Patterns

**Most common**:
```
Antecedent: HC (half cadence on V)
Consequent: PAC (perfect authentic cadence I)

Creates maximum contrast and resolution
```

**Alternative patterns**:
```
Antecedent: IAC (imperfect authentic cadence)
Consequent: PAC (perfect authentic cadence)

Antecedent: HC
Consequent: IAC (weaker resolution, may continue)

Antecedent: Deceptive (V-vi)
Consequent: PAC
```

**Key principle**: Consequent cadence stronger than antecedent

## Phrase Lengths

**Typical proportions**:
- **4 + 4 measures**: Most common (8-bar period)
- **8 + 8 measures**: Larger scale (16-bar period)
- **Asymmetrical**: Less common (4 + 6, 5 + 7, etc.)

**Hypermetric organization**:
```
Antecedent (4 bars):    1 - 2 - 3 - 4   (HC)
Consequent (4 bars):    1 - 2 - 3 - 4   (PAC)

Creates 8-bar hypermeasure with strong-weak-strong structure
```

## Musical Context

Antecedent-consequent structure serves specific purposes:
- **Question-answer**: Creates conversational quality
- **Tension-resolution**: Antecedent creates tension, consequent resolves
- **Formal balance**: Symmetrical structure provides stability
- **Melodic completion**: Two phrases form complete thought
- **Pedagogical clarity**: Teaches phrase structure and cadential relationships
- **Listener engagement**: Predictable yet satisfying formal pattern

Period structure is fundamental to Classical phrase organization.

## Examples

### Basic

**Simple parallel period** (4 + 4 measures):
```
Antecedent (mm. 1-4):
Melody: C - D - E - F, E - D - C - D
Harmony: I - IV - V - HC (ends on V)

Consequent (mm. 5-8):
Melody: C - D - E - F, E - D - C - C
Harmony: I - IV - V - PAC (ends on I)

Same melodic opening, different cadential goal
```

**Contrasting period**:
```
Antecedent (mm. 1-4):
Melody: [ascending scale C-D-E-F-G]
Harmony: I - V - HC

Consequent (mm. 5-8):
Melody: [descending arpeggio G-E-C]
Harmony: I - V - PAC

Different melodic material, related by cadential pairing
```

**Modulating period**:
```
Antecedent (mm. 1-4, C major):
Begins I (C major), modulates to V (G major)
Ends: V:PAC (cadence in G major)

Consequent (mm. 5-8, G major):
Remains in G major
Ends: V:PAC (confirms G major)

Period establishes new key (tonic → dominant)
```

**Asymmetrical period** (5 + 7 measures):
```
Antecedent (mm. 1-5):
5-bar phrase ending HC

Consequent (mm. 6-12):
7-bar phrase ending PAC (extended for emphasis)

Proportions unequal but cadential relationship intact
```

**Question-answer character**:
```
Antecedent:  "Where are we going?" (HC, V, unresolved)
Consequent:  "Here we are home." (PAC, I, resolved)

HC creates expectation; PAC fulfills expectation
```

### From Repertoire

**Mozart, Piano Sonata in C Major, K. 545, I, mm. 1-8**: Textbook parallel period, antecedent (mm. 1-4) ends HC, consequent (mm. 5-8) ends PAC, nearly identical melodic material.

**Beethoven, "Ode to Joy" theme (Symphony No. 9, IV)**: Clear 4+4 parallel period, antecedent phrases answered by consequent phrases, simple and memorable structure.

**Haydn, Symphony No. 104 ("London"), II, opening**: Parallel period with antecedent ending HC, consequent providing PAC resolution, demonstrates Classical phrase structure.

**Mozart, "Ah, vous dirai-je, Maman" (Twinkle Twinkle Little Star)**: Folk tune with perfect antecedent-consequent structure, first phrase HC, second phrase PAC.

**Clementi, Sonatinas**: Pedagogical works featuring clear period structures, excellent examples for learning antecedent-consequent relationships.

## Common Confusions

- Antecedent-consequent = paired phrases with cadential contrast (weaker → stronger)
- "Question-answer" is metaphor for cadential relationship (not literal questions)
- Antecedent phrase alone is incomplete (requires consequent for closure)
- Period = complete antecedent-consequent pair (not individual phrases)
- Parallel period = melodic similarity; contrasting period = melodic difference
- Both parallel and contrasting periods defined by cadential relationship
- HC-PAC most common pattern (but not only pattern; IAC-PAC also used)
- Consequent cadence must be stronger than antecedent (defining feature)
- Not all paired phrases are antecedent-consequent (need cadential contrast)
- Sentence ≠ period (sentence has different internal organization)
- Hypermetric: 8-bar period functions as single large "measure"
- Proportions typically symmetrical (4+4, 8+8) but can be asymmetrical
- Modulating period changes key; consequent confirms new key
- Period structure fundamental to Classical style (Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven)
- Can be nested within larger forms (periods within sonata exposition, etc.)
- Consequent may be extended (to emphasize resolution or delay closure)
