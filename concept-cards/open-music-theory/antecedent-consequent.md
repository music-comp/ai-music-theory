# Quick Definition
The two phrases of a period: the antecedent ends with a weaker cadence (usually HC) and the consequent ends with a stronger cadence (usually PAC), creating a question-answer or incomplete-to-complete relationship.

# Core Definition
The **antecedent** is the first phrase of a period, ending with a weaker cadence (most often HC). It comprises a basic idea (b.i.) followed by a contrasting idea (c.i.). The **consequent** is the second phrase, ending with a stronger cadence (most often PAC). Its b.i. is usually similar to the antecedent's, but its c.i. must differ to achieve a stronger cadence. "Antecedents are sometimes characterized as 'asking a question' to which the consequent 'provides the answer.'" Consequent is equal to or longer than antecedent. Periods may modulate during the consequent (Ch. 2).

# Prerequisites
- **Period** -- Antecedent and consequent define the period
- **Cadence** -- The cadence pattern (weak→strong) is the defining feature

# Key Properties
1. Antecedent: b.i. + c.i., weaker cadence (usually HC)
2. Consequent: b.i. + c.i., stronger cadence (usually PAC)
3. Consequent's b.i. usually similar to antecedent's
4. Consequent's c.i. differs to achieve stronger cadence
5. Parallel period (norm): consequent begins like antecedent
6. Contrasting period (rare): consequent begins differently

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. Find two cadences where the second is stronger
2. Check if both phrases begin with similar basic ideas
3. Verify each has b.i. + c.i. structure

# Context & Application
The antecedent-consequent relationship creates a satisfying arc from incompleteness to completion. In hybrid forms, antecedents can pair with continuations, and consequents can pair with presentations.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 2, Example 11): Mozart, Clarinet Concerto, III -- HC (antecedent) then PAC (consequent).
