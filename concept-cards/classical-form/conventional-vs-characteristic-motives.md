# Quick Definition
The distinction between melodic material that is unique and thematically identifying (characteristic) and material that is generic and could belong to any number of themes (conventional), with basic ideas tending toward the characteristic and cadential ideas toward the conventional.

# Core Definition
Characteristic melodic-motivic material is unique to a particular theme and identifies it as belonging to a specific work. Conventional material is generic--scales, arpeggios, stock cadential figures--that could close any number of themes. Basic ideas are typically more characteristic, establishing the theme's unique identity. Cadential ideas are typically more conventional, facilitating closure through standard patterns. The process of liquidation moves from characteristic to conventional material across the span of a sentence's continuation phrase. However, the distinction is a continuum: some basic ideas contain conventional elements, and some cadential ideas retain characteristic features.

# Prerequisites
- Basic Idea (often predominantly characteristic)

# Key Properties
- Characteristic: unique motives that identify the theme
- Conventional: generic figures (scales, arpeggios, trills, stock patterns)
- Basic ideas tend toward the characteristic end
- Cadential ideas tend toward the conventional end
- Liquidation progressively replaces characteristic with conventional material
- The distinction is a continuum, not a binary

# Construction / Recognition
1. Ask: is this melodic material unique to this theme, or could it appear in many themes?
2. Characteristic material: distinctive rhythms, intervals, contours
3. Conventional material: scales, arpeggios, repeated notes, stock cadential patterns
4. In a sentence, track the progression from characteristic (basic idea) to conventional (cadential idea)

# Context & Application
This distinction helps explain why continuation phrases often feel less "thematic" than presentations: the progressive replacement of characteristic with conventional material (liquidation) is a hallmark of the continuation process. It also explains why cadential ideas can be interchangeable across themes while basic ideas cannot.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i: basic idea (mm. 1-2) contains the characteristic arpeggiation and stepwise motives; cadential idea (mm. 7-8) uses conventional scale patterns
- The contrasting idea in a period often contains a mix of conventional and characteristic elements

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Basic Idea (typically characteristic)

## Enables
- Liquidation (the process of moving from characteristic to conventional)
- Cadential Idea (typically conventional material)

## Related
- Fragmentation (breaks down characteristic material)

## Contrasts With
- None (this is a spectrum, not an opposition between two concepts)

# Common Errors
- Treating the distinction as absolute; it is a continuum
- Assuming all basic ideas are entirely characteristic; some contain conventional elements

# Common Confusions
- **Conventional vs. "unimportant"**: conventional material is functionally essential for cadential closure; it is not musically insignificant
- **Characteristic motives vs. the basic idea**: individual characteristic motives are components within the basic idea; the basic idea is the larger building block
