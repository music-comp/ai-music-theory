# Quick Definition
Subordinate themes systematically loosen each sentential function through specific techniques: extending or repeating the presentation, weakening its tonic prolongation, extending the continuation with model-sequence technique, and extending/expanding the cadential function through evaded cadences, "one more time" technique, and expanded cadential progressions.

# Core Definition
A subordinate theme usually employs loosening devices that effect structural lengthening (extensions, expansions) and introduce various modes of harmonic destabilization (modal mixture, chromaticism, tonicization). The typical loosening techniques include: (1) extending or repeating a presentation function; (2) weakening the opening tonic prolongation of a presentation (e.g., dominant pedal); (3) extending the continuation function with model-sequence technique; (4) extending the cadential function through evaded cadences and the "one more time" technique; (5) expanding the cadential function by means of expanded cadential progressions; and (6) omitting an initiating function entirely. These techniques create a distinctly looser organization than the main theme while maintaining a coherent succession of formal functions.

# Key Properties
1. Presentation loosening: additional repetition of basic idea; repeated entire presentation; dominant pedal weakening tonic prolongation
2. Continuation loosening: model-sequence technique extends the continuation well beyond 4 mm.
3. Cadential loosening (extension): evaded cadences, deceptive cadences, abandoned cadences, "one more time" technique
4. Cadential loosening (expansion): expanded cadential progressions (ECP) replace standard cadential progressions
5. Initiating function may be omitted entirely (continuation or cadential function begins the theme)
6. Continuation and cadential often occupy separate phrases with different material (unlike tight-knit sentence)
7. Almost always results in the subordinate theme being considerably longer than the main theme
8. Modal shift, chromaticism, and tonicization of remote regions also contribute to loosening

# Construction / Recognition
- Compare the subordinate theme's organization with the main theme's tight-knit model
- Identify which sentential functions are loosened and by what means
- Note whether continuation and cadential are fused (tight-knit) or separate (loose)
- Track cadential extensions: how many attempts before the PAC succeeds?
- Check for ECP (the expanded harmonic progression replacing standard cadential progression)
- Look for modal shift, chromaticism, and tonicization as additional loosening devices

# Context & Application
The subordinate theme's looseness is not a deficiency but a deliberate compositional strategy. All loosening techniques serve to delay and dramatize the arrival of the confirming PAC (the EEC). The longer and more complex the path to the PAC, the more dramatic its eventual achievement. The 19th-century notion of melodic dualism (masculine main theme vs. feminine subordinate theme) has been abandoned in favor of this form-functional understanding of the contrast.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in F minor, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 21-48 (Ch. 9, Ex. 9.3): presentation loosened by dominant pedal; continuation extended well beyond 4 mm.; cadential function in separate phrase with ECP; cadential evasion at m. 37; PAC at m. 41; overall more than 3x the main theme's length

# Common Errors
- Treating looseness as disorganization rather than systematic deployment of specific techniques
- Identifying only one loosening technique when multiple are typically at work simultaneously
- Confusing cadential extension (evaded cadences) with cadential expansion (ECP)

# Common Confusions
- **Extension vs. expansion**: Extension = delaying the cadence through evasion and repetition; expansion = replacing the standard cadential progression with a longer, expanded one
- **Looseness ≠ weakness**: A loosened form is not a weakened form; extensions and expansions intensify the cadential arrival
- **19th-century dualism**: The contrast between main and subordinate themes is form-functional (tight-knit vs. loose), not gendered (masculine vs. feminine)
