# Quick Definition
A coda and a codetta are essentially different: a codetta is a small unit following a PAC within a theme, while a coda is a large section following the recapitulation, operating at the same hierarchical level as the exposition.

# Core Definition
Despite their terminological similarity, a coda and a codetta are essentially different in regard to both hierarchical location and formal organization. A codetta follows a PAC and resides on a hierarchical level comparable to basic, contrasting, and cadential ideas -- a relatively small unit (rarely more than four measures) that prolongs root-position tonic and circles melodically around scale-degree 1. A coda follows a recapitulation and resides on a level comparable to the exposition, development, and recapitulation -- a relatively large unit containing one or more complete coda themes, each ending with a PAC. Despite its diminutive suffix, a codetta is not a "little coda," nor is a coda a large codetta.

# Prerequisites
- **Coda**: The large-scale section following the recapitulation
- **Codetta**: The small unit following a PAC within a theme

# Key Properties
1. Hierarchical difference: codetta at idea level, coda at section level
2. Size difference: codetta rarely exceeds 4 measures; coda contains complete themes
3. Content difference: codetta prolongs I and circles around scale-degree 1; coda contains themes with PACs
4. Location difference: codetta follows PAC within a theme; coda follows the recapitulation
5. A coda itself ends with a closing section made of codettas (hierarchical nesting)
6. The confusion is widespread even among theorists

# Construction / Recognition
- **Codetta**: follows a PAC within a theme; short; prolongs root-position tonic; circles around scale-degree 1
- **Coda**: follows the recapitulation; large; contains complete themes ending with PAC; has its own closing section of codettas

# Context & Application
The distinction matters because confusing a coda with a codetta leads to misidentifying formal boundaries. A closing section made of codettas ends a theme; a coda ends the movement. The hierarchical nesting (codettas within the coda's closing section) demonstrates the structural difference.

# Examples
- Throughout Chapter 12, coda themes are shown to contain their own internal codettas (closing sections), demonstrating the hierarchical nesting

# Common Errors
- Treating a codetta as a "little coda" or a coda as a "big codetta"

# Common Confusions
- **Not a size distinction**: The difference is hierarchical and structural, not merely quantitative
- **Hierarchical nesting**: A coda contains its own codettas, showing they exist at different levels
- **Widespread confusion**: Even theorists have often had trouble distinguishing one from the other
