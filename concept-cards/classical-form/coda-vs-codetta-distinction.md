# Quick Definition
A coda and a codetta are essentially different: a codetta is a small unit following a PAC within a theme, while a coda is a large section following the recapitulation, operating at the same hierarchical level as the exposition.

# Formal Definition
Despite their terminological similarity, a coda and a codetta are essentially different in regard to both hierarchical location and formal organization. A codetta follows a perfect authentic cadence and resides on a hierarchical level comparable to that of basic, contrasting, and cadential ideas. A relatively small unit (rarely more than four measures), a codetta prolongs root-position tonic and circles melodically around ^1. By contrast, a coda follows a recapitulation and resides on a hierarchical level comparable to that of an exposition, development, and recapitulation. A coda is relatively large, containing one or more complete coda themes, each ending with a PAC.

# Construction/Recognition
| Feature | Codetta | Coda |
|---------|---------|------|
| Location | After PAC within a theme | After the recapitulation |
| Hierarchical level | Same as basic ideas | Same as exposition/development |
| Size | Rarely more than 4 measures | Multiple themes, often extensive |
| Content | Prolongs root-position I, circles around ^1 | Complete themes with PACs, closing section |
| Internal structure | Single, small unit | One or more coda themes + codettas |

# Musical Context
The distinction matters because confusing a coda with a codetta leads to misidentifying the formal boundaries of a movement. A closing section made of codettas ends a theme; a coda ends the movement.

# Common Confusions
- Despite the diminutive suffix, a codetta is not a "little coda" — they are structurally different
- A coda itself ends with a closing section made up of codettas, showing the hierarchical nesting
- The confusion is widespread even among theorists
