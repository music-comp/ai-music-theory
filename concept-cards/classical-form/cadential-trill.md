# Quick Definition
The conventional trill performed by the soloist at the end of the solo exposition and solo recapitulation, signaling the final cadential arrival and the transition to an orchestral ritornello.

# Formal Definition
The cadential trill is the conventional sign for the close of the solo exposition and the solo recapitulation in concerto form. The rhythmic and dynamic climax of the exposition is normally reached during the expansion of the final cadential dominant, in which the shortest durational values culminate in this trill. The cadential trill marks the final PAC of the subordinate-theme group, after which the orchestral ritornello begins (subordinate-key ritornello after the exposition, closing ritornello after the recapitulation). The trill elides with the beginning of the subsequent ritornello.

# Construction/Recognition
- Trill performed by the soloist on the penultimate note of the cadential progression
- Marks the rhythmic and dynamic climax of the solo section
- Located at the end of the solo exposition and solo recapitulation
- Elides with the beginning of the subsequent orchestral ritornello
- Signals the transition from solo to orchestral texture
- Occurs over the dominant at the moment of cadential resolution

# Musical Context
The cadential trill is a quintessential element of concerto style that has no exact equivalent in other forms. It serves as both a musical climax and a formal signal, marking the boundary between solo and orchestral sections.

# Examples
- Haydn, Trumpet Concerto in E-flat, Hob. VIIe:1, i, m. 83: Cadential trill elides with the beginning of the subordinate-key ritornello
- Mozart, Piano Concerto in E-flat, K. 482, i: Cadential trill at the end of the solo exposition and recapitulation

# Common Confusions
- The cadential trill is not merely an ornament; it is a structural marker that signals the end of the solo section and the transition to the orchestral ritornello.
- Students should not confuse the cadential trill (which ends the solo exposition/recapitulation) with trills elsewhere in the movement that serve purely ornamental purposes.
