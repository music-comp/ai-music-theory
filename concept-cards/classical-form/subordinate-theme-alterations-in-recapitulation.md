# Quick Definition
Changes to the subordinate theme in the recapitulation, ranging from simple transposition into the home key to major restructuring in monothematic expositions, two-part subordinate themes, and modulating subordinate themes.

# Formal Definition
As a general rule, the subordinate theme returns as it appeared in the exposition except for being tonally adjusted into the home key. Mozart and Beethoven follow this practice as a matter of course, with only occasional significant structural changes. Haydn, by contrast, regularly alters this area. Standard additions include expanded cadential areas (giving the home key's final confirmation the most powerful cadential emphasis) and deletions of repeated material.

# Construction/Recognition
- **Standard practice**: transposition to home key with minimal structural changes; register adjustments as needed
- **Standard additions**: expanded cadential area, giving home-key PAC greater emphasis than the subordinate-key PAC had in the exposition
- **Standard deletions**: material stated more than once in the exposition
- **Major alterations triggered by**:
  - Monothematic exposition: basic idea must be altered to avoid redundancy with the main theme
  - Two-part subordinate theme: internal HC may need to be eliminated to avoid overemphasizing home-key dominant
  - Modulating subordinate theme: requires complex tonal adjustment, sometimes beginning in a non-tonic region

# Musical Context
The subordinate theme's transposition into the home key resolves the fundamental tonal conflict of the movement. Its retained loose organization now serves to endow the home key with the greatest power of expression, rather than establishing a rival key.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C, K. 545, i (ex. 11.10): subordinate theme transposed to C major; melody adjusted for register; cadential phrase ornamentally changed for greater intensity
- Mozart, Violin Concerto in A, K. 219, i (ex. 11.11): cadential phrase expanded from 5 to 7 measures with virtuosic writing
- Beethoven, Overture to Coriolanus, Op. 62 (ex. 11.14): modulating subordinate theme reorganized harmonically to arrive on home-key dominant

# Common Confusions
- The subordinate theme is generally the least altered section — but Haydn is a significant exception
- Expanded cadential areas in the recapitulation serve to give the home key more emphasis than the subordinate key received in the exposition
- Modulating subordinate themes require the most complex adjustments, sometimes beginning "off tonic"
