# Quick Definition
Changes to the subordinate theme in the recapitulation, ranging from simple transposition into the home key (the norm for Mozart and Beethoven) to major restructuring triggered by monothematic expositions, two-part subordinate themes, or modulating subordinate themes.

# Core Definition
As a general rule, the subordinate theme returns as it appeared in the exposition except for being tonally adjusted into the home key. Mozart and Beethoven follow this practice as a matter of course; only occasionally do their subordinate themes exhibit significant structural changes. Haydn, on the contrary, regularly alters this area. Standard additions include expanded cadential areas (giving the home key's final confirmation the most powerful cadential emphasis in the form). Standard deletions involve material stated more than once. Three compositional situations regularly lead to major alterations: monothematic expositions (where the basic idea would redundantly reappear in the home key), two-part subordinate themes (where internal HC would overemphasize home-key dominant), and modulating subordinate themes (requiring complex tonal reorganization).

# Key Properties
1. General rule: subordinate theme returns essentially intact, transposed to home key
2. Mozart and Beethoven rarely make significant structural changes
3. Haydn regularly alters the subordinate theme area
4. Cadential area often undergoes greater expansion than in the exposition
5. The most powerful cadential emphasis is now given to the home key, not the subordinate key
6. Register adjustments may be needed to keep melodies in practical range
7. Monothematic expositions require the basic idea to be altered or eliminated
8. Two-part subordinate themes may lose the first part or internal HC
9. Modulating subordinate themes require complex harmonic reorganization
10. The subordinate theme's retained loose organization now endows the home key with greatest expressive power

# Construction / Recognition
- Compare subordinate theme in exposition and recapitulation
- Check for transposition to home key with register adjustments
- Look for expanded cadential area (more measures devoted to cadential function)
- In monothematic expositions, check whether the basic idea is altered or deleted
- In two-part subordinate themes, check whether part 1 or the internal HC has been eliminated
- In modulating themes, trace the harmonic reorganization

# Context & Application
The subordinate theme resolves the fundamental tonal conflict when transposed into the home key. Its loose organization, which in the exposition endowed the subordinate key with temporal weight, now serves the home key. This is especially characteristic of the concerto, where the dramatic cadential peroration features virtuosic "brilliant" style passage-work. Expanded cadential areas in the recapitulation ensure the most powerful cadential emphasis in the form is accorded to the home key.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C, K. 545, i (ex. 11.10): transposed to home key; melody down a fifth then up a fourth for register; cadential phrase has ornamental changes for greater intensity
- Mozart, Violin Concerto in A, K. 219, i (ex. 11.11): cadential phrase expanded from 5 to 7 measures; virtuosic highest tessitura
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in D, K. 576, i (ex. 11.12): monothematic -- second subordinate theme used to avoid redundancy; first subordinate theme interpolated later as sequential passage; resulting cadential area more expansive than either exposition theme
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 31/3, ii (ex. 11.13): modulating theme begins "off tonic" in bVII and "modulates" back to tonic
- Beethoven, Overture to Coriolanus, Op. 62 (ex. 11.14): modulating theme reorganized to arrive on home-key dominant

# Common Errors
- Assuming the subordinate theme is always significantly altered (it is usually the least altered section)
- Failing to note that Haydn is the exception who regularly alters this area

# Common Confusions
- **Mozart/Beethoven vs. Haydn**: The first two rarely make significant structural changes; Haydn regularly does
- **Expanded cadential area**: Not a deviation but a standard practice -- it gives the home-key PAC more emphasis than the exposition's subordinate-key PAC
- **Monothematic complication**: Simply omitting the basic idea at the start of the subordinate theme does not suffice; the theme must be more substantially altered
