# Quick Definition
The distinction between measures as printed in the score (notated) and the actual perceived measure lengths based on harmonic rhythm and hypermeter (real measures).

# Formal Definition
Notated measures are the bar lines in the score. Real measures reflect the actual perceived unit of metric organization based on harmonic rhythm, melodic patterning, and hypermetric grouping. Two notated measures may function as a single real measure (common in fast tempos), while a single notated measure may contain two real measures (common in slow tempos). The normative eight-measure sentence or period refers to real measures, not necessarily notated ones.

# Construction/Recognition
- Compare the rate of harmonic change to the notated bar lines
- In fast 3/4 (minuets, scherzos), two notated measures often equal one real measure
- In slow movements, one notated measure may contain two real measures
- The normative measures for formal types refer to real measures

# Musical Context
This distinction is critical for applying Caplin's formal categories. A minuet notated in sixteen measures of 3/4 may actually be an eight-real-measure sentence if the harmonic rhythm moves in two-bar units.

# Examples
- A minuet in 3/4 where harmonies change every two bars: each pair of notated measures = one real measure
- A slow movement in 4/4 with two harmonies per bar: each notated measure = two real measures

# Common Confusions
- Students often count notated measures literally and become confused when a theme has "too many" or "too few" measures for its formal type. The solution is to identify real measures based on harmonic rhythm.
