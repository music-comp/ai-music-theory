# Quick Definition
A two-measure unit that introduces the fundamental melodic-motivic material of a theme, serving as the building block from which the theme develops.

# Formal Definition
The basic idea is normatively a two-measure unit that brings in the fundamental melodic material of a theme. It frequently contains several distinct motives developed in the course of the theme. The basic idea is the fundamental building block of the theme (not the individual motive). Its characteristic motives define the uniqueness of the theme: a basic idea is immediately identifiable as belonging to a specific work.

# Construction/Recognition
- Typically two measures long (in real measures)
- Contains one or more distinct motives
- Supported by tonic harmony (in presentation and antecedent contexts)
- Its boundaries are confirmed through repetition
- Distinguished from the contrasting idea by its role as the primary melodic-motivic identity

# Musical Context
The basic idea opens sentences (where it is repeated to form a presentation) and periods (where it is followed by a contrasting idea in the antecedent). It returns at the start of consequent phrases and recapitulation sections. Detailed treatment in Chapter 3.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 1-2: contains motives "a" and "b" within a single gesture
- Beethoven, Op. 135, iii, mm. 3-4: the basic idea proper, preceded by a two-measure introduction
- Mozart, K. 525, ii, mm. 1-2: basic idea of the antecedent phrase

# Common Confusions
- The basic idea, not the motive, is the fundamental building block. Motives are components within the basic idea.
- A basic idea by itself does not indicate sentence vs. period; what follows determines the theme type.
