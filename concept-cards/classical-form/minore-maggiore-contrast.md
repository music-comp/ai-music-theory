# Quick Definition
The contrast created by shifting between major and minor modes of the same tonic, used prominently in interior themes and in theme-and-variations movements.

# Formal Definition
Minore/maggiore contrast is a technique of modal opposition within a movement that retains the same tonal center. In large ternary slow movements, the interior theme shifts immediately to the minor mode of the home key (labeled "minore"). In movements whose main theme is in minor, the interior theme shifts to major ("maggiore"). This contrast also appears as a standard feature of theme-and-variations movements, where one variation is written in the opposite mode.

# Construction/Recognition
- In large ternary: the interior theme begins immediately in the opposite mode
- The tonality (tonic pitch) generally remains the same; only the mode changes
- In theme and variations: typically one variation is designated "minore" or "maggiore"
- The modal shift may necessitate reharmonization and altered phrase functions

# Musical Context
This technique is pervasive in classical slow movements and variation sets. The minor mode typically brings a more intense, Sturm und Drang affect.

# Examples
- Haydn's large ternary slow movements: interior themes consistently shift to minore
- Haydn's alternating variations: the second theme is typically a minore

# Common Confusions
- Minore/maggiore contrast involves a change of mode while typically retaining the same tonic. This is different from modulation to a new key.
