# Quick Definition
The A' section of minuet form, which recapitulates the A section material but is seldom shorter and is often significantly expanded through loosening devices, deceptive cadences, and sometimes a new closing section.

# Core Definition
The A' section of minuet form recapitulates the material of the exposition with necessary tonal adjustments. Unlike a small ternary, which frequently contains a compressed recapitulation, the minuet's recapitulation is seldom shorter than the earlier section. It is more likely to be significantly expanded through loosening devices typical of a subordinate theme, interpolated episodes, and the deceptive cadence, which is perhaps found more often in minuets than in sonatas (where evaded cadences are more common). The expansion provides the home key with enough space to overcome the competing tonalities introduced earlier and may function as a kind of "recapitulation of the subordinate theme" when the B section has expressed that function (Caplin, pp. 646-649).

# Key Properties
1. Seldom shorter than the A section (unlike in small ternary)
2. Often significantly expanded
3. Expansion created by loosening devices typical of subordinate themes
4. Deceptive cadence is a favorite device for extending the form
5. New material may appear in an interpolated episode
6. May include a new closing section not found in the exposition
7. May separate fused functions (e.g., transition and subordinate theme) into distinct units

# Construction / Recognition
1. Compare with the A section to identify retained and altered material
2. Look for tonal adjustments to keep material in the home key
3. Identify expansion techniques: deceptive cadences, interpolated episodes, new closing sections
4. Check whether previously fused functions are now separated into distinct thematic units
5. Assess whether the expansion compensates for a B section subordinate-theme function

# Context & Application
The tendency to expand the minuet recapitulation serves two purposes. First, if the A section is shorter than the B section, a short A' would not give the home key sufficient space to resolve tonal conflict. Second, the expansion can provide a "recapitulation of the subordinate theme" to match subordinate-theme function expressed in the B section, even though the A' models itself on the A section rather than on the B section.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 632): Haydn, String Quartet in G, Op. 54, No. 1, iii, mm. 33-44 -- A' section extended by deceptive cadence at m. 34 and subsequent development of motives.
**Example 2** (p. 648): Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, iii, mm. 51-95 -- Massively expanded recapitulation separating previously fused transition and subordinate-theme functions into distinct units.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Expecting a compressed recapitulation as in a small ternary. **Correction**: The minuet recapitulation is almost never compressed and is often substantially expanded.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Why the A' section is so much longer than the A section. **Clarification**: The expansion compensates for tonal conflict by giving the home key more rhetorical weight and may implicitly recapitulate subordinate-theme function.
