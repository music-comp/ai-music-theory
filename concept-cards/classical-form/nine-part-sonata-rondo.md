# Quick Definition
An expanded sonata-rondo form with an additional refrain and couplet inserted between couplet 2 and the recapitulation, creating a nine-part structure. Found exclusively in Mozart.

# Formal Definition
Just as the five-part rondo can be expanded into a seven-part rondo, the sonata-rondo can be expanded into a nine-part sonata-rondo by adding an extra refrain and couplet between couplet 2 and the recapitulation. At least three examples in the classical repertoire, all by Mozart, feature this elaborate formal design. In all three cases, both couplet 2 and the added couplet are organized as interior themes, exploring all three standard tonal regions (minore, vi, IV). The last couplet recapitulates the subordinate theme.

# Construction/Recognition
- Nine sections: Refrain 1 - Couplet 1 - Refrain 2 - Couplet 2 - Refrain 3 - Couplet 3 (added) - Refrain 4 - Couplet 4 (recap of subordinate theme) - Refrain 5 (coda)
- The added refrain and couplet appear between the second couplet and the recapitulation
- Both couplet 2 and the added couplet are organized as interior themes
- All three standard interior-theme regions are explored (minore, vi, IV)
- Found exclusively in Mozart
- Extremely rare

# Musical Context
The nine-part sonata-rondo represents the most elaborate version of rondo form in the classical repertoire. Its rarity reflects its exceptional formal complexity. With the exception of a true development, Mozart's most impressive example (K. 494/K. 533) features all the standard options for couplets in the classical rondo.

# Examples
- Mozart, Rondo for Piano in F, K. 494 (later published as the last movement of Piano Sonata in F, K. 533): Couplet 1 = subordinate theme (no transition); Couplet 2 = double-region couplet (vi followed by IV); added couplet = minore interior theme; last couplet recapitulates subordinate theme, with an elaborate written-out cadenza before its close

# Common Confusions
- The nine-part sonata-rondo is extremely rare (only three known examples by Mozart). Students should not expect to encounter this form regularly.
- The structure should not be confused with a seven-part rondo (ABACADA), which lacks the sonata-rondo's recapitulation of the subordinate-theme complex.
