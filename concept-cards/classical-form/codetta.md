# Quick Definition
An individual unit within a closing section that prolongs tonic harmony following a PAC, ranging from a single chord to a four-measure phrase, with melodic activity centering on the tonic scale-degree.

# Core Definition
"An individual codetta can be as short as a single chord or as long as a full four-measure phrase" (Ch. 1, p. 16). "Codettas usually contain melodic-motivic material different from that found in the theme itself, though, at times, material from the opening basic idea or from the closing cadential idea may be reused within the codetta" (Ch. 1, p. 16). "Melodic activity tends to center on the tonic scale-degree in order to preserve the melodic closure achieved by the cadence and to prevent the codetta from sounding like a new beginning" (Ch. 1, p. 16). Harmonically, "a codetta prolongs the root-position tonic of the cadence" through pedal, I-V alternation, or "local tonicization of subdominant harmony" (Ch. 1, p. 16).

# Prerequisites
- **Postcadential function** -- codettas are the building blocks of postcadential closing sections

# Key Properties
1. Prolongs root-position tonic after a PAC
2. Length ranges from a single chord to a four-measure phrase
3. Melodic content centers on scale-degree 1
4. Usually different melodic material from the theme, though reuse of basic or cadential idea motives is possible
5. Harmonic prolongation via tonic pedal, I-V alternation, or tonicized IV
6. May contain a cadential progression internally (but remains postcadential in function)
7. Multiple codettas appear in sequence with repetition and fragmentation

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify material following a PAC that prolongs tonic
- Check that melodic activity centers on scale-degree 1
- Look for repetition and fragmentation patterns among codettas

# Context & Application
Codettas form the building blocks of closing sections after PACs in subordinate themes, main themes, and other formal sections. Their postcadential function is distinct from cadential function even when they contain internal cadential progressions.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 1, p. 17, ex. 1.6): Beethoven, Op. 135, iii, mm. 10-13: one-measure codetta (cadential melody transferred to cello), repeated (transferred back to upper voice, second half of m. 11), then fragmented to half-measure codetta over tonic pedal with neighboring IV (m. 12-13).

# Common Errors
- Calling a codetta "cadential" because it contains a cadential progression

# Common Confusions
- A codetta may resemble a cadential idea but serves a postcadential (not cadential) function
- "Whereas an individual codetta may indeed resemble a cadential idea, these two units of musical form remain conceptually (and experientially) distinct" (Ch. 1, p. 17)
