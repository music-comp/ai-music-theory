# Quick Definition
An individual unit within a closing section that prolongs tonic harmony following a PAC, ranging from a single chord to a four-measure phrase.

# Formal Definition
A codetta is the constituent unit of a closing section. It can be as short as a single chord or as long as a full four-measure phrase. Codettas usually contain different melodic material from the theme, though basic idea or cadential idea motives may be reused. Melodic activity centers on ^1 to preserve closure. Harmonically, codettas prolong root-position tonic through pedal, I-V alternation, or local tonicization of IV.

# Construction/Recognition
- Prolongs root-position tonic after a PAC
- Melodic content centers on ^1
- May contain a cadential progression internally (but remains postcadential in function)
- Multiple codettas appear in sequence with repetition and fragmentation

# Musical Context
Codettas form the building blocks of closing sections after PACs in subordinate themes, main themes, and other sections.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 135, iii, mm. 10-13: one-measure codetta (cadential melody in cello), repeated (m. 11), then fragmented to half-measure codetta over tonic pedal with neighboring IV

# Common Confusions
- A codetta may resemble a cadential idea but serves a postcadential (not cadential) function.
