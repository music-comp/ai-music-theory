# Quick Definition
A full-movement tripartite form used almost exclusively in slow movements, consisting of a main theme, an interior theme (often in the opposite mode), and a return of the main theme.

# Formal Definition
The large ternary form is a three-part structure used at the full-movement level, distinct from the small ternary despite superficial similarities. Its three parts are: (1) a main theme that begins and ends in the home key with a PAC, usually constructed as a small ternary or small binary; (2) an interior theme that contrasts through a change in modality (typically to the minor mode, labeled "minore"), residing in the home key but in its opposite mode; and (3) a return of the main theme, essentially restating the first part with possible ornamental changes. Unlike the small ternary, the functional labels of exposition, contrasting middle, and recapitulation do not apply.

# Construction/Recognition
- First part: main theme in a conventional form (small ternary or small binary), beginning and ending in the home key with a PAC
- Second part: interior theme, usually in the minor mode of the home key (minore)
- Third part: return of the main theme, following the same plan as the first part, often with ornamental embellishments
- The interior theme never resides in the standard subordinate key
- An optional coda may follow, often referring to material from the interior theme

# Musical Context
This form is used almost exclusively in slow movements. It appears most often in Haydn's works, with examples in Mozart and Beethoven.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Trio in A-flat, Hob. XV:14, ii: Interior theme as a small binary with reopened closure
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Hob. XVI:52, ii: Interior theme with A' section lacking cadential closure
- Haydn, String Quartet in B-flat, Op. 64/3, ii: Interior theme as truncated small ternary

# Common Confusions
- Small ternary and large ternary are fundamentally different forms. In a small ternary, the A section may modulate; in a large ternary, the first part always begins and ends in the home key. The B section of a small ternary typically ends on dominant; the interior theme of a large ternary frequently closes with tonic harmony.
- The third part is better described as a "return" than a "recapitulation," since no tonal adjustment is needed.
