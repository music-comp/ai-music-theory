# Quick Definition
Phrases within themes may deviate from their theoretically defined lengths through four standard techniques: extension (adding extra material), expansion (internally lengthening constituent members), compression (shortening constituent members), and interpolation (inserting unrelated material between two functions).

# Core Definition
Phrases of themes may deviate from their "theoretically defined" lengths. The ideas within the phrases may also be altered. These deviations are best described in reference to the formal functions affected by the various techniques. The four standard techniques are extension, expansion, compression, and interpolation. The notion of formal deviation is not meant in any pejorative or negative sense -- on the contrary, many deviations are of special aesthetic interest and beauty. The concept of deviation refers to situations that depart from theoretical models for a variety of appropriate compositional goals, and is distinct from Hepokoski and Darcy's concept of "deformation," which refers to more extreme transgressions.

# Prerequisites
- **Sentence**: One of the simple theme types subject to deviation
- **Period**: One of the simple theme types subject to deviation
- **Formal function**: The framework for understanding which functions are affected

# Key Properties
1. **Extension**: Extra material added to stretch out a function; brings more material than necessary; often possible to reconstruct a normative version by eliminating the extension
2. **Expansion**: Constituent members are internally lengthened (hemiola is a common mechanism); analogous to inflating a balloon
3. **Compression**: Constituent members shortened relative to norms; harder to reconstruct a normative version
4. **Interpolation**: Entirely new, unrelated material inserted between two logically succeeding functions; can be eliminated without disturbing syntax
5. Extension = tying small balloons together; Expansion = blowing up one balloon
6. The cadential function is most typically subjected to expansion
7. Continuation function, with its inherent fragmentation and acceleration, is less suited to expansion
8. Deviations may result in symmetrical (5+5, 6+6) or asymmetrical (4+6) grouping structures

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the normative phrase structure expected for the theme type
2. Note any departures from normative length
3. Classify the deviation: Is material added on (extension)? Internally enlarged (expansion)? Shortened (compression)? Inserted from outside (interpolation)?
4. Consider which formal function is affected by the deviation
5. Extension: the added material continues to express the ongoing function
6. Interpolation: the added material does NOT belong to either surrounding function
7. When in doubt, opt for extension rather than interpolation

# Context & Application
Phrase deviations are routine throughout the classical repertory and are far less extreme than the "deformations" described by Hepokoski and Darcy. In the period, deviations frequently take place symmetrically (if the antecedent is lengthened, the consequent is too), preserving the equilibrium of the form. In the sentence, with its forward-striving character, asymmetrical deviations are more natural. All standard phrase functions (presentation, continuation, cadential, antecedent, consequent) can be affected, though some are more naturally suited to certain techniques.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in F, K. 280, i, mm. 1-13: continuation extended with additional fragmentation and harmonic acceleration material; evaded cadence extends further
- Mozart, Symphony No. 40 in G Minor, K. 550, iii, mm. 1-14: basic idea expanded by hemiola (3/2 against 3/4); expanded ideas motivate extended continuation
- Haydn, String Quartet in B-flat, Op. 50/1, ii, mm. 1-6: continuation compressed to just two measures
- Mozart, Piano Concerto in F, K. 459, ii, mm. 1-10: ascending chromatic figure interpolated between basic idea and contrasting idea

# Common Errors
- Using "deviation" as a negative judgment -- Caplin explicitly states it is not pejorative
- Confusing extension with interpolation -- extension continues the ongoing function; interpolation inserts unrelated material
- Assuming all non-normative themes result from a single deviation technique -- multiple techniques may combine

# Common Confusions
- Extension vs. expansion: extension = adding on units (tying balloons together); expansion = swelling a unit from inside (blowing up one balloon)
- Extension vs. interpolation: extension continues the ongoing function; interpolation inserts material that belongs to neither surrounding function; when in doubt, opt for extension
- Deviation vs. deformation: deviation is routine and modest; deformation (Hepokoski/Darcy) stretches norms to their maximally expected limits or beyond
