# Quick Definition
A complete thematic unit within the coda, usually organized loosely like a subordinate theme, ending with a PAC in the home key.

# Formal Definition
The constituent coda themes are usually built in ways that resemble the loose organization of a subordinate theme, especially because of a highly expanded cadential area. Occasionally, however, a coda theme is structured as a simple tight-knit type (sentence, period, or hybrid), particularly in sonata-rondo form. Some coda themes are based on the main theme and may duplicate its form. Most coda themes close with a PAC in the home key; their formal organization thus resembles either a main theme (if tight-knit) or a subordinate theme (if loose).

# Construction/Recognition
- Usually loosely organized (like a subordinate theme) with expanded cadential area
- Occasionally tight-knit (sentence, period, hybrid), especially in sonata-rondo
- Ends with PAC in home key (resembling main or subordinate theme organization)
- Some units conclude with HC or dominant arrival, resembling a transition or development passage
- A coda may contain multiple coda themes
- Material drawn from anywhere in the movement (main theme, subordinate theme, development, or closing section)

# Musical Context
Coda themes provide the thematic substance of the coda. A typical coda contains one to three coda themes followed by a final closing section of codettas. The themes allow the composer to treat material in ways not possible during the recapitulation.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Overture to Coriolanus, Op. 62 (ex. 12.6): first coda theme (m. 244) with compound basic idea from subordinate theme, presentation, extended continuation with ascending sequence; second coda theme (m. 276) with main-theme material
- Haydn, Symphony No. 104 ("London"), iv (ex. 12.7): first coda theme (m. 276) with new presentation and expanded cadential progression (PAC m. 287); second coda theme (m. 287) with modal shift to minor, internal dominant arrival, standing on dominant, expanded cadential progression (PAC m. 309)

# Common Confusions
- Coda themes are not simply "extra themes" — they serve specific compensatory functions within the coda
- A tight-knit coda theme does not mean the coda is tight-knit as a whole
- Coda themes ending with HC function like transitions or developmental passages, not like main themes
