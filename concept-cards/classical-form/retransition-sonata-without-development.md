# Quick Definition
A transitional passage following the exposition's closing section in a sonata without development, leading the music without a break into the recapitulation.

# Formal Definition
In a sonata without development, a retransition regularly appears after the closing section of the exposition in order to lead the music, without a break, into the recapitulation. This retransition serves a function analogous to the retransition that smooths the connection between a sonata exposition and its repetition. Because the development section is absent, this retransition is essential for creating continuity.

# Construction/Recognition
- Located between the closing section and the recapitulation
- Smooths the tonal transition from subordinate key to home key
- Usually ends on the dominant of the home key

# Musical Context
This retransition is a standard feature of sonata-without-development form.
