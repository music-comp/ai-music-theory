# Quick Definition
A transitional passage following the exposition's closing section in a sonata without development, leading the music without a break into the recapitulation.

# Core Definition
In a sonata without development, a retransition regularly appears after the closing section of the exposition in order to lead the music, without a break, into the recapitulation. This retransition serves a function analogous to the retransition that smooths the connection between a sonata exposition and its repetition. Because the development section is absent, this retransition is essential for creating continuity between the exposition and the recapitulation.

# Prerequisites
- **Sonata without development**: The formal context
- **Retransition**: The general concept being applied

# Key Properties
1. Located between the closing section and the recapitulation
2. Smooths the tonal transition from subordinate key to home key
3. Usually ends on the dominant of the home key
4. Essential for continuity in the absence of a development

# Construction / Recognition
- Locate the closing section at the end of the exposition
- Identify the passage connecting it to the recapitulation
- Note the harmonic motion from subordinate key back to home-key dominant

# Context & Application
This retransition is a standard feature of sonata-without-development form, providing the continuity that a development section would otherwise supply.

- Retransition in regular sonata form (which follows the development, not the exposition)

# Common Errors
- Confusing this retransition with the beginning of a development section

# Common Confusions
- **Retransition vs. development**: This retransition leads to the recapitulation, not a repeat of the exposition; its brevity and purpose distinguish it from a development
