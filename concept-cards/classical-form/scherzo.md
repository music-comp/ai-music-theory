# Quick Definition
A variant style of the minuet featuring a faster tempo and livelier character, following the same formal organization -- no reliable formal distinction exists between scherzos and minuets despite attempts to define a separate "Scherzoform."

# Core Definition
The scherzo is a variant style of the minuet with a faster tempo and livelier character. Despite Schoenberg's and Ratz's attempts to identify a distinct "Scherzoform" (characterized by a modulatory contrasting middle using model-sequence technique in the manner of a developmental core), no reliable formal distinction exists. Many movements designated "Scherzo" seem identical to minuets in formal organization, while many "Menuetto" movements are clearly in scherzo style. Schoenberg himself reverses his position, acknowledging that model-sequence technique "often resembles the B-section of the minuet, while many minuets possess a modulatory contrast." Caplin treats the two as a single movement type in regard to formal organization, using "scherzo" only where the style is unmistakable.

# Prerequisites
- **Minuet form**: The formal organization shared by both types
- **Minuet/trio form**: The full-movement context

# Key Properties
1. Faster tempo and livelier character than a standard minuet
2. Same formal organization as minuet form
3. Score labels ("Scherzo" vs. "Menuetto") are unreliable as formal criteria
4. Intense rhythmic drive with continuous motion
5. Closing sections in scherzos often maintain prevailing rhythmic drive rather than introducing new material
6. Became increasingly common in Beethoven's works

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify by tempo and character rather than formal organization
- Note the intense rhythmic drive and continuous motion
- The closing section often recycles cadential material to maintain drive

# Context & Application
The scherzo became increasingly prominent in Beethoven's works, replacing the minuet in many instrumental cycles. The distinction is one of style and character, not formal structure.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, iii: Scherzo style with dramatic pauses, expanded recapitulation

# Common Errors
- Assuming the scherzo has a distinct formal organization from the minuet
- Relying on score designations to determine formal type

# Common Confusions
- **Style vs. form**: The scherzo differs from the minuet in style (tempo, character) but not in formal organization
- **Labels vs. reality**: Score labels are unreliable; many "Menuettos" are scherzos in style and vice versa
