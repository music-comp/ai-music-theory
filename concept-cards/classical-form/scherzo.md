# Quick Definition
A variant style of the minuet featuring a faster tempo and livelier character, following the same formal organization as the minuet.

# Formal Definition
The scherzo is a variant style of the minuet with a faster tempo and livelier character. Despite attempts to identify a distinct "Scherzoform" (Schoenberg, Ratz), no reliable formal distinction exists between scherzos and minuets. In formal organization, they are identical. Caplin treats the two styles as a single movement type.

# Construction/Recognition
- Faster tempo and livelier character than a standard minuet
- Same formal organization as minuet form
- Score designation "Scherzo" is unreliable as a formal criterion
- Intense rhythmic drive with continuous motion

# Musical Context
The scherzo became increasingly common in Beethoven's works, replacing the minuet in many instrumental cycles.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, iii
