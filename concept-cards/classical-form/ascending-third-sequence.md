# Quick Definition
The least frequently used sequential pattern in classical music, featuring ascending-third root motion with passing chords that function as "dominants" of each successive main harmony.

# Formal Definition
The ascending-third progression is the least frequently used sequential pattern in the classical repertory. Its unembellished form is rarely, if ever, found. A version employing passing chords is more viable: each passing chord is the "dominant" of the following main harmony of the sequence, thus accruing a degree of functionality. Yet even this pattern seldom appears in the literature.

# Construction/Recognition
- Root motion: consistently ascending thirds
- Unembellished form: virtually never found in the classical literature
- Embellished form with passing chords: each passing chord is the "dominant" of the following root-position harmony (ex. 2.14)
- The rarest of the six sequential categories

# Musical Context
Due to its extreme rarity, the ascending-third sequence is more a theoretical possibility than a common compositional resource in classical music. When it does appear, the passing chords provide the necessary functional momentum.

# Examples
- Embellished form with passing chords acting as dominants of each successive harmony (ex. 2.14)
