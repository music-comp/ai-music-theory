# Quick Definition
The systematic tonal plan underlying a development section, involving a hierarchy of keys, development keys, and tonicized regions drawn primarily from minor-mode regions of the home key.

# Formal Definition
The tonal organization of the development follows a clear and logical plan despite the section's characteristic instability. A hierarchy of tonal levels structures the development: (1) the home key remains the ultimate reference; (2) the subordinate key from the exposition may linger at the start; (3) one or more development keys receive cadential confirmation; and (4) various tonicized regions arise through sequential activity. The development emphasizes minor-mode regions, contrasting with the predominantly major-mode exposition.

# Construction/Recognition
- In major-mode movements: VI (submediant) is the most common primary development key, followed by III (mediant) and II (supertonic)
- In minor-mode movements: IV (subdominant) and V (dominant, minor) are used with approximately equal frequency
- The primary development key receives the strongest cadential articulation and is typically the final key before the retransition
- Secondary development keys receive weaker cadential articulation and appear earlier
- The development typically ends on the dominant of the home key

# Musical Context
The emphasis on minor modality in the development contrasts with the major-mode exposition. The subdominant rarely serves as a primary development key in major-mode movements because it does not provide modal contrast and because V/IV would poorly anticipate the home-key tonic at the recapitulation.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in A Minor, K. 310, i: development explores E minor (HK: V) as development key
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Hob. XVI:49, i: development moves through C minor (HK: VI) and F minor (HK: II)
- Mozart, Symphony No. 36 ("Linz"), K. 425, i: A minor (HK: VI) as development key

# Common Confusions
- The development is not a section of "free modulation" -- it follows predictable tonal patterns based on the home key's mode
- Tonicized regions differ from development keys: the former lack cadential confirmation, the latter receive at least a half cadence
