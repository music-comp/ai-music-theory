# Quick Definition
Passages within the coda that conclude with a half cadence or dominant arrival, functioning like transitions or developmental passages rather than theme-concluding units.

# Formal Definition
Some units of a coda conclude with a half cadence (or dominant arrival). In such cases, the passage resembles the kinds of structures found in a transition or development. A half cadence usually appears early in a coda, especially when that section starts without any obvious sense of functional beginning. At times, the initial unit of a coda is comparable to a transition, in that its principal harmonic goal is the dominant. Such a unit may also include model-sequence organization suggesting the core of a development, although the sequencing is nonmodulatory and the harmonies remain closely bound to the home key.

# Construction/Recognition
- Ends with HC or dominant arrival (not PAC)
- May feature model-sequence technique, but within the home key
- Typically appears early in the coda, before the first true coda theme
- Extensive standing on the dominant may follow
- The passage functions to build harmonic tension before the coda theme's resolution

# Musical Context
These transition-like units provide harmonic drama at the start of the coda, creating a miniature arc of tension and resolution within the coda itself. They serve to separate the coda from the recapitulation's final tonic emphasis.

# Examples
- Haydn, Symphony No. 97 in C, i (ex. 12.3): coda begins with sequential repetition of the cadential idea, continues with model-sequence technique, and arrives at HC (m. 261) with standing on the dominant — the entire passage (mm. 246-267) functions like a transitional or developmental unit
- Haydn, Piano Trio in C, Hob. XV:27, iii (ex. 12.8): coda begins with tonic prolongation that leads to a dramatic chromatic pre-dominant (raised ^4, m. 214), premature dominant arrival (m. 222), and extensive standing on the dominant (mm. 226-237)

# Common Confusions
- Model-sequence technique in the coda is nonmodulatory, unlike that in the development core
- A transition-like unit in the coda does not transition to a new key — it transitions to the dominant within the home key
- These units are not developmental in function, even if they use similar techniques
