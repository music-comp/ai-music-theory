# Quick Definition
Passages within the coda that conclude with a half cadence or dominant arrival, functioning like transitions or developmental passages but within the home key's stable tonal context.

# Core Definition
Some units of a coda conclude with a half cadence (or dominant arrival). In such cases, the passage resembles the kinds of structures found in a transition or development. A half cadence usually appears early in a coda, especially when that section starts without any obvious sense of functional beginning. At times, the initial unit of a coda is comparable to a transition, in that its principal harmonic goal is the dominant. Such a unit may also include model-sequence organization suggesting the core of a development, although the sequencing is nonmodulatory and the harmonies remain closely bound to the home key. An extensive standing on the dominant may follow.

# Prerequisites
- **Coda**: The section containing these units
- **Coda theme**: The broader category of coda thematic units

# Key Properties
1. Concludes with HC or dominant arrival (not PAC)
2. May feature model-sequence technique within the home key
3. Typically appears early in the coda, before the first true coda theme
4. An extensive standing on the dominant may follow
5. The sequencing is nonmodulatory -- harmonies remain bound to the home key
6. Creates a miniature arc of tension and resolution within the coda
7. Separates the coda from the recapitulation's final tonic emphasis

# Construction / Recognition
- Look for passages ending with HC or dominant arrival within the coda
- Note model-sequence technique that remains within the home key
- These passages typically precede the first true coda theme
- An extensive standing on the dominant often follows

# Context & Application
Transition-like units provide harmonic drama at the start of the coda. They do not transition to a new key but create tension (moving toward the dominant) before the coda theme's resolution to tonic. They help differentiate the coda from the recapitulation's tonic emphasis.

# Examples
- Haydn, Symphony No. 97 in C, i (ex. 12.3): coda begins with sequential repetition of the cadential idea, continues with model-sequence technique, arrives at HC (m. 261) with standing on the dominant; the entire passage (mm. 246-267) functions like a transitional or developmental unit; could also be analyzed as closing with an internal HC (forming the first part of a two-part coda theme)
- Haydn, Piano Trio in C, Hob. XV:27, iii (ex. 12.8): coda opens with tonic prolongation resembling a closing section; dramatic chromatic pre-dominant (raised scale-degree 4, m. 214) leads to premature dominant arrival (m. 222); extensive standing on the dominant (mm. 226-237) with unusual texture reduction

# Common Errors
- Treating these units as genuine transitions to a new key
- Confusing development-like techniques with actual development function

# Common Confusions
- **Not genuine transitions**: These units do not modulate; they move toward the dominant within the home key
- **Not developmental**: Although they may use model-sequence technique, the sequencing is nonmodulatory and tonally stable
- **Two interpretations**: A transition-like unit may also be analyzed as the first part of a two-part coda theme (analogous to a two-part subordinate theme)
