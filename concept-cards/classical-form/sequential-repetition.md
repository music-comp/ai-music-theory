# Quick Definition
A type of repetition within a continuation phrase in which a fragmented unit (the model) is transposed to different scale-degrees (the sequence), supported by a sequential harmonic progression.

# Core Definition
Sequential repetition occurs when "the entire fragment is transposed to different scale-degrees" (Ch. 1, p. 12). By convention, "we refer to the initial unit as a model and each unit of repetition as its sequence. Thus sequential repetition can also be termed model-sequence technique" (Ch. 1, p. 12). At the harmonic basis of sequential repetition is "a sequential progression of the harmonies -- that is, chords whose roots are organized according to a systematic intervallic pattern" (Ch. 1, p. 12). This is the third type of repetition identified in the chapter, "particularly characteristic of continuation function."

# Prerequisites
- **Continuation function** -- the formal context
- **Sequential progression** -- the harmonic basis

# Key Properties
1. A model is stated and then transposed (the sequence)
2. Supported by a sequential harmonic progression
3. Particularly characteristic of continuation function
4. Creates harmonic mobility and forward momentum
5. Distinguished from exact repetition (no transposition) and statement-response (I-V framework)

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify a short unit (model) that is immediately restated at a different pitch level
- Verify that the underlying harmony follows a sequential pattern (e.g., descending fifths)
- The ongoing quality of sequential harmony coordinates with continuation function's forward impetus

# Context & Application
Sequential repetition appears in continuation phrases of sentences, contrasting middles of small ternaries, and throughout looser formal regions. Its harmonic mobility makes it ideal for destabilizing formal contexts.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 1, p. 12, ex. 1.2): Beethoven, Op. 135, iii, mm. 7-9: one-measure model sequenced to different scale-degrees with descending-fifth root pattern (F-Bb-Eb-Ab-Db).

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Sequential progression (the harmonic basis)
## Enables
- Continuation function (provides characteristic harmonic mobility)
## Related
- Model-sequence technique (synonym, detailed in Ch. 6)
## Contrasts With
- Statement-response: tonic-dominant framework within presentation
- Exact repetition: no transposition

# Common Errors
- Calling any repetition "sequential" without verifying transposition and sequential harmonic support

# Common Confusions
- Sequential repetition requires harmonic sequence, not just melodic transposition
