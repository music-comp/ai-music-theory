# Quick Definition
A single thematic unit in the exposition that fuses transition and subordinate-theme functions: it modulates to the subordinate key and confirms it with a PAC, without a clear initiating function for a discrete subordinate theme.

# Formal Definition
Some expositions witness the fusion of transition and subordinate-theme functions into a single thematic unit. This unit not only modulates to the subordinate key (transition function) but also closes with a PAC to confirm that key (subordinate-theme function). It is not possible to find an appropriate initiating function for a discrete subordinate theme, even in retrospect. This fusion corresponds to Pattern 8 in Table 13.1.

# Construction/Recognition
- A single unit performs both modulation (transition) and cadential confirmation (subordinate theme)
- No point within the unit can be identified as the beginning of a discrete subordinate theme
- The unit typically ends with PAC in the subordinate key
- May look like a transition that "accidentally" closes with a PAC instead of HC
- The theoretical distinction matters: authentic cadential confirmation of the subordinate key is an essential criterion of the subordinate theme's function

# Musical Context
This fusion is relatively uncommon but theoretically important because it blurs the fundamental distinction between transition and subordinate-theme functions. Caplin argues it is more consistent to interpret such passages as fusions than to allow transitions to close with PACs, which would confuse the interthematic functions.

# Examples
- Haydn, Symphony No. 93 in D, i (ex. 13.8): after two parts of transition activity (mm. 46-61), the music leads to cadential progressions from m. 66 to PAC at m. 74; no point can be identified as a clear subordinate-theme initiation; the passage from m. 53-74 fuses both functions
- Mozart, Violin Sonata in B-flat, K. 454, i (ex. 13.9): 16-measure unit (mm. 14-29) functions as a modulating period; antecedent = main theme; consequent = fused transition/subordinate theme

# Common Confusions
- A transition that closes with PAC in the subordinate key is not simply a deviant transition — it has incorporated subordinate-theme function
- This fusion differs from an obscured boundary: in the latter, a subordinate theme can be identified (in retrospect); in the former, it cannot
- The subsequent discrete subordinate theme (if any) follows the fused unit and begins a new thematic process
