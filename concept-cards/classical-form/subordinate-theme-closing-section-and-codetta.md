# Quick Definition
Following the subordinate theme's final PAC (the EEC), a closing section consisting of codettas emphasizes root-position tonic and provides postcadential confirmation of the subordinate key -- the closing section may sometimes be retrospectively understood as the beginning of a new subordinate theme ("false closing section").

# Core Definition
The PAC closing a subordinate theme is typically followed by a postcadential closing section made up of codettas. These codettas emphasize root-position tonic harmony in the subordinate key and serve to release the energy accumulated during the drive to achieve the PAC. The closing section typically features slower rhythmic values, disjunct textures, and conventional cadential patterns that help dissipate the accumulated tension. In some cases, what initially appears to be a closing section turns out to be a false closing section that initiates a new subordinate theme. This happens when the material of the apparent codettas evolves beyond simple postcadential function into genuine thematic activity (with continuation, cadential functions, and a new PAC).

# Prerequisites
- **Essential expositional closure**: The PAC that the closing section follows
- **Closing section in subordinate theme**: The broader concept
- **Codetta**: The individual units making up the closing section

# Key Properties
1. Follows the EEC (PAC closing the subordinate theme or theme group)
2. Consists of codettas emphasizing root-position tonic
3. Postcadential function: releases accumulated energy
4. Typically features slower rhythmic values, disjunct textures
5. May be followed by a retransition leading back to the home key
6. A "false closing section" may retrospectively become a new subordinate theme
7. In the recapitulation, the closing section may be extended to provide a more decisive ending

# Construction / Recognition
- Locate the EEC (final PAC of subordinate theme/group)
- Identify postcadential codettas emphasizing tonic
- Check whether the material remains postcadential (genuine closing section) or develops into a new theme (false closing section)
- Note whether a retransition follows the closing section

# Context & Application
The closing section serves a crucial role in the exposition's overall dynamic shape. After the intense drive toward the EEC, the closing section provides necessary relaxation and confirmation. The dissipation of energy through the closing section's codettas prepares the listener for either the exposition repeat (via retransition) or the development section's new intensity. In the recapitulation, the closing section may be extended to provide a more emphatic conclusion to the movement.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in F minor, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 42-48 (Ch. 9, Ex. 9.3): three 2-m. codettas following the PAC at m. 41; the final codetta extended by a bar; slower rhythmic values and disjunct textures dissipate accumulated tension
- In the recapitulation of the same movement (Ch. 9, Ex. 9.11), the closing section is extended to form a complete sentence, whose continuation is stretched to eight measures -- the resulting structure could even be considered a "second" subordinate theme (false closing section)

# Common Errors
- Treating the closing section as a separate "closing theme" (it is postcadential, not thematic)
- Confusing the closing section with the subordinate theme's cadential phrase
- Missing a false closing section that becomes a new subordinate theme

# Common Confusions
- **Closing section vs. closing theme**: The closing section is postcadential and does not constitute a theme
- **False closing section**: What begins as codettas may evolve into a genuine theme with its own PAC
- **Recapitulation extension**: The closing section may be extended in the recapitulation beyond its exposition appearance
