# Quick Definition
A dominant arrival in which the harmonic goal (dominant) appears before the end of the prevailing melodic, rhythmic, and phrase-structural processes, creating noncongruence between harmonic arrival and melodic-motivic closure.

# Core Definition
A premature dominant arrival occurs when the final dominant arrives at a point that does not coincide with the phrase-structural or melodic ending. The harmonic goal precedes the melodic goal, and further activity -- fragmentation, liquidation, passage-work -- continues over the already-arrived dominant before the structural close. Because the dominant is not synchronized with the phrase-structural end, it cannot function as a genuine half cadence. The noncongruence between harmonic and structural processes is itself a loosening trait typical of transitions and development cores.

# Prerequisites
- **Dominant arrival**: The broader category of noncadential dominant endings
- **Half cadence**: The genuine cadential ending from which it is distinguished
- **Cadential progression**: The harmonic norm disrupted by premature arrival

# Key Properties
1. Dominant arrives while melodic-motivic processes are still ongoing
2. Noncongruence between harmonic arrival and phrase-structural end
3. Further fragmentation or liquidation continues over the dominant
4. No genuine cadential progression marks the phrase-structural end
5. Distinguished from simple dominant arrivals (where chord quality is the issue)
6. The dominant may be perfectly well-formed (root position, triad) yet still premature
7. Especially common in transitions and development cores
8. The noncongruence is itself a loosening trait

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the moment when dominant harmony first appears as a goal
- Check whether melodic and phrase-structural processes continue beyond that moment
- If the dominant arrives but the phrase is not yet over, it is premature
- Look for fragmentation, liquidation, or passage-work continuing over the dominant
- Compare with a genuine half cadence, where harmonic and structural endpoints coincide
- A subsequent standing on the dominant follows the premature arrival, not a genuine HC

# Context & Application
Premature dominant arrivals commonly appear in transitions and development cores. In transitions, the discrepancy between cadential arrival and melodic-motivic change is a loosening trait: the analyst who focuses primarily on melodic and textural content might locate the structural close later than the harmonic arrival. This creates interesting analytical tension, as harmonic and surface processes define different endpoints. In development cores, the dominant may arrive through the sequential process before the fragmentation has fully played out, yielding a premature arrival.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 7, Op. 92, i, m. 81: dominant arrives at onset of fragmentation; section continues to fermata at m. 88
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 31/3, ii, m. 16: dominant arrives but dotted-rhythm figure continues
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 4, Op. 60, i, m. 92: transition -- harmonic goal precedes melodic goal at m. 95
- Haydn, Symphony No. 102, ii, m. 41: premature dominant arrival in transition-like development unit
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in C, Hob. XVI:21, ii, m. 10: harmonic arrival and melodic-motivic arrival noncongruent

# Common Errors
- Treating the premature dominant as a half cadence simply because the chord is correctly formed
- Ignoring the continuation of melodic-motivic processes after the dominant arrives
- Confusing postcadential standing on the dominant with the continuation that follows a premature arrival

# Common Confusions
- **Not all dominant arrivals are premature**: Some are noncadential due to chord quality (inversion, seventh), not timing
- **Continuation over dominant**: The continuation over the dominant does not mean the section lacks a harmonic goal; the goal has simply arrived early
- **Vs. postcadential standing**: Do not confuse with a postcadential standing on the dominant, which follows a genuine HC
- **Analytical tension**: The discrepancy between harmonic and melodic endpoints is deliberate and analytically significant, not a shortcoming
