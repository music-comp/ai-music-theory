# Quick Definition
A transition that opens with new melodic-motivic material supported by the home-key tonic, commonly used after the main theme has closed with a PAC.

# Core Definition
Many transitions begin with new material supported by the home-key tonic. This opening is commonly used after a main theme ending with PAC, less often after HC. At first, the listener cannot be sure whether the new material represents a transition or possibly a second main theme, since only the subsequent modulation or dominant goal can confirm that a transition has been under way. (A second main theme would close with another PAC in the home key.) Despite being "new," the material often derives from the main theme's basic idea, with sufficient differences in melodic scale-degrees, harmonic support, and accompanimental setting to project the sense of a new beginning.

# Prerequisites
- **Transition**: The formal function being initiated
- **Main theme**: The preceding unit, usually ending with PAC

# Key Properties
1. New melodic-motivic material (not a direct restatement of main theme)
2. Supported by the home-key tonic
3. Commonly follows a main theme ending with PAC
4. Initial ambiguity: could be a second main theme until modulation confirms transition
5. May derive motivically from the main theme while projecting a new beginning
6. The "newness" is defined by changes in melodic contour, harmonic support, and accompaniment
7. Transition function confirmed only retrospectively

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify new melodic material at the start of the transition
- Note the home-key tonic support
- Observe the initial ambiguity between transition and second main theme
- The modulation or dominant goal retrospectively confirms transition function
- Check for motivic derivation from the main theme despite the new character

# Context & Application
Beginning with new material is one of the two most common ways to open a transition (along with main-theme material). The initial ambiguity creates formal suspense resolved by the modulation. This is especially natural after a PAC-ending main theme, where tonic resolution provides a firm platform for the new beginning.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in C, Hob. XVI:21, ii, mm. 7-8: new two-measure basic idea derives from the opening basic idea but with different melodic scale-degrees, harmonic support, and accompaniment

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Transition (the formal function)
- Main theme (the preceding unit)

## Enables
- Formal ambiguity (transition vs. second main theme)
- Retrospective confirmation of function

## Related
- Transition with main-theme material (the alternative beginning)
- False closing section in transition (another beginning type)
- Transition from nontonic region (less common beginning)

## Contrasts With
- Transition with main-theme material (restatement vs. new material)

# Common Errors
- Treating the new material as entirely unrelated to the main theme (it may derive motivically)
- Resolving the transition vs. second-main-theme ambiguity too early
- Confusing a false closing section beginning with a new-material beginning

# Common Confusions
- **"New" vs. derived**: New material may derive from the main theme's basic idea while projecting a distinctly new character
- **Ambiguity resolution**: The ambiguity is resolved retrospectively, not at the moment the new material appears
- **New material vs. false closing section**: A false closing section begins with codetta-like character; a new-material beginning has thematic character
