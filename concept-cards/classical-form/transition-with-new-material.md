# Quick Definition
A transition that opens with new melodic-motivic material supported by the home-key tonic, commonly used after the main theme has closed with a PAC.

# Formal Definition
Many transitions in the classical literature begin with new material supported by the home-key tonic. This opening is commonly used after the main theme has closed with a PAC, less often after a HC. At first, the listener cannot be sure whether the new material represents a transition or possibly a second main theme, since only the subsequent modulation to the new key or a goal dominant in the home key can confirm that a transition has been under way. A second main theme would close with another PAC in the home key. Despite being "new," the transition's opening material often derives from the main theme's basic idea, with sufficient differences in melodic scale-degrees, harmonic support, and accompanimental setting to project the sense of a new beginning.

# Construction/Recognition
1. New melodic-motivic material (not a direct restatement of the main theme)
2. Supported by the home-key tonic
3. Commonly follows a main theme ending with PAC
4. Initial ambiguity: could be a second main theme until modulation confirms transition function
5. May derive motivically from the main theme's basic idea while projecting a new beginning
6. The "newness" is defined by changes in melodic contour, harmonic support, and accompaniment
7. The transition function is confirmed only retrospectively when modulation or dominant goal appears

# Musical Context
Beginning with new material is one of the most common ways to open a transition. The initial ambiguity between transition and second main theme creates a moment of formal suspense that is resolved when the modulation begins. The derivation of the new material from the main theme's basic idea demonstrates the classical principle of motivic economy while maintaining the sense of formal progression. This opening is especially natural after a PAC-ending main theme, where the tonic resolution provides a firm harmonic platform for the new beginning.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in C, Hob. XVI:21, ii, mm. 7-8 (new two-measure basic idea derives from the opening basic idea but with different melodic scale-degrees, harmonic support, and accompaniment)
- Mozart, Piano Trio in D Minor, K. 442, iii, mm. 16-18 (new material with codetta character that develops into transition)

# Common Confusions
- "New material" does not mean entirely unrelated to the main theme -- it may derive from the basic idea while projecting a distinctly new character
- The ambiguity between transition and second main theme is resolved retrospectively, not at the moment the new material appears
- This type of opening is distinct from a false closing section, which begins with codetta-like (not thematic) character
- New material at the start of a transition is common but not required -- transitions can also begin with main-theme material
