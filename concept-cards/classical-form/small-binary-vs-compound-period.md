# Quick Definition
When the first part of a small binary ends with an HC, the repeated first part may initially suggest a compound antecedent of a sixteen-measure period. But when the repetition ends with the same HC, the compound period interpretation fails, because the antecedent of any period is never repeated prior to the onset of the consequent.

# Core Definition
In cases where the first part of a small binary ends with an HC, one might be tempted -- especially on first hearing -- to think that the entire eight-measure unit is functioning as the compound antecedent of a sixteen-measure period. When the opening unit begins to be repeated, the impression of a compound consequent in the making strengthens. But when the unit ends a second time with an HC, the compound period option is no longer viable, since the antecedent of any period is never repeated prior to the onset of the consequent. The whole effect of a periodic formation is that the first repetition of an opening unit brings stronger cadential closure. If a presumed compound antecedent is fully repeated (without achieving stronger cadential closure), the structure should be reinterpreted as the first part of a small binary.

# Prerequisites
- **Small binary**: One of the forms being distinguished
- **Compound period**: The other form being distinguished

# Key Properties
1. Both forms are sixteen measures long (8 + 8)
2. Both may begin with similar thematic material
3. The critical test: does the repetition bring stronger cadential closure?
4. If the repetition ends with the same HC: small binary (not compound period)
5. The antecedent of any period is NEVER repeated prior to the consequent
6. If a stronger cadence (PAC) follows the repeated unit: compound period
7. The repeat scheme of the small binary creates the initial confusion
8. The distinction is clarified only when the second ending of the first part is heard

# Construction / Recognition
1. Hear an eight-measure unit ending with HC
2. The unit is repeated: initially suggests compound antecedent
3. Critical moment: does the repetition end with HC (= small binary) or PAC (= compound period)?
4. If HC again: reinterpret as first part of small binary
5. If PAC: compound consequent, confirming compound period
6. The repeat scheme ||: Part 1 :|| is the source of ambiguity

# Context & Application
This distinction is important because the two forms have fundamentally different formal implications. A compound period is a single closed theme with antecedent-consequent structure; a small binary is an open form requiring a second part for closure. The distinction hinges on a simple principle: periodic forms require cadential differentiation (weak then strong). If the repetition fails to bring stronger closure, the periodic interpretation must be abandoned.

# Examples
- Beethoven, String Quartet in C-sharp Minor, Op. 131, iv, mm. 1-16: if there were no repeats, the two eight-measure units might be heard as compound antecedent + compound consequent; but the repeat scheme (each ending HC) confirms small binary
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E, Hob. XVI:31, iii: first part ends HC; repetition also ends HC; compound period interpretation abandoned in favor of small binary

# Common Errors
- Calling an 8+8 structure with HC-then-HC endings a "compound period" -- the repeated HC denies the period
- Ignoring the repeat scheme when making formal determinations
- Assuming the first hearing's impression (compound antecedent) is the correct analysis

# Common Confusions
- The antecedent of any period is NEVER repeated prior to the onset of the consequent
- If a presumed compound antecedent is fully repeated, reinterpret as first part of a small binary
- The distinction depends on cadential differentiation: weak-then-strong = period; same-then-same = binary
- Without repeats, the distinction may remain ambiguous -- the repeat scheme clarifies the form
