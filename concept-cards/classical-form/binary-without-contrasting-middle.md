# Quick Definition
A small binary whose second part lacks a clearly discernible contrasting middle, instead being organized as a single theme-like unit (often sentential) or as a passage of continuation or cadential functions exclusively.

# Core Definition
The second part of a small binary is sometimes built without a clearly discernible contrasting middle. Instead, the second part may be organized along the lines of a conventional theme-type (most often a sentence, rarely a period) or may bring a distinctly looser structure with continuation or cadential functions exclusively. Even without a complete contrasting middle, the beginning of the second part may strongly suggest that function, such as emphasizing dominant harmony or immediately bringing model-sequence technique. The distinction from a small ternary remains: no genuine recapitulation of the basic idea in the home-key tonic.

# Construction / Recognition
1. Second part organized as a single unit (not divided into B section + closing material)
2. May take the form of a sentence (presentation + continuation), with presentation featuring reversed tonic-dominant pattern
3. May consist entirely of continuation function (large eight-measure continuation)
4. May begin with suggestions of contrasting middle (dominant emphasis, model-sequence) without forming a complete B section
5. Always ends with PAC in home key
6. Opening material of second part usually connects motivically to first part

# Context & Application
Binaries without contrasting middles often arise when the first part is sentential. Since a sentence cannot easily be compressed into a four-measure recapitulation (unlike a period's antecedent or consequent), composers writing symmetrical 8 + 8 themes beginning with sentences will naturally choose the small binary form. The absence of the contrasting middle makes the overall bipartite structure even clearer, as the second part flows continuously to its cadential goal.

# Examples
- Beethoven, String Quartet in C-sharp Minor, Op. 131, iv, mm. 17-32 (second part as sentence; presentation begins with reversed dominant-tonic pattern suggesting contrasting middle)
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E, Hob. XVI:31, iii, mm. 9-16 (second part as large eight-measure continuation with model-sequence technique; no true presentation phrase)
- Beethoven, Violin Sonata in A, Op. 12/2, ii, mm. 17-32 (second part begins with prolonged III, followed by cadential phrase; no contrasting middle)

# Common Confusions
- Even without a full contrasting middle, the beginning of the second part may suggest that function through dominant emphasis -- this does not make it a true B section
- A second part organized as a sentence may suggest a period relationship with the first part (HC then PAC), but the overall form is binary, not periodic
- The second part's presentation may feature a "reversed" statement-response pattern (response followed by statement), which projects looser initiation appropriate to a second part but not to a consequent
- A large continuation function spanning the entire second part is not unusual -- it serves as the continuation for the first part's implied sentence
