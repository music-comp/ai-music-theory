# Quick Definition
A small binary whose second part lacks a clearly discernible contrasting middle, instead being organized as a single theme-like unit (often sentential) or as a passage of continuation or cadential functions exclusively.

# Core Definition
The second part of a small binary is sometimes built without a clearly discernible contrasting middle. It may be organized as a conventional theme type (most often a sentence, rarely a period) or as a distinctly looser structure with continuation or cadential functions exclusively. Even without a complete contrasting middle, the beginning of the part may strongly suggest that function through dominant emphasis or model-sequence technique. The distinction from a small ternary remains: no genuine recapitulation. The overall form may resemble a compound sentence, with part 2 serving as a large continuation for part 1.

# Prerequisites
- **Small binary**: The overarching form

# Key Properties
1. Second part organized as a single unit (not divided into B section + closing material)
2. May take the form of a sentence (with presentation featuring reversed tonic-dominant pattern)
3. May consist entirely of continuation function (large eight-measure continuation)
4. May begin with suggestions of contrasting middle (dominant emphasis, model-sequence) without forming a complete B section
5. Always ends with PAC in home key
6. Opening material usually connects motivically to first part
7. No genuine recapitulation of basic idea in home-key tonic

# Construction / Recognition
1. Check whether the second part begins with a complete contrasting middle or merely suggests one
2. Identify the overall organization (sentence, continuation, cadential, or mixed)
3. Note whether the reversed statement-response pattern (response-then-statement) projects looser initiation
4. Check for model-sequence technique suggesting but not fulfilling contrasting middle function
5. Verify PAC in home key and absence of recapitulation

# Context & Application
Binaries without contrasting middles often arise when the first part is sentential. Since a sentence cannot easily be compressed into a four-measure recapitulation (unlike a period), composers writing symmetrical 8+8 themes beginning with sentences naturally choose the binary form. The absence of contrasting middle makes the bipartite structure even clearer. The overall form may function as a large-scale sentence: part 1 as large presentation, part 2 as large continuation.

# Examples
- Beethoven, String Quartet in C-sharp Minor, Op. 131, iv, mm. 17-32: sentence with reversed dominant-tonic presentation suggesting contrasting middle; overall looser arrangement appropriate for second part
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E, Hob. XVI:31, iii, mm. 9-16: large eight-measure continuation with model-sequence technique; no true presentation phrase
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E, Op. 109, iii, mm. 9-16: second part with loose continuational organization

# Common Errors
- Assuming a small binary must have a contrasting middle -- Ratz incorrectly identified the absence of B section as the defining feature, but many small binaries DO have contrasting middles
- Labeling dominant emphasis at the start of part 2 as a complete contrasting middle when it lacks the characteristic features
- Assuming a sentence-like second part creates a period relationship with the first part

# Common Confusions
- Even without a full contrasting middle, the beginning of the second part may suggest that function through dominant emphasis -- this does not make it a true B section
- A second part organized as a sentence may suggest a period relationship with the first part (HC then PAC), but the overall form is binary, not periodic
- The reversed presentation pattern (response-statement) projects looser initiation appropriate for a central position
- A large continuation spanning the entire second part is not unusual -- it serves as the continuation for the first part's implied sentence
