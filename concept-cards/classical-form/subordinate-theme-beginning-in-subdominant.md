# Quick Definition
A deviation where the subordinate theme in the recapitulation begins in the subdominant region before being adjusted to the home key, extending the flat-region emphasis to the subordinate-theme area.

# Formal Definition
Another deviation occurs when the tendency for the recapitulation to explore flat-side regions is exploited to the extent that the subordinate theme begins in the subdominant region. To prepare for the theme's entrance, the transition is adjusted to conclude with the dominant of that region. Shortly after the subordinate theme has begun, it is further adjusted in order to remain centered in the home-key tonic.

# Construction/Recognition
- The transition ends on V of IV (instead of V of I)
- The subordinate theme begins in the subdominant region
- The theme is adjusted shortly after beginning to return to the home key
- Extends the flat-region emphasis principle beyond the transition
- The home key is eventually confirmed by the PAC ending the subordinate theme

# Musical Context
This deviation represents an extreme application of the flat-region emphasis principle. It is relatively rare but reflects the general tendency of recapitulations to explore flat-side regions before confirming the home key.

# Common Confusions
- The subordinate theme does not remain in the subdominant — it is adjusted back to the home key
- This is not a "three-key" recapitulation; the subdominant is a passing emphasis, not a structural key
- The transition must also be adjusted to prepare the subdominant rather than the tonic
