# Quick Definition
A deviation where the subordinate theme in the recapitulation begins in the subdominant region before being adjusted to the home key, extending the flat-region emphasis beyond the transition.

# Core Definition
Another deviation occurs when the tendency for the recapitulation to explore flat-side regions is exploited to the extent that the subordinate theme begins in the subdominant region. To prepare for the theme's entrance, the transition is adjusted to conclude with the dominant of that region. Shortly after the subordinate theme has begun, it is further adjusted in order to remain centered in the home-key tonic. This represents an extreme application of the flat-region emphasis principle, extending it beyond the transition into the subordinate-theme area itself.

# Prerequisites
- **Recapitulation**: The formal context
- **Flat-region emphasis**: The principle being extended to its extreme

# Key Properties
1. The transition ends on V of IV (instead of V of I)
2. The subordinate theme begins in the subdominant region
3. The theme is adjusted shortly after beginning to return to the home key
4. The home key is eventually confirmed by the PAC ending the subordinate theme
5. Extends the flat-region emphasis principle beyond the transition
6. Represents an extreme application of a standard recapitulatory technique

# Construction / Recognition
- The transition concludes on the dominant of IV (not I)
- The subordinate theme opens in the subdominant region
- Look for the point where the music shifts back to the home-key tonic
- The PAC ending the subordinate theme confirms the home key

# Context & Application
This deviation is relatively rare but reflects the general tendency of recapitulations to explore flat-side regions before confirming the home key. It intensifies the harmonic contrast between the transition and the eventual home-key confirmation.

# Common Errors
- Confusing this with a "three-key" recapitulation (the subdominant is a passing emphasis, not a structural key)

# Common Confusions
- **Not a structural key**: The subordinate theme does not remain in the subdominant; it is adjusted back to the home key
- **Transition preparation**: The transition must also be adjusted to prepare the subdominant rather than the tonic
- **Extreme flat emphasis**: This is a deviation, not a norm; it extends the standard flat-emphasis technique beyond the transition
