# Quick Definition
The formal organization of an individual component minuet within a minuet/trio movement, closely resembling the small ternary (rounded binary) with exposition (A), contrasting middle (B), and recapitulation (A').

# Formal Definition
Minuet form is the formal structure of a single component minuet. The vast majority follow the small ternary (rounded binary version), containing exposition (A), contrasting middle (B), and recapitulation (A'). Unlike the small ternary, which serves primarily as a main theme, minuet form functions as a high-level section in a full-movement form. This means attention must be directed to interthematic functions: main theme, transition, subordinate theme, development, recapitulation, and coda. Of particular importance is whether a subordinate key is confirmed, and where.

# Construction/Recognition
- Three-part structure: A (exposition), B (contrasting middle), A' (recapitulation)
- Each part is typically repeated (rounded binary)
- The A section may remain in the home key or modulate
- The B section provides contrast and usually ends on the home-key dominant
- The A' section recapitulates the A section, often with expansion
- About 10% follow binary minuet form instead

# Musical Context
Minuet form serves as the structural basis for both the minuet proper and the trio.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, iii
- Haydn, Symphony No. 98 in B-flat, iii
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in A, K. 331/300i, ii

# Common Confusions
- Minuet form and small ternary are formally analogous but functionally different. The small ternary's functions are intrathematic; minuet form's functions are interthematic.
