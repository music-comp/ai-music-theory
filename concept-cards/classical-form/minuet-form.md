# Quick Definition
The formal organization of an individual component minuet, closely resembling the small ternary (rounded binary) but operating at the interthematic level -- functioning as a high-level section in the full-movement form with main-theme, transition, and subordinate-theme functions.

# Core Definition
Minuet form is the structure of a single component minuet (either the minuet proper or the trio). The vast majority follow the small ternary (rounded binary), containing exposition (A), contrasting middle (B), and recapitulation (A'). Unlike the small ternary, which functions primarily as a main theme, minuet form functions as a high-level section in a full-movement form. This means attention must be directed to interthematic functions: main theme, transition, subordinate theme, development, recapitulation, and coda. Of particular importance is whether a subordinate key is confirmed, and where. The A section may remain entirely in the home key (nonmodulating, expressing only main-theme function) or modulate to a subordinate key (expressing main-theme, transition, and subordinate-theme functions in varying degrees). The B section is usually more complex and longer than in a small ternary. The A' section is seldom shorter than the A section and is much more likely to be significantly expanded.

# Prerequisites
- **Small ternary**: The formal model on which minuet form is based
- **Minuet/trio form**: The full-movement context

# Key Properties
1. Three-part structure: A (exposition), B (contrasting middle), A' (recapitulation)
2. Each part is typically repeated (rounded binary)
3. Functions at the interthematic level (unlike the intrathematic small ternary)
4. Whether the A section modulates is of particular importance
5. The B section is usually more complex and longer than in a small ternary
6. The A' section is seldom compressed and is often significantly expanded
7. About 10% of component minuets follow binary minuet form instead

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the A section and determine whether it modulates
- Assess which interthematic functions are expressed (main theme, transition, subordinate theme)
- Note the B section's complexity relative to a small ternary B section
- Check whether the A' section is expanded beyond the A section's scope
- Look for a closing section or coda

# Context & Application
The distinction between intrathematic functions (small ternary) and interthematic functions (minuet form) is the fundamental analytical difference. In the small ternary, the components are like presentation, continuation, and cadential function within a theme. In minuet form, they are like main theme, development, and recapitulation within a movement.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, iii: Modulating A section with fused functions; extensively expanded A' section
- Haydn, Symphony No. 98 in B-flat, iii: Multiple subordinate themes in the A section
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in A, K. 331/300i, ii: Subordinate theme beginning directly in the new key

# Common Errors
- Analyzing the A section's components as intrathematic (like a small ternary) rather than interthematic
- Expecting the A' section to be compressed (as in many small ternaries)
- Treating all minuets as simple small ternaries without considering interthematic functions

# Common Confusions
- **Minuet form vs. small ternary**: Formally analogous but functionally different; the small ternary's functions are intrathematic, the minuet form's are interthematic
- **Modulation significance**: Unlike in a small ternary (where modulation is a minor concern), modulation in a minuet's A section may represent the principal tonal conflict of the entire movement
