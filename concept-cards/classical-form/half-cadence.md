# Quick Definition
A cadential progression in which the dominant itself becomes the goal harmony, providing a weaker tonal confirmation than an authentic cadence and leaving the progression structurally incomplete.

# Formal Definition
In the half-cadential progression, the dominant itself becomes the goal harmony and occupies the ultimate position. To acquire the requisite stability for an ending harmony, the dominant must take the form of a root-position triad without a dissonant seventh (which would overly destabilize it). The half cadence (HC) provides a weaker confirmation of tonality than the authentic cadence. Half-cadential progressions can contain the same harmonies as authentic cadential ones (initial tonic, pre-dominant), with the adjustment that the final chord is V (consonant triad) rather than continuing to a tonic resolution.

# Construction/Recognition
- The progression ends on a root-position V (consonant triad, no seventh)
- Complete: I(6) - pre-dominant - V
- Incomplete: omits initial tonic or pre-dominant
- All authentic cadential paradigms apply, adjusted to end on V
- Additional paradigms approach V by descending motion from b^6 (ex. 2.10)
- HC often followed by a standing on the dominant (postcadential prolongation of V)

# Musical Context
The half cadence closes antecedent phrases in periods, ends some sentence themes (creating less complete closure than PAC), and concludes contrasting middles in small ternaries. Its structural incompleteness demands continuation--either a consequent phrase (in periods), or a recapitulation (in small ternaries). The standing on the dominant frequently follows an HC.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, m. 8: HC closes the main theme sentence
- Beethoven, Op. 15, ii, m. 12: HC closes the contrasting middle of a small ternary
- Mozart, K. 525, ii, m. 4: HC closes the antecedent of a period
- Special approach: b^6 descending to ^5 in the bass (ex. 2.10)

# Common Confusions
- The dominant in a half cadence must be a consonant triad (no seventh). Adding a seventh would destabilize it and make it function as a penultimate chord seeking resolution.
- HC is a genuine cadence (it confirms tonality, albeit weakly), not merely an interrupted progression.
- A half cadence creates structural incompleteness that demands formal continuation.
