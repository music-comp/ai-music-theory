# Quick Definition
The opening ritornello remains fundamentally in the home key, reserving the establishment of the subordinate key for the soloist in the solo exposition, though the subordinate key may be briefly touched upon without being confirmed.

# Core Definition
If the opening ritornello strongly resembles a sonata-form exposition, it lacks one of its principal characteristics: a genuine tonal conflict between home and subordinate keys. In most cases, the ritornello remains entirely in the home key. In this way, the solo part can be given the opportunity to produce one of the major tonal events of the work, namely, the establishment of the subordinate key. If the orchestra alone does not depart from the home key, then strong expectations are generated for the solo to accomplish this task. Occasionally the unit following the ritornello's transition begins in the subordinate key but then returns, usually rather quickly, to the home key without cadencing in the new key. Seldom does a modulating transition lead to a theme that confirms the subordinate key (Caplin, pp. 703-704).

# Key Properties
1. Ritornello remains fundamentally in the home key
2. Absence of genuine tonal conflict
3. Subordinate key may be briefly touched but not confirmed
4. Modulation is reserved for the solo exposition
5. Generates expectations for the solo's tonal action
6. Very rarely does the ritornello confirm the subordinate key

# Construction / Recognition
1. Check whether the ritornello modulates or stays in the home key
2. If the subordinate key appears, determine whether it is confirmed cadentially
3. Brief touches on the subordinate key that return quickly are not genuine modulations
4. The ritornello's staying in the home key is a structural choice, not a limitation

# Context & Application
The tonal stability of the opening ritornello is explained by the concerto's aesthetic goals: reserving the modulation for the solo allows the soloist to create one of the movement's principal tonal events. This is a key distinction from symphonic sonata form.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Expecting the opening ritornello to modulate like a sonata exposition. **Correction**: The ritornello normally stays in the home key, reserving modulation for the solo.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Whether a brief touch on the subordinate key counts as modulation. **Clarification**: Only if the key is cadentially confirmed, which seldom happens in the ritornello.
