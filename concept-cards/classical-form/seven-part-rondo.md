# Quick Definition
An expanded five-part rondo (ABACADA) with a third couplet and fourth refrain added, providing three contrasting couplets in different tonal regions.

# Formal Definition
The five-part rondo can be enlarged by adding a third couplet and a fourth refrain, creating a seven-part rondo (traditionally ABACADA). Only a few works in the classical repertory follow this procedure. The additional couplet (couplet 3) is usually constructed as an interior theme whose tonal region and formal organization contrast with those of earlier interior themes. The three couplets typically explore different standard tonal regions (minore, vi, IV), providing maximum tonal variety.

# Construction/Recognition
- Seven sections: Refrain 1 - Couplet 1 - Refrain 2 - Couplet 2 - Refrain 3 - Couplet 3 - Refrain 4
- All refrains in the home key (I)
- Three couplets in contrasting tonal regions
- Couplet 3 typically an interior theme in a region not previously used
- Rare in the classical repertory

# Musical Context
The seven-part rondo is uncommon but represents the logical extension of the five-part rondo principle. It provides maximum variety through three contrasting couplets while maintaining the rondo's characteristic refrain returns.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Trio in D, Hob. XV:16, iii: Couplet 1 = interior theme, minore, small ternary; Couplet 2 = interior theme, vi, small binary; Couplet 3 = interior theme, IV, small ternary
- Mozart, Clarinet Trio in E-flat, K. 498, iii: Couplet 1 = subordinate-theme group (no transition); Couplet 2 = interior theme, vi, small ternary; Couplet 3 = interior theme, IV, small ternary
- Beethoven, Violin Sonata in A Minor, Op. 23, iii: Couplet 1 = transition/subordinate-theme fusion; Couplet 2 = interior theme, maggiore, incomplete form; Couplet 3 = interior theme, vi, small binary

# Common Confusions
- The seven-part rondo should not be confused with the sonata-rondo (ABACABA). In the seven-part rondo, the couplets are labeled C, D (or B, C, D) because they contain different material; in the sonata-rondo, couplet 3 recapitulates couplet 1's material.
- This form is rare; students should not expect to encounter it frequently.
