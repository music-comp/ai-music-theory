# Quick Definition
The often-difficult task of locating where the coda begins, defined as the point where the recapitulation's music no longer corresponds to the exposition, even if that moment is not perceived as a structural beginning.

# Formal Definition
Unlike an exposition, development, or recapitulation, whose beginning is usually articulated by a clear initiating function, the onset of the coda is often not readily perceived. In many cases, the coda is obviously under way before it expresses any sense of formal initiation. For this reason, the "start" of the coda is best located at that moment when the music of the recapitulation no longer corresponds to that of the exposition, even if that moment is not perceived as a structural beginning.

# Construction/Recognition
- **Clear start**: rhythmic break, texture change, new initiating unit (basic idea or presentation); sometimes indicated by double-bar repeat signs
- **Ambiguous start**: coda begins with a medial or concluding function (continuation, cadential, sequential activity)
- **Rule of thumb**: locate where the recapitulation departs from the exposition's path
- Frequently the coda starts with a new model-sequence passage or sequentially repeats the last idea of the recapitulation
- If the exposition closes with a retransition, the recapitulation may have a similar passage leading to IV or II for the coda
- A true structural beginning may not appear until later in the coda (if at all)

# Musical Context
The difficulty of locating the coda's start reflects the fact that it is an optional section without a mandatory formal opening. Many codas grow organically out of the recapitulation's closing material.

# Examples
- Mozart, String Quintet in G Minor, K. 516, i (ex. 12.1): coda starts at m. 232 notationally (after repeat sign) but the functional beginning is not sensed until m. 235 with the main theme's basic idea
- Mozart, Symphony No. 36 ("Linz"), K. 425, i (ex. 12.2): coda starts at m. 269 where music departs from the exposition's path
- Haydn, Symphony No. 97 in C, i (ex. 12.3): coda starts with sequential repetition of the cadential idea; no true beginning until m. 279
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, iv (ex. 12.4): coda starts at m. 140 (where music departs from exposition) but the functional beginning is the return of main-theme material at m. 143

# Common Confusions
- The notational start (repeat signs, double bar) may differ from the audible start (a new beginning)
- The analytical start (where the music departs from the exposition) may differ from both
- Students should not expect every coda to begin with a clear new theme
