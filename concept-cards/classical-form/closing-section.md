# Quick Definition
A postcadential passage following a PAC that consists of a series of codettas, prolonging the tonic and dissipating energy through a recessive dynamic.

# Formal Definition
A closing section consists of a series of codettas following a PAC. The initial codetta is typically repeated, then fragmented. Its grouping structure can resemble a sentence. A closing section fulfills postcadential function--following the structural end of the theme. It features a recessive dynamic, dissipating accumulated energy. Codettas prolong root-position tonic through pedal, I-V alternation, or local tonicization of IV. Melodic activity centers on ^1.

# Construction/Recognition
- Follows a PAC (the structural end of the theme)
- Consists of codettas (repetition + fragmentation)
- Prolongs root-position tonic harmony
- Melodic activity centers on ^1
- Features recessive dynamic

# Musical Context
Closing sections appear after PAC-ending themes in sonata expositions, minuets, and other forms. Sometimes called "closing themes" in traditional theories, but Caplin insists they are postcadential, not thematic.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 42-48: three short codettas following the subordinate theme's PAC
- Beethoven, Op. 135, iii, mm. 10-13: codettas based on cadential idea with fragmentation

# Common Confusions
- A closing section is postcadential, not cadential. Only material leading to the cadence has cadential function.
