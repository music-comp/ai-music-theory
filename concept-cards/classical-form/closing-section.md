# Quick Definition
A postcadential passage following a PAC that consists of a series of codettas, prolonging the tonic and dissipating energy through a recessive dynamic, with a grouping structure that can resemble a sentence.

# Core Definition
"A closing section to a theme consists of a series of codettas; rarely does a closing section contain a single codetta" (Ch. 1, p. 16). "In most cases the initial codetta is repeated, after which fragmentation brings reduced versions of the same codetta or else entirely new ones. The grouping structure of an extensive closing section can therefore resemble a sentence" (Ch. 1, p. 16). Traditional theories sometimes call closing sections "closing themes" or "cadence themes," but "these codettas do not constitute a genuine theme" and are "clearly postcadential in function" (Ch. 1, p. 20).

# Prerequisites
- **Postcadential function** -- the broader category
- **Codetta** -- the constituent unit

# Key Properties
1. Follows a PAC (the structural end of the theme)
2. Consists of a series of codettas (repetition + fragmentation)
3. Prolongs root-position tonic harmony
4. Melodic activity centers on scale-degree 1
5. Features recessive dynamic
6. Grouping structure can resemble a sentence (codetta + repetition + fragmentation)

# Construction / Recognition
- Locate the PAC that ends the theme proper
- Everything following that prolongs tonic = closing section
- Identify individual codettas and their repetition/fragmentation pattern

# Context & Application
Closing sections appear after PAC-ending themes in sonata expositions, minuets, and other forms. They are an important part of the subordinate theme complex, where they follow the essential expositional closure.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 1, p. 17, ex. 1.6): Beethoven, Op. 135, iii, mm. 10-13: one-measure codetta (cadential melody in cello), repeated (upper voice, m. 11), then fragmented to half-measure codetta over tonic pedal with neighboring IV.
**Example 2** (Ch. 1, p. 20): Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 42-48: three short codettas following the subordinate theme's PAC.

# Common Errors
- Calling a closing section a "cadential area" or "cadence phrase"

# Common Confusions
- A closing section is postcadential, not cadential. "Only the material leading to the cadential arrival can truly be said to fulfill cadential function" (Ch. 1, p. 17)
- An individual codetta may contain a cadential progression internally but still serves a postcadential role
