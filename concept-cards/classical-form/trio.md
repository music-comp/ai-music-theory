# Quick Definition
The second component minuet in a minuet/trio movement, providing contrast through simpler texture, more diatonic harmony, and sometimes a change of mode or tonality.

# Core Definition

# Prerequisites
See front matter.

# Key Properties
See Core Definition.
The trio follows the norms of minuet form but generally brings simplification and relaxation rather than increased complexity. It tends to be shorter and more symmetrical. About half remain in the same key; one-quarter shift mode; one-quarter change key. The composer may forge stronger overall form by making the trio dependent on the minuet through modal/tonal changes, retransitions, or structural incompleteness (similar to incomplete interior themes in large ternary form).

# Construction / Recognition
- Provides contrast in melody, rhythm, and texture
- Generally simpler than the minuet proper
- Shorter and more symmetrical
- May be structurally incomplete (open on dominant), creating dependence on the minuet
- A retransition may link the trio to the da capo

# Context & Application
The trio provides contrast while maintaining the same meter and tempo.

# Common Confusions
- When the trio changes mode or remains incomplete, it resembles an interior theme. But many trios are in the same key and fully closed, creating a different relationship.
