# Quick Definition
A dramatic, emotionally intense musical character associated with transitions, featuring forte dynamics, minor-mode inflections, and agitated rhythmic patterns that contribute to the energy gain and tonal destabilization of the transition.

# Formal Definition
Sturm und Drang character refers to a musical style of heightened dramatic intensity associated with the German literary movement of the same name. In the context of classical form, this character frequently appears in transitions, where its dramatic intensity serves the function of tonal destabilization. The style is marked by forte or fortissimo dynamics, minor-mode inflections (even in major-key movements), syncopation, sudden dynamic contrasts, and agitated rhythmic patterns. It is particularly effective when the transition begins from a nontonic region (especially VI), where the immediate modal contrast reinforces the Sturm und Drang effect.

# Construction/Recognition
1. Forte or fortissimo dynamics (often in contrast to preceding piano)
2. Minor-mode inflections or modal contrast
3. Agitated rhythmic patterns
4. Syncopation and sforzandi
5. Dramatic, emotionally intense character
6. Often associated with the beginning of the transition
7. Particularly effective when beginning from VI (submediant)
8. Contributes to energy gain and forward drive

# Musical Context
The Sturm und Drang character serves the transition's formal function by creating a sense of dramatic urgency appropriate to tonal destabilization. Its emotional intensity contrasts with both the preceding main theme (typically more balanced and contained) and the subsequent subordinate theme (which often begins in relative calm). The style is named after the late-18th-century German literary movement emphasizing emotional extremity and dramatic conflict, qualities that map naturally onto the transition's role of disrupting tonal stability.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in F, K. 332/300k, i, m. 23 (abrupt shift to forte dynamic and Sturm und Drang style at the transition beginning from VI; dramatic contrast with preceding codettas)

# Common Confusions
- Sturm und Drang character is a STYLE descriptor, not a formal function -- it describes the manner of expression, not the formal role
- Not all transitions feature Sturm und Drang character; many use brilliant style or other textures instead
- Sturm und Drang in a transition is functionally motivated (tonal destabilization), not merely decorative
- The term applies to a broader aesthetic movement, but Caplin uses it specifically to describe the character of certain transitional passages
