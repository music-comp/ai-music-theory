# Quick Definition
A two-part sonata form consisting of an exposition followed directly by a recapitulation, with no development section and no exposition repeat.

# Formal Definition
Sonata without development consists of a sonata exposition followed immediately by a recapitulation, to which a coda may optionally be appended. The exposition is never repeated. A retransition regularly appears between the exposition and recapitulation. The recapitulation follows the same norms as in a regular sonata, with a secondary development frequently appearing to compensate for the absent development. The form is employed most often by Mozart, with significant examples by Haydn and Beethoven.

# Construction/Recognition
- Exposition organized like a standard sonata exposition, but never repeated
- A retransition connects the exposition to the recapitulation
- No development section intervenes
- A secondary development often appears in the recapitulation
- An optional coda may be appended
- A truncated recapitulation (main theme only) creates resemblance to large ternary

# Musical Context
This form is most commonly used in slow movements but occasionally appears in fast movements and overtures. It maintains the tonal drama of sonata form while avoiding the length a development section would add.

# Examples
- Mozart: Symphony No. 39, K. 543, ii; String Quintet in C, K. 515, iii; Piano Sonata in F, K. 332/300k, ii
- Haydn: String Quartet in B-flat, Op. 33/4, iii
- Beethoven: Piano Sonata in F Minor, Op. 2/1, ii; Piano Sonata in D Minor, Op. 31/2, ii
- Truncated recapitulation: Mozart, Clarinet Quintet in A, K. 581, ii; Haydn, String Quartet in G, Op. 33/5, ii

# Common Confusions
- On first hearing, the listener cannot distinguish a sonata without development from a regular sonata whose exposition is being repeated. The rondo interpretation is excluded only once the subordinate theme appears in the home key.
- A truncated recapitulation closely resembles a large ternary, but the two should be distinguished based on real-time experience.
