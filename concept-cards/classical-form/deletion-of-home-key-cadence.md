# Quick Definition
The omission of the main theme's closing cadence in the recapitulation, justified by the ample opportunity to confirm the home key later in the subordinate theme.

# Core Definition
Since there is ample opportunity to confirm the home key later in the recapitulation (in the subordinate theme), a cadence to end the main theme is dispensable. A subsequent closing section may also be omitted because the home key hardly requires reinforcement at this point in the form. This deletion reflects the main theme's changed function: home-key confirmation is no longer its primary role. The home key was reestablished toward the end of the development and will receive ultimate confirmation in the subordinate-theme area.

# Prerequisites
- **Recapitulation**: The formal context
- **Main theme**: The section whose cadence is deleted
- **Authentic cadence**: The cadence type that is omitted

# Key Properties
1. The main theme's PAC is dispensable because the subordinate theme will confirm the home key
2. Associated closing sections may also be deleted
3. The home key was already reestablished at the end of the development
4. The main theme may end on a non-tonic harmony (e.g., I6) and flow into the transition
5. Often occurs alongside other main-theme alterations (deletion of restatements, secondary development)
6. May enable or facilitate fusion of main theme and transition

# Construction / Recognition
- The main theme in the exposition closes with a PAC, but in the recapitulation this cadence is omitted
- The main theme may end with a half cadence instead, or flow directly into the transition
- Check whether associated closing sections have also been deleted
- Note what replaces the cadential function (direct move to transition, secondary development, etc.)

# Context & Application
The home key has already been reestablished at the end of the development. The recapitulation's main theme functions primarily as a signal of "return" and a local harmonic resolution of the development's final dominant. Extended home-key confirmation is postponed to the subordinate theme area, where the most powerful cadential emphasis in the form is accorded to the home key.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in A, Op. 2/2, i (ex. 11.1): main theme's cadence eliminated alongside deletion of restatement
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in G, K. 283, i (ex. 11.2): new continuation ends on I6 with no cadential closure; transition follows immediately

# Common Errors
- Treating cadence deletion as a compositional weakness or oversight

# Common Confusions
- **Not a weakness**: Deletion of the cadence is a deliberate compositional choice reflecting the changed formal needs of the recapitulation
- **Functional motivation**: The cadence is omitted because the subordinate theme will provide home-key confirmation; the main theme no longer needs to bear this burden
- **Enabling fusion**: This deletion sometimes enables or facilitates the fusion of main theme and transition
