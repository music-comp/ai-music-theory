# Quick Definition
After a PAC-ending main theme, codettas appear to constitute a postcadential closing section but are retrospectively understood to initiate the transition when the music modulates or otherwise departs from home-key tonic instead of remaining postcadential.

# Core Definition
A main theme that ends with a PAC can be followed by a closing section consisting of codettas. On occasion, however, such codettas merge directly into material that no longer seems part of a closing section but rather is more typical of a transition. In these cases, the codettas appear at first to have a postcadential function in relation to the main theme, but they are then understood retrospectively to initiate (usually as a presentation) the transition proper. We can thus refer to this situation as a false closing section, one that "becomes" the initiating unit of the transition. The reinterpretation typically occurs when the repeated codetta idea moves to a nontonic harmony (often vi) rather than remaining on the tonic.

# Prerequisites
- **Transition**: The formal function that the false closing section initiates
- **Closing section**: The postcadential function initially implied
- **Codetta**: The individual units that make up the apparent closing section
- **Main-theme function in sonata**: The preceding formal unit ending with PAC

# Key Properties
1. Main theme must end with PAC (precondition for a closing section to seem plausible)
2. Codettas initially appear postcadential (tonic harmony, conventional material)
3. Retrospective reinterpretation: codettas "become" the presentation of the transition
4. The moment of departure from tonic harmony signals the reinterpretation
5. vi (submediant) is a common harmony triggering the departure
6. The transition arrow notation (=>) captures the "becoming" process
7. A genuine closing section after a PAC typically separates from the transition with a rhythmic break

# Construction / Recognition
- After a PAC, note codetta-like material
- Check whether the codettas remain on tonic (genuine closing section) or depart (false closing section)
- If the material leads to nontonic harmony and then modulates, reinterpret the codettas as a transition's opening
- Note the codetta's basic idea becoming the transition's basic idea through this reinterpretation
- The notation "false closing section => presentation" captures the process

# Context & Application
The false closing section is one of four principal opening strategies for transitions (alongside new material, main-theme material, and nontonic region). It creates an especially smooth connection between main theme and transition because the listener initially hears no formal break whatsoever. The retrospective nature of the reinterpretation is analytically significant: the transition's beginning is confirmed only when the music clearly departs from postcadential function.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Trio in E-flat, H. 30, i, mm. 15-26 (Ch. 11, Ex. 11.8): main theme PAC at m. 16; violin sounds codetta-like 2-m. ideas (mm. 16-19); piano repeats embellished version (mm. 20-23); move to VI at m. 24 signals transition; the codettas are retrospectively understood as presentation of a two-part transition's first part
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in C, Op. 2/3, iv (Ch. 11, Ex. 11.18): false closing section leads to what appears to be an A' section of a small ternary, retrospectively understood as transition

# Common Errors
- Treating all postcadential codettas as part of a genuine closing section
- Missing the moment of harmonic departure that triggers the reinterpretation
- Not recognizing the retrospective nature of the false closing section

# Common Confusions
- **Prospective vs. retrospective**: The false closing section is understood only in retrospect -- initially it sounds postcadential
- **Genuine vs. false**: A genuine closing section stays on tonic; a false one departs to nontonic harmony
- **The break**: A genuine closing section typically ends with a distinct rhythmic break before the transition; a false closing section merges directly into transitional material
