# Quick Definition
The central section of sonata form that generates the greatest degree of tonal and phrase-structural instability, standing between the exposition and recapitulation.

# Formal Definition
As a formal unit, the development stands between an exposition and a recapitulation. As a formal function, a development generates the greatest degree of tonal and phrase-structural instability in the movement and thus motivates a restoration of stability (to be accomplished by the recapitulation). It is a higher-level analogue to the contrasting middle of the small ternary form, featuring looser organization, emphasis on sequential progressions, avoidance of authentic cadential closure in the home key, and an ending on dominant harmony.

# Construction/Recognition
- Typically begins with a pre-core, followed by a core (model-sequence-fragmentation)
- Features extensive sequential progressions and tonal exploration
- Avoids authentic cadential closure in the home key
- Usually ends with a standing on the dominant of the home key
- Normally contains multiple themelike units
- Dynamic level typically forte during core sections
- Material usually derives from the exposition, though new ideas may appear

# Musical Context
The development section appears in sonata form, sonata-rondo, and concerto movements. It explores minor-mode regions of the home key (VI, III, II in major; IV, V in minor). Mozart and Beethoven favor pre-core/core technique; Haydn often constructs developments without a genuine core.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in A Minor, K. 310, i (ex. 10.3): clear core with model, sequence, fragmentation, and standing on the dominant
- Haydn, Symphony No. 102 in B-flat, ii (ex. 10.17): development without a core, using transition-like units
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Hob. XVI:49, i (ex. 10.4): two-core development

# Common Confusions
- The development is not simply a "free" section with haphazard modulations; it follows a clear tonal plan
- "Development" refers both to a formal unit (a section) and a formal function (generating instability)
- A development section differs from a contrasting middle primarily in length, complexity, and number of thematic units
