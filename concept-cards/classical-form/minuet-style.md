# Quick Definition
The characteristic stylistic traits of the classical minuet: triple meter (usually 3/4), moderate tempo, predominantly quarter- and eighth-note values, and relatively simple homophonic texture, frequently featuring a quarter-note anacrusis.

# Core Definition
Minuet style is defined by a specific collection of stylistic traits: triple meter (usually notated as 3/4), a moderate tempo, a preponderance of quarter- and eighth-note rhythmic values (with occasional half and sixteenth notes), and a relatively simple, homophonic texture (though imitative passages arise occasionally). Unlike the Baroque minuet, whose phrases almost always begin directly on the downbeat, the classical minuet frequently features a quarter-note upbeat ("anacrusis") to help launch the dance (Caplin, p. 629).

# Key Properties
1. Triple meter, usually notated as 3/4
2. Moderate tempo
3. Predominantly quarter-note and eighth-note rhythmic values
4. Relatively simple, homophonic texture
5. Frequent quarter-note anacrusis (unlike Baroque minuet)

# Construction / Recognition
1. Check for 3/4 time signature with moderate tempo marking
2. Look for predominantly quarter- and eighth-note rhythmic activity
3. Note the homophonic texture as default
4. Identify quarter-note upbeats launching phrases

# Context & Application
The classical minuet derives from a Baroque dance type of the same name. By the mid-18th century, most Baroque dance types (allemande, courante, sarabande, gigue) were replaced by other forms, but the minuet survived and was incorporated into classical genres such as the symphony, quartet, and solo sonata.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming all minuets must begin on the downbeat. **Correction**: The classical minuet frequently begins with a quarter-note anacrusis, unlike the Baroque minuet.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Conflating minuet style with scherzo style. **Clarification**: Both share the same formal organization but differ in tempo, character, and rhythmic intensity.
