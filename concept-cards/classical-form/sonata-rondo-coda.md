# Quick Definition
In sonata-rondo form, the coda is a required element (unlike in regular sonata form) because it includes the final return of the main theme; the coda begins at the point where the recapitulation stops corresponding to the exposition.

# Core Definition
In sonata-rondo form, the coda is a required element because it contains the final return of the main theme (refrain 4). Unlike a regular sonata, where the coda is optional, the sonata-rondo must include a distinct section after the recapitulation of the subordinate-theme complex. The relation of the final refrain to the coda is complicated: sometimes the final refrain appears directly after the closing section of the recapitulation, but sometimes the recapitulation leads into music already belonging to the coda, and the refrain returns only later. To simplify, the coda starts at the point where the music of the recapitulation stops corresponding to the exposition. The rondo refrain always appears somewhere in the coda, often at its very beginning, but sometimes only after the coda is under way (Caplin, pp. 682-683).

# Key Properties
1. Required element of sonata-rondo (unlike optional coda in regular sonata)
2. Contains the final return of the main theme (refrain 4)
3. Begins where the recapitulation stops corresponding to the exposition
4. The final refrain may appear at the coda's beginning or later within it
5. The coda "embraces" the final refrain rather than following it

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify where the recapitulation ceases to correspond to the exposition
2. That point marks the beginning of the coda
3. Locate the final return of the refrain within the coda
4. The coda may include developmental qualities, new material, references to couplets, or additional refrain statements

# Context & Application
The required coda distinguishes the sonata-rondo from the regular sonata, where the coda is optional. This requirement exists because the rondo aesthetic demands a final statement of the refrain, which has no analogue in sonata form.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating the sonata-rondo coda as optional. **Correction**: The coda is required in sonata-rondo because it must contain the final refrain return.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Whether the coda follows or embraces the final refrain. **Clarification**: The coda embraces the final refrain; the refrain appears within the coda, not before it.
