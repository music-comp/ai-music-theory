# Quick Definition
In a compound sentence, the continuation and cadential functions can often be fully expressed in fewer than the eight measures offered by the model form, resulting in a compressed continuation of only four measures. The composer typically restores the sixteen-measure length through repetition of the continuation phrase or by adding a postcadential extension or standing on the dominant.

# Formal Definition
Whereas it is in the nature of the period to maintain balance between its antecedent and consequent functions, the sentence, with its forward-striving character, has less need to preserve symmetrical phrase structure. The formal functions of continuation and cadence can often be fully expressed in fewer than the eight measures offered by the model form. Many compound sentences compress their continuation and cadential functions into a single four-measure continuation or continuation => cadential phrase, like that of a simple sentence. Even when the continuation is compressed, the composer may restore a semblance of symmetrical organization by repeating the continuation phrase or by adding a postcadential extension.

# Construction/Recognition
1. Continuation occupies only four measures (mm. 9-12) instead of the normative eight
2. Fragmentation may jump from four-measure units directly to one-measure units (4:1 ratio instead of 2:1)
3. Symmetry may be restored by:
   - Repeating the entire continuation phrase (if first attempt ends with DC, repeat achieves PAC)
   - Adding a standing on the dominant after HC
   - Postcadential extension
4. The total theme length typically remains sixteen measures despite the compression

# Musical Context
The compressed continuation is the deviation technique most characteristic of the sixteen-measure sentence. It reflects the sentence's essential character: the ability to achieve its goals (fragmentation, acceleration, cadence) efficiently, without requiring the full time span that a symmetric structure would suggest. The various methods of restoring sixteen-measure length (repetition, standing on the dominant) are themselves formally significant events.

# Examples
- Haydn, String Quartet in G, Op. 77/1, i, mm. 1-14: continuation compressed to six measures with 4:1 fragmentation ratio; descending third sequence
- Mozart, Clarinet Trio in E-flat, K. 498, i, mm. 1-16: four-measure continuation => cadential ending with DC at m. 12; phrase repeated to achieve PAC, restoring symmetry
- Haydn, Symphony No. 83 ("The Hen"), i, mm. 1-16: compressed continuation to HC at m. 12; four-measure standing on the dominant extends passage to sixteen measures

# Common Confusions
- Compressed continuation does not mean the compound sentence is shorter than sixteen measures — restoration techniques typically bring it back to the normative length
- A compressed continuation is not a deficiency — it reflects the sentence's inherent efficiency and forward drive
- The 4:1 fragmentation ratio (four-measure to one-measure) is more aggressive than the normative 2:1 ratio but is entirely natural in the compressed context
