# Quick Definition
A specific passage in the development whose primary function is to modulate back to the home key following the cadential articulation of a development key.

# Core Definition
Although most developments express a general retransition function (returning from subordinate or development key to home key), only some include a specific passage whose primary function is retransitional. It is preferable to restrict the term to those passages in particular. Most typically, a retransition is a complete phrase or full themelike unit that follows the cadential articulation of a development key. At times, the retransition may consist of the home-key dominant exclusively, but only when that harmony directly follows the dominant of the preceding development key. A retransition sometimes starts with reference to the main theme's opening material in the development key, creating a false recapitulation that eventually leads back to the home key for the true recapitulation.

# Prerequisites
- **Development section**: The formal context
- **Standing on the dominant**: Often concludes the retransition
- **Development key**: The key from which the retransition departs

# Key Properties
1. Follows the cadential articulation of a development key
2. Modulates back to the home key
3. May begin with a false closing section (codettas that become retransitional)
4. Ends with HC confirming the tonal return, followed by standing on the dominant
5. May begin with false recapitulation (main-theme material in development key)
6. The standing on the dominant is a postcadential prolongation, not the retransition itself
7. Not all developments have a distinct retransition
8. The term should be restricted to passages with clearly retransitional primary function

# Construction / Recognition
- Locate the cadential confirmation of a development key (PAC or HC)
- Identify the passage that modulates back to the home key
- Note whether it begins with false closing section or false recapitulation
- Find the HC in the home key that confirms the return
- Observe the standing on the dominant that typically follows
- Distinguish the retransition from the standing on the dominant (different functions)

# Context & Application
By the time the standing on the dominant begins, the home key has already been achieved (confirmed by HC or dominant arrival). If the term retransition is to be used, it should be applied BEFORE the standing on the dominant -- at the moment when the modulation to the home key takes place. Not every development has a distinct retransition; in many, the core leads directly to the home-key dominant.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Hob. XVI:49, i, mm. 85-96 (ex. 10.18): retransition begins with false closing section; modulation to home key at m. 87; HC at m. 91; standing on dominant mm. 91-96
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in G, Op. 14/2, i, mm. 98-124 (ex. 10.20): false recapitulation begins like main theme but in development key; modulation back to home key; HC at m. 107; standing on dominant to m. 125
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in B-flat, K. 333, i, mm. 87-97: after pseudo-core ends, home-key dominant reintroduced at m. 87; standing on dominant for remainder of development

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Development section (the formal context)
- Development key (the key being left behind)

## Enables
- Recapitulation (prepared by the retransition's return to home key)
- Standing on the dominant (follows the retransition's HC)

## Related
- False recapitulation (may begin the retransition)
- Standing on the dominant (postcadential prolongation following the retransition)
- False closing section (may begin the retransition)

## Contrasts With
- Transition (moves from home key to subordinate key; retransition moves back to home key)

# Common Errors
- Equating the standing on the dominant with the retransition (they are different functions)
- Applying "retransition" to every development that returns to the home key (should be reserved for distinct passages)
- Confusing a false recapitulation with the actual recapitulation

# Common Confusions
- **Retransition vs. standing on dominant**: The standing on the dominant is a postcadential prolongation; the retransition is the passage that modulates back to the home key
- **General vs. specific retransition**: Most developments return to the home key (general retransition function), but only some contain a distinct retransitional passage (specific)
- **False recapitulation**: Material that sounds recapitulatory but occurs in the wrong key; eventually redirected to the true home key
