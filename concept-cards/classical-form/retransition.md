# Quick Definition
A specific passage in the development whose primary function is to modulate back to the home key following the cadential articulation of a development key.

# Formal Definition
Although most developments express a general retransition function (returning from subordinate or development key to the home key), only some include a specific passage whose primary function is retransitional. Most typically, a retransition is a complete phrase or full themelike unit following the cadential articulation of a development key. It may consist of the home-key dominant exclusively, but only when that harmony directly follows the dominant of the preceding development key. A retransition sometimes starts with a false recapitulation.

# Construction/Recognition
- Follows the cadential confirmation of a development key
- Modulates back to the home key
- May begin with a false closing section
- Ends with a half cadence confirming the tonal return, followed by standing on the dominant
- Sometimes begins with a false recapitulation

# Musical Context
Not all developments have a distinct retransition -- in many cases, the core leads directly to the home-key dominant.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Hob. XVI:49, i (ex. 10.18): retransition (mm. 85-96) begins with false closing section
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in G, Op. 14/2, i (ex. 10.20): false recapitulation at start of retransition

# Common Confusions
- The standing on the dominant is not itself the retransition
- Not every development has a distinct retransition
- The term should be reserved for passages with a clearly retransitional primary function
