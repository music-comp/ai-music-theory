# Quick Definition
The thematic unit in a sonata exposition that confirms the subordinate key through PAC, employing a distinctly looser organization than the main theme with extended and expanded formal functions.

# Formal Definition
The subordinate theme confirms the subordinate key through PAC. It contains the same formal functions as the main theme (presentation, continuation, cadential) but these assume a distinctly looser form through extensions, expansions, harmonic ambiguity, and asymmetrical groupings. Its primary function is confirming the new tonal center. Detailed treatment in Chapter 8.

# Construction/Recognition
- Confirms subordinate key with PAC
- Begins with a new basic idea (often in a presentation phrase)
- Continuation typically extended; cadential function typically expanded (E.C.P.)
- Asymmetrical phrase groupings
- May include closing section after PAC
- Presentation may be loosened by harmonic ambiguity (dominant pedal)

# Musical Context
The subordinate theme is the primary cadential goal of the exposition. Its loose organization contrasts with the main theme's tight-knit design.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 21-48: presentation (mm. 21-24, dominant pedal), extended continuation (mm. 25-32), expanded cadential phrases (mm. 33-41, with evasion), closing section (mm. 42-48). Asymmetrical 4 + 8 + 4 + 5 + 7.

# Common Confusions
- The subordinate theme's looseness uses specific techniques (extension, expansion, cadential evasion), not disorganization.
