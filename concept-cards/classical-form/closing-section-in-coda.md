# Quick Definition
The final series of codettas that ends the coda following the last PAC, sometimes using new material, sometimes restoring material deleted from the recapitulation.

# Core Definition
Most codas end with a closing section (made up of codettas) that follows the final PAC of the home key. The melodic-motivic content of this closing section may be new, or it may be based on material appearing earlier in the movement. Sometimes the closing section is extremely short, or it may be omitted altogether. If the closing section from the recapitulation has been eliminated (because the coda starts immediately after the subordinate-theme group), the coda will likely restore that deleted material as its own closing section. When that is the case, the coda can sometimes give the impression of being interpolated in, rather than following on, the recapitulation. Sometimes main-theme material is repurposed as the coda's closing section, without receiving cadential closure -- the root-position tonic is retained obsessively.

# Prerequisites
- **Coda**: The section this closing section concludes
- **Codetta**: The constituent unit

# Key Properties
1. Follows the final PAC of the coda
2. Made up of codettas prolonging root-position tonic
3. Content may be new or drawn from earlier in the movement
4. May be very short or absent
5. May restore deleted closing section from the recapitulation
6. When restoring deleted material, the coda seems "interpolated"
7. Main-theme material may function as closing section (without cadential closure)

# Construction / Recognition
- Locate the final PAC of the coda
- Identify the subsequent codettas prolonging tonic
- Check whether the material is new, from the exposition, or restored from a deleted recapitulation closing section
- Note whether main-theme material appears as codettas rather than a theme

# Context & Application
The closing section provides the very last sounds of the movement. Its character determines the final impression: triumphant fanfare, quiet dissolution, or simple tonic affirmation.

# Examples
- Haydn, Symphony No. 97 in C, i (ex. 12.9): main-theme fanfare returns at m. 279 without cadential activity; root-position tonic retained obsessively; functions as closing section rather than a coda theme
- Haydn, Piano Trio in C, Hob. XV:27, iii (ex. 12.8): closing section deleted from the recapitulation reappears as the coda's own closing section

# Common Errors
- Confusing main-theme material functioning as a closing section (no cadential closure) with a true coda theme (PAC)

# Common Confusions
- **Hierarchical nesting**: The closing section of the coda consists of codettas, just as the exposition's closing section does -- demonstrating that codettas exist at a lower hierarchical level
- **Interpolation effect**: When the coda restores the recapitulation's deleted closing section, the coda seems "inserted" between the subordinate theme and the closing section
- **Theme vs. closing section**: Main-theme material without cadential closure functions as part of the closing section, not as a separate coda theme
