# Quick Definition
A four-measure unit at the beginning of the second part of a small binary that is organized identically to the B section of a small ternary, emphasizing dominant harmony, but is followed by non-recapitulatory material rather than a return of the basic idea.

# Core Definition
The second part of a small binary often begins with a four-measure unit identical in organization to a contrasting middle of the small ternary: it emphasizes dominant harmony, creates tonal contrast, and may end with an HC. If a recapitulation were to follow, a rounded binary (small ternary) would result. Instead, the material that follows expresses continuation, cadential, consequent, or further dominant prolongation, confirming the binary interpretation. The B section's motivic material often derives from the first part's basic idea, which further dampens recapitulatory expectations.

# Prerequisites
- **Small binary**: The overarching form
- **Contrasting middle**: The formal function being applied

# Key Properties
1. Four-measure unit at the beginning of the second part
2. Emphasizes dominant harmony (standing on dominant, model-sequence, etc.)
3. Followed by non-recapitulatory material (continuation, cadential, consequent, or further dominant prolongation)
4. Material following B may relate to the first part's continuation or be entirely new
5. B section's motivic material often derives from the first part's basic idea
6. May even modulate to and cadentially confirm a subordinate key (with retransition back)
7. The absence of subsequent recapitulation is what confirms binary (not ternary) form

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify dominant emphasis at the beginning of part 2
2. Check whether the material following the B section recapitulates the basic idea (if so: ternary)
3. If no recapitulation: identify the function of the subsequent material
4. Note whether the B section's melodic material derives from the first part

# Context & Application
The contrasting middle in a binary creates a momentary expectation for recapitulation, which is then denied. This unfulfilled expectation is one of the defining features of the small binary form. After an elaborate B section with cadence in a subordinate key and retransition, the absence of recapitulation may be particularly surprising, but it confirms the binary interpretation.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Trio in A-flat, Hob. XV:14, ii, mm. 9-12: dominant version of basic idea leads to HC; followed by continuation from first part, tonally adjusted
- Beethoven, Fifteen Variations, Op. 35, mm. 74-77: standing on dominant; followed by cadential phrase with ECP
- Beethoven, Violin Sonata in A, Op. 30/1, iii, mm. 17-20: standing on dominant; followed by new consequent
- Mozart, String Quartet in D Minor, K. 421, iv, mm. 9-20: elaborate B section modulating to III with PAC, retransition, then cadential phrase emphasizing Neapolitan
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E, Hob. XVI:22, iii, mm. 9-12: contrasting middle closing with HC; followed by further dominant prolongation

# Common Errors
- Assuming a contrasting middle makes the form ternary -- only recapitulation would do that
- Confusing a phrase beginning with basic-idea material serving cadential function (over ECP) with a recapitulation
- Failing to distinguish B section material that happens to derive from the basic idea from a genuine recapitulatory return

# Common Confusions
- A contrasting middle in the second part does NOT make the form ternary -- only recapitulation would do that
- The B section material often derives from the first part's basic idea (unlike small ternaries where B typically contrasts motivically)
- After an elaborate B section with cadence and retransition, the absence of recapitulation may be surprising but confirms binary
- A phrase using basic idea material over an ECP is cadential, not recapitulatory
