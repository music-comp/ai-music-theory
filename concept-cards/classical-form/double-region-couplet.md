# Quick Definition
A type of couplet 2 found exclusively in Mozart's sonata-rondos that emphasizes two tonal regions (typically submediant and subdominant), with a distinct thematic unit associated with each region.

# Core Definition
In a number of Mozart's sonata-rondos, couplet 2 emphasizes two tonal regions -- usually submediant and subdominant. The phrase-structural organization does not result in two interior themes; rather, each region is associated with a distinct thematic unit, only one of which may be an interior theme. Both units may be constructed as simpler main-theme types (sentence, period, or hybrid), which may be more or less tight knit and may even be left incomplete. Most often, the submediant region precedes the subdominant, but the reverse order occurs occasionally. The two regions are sometimes linked by a transition, which may range from a short phrase to a lengthy developmental passage. This type is used exclusively by Mozart.

# Construction / Recognition
- Located in couplet 2 position in a sonata-rondo
- Two tonal regions emphasized, typically vi and IV
- Each region has its own distinct thematic unit
- Units may be simple forms (sentence, period, hybrid), not necessarily interior themes
- Usually vi precedes IV (but sometimes reversed)
- A transition may link the two regions
- The couplet typically ends with a retransition that may leave the second theme incomplete
- Used exclusively by Mozart

# Context & Application
The double-region couplet represents a distinctively Mozartean approach to couplet 2 in sonata-rondo form. It provides an alternative to both the development and the single interior theme, offering tonal variety through two contrasting regions rather than developmental motivic working-out.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in B-flat, K. 333/315c, iii, mm. 55-102: Refrain 2 closes at m. 56; transition material from couplet 1 returns; first unit in vi (eight-measure sentence, HC at m. 72); brief transition to IV; second unit (loose sentence beginning m. 76) never achieves closure; retransition from m. 90 returns to home-key dominant at m. 102
- Mozart, Rondo for Piano in F, K. 494/K. 533: Couplet 2 contains a double-region couplet (vi followed by IV)

# Common Confusions
- The double-region couplet is not the same as two consecutive interior themes in different regions. The thematic units may be simpler main-theme types rather than full interior themes.
- This device is used exclusively by Mozart; students should not expect to find it in Haydn or Beethoven.
