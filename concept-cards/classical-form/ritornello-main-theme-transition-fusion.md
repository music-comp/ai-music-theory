# Quick Definition
A common organization of the opening ritornello in which the first part fuses main-theme and transition functions (ending with an HC) and the second part functions as a subordinate theme, creating a bipartite rather than tripartite ritornello.

# Core Definition
Sometimes the opening ritornello appears divided into two principal parts based on cadential articulations. The first part ends with an HC in the home key; the second begins with completely different material and ends with a PAC, usually followed by a closing section. This should not be interpreted as a periodic structure, because the new material in the second part gives no impression of being a consequent. Rather, the first part fuses main-theme and transition functions -- a situation discussed in connection with sonata-form recapitulations but rarely appearing in an exposition. The second part corresponds to the subordinate theme. Mozart, in particular, prefers this type of ritornello for more modest concertos, especially those featuring wind instruments (Caplin, pp. 704-705).

# Key Properties
1. Ritornello divided into two parts, not three
2. First part ends with home-key HC
3. Second part begins with new material (not a consequent)
4. First part fuses main-theme and transition functions
5. Second part functions as subordinate theme
6. Preferred by Mozart for wind concertos and more modest works

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify whether the ritornello divides into two or three principal parts
2. If two parts: the first ends with HC, the second with PAC
3. Check that the second part introduces entirely new material (not consequent-like)
4. Recognize the first part as fusing MT and TR functions

# Context & Application
This fusion creates a more compact ritornello, appropriate for smaller-scale concertos. In the solo exposition, a more normal main theme typically emerges, with a continuation phrase that belongs to the style and character of the opening compound basic idea.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 705): Mozart, Horn Concerto in E-flat, K. 447, i, mm. 1-13 -- opening 8-measure unit ends with HC; subsequent unit brings differing melodic-motivic content with transition-like restless harmonic activity. The solo exposition then produces a more normal main theme.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Interpreting the two-part ritornello as a period (antecedent-consequent). **Correction**: The second part introduces new material, not a consequent response.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Whether the fusion indicates a deficient ritornello. **Clarification**: It is a standard option, especially favored by Mozart for wind concertos.
