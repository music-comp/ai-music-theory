# Quick Definition
A brief passage following the closing section's final codetta that brings the music back to the home key to lead smoothly into the repeat of the exposition, sometimes growing directly from the codetta, sometimes using completely different material, and occasionally anticipating main-theme motives.

# Core Definition
The final codetta of the closing section is sometimes followed by a retransition, a passage that functions to bring the music back to the home key and lead smoothly to a repeat of the exposition. The material of the retransition sometimes grows directly out of the final codetta. At other times, the content of the retransition is quite different from the closing section. On occasion, the retransition anticipates main-theme motives in order to prepare for the repeated exposition. The retransition is a postcadential, after-the-end function that provides a smooth tonal and thematic transition from the subordinate key back to the home key.

# Prerequisites
- **Closing section in subordinate theme**: The formal unit that precedes the retransition
- **Exposition repeat**: The structural reason for the retransition's existence
- **Subordinate theme**: The broader formal context

# Key Properties
1. Follows the closing section's final codetta
2. Functions to bring music back to the home key
3. Leads into the exposition repeat
4. Material may grow from the final codetta or be entirely new
5. May anticipate main-theme motives
6. Postcadential function (follows the EEC and closing section)
7. Not always present (some expositions use first/second ending notation instead)

# Construction / Recognition
- Locate the exposition repeat sign or written-out repeat
- Check the measures immediately before the repeat for material leading back to the home key
- Compare the material with the final codetta: does it grow from it or introduce new content?
- Note whether main-theme motives are anticipated

# Context & Application
The retransition is one of several postcadential functions that can follow the subordinate theme's closing section. Its presence depends on whether the exposition is repeated and whether the tonal distance between the subordinate key and the home key requires a preparatory passage. In minor-mode movements where the subordinate key is the relative major, the retransition may need to effect a more substantial tonal shift.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in F minor, Op. 2/1, i (Ch. 9, Exs. 9.3): closing section followed by retransition leading back to F minor for the exposition repeat
- Many expositions include a retransition within the "first ending" of the repeat structure

# Common Errors
- Treating the retransition as part of the closing section
- Ignoring the retransition when it appears within a first-ending bracket
- Confusing the retransition's tonal function with a genuine modulation

# Common Confusions
- **Retransition vs. codetta**: The retransition has a specifically directional tonal function (leading back to HK), while codettas simply prolong the subordinate key
- **First ending**: The retransition often appears within the first-ending bracket, making it easy to overlook
- **Not always present**: Some expositions simply end with the closing section and use a first/second ending notation
