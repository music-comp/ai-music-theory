# Quick Definition
A main theme constructed with loosening techniques normally associated with subordinate themes (redundant repetitions, extensions, expansions) while still remaining tighter than the actual subordinate theme of the movement.

# Formal Definition
Some main themes are constructed in a manner considerably less tightly knit than would ordinarily be expected. At times, the loosening techniques resemble those of a subordinate theme: redundant repetitions, cadential extensions and expansions, and internal dominant arrivals. Although the main theme's form-functional characteristics may be like those of a subordinate theme, the melodic, rhythmic, and textural aspects usually remain typical of a main theme (especially "opening up" gestures and marked discontinuities). As a rule, a loosely organized nonconventional main theme is still more tightly knit than the subordinate theme appearing later in the exposition.

# Construction/Recognition
- Features loosening devices: redundant repetitions, cadential extensions, expanded cadential progressions
- May include internal dominant arrivals or modulations
- Melodic and rhythmic characteristics remain typical of a main theme (opening gestures, textural variety)
- Always more tightly knit than the subsequent subordinate theme when compared directly
- May resemble a period at a general level but with various expansions rendering it quite loose

# Musical Context
Loosely organized main themes are relatively uncommon but create works with a distinctive character, often one of breadth and expansiveness at the very start of the movement.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in A, Op. 2/2, i (ex. 13.6): opening 8 measures give the initial impression of a sentence, but lack of harmonic activity in the "continuation" (mm. 5-8) prevents this classification; the entire 8-bar group functions as a primary initiating unit; subsequent measures include modulation to V (E major), IAC extended postcadentially; return of basic idea creates a highly expanded antecedent/consequent structure; yet compared with the subordinate theme (ex. 8.12), it is decidedly tighter

# Common Confusions
- A loosely organized main theme is not a subordinate theme — the comparison between them always reveals the main theme to be tighter
- The presence of loosening devices does not change the theme's formal function as the exposition's initiating unit
- The distinction between a loose main theme and a tight subordinate theme requires comparing both within the same movement
