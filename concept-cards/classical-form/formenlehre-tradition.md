# Quick Definition
The German theoretical tradition of systematically teaching musical form through comprehensive treatises, which Caplin's theory revives on more secure foundations drawing from Schoenberg and Ratz.

# Formal Definition
Formenlehre ("teaching of form") is the subdiscipline of music theory devoted to defining and classifying the formal organization of musical compositions. Caplin's immediate sources of inspiration are Arnold Schoenberg's Fundamentals of Musical Composition and Erwin Ratz's Einfuhrung in die musikalische Formenlehre. Central to these theorists is the notion that formal units play specific roles (formal functions) in articulating a work's overall structure. Ratz proposed a fundamental model (Urform) of five formal functions operating at a relatively background level; Caplin extends this to identify a multitude of functions at all hierarchical levels.

# Construction/Recognition
- The Schoenberg/Ratz lineage identifies formal functions as the core analytical tool
- Caplin's theory is empirical and descriptive, not deductive or prescriptive
- Categories are defined as "ideal types" (Idealtypen, following Dahlhaus and Weber)
- Categories are strictly defined but flexibly applied in analysis

# Musical Context
The Formenlehre tradition had largely been abandoned due to Schenker's critique, historicist attitudes, and the "new musicology's" mistrust of systematic models. Caplin revives it by restricting the repertory to the high Viennese classical style (ca. 1780-1810) and grounding the theory in harmonic progression rather than motivic content.

# Examples
- Schoenberg: foundational source for concepts of sentence, basic idea, liquidation
- Ratz: proposed the five-function Urform model
- Caplin: extends these into a comprehensive, multi-level theory of formal functions

# Common Confusions
- Caplin's theory is not a rigid prescriptive system; categories are ideal types that allow flexible analytical application.
- The theory is restricted to the high Viennese classical style (ca. 1780-1810), not all tonal music.
