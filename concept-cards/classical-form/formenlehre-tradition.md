# Quick Definition
The German theoretical tradition of systematically teaching musical form through comprehensive treatises, which Caplin revives on more secure foundations drawing from Schoenberg and Ratz.

# Core Definition
*Formenlehre* ("teaching of form") is the subdiscipline of music theory devoted to defining and classifying the formal organization of musical compositions. Caplin's immediate sources of inspiration are Arnold Schoenberg's *Fundamentals of Musical Composition* and Erwin Ratz's *Einfuhrung in die musikalische Formenlehre*. Central to these theorists is the notion that formal units play specific roles (formal functions) in articulating a work's overall structure. Ratz proposed a fundamental model (*Urform*) of five formal functions operating at a relatively background level; Caplin extends this to identify "a multitude of functions discernible at all levels in a classical movement" (Introduction, p. 3).

# Prerequisites
None -- this is a foundational orienting concept.

# Key Properties
1. Formenlehre systematically defines and classifies formal organization
2. Caplin's theory draws on Schoenberg's sentence/period concepts and Ratz's five-function Urform
3. The tradition had been abandoned due to Schenker's critique, historicist attitudes, and the "new musicology's" mistrust of systematic models
4. Caplin revives it by restricting the repertory to the high Viennese classical style (ca. 1780-1810) and grounding the theory in harmonic progression

# Construction / Recognition
- The Schoenberg/Ratz lineage identifies formal functions as the core analytical tool
- Caplin's theory is empirical and descriptive, not deductive or prescriptive
- Categories are defined as "ideal types" (*Idealtypen*, following Dahlhaus and Weber)
- Categories are strictly defined but flexibly applied in analysis

# Context & Application
The theory is restricted to the instrumental music of Haydn, Mozart, and Beethoven (ca. 1780-1810). This restriction reflects the extraordinary conventionality of formal functions in this style, which enables the establishment of general principles. The theory investigates procedures in increasing order of complexity, following the hierarchy from local to global within an individual movement.

# Common Errors
- Treating Caplin's theory as prescriptive or deductive when it is empirical and descriptive
- Applying the theory beyond the high classical style without adjustment

# Common Confusions
- Caplin's theory is not a rigid prescriptive system; categories are ideal types that allow flexible analytical application
- The theory is restricted to the high Viennese classical style (ca. 1780-1810), not all tonal music
- Despite drawing on Schoenberg, Caplin deliberately sets aside Schoenberg's preoccupation with Grundgestalt and developing variation
