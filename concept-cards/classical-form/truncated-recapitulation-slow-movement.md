# Quick Definition
A deviation in sonata without development form where the recapitulation includes only the main theme, eliminating the transition, subordinate theme, and closing section -- creating a form that resembles a large ternary but is experienced as a sonata.

# Core Definition
In a number of works by Mozart and Haydn, the exposition of a sonata without development is followed only by the main theme, whose structure is the same as in the exposition. The rest of the recapitulation (transition, subordinate theme, closing section) is eliminated, though a coda may be added. When the main theme is built as a small ternary (or binary), the resemblance to the large ternary is even greater: the subordinate-theme complex functions like an interior theme. Despite the similarity, it is preferable to regard the two forms as distinct, because the listener experiences the movement as a sonata without development until the transition and subordinate theme fail to appear in the recapitulation. The possibility of reinterpreting the movement as a large ternary emerges only very much after the fact.

# Prerequisites
- **Sonata without development**: The form in which the truncation occurs
- **Truncated recapitulation**: The general concept of eliminating material from the recapitulation
- **Large ternary form**: The form it resembles

# Key Properties
1. Recapitulation includes only the main theme
2. Transition, subordinate theme, and closing section are eliminated
3. A coda may follow the truncated recapitulation
4. The form resembles a large ternary when the main theme is a small ternary
5. The subordinate-theme complex resembles an interior theme in retrospect
6. The listener's real-time experience is of a sonata without development
7. Reinterpretation as large ternary is retrospective only
8. The two forms should be considered distinct despite their similarity

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the sonata without development form (exposition + retransition + recapitulation)
- Note whether the recapitulation contains only the main theme
- If transition and subordinate theme are absent: truncated recapitulation
- Compare the result with a large ternary (main theme = outer parts; subordinate-theme complex = interior theme)
- Maintain the sonata-without-development interpretation based on real-time listening experience

# Context & Application
This technique creates a significant formal ambiguity. It also often results in the movement ending on the dominant (if the main theme fuses with transition function and ends on V), forging a stronger connection to the following movement.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in C, H. 21, ii, mm. 40-50 (ex. 17.13b): truncated recapitulation; main theme fuses with transition, ending on dominant; Presto follows immediately
- Haydn, String Quartet in B-flat, Op. 33/4, iii: recommended for analysis of truncated recapitulation

# Common Errors
- Automatically reinterpreting the form as a large ternary (the real-time experience matters)
- Assuming the elimination of the subordinate theme is analytically incomplete (it is a deliberate compositional choice)
- Missing the formal ambiguity

# Common Confusions
- **Sonata without development vs. large ternary**: The listener's real-time experience distinguishes them -- the sonata interpretation holds until the subordinate theme fails to appear
- **Truncated vs. missing**: The recapitulation is deliberately truncated, not accidentally incomplete
- **Coda function**: A coda added after the truncated recapitulation may compensate for the eliminated material
