# Quick Definition
The transition is the section most likely to be altered in the recapitulation, requiring tonal adjustment if originally modulatory, and commonly undergoing deletions, compressions, secondary development, and emphasis on flat regions.

# Core Definition
If the original transition is modulatory, the recapitulation's transition must be tonally adjusted to remain in the home key. The adjustment can occur at any point and by various harmonic and phrase-structural means. Frequently, the subdominant region is tonicized, promoting (as a pre-dominant) a logical succession to the dominant. If the original transition is nonmodulating, tonal adjustment is not necessary. Three additional alteration types commonly appear: (1) deletions and compressions, especially of initial tonic-prolongation passages no longer needed; (2) additional model-sequence technique (secondary development); and (3) emphasis on flat tonal regions, providing harmonic contrast between the tonic-focused main theme and subordinate theme.

# Prerequisites
- **Recapitulation**: The formal context
- **Transition**: The original section being altered
- **Tonal adjustment**: The essential procedure for modulatory transitions

# Key Properties
1. Most likely section to be altered in the recapitulation
2. Modulatory transitions must be tonally adjusted; nonmodulating ones may retain original structure
3. Deleted passages are usually from the beginning (tonic prolongations no longer needed)
4. Two-part transitions are typically compressed into one-part nonmodulating structures
5. Flat-region emphasis provides the best harmonic contrast in the recapitulation
6. The return from flat regions to home-key diatonicism makes the subordinate theme sound "fresh and revitalized"
7. The transition destabilizes the home key for a new reason: to permit the subordinate theme to sound fresh when transposed
8. In the most extreme case, the entire transition may be eliminated

# Construction / Recognition
- Compare the transition in exposition and recapitulation measure by measure
- Identify the crux (point where tonal path diverges)
- Note deleted tonic-prolongation passages from the opening
- Look for newly composed sequential passages (secondary development)
- Identify tonicizations of flat-side regions (IV, bVI, bIII, bII)
- Check whether a two-part transition has been compressed to one part

# Context & Application
In the exposition, the transition functions to destabilize the home key and establish a subordinate key. In the recapitulation, it continues to destabilize the home key but for different reasons -- to permit the subordinate theme to sound fresh and to prevent tonal monotony. The transition offers the best opportunity for flat-region emphasis since both the main theme and subordinate theme stress diatonic home-key harmonies. The return from flat regions corresponds to the move from home key to subordinate key in the exposition.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 7, Op. 92, i (ex. 11.4): enormous tonic-pedal passage deleted; new extended continuation with secondary development; same standing on dominant retained
- Mozart, Violin Sonata in C, K. 403, i (ex. 11.5): shift to parallel minor and bVI; new model-sequence passage; premature dominant arrival
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C Minor, K. 457, i (ex. 11.6): canonic variation of basic idea; new material in tonicized bIII (Neapolitan); expanded pre-dominant leading to HC
- Haydn, Piano Trio in E-flat, Hob. XV:30, i (ex. 11.7): two-part transition compressed into single unit; mode change initiates new passage; eliminates redundant and functionally inappropriate material

# Common Errors
- Assuming the transition must retain the same proportions as in the exposition
- Overlooking flat-region emphasis as a deliberate compositional strategy

# Common Confusions
- **Tonal adjustment vs. transposition**: The transition is not simply transposed; it is recomposed with new harmonic progressions
- **Functional motivation**: Tonic-prolongation passages are deleted because they are no longer needed, not arbitrarily
- **Flat-region purpose**: The shift to flat regions is a strategic contrast making the subsequent return to diatonicism feel like the exposition's key change
- **Nonmodulating transitions**: These may remain unchanged, since tonal adjustment is the primary motivating factor for alterations
