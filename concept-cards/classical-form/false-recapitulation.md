# Quick Definition
A passage in the development that begins like the recapitulation by restating the main theme's opening material but in the wrong key, creating a momentary illusion of return.

# Formal Definition
A retransition sometimes starts with reference to the opening material from the main theme, usually in the development key just confirmed by a prior half cadence. The gesture is recapitulatory in melodic-motivic content but the tonal requirements of a genuine recapitulation are not met. Eventually, the music modulates to the home key for the true recapitulation. The passage from false to real recapitulation functions as a retransition.

# Construction/Recognition
- Main theme's basic idea appears in a key other than the home key
- Creates a momentary expectation of the recapitulation
- Eventually recognized as "false" when the music modulates to the home key
- Often appears after the core, at the beginning of the retransition

# Musical Context
False recapitulations are a dramatic device playing on the listener's expectations, particularly effective after long development sections.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in G, Op. 14/2, i (ex. 10.20): after the core concludes on V of Bb (HK: bIII), a new part begins like the main theme but in Bb; music modulates to G for the real recapitulation at m. 125

# Common Confusions
- A false recapitulation is a deliberate dramatic device, not a mistake
- Not every restatement of main-theme material in the development constitutes a false recapitulation
