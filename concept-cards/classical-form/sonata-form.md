# Quick Definition
The most important large-scale formal type in classical instrumental music, consisting of three principal functions — exposition, development, and recapitulation — with optional slow introduction and coda.

# Formal Definition
Sonata form consists of three large-scale functions: exposition, development, and recapitulation. Two additional functions are sometimes included: a slow introduction before the exposition and a coda after the recapitulation. In its large-scale tonal and form-functional organization, sonata form is analogous to the small ternary form. The exposition establishes two contrasting tonal regions; the development generates tonal and phrase-structural instability; the recapitulation resolves the tonal conflict by restating exposition material in the home key. The form exhibits both ternary (three functional sections) and binary (exposition repeat; development-recapitulation repeat) characteristics.

# Construction/Recognition
- **Exposition**: establishes home key and subordinate key; contains main theme, transition, subordinate theme with closing section; normally repeated
- **Development**: explores various tonal regions; features pre-core/core technique or alternatives; ends on home-key dominant
- **Recapitulation**: restates exposition in the home key; tonally adjusts transition and subordinate theme
- **Slow introduction** (optional): precedes exposition; usually in slow tempo; ends on dominant
- **Coda** (optional): follows recapitulation; after-the-end function; compensatory functions
- Development and recapitulation sometimes repeated together (especially pre-1780)

# Musical Context
Almost every multimovement work (except the concerto) contains at least one sonata-form movement. The form is the period's most highly developed and complex compositional design, revealing composers' greatest technical skill and expressive potential. It continued to influence later musical styles through the 20th century.

# Common Confusions
- The ternary vs. binary debate is a false dichotomy — sonata form exhibits both characteristics
- "Sonata form" is not the same as "sonata" (a multi-movement genre); sonata form is a formal type used within individual movements
- The form is not a rigid template but a set of functional principles that composers realize in individual ways
