# Quick Definition
A home-key PAC occurring within the A section of a modulating minuet exposition, before the section's final cadence in the subordinate key, thereby highlighting main-theme function.

# Core Definition
An early authentic cadence is a PAC in the home key that appears within a modulating A section of a minuet, before the section closes with an authentic cadence in the subordinate key. This cadence closes either a single phrase of exclusively cadential function or a phrase with the formal characteristics of a consequent. It highlights main-theme function by giving the home key strong cadential confirmation before the modulation. The early authentic cadence is typical of minuet form but rarely found in the small ternary theme type, where two strong cadences in juxtaposition would create too great a dramatic conflict so early in a unit that functions as a main theme within a larger formal context (Caplin, pp. 636-637).

# Prerequisites
- **Minuet form** -- The formal context where this cadence appears
- **Cadential function** -- Understanding of cadential types and their formal weight

# Key Properties
1. A PAC in the home key within a modulating A section
2. Appears before the section's final cadence in the subordinate key
3. Highlights main-theme function for the minuet as a whole
4. Typical of minuet form, rare in small ternary
5. Followed by another thematic unit that modulates or begins in the subordinate key

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify a modulating A section in a minuet
2. Look for a PAC in the home key occurring before the final cadence
3. The subsequent music modulates or begins directly in the subordinate key
4. The material before the early PAC expresses main-theme function

# Context & Application
In the small ternary, the juxtaposition of two strong cadences (home key PAC followed by subordinate key PAC) would create too great a dramatic conflict early in what functions as a main theme. In the minuet, however, the early authentic cadence helps highlight a broader expression of thematic functionality, distinguishing main-theme function from subsequent transition and subordinate-theme functions within the A section.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 639): Beethoven, Violin Sonata in F ("Spring"), Op. 24, iii, mm. 1-8 -- first phrase closes with early PAC at m. 4, second phrase modulates to dominant.
**Example 2** (p. 640): Haydn, Symphony No. 98 in B-flat, iii, mm. 1-20 -- early PAC at m. 4 created by an expanded cadential progression, followed by modulation to subordinate key.
**Example 3** (p. 641): Mozart, Piano Sonata in A, K. 331, ii, mm. 1-18 -- early PAC at m. 10 closes a complete main theme, after which subordinate theme begins directly in the new key.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Expecting an early authentic cadence in a small ternary. **Correction**: This cadence is typical of minuet form but rare in the small ternary.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Treating the early PAC as the final cadence of the exposition. **Clarification**: It marks the end of main-theme function, not the end of the A section; the section continues with transition/subordinate-theme material.
