# Quick Definition
The three ways a rondo refrain can return after a couplet: complete (full original structure), abridged (only part of the structure, still cadentially closed), or incomplete (lacking cadential closure or cadencing in a non-home key).

# Core Definition
When the rondo refrain returns after a couplet, it may appear in one of three forms: (1) complete -- the theme's full structure is brought back, sometimes with ornamental changes; (2) abridged -- only a portion of the original theme (usually the A or A' section of a ternary) is brought back, but it still concludes with a PAC in the home key; (3) incomplete -- the refrain either lacks cadential closure or concludes with a cadence in some tonal region other than the home key. An incomplete refrain may be extended to become a transitional passage leading to the key of the following couplet. In sonata-rondos, Mozart frequently eliminates refrain 3 or refrain 4 entirely.

# Construction / Recognition
- Complete return: full original structure preserved (possibly with ornamental changes)
- Abridged return: only part of the original theme (e.g., A section of a ternary), still ending with PAC in home key
- Incomplete return: no PAC in home key; may end with cadence in another key or lack cadential closure entirely
- Incomplete refrains may be extended into transitional passages
- In sonata-rondos, one refrain may be eliminated entirely (a fourth type: absent)
- If Mozart retains refrain 3, he usually eliminates refrain 4 (and vice versa)

# Context & Application
The variety of refrain return types gives the composer flexibility in pacing the rondo and managing formal expectations. Complete returns provide maximum stability and satisfaction; abridged returns maintain forward momentum; incomplete returns create continuity with the following couplet. The choice of return type is a significant compositional decision that affects the character of the entire rondo.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Hob. XVI:49, iii: Refrain 2 is incomplete, consisting only of the modulating A section of the original small ternary; the final cadence is reinterpreted as a HC when the minore couplet 2 begins
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 3, Op. 55, ii: Refrain 2 incomplete, moving toward IV and cadencing there; couplet 2 elides with end of refrain

# Common Confusions
- An abridged refrain is not the same as an incomplete refrain. An abridged refrain still achieves PAC in the home key; an incomplete refrain does not.
- The elimination of a refrain entirely (as Mozart sometimes does with refrain 3 or 4) is a distinct deviation from the norm, not merely an incomplete return.
