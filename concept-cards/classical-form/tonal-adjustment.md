# Quick Definition
The process of modifying the harmonic-tonal content of exposition material in the recapitulation so that the subordinate theme remains in the home key instead of modulating to the subordinate key.

# Formal Definition
If the original transition is modulatory, the one in the recapitulation must be tonally adjusted to remain in the home key. The adjustment can be accomplished by any number of harmonic and phrase-structural means and can occur at any place in the transition. Frequently, the subdominant region is tonicized, promoting (as a pre-dominant) a logical succession to the dominant to end the transition. The subordinate theme is also tonally adjusted — transposed into the home key, sometimes requiring register changes and altered cadential progressions.

# Construction/Recognition
- **In the transition**: modulatory passages are replaced with home-key-oriented progressions; often the subdominant is tonicized
- **In the subordinate theme**: material transposed to the home key; register adjustments (melody may shift up or down an octave to stay in a practical range)
- **Point of adjustment (crux)**: the specific moment where the recapitulation's tonal path diverges from the exposition's
- If the transition was originally nonmodulating, tonal adjustment may not be necessary
- Modulating subordinate themes require additional adjustment to avoid ending in a wrong key

# Musical Context
Tonal adjustment is the essential technical procedure of the recapitulation. Every sonata recapitulation must resolve the tonal conflict of the exposition by keeping the subordinate theme in the home key, and the transition must be adjusted accordingly.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C, K. 545, i (ex. 11.10): subordinate theme transposed to C major; melody initially down a fifth, then up a fourth to maintain register
- Haydn, Piano Trio in E-flat, Hob. XV:30, i (ex. 11.7): two-part transition compressed into one, eliminating redundant modulating passages
- Beethoven, Overture to Coriolanus, Op. 62 (ex. 11.14): modulating subordinate theme requires complex harmonic reorganization

# Common Confusions
- Tonal adjustment is not simply "transposing everything down a fifth" — it involves creative recomposition of harmonic progressions
- The point of adjustment may occur at different places in different movements — it is not fixed
- Register changes resulting from tonal adjustment are practical necessities, not expressive choices (though they may have expressive consequences)
