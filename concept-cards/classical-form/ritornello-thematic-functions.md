# Quick Definition
The opening ritornello distributes ideas as a tight-knit main theme (closing with PAC, never HC, favoring compound sentence), a transition beginning with new material and ending with a home-key HC, and one or more subordinate themes followed by a closing section of codettas -- all remaining in the home key.

# Core Definition
The opening ritornello begins with a tight-knit main theme closing with a PAC. Like a rondo but unlike a sonata, a concerto main theme does not close with an HC. The compound sentence is particularly favored. The main theme is followed by a more loosely organized thematic region ending with a home-key HC -- a unit resembling a nonmodulating transition. Unlike sonata expositions, in which the transition can begin like the main theme, the transition of a concerto ritornello almost always begins with new material. The next thematic unit continues in the home key but, because of its placement following a transition, gives the impression of being a subordinate theme, effecting a modest degree of formal loosening. Additional subordinate themes may follow, and the ritornello ends with a closing section made up of codettas (Caplin, pp. 702-703).

# Key Properties
1. Main theme: tight-knit, closes with PAC (never HC)
2. Compound sentence favored for main theme
3. Transition: always begins with new material, ends with home-key HC
4. Subordinate theme(s): remain in home key, modest formal loosening
5. Closing section: made up of codettas
6. All functions remain in the home key
7. Transition begins with new material (unlike sonata)

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the main theme closing with PAC
2. Locate the transition (new material) ending with home-key HC
3. Find the subordinate theme(s) with modest loosening
4. Identify the closing section's codettas
5. Verify all sections remain in the home key

# Context & Application
These thematic functions create the expectation framework that the solo exposition will then modify. The initial associations between material and function are subject to reinterpretation throughout the movement.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 696): Mozart, Piano Concerto in E-flat, K. 482, i, mm. 50-76 -- transition ends on home-key dominant, two subordinate themes follow with tight-knit organization.
**Example 2** (p. 701): Mozart, Violin Concerto in A, K. 219, i, mm. 1-39 -- complete opening ritornello with identifiable main theme, transition, subordinate theme, and closing section.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Expecting the ritornello transition to begin with main-theme material (as in some sonata expositions). **Correction**: The concerto ritornello transition almost always begins with new material.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Whether the ritornello "subordinate theme" really functions as one. **Clarification**: It functions as one by virtue of its placement after a transition, but it remains in the home key and is relatively tight-knit.
