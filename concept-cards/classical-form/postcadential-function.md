# Quick Definition
A framing function that follows the cadential arrival at the structural end of a theme, prolonging the final harmony and dissipating accumulated energy through a recessive dynamic.

# Formal Definition
Postcadential function follows the point of cadential arrival at the theme's structural end. It appears in two main varieties: (1) after PAC, a closing section with codettas; (2) after HC, a standing on the dominant. Both prolong the final harmony and feature a recessive dynamic. Codettas of postcadential function are frequently reinterpreted retrospectively as having an initiating function.

# Construction/Recognition
- Follows the structural cadence (theme's end point)
- After PAC: closing section with codettas (prolongs tonic)
- After HC: standing on the dominant (prolongs dominant)
- Features recessive dynamic (energy dissipation)

# Musical Context
Postcadential functions appear in sonata expositions (closing sections), contrasting middles (standing on the dominant), and at the end of main themes.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 42-48: closing section after subordinate theme PAC
- Beethoven, Op. 15, ii, mm. 12-14: standing on the dominant after contrasting middle HC

# Common Confusions
- Postcadential is not cadential. Cadential function leads to the cadence; postcadential follows it.
