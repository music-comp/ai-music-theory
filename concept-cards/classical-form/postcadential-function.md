# Quick Definition
A framing function that follows the cadential arrival at the structural end of a theme, prolonging the final harmony and dissipating energy through a recessive dynamic, appearing as either a closing section (after PAC) or a standing on the dominant (after HC).

# Core Definition
Postcadential function follows the point of cadential arrival at the theme's structural end. It appears in two main varieties: (1) after a PAC, "a closing section containing codettas"; (2) after a HC, "a standing on the dominant" (Ch. 1, p. 16). "Both types of postcadential function prolong the final harmony of their preceding cadence. In addition, both tend to feature a recessive dynamic, in which the energy accumulated in the motion toward the cadential goal is dissipated" (Ch. 1, p. 16).

# Prerequisites
- **Cadential function** -- the function that precedes postcadential
- **Authentic cadence** / **Half cadence** -- the cadence types that postcadential follows

# Key Properties
1. Follows the structural cadence (the theme's end point)
2. After PAC: closing section with codettas (prolongs tonic)
3. After HC: standing on the dominant (prolongs dominant)
4. Prolongs the final harmony of the preceding cadence
5. Features recessive dynamic (energy dissipation)
6. Codettas can be reinterpreted retrospectively as initiating function

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the structural cadence (PAC or HC)
- Everything following that cadence while prolonging its final harmony is postcadential
- Check for recessive dynamic (decrease in tension, dynamics, density)

# Context & Application
Postcadential functions appear after themes in sonata expositions (closing sections), within contrasting middles (standing on the dominant), and at the end of main themes. The distinction between cadential and postcadential function is crucial: only material leading to the cadential arrival has cadential function.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 1, p. 17, ex. 1.6): Beethoven, Op. 135, iii, mm. 10-13: closing section after PAC; one-measure codetta (cadential melody transferred to cello), repeated, then fragmented to half-measure codetta over tonic pedal with neighboring IV.
**Example 2** (Ch. 1, p. 14, ex. 1.4): Beethoven, Op. 15, ii, mm. 12-14: standing on the dominant after HC in the contrasting middle; dominant prolonged by V/V neighboring chords, with fragmentation.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Cadential function (postcadential follows it)
## Enables
- Closing sections and codettas (specific postcadential forms)
- Standings on the dominant
## Related
- Introduction function (the complementary framing function: before-the-beginning)
## Contrasts With
- Cadential function: cadential leads TO the cadence; postcadential follows it
- Introduction function: progressive dynamic (buildup) vs. recessive dynamic (dissipation)

# Common Errors
- Calling postcadential material "cadential" because it may contain cadential progressions internally

# Common Confusions
- Postcadential is not cadential. "Only the material leading to the cadential arrival -- the point that marks the structural end of the theme -- can truly be said to fulfill cadential function. A closing section (and its constituent codettas), on the contrary, plays an entirely different role, namely, a postcadential one" (Ch. 1, p. 17)
