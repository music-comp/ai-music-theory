# Quick Definition
A loosening technique in which the internal components of a formal function are lengthened beyond their normative size, stretching the function from within.

# Formal Definition
Expansion involves the internal lengthening of component members of a formal function over their normative size in tight-knit themes. It resembles rhythmic augmentation but does not require strict proportional maintenance. A single harmony of a cadential progression can become highly expanded relative to others. Expansion is most commonly associated with cadential function, producing the expanded cadential progression (E.C.P.).

# Construction/Recognition
- Compare component lengths to normative sizes in tight-knit contexts
- Expanded cadential progressions fill entire four-measure (or longer) phrases
- Proportional relationships between harmonies may be unequal

# Musical Context
Expansion is a key loosening technique in subordinate themes, where cadential progressions are stretched to support entire phrases.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 33-41: cadential progression I6-II6-V(7)-I expanded to fill entire phrases, compared to two-measure cadential area in the main theme

# Common Confusions
- Expansion stretches from within (internal lengthening); extension adds on from without (appending material). They are different processes.
