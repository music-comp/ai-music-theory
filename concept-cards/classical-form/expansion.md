# Quick Definition
A loosening technique in which the internal components of a formal function are lengthened beyond their normative size, stretching the function from within rather than appending material from without.

# Core Definition
"Expansion involves the internal lengthening of component members of the function over their normative size in tight-knit themes" (Ch. 1, p. 21). "This loosening technique is most commonly associated with cadential function, in which the individual harmonies of the cadential progression are lengthened compared with their relatively compressed appearance (usually in two measures) at the end of a tight-knit phrase" (Ch. 1, p. 21). Expansion "resembles the rhythmic technique of 'augmentation'" but "does not require that the durational proportions of the normative form be strictly maintained. A single harmony of the cadential progression, for example, can become highly expanded in relation to the other harmonies" (Ch. 1, p. 21).

# Prerequisites
- **Cadential progression** -- the most commonly expanded structure
- **Tight-knit vs. loose organization** -- expansion is a loosening technique

# Key Properties
1. Internal components are lengthened beyond their normative size
2. Most commonly associated with cadential function
3. Produces the expanded cadential progression (E.C.P.)
4. Does not require strict proportional maintenance (unlike augmentation)
5. A single harmony may be expanded disproportionately
6. Distinct from extension (which adds material from without)

# Construction / Recognition
- Compare component lengths to normative sizes in tight-knit contexts
- If individual harmonies of a cadential progression fill entire phrases, expansion has occurred
- Proportional relationships between harmonies may be unequal

# Context & Application
Expansion is a key loosening technique in subordinate themes, where cadential progressions are stretched to support entire phrases (the expanded cadential progression). It creates the distinctly looser organization characteristic of subordinate themes.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 1, p. 21): Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 33-41: cadential progression I6-II6-V(7)-I expanded to fill entire phrases, compared with the two-measure cadential area in the main theme (mm. 7-8).

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Cadential progression (the structure most commonly expanded)
## Enables
- Expanded cadential progression (the specific result of cadential expansion)
- Loose organization in subordinate themes
## Related
- Extension (the complementary loosening technique)
## Contrasts With
- Extension: expansion stretches from WITHIN (internal lengthening); extension adds on from WITHOUT (appending material)

# Common Errors
- Confusing expansion with extension
- Treating expansion as identical to augmentation (expansion does not require strict proportional maintenance)

# Common Confusions
- Expansion stretches from WITHIN; extension adds on from WITHOUT. They are different processes that can co-occur
- Expansion resembles augmentation but is more flexible in proportions
