# Quick Definition
The compound (sixteen-measure) sentence consists of an eight-measure compound presentation (containing a compound basic idea and its repetition over tonic prolongation) followed by an eight-measure continuation with fragmentation, harmonic acceleration, and cadential closure (usually PAC).

# Core Definition
The compound sentence is a sixteen-measure theme type built out of an eight-measure compound presentation followed by an eight-measure continuation that ends with a cadence (usually a PAC, sometimes an HC, rarely an IAC). The compound presentation consists of a four-measure compound basic idea and its repetition (either exact, statement-response, or sequential), supported by a tonic prolongational progression. The continuation features the standard characteristics: fragmentation, harmonic acceleration, increased surface rhythms, and harmonic sequence. The first stage of fragmentation sees grouping size reduced to two measures (relative to the preceding four-measure compound basic ideas). Because it projects a more expansive structure than the simple sentence, the compound sentence is often used to begin large, public genres such as the symphony and concerto.

# Prerequisites
- **Sentence**: The fundamental theme type whose presentation-continuation principle extends to the compound level
- **Compound basic idea**: The four-measure initiating unit that is repeated in the compound presentation

# Key Properties
1. Sixteen real measures: 8 (compound presentation) + 8 (continuation)
2. Compound presentation: c.b.i. (mm. 1-4) + repetition of c.b.i. (mm. 5-8), supported by tonic prolongation
3. Repetition types: statement-response most common, exact less frequent, sequential rare
4. Continuation: fragmentation (4-measure units to 2-measure, then possibly 1-measure), harmonic acceleration, sequential harmonies
5. Cadential progression usually expanded to fill final four measures
6. The compound presentation does not end with a cadence
7. There is no "compound continuation" -- the continuation is structurally the same as in a simple sentence, only longer
8. Often ends with PAC; HC possible (followed by standing on the dominant)

# Construction / Recognition
1. **Presentation (mm. 1-8)**: compound basic idea (mm. 1-4) + repetition (mm. 5-8), both over tonic prolongation
2. Check that the statement-response repetition does not create a genuine antecedent-consequent (period) -- usually the harmonic motion is exclusively prolongational
3. **Continuation (mm. 9-16)**: fragmentation (4-measure to 2-measure, then 1-measure), harmonic acceleration, sequential progressions
4. Cadential progression usually expanded to fill final four measures
5. **Compressed continuation variant**: continuation functions expressed in only four measures (mm. 9-12), with repetition or standing on the dominant restoring sixteen-measure length

# Context & Application
The compound sentence's forward-driving character is even more pronounced than in the simple sentence due to the greater span and more dramatic fragmentation process. It is especially associated with large public genres (symphonies, concertos) because its expansive presentation establishes material with appropriate grandeur. It is less frequently found opening smaller, private genres (solo or duo sonatas). The compressed continuation is a characteristic deviation -- since continuation and cadence can often be fully expressed in fewer than eight measures, the composer may compress the continuation and restore symmetry through repetition or extension.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Concerto No. 3 in C Minor, Op. 37, i, mm. 1-16: model form; statement-response repetition of c.b.i.; continuation develops second motive with fragmentation, harmonic acceleration, and descending-third sequential progression
- Beethoven, Violin Concerto in D, Op. 61, i, mm. 2-18: 1-m. thematic introduction; statement-response (I-V, V-I) repetition; continuation with fragmentation and extended cadential function
- Mozart, Clarinet Trio in E-flat, K. 498, i, mm. 1-16: compressed continuation with DC at m. 12, motivating repetition to achieve PAC
- Haydn, Symphony No. 83 ("The Hen"), i, mm. 1-16: compressed continuation with HC at m. 12, followed by four-measure standing on the dominant

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Sentence (the fundamental presentation-continuation principle)
- Compound basic idea (the four-measure initiating unit)
- Tonic prolongation (supports the compound presentation)

## Enables
- Compressed continuation (characteristic deviation of the compound sentence)
- Standing on the dominant (postcadential function after HC endings)

## Related
- Compound period (the other compound theme type)
- Fragmentation (the key continuational process, now operating at a larger scale)

## Contrasts With
- Compound period: in the compound sentence, m. 9 brings continuation (fragmentation, new material); in the compound period, m. 9 brings the return of the basic idea (consequent function)

# Common Errors
- Confusing a compound presentation with a period when the c.b.i. is repeated as statement-response (I-V, V-I) -- check that the harmonic motion is exclusively prolongational, not cadential
- Calling the continuation "compound continuation" -- Caplin explicitly notes there is no such thing, as the continuation is not more complex, only potentially longer
- Assuming the compound sentence must be exactly sixteen measures -- compressed continuations, extensions, and other deviations alter the length

# Common Confusions
- The compound sentence's presentation contains a *compound basic idea* and its repetition, not a simple basic idea
- Compressed continuation does not mean the sentence is shorter than sixteen measures -- repetition, extension, or standing on the dominant typically restores the normative length
- Do not confuse the compound sentence's 8+8 structure with a compound period's 8+8 -- in the sentence, the second half is a continuation (no return of basic idea at m. 9); in the period, it is a consequent (basic idea returns at m. 9)
- Statement-response repetition (I-V then V-I) in the presentation may suggest antecedent-consequent, but the exclusively prolongational harmonic context prevents genuine periodic structure
