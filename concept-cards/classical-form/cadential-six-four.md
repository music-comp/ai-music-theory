# Quick Definition
A chord built over scale-degree 5 that embellishes the dominant in a cadential progression, resolving its upper voices (scale-degrees 1 and 3) downward to form the dominant chord proper (scale-degree 7 and scale-degree 2).

# Core Definition
"The principal embellishment of dominant harmony" in a cadential progression "occurs through the use of a 'cadential six-four' chord constructed over the fifth scale-degree" (Ch. 2, p. 27). "The frequent use of the cadential six-four helps clarify, perhaps, why II6 is preferred to IV as the main pre-dominant harmony in cadential progressions": II6 "creates a more active and directed melodic line, in which the motion from the second scale-degree to the leading-tone can be filled in by a passing tone, supported by the cadential six-four" (Ch. 2, p. 27). When IV is used instead, "the melody tends to be static, and adding a six-four embellishment would further emphasize the tonic scale-degree" redundantly.

# Prerequisites
- **Cadential progression** -- the context in which the six-four appears
- **Dominant function** -- which the six-four embellishes
- **Pre-dominant function** -- which precedes it

# Key Properties
1. Built over scale-degree 5 in the bass voice
2. Upper voices contain scale-degrees 1 and 3 (resolving to 7 and 2)
3. Functions as an embellishment of V, NOT as a tonic chord in second inversion
4. Creates a suspension-like resolution: 6-5 and 4-3 above the bass
5. Typically placed between pre-dominant and root-position V
6. II6 creates better voice-leading than IV when preceding the six-four
7. One of the most frequently used embellishments in classical cadential progressions

# Construction / Recognition
- Scale-degree 5 in bass with scale-degrees 1 and 3 above
- Resolves to V (scale-degrees 7 and 2 above scale-degree 5)
- Follows the pre-dominant, precedes the root-position dominant proper

# Context & Application
The cadential six-four is almost a standard component of the classical cadential formula. It bridges the pre-dominant and the dominant, creating smooth voice-leading and harmonic weight on the dominant arrival.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 2, ex. 2.6): II6-I6/4-V-I: the standard cadential progression with six-four embellishment, creating the melodic line scale-degrees 2-1-7 over bass scale-degrees 4-5-5.

# Common Errors
- Analyzing the cadential six-four as a tonic chord in second inversion (I6/4)

# Common Confusions
- The cadential six-four is NOT a tonic chord in second inversion. It functions as a dominant embellishment
- Many textbooks confusingly analyze this as a tonic chord, but Caplin (following the German functional tradition) treats it as a dominant embellishment with suspension-like resolution
