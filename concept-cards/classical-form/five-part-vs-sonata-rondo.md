# Quick Definition
The five-part rondo (ABACA) has three refrains and two couplets without a recapitulated subordinate-theme complex; the sonata-rondo (ABACABA) adds a recapitulation and coda, combining rondo alternation with sonata tripartite organization into the most complex classical form.

# Core Definition
The five-part rondo (ABACA) consists of three statements of the refrain alternating with two couplets. The first couplet may be either a subordinate-theme complex or an interior theme; the second couplet is usually an interior theme. A coda is optional. The sonata-rondo (ABACABA) combines four refrains and three couplets, merging rondo alternation with sonata tripartite organization. The initial refrain and couplet constitute a sonata exposition (though never repeated); the third refrain and couplet constitute a recapitulation. The second couplet may be a development or interior theme. When organized as an interior theme, the form is considerably more rondo-like than sonata-like. Unlike a regular sonata, the coda is required because it includes the final refrain return. The sonata-rondo is "perhaps the most complex of the classical forms" (Caplin, pp. 664-667).

# Key Properties
1. Five-part rondo: 3 refrains, 2 couplets (ABACA); optional coda
2. Sonata-rondo: 4 refrains, 3 couplets (ABACABA); required coda
3. Key difference: sonata-rondo has recapitulation of subordinate-theme complex
4. Five-part rondo: no recapitulation of subordinate material
5. Sonata-rondo: exposition never repeated (unlike sonata form)
6. Sonata-rondo: most complex classical form
7. Five-part rondo: most often for slow movements; sonata-rondo: fast finales

# Construction / Recognition
1. Count refrains and couplets
2. If three refrains (ABACA): five-part rondo
3. If four refrains with recapitulated subordinate material (ABACABA): sonata-rondo
4. If four refrains without recapitulated subordinate material (ABACADA): seven-part rondo
5. Check whether the second couplet is a development or interior theme
6. Check for the required coda in sonata-rondo

# Context & Application
The distinction between these forms is crucial for formal analysis. The key diagnostic criterion is whether couplet 3 recapitulates the subordinate-theme material of couplet 1. If yes, the form is a sonata-rondo; if no, it is either a five-part rondo (with only two couplets) or a seven-part rondo (with three couplets but no recapitulation).

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing a seven-part rondo (ABACADA) with a sonata-rondo (ABACABA). **Correction**: The seven-part rondo lacks a recapitulated subordinate-theme complex; the sonata-rondo has one.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Whether any movement with seven parts is a sonata-rondo. **Clarification**: Only if the third couplet recapitulates the first couplet's subordinate-theme complex.
