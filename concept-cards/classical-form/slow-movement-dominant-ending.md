# Quick Definition
A practice, rooted in high-baroque convention, in which a slow movement ends on dominant harmony rather than tonic, with resolution occurring only at the beginning of the following movement.

# Core Definition
A number of slow movements, particularly by Haydn, end with dominant harmony rather than achieving final tonic closure. The resolution to tonic occurs only at the beginning of the following movement, forging a stronger bond between movements and undermining the slow movement's independence. Three scenarios produce this outcome: (1) a regular PAC is followed by an appended passage that leads to a final dominant; (2) the music finds itself "stuck" rather unexpectedly on the dominant, which becomes the de facto ending harmony; (3) an expected closing authentic cadence fails to materialize, and a half cadence or dominant arrival appears instead, usually after a deceptive cadence.

# Prerequisites
- **Slow-movement sonata form**: The formal context
- **Half cadence**: The cadence type that may substitute for the expected PAC
- **Dominant arrival**: The harmonic event that becomes the ending

# Key Properties
1. The movement's final harmony is V rather than I
2. Tonic resolution occurs at the start of the next movement
3. Creates inter-movement continuity and a stronger bond between movements
4. A remnant of high-baroque practice
5. Three distinct scenarios for producing the dominant ending
6. Particularly associated with Haydn

# Construction / Recognition
- Check the final harmony of the movement -- if V rather than I, this is a dominant ending
- Identify which scenario applies: appended passage, unexpected "sticking," or failed PAC
- Note the connection to the following movement's opening

# Context & Application
This practice reflects the gradual weakening of movement independence in the classical period, anticipating Beethoven's more radical inter-movement connections. The dominant ending is not an error or incomplete movement but a deliberate formal device.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E, Hob. XVI:31, ii: The recapitulation ends with a standing on the dominant rather than a PAC, with resolution only at the start of the next movement

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Half cadence and dominant arrival (the harmonic events used)

## Enables
- Inter-movement continuity
- Weakened movement boundaries

## Related
- Deceptive cadence (often precedes the failed PAC in scenario 3)
- Large ternary form (may also end this way)

## Contrasts With
- Standard movement closure with PAC in home key

# Common Errors
- Treating a dominant ending as an incomplete or defective movement
- Failing to recognize the deliberate connection to the following movement

# Common Confusions
- **Intentional vs. incomplete**: A dominant ending is a deliberate formal device, not an incomplete movement
