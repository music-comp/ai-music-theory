# Quick Definition
A practice, rooted in high-baroque convention, in which a slow movement ends on dominant harmony, with tonic resolution occurring only at the beginning of the following movement.

# Formal Definition
A number of slow movements, particularly by Haydn, end with dominant harmony rather than achieving final tonic closure. The resolution to tonic occurs only at the beginning of the following movement, forging a stronger bond between movements. Three scenarios arise: (1) a regular PAC is followed by an appended passage leading to a final dominant; (2) the music finds itself "stuck" on the dominant; (3) an expected PAC fails to materialize and a HC appears instead, usually after a deceptive cadence.

# Construction/Recognition
- The movement's final harmony is V rather than I
- Tonic resolution occurs at the start of the next movement
- Creates inter-movement continuity

# Musical Context
A remnant of high-baroque practice, found most often in Haydn's slow movements.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E, Hob. XVI:31, ii: The recapitulation ends with a standing on the dominant

# Common Confusions
- A dominant ending is a deliberate formal device, not an incomplete movement.
