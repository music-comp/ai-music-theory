# Quick Definition
A subordinate theme that begins with a standing on the dominant (carried over from the transition's ending), yielding directly to continuation or cadential functions without any standard initiating function, blurring the boundary between transition and subordinate theme.

# Core Definition
The boundary between transition and subordinate theme can become somewhat blurred when the dominant from the end of the transition is held over to provide harmonic support for a standing on the dominant at the beginning of the subordinate theme. Such a standing on the dominant would NOT be followed by one of the standard initiating functions (presentation, compound basic idea), for we would then have the impression that the standing on the dominant belongs to the end of the transition and that the initiating function is the true structural beginning of the subordinate theme. Instead, the standing on the dominant at the structural beginning of the theme yields directly to continuation or cadential functions. In most cases, a marked change of melodic-motivic material helps indicate where the transition ends and the subordinate theme begins.

# Key Properties
1. Dominant harmony from the transition's end is held over into the subordinate theme
2. The standing on the dominant yields directly to continuation or cadential function
3. No standard initiating function (presentation, CBI) follows the dominant
4. A marked change of melodic-motivic material signals the new thematic unit
5. The transition may have its own standing on the dominant before this one
6. The distinction from a "weak tonic prolongation" depends on what the melodic material implies
7. If the material implies tonic prolongation: it is a loosened presentation (not a standing on dominant)
8. If the material implies dominant prolongation: it is a standing on the dominant

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the transition's final dominant (HC or dominant arrival)
- Note whether dominant harmony continues into the next passage
- Check if new melodic-motivic material appears (signaling a new thematic unit)
- Determine whether the passage following the dominant emphasis is continuation or cadential (not presentation)
- Distinguish from a presentation with weakened tonic prolongation (dominant pedal undermining tonic)
- Compare the transition's standing on the dominant with the subordinate theme's opening

# Context & Application
This technique creates a particular blurring of the transition-subordinate theme boundary, since the harmonic content (dominant prolongation) is shared between the two functions. However, the change in melodic material typically provides sufficient contrast to mark the boundary. The subsequent PAC confirms that a subordinate theme was indeed under way. The resulting theme is typically compact and tight-knit despite the unusual opening, and is often followed by an expanded repetition to provide the necessary loosening.

# Examples
- Beethoven, String Quartet in D, Op. 18/3, i, mm. 43-59 (Ch. 12, Ex. 12.13): transition ends with HC at m. 47 and standing on dominant (mm. 48-51); completely new melodic material at m. 51 signals a new standing on the dominant belonging to the subordinate theme; a 4-m. continuation leads to PAC at m. 57; the theme then receives an expanded repetition beginning at m. 58

# Common Errors
- Assuming all dominant emphasis after the transition belongs to the transition
- Confusing a dominant pedal weakening tonic prolongation (= loosened presentation) with a genuine standing on the dominant (= no tonic prolongation)
- Missing the change of melodic-motivic material that signals the new thematic unit

# Common Confusions
- **Standing on dominant vs. weak tonic prolongation**: If the melodic-motivic material implies a tonic prolongation, it is a loosened presentation with dominant pedal; if the material implies a dominant prolongation, it is a standing on the dominant
- **Ownership of the dominant**: Compare the melodic content before and after the transition's structural end; new material signals the subordinate theme
- **Not a loose boundary**: In most cases, the change of material provides clear articulation despite the shared harmonic content
