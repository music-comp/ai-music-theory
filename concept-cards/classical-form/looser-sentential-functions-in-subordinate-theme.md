# Quick Definition
Most subordinate themes are constructed from sentential functions (presentation, continuation, cadential), but one or more of these functions acquires a loose organization: presentation may be extended or weakened, continuation is typically extended by model-sequence technique, and cadential function usually receives its own distinct phrase with expanded cadential progressions and evaded cadences.

# Core Definition
Most subordinate themes are constructed out of the three sentential functions: presentation, continuation, and cadential. One or more of these functions usually acquires a loose organization by means of various compositional techniques. Presentation function can be loosened by an additional repeat of the basic idea, by repeating the entire presentation itself, or by weakening the tonic prolongation supporting the presentation (e.g., dominant pedal). Continuation function is typically extended well beyond the standard 4-measure length, often through model-sequence technique. Cadential function is typically both expanded (through ECPs) and extended (through evaded cadences and the "one more time" technique). Crucially, whereas the tight-knit sentence normally fuses continuation and cadential functions into a single "continuation phrase," the subordinate theme typically gives these two functions their own distinct phrases, possessing different melodic-motivic content and harmonic progressions.

# Prerequisites
- **Subordinate theme**: The formal context
- **Presentation, continuation, cadential functions**: The functions being loosened
- **Sentence**: The tight-knit model against which loosening is measured

# Key Properties
1. Presentation loosening: additional basic-idea repetition, repeated presentation, weakened tonic prolongation
2. Continuation loosening: extended well beyond 4 mm. via model-sequence technique
3. Cadential loosening (expansion): expanded cadential progressions replace standard progressions
4. Cadential loosening (extension): evaded cadences, deceptive cadences, "one more time" technique
5. Continuation and cadential occupy SEPARATE phrases (unlike fused continuation phrase in tight-knit sentence)
6. The separate phrases have DIFFERENT melodic-motivic content
7. Periodic functions (antecedent, consequent) appear less frequently than sentential functions
8. Small ternary structures rarely occur in subordinate themes

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the sentential functions (presentation, continuation, cadential)
- Note whether continuation and cadential are fused (tight-knit) or separate (loose)
- Check for loosening techniques in each function
- Compare the length and complexity of each function with the main theme's model
- The subordinate theme's cadential phrase typically has different material from the continuation

# Context & Application
The separation of continuation and cadential into distinct phrases is one of the most reliable markers of subordinate-theme loosening. In a tight-knit sentence, the continuation phrase encompasses both continuation and cadential functions in a single, efficient unit. In the subordinate theme, these functions are unpacked into separate phrases, each with its own melodic content and harmonic progression. This separation alone significantly increases the theme's length and complexity relative to the main theme.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in F minor, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 21-41 (Ch. 9, Ex. 9.3): presentation (mm. 21-24, loosened by dominant pedal); extended continuation (mm. 25-32); expanded cadential phrase (mm. 33-41, with ECP and evasion at m. 37); continuation and cadential have different melodic material and harmonic progressions
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C, K. 309, i (Ch. 12, Ex. 12.3): additional repetition of basic idea in presentation; expanded periodic design in consequent

# Common Errors
- Analyzing the subordinate theme as a simple sentence when continuation and cadential are separate
- Failing to distinguish between cadential extension (evaded cadences) and cadential expansion (ECP)
- Treating the continuation as ending at the first cadential gesture (it may continue much longer)

# Common Confusions
- **Fused vs. separate**: In tight-knit sentences, continuation + cadential = one phrase; in subordinate themes, they typically = two separate phrases
- **Extension vs. expansion**: Extension delays the cadence through evasion; expansion replaces the standard progression with a longer one
- **Periodic functions in ST**: Antecedent-consequent does appear but less often; when it does, the consequent is usually expanded
