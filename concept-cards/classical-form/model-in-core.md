# Quick Definition
A relatively long thematic unit (normally 4-8 measures) established at the beginning of a development core, designed to be sequentially repeated and subsequently fragmented.

# Formal Definition
The model of a core is constructed as a relatively long unit, normally four to eight measures. It must be sufficiently large to project a sense of structural beginning and to permit extensive fragmentation. The model itself may contain repeated material, but these internal repetitions cannot always be easily classified according to standard types. The melodic-motivic content may be drawn from any previous material of the movement or may be new.

# Construction/Recognition
- Typically 4-8 measures in length
- May contain internal repetitions or subdivisions
- Often has a sentential quality (presentation + continuation elements)
- Content drawn from exposition material or newly composed
- If quite long, may contain multiple phrases with differing formal functions
- Internal sequential repetitions must not obscure the larger-scale sequence of the full model

# Musical Context
The model establishes the character of the core. Longer models (8+ measures) may have their own internal formal organization, sometimes resembling a sentence.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in A Minor, K. 310, i (ex. 10.3): four-measure model of Sturm und Drang character over dominant harmony of E minor
- Mozart, String Quintet in E-flat, K. 614, i (ex. 10.6): ten-measure model with sentential organization
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in F Minor, Op. 2/1, i (ex. 10.8): eight-measure model based on subordinate theme material

# Common Confusions
- The model is not simply a "motive" -- it is a substantial multi-measure unit
- Internal repetitions within the model should not be confused with the larger sequential repetition of the full model
