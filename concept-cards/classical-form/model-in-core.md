# Quick Definition
A relatively long thematic unit (normally 4-8 measures) established at the beginning of a development core, designed to be sequentially repeated and subsequently fragmented.

# Core Definition
The model of a core is constructed as a relatively long unit, normally four to eight measures. It must be sufficiently large to project a sense of structural beginning and to permit extensive fragmentation. The model itself may contain repeated material, but these internal repetitions cannot always be easily classified. The melodic-motivic content may be drawn from any previous material in the movement or may be entirely new. Longer models (8+ measures) may have their own internal formal organization, sometimes resembling a sentence with presentation and continuation elements. The model may contain internal sequential repetitions at a smaller scale that should not be confused with the larger-scale sequence of the full model.

# Prerequisites
- **Core technique**: The organizational method the model serves
- **Model-sequence technique**: The larger concept of sequential repetition

# Key Properties
1. Typically 4-8 measures in length
2. Must be large enough to project structural beginning and permit fragmentation
3. May contain internal repetitions or subdivisions
4. Often has sentential quality (presentation + continuation elements)
5. Content drawn from exposition material or newly composed
6. Internal sequential repetitions at smaller scale must not be confused with full-model sequence
7. The sequence may alter the model (eliminating later phrases)

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the first statement of the model (before sequential repetition begins)
- Verify that it is 4-8 measures (large enough for core status)
- Check for internal organization (sentential, with presentation + continuation)
- Note the source of melodic-motivic content (exposition material or new)
- Distinguish internal repetitions from the larger sequential pattern

# Context & Application
The model establishes the character and material of the core. Its size distinguishes core technique from smaller-scale model-sequence technique in continuation phrases. Longer models may have their own internal formal organization.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in A Minor, K. 310, i (ex. 10.3): four-measure model of Sturm und Drang character over dominant harmony of E minor
- Mozart, String Quintet in E-flat, K. 614, i (ex. 10.6): ten-measure model with sentential organization (presentation + continuation)
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in F Minor, Op. 2/1, i (ex. 10.8): eight-measure model based on subordinate theme material

# Common Errors
- Treating a motive or short phrase as a core model (must be 4-8 measures)
- Confusing internal repetitions within the model with the larger sequential pattern
- Failing to note that the model may be altered in its sequential repetitions

# Common Confusions
- **Model vs. motive**: The model is a substantial multi-measure unit, not a short motive
- **Internal vs. external sequence**: Internal repetitions within the model are distinct from the sequential repetition of the full model
- **Model alteration**: The sequence may alter the model, especially eliminating later phrases
