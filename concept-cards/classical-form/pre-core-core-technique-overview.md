# Quick Definition
The most common organizational technique for development sections: a pre-core of lesser emotional intensity precedes a core that establishes a relatively large model (4-8 mm.), sequences it, fragments it, and leads to an HC or dominant arrival followed by a standing on the dominant -- the core's grouping resembles a sentence but lacks genuine tonic prolongation.

# Core Definition
Development sections are considerably less conventional in their phrase-structural organization than expositions. One mode of organization, however, appears frequently: the pre-core/core technique. The core of a development establishes a relatively large-sized model (4-8 mm.), which is sequenced one or more times. Subsequent fragmentation leads to an HC (or dominant arrival) of either the home key or a development key, after which there appears a standing on the dominant. The core typically projects an emotional quality of instability, restlessness, and dramatic conflict. A core is usually preceded by a pre-core, a passage of lesser emotional intensity than the core. The pre-core often contains the main theme's basic idea (still in the subordinate key) and may present only initiatory (presentation-like) function, with the core providing the "continuation" to this "presentation."

# Key Properties
1. Pre-core: lesser emotional intensity, often uses main-theme material in subordinate key
2. Pre-core may present only initiatory function (structurally incomplete)
3. Core: model (4-8 mm.) + sequence + fragmentation + HC/dominant arrival + standing on dominant
4. Core projects instability, restlessness, dramatic conflict
5. Core's grouping resembles an extended compound sentence but is NOT a genuine sentence
6. The model and sequence do not prolong tonic harmony, so no genuine presentation exists
7. Standing on the dominant ends the development (prepares for recapitulation)
8. If the HC is in the home key, the recapitulation normally follows
9. If the HC is in a development key, more developmental material may follow

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the model: a relatively large-sized unit (4-8 mm.) that initiates sequential repetition
- The model marks the beginning of the core; material before it is the pre-core
- Track the sequence(s): one or more repetitions of the model, possibly truncated
- Note fragmentation following the sequence
- Locate the HC or dominant arrival (in the home key or a development key)
- Identify the standing on the dominant as the development's final event

# Context & Application
The pre-core/core technique is presented as the most common organizational scheme for development sections, though developments are considerably less predictable than expositions. The Beethoven Op. 2/1 first movement is used as the primary illustration in Ch. 9. The core's sentential-like grouping (model-sequence-fragmentation) superficially resembles a sentence, but the lack of tonic prolongation in the model-sequence unit prohibits genuine presentation function -- a key theoretical distinction (see "Sentential Grouping Structures" text box).

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in F minor, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 49-100 (Ch. 9, Exs. 9.5-9.7): pre-core (mm. 49-54, basic idea in subordinate key, presentation-like); core (mm. 55-81, 8-m. model based on subordinate-theme material, incomplete sequence, new 2-m. models, fragmentation, HC preparation); standing on dominant in two parts (mm. 82-100, first part with new motive, second part with return of sixteenth-note triplet figure)

# Common Errors
- Labeling the core as a "sentence" (it lacks genuine tonic prolongation in the model-sequence unit)
- Treating the standing on the dominant as a sentence (it lacks harmonic activity and cadence)
- Missing the pre-core because it is less dramatically characterized than the core
- Assuming all developments follow pre-core/core technique (they do not)

# Common Confusions
- **Core ≠ sentence**: The model-sequence grouping resembles presentation, but without tonic prolongation it is not a genuine presentation, so the core is not a genuine sentence
- **Pre-core = initiatory**: The pre-core often functions as a "presentation" to the core's "continuation" at a higher structural level
- **Two landmarks**: The model identification (core beginning) and the HC + standing on dominant (core end) are the two principal landmarks of the development
