# Quick Definition
Concerto form is an independent formal type derived from Baroque ritornello form and strongly infused with sonata-form elements, not a variant of sonata form; the three solo sections function as exposition, development, and recapitulation while three ritornellos provide the textural frame.

# Core Definition
A defining feature of the classical concerto is its employment of a formal scheme derived from the Baroque ritornello form and strongly infused with elements of classical sonata form. This concerto form is used in the first movement of all classical concertos, sometimes in slow movements, but rarely in finales (which typically use sonata-rondo). The form combines ritornello and sonata elements: like the Baroque period, ritornellos alternate with solo sections; like the classical sonata, the three solo sections function largely as exposition, development, and recapitulation. The key distinction is that concerto form is an independent form, not a variant of sonata form. Its opening section is a ritornello, not an "orchestral exposition," and it derives from Baroque antecedents that predate the classical sonata (Caplin, pp. 694-695).

# Key Properties
1. Independent form, not a variant of sonata form
2. Derived from Baroque ritornello form
3. Infused with sonata-form elements
4. Three solo sections function as exposition, development, recapitulation
5. Three ritornellos provide the textural frame
6. Used in first movements of all classical concertos
7. Sometimes in slow movements, rarely in finales

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the alternation of tutti and solo sections (ritornello principle)
2. Identify exposition-development-recapitulation functions (sonata principle)
3. Note that the opening section is a ritornello, not a sonata exposition
4. Observe the lack of a coda section

# Context & Application
Understanding concerto form as an independent tradition, rather than as modified sonata form, is essential for proper analysis. Both traditions contribute to the concerto, but neither fully explains it.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating concerto form as a variant of sonata form. **Correction**: Concerto form is an independent form with its own historical origins.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Whether the opening ritornello is an "orchestral exposition." **Clarification**: Caplin prefers "ritornello" to preserve the form's independence from sonata terminology.
