# Quick Definition
A rondo form (ABACA) in which an opening refrain returns twice, alternating with two contrasting couplets set in different tonal regions. A coda may be appended.

# Core Definition
The five-part rondo (traditionally ABACA) is one of two main categories of classical rondo form. An opening refrain returns twice, alternating with two contrasting couplets of differing musical content and organization. The refrain appears at all times in the home key; the two couplets are set in different tonal regions. Couplet 1 may be constructed as either a subordinate-theme complex (transition, subordinate theme, closing section, retransition) or an interior theme. Couplet 2 is most often an interior theme in a different tonal region from couplet 1, but may instead assume a development-like organization. A coda is sometimes appended after refrain 3.

# Construction / Recognition
- Five sections: Refrain 1 (A) - Couplet 1 (B) - Refrain 2 (A) - Couplet 2 (C) - Refrain 3 (A)
- Refrain 1: main theme, always in home key (I), closes with PAC
- Couplet 1: subordinate-theme complex (V) or interior theme (minore, vi)
- Refrain 2: first return of main theme (I), complete, abridged, or incomplete
- Couplet 2: interior theme (minore, IV, vi) or development-like unit
- Refrain 3: final return of main theme (I)
- Optional coda following refrain 3
- The rondo form is not confirmed until the appearance of couplet 2

# Context & Application
The five-part rondo is one of the simpler full-movement forms and is used for slow movements and finales. It is less complex than the sonata-rondo. As a slow-movement form, it is one of the options discussed alongside sonata, large ternary, sonata without development, and theme and variations.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C, K. 545, iii: Couplet 1 as compressed subordinate-theme complex
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in G, Hob. XVI:39, i: Couplet 1 as minore interior theme (truncated small ternary)
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 3 ("Eroica"), Op. 55, ii: Couplet 2 as development-like fugal passage

# Common Errors
See Common Confusions.

# Common Confusions
- On first hearing, the five-part rondo cannot be distinguished from other forms until couplet 2 appears. After couplet 1, the return of the refrain could suggest a repeated sonata exposition, a sonata without development, or a large ternary.
- The five-part rondo is distinct from the sonata-rondo (ABACABA). The five-part rondo lacks the recapitulation of the subordinate-theme complex that defines the sonata-rondo.
