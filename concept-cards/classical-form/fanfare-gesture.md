# Quick Definition
A heraldic, declamatory opening style found in some main themes, built over a long tonic pedal, that projects a strong opening but obscures conventional phrase-functional classification.

# Formal Definition
Some main themes begin with a long tonic pedal, over which may be found gestures that evoke a heraldic, fanfare style. Such powerful passages are ideal for projecting a strong opening, but they often obscure a clear sense of basic idea and its repetition, or its juxtaposition with a contrasting idea. The lack of a conventional initiating function makes it difficult to classify such a theme as one of the standard types. The theme often has a general sentential quality (cadential function is obvious, rhythmic activity accelerates) but presentation and continuation functions cannot be clearly distinguished.

# Construction/Recognition
- Long tonic pedal supporting the entire passage (or most of it)
- Unison or octave textures, often tutti
- Heraldic, declamatory rhythmic profiles (dotted rhythms, repeated notes, triadic figures)
- Strong sense of formal opening despite lack of clearly demarcated basic idea
- Often leads to a cadential passage after rhythmic acceleration
- Typically in the first 8-16 measures

# Musical Context
Fanfare gestures create an unmistakable sense of formal beginning and establish the movement's character. They are especially common in orchestral works (symphonies, overtures) and appear in some piano sonatas. The fanfare style is also characteristic of slow introductions.

# Examples
- Haydn, Symphony No. 97 in C, i (ex. 13.4): full orchestra in unisons and octaves sounding fanfare-like gestures over 8 measures; the grouping structure is too ambiguous to demarcate a conventional basic idea and contrasting idea; cadential function appears at m. 21

# Common Confusions
- A fanfare opening is not an introduction — it is the main theme itself, with cadential and phrase-structural substance
- The lack of a clear basic idea does not mean the theme lacks form-functional content
- Fanfare gestures in slow introductions serve a different function (solemnity, anticipation) than in main themes (formal initiation)
