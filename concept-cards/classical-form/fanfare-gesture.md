# Quick Definition
A heraldic, declamatory opening style found in some main themes, built over a long tonic pedal, that projects a strong opening but obscures conventional phrase-functional classification.

# Core Definition
Some main themes begin with a long tonic pedal, over which may be found gestures that evoke a heraldic, fanfare style. Such powerful passages are ideal for projecting a strong opening, but they often obscure a clear sense of basic idea and its repetition, or its juxtaposition with a contrasting idea. The lack of a conventional initiating function makes it difficult to classify such a theme as one of the standard types. The theme often has a general sentential quality (cadential function is obvious, rhythmic activity accelerates) but presentation and continuation functions cannot be clearly distinguished. Fanfare gestures also characterize slow introductions, where they combine with uncertainty and instability.

# Prerequisites
- **Nonconventional main theme**: The broader category these themes belong to

# Key Properties
1. Long tonic pedal supporting the passage
2. Unison or octave textures, often tutti
3. Heraldic, declamatory rhythmic profiles (dotted rhythms, repeated notes, triadic figures)
4. Strong sense of formal opening despite unclear basic idea
5. General sentential quality but presentation/continuation cannot be distinguished
6. Often leads to cadential passage after rhythmic acceleration
7. Also characteristic of slow introductions (combining solemnity with uncertainty)

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify a long tonic pedal at the opening
- Note unison/octave textures and heraldic rhythmic profiles
- Observe the difficulty of demarcating a conventional basic idea
- Look for a general acceleration of rhythmic activity
- Note cadential function near the end

# Context & Application
Fanfare gestures create an unmistakable sense of formal beginning and establish the movement's character. They are especially common in orchestral works (symphonies, overtures). In slow introductions, fanfare gestures serve a different function (solemnity, anticipation) than in main themes (formal initiation).

# Examples
- Haydn, Symphony No. 97 in C, i (ex. 13.4): full orchestra in unisons and octaves; fanfare-like gestures over 8 measures; grouping structure too ambiguous for conventional basic idea/contrasting idea; cadential function at m. 21; general sentential quality
- Mozart, Symphony No. 38 ("Prague"), K. 504, i (ex. 13.2): codetta at mm. 43-45 contains fanfare motives that compensate for minimal root-position tonic at the opening

# Common Errors
- Confusing a fanfare opening with an introduction (it is the main theme itself)
- Assuming lack of a clear basic idea means lack of form-functional content

# Common Confusions
- **Not an introduction**: A fanfare opening is the main theme itself, with cadential substance
- **Sentential quality**: The general sentential quality (cadence, acceleration) is present even without clear presentation/continuation boundaries
- **Slow introduction difference**: Fanfare gestures in slow introductions serve solemnity and anticipation, not formal initiation
