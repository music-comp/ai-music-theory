# Quick Definition
One of the most vigorous debates in music theory history: whether the form with exposition (A), contrasting middle (B), and recapitulation (A') should be understood as fundamentally two-part (binary) or three-part (ternary).

# Core Definition
Binary advocates argue: (1) the two sections are often similar in length; (2) when A modulates, the tonal process (away from and return to home key) is bipartite; (3) the B section is structurally dependent on A'. Ternary advocates argue that the binary view minimizes the genuinely contrasting middle and the recapitulation of opening material. Caplin concludes both views say something important and neither should be rejected, though he emphasizes the ternary approach for its compatibility with formal functionality.

# Construction / Recognition
1. **Binary arguments**: Two-part repeat scheme; similar section lengths; bipartite tonal process; B depends on A'
2. **Ternary arguments**: Three distinct functions; genuine contrast in B; meaningful return in A'
3. **Resolution**: Both views compatible; "small ternary" for functional analysis, "rounded binary" for bipartite repeat structure

# Context & Application
The debate has deep historical roots. Caplin's framework resolves it by acknowledging both perspectives while emphasizing formal functionality.

# Examples
- Applies to virtually all small ternaries/rounded binaries in the classical repertory

# Common Confusions
- The debate is not about section count (everyone agrees on A, B, A') but about which organizational principle is fundamental
- Caplin does NOT reject the binary view entirely
- The small binary (Ch. 7) is a genuinely different form, not part of this debate
