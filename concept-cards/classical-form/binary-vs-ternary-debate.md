# Quick Definition
One of the most vigorous debates in music theory history: whether the form ||: A :||: B + A' :|| should be understood as fundamentally two-part (binary) or three-part (ternary).

# Core Definition
Binary advocates argue: (1) the two repeated parts are often similar in length; (2) when A modulates, the tonal process (away from and return to home key) is bipartite; (3) the B section is structurally dependent on A'. Ternary advocates argue that the binary view minimizes two significant aspects: the genuinely contrasting middle, and the recapitulation of opening material. Caplin concludes both views say something important and neither should be rejected. He emphasizes the ternary approach for its greater compatibility with issues of formal functionality but uses "rounded binary" to acknowledge the bipartite repeat structure.

# Prerequisites
- **Small ternary**: The ternary interpretation of the form
- **Rounded binary**: The binary interpretation of the form

# Key Properties
1. Binary arguments: two-part repeat scheme; similar section lengths; bipartite tonal process; B depends on A'
2. Ternary arguments: three distinct functions; genuine contrast in B; meaningful return in A'
3. Both views are compatible with each other and with the music
4. Caplin uses "small ternary" for functional analysis, "rounded binary" for bipartite repeat structure
5. The debate has deep historical roots in theories of form

# Construction / Recognition
1. Recognize that the same form can be viewed from both perspectives
2. Use "small ternary" when discussing formal functions (exposition, contrasting middle, recapitulation)
3. Use "rounded binary" when discussing the repeat structure
4. Do not treat the two labels as mutually exclusive

# Context & Application
The debate has deep historical roots. Caplin's framework resolves it by acknowledging both perspectives while emphasizing formal functionality. The resolution is important pedagogically because it frees the analyst from having to choose one interpretation at the expense of the other.

# Examples
- Applies to virtually all small ternaries/rounded binaries in the classical repertory

# Common Errors
- Treating this as an either/or question -- both views capture something real about the music
- Confusing this debate with the distinction between small ternary and small binary -- the latter is a genuinely different form
- Assuming Caplin entirely rejects the binary view

# Common Confusions
- The debate is not about section count (everyone agrees on A, B, A') but about which organizational principle is fundamental
- Caplin does NOT reject the binary view entirely -- he acknowledges both perspectives
- The small binary (Ch. 8) is a genuinely different form, not part of this debate
