# Quick Definition
Three types of cadential deviation prevent an implied authentic cadence from occurring: the deceptive cadence (final tonic replaced by substitute harmony, event groups backward as a goal), the evaded cadence (post-dominant event groups forward as a new beginning, no closure achieved), and the abandoned cadence (the cadential dominant itself is undermined or omitted).

# Core Definition
In some formal situations, an authentic cadence is promised but does not actually occur. Three types of cadential deviation are responsible: (1) the deceptive cadence, where the final root-position tonic is replaced by another harmony (usually VI) but the event groups backward as a goal; (2) the evaded cadence, where the post-dominant event groups forward as a new beginning, leaving the cadential process unresolved; (3) the abandoned cadence, where the cadential dominant is itself undermined (inverted) or omitted. The distinction between deceptive and evaded cadences depends critically on grouping structure, not just harmony. Following any cadential deviation, the music usually "backs up" to previous material for another attempt at the cadence.

# Prerequisites
- **Authentic cadence**: The cadence being thwarted
- **Cadential progression**: The harmonic pattern leading to the expected cadence

# Key Properties
1. **Deceptive cadence**: Substitute harmony replaces final tonic; event groups BACKWARD as a goal (partial closure); deceptive cadential progression results
2. **Evaded cadence**: Post-dominant event groups FORWARD as a new beginning (NO closure); most dramatic technique; dominant often resolves to I6; "one more time" technique
3. **Abandoned cadence**: Problem with the cadential dominant itself -- undermined (inverted) or omitted; may or may not have a specific moment of denied arrival
4. All three deviations apply only to authentic cadences, not half cadences
5. Grouping structure is as important as harmony in distinguishing deviation types
6. A deceptive *resolution* (V to VI in any context) is NOT the same as a deceptive *cadence* (only in truly cadential situations)
7. Following any deviation, the music usually attempts the cadence again

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify a cadential progression approaching resolution
2. Check what happens at the expected moment of closure:
   - Substitute harmony that groups backward = deceptive cadence
   - Event that groups forward as new beginning = evaded cadence
   - Dominant undermined/omitted before resolution = abandoned cadence
3. Note the grouping: does the post-dominant event end the current phrase or begin the next?
4. Distinguish deceptive *cadence* from deceptive *resolution* (the latter is noncadential)

# Context & Application
Cadential deviations are infrequent in simple main themes but appear much more regularly in subordinate themes and loosely organized sections. The evaded cadence is the most dramatic technique, creating a marked disruption in grouping structure. The deceptive cadence achieves partial closure (making it less disruptive). The abandoned cadence addresses a problem with the dominant itself rather than with the resolution. The "one more time" technique, coined by Janet Schmalfeldt, specifically describes the effect of evaded cadences where previous material is restated.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in A Minor, K. 310, iii, mm. 1-20 (R = 2N): deceptive cadence at m. 16; V resolves to VI; event groups backward; contrasting idea repeated with variant
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in F, K. 280, i, mm. 1-13: evaded cadence at m. 10; post-dominant material groups forward, referencing m. 7; continuation runs through "one more time"
- Beethoven, Violin Sonata in E-flat, Op. 12/3, i, mm. 1-13: abandoned cadence at m. 8; dominant undermined by stepwise bass ascent to six-five inversion

# Common Errors
- Labeling every V-VI motion a "deceptive cadence" -- only use the term in truly cadential situations
- Confusing deceptive with evaded based solely on harmony -- the distinction lies in grouping (backward = deceptive; forward = evaded)
- Assuming all cadential deviations are equally dramatic -- they form a hierarchy from partial closure (deceptive) to no closure (evaded)

# Common Confusions
- Deceptive vs. evaded: the distinction is in GROUPING, not just harmony; deceptive = event groups backward (goal); evaded = event groups forward (new beginning)
- Deceptive cadence vs. deceptive resolution: save "deceptive cadence" for cases where you could change VI to I and recognize a genuine cadence
- "One more time" technique: coined by Schmalfeldt specifically for evaded cadences, though the concept of returning to prior material applies after other deviations too
- An evaded cadence can occur even if V resolves to root-position I, as long as the tonic groups forward as a beginning
