# Quick Definition
The coda's function of recalling material from the development section, especially at the coda's start, where the analogous formal positions of development and coda (both following a closing section) create a natural correspondence.

# Core Definition
Whereas the recapitulation requires the composer to rework the exposition, an opportunity to bring back material from the development section is not necessarily afforded by any standard full-movement form. If the composer wishes to refer to ideas from the development, the logical place is in the coda, especially at its start. Both the development and coda follow on similar material (the closing section of the exposition and recapitulation respectively), making the opening of the coda an appropriate location for such references. The early part of the coda is also appropriate for destabilizing the prevailing emphasis on the home-key tonic, especially through model-sequence technique that may resemble a development core -- though the sequencing is nonmodulatory and harmonies remain closely bound to the home key. The analogous formal positions have led some theorists to view the coda as a "terminal development" (particularly in Beethoven), but following Kerman's critique, Caplin argues that these sections are fundamentally different in tonal and formal organization.

# Prerequisites
- **Coda**: The section containing the reference
- **Compensatory functions**: The broader category
- **Development section**: The source of the recalled material

# Key Properties
1. The recapitulation has no built-in opportunity to reference development material
2. The coda provides the logical place, especially at its start
3. Development and coda occupy analogous formal positions (both follow a closing section)
4. Development-like techniques (sequences, model-sequence) in the coda remain within the home key
5. The coda is NOT a "terminal development" -- its tonal organization is fundamentally stable
6. This view follows Kerman's critique of earlier theories

# Construction / Recognition
- Look for development material (especially from the core) appearing in the coda
- Most commonly found at the start of the coda
- Note that sequential/developmental techniques remain within the home key
- Distinguish from the actual development's modulatory instability

# Context & Application
The coda's reference to development material should not be confused with the coda functioning as a development. The two sections are fundamentally different: the development generates maximum tonal instability, while the coda maintains stability. The reference is a feature of the coda, not its defining characteristic.

# Examples
- Mozart, Symphony No. 36 ("Linz"), K. 425, i (ex. 12.2): opening of the coda clearly derives from the core of the development (ex. 10.7a, mm. 128ff.); the rest of the coda "develops" this material entirely in the stable home key; although the source is the retransition (ex. 10.7a, mm. 119-21), the treatment in the development core gives the impression of new material

# Common Confusions
- **Not a terminal development**: The coda's tonal organization is fundamentally stable; the development's is fundamentally unstable
- **Technique vs. function**: Development-like techniques in the coda do not make the coda a development
- **Kerman's critique**: The notion of the coda as a terminal development has "fallen into disrepute" following Kerman's analysis
