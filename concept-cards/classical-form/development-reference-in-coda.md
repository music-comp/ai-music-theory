# Quick Definition
The coda's function of recalling material from the development section, particularly at the coda's start, where the analogous formal positions of development and coda (both following a closing section) create a natural correspondence.

# Formal Definition
Whereas the recapitulation requires the composer to rework the exposition, an opportunity to bring back material from the development section is not necessarily afforded by any standard full-movement form. The coda provides a logical place to refer to development ideas, especially at its start. Both the development and the coda follow similar material (the closing section of the exposition and recapitulation respectively), making the opening of the coda an appropriate location for such references. The early part of the coda is also appropriate for destabilizing the home-key tonic through model-sequence technique resembling a development, though entirely in the stable context of the home key.

# Construction/Recognition
- Development material (often from the core) appears in the coda
- Most commonly found at the start of the coda
- Sequential and developmental techniques may be used, but within the home key
- The material is "developed" in a stable tonal context, unlike the actual development
- The analogous formal positions create a natural correspondence

# Musical Context
The coda's reference to development material has led some theorists to view the coda as a "terminal development," particularly in Beethoven. However, Caplin (following Kerman) argues that the two sections are fundamentally different in tonal and formal organization, so the coda merely refers to development material without functioning as a development.

# Examples
- Mozart, Symphony No. 36 ("Linz"), K. 425, i (ex. 12.2): opening of the coda clearly derives from the core of the development (ex. 10.7a, mm. 128ff.); the rest of the coda "develops" this material entirely in the home key

# Common Confusions
- The coda is not a "terminal development" — its tonal organization is fundamentally stable, unlike the development proper
- Development-like techniques in the coda (sequences, model-sequence) remain within the home key
- The reference to development material is a feature of the coda, not its defining characteristic
