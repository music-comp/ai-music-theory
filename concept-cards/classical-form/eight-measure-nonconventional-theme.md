# Quick Definition
A main theme organized nonconventionally (not a sentence, period, hybrid, or ternary) but occupying the standard eight measures, retaining a distinct sense of tight-knit organization through its compact proportions despite formal irregularities such as asymmetrical grouping structures.

# Core Definition
Some main themes are organized in a nonconventional manner but still occupy eight measures, just like the simple sentence, period, or hybrid. As a result, the nonconventional theme retains a distinct sense of tight-knit organization despite its formal irregularities. Most often, the grouping structure of the eight measures is asymmetrical (e.g., 2+3+3, 3+3+2, 3+2+3), yet the internal units nonetheless manifest a definite expression of phrase functionality. The theme may have a general sentential or periodic quality without fully qualifying as either type. The standard formal functions (basic idea, continuation, cadential) are identifiable, but they do not organize themselves in standard ways.

# Prerequisites
- **Nonconventional main theme**: The broader category
- **Sentence and period**: The conventional types against which nonconventionality is measured

# Key Properties
1. Occupies the standard eight measures
2. Cannot be accommodated by standard categories (sentence, period, hybrid, ternary)
3. Asymmetrical internal grouping structure (e.g., 2+3+3, 3+3+2)
4. Internal units express identifiable phrase functions
5. May have general sentential or periodic quality without fully qualifying
6. Retains tight-knit organization through compact proportions
7. Bottom-up analysis is recommended (group surface units, then assign functions)

# Construction / Recognition
- Note the theme occupies exactly eight measures
- Attempt to classify as sentence, period, hybrid -- if none fits, it is nonconventional
- Use bottom-up analysis: group material into basic units on the surface, then establish higher-level groupings
- Assign functional labels to the groups (initiating, medial, concluding)
- Note asymmetrical subdivisions
- The theme may be "more sentential than periodic" or vice versa without being either type

# Context & Application
The bottom-up analytical approach is recommended for nonconventional themes. First consider the harmonic organization. Then group material into basic units. Establish higher-level groupings. Finally assign formal functions. Even when the theme is nonconventional, its component units usually exhibit some logical sense of functionality, even if we can speak only generally of an "initiating function," a "medial function," or a "concluding function." What makes the theme nonconventional is that these functions do not organize themselves in standard ways, not that there is no sense of functionality at all.

# Examples
- Haydn, String Quartet in G minor ("Rider"), Op. 74/3, iv, mm. 1-8 (Ch. 10, Ex. 10.1): 2+3+3 grouping; opening basic idea (1+1), continuation (3 mm., rising bass), cadential HC (3 mm.); "more sentential than periodic" but not a genuine sentence
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E minor, H. 34, i, mm. 1-8 (Ch. 10, Ex. 10.3): 3+3+2 grouping; initiating (mm. 1-3, tonic), continuation+cadential (mm. 4-6, HC), postcadential standing on dominant (mm. 7-8)
- Mozart, Symphony No. 38 ("Prague"), K. 504, i, mm. 37-45 (Ch. 10, Ex. 10.2): introductory m. 37, continuation (mm. 38-43, IAC), codetta (mm. 44-45); circumvents any clearly defined initiating unit

# Common Errors
- Forcing a conventional label (sentence or period) on a theme that does not fully qualify
- Using top-down analysis on a theme that requires bottom-up approach
- Treating nonconventionality as absence of functionality

# Common Confusions
- **Not disorganized**: Nonconventional does not mean nonfunctional; functions are present but arranged nonstandard
- **Eight measures = tight-knit**: The compact eight-measure span preserves tight-knit organization regardless of internal irregularities
- **Bottom-up vs. top-down**: Top-down analysis (start with sentence/period label) fails here; use bottom-up (start with surface grouping, then assign functions)
