# Quick Definition
An interpolation is musical material inserted between two logically succeeding formal functions that seems to belong to neither function. It is distinguished from extension by the absence of motivic connection with the preceding function, and it can be easily removed to restore a normative structure.

# Formal Definition
An interpolation can be defined as musical material that is inserted between two logically succeeding formal functions, yet seeming not to belong to either function. An interpolation is distinguished from an extension largely through the absence of a motivic connection between the interpolated material and its preceding function, so that this function does not appear to be stretched out in time. An extension, on the contrary, usually has an intimate motivic connection with the material that it is extending. An interpolation can easily be eliminated in order to restore a more normative grouping structure.

# Construction/Recognition
1. Material appears between two adjacent formal functions
2. Does not motivically relate to the preceding function (distinguishing it from extension)
3. Does not belong to the following function
4. Can be removed to create a normative version of the passage
5. May nonetheless play a significant role in larger melodic or harmonic processes
6. May occur in both antecedent and consequent to maintain symmetry

# Musical Context
Interpolations expand the phrase without disrupting the fundamental formal logic. Despite seeming incidental, interpolated passages can play significant roles in articulating larger-scale melodic processes — filling in intervallic gaps, reasserting melodic energy, or preparing subsequent material. They are found in both symmetrical and asymmetrical deviations.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Concerto in F, K. 459, ii, mm. 1-10: rising eighth-note figure interpolated after the basic idea (m. 2, second half) in both antecedent and consequent; the figure fills in the melodic leap of the basic idea, participating in larger melodic processes despite seeming unrelated; can be excised to create normative 4+4 period
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C, K. 279/189d, iii, mm. 7-8: two interpolated measures in the consequent, belonging to neither the basic idea nor the contrasting idea

# Common Confusions
- Interpolation vs. extension: interpolation lacks motivic connection with the preceding material; extension is motivically continuous with what precedes it
- Despite seeming "foreign," interpolated material may serve important larger-scale functions (melodic, registral, rhythmic) — do not dismiss it as purely incidental
- Interpolation is a neutral term — it does not imply the material is unimportant, only that it is formally insertional
