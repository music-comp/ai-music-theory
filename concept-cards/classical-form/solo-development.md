# Quick Definition
The development section in concerto form, typically featuring more rhapsodic, improvisatory character with conventional passage-work rather than the intensive motivic working-out (motivische Arbeit) characteristic of symphonic developments.

# Formal Definition
The solo development of concerto form is organized for all intents and purposes the same as that of sonata form. Two distinctive features relate to the concerto's textural dynamics: (1) since this section corresponds to the "second solo" of ritornello form, it usually begins with a reappearance of the solo part after its silence during the subordinate-key ritornello; (2) the development tends to be less a working-out of prominent motivic material and more a rhapsodic improvisation, often employing conventional passage-work (scales, arpeggiations, and the like). The motivische Arbeit typical of Haydn's or Beethoven's symphonies would employ polyphonic textures that direct attention to the orchestra rather than the solo; conversely, figurational patterns foster greater virtuosic display while the orchestra remains largely in the background.

# Construction/Recognition
- Begins with reappearance of the solo after silence during subordinate-key ritornello
- More improvisatory and rhapsodic character than symphonic developments
- Employs conventional passage-work (scales, arpeggiations) rather than intensive motivic development
- Orchestra remains largely in the background
- Figurational patterns highlight the soloist's virtuosity
- Formal organization otherwise follows the norms of sonata-form development

# Musical Context
The solo development showcases the soloist's improvisatory and virtuosic abilities rather than providing the motivic working-out characteristic of chamber and symphonic music. This reflects the concerto's fundamental aesthetic goal of highlighting the soloist.

# Examples
- Concerto developments by Mozart and Beethoven that feature extensive figurational passage-work for the soloist

# Common Confusions
- The concerto development is not necessarily less complex than a symphonic development; it simply emphasizes different compositional techniques (virtuosic passage-work vs. motivic working-out).
- The orchestra's subordinate role in the development does not mean it is purely accompanimental; it still provides harmonic support and may engage in dialogue with the solo.
