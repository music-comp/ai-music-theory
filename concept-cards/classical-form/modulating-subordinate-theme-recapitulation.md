# Quick Definition
When the exposition's subordinate theme modulates internally, the recapitulation requires complex tonal adjustment -- the theme may begin "off tonic" or in a non-tonic region and must be redirected to end in the home key.

# Core Definition
If the exposition contains a modulating subordinate theme, the tonal adjustment in the recapitulation will be more complicated than usual. In some cases, the subordinate theme can still give the impression of modulating by beginning in some other tonal region (of the home key) and eventually returning to emphasize the tonic region. In other cases, the theme begins directly on the home-key tonic and so loses some of its modulatory character. Simple transposition does not work because it would end the theme in a wrong key. Creative harmonic recomposition is required to redirect the tonal motion back to the home key.

# Prerequisites
- **Recapitulation**: The formal context

# Key Properties
1. Simple transposition does not work (would end in wrong key)
2. Two solutions: begin off-tonic to preserve modulatory impression, or begin on tonic and lose modulatory character
3. Harmonies must be reorganized to arrive on home-key dominant
4. The "off-tonic" beginning may use the same secondary dominant as in the exposition, resolving differently
5. The resulting tonal detour is not a genuine modulation but a tonal excursion within the home key

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify whether the exposition's subordinate theme modulates internally
- In the recapitulation, check whether the theme begins in a non-tonic region
- Trace the harmonic reorganization needed to arrive on home-key dominant
- Note whether the modulatory character is preserved or lost

# Context & Application
Modulating subordinate themes are a particular feature of Beethoven's style. They create the greatest challenges for tonal adjustment because simple transposition would land in the wrong key.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 31/3, ii (ex. 11.13): transition ends on V/VI as in exposition; subordinate theme begins in bVII region; Beethoven introduces octave Db as link (sounds like deceptive resolution of V/VI but functions as dominant of bVII); theme eventually "modulates" back to tonic
- Beethoven, Overture to Coriolanus, Op. 62 (ex. 11.14): subordinate theme begins directly in home-key major; Beethoven reorganizes harmonies to arrive on home-key dominant at m. 206; eventual E minor reached at m. 198 but redirected

# Common Errors
- Assuming simple transposition works for modulating subordinate themes
- Failing to trace the harmonic reorganization in detail

# Common Confusions
- **Not a genuine modulation**: The off-tonic beginning and tonal motion in the recapitulation create a tonal detour, not a real modulation
- **Variable solutions**: Some themes preserve modulatory character (off-tonic beginning); others lose it (direct tonic beginning)
- **Linking harmonies**: The connection between transition ending and subordinate theme beginning may require creative reinterpretation of chords
