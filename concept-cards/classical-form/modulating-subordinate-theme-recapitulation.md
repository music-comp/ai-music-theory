# Quick Definition
When the exposition's subordinate theme modulates internally, the recapitulation requires complex tonal adjustment — the theme may begin "off tonic" or in a non-tonic region and must be redirected to end in the home key.

# Formal Definition
If the exposition contains a modulating subordinate theme, the tonal adjustment in the recapitulation will be more complicated than usual. In some cases, the subordinate theme can still give the impression of modulating by beginning in some other tonal region (of the home key) and eventually returning to emphasize the tonic region. In other cases, the theme begins directly on the home-key tonic and so loses some of its modulatory character.

# Construction/Recognition
- Identify whether the exposition's subordinate theme modulates internally
- In the recapitulation, the theme may begin in a non-tonic region (e.g., IV, bVII) to preserve the sense of tonal motion
- The harmonies must be reorganized to arrive on the home-key dominant and eventually achieve a PAC in the home key
- Sometimes the theme begins on the tonic directly, losing its original modulatory quality
- Register and melodic adjustments may be needed alongside harmonic ones

# Musical Context
Modulating subordinate themes are a particular feature of Beethoven's style. They create additional challenges for the recapitulation because a simple transposition would end in a wrong key.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 31/3, ii (ex. 11.13): transition ends on V/VI as in exposition; subordinate theme begins in bVII region and "modulates" back to tonic by the end
- Beethoven, Overture to Coriolanus, Op. 62 (ex. 11.14): subordinate theme begins directly in home-key major; harmonies reorganized to arrive on home-key dominant (m. 206)

# Common Confusions
- A modulating subordinate theme in the recapitulation does not create a genuine modulation — it is a tonal detour that returns to the home key
- Simple transposition does not work for these themes; creative harmonic recomposition is required
- The "off-tonic" beginning may use the same secondary dominant as in the exposition, resolving to a different chord
