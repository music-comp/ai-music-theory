# Quick Definition
Interior themes in rondo couplets can be structured as complete or truncated small ternaries, small binaries, sentential structures, or nonconventional forms, and are normally followed by a retransition that may be incorporated into a contrasting middle when the small ternary is truncated.

# Core Definition
Interior themes find widespread use within rondo forms. The second couplet of most five-part rondos features this thematic function, and the first couplet may also be built as an interior theme (especially in Haydn). Within a sonata-rondo, the second couplet may also be an interior theme. As in the large ternary, the interior theme is set as a minore or in the submediant or subdominant regions. The theme may be structured in diverse ways: as a complete or cadentially open small ternary, as a small binary, as a sentential structure, or as a nonconventional form. Sometimes the small ternary underlying an interior theme is truncated (its A' section omitted). No matter how the interior theme is structured, it is normally followed by a retransition. In some cases, retransition function is incorporated into a contrasting middle when the small ternary is truncated, since that section becomes the last part of the couplet (Caplin, pp. 675-677).

# Prerequisites
- **Interior theme (rondo)** -- The couplet type being structured
- **Interior theme (large ternary)** -- The analogous technique
- **Small ternary** -- The most common structural model

# Key Properties
1. Set in minore, submediant, or subdominant regions
2. Most commonly structured as a small ternary (complete or modified)
3. Small ternary may be cadentially open or truncated (A' omitted)
4. May also be sentential, small binary, or nonconventional
5. Normally followed by a retransition
6. Retransition may be incorporated into the contrasting middle when truncated
7. More structurally diverse than interior themes in large ternary

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the couplet as residing in minore, submediant, or subdominant
2. Determine the underlying theme type (small ternary, sentence, etc.)
3. Check whether the form is complete, open-ended, or truncated
4. Locate the retransition and determine whether it is separate or incorporated

# Context & Application
The structural diversity of rondo interior themes reflects the form's flexibility. Unlike the large ternary, where the interior theme has a more fixed relationship to the main theme, the rondo interior theme serves primarily as contrasting material between refrain returns.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 676): Haydn, Piano Sonata in G, H. 39, i, mm. 17-34 -- first couplet as minore interior theme built as truncated small ternary; retransition function incorporated into the contrasting middle.
**Example 2** (p. 669): Beethoven, Piano Sonata in C minor ("Pathetique"), Op. 13, iii, mm. 79-122 -- second couplet as interior theme in the submediant, structured as an incomplete small ternary with modulating period, retransition with standing on the dominant.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Expecting all rondo interior themes to follow the same structural model. **Correction**: Interior themes can be structured in diverse ways, from complete small ternaries to nonconventional forms.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Whether a truncated small ternary lacks a retransition. **Clarification**: The retransition function is incorporated into the contrasting middle when the A' section is omitted.
