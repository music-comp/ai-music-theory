# Quick Definition
The tendency for the A' section of a minuet to be significantly expanded beyond the A section, using loosening devices, new material, and cadential extensions to restore tonal stability and compensate for earlier subordinate-theme function.

# Key Properties
See Core Definition.
Unlike the small ternary, which frequently contains a compressed recapitulation, the A' section of a minuet is seldom shorter than the A section and is much more likely to be significantly expanded. The expansion is created by loosening devices typically associated with a subordinate theme: deceptive cadences (particularly common in minuets), interpolated episodes of new material, and extended cadential areas. The expansion can be explained on several grounds: (1) if the A section is shorter than the B section, an equally short A' may not give the home key enough space to reassert itself; (2) the expansion provides a kind of "recapitulation of the subordinate theme" by matching the subordinate-theme function expressed in an earlier B section; (3) the deceptive cadence is a particularly effective device for delaying closure.

# Construction / Recognition
- The A' section is longer than the A section
- Loosening devices are present: deceptive cadences, new material, interpolated episodes
- Extended cadential areas may appear
- In extreme cases, the recapitulation separates transition and subordinate-theme functions that were fused in the exposition into their own distinct thematic units
- A new closing section may be added
- The recapitulation may be followed by a coda

# Context & Application
The expanded recapitulation is one of the most distinctive features of minuet form, distinguishing it from the typically compressed recapitulation of the small ternary. This expansion compensates for the compact A section and restores tonal stability after the B section's tonal explorations.

# Examples
- Haydn, String Quartet in G, Op. 54/1, iii, mm. 33-44: A' section expanded with a deceptive cadence and development of exposition motives
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, iii, mm. 51-95: Extensively expanded recapitulation that separates the fused transition/subordinate-theme functions of the A section into distinct units, with shift to minor, development, new presentation, and deceptive cadences

# Common Confusions
- Students may expect the A' section to simply restate the A section with tonal adjustments, as in many small ternaries. In minuet form, the A' section frequently goes well beyond the A section's scope.
- The expansion is not arbitrary; it serves specific formal functions, particularly compensating for subordinate-theme expression and establishing tonal stability.
