# Quick Definition
The first large-scale section of sonata form, establishing two contrasting tonal regions through a succession of cadential goals articulated by the main theme, transition, and subordinate theme.

# Core Definition
The sonata exposition modulates to a subordinate key, giving rise to the central tonal characteristic of the form: the dramatic establishment of two contrasting tonal regions. The exposition contains three interthematic functions -- main theme (establishing the home key), transition (destabilizing the home key and leading to the subordinate key), and subordinate theme (confirming the subordinate key with PAC) -- arranged in a specific cadential sequence. The exposition is normally repeated. The minimum cadential requirements are at least one home-key cadence and a PAC in the subordinate key. Eight standard cadential patterns (Table 13.1) describe the possible arrangements of cadential goals and interthematic functions.

# Prerequisites
- **Sonata form**: The formal framework
- **Main theme**: Establishes the home key
- **Transition**: Destabilizes the home key
- **Subordinate theme**: Confirms the subordinate key

# Key Properties
1. First large-scale section of sonata form
2. Necessarily modulates to a subordinate key (V in major, III in minor)
3. Contains three interthematic functions: main theme, transition, subordinate theme
4. Minimum cadential requirements: one HK cadence + PAC in SK
5. Five potential cadential goals in the complete tonal curve
6. Eight standard cadential patterns (Table 13.1)
7. Normally repeated (indicated by double-bar lines)
8. Presents the movement's melodic-motivic material in its original form
9. Establishes the tonal conflict driving the rest of the movement
10. May include a closing section with codettas and/or retransition

# Construction / Recognition
- **Main theme**: confirms the home key (PAC or HC); tight-knit organization
- **Transition**: destabilizes the home key; modulates to the subordinate key; ends with HC or dominant arrival
- **Subordinate theme**: confirms the subordinate key with PAC; loose organization
- **Closing section**: codettas following the final PAC; may include a retransition
- Identify which of the eight cadential patterns applies

# Context & Application
The exposition presents the movement's material in its original form and establishes the tonal conflict. Its repeat ensures the listener absorbs both the material and the tonal plan. The tonal conflict (structural dissonance between home key and subordinate key) drives the development and is resolved in the recapitulation.

# Common Errors
- Treating the exposition as simply "the themes" rather than a tonal process articulated by cadences
- Assuming the main theme must always end with PAC (HC is possible)

# Common Confusions
- **Tonal process**: The exposition is not simply a collection of themes but a tonal process articulated by cadences
- **Main theme cadence**: The main theme does not always end with PAC; HC is possible (especially in minor mode)
- **Boundary ambiguity**: The boundary between transition and subordinate theme may be obscured or fused (see separate cards)
- **Repeat**: The exposition repeat is normative, not optional, in the classical period
