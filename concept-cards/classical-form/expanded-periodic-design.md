# Quick Definition
A loosening technique in which periodic functions (antecedent and consequent) appear in a subordinate theme with the consequent expanded through techniques like expanded cadential progressions, creating a looser organization than a standard period.

# Formal Definition
Antecedent and consequent functions occur less frequently in subordinate-theme construction than sentential functions. When periodic functions do appear, the antecedent usually retains its conventional size (four or eight measures), but the consequent is often expanded to create a looser organization. A period is rarely found if the exposition contains just a single subordinate theme. In cases of multiple subordinate themes, one of the themes (usually the first but occasionally the last) may assume this tight-knit periodic form with the consequent expanded by means of expanded cadential progressions or other loosening techniques.

# Construction/Recognition
1. Antecedent phrase of conventional size (usually 4 or 8 measures)
2. Consequent phrase expanded beyond its normative length
3. Expansion typically through expanded cadential progression in the consequent
4. The period retains antecedent-consequent cadential differentiation (HC then PAC)
5. The expanded consequent makes the overall structure looser than a conventional period
6. Rarely appears as the only subordinate theme; more common in subordinate-theme groups
7. Often found in the first subordinate theme of a group

# Musical Context
The expanded periodic design represents a compromise between tight-knit and loose organization. The basic periodic structure (with its antecedent-consequent relationship) provides a degree of stability, while the expanded consequent introduces the looseness appropriate to subordinate themes. This design is particularly useful when the composer wants one subordinate theme in a group to be relatively tight knit while reserving more extreme loosening for other themes.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in D, K. 576, i, mm. 42-53 (second subordinate theme: 4-measure antecedent; consequent expanded by expanded cadential progression in mm. 48-53; basic idea of new theme derived from contrasting idea of main theme)

# Common Confusions
- An expanded periodic design is NOT a standard period -- the expanded consequent makes it a distinctly loose structure
- The antecedent-consequent cadential pattern (HC then PAC) does not by itself create a period; the typical internal characteristics must also be present
- A two-part subordinate theme with HC then PAC is NOT a period if the second part begins with a NEW basic idea (see Two-Part Subordinate Theme)
- The expanded consequent's looseness is relative to the antecedent's tightness -- the comparison within the period creates the loosening effect
