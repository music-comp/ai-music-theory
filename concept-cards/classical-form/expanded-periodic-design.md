# Quick Definition
A loosening technique in which periodic functions (antecedent and consequent) appear in a subordinate theme, with the consequent expanded through techniques like expanded cadential progressions, creating a looser organization than a standard period.

# Core Definition
Antecedent and consequent functions occur less frequently in subordinate-theme construction than do the sentential functions of presentation, continuation, and cadential. When periodic functions do appear, the antecedent usually retains its conventional size of four or eight measures, but the consequent is often expanded in order to create a looser organization. A period is rarely found if the exposition contains just a single subordinate theme. In cases of multiple subordinate themes, one of the themes (usually the first but occasionally the last) may assume this tight-knit periodic form with the consequent expanded by means of expanded cadential progressions or other loosening techniques. The expanded periodic design represents a compromise between tight-knit and loose organization.

# Prerequisites
- **Period**: The tight-knit theme type being loosened
- **Antecedent**: The first phrase, retaining conventional size
- **Consequent**: The second phrase, expanded beyond its normative length
- **Subordinate theme**: The formal context requiring loose organization
- **Expanded cadential progression**: The typical means of consequent expansion

# Key Properties
1. Antecedent phrase of conventional size (usually 4 or 8 measures)
2. Consequent phrase expanded beyond its normative length
3. Expansion typically through expanded cadential progression in the consequent
4. Retains antecedent-consequent cadential differentiation (HC then PAC)
5. The expanded consequent makes the overall structure looser than a conventional period
6. Rarely appears as the only subordinate theme; more common in subordinate-theme groups
7. Often found in the first subordinate theme of a group
8. The basic periodic structure provides stability while the expansion provides looseness

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify an antecedent phrase of conventional size ending with HC
- Look for a consequent phrase that exceeds its expected length
- Check whether the expansion is achieved through an expanded cadential progression
- Verify that the antecedent-consequent cadential pattern (HC then PAC) is present
- Distinguish from a two-part subordinate theme: in the expanded periodic design, the consequent begins with the SAME basic idea as the antecedent; in a two-part subordinate theme, the second part begins with a NEW basic idea

# Context & Application
The expanded periodic design is particularly useful when the composer wants one subordinate theme in a group to be relatively tight knit while reserving more extreme loosening for other themes. It provides the stability of periodic organization while introducing the looseness appropriate to subordinate themes. The basic periodic structure (with its antecedent-consequent relationship) provides a degree of predictability, while the expanded consequent introduces surprise and formal extension.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in D, K. 576, i, mm. 42-53: second subordinate theme with 4-measure antecedent; consequent expanded by expanded cadential progression in mm. 48-53; basic idea derived from contrasting idea of main theme (same pitches and rhythm, different scale-degree functions)

# Common Errors
- Calling any two-phrase subordinate theme ending HC-PAC a period
- Treating the expanded consequent as a separate theme
- Confusing the expanded periodic design with the two-part subordinate theme

# Common Confusions
- **Not a standard period**: The expanded consequent makes it a distinctly loose structure, unlike a balanced period
- **HC-PAC pattern**: The antecedent-consequent cadential pattern (HC then PAC) does not by itself create a period; the typical internal characteristics must also be present
- **Vs. two-part subordinate theme**: A two-part subordinate theme with HC then PAC is NOT a period if the second part begins with a NEW basic idea
- **Relative looseness**: The expanded consequent's looseness is relative to the antecedent's tightness; the comparison creates the loosening effect
- **"Antecedent-consequent functionality"**: Caplin notes that an IAC followed by PAC can express antecedent-consequent functionality without constituting a true period
