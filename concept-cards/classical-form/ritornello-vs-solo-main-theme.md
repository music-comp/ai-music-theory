# Quick Definition
The relationship between the main theme as presented by the orchestra in the opening ritornello and as presented by the soloist in the solo exposition, which may be the same, varied, or entirely different (alternative main theme).

# Formal Definition
The first unit of the solo exposition usually functions as a main theme. Most frequently, the solo main theme brings back material of the ritornello main theme in the same formal plan, sometimes with structural alterations (loosening devices, cadential extensions, new material) or variation procedures that imbue it with greater virtuosic character, allowing the solo to appropriate the theme for itself. When the ritornello main theme is highly orchestral and not idiomatic for the solo instrument, an entirely new alternative main theme is introduced. The relationship between the two main themes has implications for the entire movement: the recapitulation usually begins with the ritornello main theme, and the alternative main theme may reappear at the start of the development, as part of a recapitulation theme group, or may not return at all.

# Construction/Recognition
- Same theme: Solo restates the ritornello main theme with possible structural changes or ornamental variations
- Varied theme: Solo presents the ritornello theme with loosening devices, extensions, or added material
- Alternative theme: Entirely new theme, more idiomatic for the solo instrument
- The ritornello main theme is typically preferred for the recapitulation (strongest sense of return)
- An alternative main theme may appear in the development or recapitulation theme group
- The relationship governs the transition's material (per Principles 1 and 2)

# Musical Context
This relationship reflects the fundamental tension in concerto form between orchestral and solo identities. The soloist must either make the ritornello theme its own or introduce a theme better suited to its instrument, creating a web of thematic relationships that plays out across the entire movement.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Concerto in D Minor, K. 466, i: The orchestral opening (brooding, polyphonic, full-orchestra crescendo) cannot be replicated on piano; an alternative main theme (m. 77) is lyrical and pianistic; Mozart never attempts to imitate the orchestral texture in the piano part
- Many Mozart concertos where the solo main theme is a varied version of the ritornello main theme

# Common Confusions
- When the solo main theme varies the ritornello main theme, the variation should not be viewed as a failure to reproduce the original. The variation serves the purpose of making the theme soloistic and virtuosic.
- An alternative main theme is not a subordinate theme or a secondary idea; it is the primary main theme of the solo exposition, with full main-theme function.
