# Quick Definition
A passage at the end of a rondo couplet that leads back to the home key in preparation for the return of the refrain, typically longer and more elaborate than retransitions in sonata form.

# Formal Definition
The retransition in rondo form follows the close of a couplet (or emerges out of an incomplete couplet) and prepares the return of the refrain by leading to the home-key dominant. In rondo form, dramatic intensification is generally associated more with the returns of the refrain than with the appearance of couplets. Consequently, the retransition leading back to the refrain is usually longer and more elaborate than what might be found at the end of a sonata exposition. Motives anticipating the refrain's basic idea are often included to heighten expectations for its eventual return. In sonata-rondos, the retransition after a development or interior-theme couplet 2 can be highly developmental, even to the extent of resembling a core.

# Construction/Recognition
- Located at the end of a couplet, before the return of the refrain
- Leads to the home-key dominant to prepare the refrain's return
- Typically longer and more elaborate than in sonata form
- Often anticipates the refrain's basic idea motivically
- May follow the close of a couplet or emerge from an incomplete couplet
- In sonata-rondos, may be highly developmental (resembling a core)
- After couplet 2 of a sonata-rondo: may include false recapitulations

# Musical Context
The retransition is a crucial structural element in rondo form because the rondo places its primary dramatic emphasis on the returns of the refrain. The elaborate retransition builds anticipation for the refrain's return, making each reappearance a dramatic event rather than a mere repetition.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C, K. 545, iii: Four-measure retransition after the subordinate-theme complex of couplet 1
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in B-flat, K. 333/315c, iii, mm. 90-102: Extended retransition emerging from the incomplete second unit of a double-region couplet

# Common Confusions
- The rondo retransition is characteristically longer and more elaborate than retransitions in other forms. Students accustomed to brief retransitions in sonata form should expect a more substantial passage in rondos.
- The motivic anticipation of the refrain's basic idea within the retransition should not be confused with the refrain itself. The refrain proper begins only when the home key and the theme's full structure are established.
