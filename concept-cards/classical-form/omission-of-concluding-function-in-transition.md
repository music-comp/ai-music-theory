# Quick Definition
A transition ending in which the final dominant gives no sense of being an ending harmony, resolving directly to tonic at the beginning of the subordinate theme with little or no rhythmic break.

# Core Definition
Now and then, the final dominant of a transition gives no sense of being an ending harmony whatsoever. The dominant does not appear to be the goal of the progression; it receives no emphasis; and it resolves directly to tonic at the beginning of the subordinate theme with little or no rhythmic break. With a minimal sense of functional end for the transition, it can be difficult to determine where the subordinate theme begins. Usually, however, the composer provides some means of expressing a beginning, such as a new basic idea in the context of a presentation phrase. Because the transition lacks a concluding function, an internal HC and standing on the dominant often appear within the subordinate theme to restore the missing dominant emphasis.

# Prerequisites
- **Transition**: The formal unit lacking its ending
- **Dominant arrival in transition**: The more typical noncadential ending (which this goes beyond)
- **Internal half cadence**: The compensatory device that often appears in the subordinate theme

# Key Properties
1. Final dominant receives no special emphasis or elongation
2. No standing on the dominant follows
3. Dominant resolves directly to tonic with little or no rhythmic break
4. No sense of an "ending" at the transition's close
5. Difficult to determine where the subordinate theme begins
6. New basic idea or presentation phrase usually signals the ST's start
7. The ST often compensates with an internal HC and standing on the dominant
8. The missing dominant emphasis is "regained" within the subordinate theme
9. Represents the extreme end of a continuum from clear HC to absent concluding function

# Construction / Recognition
- Check whether the transition's final dominant receives any emphasis
- Note the absence of standing on the dominant
- Observe whether the dominant resolves directly to tonic without break
- Identify the subordinate theme's beginning through new material and tonic support
- Look for compensatory internal HC within the subordinate theme
- This is the most fluid transition-to-ST boundary

# Context & Application
The omission of concluding function represents the extreme end of a continuum from clear HC articulation to complete absence of concluding function. The structural consequence is significant: the subordinate theme must compensate for missing dominant emphasis by providing its own internal HC and standing on the dominant. This creates a cause-and-effect relationship between transition ending and subordinate-theme structure.

# Examples
- Haydn, Symphony No. 90, iv, mm. 28-35: transition of brilliant passage-work prolonging tonic; dominant at m. 29 resolves directly to tonic at m. 30; no "ending"; main-theme basic idea returns at m. 30 to begin subordinate theme
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C Minor, K. 457, i, mm. 19-37: transition ends mm. 22-23 with subordinate-key dominant; no cadential articulation, no standing on dominant; ST begins m. 23 with presentation on root-position tonic; internal HC at m. 30 compensates

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Transition (the formal unit lacking its ending)
- Dominant arrival (a less extreme noncadential ending)

## Enables
- Fluid transition-ST boundary (no break between formal units)
- Compensatory internal HC (subordinate theme restores dominant emphasis)

## Related
- Medial caesura (absent when concluding function is omitted)
- Internal half cadence (compensatory device in the subordinate theme)
- Standing on the dominant (absent from transition, restored in ST)

## Contrasts With
- Half cadence (clear concluding function)
- Dominant arrival (noncadential but still perceivable as an ending)

# Common Errors
- Treating the omission as a compositional deficiency (it is a deliberate formal choice)
- Failing to note the compensatory structure in the subordinate theme
- Merging the transition and subordinate theme into a single unit (they remain separate)

# Common Confusions
- **Deliberate choice**: The omission is not a deficiency but has structural consequences (compensatory internal HC)
- **Boundary identification**: Even without concluding function, the ST's beginning can usually be identified through new material and tonic support
- **Predictable compensation**: The missing dominant emphasis at the transition's end is predictably compensated within the subordinate theme
- **Two units**: The absence of a medial caesura does not mean the transition and ST are the same formal unit
