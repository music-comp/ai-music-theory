# Quick Definition
Codettas are the building blocks of closing sections, varying in size from a single chord to a four-measure phrase. Their melody circles around or descends to the tonic scale degree, their harmony prolongs tonic (often with subdominant emphasis), and they avoid reopening melodic space in a manner that might suggest a new basic idea.

# Core Definition
Individual codettas vary considerably in size and content but share common characteristics. Size ranges from a single chord to a full four-measure phrase (rarely longer, to avoid becoming a complete theme). Harmony is most often tonic prolongational, with a tonic pedal frequently employed. Some codettas bring cadential progressions. A marked emphasis on the subdominant is highly characteristic, functioning as a dynamic counterweight to the cadential dominant: the subdominant acts as an agent of relaxation, helping create the recessive dynamic typical of codettas. Melodic content tends to circle around the tonic scale degree or descend to it, avoiding opening melodic space that would suggest a new beginning. When a closing section consists of different codettas, fragmentation of the grouping structure is typically observed.

# Key Properties
1. Size: single chord to four measures (rarely longer)
2. Harmony: tonic prolongational most common; tonic pedal frequent; cadential progressions possible; subdominant emphasis characteristic
3. Melody: circles around or descends to tonic scale degree; avoids reopening melodic space; tonic-centered motion (1-3-1 or 1-5-1 typical)
4. Subdominant emphasis: counterweight to cadential dominant; agent of relaxation
5. The so-called "plagal cadence" (I-IV-I) is almost always a codetta, not a genuine cadence
6. Codetta with deceptive resolution: first codetta resolves deceptively to VI, repeat resolves to I
7. Material may resemble or be identical to the cadential idea from the theme
8. Fragmentation: closing sections typically show decreasing codetta size
9. Function: after-the-end; must not project a sense of formal beginning

# Construction / Recognition
1. Locate the PAC that closes the theme
2. Identify material following the cadence
3. Check for tonic prolongation, tonic pedal, subdominant emphasis
4. Confirm melody circles around or descends to tonic (no reopening of melodic space)
5. Note the size and number of codettas
6. Look for fragmentation pattern (progressively shorter codettas)
7. Distinguish from a genuine cadence: codettas are postcadential, not cadential

# Context & Application
Codettas appear not just at the end of simple themes but throughout classical forms wherever a PAC requires postcadential emphasis or resolution. Many musicians fail to distinguish between cadences and codettas, often saying "the piece finishes with a cadence repeated several times" when those final measures are actually codettas. The final measures of a work rarely bring cadences -- they bring codettas.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in A, K. 331, iii, mm. 122-127: codettas built exclusively on root-position tonic harmony
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Hob. XVI:52, i, mm. 111-116: energetic closing section with varied codettas, all on tonic prolongations
- Mozart, String Quartet in E-flat, K. 428, ii, mm. 92-96: codetta with subdominant emphasis; chromatic line (8-sharp7-6-natural7-8) over tonic pedal
- Beethoven, Violin Sonata in A, Op. 30/1, i, mm. 20-27: codetta built over expanded cadential progression (different from the theme's cadential progression)

# Common Errors
- Labeling codettas as "additional cadences" -- they are postcadential, not cadential
- Identifying a "plagal cadence" (I-IV-I) when the passage is really a codetta with subdominant emphasis
- Treating codettas as part of the theme's formal structure rather than as postcadential framing

# Common Confusions
- Codettas are NOT cadences, even when supported by cadential progressions -- they are after-the-end material
- The "plagal cadence" (often as an "Amen" figure) is almost always a codetta with subdominant emphasis
- If you are tempted to speak of "additional cadences" following the final cadence, consider whether these are better understood as codettas
- A codetta with material identical to the cadential idea is still postcadential in function
