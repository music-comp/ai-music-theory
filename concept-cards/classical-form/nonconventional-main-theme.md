# Quick Definition
A main theme whose formal organization cannot be easily classified as a sentence, period, or hybrid, yet still fulfills the main theme's function of establishing the home key and defining tight-knit organization.

# Core Definition
A significant minority of main themes have a nonconventional formal organization; they cannot be easily accommodated by standard categories (sentence, period, hybrids). A nonconventional theme should, in principle, be distinguished from a conventional one that "deviates from the norm," though this distinction is not always facile. Despite thematic nonconventionality (a criterion of loose organization), a nonconventional main theme is usually more tightly knit than subsequent thematic units. Many are eight measures in length. Types include: (1) eight-measure themes with asymmetrical grouping but definite intrathematic functions; (2) tonic-pedal fanfare themes that obscure conventional phrase functions; (3) themes omitting initiating function, starting "in the middle" or "toward the end"; and (4) loosely organized themes resembling subordinate themes in technique but remaining tighter in practice.

# Prerequisites
- **Main-theme function in sonata**: The function these themes still serve
- **Sentence, period**: The conventional types they cannot be classified as

# Key Properties
1. Cannot be easily classified as sentence, period, or hybrid
2. Usually more tightly knit than subsequent units despite nonconventionality
3. Many retain eight-measure length
4. Internal units usually express definite intrathematic functions
5. Four common types: asymmetrical eight-measure, fanfare, omitted initiation, loosely organized
6. Distinguished from conventional themes that "deviate from the norm" (though the line is blurry)
7. Especially characteristic of Haydn

# Construction / Recognition
- Attempt to classify as sentence, period, or hybrid
- If classification fails, identify the type of nonconventionality
- Note whether the theme is eight measures (retaining tight-knit length despite irregular grouping)
- Identify intrathematic functions (initiation, continuation, cadential) despite unconventional boundaries
- Compare with the subordinate theme to confirm relative tight-knit organization

# Context & Application
Nonconventional main themes are found across all three composers but are especially characteristic of Haydn. They demonstrate that formal functionality does not require conventional phrase structures. The key criterion is comparison: a nonconventional main theme is still more tightly knit than its movement's subordinate theme.

# Examples
- Haydn, String Quartet in G Minor ("Rider"), Op. 74/3, iv (ex. 13.1): 2+3+3 grouping; more sentential than periodic but not a genuine sentence
- Mozart, Symphony No. 38 ("Prague"), K. 504, i (ex. 13.2): one-measure introduction leads directly to continuation, bypassing initiation; IAC at m. 43; codetta with fanfare motives compensates for minimal root-position tonic
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E Minor, Hob. XVI:34, i (ex. 13.3): 3+3+2 asymmetry; measures 1-3 clearly initiating but neither expanded basic idea nor compressed presentation; related to sentence but insufficiently expressed
- Haydn, Symphony No. 97 in C, i (ex. 13.4): tonic-pedal fanfare; unison/octave gestures; general sentential quality but presentation and continuation cannot be distinguished
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in A, Op. 2/2, i (ex. 13.6): loosely organized with repetitions, expansions, extensions; yet decidedly tighter than its subordinate theme (ex. 8.12)

# Common Errors
- Treating nonconventional themes as "defective" or formally invalid
- Forcing nonconventional themes into conventional categories

# Common Confusions
- **Not defective**: A nonconventional theme fulfills main-theme functions through different means
- **Eight measures does not guarantee conventionality**: The internal grouping may be entirely irregular
- **Relative looseness**: A loosely organized main theme is still tighter than its movement's subordinate theme
- **Deviation vs. nonconventionality**: Some examples may be "extreme deviants" of a conventional type rather than truly nonconventional
