# Quick Definition
A main theme whose formal organization cannot be easily classified as a sentence, period, or hybrid, yet still fulfills the main theme's function of establishing the home key and defining tight-knit organization.

# Formal Definition
A significant minority of main themes have a nonconventional formal organization — they cannot be easily accommodated by standard categories (sentence, period, hybrids). A nonconventional theme should, in principle, be distinguished from a conventional one that "deviates from the norm," though this distinction is not always clear. Despite thematic nonconventionality (a criterion of loose organization), a nonconventional main theme is usually more tightly knit than subsequent units. Many are eight measures long. Types include: eight-measure asymmetrical themes, tonic-pedal fanfare themes, themes omitting initiating function, and loosely organized themes.

# Construction/Recognition
- Cannot be classified as standard sentence, period, or hybrid
- Often retains eight-measure length despite formal irregularities
- Usually more tightly knit than the subordinate theme
- Common types:
  - **Asymmetrical eight-measure themes**: irregular internal grouping (e.g., 2+3+3)
  - **Tonic-pedal fanfare themes**: long tonic pedal with heraldic gestures obscuring conventional phrase functions
  - **Omitted initiating function**: theme begins with continuation or cadential function
  - **Loosely organized**: resembles a subordinate theme in technique but remains tighter in practice
- Internal units usually express definite intrathematic functions despite the nonconventional design

# Musical Context
Nonconventional main themes are found across all three composers but are especially characteristic of Haydn, whose formal inventiveness frequently produces unique solutions.

# Examples
- Haydn, String Quartet in G Minor ("Rider"), Op. 74/3, iv (ex. 13.1): 2+3+3 grouping structure; more sentential than periodic but not a genuine sentence
- Mozart, Symphony No. 38 ("Prague"), K. 504, i (ex. 13.2): moves from introduction directly to continuation, bypassing conventional initiation
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E Minor, Hob. XVI:34, i (ex. 13.3): 3+3+2 asymmetry with ambiguous formal functions
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in A, Op. 2/2, i (ex. 13.6): loosely organized with repetitions, expansions, and extensions

# Common Confusions
- A nonconventional theme is not a "defective" theme — it fulfills the main theme's formal functions through different means
- Eight measures does not guarantee a conventional form; the internal grouping may be entirely irregular
- A loosely organized main theme is still tighter than its movement's subordinate theme
