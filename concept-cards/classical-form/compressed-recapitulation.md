# Quick Definition
A recapitulation in a small ternary that is shorter than the exposition, typically reducing an eight-measure periodic exposition to a single four-measure consequent phrase by eliminating the internal HC and second basic idea statement.

# Formal Definition
The shorter length is achieved by eliminating functionally redundant material. When the exposition is an 8-measure period, the compressed A' eliminates both the interior HC (dispensable because the dominant was emphasized at the end of B) and the second basic idea statement (established in memory). The result is a single 4-measure phrase, sometimes identical to the original consequent, sometimes combining elements from different parts of the exposition, or even introducing a new contrasting idea.

# Construction/Recognition
1. A' shorter than A (most commonly 4 vs. 8 measures)
2. Internal HC eliminated (dominant already emphasized by B)
3. Second basic idea statement omitted
4. May be identical to original consequent, or combine elements differently
5. May introduce a new contrasting idea

# Musical Context
Extremely common in small ternaries with periodic expositions. Prevents redundancy while fulfilling essential requirements: return of basic idea and PAC in home key.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in G Minor, Op. 49/1, ii, m. 13 (consequent transposed up an octave)
- Mozart, Eine kleine Nachtmusik, K. 525, ii, mm. 13-16 (single consequent combining elements)
- Mozart, String Quartet in E-flat, K. 428/421b, iv, mm. 27-34 (combines elements from two exposition themes)

# Common Confusions
- Not simply a "cut" -- the remaining material may be reorganized with different functional significance
- Not all short A' sections are compressed -- some expositions are themselves short
