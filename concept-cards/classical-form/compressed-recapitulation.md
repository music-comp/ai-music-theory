# Quick Definition
A recapitulation in a small ternary that is shorter than the exposition, typically reducing an eight-measure periodic exposition to a single four-measure consequent phrase by eliminating the internal HC and second basic idea statement.

# Core Definition
The shorter length is achieved by eliminating functionally redundant material. When the exposition is an 8-measure period, the compressed A' eliminates both the interior HC (dispensable because the dominant was emphasized at the end of B) and the second basic idea statement (established in memory). The result is a single 4-measure consequent phrase. This phrase may be identical to the original consequent, combine elements from different parts of the exposition, or introduce a new contrasting idea. The compression may also arise from unique features of the individual theme.

# Prerequisites
- **Recapitulation in small ternary**: The section being compressed

# Key Properties
1. A' shorter than A (most commonly 4 vs. 8 measures)
2. Internal HC eliminated (dominant already emphasized by B section)
3. Second basic idea statement omitted (idea already established in memory)
4. May be identical to original consequent, or combine elements differently
5. May introduce a new contrasting idea
6. The single resulting phrase typically functions as a consequent
7. Contributes to looser organization relative to exposition (in most cases)

# Construction / Recognition
1. Compare A and A' measure by measure
2. Identify what material has been eliminated and why
3. Check whether the resulting phrase is identical to the original consequent or a new combination
4. Note that the compression is justified by the prior B section's dominant emphasis

# Context & Application
Extremely common in small ternaries with periodic expositions. Prevents redundancy while fulfilling the two essential requirements of the recapitulation: return of basic idea and PAC in home key. The compression logic is specific: the B section already provided dominant emphasis, so the A' need not repeat it with an internal HC; the basic idea is already familiar, so it need not be restated.

# Examples
- Mozart, Eine kleine Nachtmusik, K. 525, ii, mm. 13-16: single consequent combining elements from both exposition phrases
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in D, Op. 28, ii, mm. 17-22: basic idea extended with sequence; six measures overall (still compressed from original 8)
- Mozart, String Quartet in E-flat, K. 428, iv, mm. 27-34: combines compound basic idea from first exposition theme with consequent from second

# Common Errors
- Treating the compressed recapitulation as formally deficient rather than functionally efficient
- Failing to recognize that the remaining material may be reorganized with different functional significance
- Assuming all short A' sections are compressed -- some expositions are themselves short

# Common Confusions
- Not simply a "cut" -- the remaining material may be reorganized with different functional significance
- Not all short A' sections are compressed -- some expositions are themselves short
- The compression is motivated by the specific formal context (B section's dominant emphasis makes HC redundant)
