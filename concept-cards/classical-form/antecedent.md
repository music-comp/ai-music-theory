# Quick Definition
The opening four-measure phrase of a period, consisting of a basic idea followed by a contrasting idea and ending with a weak cadence (HC or IAC), which demands completion by a consequent.

# Core Definition
The antecedent begins with a two-measure basic idea followed by a two-measure contrasting idea, leading to a weak cadence (typically HC or IAC). The weak cadential closure creates the structural need for a consequent that provides stronger closure. Distinguished from a presentation phrase by two features: (1) contrasting rather than repeated material follows the basic idea, and (2) the phrase ends with a cadence (presentations do not).

# Prerequisites
- **Basic idea** -- opens the antecedent
- **Contrasting idea** -- follows the basic idea; must be "not-a-repetition"

# Key Properties
1. Four measures: basic idea (mm. 1-2) + contrasting idea (mm. 3-4)
2. Ends with weak cadence: HC (most common) or IAC
3. Does not end with PAC (which would provide full closure and eliminate the need for a consequent)
4. Contrasting idea often features continuation-like characteristics (fragmentation, harmonic acceleration)
5. The contrasting idea must be sufficiently distinct from the basic idea to prevent the perception of a presentation

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify a two-measure basic idea
2. Confirm the following two measures bring a contrasting idea (not a repetition)
3. Verify a weak cadence closes the phrase
4. The presence of a cadence distinguishes it from a presentation

# Context & Application
The antecedent opens period-form themes common as main themes in sonata movements and slow movements. Detailed treatment in Chapter 4.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 1, p. 12, ex. 1.3): Mozart, K. 525, ii, mm. 1-4: basic idea (mm. 1-2) + contrasting idea with "obvious fragmentation and a degree of harmonic acceleration" (mm. 3-4), ending with HC.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Basic idea and contrasting idea
## Enables
- Period form (antecedent + consequent)
- Cadential differentiation (weak cadence demanding stronger closure)
## Related
- Half cadence (the most common closing cadence)
## Contrasts With
- Presentation: presentation repeats the basic idea without cadence; antecedent follows with contrasting idea and closes with cadence
- Consequent: antecedent has weak cadence; consequent has stronger cadence

# Common Errors
- Confusing an antecedent with a presentation when the contrasting idea is subtle

# Common Confusions
- A PAC at the end would provide complete closure and eliminate the need for a consequent
- The contrasting idea is "not-a-repetition" -- it need not be dramatically different, "just distinct enough to prevent the perception of presentation function" (Ch. 1, p. 13)
