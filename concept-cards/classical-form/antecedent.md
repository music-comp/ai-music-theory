# Quick Definition
The opening four-measure phrase of a period, consisting of a basic idea followed by a contrasting idea and ending with a weak cadence, which demands completion by a consequent.

# Formal Definition
The antecedent begins with a two-measure basic idea followed by a two-measure contrasting idea, leading to a weak cadence (typically HC or IAC). The weak cadential closure creates the structural need for a consequent that provides stronger closure. Distinguished from a presentation phrase by: (1) contrasting rather than repeated material, and (2) cadential ending.

# Construction/Recognition
- Four measures: basic idea (mm. 1-2) + contrasting idea (mm. 3-4)
- Ends with weak cadence: HC (most common) or IAC
- Does not end with PAC
- Contrasting idea often features continuation-like characteristics (fragmentation, harmonic acceleration)

# Musical Context
The antecedent opens period-form themes common as main themes in sonata movements and slow movements. Detailed treatment in Chapter 4.

# Examples
- Mozart, K. 525, ii, mm. 1-4: basic idea (mm. 1-2) + contrasting idea with fragmentation (mm. 3-4), ending HC

# Common Confusions
- A PAC at the end would provide complete closure and eliminate the need for a consequent.
- The contrasting idea is "not-a-repetition"--it need not be dramatically different, just distinct enough to prevent the perception of presentation function.
