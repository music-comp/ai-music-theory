# Quick Definition
A thematic unit in a development section that occupies the structural position of the core but is organized along different formal lines, such as a transition-like or subordinate-themelike unit.

# Formal Definition
In place of a core, a variety of phrase-structural options may be employed: (1) a pseudo-core; (2) a transition-like unit beginning in one key and modulating to another with a half cadence; or (3) a subordinate-themelike unit aiming toward authentic cadential closure in a development key. Haydn generally constructs developments without a core.

# Construction/Recognition
- **Transition-like**: begins with initiating function, modulates, closes with HC or dominant arrival
- **Subordinate-themelike**: harmonic goal is an authentic cadence in a development key; features loosening devices
- **Pseudo-core**: dramatic character of a core but without model-sequence technique
- Some developments model the organization of an exposition with pre-core as main theme, transition-like unit, and subordinate-themelike unit

# Musical Context
Core substitutes are especially characteristic of Haydn's developments.

# Examples
- Haydn, Symphony No. 102, ii (ex. 10.17): transition-like unit with presentation and continuation
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Hob. XVI:49, i (ex. 10.18): four-part development modeling exposition organization

# Common Confusions
- The absence of a core does not mean the development lacks organization
- A transition-like core substitute differs from a transition-like pre-core in structural position
- A subordinate-themelike unit differs from a true subordinate theme in that its PAC is in a development key
