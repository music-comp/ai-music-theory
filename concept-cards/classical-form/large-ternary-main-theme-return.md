# Quick Definition
The third part of a large ternary form, restating the main theme essentially as it was in the first part, often with ornamental embellishment but without tonal adjustment.

# Formal Definition
Because the main theme of a large ternary essentially resides in the home key, the return does not require any tonal adjustment. In the majority of cases, the third part is structured the same as the first part, though often with considerable ornamental changes. It is more appropriate to speak of a "return" rather than a "recapitulation." The returning theme may be expanded through interpolations, extensions, or added material. A coda may follow, often of great scope (especially in Beethoven), frequently referring to material from the interior theme.

# Construction/Recognition
- The main theme returns after the interior theme, in the same key and structure
- Ornamental embellishment (especially melodic) is common
- No tonal adjustment is needed
- The structure may be expanded
- A coda may follow, often referring to interior-theme material

# Musical Context
The return provides formal closure after the contrasting interior theme. The ornamental embellishment connects this to variation technique.

# Examples
- Beethoven's large ternary slow movements frequently conclude with extensive codas referencing the interior theme

# Common Confusions
- The third part should be understood as a "return" rather than a true "recapitulation," since no tonal conflict needs to be resolved.
