# Quick Definition
Harmonies whose primary purpose is to lead to the dominant, including two main types: those built above the fourth scale-degree (II6, IV, bII6) and those derived from V/V (secondary dominants and augmented sixths).

# Core Definition
"The third fundamental function comprises a variety of harmonies whose primary purpose is to lead to the dominant" (Ch. 2, p. 23). The term "pre-dominant" is preferred over "subdominant" because the function "includes a number of harmonic formations not directly related to the harmony built on the fourth degree of the scale" (Ch. 2, p. 23). Pre-dominants relate to two main types: "(1) those built above the fourth degree of the scale and (2) those derived from the dominant of the dominant (V/V)" (Ch. 2, p. 23).

# Key Properties
1. Fourth-degree type: II6 (most characteristic), IV, bII6 ("Neapolitan"/"phrygian," especially in minor)
2. Secondary dominant type: V/V, V6/V, VII7/V (featuring chromatically raised scale-degree 4 as leading-tone of V, usually in bass)
3. Augmented sixth chords: Italian, German, French (built over b6 of minor; occasionally over raised scale-degree 4)
4. II6 is the MORE CHARACTERISTIC pre-dominant in classical style, not IV (contrary to many textbooks)
5. II6 and IV can be enriched by added sevenths and modal mixture
6. Pre-dominant is the most variable element of the cadential formula
7. Can be omitted in incomplete cadential progressions
8. Consecutive diminished sevenths can prolong pre-dominant function

# Construction / Recognition
- II6 (most typical), IV, bII6 in the bass = fourth-degree type
- Raised scale-degree 4 in bass progressing to scale-degree 5 = secondary dominant type
- Augmented sixth on b6 = augmented sixth type
- Pre-dominant always progresses to dominant

# Context & Application
Pre-dominant function occupies the middle position in the cadential progression (I - PD - V - I). It is the most flexible element, with many possible harmonic choices. The choice of pre-dominant affects the character and intensity of the cadential drive.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 2, ex. 2.5a): Basic cadential progression with II6: I-II6-V-I.
**Example 2** (Ch. 2, ex. 2.7a-b): Neapolitan sixth: I-bII6-V-I (especially in minor).
**Example 3** (Ch. 2, ex. 2.7c-h): Chromatic pre-dominants: VII7/V, with raised scale-degree 4 in bass. Two consecutive diminished sevenths can prolong pre-dominant (first on scale-degree 4, second on raised scale-degree 4).
**Example 4** (Ch. 2, p. 24): Augmented sixths (Italian, German, French): built over b6 of minor, revealing kinship to V/V when built over raised scale-degree 4.
