# Quick Definition
Harmonies whose primary purpose is to lead to the dominant, including chords built above the fourth scale-degree (II6, IV, bII6) and secondary dominants of V (V/V, VII7/V, augmented sixth chords).

# Formal Definition
Pre-dominant function comprises a large number of harmonies whose primary purpose is to lead to the dominant. The term "pre-dominant" is preferred over "subdominant" because this function includes harmonies not directly related to the IV chord. Pre-dominants generally relate to one of two main types: (1) those built above the fourth degree of the scale (II6, IV, bII6), and (2) those derived from the dominant of the dominant (V/V, V6/V, VII7/V). The three varieties of augmented sixth chords (Italian, German, French) are an important subclass, usually built over the sixth degree of the natural minor scale.

# Construction/Recognition
- Fourth-degree type: II6 (most characteristic in classical style), IV, bII6 ("Neapolitan" or "phrygian" sixth, especially in minor)
- Secondary dominant type: V/V, V6/V, VII7/V (featuring chromatically raised ^4 as leading-tone of V, usually in bass)
- Augmented sixth chords: Italian, German, French (built over b^6 of minor scale; occasionally over raised ^4)
- II6 and IV can be enriched by added sevenths or modal mixture
- II6 is the more characteristic pre-dominant in classical style, not IV as many textbooks suggest

# Musical Context
Pre-dominant function occupies the middle position in the cadential progression (I - pre-dominant - V - I). It is the most variable element of the cadential formula, with many possible harmonic choices. Pre-dominant harmony can be omitted in incomplete cadential progressions. The choice of pre-dominant affects the character and intensity of the cadential drive.

# Examples
- Basic: I-II6-V-I (the most typical cadential progression in the classical style)
- Neapolitan: I-bII6-V-I (especially in minor-mode contexts)
- Secondary dominant: I-VII7/V-V-I (raised ^4 in bass, chromatic approach to dominant)
- Augmented sixth: I-It6/Ger6/Fr6-V-I (built over b^6, chromatic approach)
- Consecutive diminished sevenths prolonging pre-dominant: the first on ^4, the second on raised ^4

# Common Confusions
- II6 is the more characteristic pre-dominant in classical style than IV, contrary to what many harmony textbooks suggest.
- The augmented sixth chords, built over b^6, are pre-dominant harmonies (leading to V), revealing their kinship to secondary dominants of V when built over raised ^4.
- Pre-dominant function is the most flexible element of the cadential formula and offers the greatest variety of harmonic choices.
