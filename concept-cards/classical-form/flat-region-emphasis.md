# Quick Definition
The tendency in recapitulations to tonicize regions on the "flat" side of the tonal spectrum — subdominant, bVI, bIII, and the Neapolitan — creating harmonic contrast within the home key.

# Formal Definition
The harmonic plan of the main theme and especially the transition is sometimes altered in the recapitulation to emphasize the "flat" side of the tonal spectrum — regions that introduce chromatically lowered scale-degrees, such as the subdominant (IV), the lowered mediant (bIII), the lowered submediant (bVI), and the Neapolitan (bII). This shift in the flat direction is particularly appropriate in the transition because the subsequent return to diatonic progressions of the home key at the subordinate theme shifts the music back to the "sharp" side, corresponding to the exposition's move from home key to subordinate key.

# Construction/Recognition
- Most commonly appears in the transition of the recapitulation
- Also found in the main theme (especially if the transition is left relatively unaltered)
- Flat-side regions include: IV (subdominant), bVI (lowered submediant), bIII (lowered mediant), bII (Neapolitan)
- The return to diatonic home-key progressions at the subordinate theme creates a "sharp-side" shift that makes the music sound fresh and revitalized
- Particularly important when both main and subordinate themes emphasize diatonic home-key harmonies

# Musical Context
Since the recapitulation presents the subordinate theme in the home key (rather than the subordinate key), the transition needs new means of creating harmonic contrast. Flat-region emphasis provides this contrast, functioning analogously to the exposition's modulation to the subordinate key.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 7 in A, Op. 92, i (ex. 11.3): main theme transposed to subdominant, then shifts to minor (further flat-side emphasis)
- Mozart, Violin Sonata in C, K. 403 (ex. 11.5): transition opens with home-key parallel minor, moves to Ab major (bVI)
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C Minor, K. 457, i (ex. 11.6): transition moves quickly to bIII region, uses Neapolitan as expanded pre-dominant

# Common Confusions
- Flat-region emphasis is not random chromaticism — it serves a specific compensatory function, replacing the exposition's sharp-side modulation
- The return from flat regions to home-key diatonicism mirrors the exposition's move from home key to subordinate key
- Flat-region emphasis differs from the minor-mode emphasis of the development; it occurs within the recapitulation's fundamentally stable tonal context
