# Quick Definition
A phrase-structural alteration that shortens a phrase below its normative length, creating a more compact formal expression, often by eliminating material or compacting the grouping structure.

# Core Definition
Compression (or phrase-structural compression) shortens a phrase below its normative length. In a sentence, the presentation or continuation may be compressed from four measures to fewer. In a period, both antecedent and consequent may be compressed symmetrically (e.g., 3+3 instead of 4+4), or only one phrase may be shortened to create asymmetry. Compression creates a more compact, efficient formal expression. In compound sentences, the continuation is frequently compressed from eight measures to four, restoring the phrase to the dimensions of a simple sentence's continuation.

# Prerequisites
- Sentence or Period (the normative structures being compressed)

# Key Properties
- Shortens phrases below normative length (typically below 4 measures)
- Can affect any phrase component
- Often creates symmetrical compression (both phrases shortened equally)
- In compound sentences, compressed continuation is very common
- The compressed phrase still expresses its full functional content in fewer measures
- May create interesting sub-grouping structures (e.g., 2+1 / 1+2)

# Construction / Recognition
1. Compare the phrase length to its normative size
2. If shorter than expected, identify what has been compressed or eliminated
3. Verify that the formal function is still fully expressed despite the shorter span
4. In periods, check whether the compression is symmetrical or asymmetrical

# Context & Application
Compression appears in sentences, periods, and compound themes. It is especially characteristic of the compound sentence, where the continuation is often compressed from eight measures to four. Compression also appears in recapitulations of small ternaries, where functionally redundant material may be eliminated.

# Examples
- Beethoven, String Quartet in G, Op. 18/2, ii, mm. 1-6: symmetrical 3+3 period with compressed antecedent (2+1) and consequent (1+2)
- Haydn, String Quartet in G, Op. 77/1, i, mm. 1-14: compound sentence with compressed continuation (fragmentation jumps directly from 4-measure to 1-measure units)

## Related

## Contrasts With
- Extension (lengthening after structural end)
- Expansion (internal lengthening)

# Common Errors
- Confusing compression with an incomplete phrase; compression still expresses full functional content
- Failing to identify compression when the phrase sounds "complete" despite being shorter than normative

# Common Confusions
- **Compression vs. elision**: compression shortens a phrase internally; elision overlaps the end of one phrase with the beginning of the next
- **Compressed continuation vs. absent continuation**: a compressed continuation still expresses its function (fragmentation, acceleration) in fewer measures; an absent continuation simply skips the function
