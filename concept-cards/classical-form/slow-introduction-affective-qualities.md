# Quick Definition
The characteristic combination of two seemingly incompatible expressive worlds in a slow introduction: the stately and solemn alongside the uncertain and anticipatory.

# Formal Definition
A notable characteristic of slow introductions in the classical style is the special way in which they unite the expression of two seemingly incompatible affective worlds. The realm of the stately, heraldic, and solemn is combined with the realm of the anticipatory, uncertain, and unstable. Solemnity is expressed by slow tempo, fanfare gestures, tutti textures, dotted rhythms, and an initial forte dynamic. Anticipation is created by marked discontinuities in melody, rhythm, texture, and dynamics; harmonic instabilities; minor modality; and chromaticism (at times extreme). Some introductions play down the stately character but almost always contain something uncertain and hesitant.

# Construction/Recognition
- **Solemn qualities**: slow tempo, fanfare gestures, tutti orchestration, dotted rhythms, forte dynamics, heraldic/declamatory character
- **Anticipatory qualities**: rests and fermatas creating discontinuity, sudden dynamic changes, unstable harmonies, chromaticism, minor modality (in major-key works), uncertain/hesitant gestures
- Both qualities coexist, often alternating within phrases
- The anticipatory quality builds expectation for the exposition's livelier character

# Musical Context
This dual affective quality distinguishes classical slow introductions from the more uniformly grave introductions of the baroque era. The combination of gravity and instability creates a unique expressive tension that heightens the impact of the exposition's beginning.

# Examples
- Haydn, Symphony No. 104 ("London"), i (ex. 13.11): fortissimo fanfare with fermatas (solemn); missing harmonic third and dangling leading tones create uncertainty; continued use of fanfare rhythms maintains heraldic impression while the content becomes hesitant
- Beethoven, Violin Sonata in A Minor ("Kreutzer"), Op. 47, i (ex. 13.12): stately sarabande-like opening (solemn); unaccompanied violin creates textural gap (uncertain); shift to minor and chromaticism (anticipatory)

# Common Confusions
- The solemn and anticipatory qualities are not contradictory — they work together to create the introduction's unique character
- Not all introductions are equally solemn; some are primarily anticipatory/uncertain
- The slow tempo itself is not sufficient to identify a slow introduction — the affective combination is key
