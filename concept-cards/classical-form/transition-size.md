# Quick Definition
Transitions are frequently quite short (often less than eight measures, sometimes only four), rather than occupying the one-third of the exposition that their status as a "principal formal function" might suggest -- many passages traditionally labeled as transition belong, from a form-functional perspective, to the subordinate-theme group.

# Core Definition
Knowing that the transition constitutes one of three principal formal functions of the exposition, one may believe that the transition should occupy about one-third of the exposition as a whole. Very rarely is this the case. Rather, the transition is frequently quite short, often taking up less than eight measures and even sometimes only four. Many passages that more traditional approaches to sonata form have identified as belonging to the transition are better understood, from the form-functional perspective advocated here, as belonging to the opening part of the subordinate-theme group.

# Prerequisites
- **Transition**: The formal function in question

# Key Properties
1. Transitions are typically SHORT (often < 8 mm., sometimes only 4)
2. The transition does NOT typically occupy one-third of the exposition
3. Many traditionally labeled "transition" passages are actually subordinate-theme openings
4. The form-functional approach often reassigns material from transition to subordinate theme
5. The transition's structural end (HC or dominant arrival) determines its actual extent

# Construction / Recognition
- Do not assume the transition is proportional to the other formal functions
- Use the HC or dominant arrival to identify the transition's structural end
- Material following the HC/dominant arrival that is in the subordinate key may belong to the subordinate theme
- Short transitions (4-8 mm.) are the norm, not the exception

# Context & Application
This observation has significant analytical consequences. Traditional analyses often assign too much material to the transition, especially when sequential passages or modulatory material occur in the subordinate key. From Caplin's form-functional perspective, the transition's function is limited to destabilization and dominant arrival; once the dominant is achieved, subsequent material (even if it continues to modulate or seems transitional in character) may belong to the subordinate-theme group.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in C, H. 21, ii, mm. 7-12 (Ch. 11, Ex. 11.15): transition of only 6 measures
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 31/3, ii, mm. 28-35 (Ch. 11, Ex. 11.17): transition consisting of a single harmony (V7/vi) -- only a few measures
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C minor, K. 457, i, mm. 17-22 (Ch. 11, Ex. 11.20): transition of approximately 6 measures with no concluding function

# Common Errors
- Assuming the transition should be approximately one-third of the exposition
- Assigning material to the transition that belongs to the subordinate-theme group
- Treating sequential passages in the subordinate key as "still the transition"

# Common Confusions
- **Not proportional**: The three principal functions (MT, TR, ST) do NOT occupy equal portions of the exposition
- **Traditional vs. form-functional**: Traditional analyses often make the transition much larger than the form-functional approach warrants
- **Short is normal**: Massive, lengthy transitions are exceptions, not the norm
