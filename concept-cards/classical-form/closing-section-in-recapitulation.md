# Quick Definition
The closing section of the subordinate theme in the recapitulation, which usually reappears similar to the exposition but may be altered or extended depending on whether a coda follows.

# Formal Definition
The closing section of the subordinate theme (group) usually reappears in the recapitulation in much the same way as it did in the exposition. If the recapitulation is followed by a coda, the final codettas may be altered or eliminated. In the absence of a genuine coda, the closing section may be extended to impart a more decisive sense of conclusion to the movement as a whole. If the exposition closes with a retransition leading back to the home key, the recapitulation may bring a similar "retransition" leading to the subdominant region for the beginning of the coda.

# Construction/Recognition
- Generally matches the exposition's closing section
- May be extended when no coda follows (providing final closure)
- May be shortened or altered when a coda follows (avoiding redundancy)
- A "retransition" at the end may lead to IV for the coda (rather than back to I)
- This "retransition" functions more as a transition to the coda than a true retransition

# Musical Context
The closing section's treatment depends on the larger formal context: whether the movement has a coda, and if so, how the coda begins. The passage connecting the recapitulation to the coda often uses material from the exposition's retransition.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in F Minor, Op. 2/1, i (ex. 11.15): closing section extended to form a complete sentence with an eight-measure stretched continuation
- Mozart, String Quintet in G Minor, K. 516, i (ex. 12.1): "retransition" at end of recapitulation leads to IV for the coda
- Mozart, Symphony No. 36 ("Linz"), K. 425, i (ex. 12.2): "retransition" leads to II for the coda

# Common Confusions
- The "retransition" at the end of the recapitulation does not actually return to the home key (as a true retransition would) — it modulates to another region for the coda
- Extended closing sections in movements without codas serve the same function that a coda would otherwise provide
