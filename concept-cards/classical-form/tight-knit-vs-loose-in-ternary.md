# Quick Definition
The systematic comparison of formal organization across the three sections of a small ternary, using seven criteria to evaluate relative degrees of tight-knit and loose expression.

# Formal Definition
Caplin identifies seven criteria: (1) Tonality -- home key tightest, modulation loosest; (2) Cadence -- PAC tightest, no cadence loosest; (3) Harmony -- cadential/tonic prolongational tightest, sequential loosest; (4) Grouping structure -- symmetrical (powers of 2) tightest; (5) Functional efficiency -- efficient tighter, redundant looser; (6) Motivic uniformity -- uniform tighter, diverse looser; (7) Formal conventionality -- period tightest, sentence least tight, hybrids between.

# Construction/Recognition
1. A vs. B: A is relatively tight knit; B is distinctly looser (in virtually all small ternaries)
2. A vs. A': If identical, same tightness; if altered, A' usually looser (exception: tonally adjusted A' staying in home key may be tighter than modulatory A)
3. B vs. A': If A' closely models A, it retains tightness relative to B
4. Criteria can conflict, making comparison difficult

# Musical Context
These criteria interact in complex ways. Sometimes all align; other times different criteria pull in opposite directions. Analyzing specific loosening techniques is often more productive than ranking overall looseness.

# Examples
- Mozart, Eine kleine Nachtmusik, K. 525, ii: A very tight knit (period); B distinctly loose (dominant prolongation, no cadence); A' intermediate
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, ii: A tightest; B loose; A' markedly loose (comparing B and A' is problematic)

# Common Confusions
- "Loose" is NOT "weak" -- it is a descriptive category
- The period is the tightest conventional type, NOT the sentence
- A tonally adjusted A' (staying in home key) may be tighter than the modulatory A it models
