# Quick Definition
The rondo form is not fully confirmed until the appearance of couplet 2; on first hearing, refrain 2 may suggest a repeated sonata exposition, a sonata without development, or the third part of a large ternary, depending on the structure of couplet 1.

# Core Definition
The first hearing of refrain 2 can elicit quite divergent formal interpretations depending on the structure of the preceding first couplet. If the first couplet is built as a subordinate-theme complex, the return of the main theme may appear to mark the repeat of a sonata exposition or the beginning of a recapitulation in a sonata without development. If the first couplet is an interior theme (in a slow movement), the return may suggest the third part of a large ternary. In a fast movement, an interior theme following the main theme would immediately suggest a rondo form because the large ternary is rarely used for fast movements. In both situations, the rondo form is not fully confirmed until the appearance of couplet 2 (Caplin, p. 678).

# Key Properties
1. Rondo form is not confirmed until couplet 2 appears
2. After a subordinate-theme complex, refrain 2 may suggest sonata exposition repeat
3. After an interior theme in a slow movement, refrain 2 may suggest large ternary
4. In fast movements, interior theme after main theme immediately suggests rondo
5. The ambiguity is part of the listener's experience on first hearing

# Construction / Recognition
1. At refrain 2, consider what alternative formal interpretations are possible
2. Wait for couplet 2 to confirm the rondo form
3. Consider the tempo: fast movements with interior themes immediately suggest rondo
4. Consider the couplet type: subordinate-theme complex creates more ambiguity

# Context & Application
This principle is important for understanding the temporal experience of form -- how the listener's understanding evolves during the course of the movement. It also explains why the appearance of couplet 2 is a pivotal moment in formal identification.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Identifying a movement as a rondo after only hearing refrain 2. **Correction**: Rondo form is not confirmed until couplet 2 reveals that the form is not a sonata or large ternary.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Whether the formal ambiguity is a flaw. **Clarification**: The ambiguity is an inherent feature of the listening experience and part of the form's design.
