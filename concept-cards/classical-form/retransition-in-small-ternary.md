# Quick Definition
A passage within the contrasting middle of a small ternary that modulates back to the home key after the B section has confirmed a subordinate key through authentic cadential closure.

# Core Definition
When the contrasting middle modulates to a subordinate key and confirms it with an authentic cadence, the composer may add a retransition to return to the home-key dominant for the recapitulation. This can range from a single chord (adding a seventh to the new "tonic" to convert it into the dominant of the home key) to a full phrase featuring model-sequence technique. A reduction in texture and anticipation of basic-idea motives may also occur.

# Construction / Recognition
1. **Minimal**: Single chord -- adding seventh converts tonic of subordinate key to dominant of home key
2. **Extended**: Up to four or more measures with model-sequence technique
3. Leads to home-key dominant (HC or dominant arrival)
4. Appears after authentic cadence in subordinate key within B section

# Context & Application
Retransitions are needed when the B section confirms a subordinate key through cadential closure. A modulating B section typically follows a nonmodulatory A section.

# Examples
- Haydn, Symphony No. 101 ("The Clock"), iv, mm. 17-20 (model-sequence leading to home-key HC)
- Mozart, String Quartet in D Minor, K. 421/417b, ii, mm. 13-14 (single chord: VI of home key)

# Common Confusions
- A retransition actively MODULATES back; a standing on the dominant simply PROLONGS an already-present dominant
- A single chord can serve as an entire retransition
