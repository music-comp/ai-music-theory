# Quick Definition
The second four-measure phrase of a period, which repeats the antecedent's basic idea but concludes with a stronger cadence (typically PAC), providing the closure that the antecedent left incomplete.

# Core Definition
"The consequent phrase of the period repeats the antecedent but concludes with a stronger cadence" (Ch. 1, p. 13). The basic idea returns in the opening two measures, then leads to a contrasting idea "which may or may not be based on that of the antecedent" (Ch. 1, p. 13). The consequent fulfills the structural promise implied by the antecedent's weak closure. In small ternary recapitulations, a single consequent phrase can fulfill recapitulation function by returning the basic idea and closing with PAC in the home key.

# Prerequisites
- **Antecedent** -- the first phrase whose weak cadence demands the consequent
- **Basic idea** -- which returns at the start of the consequent

# Key Properties
1. Four measures: returning basic idea (mm. 5-6) + contrasting idea (mm. 7-8)
2. Ends with a stronger cadence than the antecedent (typically PAC)
3. The contrasting idea may differ entirely from that of the antecedent
4. Can serve as the sole recapitulation in a small ternary form

# Construction / Recognition
1. Confirm the basic idea returns from the antecedent
2. Note the contrasting idea (may be same or different from antecedent's)
3. Verify stronger cadential closure than the antecedent

# Context & Application
The consequent completes the period form through cadential differentiation (weak then strong). In small ternary forms, a single consequent phrase can substitute for a full recapitulation of the exposition, as long as it begins with the basic idea and closes with PAC in the home key.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 1, p. 13, ex. 1.3): Mozart, K. 525, ii, mm. 5-8: basic idea returns, "distinctly different melody for the contrasting idea of the consequent," PAC.
**Example 2** (Ch. 1, p. 15, ex. 1.4): Beethoven, Op. 15, ii, mm. 15-18: single consequent phrase fulfills recapitulation function in small ternary. The four measures "consist of a restatement of the basic idea from measures 1-2 and a new contrasting idea which leads to a perfect authentic cadence."

# Common Errors
- Assuming the consequent must use the same contrasting idea as the antecedent

# Common Confusions
- The consequent is not merely a transposition of the antecedent; it must provide a stronger cadence
- In small ternary, a single consequent can replace a full recapitulation because "this phrase alone fulfills the two primary conditions of recapitulation function -- opening with the basic idea from the A section and closing with a perfect authentic cadence in the home key" (Ch. 1, p. 15)
