# Quick Definition
The second four-measure phrase of a period, which repeats the antecedent's basic idea but concludes with a stronger cadence, providing the closure that the antecedent left incomplete.

# Formal Definition
The consequent repeats the antecedent but concludes with a stronger cadence, usually a PAC. The basic idea returns in the opening two measures, then leads to a contrasting idea (which may or may not match the antecedent's). The consequent fulfills the structural promise implied by the antecedent's weak closure. In small ternary recapitulations, a single consequent phrase can fulfill recapitulation function.

# Construction/Recognition
- Four measures: returning basic idea (mm. 5-6) + contrasting idea (mm. 7-8)
- Ends with stronger cadence than antecedent (typically PAC)
- The contrasting idea may differ from that of the antecedent

# Musical Context
The consequent completes the period form through cadential differentiation (weak -> strong). Detailed treatment in Chapter 4.

# Examples
- Mozart, K. 525, ii, mm. 5-8: basic idea returns, distinctly different contrasting idea, PAC
- Beethoven, Op. 15, ii, mm. 15-18: single consequent fulfills recapitulation function in small ternary

# Common Confusions
- The consequent is not merely a transposition of the antecedent; it must provide a stronger cadence.
