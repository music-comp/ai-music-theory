# Quick Definition
A formal section's conclusion that lacks a genuine cadence, ending instead with a dominant arrival or premature dominant arrival, resulting in a significantly looser formal expression.

# Formal Definition
A noncadential ending occurs when a formal section concludes without a genuine cadence. The section may reach its harmonic goal (typically the dominant), but the manner of arrival prevents it from functioning as a true cadential goal. This can happen when the entire section is a prolongation, when the final dominant is inverted or dissonant, or when the dominant arrives prematurely. The lack of cadential closure is one of the strongest markers of loose formal organization.

# Construction/Recognition
1. No authentic cadential progression concludes the section
2. Final dominant may be inverted, contain a seventh, or arrive prematurely
3. Often marked by fermata to compensate for lack of cadential articulation
4. Results in significantly looser organization

# Musical Context
Characteristic of contrasting middle sections and transitions. In the tight-knit/loose continuum, noncadential closure represents the loosest possibility.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in G Minor, Op. 49/1, ii, m. 12 (V^4/3 with fermata)
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 7, Op. 92, i, mm. 81-88 (premature arrival, no cadence)
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 31/3, ii, mm. 10-19 (premature arrival)

# Common Confusions
- A noncadential ending does not mean the section lacks a harmonic goal -- the dominant IS the goal
- A fermata does not create a cadence; it merely emphasizes the ending function
