# Quick Definition
A return of the rondo refrain that is shortened to the extent that it either lacks cadential closure in the home key or concludes with a cadence in some other tonal region, often merging directly with the following couplet.

# Core Definition
An incomplete refrain is a return of the main theme that fails to achieve cadential closure in the home key. It may either lack any cadence at all or conclude with a cadence in some other tonal region. When incomplete, the refrain often merges with the following couplet without a clear dividing line. In a sonata-rondo, the incomplete refrain 2 may consist only of an antecedent unit, with the consequent departing from the antecedent's plan to become a transition-like pre-core leading into the development. Alternatively, the refrain may fail to achieve cadential closure and merge with transitional material (Caplin, pp. 677-680).

# Prerequisites
- **Refrain (rondo)** -- The thematic element left incomplete
- **Refrain return types** -- The broader category

# Key Properties
1. Lacks cadential closure in the home key
2. May cadence in a non-home tonal region
3. Often merges with the following couplet without clear boundary
4. In sonata-rondo, may consist only of the antecedent from the original refrain
5. Consequent may become a transition-like pre-core for the development

# Construction / Recognition
1. Check whether the returning refrain achieves a PAC in the home key
2. If no home-key PAC: the refrain is incomplete
3. Determine whether the refrain's cadence is reinterpreted (e.g., PAC in subordinate key becoming HC in home key)
4. Assess where the refrain ends and the next couplet begins

# Context & Application
Incomplete refrains are particularly important in sonata-rondo form, where refrain 2 precedes the development. An incomplete refrain 2 creates a smooth transition into the development section, avoiding the sense of a full formal close that would be followed by a fresh departure.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 677): Haydn, Piano Sonata in E-flat, H. 49, iii, mm. 58-64 -- incomplete refrain 2 consists only of the modulating A section of the original small ternary; the cadence in B-flat is reinterpreted as an HC in the home key when the minore couplet begins.
**Example 2** (p. 678): Beethoven, Symphony No. 3 ("Eroica"), Op. 55, ii, mm. 105-15 -- incomplete refrain 2 moves toward the subdominant and cadences there, with couplet 2 eliding with the end of the refrain.
**Example 3** (p. 681): Mozart, Piano Trio in B-flat, K. 502, iii, mm. 86-103 -- antecedent unit closes with reinterpreted HC; consequent departs to become transition-like pre-core.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Confusing incomplete with abridged refrains. **Correction**: Abridged refrains maintain home-key PAC; incomplete refrains do not.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Where the incomplete refrain ends and the couplet begins. **Clarification**: The boundary may be ambiguous; Caplin notes that "it is difficult to discern a clear dividing line."
