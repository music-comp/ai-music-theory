# Quick Definition
A cadential failure in which the cadential progression itself is disrupted by altering or eliminating the cadential dominant, causing the progression to lose its sense of direction before a new cadential attempt begins.

# Core Definition
An abandoned cadence occurs when the composer initiates what seems to be a cadential progression but then "abandons" the progression by either inverting the cadential dominant or allowing the progression to bypass that dominant entirely. Unlike the deceptive and evaded cadences (where the cadential dominant remains intact), the abandoned cadence alters or eliminates the dominant itself. The cadential progression "loses its sense of direction" and the music tends to wander off before eventually returning to cadential activity. Following an abandoned cadence, continuation function typically resumes before a new cadential progression leads to a PAC.

# Prerequisites
- **Cadential function**: The function being disrupted
- **Cadential progression**: The harmonic pattern being abandoned
- **Subordinate theme**: The formal context where this technique is appropriate

# Key Properties
1. A cadential progression is initiated (initial tonic, pre-dominant present)
2. The cadential dominant is compromised: inverted, converted from root position to inversion, or omitted
3. The dominant in inversion cannot function as a cadential harmony
4. Following the abandonment, continuation function typically resumes
5. Eventually a new cadential progression appears leading to PAC
6. Less dramatic than evaded cadence but promotes significant formal loosening
7. The music "wanders off" before returning on track toward another cadence

# Construction / Recognition
- A cadential progression begins normally (I or pre-dominant in place)
- The cadential dominant fails: inverted, weakened, or bypassed
- The progression loses cadential direction
- Continuation function resumes (unlike evaded cadence where cadential function restarts immediately)
- A new cadential progression must eventually be initiated from scratch

# Context & Application
The aesthetic impression of an abandoned cadence is entirely different from an evaded cadence. A partial cadential goal is neither achieved (as with DC) nor even immediately promised (as with evaded cadence). The abandoned cadence is less dramatic but nonetheless promotes significant formal loosening, since the cadential progression loses its sense of direction entirely. Cadential abandonment is particularly appropriate to the formal conventions of subordinate themes. Evaded cadences seldom appear in main themes; the abandoned cadence may appear there but is less common.

# Examples
- Haydn, String Quartet in B Minor, Op. 64/2, i, mm. 26-34: after evaded cadence at m. 26, expanded cadential progression begins; cadential function abandoned at m. 28 when pre-dominant VII7/V leads nowhere cadential; cadence restored at m. 32 with II6, achieving PAC at m. 34
- Beethoven, Overture to Coriolanus, Op. 62, mm. 92-102: expanded Neapolitan sixth implies cadential progression at m. 92; cadence abandoned at m. 96 when pre-dominant moves to diminished seventh in inversion; continuation resumes with sequence; cadential function restored at m. 99 with IV6; PAC at m. 102

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Cadential progression (the harmonic pattern being disrupted)
- Cadential function (the formal function being abandoned)

## Enables
- Formal loosening (the music wanders before returning to cadential activity)
- Continuation resumption (abandoned cadence typically leads to continuation function)

## Related
- Cadential extension (the broader category of delayed cadence)
- Evaded cadence (resolution present but grouping prevents closure)
- Deceptive cadence (substitute harmony provides partial closure)

## Contrasts With
- Evaded cadence: the cadential dominant remains intact in evaded cadence but the TONIC resolution fails; in abandoned cadence, the DOMINANT is compromised
- Deceptive cadence: the dominant resolves but to a substitute in DC; in abandoned cadence, the dominant itself fails

# Common Errors
- Confusing abandoned cadence with passages that simply lack a cadential progression
- Failing to recognize that the dominant in inversion cannot function as a cadential harmony
- Expecting cadential function to resume immediately (continuation function typically intervenes)

# Common Confusions
- **Abandoned vs. evaded**: In abandoned cadences, the cadential DOMINANT is compromised; in evaded cadences, the dominant remains intact but the TONIC resolution fails to provide closure
- **Abandoned vs. no cadence**: An abandoned cadence specifically INITIATES a progression and then disrupts it -- a passage without any cadential progression at all is not an abandonment
- **Drama vs. loosening**: The abandoned cadence is LESS dramatic than the evaded cadence but creates MORE formal loosening because the music loses cadential direction entirely
