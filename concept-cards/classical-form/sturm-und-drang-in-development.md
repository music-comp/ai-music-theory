# Quick Definition
The characteristic emotional quality of the development core -- instability, restlessness, and dramatic conflict -- expressed through forte dynamics, continuous rhythmic activity, and minor-mode emphasis.

# Formal Definition
The core typically projects instability, restlessness, and dramatic conflict with forte dynamics and conventionalized accompanimental patterns. Polyphonic devices (imitation, canon, fugal entries) contribute to textural complexity. This contrasts with the pre-core, "the calm before the storm."

# Construction/Recognition
- Forte dynamic level with thick, active textures
- Continuous rhythmic activity, often with conventionalized accompanimental patterns
- Minor-mode emphasis
- Strong sense of "working out" (Durchfuhrung)
- Contrasts with the softer, hesitant pre-core

# Musical Context
The Sturm und Drang aesthetic represents the moment of greatest emotional intensity in the movement, essential to the development's function of generating instability.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in A Minor, K. 310, i (ex. 10.3): four-measure model of Sturm und Drang character
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in B-flat, K. 333, i (ex. 10.16): pseudo-core with Sturm und Drang character despite lacking model-sequence technique

# Common Confusions
- Sturm und Drang character can appear in a pseudo-core as well as a genuine core
- The term refers to an expressive quality, not the broader historical movement of the 1770s
