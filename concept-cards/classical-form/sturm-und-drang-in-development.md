# Quick Definition
The characteristic emotional quality of the development core -- instability, restlessness, and dramatic conflict -- expressed through forte dynamics, continuous rhythmic activity, and minor-mode emphasis.

# Core Definition
The core typically projects instability, restlessness, and dramatic conflict with forte dynamics and conventionalized accompanimental patterns. Polyphonic devices (imitation, canon, fugal entries) may contribute to textural complexity. This dramatic character contrasts with the pre-core, which can be likened to "the calm before the storm." The Sturm und Drang quality is essential to the development's function of generating maximum instability in the movement. This character can also appear in a pseudo-core (which lacks model-sequence technique but shares the dramatic expression).

# Prerequisites
- **Core technique**: The organizational method projecting this character
- **Development section**: The formal context

# Key Properties
1. Forte dynamic level with thick, active textures
2. Continuous rhythmic activity with conventionalized accompanimental patterns
3. Minor-mode emphasis
4. Strong sense of "working out" (Durchfuhrung)
5. Instability, restlessness, and dramatic conflict
6. Polyphonic devices may contribute (imitation, canon, fugal entries)
7. Contrasts with the softer, hesitant, anticipatory pre-core
8. Can appear in pseudo-cores as well as genuine cores

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify forte dynamic level and thick textures
- Note continuous rhythmic activity
- Observe minor-mode emphasis and dramatic character
- Compare with the preceding pre-core (should be markedly more intense)
- Determine whether the character accompanies genuine model-sequence technique (core) or not (pseudo-core)

# Context & Application
The Sturm und Drang aesthetic represents the moment of greatest emotional intensity in the movement. It is essential to the development's function of generating instability that motivates the recapitulation's restoration of stability. The term refers to an expressive quality, not the broader historical movement of the 1770s.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in A Minor, K. 310, i (ex. 10.3): four-measure model of Sturm und Drang character with genuine core technique
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in B-flat, K. 333, i (ex. 10.16): pseudo-core with Sturm und Drang character despite lacking model-sequence technique

# Common Errors
- Assuming Sturm und Drang character guarantees genuine core technique (pseudo-cores share the character)
- Confusing the expressive term with the historical movement of the 1770s
- Expecting Sturm und Drang character in all developments (some are more restrained)

# Common Confusions
- **Core vs. pseudo-core character**: Sturm und Drang character can appear in both; the distinction is organizational (model-sequence technique), not expressive
- **Historical vs. expressive meaning**: The term refers to an expressive quality, not the broader 18th-century literary/musical movement
- **Pre-core contrast**: The "calm before the storm" metaphor highlights the essential contrast between pre-core and core
