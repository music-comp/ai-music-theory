# Quick Definition
The coda's function of shaping a concluding dynamic arc — either progressive (building to a climactic close) or recessive (fading to a quiet end) — that may differ from or surpass the recapitulation's dynamic profile.

# Formal Definition
If a movement ends with the recapitulation, the concluding dynamic curve conforms to that established by the closing section of the exposition. If the exposition closes fortissimo, the recapitulation does too. The coda gives the composer an opportunity to shape a different dynamic trajectory. Some codas build to the most powerful climax in the movement (progressive dynamic). Others close with a marked recessive dynamic, sometimes leading to a complete dissolution of the texture.

# Construction/Recognition
- **Progressive dynamic curve**: builds from piano to the movement's most powerful climax; strong final PAC
- **Recessive dynamic curve**: gradual decrescendo leading to pianissimo; systematic deceleration of rhythmic activity; dissolution of texture
- The coda's dynamic curve may directly contradict the recapitulation's
- Some codas combine both: building to a climax then subsiding
- The choice of curve is often related to the programmatic or expressive character of the movement

# Musical Context
The dynamic curve of the coda provides the final emotional impression of the movement. A progressive curve creates a sense of triumph or resolution; a recessive curve creates finality, dissolution, or tragedy.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Overture to Coriolanus, Op. 62 (ex. 12.6): the recapitulation's fortissimo ending is cut off; the coda begins piano (m. 242), builds to fortissimo at main-theme return (m. 276), then undergoes a gradual decrescendo from m. 290 to pianissimo pizzicati at the end — reflecting the tragedy of Coriolanus; systematic deceleration of the eighth-note motive (mm. 297-310)

# Common Confusions
- A recessive dynamic curve does not mean the coda is weak — it is a deliberate expressive choice
- Progressive and recessive curves can both appear in the same coda (building up then fading)
- The dynamic curve is distinct from the formal structure — a loosely organized coda theme can have a strongly progressive dynamic
