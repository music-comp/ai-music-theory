# Quick Definition
The expression of subordinate-theme function in a minuet, created by the cadential confirmation of a subordinate key, which may appear in the A section, B section, or recapitulation.

# Core Definition

# Prerequisites
See front matter.

# Key Properties
See Core Definition.
Subordinate-theme function in a modulating A section is always expressed at least minimally by a cadential confirmation of the subordinate key. The expression becomes more palpable when the change of key is accompanied by loosening devices typical of subordinate themes. Subordinate-theme function may be fused with transition function or may occupy its own distinct group. Multiple subordinate themes are possible when additional PACs in the subordinate key follow the first. In nonmodulating A sections, subordinate-theme function may shift to the B section, where it is expressed through modulation to and cadential confirmation of a subordinate key. Subordinate-theme function may also be expressed in the recapitulation through the expansion of the A' section.

# Construction / Recognition
- Always expressed at least minimally by a cadential confirmation of the subordinate key
- More strongly expressed when accompanied by loosening devices
- May be fused with transition function or given its own distinct group
- Multiple subordinate themes possible with additional PACs in the subordinate key
- May appear in the A section (if modulating), B section (if A is nonmodulating), or expanded A' section
- A closing section may follow the final cadence

# Context & Application
Subordinate-theme function in a minuet is important because the A section may represent the principal tonal conflict of the movement. Whether this function appears in the A or B section determines much about the formal character of the minuet. Rarely is subordinate-theme function absent from the entire minuet/trio form.

# Examples
- Haydn, Symphony No. 98 in B-flat, iii: Subordinate key confirmed at m. 12 with expanded cadential progression; a second subordinate theme begins at m. 12
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in A, K. 331/300i, ii: Subordinate theme begins directly in the new key at m. 11, bypassing transition function
- Mozart, String Quartet in A, K. 464, ii, mm. 13-28: A full subordinate theme with presentation, extended continuation, deceptive cadence, and cadential closure
- Haydn, Symphony No. 87 in A, iii: Subordinate-theme function expressed in the B section following a nonmodulating A section

# Common Confusions
- Subordinate-theme function can appear in either the A section or the B section; students should not assume it must be in the A section.
- When subordinate-theme function appears in the B section, the A' section may provide a compensatory "recapitulation" of this function through its expansion, but it does not literally restate the B section's subordinate theme.
