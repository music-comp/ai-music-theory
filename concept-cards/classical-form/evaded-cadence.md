# Quick Definition
The most dramatic technique for extending cadential function, in which the musical event following the cadential dominant groups forward as a new beginning rather than backward as a goal, leaving the cadential process unresolved.

# Core Definition
An evaded cadence occurs when the prevailing harmonic, melodic, rhythmic, and textural processes leading to a cadential goal fail to reach their projected resolution. The musical event directly following the cadential dominant is perceived to group with subsequent material, not with the material leading up to the dominant. The event that appears does not sound like a structural end but rather like a new beginning (or even a new middle). The cadential dominant receives harmonic resolution (often to I6) but NOT formal closure. This is distinct from both a deceptive cadence (where a substitute harmony groups backward as a partial goal) and an abandoned cadence (where the cadential dominant itself is disrupted).

# Prerequisites
- **Cadential function**: The function being thwarted
- **Cadential progression**: The harmonic pattern approaching resolution
- **Subordinate theme**: The primary formal context

# Key Properties
1. Cadential dominant resolves but the resolution groups FORWARD (new beginning), not BACKWARD (goal)
2. Distinct change in texture, dynamics, and accompaniment marks the new beginning
3. Melodic line is usually interrupted in its projected resolution to 1 (or 3)
4. Melody often leaps back up to 5 to attempt another descent
5. Most commonly, cadential dominant moves to I6 (inverted tonic prohibits hearing a true cadence)
6. I6 easily functions as the start of another cadential progression
7. V may move to V7 just before the evasion to lead more smoothly into I6
8. May also use VI, IV6, VII6/V, or even root-position I (if other factors strongly project new beginning)
9. Can chain multiple evasions, creating extended cadential areas
10. The "one more time" technique is a specific variety of cadential evasion

# Construction / Recognition
- Look for a cadential progression approaching resolution where the post-dominant event groups forward
- Textural/dynamic/registral changes at the moment of "resolution" signal new beginning
- I6 is the most common post-dominant harmony (inverted tonic prevents formal cadence)
- The melody typically fails to complete its descent to the tonic scale-degree
- Multiple evasions in succession create increasingly intense cadential pressure

# Context & Application
The evaded cadence is unquestionably the most dramatic technique for extending cadential function. The imminent closure is thwarted at the last moment and then quickly reattempted. The lack of any event representing formal closure, combined with the breaking off of a highly goal-directed process, arouses powerful expectation for further cadential action. This effect is particularly suited to subordinate themes, where dramatizing the subordinate key is a principal aesthetic objective. Evaded cadences seldom appear in main themes (partial closure via IAC or DC is more typical) and rarely in transitions (which approach HC, not authentic cadence).

# Examples
- Haydn, String Quartet in B Minor, Op. 64/2, i, m. 26: dominant resolves to I6; dramatic change of dynamics, texture, and modal shift mark new beginning
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in C, Hob. XVI:35, i, m. 60: cadential idea evaded, restated "one more time" to achieve PAC at m. 62
- Mozart, Violin Concerto in A, K. 219, i, mm. 100-112: multiple evaded cadences at mm. 104 and 108, cadential idea restated, PAC achieved at m. 112
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in F Minor, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 33-41: bass descends stepwise through VI onto I6, which initiates repetition of cadential phrase

- Abandoned cadence (disruption of the progression itself)

## Contrasts With
- Deceptive cadence: in DC, substitute harmony GROUPS BACKWARD as a goal (partial closure achieved); in evaded cadence, event GROUPS FORWARD as new beginning (NO closure achieved)
- Abandoned cadence: in abandoned cadence, the cadential DOMINANT is compromised; in evaded cadence, the dominant remains intact but the TONIC resolution fails to provide closure

# Common Errors
- Confusing evaded cadence with deceptive cadence based solely on harmony (the distinction is in grouping, not harmony)
- Assuming a cadence is evaded just because the melody doesn't reach 1
- Calling any repetition after failed closure "one more time" technique

# Common Confusions
- **Evaded vs. deceptive**: The distinction lies in whether what follows groups BACKWARD (DC = partial goal) or FORWARD (evaded = new beginning)
- **"One more time" technique**: A specific type of evaded cadence where previous cadential material is restated -- not all evaded cadences involve restating the same material
- **Root-position I after evasion**: A cadence can be evaded even if the dominant resolves to root-position I, as long as other factors (texture, dynamics, register, melodic interruption) strongly project a new beginning
- **Repetition after IAC/DC vs. "one more time"**: Repeating material after an IAC or DC is NOT "one more time" technique -- those cadences achieve partial closure, so the aesthetic is fundamentally different
