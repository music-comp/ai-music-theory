# Quick Definition
The most common and dramatic way of extending cadential function in a subordinate theme, in which the musical event following the cadential dominant groups forward as a new beginning rather than backward as a goal, leaving the cadential process unresolved.

# Formal Definition
An evaded cadence occurs when the prevailing harmonic, melodic, rhythmic, and textural processes leading to a cadential goal fail to reach their projected resolution. The musical event directly following the cadential dominant is perceived to group with subsequent material, not with the material leading up to the dominant. The event that appears does not sound like a structural end but rather like a new beginning (or even a new middle). The music is left "hanging in the air" without formal resolution. The cadential dominant receives harmonic resolution (often to I6) but NOT formal closure. This is distinct from both a deceptive cadence (where a substitute harmony groups backward as a partial goal) and an abandoned cadence (where the cadential dominant itself is disrupted).

# Construction/Recognition
1. Cadential dominant resolves but the resolution groups FORWARD (new beginning), not BACKWARD (goal)
2. Distinct change in texture, dynamics, and accompaniment marks the new beginning
3. Melodic line is usually interrupted in its projected resolution to ^1 (or ^3)
4. Melody often leaps back up to ^5 to attempt another descent
5. Most commonly, cadential dominant moves to I6 (inverted tonic prohibits hearing a true cadence)
6. I6 then easily functions as the start of another cadential progression
7. V may move to V^7 just before the evasion to lead more smoothly into I6
8. May also use VI, IV6, VII6/V, or even root-position I (if other factors strongly project new beginning)
9. Can chain multiple evasions together, creating extended cadential areas
10. The "one more time" technique is a specific variety of cadential evasion

# Musical Context
The evaded cadence is unquestionably the most dramatic technique for extending cadential function. The imminent closure is thwarted at the last moment and then quickly reattempted. The lack of any event representing formal closure, combined with the breaking off of a highly goal-directed process, arouses powerful expectation for further cadential action. This effect is particularly suited to subordinate themes, where dramatizing the subordinate key is a principal aesthetic objective. Evaded cadences are seldom found in main themes, where the need for dramatic cadential articulation is less pressing. Chains of evaded cadences rarely appear in transitions, since transitions end with half cadences rather than the authentic cadences that evaded cadences approach.

# Examples
- Haydn, String Quartet in B Minor, Op. 64/2, i, m. 26 (dominant resolves to I6; dramatic change of dynamics, texture, and modal shift mark new beginning)
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in C, Hob. XVI:35, i, m. 60 (cadential idea evaded, restated "one more time" to achieve PAC at m. 62)
- Mozart, Violin Concerto in A, K. 219, i, mm. 100-112 (multiple evaded cadences; at mm. 104 and 108, cadential idea restated, eventually achieving PAC at m. 112)
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in F Minor, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 33-41 (bass descends stepwise through VI onto I6, which initiates repetition of cadential phrase)

# Common Confusions
- An evaded cadence is NOT a deceptive cadence: in a DC, the substitute harmony GROUPS BACKWARD as a goal (partial closure achieved); in an evaded cadence, what follows GROUPS FORWARD as a new beginning (NO closure achieved)
- The "one more time" technique is a SPECIFIC TYPE of evaded cadence where previous cadential material is restated -- not all evaded cadences involve restating the same material
- A cadence can be evaded even if the dominant resolves to root-position I, as long as other factors (texture, dynamics, register, melodic interruption) strongly project a new beginning
- Repeating material after an IAC or DC is NOT "one more time" technique -- those cadences achieve partial closure, so the aesthetic is fundamentally different
