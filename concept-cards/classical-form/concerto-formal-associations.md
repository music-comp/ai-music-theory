# Quick Definition
The technique whereby musical ideas first presented with one formal function in the opening ritornello reappear in different formal contexts throughout the concerto, creating multiple associations that are especially prominent in concerto form.

# Core Definition
The opening ritornello forges an initial association between various musical ideas and their form-functional expression. Since subsequent sections of the concerto rarely bring back the content of the opening ritornello in the same order, it is always of analytical interest to observe how ritornello ideas are linked to varying formal units throughout the rest of the movement. For example, main-theme material of the ritornello frequently functions to begin the transition in the solo exposition; an idea first presented as a subordinate theme in the ritornello may return only in the development. While the creation of multiple associations is found throughout classical forms, it is especially prominent in concerto form, where each of the five subsequent sections has the possibility of granting a new formal interpretation to material originally presented in the opening ritornello (Caplin, pp. 705-706).

# Prerequisites
- **Concerto form** -- The formal structure where this occurs
- **Opening ritornello** -- The source of the initial material-function associations
- **Solo exposition** -- Where material is first reassigned

# Key Properties
1. Material first presented with one function may reappear with a different function
2. Main-theme material may become transition material in the solo exposition
3. Subordinate-theme material may appear only in the development
4. Five subsequent sections each offer opportunities for reinterpretation
5. Especially prominent in concerto form compared to other classical forms
6. Creates a rich web of formal associations across the movement

# Construction / Recognition
1. Catalog the thematic ideas and their functions in the opening ritornello
2. Track where each idea reappears in subsequent sections
3. Note changes in formal function (e.g., MT material becoming TR material)
4. Assess how the redistribution affects the overall formal logic

# Context & Application
This principle is central to concerto analysis. Understanding how material migrates across formal functions explains why the solo recapitulation often differs significantly from both the ritornello and the solo exposition -- it must incorporate material from both in new functional contexts.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Expecting material to maintain its original formal function throughout the concerto. **Correction**: Reassignment of material to new functions is a fundamental feature of concerto form.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Whether material reassignment indicates poor organization. **Clarification**: It is a sophisticated compositional technique that enriches the formal web of the concerto.
