# Quick Definition
Eight standard ways that cadential goals and interthematic functions combine in a sonata exposition (Table 13.1), ranging from simple sentence-based themes to complex fusions of transition and subordinate theme.

# Core Definition
Table 13.1 presents eight patterns relating cadential goals to form-functional patterns in the exposition. The complete tonal curve projects five cadential goals: (1) partial home-key confirmation by HC, (2) full home-key confirmation by PAC, (3) home-key destabilization by HC or dominant arrival, (4) partial subordinate-key confirmation by HC or dominant arrival, and (5) full subordinate-key confirmation by PAC. Any of the first four cadences can be omitted, but the PAC in the subordinate key must always appear. The exposition must also contain at least one cadence confirming the home key.

# Prerequisites
- **Sonata form**: The formal framework
- **Sonata exposition**: The section containing these patterns
- **Main theme, transition, subordinate theme**: The functions that produce the cadences

# Key Properties
1. Eight standard patterns (other patterns are usually variants)
2. Five potential cadential goals in the complete form
3. The PAC in the subordinate key is always required
4. At least one home-key cadence is also required
5. Any of the first four cadences can be omitted
6. Pattern 5 (all five cadences) is relatively rare
7. Pattern 8 (Tr/ST fusion) is the most unusual
8. Patterns relate specific cadences to specific interthematic functions

# Construction / Recognition
1. **Pattern 1**: MT (sentential) ends PAC in HK; Tr modulates; ST ends PAC in SK -- most common
2. **Pattern 2**: MT ends HC in HK; Tr modulates; ST ends PAC in SK -- home key only partially confirmed
3. **Pattern 3**: MT (periodic) has antecedent HC + consequent PAC in HK; Tr modulates; ST ends PAC in SK
4. **Pattern 4**: MT ends PAC in HK; Tr nonmodulating with HC in HK; ST ends PAC in SK -- no prior SK partial confirmation
5. **Pattern 5**: MT ends PAC in HK; Tr nonmodulating with HC; ST pt 1 has HC in SK; ST pt 2 ends PAC in SK -- all five cadential goals present
6. **Pattern 6**: MT ends PAC in HK; Tr pt 1 HC in HK; Tr pt 2 HC in SK; ST ends PAC in SK
7. **Pattern 7**: MT as incomplete small ternary: A section PAC; B section HC; A' becomes Tr; ST ends PAC in SK
8. **Pattern 8**: MT ends HC in HK; Tr/ST fusion ends PAC in SK -- transition and subordinate theme fused

# Context & Application
These eight patterns provide a systematic framework for analyzing any sonata exposition. They show how the same cadential requirements (at minimum: one HK cadence + PAC in SK) can be fulfilled by very different formal organizations. Most expositions fit one of these eight or a recognizable variant.

# Examples
- Pattern 5 with periodic main theme: all five cadential goals occur -- the most complete tonal curve
- Pattern 8 (Tr/ST fusion): discussed in detail as "transition/subordinate-theme fusion" later in Chapter 13
- Pattern 3 is common in periodic main themes
- Pattern 1 is the most frequently encountered

# Common Errors
- Treating these as rigid templates rather than common patterns with variants
- Assuming all five cadences must appear (Pattern 5 is relatively rare)

# Common Confusions
- **Not rigid templates**: These are common patterns; most expositions fit one or a variant
- **PAC in SK required**: The only truly required cadence; at least one HK cadence is also needed
- **Pattern 8 (fusion)**: The most unusual pattern, with no clear boundary between transition and subordinate theme
- **Variants**: Other patterns seen occasionally can usually be understood as variants of these eight
