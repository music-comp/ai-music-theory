# Quick Definition
Eight standard ways that cadential goals and interthematic functions combine in a sonata exposition, ranging from simple sentence-based themes to complex fusions of transition and subordinate theme.

# Formal Definition
Table 13.1 presents eight patterns relating cadential goals to form-functional patterns in the exposition. Each pattern specifies which cadences are present or omitted and which formal functions (main theme, transition, subordinate theme) produce them. Other patterns seen occasionally can usually be understood as variants of these eight.

# Construction/Recognition
1. **Pattern 1**: MT (sentential) ends PAC in HK; Tr modulates; ST ends PAC in SK — most common
2. **Pattern 2**: MT ends HC in HK; Tr modulates; ST ends PAC in SK — home key only partially confirmed
3. **Pattern 3**: MT (periodic) has antecedent HC + consequent PAC in HK; Tr modulates; ST ends PAC in SK
4. **Pattern 4**: MT ends PAC in HK; Tr nonmodulating with HC in HK; ST ends PAC in SK
5. **Pattern 5**: MT ends PAC in HK; Tr nonmodulating with HC; ST pt 1 has HC in SK; ST pt 2 ends PAC in SK — all five cadential goals present
6. **Pattern 6**: MT ends PAC in HK; Tr pt 1 HC in HK; Tr pt 2 HC in SK; ST ends PAC in SK
7. **Pattern 7**: MT built as incomplete small ternary — A section PAC; B section HC; A' section becomes Tr; ST ends PAC in SK
8. **Pattern 8**: MT ends HC in HK; Tr/ST fusion ends PAC in SK — transition and subordinate theme fused

# Musical Context
These eight patterns provide a systematic framework for analyzing any sonata exposition. They show how the same cadential requirements (at minimum: one HK cadence + PAC in SK) can be fulfilled by very different formal organizations.

# Examples
- Pattern 5 variant with periodic main theme: all five cadential goals occur — the most complete tonal curve
- Pattern 8 (fusion of Tr/ST): discussed in detail as "transition/subordinate-theme fusion" later in Chapter 13

# Common Confusions
- These are not rigid templates but common patterns; most expositions fit one of these eight (or a variant)
- Pattern 5 (all five cadences) is relatively rare; most expositions omit at least one cadence
- Pattern 8 (Tr/ST fusion) is the most unusual, with no clear boundary between transition and subordinate theme
