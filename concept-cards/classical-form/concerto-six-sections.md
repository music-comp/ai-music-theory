# Quick Definition
Concerto form consists of six principal sections: opening ritornello, solo exposition, subordinate-key ritornello, solo development, solo recapitulation, and closing ritornello -- with no section functioning as a coda.

# Core Definition
Concerto form consists of six principal sections, each combining ritornello and sonata-form principles. (1) The opening ritornello resembles the thematic organization of a sonata exposition but remains in the home key and minimizes loosening. (2) The solo exposition is organized like a sonata exposition, effecting modulation to the subordinate key with significant formal loosening. (3) The subordinate-key ritornello, occupying the position of a closing section, provides orchestral reinforcement of the new key. (4) The solo development functions like a sonata development. (5) The solo recapitulation resolves tonal conflict while synthesizing material from both the ritornello and the solo exposition. (6) The closing ritornello, analogous to a closing section, completes the structural frame, usually interrupted by a cadenza. Concerto form does not contain a section functioning as a coda (Caplin, pp. 695-696).

# Key Properties
1. Six sections: opening ritornello, solo exposition, subordinate-key ritornello, solo development, solo recapitulation, closing ritornello
2. Three ritornellos for orchestra alone; three solo sections with accompaniment
3. No coda section
4. Opening and closing ritornellos create a textural frame
5. Solo sections correspond to sonata exposition, development, recapitulation
6. The form is used in first movements of all classical concertos

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the opening ritornello (orchestra alone, home key)
2. Locate the solo entrance beginning the solo exposition
3. Find the subordinate-key ritornello (orchestra alone, after the cadential trill)
4. Identify the solo development
5. Locate the solo recapitulation
6. Find the closing ritornello (with cadenza)

# Context & Application
This six-section scheme is the standard model for classical concerto first movements. The form sometimes appears in slow movements but rarely in finales (which typically use sonata-rondo). Understanding all six sections and their functions is essential for concerto analysis.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Expecting a coda section in concerto form. **Correction**: Concerto form does not include a coda; the closing ritornello serves a different function.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Whether the six sections map directly onto sonata form. **Clarification**: While the solo sections function like exposition, development, and recapitulation, the three ritornellos have no direct sonata-form analogue.
