# Quick Definition
Rondo main themes are more tightly knit than sonata main themes: they always close with a PAC (never HC), favor periodic forms over sentences, and are typically conventional and symmetrical, with the 8-m. period and small ternary being especially common.

# Core Definition
In general, main themes in rondo forms are more tightly knit than those in sonata form. A rondo theme always closes with a PAC, never an HC. Rondo refrains tend to be conventional and symmetrical, whereas the looser, nonconventional themes frequently found in a sonata rarely appear. Unlike the sonata, the use of sentences or sentence-like hybrids for the main theme is shunned in favor of periodic forms. The 8-m. period frequently appears in rondo themes, either as the first part of a small ternary (or small binary) or as a stand-alone theme type. The small ternary theme type is commonly used in the refrain of a five-part rondo. The main theme proper may be followed by a closing section, and at times a second main theme in the home key may even appear as part of the opening refrain (Caplin, pp. 672-673).

# Key Properties
1. Always closes with a PAC (never HC)
2. Conventional and symmetrical
3. Periodic forms preferred; sentences shunned
4. 8-m. period is especially common
5. Small ternary frequently used, especially in five-part rondo
6. May be followed by a closing section
7. A second main theme may appear as part of the opening refrain
8. Nonconventional themes are rare

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the refrain and determine its theme type
2. Verify PAC closure in the home key
3. Check for periodic organization (antecedent-consequent)
4. If a small ternary, look for the 8-m. period as the A section
5. Assess symmetry and tight-knit organization

# Context & Application
The tighter organization of rondo refrains reflects the form's aesthetic: the refrain must be immediately recognizable upon each return, and conventional, symmetrical themes are most easily recognized and remembered.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 667): Mozart, Piano Sonata in C, K. 545, iii -- refrain as a simple 8-m. period.
**Example 2**: Many five-part rondo refrains use a small ternary with an 8-m. period as the A section.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Analyzing a rondo refrain as if it might end with an HC. **Correction**: Rondo refrains always close with a PAC.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Why sentences are uncommon in rondo refrains. **Clarification**: The periodic structure provides clearer tonal closure and greater symmetry, both essential for a theme that must be recognized upon each return.
