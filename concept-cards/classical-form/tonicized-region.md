# Quick Definition
A tonal area in the development that receives emphasis through tonicization but lacks the cadential confirmation needed to establish it as a true development key.

# Formal Definition
Tonicized regions reside at the bottom of the tonal hierarchy. They do not receive cadential confirmation but can still acquire considerable structural importance if associated with an initiating formal function (such as a basic idea, presentation, or compound basic idea). Tonicization differs from modulation in that the new tonic is disassociated from cadential articulation and is perceived as a localized tonal emphasis within a prevailing key.

# Construction/Recognition
- Lacks cadential confirmation (no HC, PAC, or dominant arrival in the region)
- May be associated with an initiating function, giving it more weight
- Regions arising from sequential activity have less structural potential
- Very local tonicizations (chromatic inflections) lie at the very bottom of the hierarchy

# Musical Context
In major-mode developments, both IV and bVI frequently serve as initiating regions. Not every development confirms a development key -- shorter developments may rely entirely on tonicized regions for tonal contrast.

# Examples
- Mozart, String Quintet in E-flat, K. 614, i (ex. 10.6): Ab major (HK: IV) serves as an initiating region for the core model

# Common Confusions
- Tonicization is not the same as modulation: the former lacks cadential confirmation
- A region that begins as a tonicization can be "promoted" to a key if cadential confirmation follows
