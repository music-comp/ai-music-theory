# Quick Definition
The concluding passage of the development section, normally arriving on the dominant of the home key to prepare for the recapitulation, sometimes with exceptional harmonic substitutions.

# Core Definition
The final harmony of the development is usually the dominant of the home key. Two exceptional situations motivate a different harmony. First, if the recapitulation does not begin with the home-key tonic, the final harmony of the development is usually one that leads most naturally into the recapitulation's opening harmony. Second, the composer may end the development with a harmony that substitutes for the normal home-key dominant, such as V/VI or V/III. Both of these harmonies contain the leading tone of the home key, a necessary condition for dominant functionality. To mitigate an abrupt resolution to the tonic (due to a cross-relation between the harmonies), the texture at the very end of the development is usually reduced to a single voice.

# Prerequisites
- **Development section**: The formal context in which the ending occurs
- **Standing on the dominant**: The postcadential prolongation that typically precedes the recapitulation
- **Core technique**: The developmental process whose concluding function produces the ending

# Key Properties
1. Standard ending: half cadence in home key followed by standing on the dominant
2. Standing on the dominant often features anticipatory motives from the main theme's basic idea
3. Exception 1: non-tonic recapitulation opening requires a different preparatory harmony
4. Exception 2: substitute dominants (V/VI, V/III) contain the leading tone
5. Texture typically reduced to a single voice when using substitute dominants (to avoid cross-relations)
6. Textural reduction is part of a broader liquidation process common at the end of developments
7. The resolution of dominant to tonic at the start of the recapitulation is NOT an authentic cadence

# Construction / Recognition
- Standard ending: half cadence in home key followed by standing on the dominant
- Exceptional: pre-dominant harmony (e.g., VII6/5 of V) when recapitulation begins on V7
- Substitute dominants: V/VI or V/III (both contain leading tone)
- Texture reduction at the end to avoid cross-relations with substitute dominants
- Check whether the recapitulation begins on tonic or some other harmony

# Context & Application
The development ending creates maximum harmonic tension, building expectation for the "double return" of main-theme material and home-key tonic. Caplin cautions against interpreting the V-I resolution at the start of the recapitulation as an authentic cadence, even when the opening tonic appears with great dynamic force. The dominant at the end of the development is an "ultimate" dominant that resolves to initiate, not conclude.

# Examples
- Haydn, Symphony No. 92 ("Oxford"), i, mm. 121-30 (ex. 13.17): recapitulation begins on V7, so development ends on pre-dominant VII6/5/V
- Beethoven, Violin Sonata in F ("Spring"), Op. 24, i, mm. 118-27 (ex. 13.18): ends on V/VI resolving directly to I; cross-relation mitigated by texture reduction

# Common Errors
- Interpreting the V-I resolution at the start of the recapitulation as an authentic cadence
- Assuming the development must always end on V of the home key
- Failing to notice textural reduction as a signal of substitute-dominant usage

# Common Confusions
- **Development ending vs. authentic cadence**: The dominant at the end of the development resolves to initiate the recapitulation, not to conclude a phrase -- this is NOT an authentic cadence
- **Standing on the dominant vs. retransition**: The standing on the dominant is postcadential; the modulation back occurs at the half cadence
- **Substitute dominants**: V/VI and V/III function as dominant substitutes because they contain the leading tone
