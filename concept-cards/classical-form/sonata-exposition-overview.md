# Quick Definition
The opening large-scale section of sonata form, featuring three principal interthematic functions -- main theme (home key), transition (destabilization/modulation), and subordinate theme (subordinate key) -- arranged in a conventionalized tonal and structural hierarchy from tight-knit to progressively loose.

# Core Definition
In a sonata-form exposition, "the main theme expresses the home key through cadential closure. Likewise, a later-occurring subordinate theme confirms the subordinate key. Standing between these two functions is the transition, a themelike unit that destabilizes the home key and (usually) modulates to the subordinate key" (Ch. 1, p. 18). The three functions display a progressive loosening: "the main theme is the most tight-knit unit in a sonata exposition, whereas the transition and subordinate theme are distinctly looser in structure" (Ch. 1, p. 17). The development section is "almost always distinctly looser than any of the interthematic functions in the exposition" (Ch. 1, p. 21).

# Key Properties
1. Three principal functions: main theme, transition, subordinate theme
2. Tonal plan: home key (MT) -> modulation (TR) -> subordinate key (ST)
3. Structural hierarchy: tight-knit (MT) -> loose (TR) -> looser (ST)
4. The development section is typically the loosest section in the movement
5. Each function defined by both tonal role and degree of tight-knit/loose organization
6. The exposition presents the movement's melodic-motivic material and establishes the tonal conflict

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the three interthematic functions by their tonal roles
- Assess the progressive loosening from main theme to subordinate theme
- Note the tonal plan (home key to subordinate key)

# Context & Application
The exposition opens the sonata-form movement, presenting the tonal conflict (home key vs. subordinate key) that drives the development and is resolved in the recapitulation. Detailed treatment in Chapters 7-9 and 13.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 1, pp. 18-21): Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i: main theme (mm. 1-8, F minor, tight-knit sentence), transition (mm. 9-20, modulates to Ab major, loose), subordinate theme (mm. 21-48, Ab major, distinctly loose, with closing section mm. 42-48).

# Common Errors
- Treating the three functions as equal in organization (they display a deliberate hierarchy)

# Common Confusions
- Tonal considerations are central to interthematic functions but not sufficient; phrase-structural organization (tight-knit/loose) also differentiates the functions
- The exposition is not just "the themes" but a tonal process articulated by cadences
