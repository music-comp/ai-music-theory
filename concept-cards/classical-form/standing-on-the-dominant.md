# Quick Definition
A postcadential prolongation of the dominant harmony following a half cadence, often employing V/V as a neighboring chord, with melodic-motivic content that may be new or derived from the cadential idea.

# Core Definition
"When a theme (or a portion thereof) ends with a half cadence, the final harmony can be prolonged by means of a postcadential standing on the dominant" (Ch. 1, p. 17). The dominant is prolonged "through the use of its own dominant (V/V) as neighboring chords" (Ch. 1, p. 17). The melodic-motivic content of a standing on the dominant may be based on the half-cadence material or may be "entirely new" (Ch. 1, p. 17).

# Prerequisites
- **Postcadential function** -- the broader category
- **Half cadence** -- the cadence type that precedes the standing

# Key Properties
1. Follows a half cadence
2. Prolongs dominant harmony (the final chord of the HC)
3. V/V frequently serves as a neighboring chord within the prolongation
4. May feature fragmentation
5. Features recessive dynamic
6. Melodic content may derive from the HC material or be entirely new

# Construction / Recognition
- Locate the half cadence
- Identify dominant prolongation following it (pedal, neighboring V/V, etc.)
- The standing on the dominant is postcadential -- it follows the cadence

# Context & Application
Standings on the dominant appear after HCs in contrasting middles of small ternaries, at the end of transitions (prolonging the dominant of the subordinate key), and at the end of development sections (preparing the recapitulation). They build expectation for the resolution to tonic.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 1, p. 14, ex. 1.4): Beethoven, Op. 15, ii, mm. 12-14: after HC at m. 12, dominant prolonged by V/V neighboring chords, with fragmentation in mm. 12-13. Based on the half-cadence material itself.
**Example 2** (Ch. 1, p. 19, ex. 1.7): Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 17-20: after HC in Ab major at m. 16, standing on the dominant created by twice repeating the half-cadence idea for four measures.

## Related

- Pedal point (the most common technique for dominant prolongation)
## Contrasts With
- Closing section: prolongs dominant (after HC) vs. prolongs tonic (after PAC)

# Common Errors
- Confusing the standing on the dominant with a cadential dominant arrival

# Common Confusions
- The standing on the dominant is postcadential -- it comes after the cadence, not before
- A standing on the dominant is not a half cadence itself; it FOLLOWS the half cadence
