# Quick Definition
The coda's primary temporal function of expressing the quality of "after-the-end" -- music that follows the completion of the movement's fundamental formal and tonal processes.

# Core Definition
Because a movement's structural close is fulfilled by the recapitulation, the primary function of a coda is to express the temporal quality of "after-the-end." This characterization can seem to conflict with the coda's role as the movement's "genuine conclusion" (completing secondary processes). The conflict is reconciled by recognizing that the fundamental tonal, melodic, and phrase-structural processes are completed by the recapitulation (hence "after-the-end"), while secondary processes often attain closure only in the coda (hence "genuine conclusion"). The coda does not normally initiate significant tonal changes that might undermine this after-the-end expression. It avoids introducing new material calling for further development or initiating processes that cannot be completed, governed by the aesthetic ideal that "once the music finally stops, the listener should have no desire to hear anything more."

# Prerequisites
- **Coda**: The section expressing this function
- **Recapitulation**: The section that completes the fundamental processes

# Key Properties
1. Primary function of the coda
2. Fundamental tonal and formal processes are already completed by the recapitulation
3. The coda addresses secondary processes not yet resolved
4. "After-the-end" and "genuine conclusion" are reconciled by distinguishing primary from secondary processes
5. Avoids introducing new developmental material
6. Does not initiate processes that cannot be completed
7. The coda rarely initiates significant tonal changes
8. Analogous at a higher level to the closing section's postcadential function

# Construction / Recognition
- The coda follows the recapitulation's completion of fundamental processes
- Remains primarily in the home key
- Does not introduce genuinely new material requiring development
- Does not start processes that cannot be completed within the coda

# Context & Application
The after-the-end function is analogous at a higher level to the function of a closing section (codettas) that follows a PAC ending a theme. Both express postcadential continuation, but the coda operates at the level of the entire movement.

# Examples
- All codas embody this function; the concept is illustrated through the fundamental constraint that codas must not create expectations for further music

# Common Errors
- Treating "after-the-end" as meaning the coda is unimportant
- Introducing genuinely new developmental material in a coda

# Common Confusions
- **Not contradictory with "conclusion"**: "After-the-end" applies to primary processes; "genuine conclusion" applies to secondary processes
- **Not unimportant**: The coda's after-the-end quality does not diminish its musical significance
- **Tonal stability**: The coda's avoidance of significant tonal change supports the after-the-end expression
