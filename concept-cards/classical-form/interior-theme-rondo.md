# Quick Definition
A rondo couplet organized as an interior theme, typically in the minore, subdominant, or submediant, standing between two statements of the refrain and resembling the interior theme of a large ternary.

# Core Definition
An interior theme in rondo form is a couplet that follows the refrain directly without transition, usually residing in a contrasting tonal region: minore (favored by Haydn), subdominant (favored by Mozart for couplet 2), or submediant. The interior theme is typically modeled on a small ternary (or small binary), which may be complete, incomplete, or truncated. Its formal organization is even more diverse than in the large ternary. The couplet ends with a retransition leading back to the home key for the refrain's return. When couplet 1 is an interior theme (Haydn's preference), the refrain and couplet together resemble the first two parts of a large ternary rather than a sonata exposition.

# Construction / Recognition
- Follows the refrain directly, without a transition
- Typically in minore, IV, or vi
- Couplet 1 interior themes: most likely minore (or maggiore for minor-mode works) or vi; rarely IV
- Couplet 2 interior themes: any standard region -- minore (Haydn), IV (Mozart), vi
- Form usually based on small ternary or small binary, which may be:
  - Complete (followed by retransition)
  - Incomplete (closed theme reopened on repeat)
  - Truncated (recapitulation eliminated, ending with contrasting middle)
  - Nonconventional
- Ends with a retransition or merges into one from an incomplete form

# Context & Application
Interior themes in rondos function analogously to interior themes in large ternary form, occupying a central position between statements of the main theme. The interior-theme option for couplet 1 is favored by Haydn, while Mozart prefers the subordinate-theme complex option. For couplet 2 in both five-part rondos and sonata-rondos, the interior theme is a standard choice.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in G, Hob. XVI:39, i, mm. 17-34: Couplet 1 as minore interior theme; truncated small ternary with A section modulating to relative major, B section with model-sequence technique arriving on home-key dominant at m. 28
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 3, Op. 55, ii, mm. 105-15: Couplet 2 beginning with an incomplete refrain moving to IV

# Common Confusions
- Interior themes in rondos are distinct from subordinate themes: they never reside in the standard subordinate key (V in major, III in minor) and do not require the same cadential confirmation.
- An interior theme in couplet 1 makes the rondo initially sound like a large ternary; the rondo form is confirmed only when couplet 2 appears after refrain 2.
- Interior themes appear in both couplet 1 and couplet 2 positions but typically in different tonal regions. Students should note which region is used in each couplet.
