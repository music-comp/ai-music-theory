# Quick Definition
The opening eight-measure section of a small binary, usually constructed as a conventional theme (sentence, period, or hybrid) that may remain in the home key or modulate, and may close with any cadence type (PAC, HC, or rarely IAC).

# Core Definition
The first part of a small binary is most often built in the same way as the exposition of the rounded binary: as a simple eight-measure theme. It may remain in the home key or modulate to a subordinate key. Unlike an A section of a small ternary, the first part may close with a half cadence. When ending with a PAC, the first part cannot be distinguished from a small ternary exposition until the second part reveals the absence of recapitulation. When ending with an HC, periodic qualities may be present (the second phrase begins like a consequent) but the phrase "fails" to achieve the expected authentic cadence, resulting in a "failed consequent."

# Prerequisites
- **Small binary**: The overarching form

# Key Properties
1. Normatively 8 measures long, usually repeated
2. Built as sentence, period, or hybrid
3. May end with HC, PAC, or (rarely) IAC
4. HC ending strongly suggests binary (not ternary) interpretation
5. May remain in home key or modulate
6. When repeated via repeat signs, a HC ending may initially suggest a compound antecedent
7. A "failed consequent" arises when the second phrase restates the basic idea but ends with HC instead of PAC
8. The failed consequent is a nonconventional form (not a true antecedent, which would be nonmodulatory)

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the theme type (sentence, period, hybrid)
2. Note the closing cadence type (PAC, HC, IAC)
3. If HC: check whether the second phrase has consequent-like qualities (basic idea return) but fails to achieve PAC
4. Consider whether the form could be a small ternary exposition (PAC ending) or is more likely binary (HC ending)
5. Definitive identification requires hearing the second part

# Context & Application
The first part establishes the primary thematic material. When it ends with an HC, the subsequent repetition may initially imply a compound antecedent, but when the repetition also ends with HC, the compound period interpretation fails and a small binary is strongly suggested. The choice of cadence type significantly affects the listener's formal expectations.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Trio in A-flat, Hob. XV:14, ii, mm. 1-8: sentence ending with PAC in subordinate key (identical to what might be found in A section of small ternary)
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E, Hob. XVI:31, iii, mm. 1-8: antecedent followed by modulating phrase ending with HC ("failed consequent")
- Beethoven, Violin Sonata in A, Op. 30/1, iii, mm. 1-8: compound basic idea followed by HC-ending phrase
- Beethoven, String Quartet in C-sharp Minor, Op. 131, iv, mm. 1-8: sentence ending with HC

# Common Errors
- Assuming a first part ending with PAC must be a small ternary exposition -- binary vs. ternary cannot be determined until the second part
- Labeling the second phrase of a failed consequent as an "antecedent" -- a true antecedent is nonmodulatory
- Treating the repetition of part 1 via repeat signs as a consequent answering an antecedent

# Common Confusions
- A first part ending with PAC is indistinguishable from a small-ternary exposition until the second part is heard
- A first part ending with HC does NOT automatically make the theme binary -- it merely makes ternary unlikely
- A "failed consequent" is a nonconventional form, not a true antecedent
- The repetition of the first part is not the same as a consequent answering an antecedent
