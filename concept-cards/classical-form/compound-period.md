# Quick Definition
The compound (sixteen-measure) period consists of an eight-measure compound antecedent followed by an eight-measure compound consequent, where each unit is itself built as a sentence or hybrid theme. The antecedent ends with a weak cadence and the consequent repeats it with a stronger cadence (usually PAC).

# Core Definition
The compound sixteen-measure period consists of an eight-measure compound antecedent followed by an eight-measure compound consequent. The compound antecedent is built as one of three simple theme types: (1) an eight-measure sentence (presentation + continuation), (2) an eight-measure hybrid (compound basic idea + continuation), or (3) an eight-measure hybrid (antecedent + continuation). It usually closes with a half cadence (either regular or reinterpreted). The compound consequent repeats the antecedent but closes with a PAC. The return of the opening basic idea at m. 9 signals consequent function, and this return must sound fresh -- so the basic idea should not "return" prematurely at any earlier point in the theme.

# Prerequisites
- **Period**: The fundamental theme type whose antecedent-consequent principle extends to the compound level
- **Antecedent**: The formal function of the first half, now expanded to eight measures
- **Consequent**: The formal function of the second half, signaled by the return of the basic idea
- **Compound basic idea**: A frequent building block of the compound antecedent

# Key Properties
1. Sixteen real measures: 8 (compound antecedent) + 8 (compound consequent)
2. Compound antecedent ends with a weak cadence (HC, reinterpreted HC, rarely IAC)
3. Compound consequent ends with a PAC
4. Basic idea returns at m. 9 to signal higher-level consequent function
5. Three main categories based on internal organization of the compound antecedent
6. A four-measure consequent is NOT normally found within the compound antecedent (to preserve the impact of the m. 9 return)
7. The compound consequent's cadential progression is often expanded relative to the antecedent's
8. The continuation phrases in antecedent and consequent may differ in melodic content

# Construction / Recognition
Three main categories based on the internal organization of the large antecedent:

1. **Antecedent = presentation + continuation** (two sentences): both halves organized as standard sentences
2. **Antecedent = compound basic idea + continuation** (two hybrids): c.b.i. as first half of each eight-measure unit
3. **Antecedent = antecedent + continuation** (two hybrids): four-measure antecedent + four-measure continuation in each unit

Key structural rules:
- The basic idea returns at m. 9 to signal the large-scale consequent
- Classical composers avoid using a simple period (antecedent + consequent) as the eight-measure unit because the basic idea's return at m. 5 would preempt the higher-level consequent at m. 9
- The reinterpreted HC is particularly useful at m. 8 when the compound antecedent modulates

# Context & Application
The compound period is one of the most important large-scale theme types. The avoidance of a lower-level consequent is a deliberate compositional strategy to preserve the dramatic impact of the basic idea's return at m. 9. This is why sentences and hybrid types 1 and 3 are preferred as building blocks -- they either repeat the basic idea immediately (sentence) or state it only once (hybrids 1 and 3). The compound period appears across all genres and formal positions. Most textbooks would call this a "double period," but that term incorrectly implies a period within a period.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Concerto No. 2 in B-flat, Op. 19, iii, mm. 1-17: category 1 (presentation + continuation); consequent expanded by one bar for cadential emphasis
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in A-flat, Op. 26, i, mm. 1-16: category 3 (antecedent + continuation); basic idea returns at m. 9 for compound consequent
- Mozart, Piano Concerto in A, K. 488, i, mm. 1-16: category 2 (c.b.i. + continuation); continuation phrases differ in melodic content
- Mozart, String Quartet in C ("Dissonance"), K. 465, iv, mm. 1-16: category 3; consequent's final phrase supported by ECP

# Common Errors
- Labeling this a "double period" -- Caplin explicitly rejects this term because a genuine period-within-a-period rarely arises
- Failing to check whether the basic idea at m. 5 (if present) has its original harmonization -- different harmonies confirm continuation function, not consequent
- Assuming the compound consequent must be identical to the compound antecedent -- the cadential function is often significantly expanded

# Common Confusions
- A "double period" (antecedent + consequent at both levels) is avoided by classical composers because the basic idea would appear at both m. 5 and m. 9, undermining the impact of the higher-level consequent
- Hybrid type 4 (c.b.i. + consequent) is also avoided in compound periods for the same reason
- The consequent's cadential expansion is analogous to the expanded contrasting idea of a simple period's consequent
- When material at m. 5 resembles the basic idea, check the harmonization -- different harmonies confirm continuation function
