# Quick Definition
A loosening technique in which the tonic prolongation supporting a presentation phrase is destabilized through inversion, metrically accented subordinate harmonies, or an underlying dominant pedal.

# Formal Definition
Presentation function can acquire a loose expression if its supporting tonic prolongation is weakened or destabilized. The strongest, most stable prolongation features the tonic in root position with subordinate harmonies on weak metrical positions. A tonic prolongation can be weakened by: (1) inverting the prolonged harmony (I6 instead of I), (2) placing subordinate harmonies on metrically accented positions, or (3) undermining the prolongation with a dominant pedal. A dominant pedal can create the experience of two simultaneous prolongations on different hierarchical levels -- tonic at the foreground, dominant at the background. The resulting harmonic instability is appropriate for subordinate-theme loosening.

# Construction/Recognition
1. **Inverted tonic**: I6 instead of root-position I; embellished by V^4/3 chords (which must resolve to I6)
2. **Accented subordinate harmonies**: V or other subordinate chords placed on strong beats within the tonic prolongation
3. **Dominant pedal**: Bass voice sustains dominant (from end of transition) while upper voices project tonic prolongation
4. Compare with main theme's tonic prolongation to assess the degree of weakening
5. The weaker the tonic prolongation, the looser the presentation function

# Musical Context
This technique is particularly effective because it operates at the very beginning of the subordinate theme, signaling immediately that the theme will be more loosely organized than the main theme. When a dominant pedal from the transition continues into the subordinate theme's presentation, it blurs the boundary between transition and theme, creating another layer of looseness. The weakened prolongation contrasts with the typically firm root-position tonic pedal of main-theme presentations.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C, K. 545, i, mm. 13-16 (presentation prolongs I6 with V^4/3 embellishment; compare with root-position tonic pedal opening main theme, mm. 1-4)
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in F Minor, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 21-24 (dominant pedal in bass undermines foreground tonic prolongation in upper voices)

# Common Confusions
- A weakened tonic prolongation is NOT a dominant prolongation -- the foreground harmonic function is still tonic; it is merely less stable
- A dominant pedal underlying a presentation is distinct from a standing on the dominant: the former implies tonic function at the foreground; the latter implies dominant prolongation throughout
- Compare with the main theme's tonic prolongation to recognize the weakening -- the technique is relative, not absolute
- Subordinate harmonies on metrically strong positions do not change the prolongational function; they merely weaken its expression
