# Quick Definition
The recapitulatory section in a sonata-rondo consisting of refrain 3 (recapitulation of main theme) and couplet 3 (recapitulation of subordinate-theme complex in the home key).

# Core Definition
In sonata-rondo form, refrain 3 and couplet 3 together constitute the recapitulation. Refrain 3 corresponds to the beginning of a sonata recapitulation, bringing back the rondo's main theme. Couplet 3 recapitulates the subordinate-theme complex from couplet 1, now transposed to the home key. Unlike the final refrain of a five-part rondo, which is better described as a "return" than a "recapitulation," refrain 3 of a sonata-rondo genuinely corresponds to a sonata recapitulation. One standard deviation (frequently adopted by Mozart) eliminates refrain 3, beginning the recapitulation directly with the subordinate-theme group. In some Beethoven works, couplet 3 recapitulates in IV rather than I.

# Construction / Recognition
- Refrain 3: recapitulation of main theme in home key; usually brings back the complete refrain structure
- Couplet 3: recapitulation of subordinate-theme complex in home key
- Subordinate theme may or may not achieve PAC closure (as in exposition)
- Mozart sometimes eliminates refrain 3 and begins recapitulation with the subordinate-theme group
- When refrain 3 is eliminated, transition material from couplet 1 often prepares for the subordinate theme
- Beethoven sometimes recapitulates couplet 3 in IV rather than I
- A subordinate theme closed in the exposition may be left open in the recapitulation, merging into the coda

# Context & Application
The recapitulatory section is where the sonata-rondo most clearly reveals its sonata-form lineage. The transposition of the subordinate-theme complex to the home key resolves the tonal conflict established in the exposition, just as in a regular sonata recapitulation.

# Examples
- Mozart's sonata-rondos frequently eliminate refrain 3, proceeding directly from couplet 2 to the recapitulated subordinate theme
- Beethoven's sonata-rondos occasionally recapitulate couplet 3 in IV, related to the tendency toward flat-side emphasis in sonata recapitulations

# Common Confusions
- In a five-part rondo, the final refrain is better described as a "return" than a "recapitulation," since it lacks tonal adjustment and other recapitulatory aspects. Only in the sonata-rondo does refrain 3 constitute a genuine recapitulation.
- Mozart's elimination of refrain 3 does not eliminate the recapitulation; it merely begins the recapitulation with the subordinate-theme complex rather than the main theme.
