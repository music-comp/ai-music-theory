# Quick Definition
A harmonic progression that confirms a tonal center, consisting of the fundamental functions tonic-predominant-dominant-tonic (authentic) or ending on dominant (half), with specific voice-leading and root-position requirements.

# Formal Definition
A cadential progression confirms that an implied tonality is indeed the actual tonality of a musical passage. The strongest confirmation is achieved by an authentic cadential progression; a weaker confirmation by a half-cadential progression. The complete authentic cadential progression is made up of the fundamental harmonic functions in the temporal sequence: initial tonic - pre-dominant - dominant - final tonic. An incomplete cadential progression occurs when the initial tonic or pre-dominant (or both) is omitted. For sufficient cadential strength, both the dominant and the final tonic must be in root position, exposing the fundamental-bass motion of a descending fifth in the bass voice.

# Construction/Recognition
- Complete authentic: I(6) - pre-dominant (II6, IV, etc.) - V(7) - I
- Incomplete authentic: omits initial tonic, pre-dominant, or both
- Both V and final I must be in root position for authentic cadential force
- Initial tonic is usually in first inversion (I6); occasionally root position
- The dominant may be embellished by the cadential six-four (I6/4 over ^5)
- Three categories: authentic, half-cadential, and deceptive

# Musical Context
Cadential progressions are the harmonic foundation of cadential function in all theme types. They appear compressed (two measures) in tight-knit themes and expanded (four or more measures) in subordinate themes. The choice and elaboration of the pre-dominant element offers the greatest variety within the formula.

# Examples
- Basic authentic: I-II6-V-I (ex. 2.5a); the version with II6 is more typical of classical style than I-IV-V-I
- With cadential six-four: I-II6-I6/4-V-I (ex. 2.6)
- With chromatic pre-dominant: I-VII7/V-V-I or I-augmented sixth-V-I (ex. 2.7c-h)
- With initial tonic embellishment: I-V7(neighbor)-I-pre-dominant-V-I (ex. 2.8b)

# Common Confusions
- II6 is more characteristic than IV as the pre-dominant in classical cadential progressions, contrary to many textbook presentations.
- The cadential six-four is an embellishment of dominant function, not a tonic chord in second inversion.
- If the final tonic is inverted, the progression becomes deceptive or loses its cadential force.
