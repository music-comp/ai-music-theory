# Quick Definition
Caplin restricts the term "retransition" to those passages in the development whose primary function is to return the music to the home key -- not to the standing on the dominant (which is postcadential and occurs after the home key has already been achieved).

# Core Definition
Traditional theories normally recognize a "retransition" at the end of most development sections, especially in connection with the standing on the dominant. Caplin identifies a problem: by the time the standing on the dominant begins, the home key has already been achieved, confirmed by the half cadence (or dominant arrival). If "retransition" is to be used consistently, it should be applied before the standing on the dominant, in connection with where the modulation to the home key actually takes place. But this modulation usually occurs within a broader sequential passage of the core and rarely marks the beginning of a new unit. Therefore, Caplin restricts the term to those passages, separate from the core, whose primary function is to return the music back home and to prepare for the return of the main theme. Typically, a retransition is a complete phrase or full thematic unit following the cadential articulation of a development key.

# Prerequisites
- **Development section**: The formal context
- **Standing on the dominant**: The postcadential prolongation that is NOT the retransition
- **Development key**: The key from which the retransition departs

# Key Properties
1. Traditional usage equates retransition with the standing on the dominant -- Caplin rejects this
2. By the time the standing on the dominant begins, the home key is already confirmed
3. The retransition should be identified where the modulation to the home key actually occurs
4. Most often, this modulation happens within the core's sequential passage (not a separate unit)
5. Caplin restricts "retransition" to distinct passages whose PRIMARY function is returning home
6. A retransition follows the cadential articulation of a development key
7. It is typically a complete phrase or full thematic unit
8. It ends with an HC (or dominant arrival) of the home key, followed by standing on the dominant
9. A retransition may begin with a false recapitulation (main-theme material in the development key)
10. Not all developments have a distinct retransition

# Construction / Recognition
- Locate the cadential confirmation of a development key (PAC or HC)
- Identify the passage that modulates back to the home key (this is the retransition)
- Note whether it begins with a false recapitulation or false closing section
- Find the HC confirming the tonal return
- The standing on the dominant follows but is NOT part of the retransition
- If the core leads directly to the home-key dominant, there is no retransition proper

# Context & Application
This terminological clarification is pedagogically important because it helps students distinguish between (a) the modulation back to the home key (retransition proper) and (b) the postcadential dominant prolongation (standing on the dominant). The two have fundamentally different formal functions.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in B-flat, H. 41, i, mm. 85-96 (ex. 13.11): PAC in development key G minor at m. 85; false closing section becomes retransition; modulation to home key at m. 87; HC at m. 91; standing on dominant mm. 91-96
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in B-flat, K. 333, i, mm. 87-95 (ex. 13.12): home-key dominant introduced directly after development-key standing on dominant; retransition consists exclusively of the standing on the home-key dominant
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in G, Op. 14/2, i, mm. 98-124 (ex. 13.13): false recapitulation in development key (E-flat); modulates back to home key; HC at m. 107; substantial standing on dominant

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Development section (the formal context)
- Standing on the dominant (the postcadential prolongation that FOLLOWS the retransition)

## Enables
- Recapitulation (prepared by the retransition's return to the home key)

## Related
- False recapitulation (may begin the retransition)
- Typical formal plans of the development (retransition appears in Plan 3)

## Contrasts With
- Standing on the dominant (postcadential vs. transitional function)
- Traditional usage of "retransition" (which conflates the two)

# Common Errors
- Equating the standing on the dominant with the retransition (they are different functions)
- Applying "retransition" to every development that returns to the home key
- Confusing the false recapitulation (in the development key) with the actual recapitulation (in the home key)

# Common Confusions
- **Retransition vs. standing on the dominant**: The standing on the dominant is postcadential (the home key is already confirmed); the retransition is pre-cadential (it effects the modulation)
- **General vs. specific**: Most developments return to the home key (general retransition function), but only some have a distinct retransitional passage (specific)
- **False recapitulation**: Main-theme material that sounds recapitulatory but is in the wrong key; it initiates the retransition, not the recapitulation
