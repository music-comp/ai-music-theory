# Quick Definition
An alternative label for the small ternary form when its two-part character is emphasized through repeat signs: ||: A :||: B + A' :||.

# Formal Definition
The rounded binary is essentially the same form as the small ternary. Both contain exposition (A), contrasting middle (B), and recapitulation (A'). The term "rounded binary" is used when the repetition scheme gives prominence to a bipartite structure: A is immediately repeated, then B and A' are repeated together. The qualifier "rounded" recognizes the return of opening material. Since these repetitions produce a distinctly two-part design, many theorists have labeled it a "binary" form.

# Construction/Recognition
1. Same three functions as small ternary: A, B, A'
2. Repeat scheme: ||: A :||: B + A' :||
3. The two-part repeat structure somewhat obscures the tripartite functional organization
4. Repeated sections may be written out with ornamental variations

# Musical Context
Appears extensively in minuet and trio movements, theme-and-variation themes, and as main themes in various forms. Derives historically from baroque binary forms.

# Examples
- Most minuet and trio movements in Haydn, Mozart, and Beethoven
- Theme-and-variation themes

# Common Confusions
- Rounded binary and small ternary are the SAME form -- both have exposition, contrasting middle, and recapitulation
- Do not confuse with small binary, which genuinely lacks recapitulation
