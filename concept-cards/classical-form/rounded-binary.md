# Quick Definition
An alternative label for the small ternary form when its two-part character is emphasized through repeat signs: ||: A :||: B + A' :||.

# Core Definition
The rounded binary is essentially the same form as the small ternary. Both contain exposition (A), contrasting middle (B), and recapitulation (A'). The term "rounded binary" is used when the repetition scheme gives prominence to a bipartite structure: A is immediately repeated, then B and A' are repeated together. The qualifier "rounded" recognizes the return of opening material. The repetitions may be indicated with repeat signs or written out to introduce variations. From a functional point of view, the small ternary and rounded binary are identical forms.

# Prerequisites
- **Small ternary**: The form that the rounded binary instantiates with its specific repeat scheme

# Key Properties
1. Same three functions as small ternary: A, B, A'
2. Repeat scheme: ||: A :||: B + A' :||
3. The two-part repeat structure somewhat obscures the tripartite functional organization
4. Repeated sections may be written out with ornamental variations
5. Functionally identical to small ternary

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the three functional sections (A, B, A')
2. Note the repeat scheme packaging them into two parts
3. Written-out repetitions may introduce melodic, textural, or dynamic variations
4. All analytical observations about small ternary apply equally to rounded binary

# Context & Application
Appears extensively in minuet and trio movements, theme-and-variation themes, and as main themes in various forms. The historical debate about whether this form is fundamentally binary or ternary is one of the most vigorous in music theory history, resolved by Caplin through acknowledging both perspectives while emphasizing formal functionality.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in D, Hob. XVI:37, iii, mm. 1-20: with repeat signs
- Most minuet and trio movements in Haydn, Mozart, and Beethoven
- Theme-and-variation themes with repeat scheme

# Common Errors
- Treating rounded binary as a fundamentally different form from small ternary -- they are functionally identical
- Confusing with small binary, which genuinely lacks recapitulation
- Calling it "binary" without the "rounded" qualifier, which obscures the presence of recapitulation

# Common Confusions
- Rounded binary and small ternary are the SAME form -- both have exposition, contrasting middle, and recapitulation
- Do not confuse with small binary, which genuinely lacks recapitulation
- The debate about binary vs. ternary classification is resolved by acknowledging both organizational perspectives
