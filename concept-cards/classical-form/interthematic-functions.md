# Quick Definition
The formal functions that distinguish the various themes and themelike units within a movement, such as main theme, transition, and subordinate theme, defined by their relationship to tonality.

# Formal Definition
Interthematic functions are the formal roles that different themes or themelike units play within a movement. Just as intrathematic functions differentiate the parts of themes, interthematic functions differentiate the themes themselves. These functions occur in a conventionalized order depending on the full-movement form and give rise to a higher-level formal syntax. Their definition depends fundamentally on tonal relationships--which key each theme confirms, destabilizes, or establishes.

# Construction/Recognition
- Main theme: confirms the home key through cadential closure; most tight-knit
- Transition: destabilizes the home key and usually modulates to the subordinate key
- Subordinate theme: confirms the subordinate key; distinctly looser than main theme
- Each distinguished by tonal role, degree of tight-knit vs. loose organization, and phrase-structural design

# Musical Context
Interthematic functions are most prominent in sonata-form expositions: main theme -> transition -> subordinate theme (-> closing section). Other movement types have their own interthematic syntaxes.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i: main theme (mm. 1-8), transition (mm. 9-20), subordinate theme (mm. 21-48)

# Common Confusions
- Interthematic functions are defined primarily by tonal relationships (which key is confirmed or destabilized), while intrathematic functions are defined by local harmonic progressions.
