# Quick Definition
A main-theme construction found in expansive Beethoven works where the opening unit is enlarged beyond a typical compound basic idea, immediately repeated to form a large presentationlike unit, with a subsequent continuation that is "compressed" relative to the grouping size proposed by the presentation.

# Core Definition
In some expansive works by Beethoven, the main theme acquires an overall sentential form, but the initiating units are enlarged beyond what would normally be found in the compound basic idea of a compound sentence. The immediate repetition of this opening unit, occasionally sequential in nature, creates a large presentationlike unit, upon which a continuation follows. This continuation is often "compressed" in relation to the grouping size proposed by the presentation -- that is, the continuation occupies fewer measures than the presentation would lead one to expect. At times, the opening unit might be sufficiently large that it appears to close with its own cadence, but such a cadence should be seen as one of "limited scope," since it does not bring any real sense of closure for the sentential theme as a whole.

# Prerequisites
- **Sentence**: The theme type being expanded
- **Presentation function**: The function being enlarged
- **Compound sentence**: The closest conventional model
- **Main-theme function in sonata**: The formal context

# Key Properties
1. Opening unit enlarged beyond standard compound basic idea
2. Immediate repetition (possibly sequential) creates large presentationlike unit
3. Continuation is "compressed" relative to the presentation's size
4. Internal cadences may occur but are of "limited scope"
5. Never a PAC in the home key within the opening unit (that would close the theme)
6. Characteristic of expansive Beethoven works
7. The overall form is sentential despite the enormous proportions
8. The theme remains tight-knit relative to the subsequent subordinate theme

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify an opening unit larger than a standard compound basic idea (often 8-9+ measures)
- Look for immediate repetition of the entire unit (possibly with melodic variations)
- Note whether the continuation is shorter than the presentation would predict
- Check for internal cadences and determine if they are of "limited scope"
- Verify no PAC in the home key occurs within the presentation (only at the theme's end)

# Context & Application
The large-scale presentation is a hallmark of Beethoven's expansive style. It creates a main theme of extraordinary proportions while still maintaining the sentential principle of presentation followed by continuation. The compression of the continuation relative to the presentation creates a particular formal effect: the theme seems to accelerate toward its cadential goal after the extensive opening.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Violin Sonata in A minor ("Kreutzer"), Op. 47, i, mm. 19-45: 9-m. opening unit repeated (18 mm. total presentation), followed by 9-m. continuation "compressed" relative to the 18-m. presentation; internal progression in C major functions as tonicization of III, not a genuine cadence (Ch. 10, Ex. 10.6)

# Common Errors
- Treating internal cadences as genuine structural closures (they are of "limited scope")
- Analyzing the compressed continuation as a separate theme
- Interpreting the theme as two separate themes rather than a single expanded sentence

# Common Confusions
- **Limited-scope cadences**: Cadences within the opening unit do not close the theme; they function locally within the larger sentential structure
- **Compression is relative**: The continuation is "compressed" only relative to the enormous presentation -- it may still be quite long in absolute terms
- **Still tight-knit**: Despite its size, the large-scale presentation theme remains tighter than the subsequent subordinate theme
