# Quick Definition
The opening thematic unit of a sonata-form exposition that expresses the home key through cadential closure and displays the most tight-knit formal organization of any unit in the exposition.

# Formal Definition
The main theme expresses the home key through cadential closure. It is the most tight-knit unit in the exposition, characterized by harmonic-tonal stability, cadential confirmation, unity of melodic-motivic material, efficiency of functional expression, and symmetrical phrase groupings. It is typically built as a conventional theme type (sentence, period, small ternary, or hybrid).

# Construction/Recognition
- Confirms the home key through cadential closure (HC or PAC)
- Most tight-knit organization in the exposition
- Typically a conventional theme type
- Symmetrical phrase groupings (usually 4 + 4)
- Unified motivic material; nothing superfluous

# Musical Context
The main theme opens the sonata-form exposition and establishes the home key. Its tight-knit character contrasts with the looser transition and subordinate theme. Main themes in other movement types discussed in Chapters 13-17.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 1-8: model sentence in F minor, symmetrical 4 + 4, HC closure
