# Quick Definition
A fundamental distinction between formal passages that are compact, stable, and conventional (tight-knit) versus those that are extended, unstable, and less predictable (loose), defined by multiple interacting musical parameters.

# Core Definition
Tight-knit organization is characterized by "harmonic-tonal stability, cadential confirmation, unity of melodic-motivic material, efficiency of functional expression, and symmetrical phrase groupings." Loose organization is characterized by "harmonic-tonal instability, evasion or omission of cadence, diversity of melodic-motivic material, inefficiency or ambiguity of functional expression, and asymmetrical phrase groupings (arising through extensions, expansions, compressions, and interpolations)" (Ch. 1, p. 17).

# Prerequisites
- **Formal function** -- understanding what functional efficiency/ambiguity means
- **Grouping structure** -- understanding symmetrical vs. asymmetrical groupings

# Key Properties
1. Tight-knit: harmonic-tonal stability, cadential confirmation
2. Tight-knit: unified motivic material from the basic idea
3. Tight-knit: compact, efficient functional expression (nothing superfluous)
4. Tight-knit: symmetrical phrase groupings (4 + 4, 2 + 2 + 4)
5. Loose: harmonic instability, modulation, sequential progressions
6. Loose: cadential evasion, deception, or delay
7. Loose: diverse or new motivic material
8. Loose: extended or expanded phrases creating asymmetry
9. These are relative, not absolute, descriptions

# Construction / Recognition
- Compare a passage's organization to the normative tight-knit models (sentence, period)
- Identify loosening devices: extensions, expansions, harmonic instability, cadential evasion
- Assess degree of functional efficiency vs. ambiguity or redundancy
- Note symmetry vs. asymmetry in grouping structure

# Context & Application
In sonata form, the main theme is the most tight-knit unit, whereas the transition and subordinate theme are progressively looser. The development section is typically "the loosest part of an entire sonata movement," combining "harmonic-tonal instability with phrase-structural extensions and expansions" (Ch. 1, p. 21). Loose organization is not a deficiency but is deliberately appropriate to certain formal functions.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 1, p. 18): Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 1-8 (main theme): model sentence with symmetrical 4 + 4 grouping, tonic prolongation, unified motives, efficient functional expression.
**Example 2** (Ch. 1, pp. 19-21): Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 21-48 (subordinate theme): asymmetrical 4 + 8 + 4 + 5 + closing section grouping; dominant pedal undermining tonic, extended continuation, expanded cadential progression, cadential evasion.
**Example 3** (Ch. 1, pp. 18-19): Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 9-20 (transition): harmonic-tonal instability, modulation, asymmetrical 2 + 6 + 4 grouping, no full presentation phrase, cadential obscurity.

# Common Errors
- Treating tight-knit/loose as a binary rather than a spectrum
- Judging loose organization as compositionally inferior

# Common Confusions
- These are not binary categories but relative descriptions; a passage is tight-knit or loose in comparison to other passages
- Loose organization does not imply poor structure or compositional weakness; loosening techniques are deliberate and appropriate to certain formal functions
- It can be difficult to judge whether a transition or subordinate theme is "more loose" in a given exposition, but the development section is almost always the loosest part of the movement
