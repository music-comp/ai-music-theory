# Quick Definition
A fundamental distinction between formal passages that are compact, stable, and conventional (tight-knit) versus those that are extended, unstable, and less predictable (loose).

# Formal Definition
Tight-knit organization is characterized by harmonic-tonal stability, cadential confirmation, unity of melodic-motivic material, efficiency of functional expression, and symmetrical phrase groupings. Loose organization is characterized by harmonic-tonal instability, evasion or omission of cadence, diversity of melodic-motivic material, inefficiency or ambiguity of functional expression, and asymmetrical phrase groupings (arising through extensions, expansions, compressions, and interpolations).

# Construction/Recognition
Tight-knit features:
- Clear tonic prolongation and cadential closure
- Unified motivic material derived from the basic idea
- Compact, efficient functional expression (nothing superfluous)
- Symmetrical phrase groupings (4 + 4, 2 + 2 + 4)

Loose features:
- Harmonic instability, modulation, sequential progressions
- Cadential evasion, deception, or delay
- Diverse or new motivic material
- Extended or expanded phrases creating asymmetry

# Musical Context
In sonata form, the main theme is the most tight-knit unit, while the transition and subordinate theme are progressively looser. The development section is typically the loosest part of a movement, combining harmonic-tonal instability with phrase-structural extensions and expansions.

# Examples
- Tight-knit: Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 1-8 (main theme sentence, symmetrical 4 + 4)
- Loose: Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 21-48 (subordinate theme, asymmetrical 4 + 8 + 4 + 5 + closing section)

# Common Confusions
- These are not binary categories but relative descriptions; a passage is tight-knit or loose in comparison to other passages.
- Loose organization does not imply poor structure or compositional weakness. Loosening techniques are deliberate and appropriate to certain formal functions.
