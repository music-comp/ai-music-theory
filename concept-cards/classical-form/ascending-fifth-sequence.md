# Quick Definition
A sequential progression with ascending-fifth (or descending-fourth) root motion that entirely lacks the dominant-to-tonic functional drive of the descending-fifth sequence, typically moving from I to VI before breaking the pattern to proceed to pre-dominant harmony.

# Core Definition
"The strong functional drive exhibited by the descending fifth pattern is entirely absent in sequential progressions by ascending fifths" (Ch. 2, p. 31). "Most such sequences begin with tonic harmony and progress to the submediant, at which point the sequential chain is broken and the music proceeds to pre-dominant harmony (usually IV)" (Ch. 2, p. 31). The ascending-fifth motion reverses the dominant-to-tonic relationship, which is why it lacks functional expression.

# Prerequisites
- **Sequential progression** -- the broader category

# Key Properties
1. Root motion: consistently ascending fifths (or descending fourths)
2. Typical pattern: I-V-II-VI, then breaks to IV
3. Entirely lacks dominant-to-tonic functional implication
4. Sequential chain typically breaks at VI
5. Less common than descending-fifth sequences

# Construction / Recognition
- Ascending-fifth root motion
- Pattern usually breaks at VI, proceeding to IV

# Context & Application
Ascending-fifth sequences create harmonic motion without strong tonal implications. Less common than descending-fifth sequences.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 2, ex. 2.12): Basic pattern I-V-II-VI, breaking to IV.
