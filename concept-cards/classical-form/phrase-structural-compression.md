# Quick Definition
Phrase-structural compression shortens a phrase below its normative length while preserving its essential formal functions. In a period, both antecedent and consequent may be compressed (e.g., 3+3 instead of 4+4), maintaining symmetry despite the reduced proportions.

# Core Definition
A symmetrically altered period can result from the technique of phrase-structural compression. Both the antecedent and consequent are reduced below their normative four-measure lengths, yet the essential formal functions (basic idea, contrasting idea, cadence) remain identifiable within the compressed structure. The internal grouping of ideas within each compressed phrase may itself be asymmetrical (e.g., antecedent 2+1, consequent 1+2), creating a subtle counterpoint to the phrase-level symmetry.

# Construction / Recognition
1. Phrases shorter than the normative four measures
2. Essential functions still present: basic idea, contrasting idea (or equivalent), cadence
3. Typically applied symmetrically (both phrases compressed equally)
4. Internal idea groupings may be asymmetrical despite phrase-level symmetry
5. The compression is achieved by shortening individual components (ideas, cadential material)

# Context & Application
Phrase-structural compression creates urgency and conciseness within a period. It is the opposite of expansion and extension — rather than adding material, it condenses the essential formal elements into a smaller time span. Compressed periods are relatively uncommon but demonstrate that Caplin's formal categories are flexible enough to accommodate non-normative phrase lengths.

# Examples
- Beethoven, String Quartet in G, Op. 18/2, ii, mm. 1-6: symmetrical 3+3 period; antecedent has 2+1 grouping (two-measure basic idea, one-measure HC); consequent has 1+2 grouping (one measure of basic idea broken off, two-measure contrasting idea with PAC); the 3+3 symmetry at phrase level complemented by asymmetrical 2+1, 1+2 at the idea level

# Common Errors
- Assuming compression eliminates formal functions (it condenses them, not removes them)
- Confusing compression with truncation (compression preserves formal completeness)
- Missing the interplay between phrase-level symmetry and idea-level asymmetry

# Common Confusions
- Compression does not eliminate formal functions — it condenses them into fewer measures; all essential components must still be identifiable
- Do not confuse compression with abbreviation or truncation — compression maintains formal completeness, while truncation would leave functions unfulfilled
- The internal asymmetry of ideas (2+1, 1+2) within a symmetrical phrase structure (3+3) operates at a different analytical level than the phrase-level symmetry
