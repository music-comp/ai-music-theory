# Quick Definition
The coda's function of resolving compositional processes or "problems" initiated earlier in the movement -- such as cadential ambiguity, modal disturbance, or tonal displacement -- that could not be appropriately resolved in the recapitulation.

# Core Definition
Many compositional processes initiated in a work imply particular modes of continuation. Some implications are realized immediately; others are realized only much later; and still others may never be realized at all. The coda gives the composer the last opportunity to realize an earlier implication, often one that would not have found an appropriate realization earlier in the movement. These implications usually arise in relation to the main theme, particularly a "problem" or "disturbance" that is not resolved until the coda. Types of implications include: tonal displacement (main theme in subdominant implying later home-key statement), cadential ambiguity (unclear cadence implying later unequivocal closure), modal disturbance (unusual mode shift implying correction), and melodic/rhythmic implications (intense material implying a simpler version).

# Prerequisites
- **Coda**: The section realizing the implications
- **Compensatory functions**: The broader category

# Key Properties
1. The most sophisticated of the compensatory functions
2. Implications usually arise in the main theme
3. The coda is the last opportunity for realization
4. Some implications are never realized (and need not be)
5. Types include: tonal displacement, cadential ambiguity, modal disturbance, melodic/rhythmic implications
6. Requires tracing compositional processes across the entire movement
7. The resolution often involves presenting main-theme material in a corrected or simplified form

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify compositional "problems" or anomalies in the exposition (unusual harmony, modal disturbance, cadential ambiguity, tonal displacement)
- Trace whether this problem is addressed in the development or recapitulation
- Look for its resolution in the coda
- The resolution often involves the main-theme material presented in a way that corrects the original disturbance

# Context & Application
The realization of implications is the most analytically demanding of the compensatory functions. It requires attention to compositional processes spanning the entire movement. Not every anomaly generates an implication that must be realized; some remain permanently unrealized.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Overture to Coriolanus, Op. 62 (ex. 12.6): (1) main theme recapitulated in subdominant (m. 152) implies later home-key restatement, realized at m. 276; (2) cadential ambiguity at exposition's main-theme close (HC at m. 13 or elided PAC at m. 15?) implies later unequivocal closure, realized at m. 296
- Mozart, Symphony No. 39, K. 543, ii (ex. 12.10): unusual modal shift to minor in exposition's main theme (mm. 22-25) implies the theme may return without this disturbance; coda version (m. 144) eliminates explicit modal shift; also restores PAC deleted from the recapitulation; the pitch Cb from mm. 22-25 is itself realized as significant tonal region (Cb/B minor) at recapitulation m. 96
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in C Minor, Op. 10/1, ii (ex. 12.11): exposition's continuation with intense rhythmic and melodic activity (double-dotted figure, rapid ascents) implies a simpler continuation; coda (mm. 95-102) provides the "closing down" version with linear descent in even note values

# Common Errors
- Assuming every anomaly must be realized (some remain permanently unrealized)
- Treating realizations as obvious -- they often require careful analytical attention

# Common Confusions
- **Not always obvious**: The realization is not always a literal correction; it may involve a transformed version
- **Not exhaustive**: Not every compositional anomaly generates an implication that must be realized
- **Analytical sophistication**: This function requires tracing processes across the entire movement; it is not always apparent on first hearing
- **Multiple realizations**: A single coda may realize several different implications simultaneously
