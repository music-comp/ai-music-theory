# Quick Definition
A minority variant of minuet form (~10% of cases) constructed as a two-part form resembling small binary, where the second part lacks a recapitulatory return of the opening basic idea.

# Formal Definition
Binary minuet form is a two-part form, each part repeated, that resembles the small binary form. Part 1 resembles the A section of regular minuet form, except that it more frequently closes with a HC. Part 2 often contains material that functions like a contrasting middle but does not include a recapitulatory articulation (a return of the opening basic idea in the home key). Although the opening does not return within the normal boundaries of the form, this material may reappear in a closing section or coda, fulfilling a compensatory function.

# Construction/Recognition
- Two parts, each repeated
- Part 1 resembles the A section of regular minuet form but more often ends with a HC
- Part 2 has contrasting-middle characteristics but no recapitulatory return of the opening idea
- The opening idea may return in a closing section or coda (compensatory function)
- Occurs in approximately 10% of component minuets

# Musical Context
Binary minuet form is less common than the standard ternary minuet form but appears with enough frequency to represent a legitimate alternative. The absence of a recapitulation within the form's normal boundaries can be compensated for by a closing section or coda that brings back the opening material.

# Examples
- Mozart, Serenade in D ("Haffner"), K. 250/248b, iii, mm. 1-40: Part 1 closes at m. 12 with an early PAC and a subordinate-key confirmation; Part 2 begins with a standing on the dominant (mm. 13-24); the music of mm. 5-8 returns at mm. 25-28 but the opening phrase reappears only at the end (mm. 37-40) as a cadential unit that closes the preceding material

# Common Confusions
- Binary minuet form should not be confused with a minuet form whose recapitulation is merely compressed. In binary minuet form, there is no recapitulatory return of the opening basic idea within the form's normal boundaries at all.
- The reappearance of the opening material in a closing section or coda is a compensatory function, not a true recapitulation; it occurs after the form's structural close.
