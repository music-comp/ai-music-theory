# Quick Definition
A harmonic progression in which chords are linked by a consistent intervallic root pattern and melodic-contrapuntal design, projecting harmonic mobility independent of normal functional syntax.

# Formal Definition
Sequential progressions involve harmonies arranged according to a consistent intervallic pattern among the individual voices of the chords. Although some sequential progressions exhibit a degree of harmonic functionality among their constituent chords, this aspect is secondary to their fundamental purpose--to move the music away from, or return it to, a particular harmonic function or tonal center. A sequential progression normally begins with a chord that has a definite harmonic function within a key; the subsequent chords are linked by a particular melodic-contrapuntal pattern and consistent root motion; the final chord restores a clear functional meaning. Sequential progressions are classified by the interval of root motion: descending or ascending fifths, thirds, or seconds (six categories).

# Construction/Recognition
- Consistent root-motion pattern throughout the progression
- Consistent melodic-contrapuntal voice-leading pattern
- Begins and ends on functionally clear harmonies
- Six categories: descending fifth, ascending fifth, descending third, ascending third, descending second, ascending second
- The same root progression can yield different contrapuntal patterns depending on voice distribution
- May be embellished by chromatic alterations, added dissonances, chord inversions

# Musical Context
Sequential progressions are especially suitable for destabilizing harmonic activity. They are characteristic of continuation function, contrasting middles, transitions, and development sections. Their open-ended, forward-driving quality contrasts with the stability of prolongational progressions and the goal-directed quality of cadential progressions.

# Common Confusions
- Sequential progressions weaken harmonic-tonal clarity despite having local functional implications at each link. The overall direction remains somewhat in doubt until the sequence concludes.
- The same root-motion pattern can produce different contrapuntal patterns; sequences are more reliably classified by root motion than by voice-leading pattern.
