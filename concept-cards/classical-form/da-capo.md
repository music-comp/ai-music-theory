# Quick Definition
The restatement of the minuet proper following the trio, indicated in the score by "Menuet da capo" rather than being written out.

# Formal Definition
The da capo is the third part of minuet/trio form, in which the minuet proper is performed again after the trio. It is not usually written out (unless ornamental variations are desired). Instead, "Menuet da capo" or a variant is indicated. The da capo represents a literal restatement, not a recapitulation. A coda may follow.

# Construction/Recognition
- Indicated by "Menuet da capo" in the score
- The minuet proper is performed again exactly as before
- Not usually written out
- May be followed by a coda

# Musical Context
The da capo derives from baroque suite convention and is the standard concluding element of minuet/trio form.

# Examples
- Haydn, String Quartet in C, Op. 20/2, iii: Truncated trio leads directly to "Menuetto D.C."

# Common Confusions
- The da capo is a literal restatement, not a "recapitulation" in the sonata-form sense.
