# Quick Definition
A theme-type consisting of three sections -- exposition (A), contrasting middle (B), and recapitulation (A') -- that functions as a self-contained formal unit within larger movement forms.

# Core Definition
The small ternary is one of the most important forms in classical instrumental music. It consists of three main sections expressing the formal functions of exposition (A), contrasting middle (B), and recapitulation (A'). The exposition is a tight-knit theme (most often a period) ending with a PAC in the home key or a subordinate key. The contrasting middle emphasizes dominant harmony and is more loosely organized. The recapitulation brings back the basic idea in the home key and closes with a PAC. Two basic notions lie at the heart of this design: (1) a relatively closed thematic unit is juxtaposed with a structurally open unit of contrasting content and formal organization, and (2) the original unit is brought back in a manner that ensures complete closure. When sections are repeated with the scheme ||: A :||: B + A' :||, the form is traditionally termed a rounded binary.

# Prerequisites
- **Sentence**: One of the possible theme types for the A section
- **Period**: The most common theme type for the A section
- **Hybrid themes**: Alternative theme types for the A section

# Key Properties
1. Three sections: A (exposition), B (contrasting middle), A' (recapitulation)
2. A section: tight-knit, closes with PAC (home key or subordinate key), usually a period
3. B section: loose, emphasizes dominant harmony, ends with HC or dominant arrival
4. A' section: returns basic idea in home key, closes with PAC
5. B section never elides with A or A' sections
6. When A modulates, A' must be tonally adjusted to remain in home key
7. Rounded binary version: ||: A :||: B + A' :||
8. From a functional point of view, small ternary and rounded binary are identical forms
9. The functional terms (exposition, contrasting middle, recapitulation) relate to those used in sonata form

# Construction / Recognition
1. **Exposition (A)**: Tight-knit theme (period, sentence, or hybrid), 8 measures normatively, closes with PAC
2. **Contrasting middle (B)**: Looser organization, emphasizes dominant harmony, ends with HC or dominant arrival; organized as standing on the dominant, continuation phrase, or loosely sentential
3. **Recapitulation (A')**: Returns basic idea in home key, closes with PAC; may be exact, compressed, or expanded
4. B section never elides with A or A' sections
5. When A modulates, A' must be tonally adjusted to remain in home key
6. The subordinate key is usually V in major, III (relative major) in minor

# Context & Application
The small ternary serves as the theme for most theme-and-variations movements, the main theme of sonatas, rondos, and concertos, and the basis for minuet/trio forms. Its tonal plan (exposition establishing key, contrasting middle destabilizing, recapitulation confirming) provides the model for full-movement sonata form. The distinction between tight-knit (A) and loose (B) organization is introduced here and becomes a central analytical concept throughout the study of larger forms.

# Examples
- Mozart, Eine kleine Nachtmusik, K. 525, ii: nonmodulating period as A; standing on the dominant as B; compressed consequent as A'
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in D, Hob. XVI:37, iii, mm. 1-20: modulating hybrid as A; continuation with model-sequence as B; tonally adjusted A'
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, ii, mm. 1-24: nonmodulating sentence as A; standing on dominant as B; expanded A' with new continuation and deceptive cadence
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in D, Op. 28, ii, mm. 1-22: modulating period as A; extended standing on dominant as B; compressed A' with sequence

## Contrasts With
- Small binary: small ternary HAS recapitulation; small binary does NOT
- Compound period: both are 16+ measures, but compound period lacks contrasting middle

# Common Errors
- Calling the dominant-to-tonic resolution between B and A' an "authentic cadence" -- it represents harmonic initiation of the recapitulation, not cadential closure
- Placing an HC label at the end of a standing on the dominant -- if there is no harmonic change (no cadential progression), there can be no cadence
- Assuming the A section must remain in the home key -- many A sections modulate to a subordinate key

# Common Confusions
- The small ternary and rounded binary are essentially the same form -- both contain exposition, contrasting middle, and recapitulation
- The resolution of dominant at the end of B to tonic at the start of A' is NOT an authentic cadence -- it represents harmonic initiation of the recapitulation
- A standing on the dominant does NOT end with a cadence (no harmonic progression = no cadence)
- When the A section modulates and is then repeated, do not call the final cadence a "reinterpreted HC" -- it is a PAC in the subordinate key
