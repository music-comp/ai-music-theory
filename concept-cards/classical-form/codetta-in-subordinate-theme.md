# Quick Definition
A short postcadential unit that prolongs the tonic following an authentic cadence in a subordinate theme, typically featuring a tonic pedal, prolongation of the tonic scale-degree in the soprano, and conventionalized melodic patterns.

# Core Definition
A codetta is a postcadential unit that prolongs the tonic at the end of an authentic cadence, often within a recessive dynamic that helps dissipate accumulated energy. Multiple codettas form a closing section. Codetta characteristics include: tonic pedal in the bass, prolongation of the tonic scale-degree (1) in the soprano, and use of conventionalized melodic material (scalar or arpeggiated patterns). Codettas can also establish motivic references, especially to the main theme. In closing sections, subsequent codettas are usually shorter than preceding ones, creating fragmentation. The codetta character may also appear at the beginning of a false closing section.

# Prerequisites
- **Closing section**: The formal context for codettas
- **Authentic cadence**: The cadence the codetta follows

# Key Properties
1. Follows an authentic cadence (usually PAC)
2. Tonic pedal in the bass
3. Tonic scale-degree prolonged in soprano
4. Conventionalized melodic patterns (scales, arpeggios) or motivic references
5. Often within recessive dynamic (sometimes progressive for decisive close)
6. Typically short: 1-4 measures
7. Often repeated, then followed by shorter codettas (fragmentation)
8. Subdominant emphasis as characteristic embellishment of tonic pedal

# Construction / Recognition
- Locate following a PAC in the subordinate key
- Note tonic pedal, 1 prolongation in soprano, and conventionalized patterns
- Track progressive shortening across multiple codettas
- Distinguish from thematic material (codettas lack presentation, continuation, cadential functions)
- Watch for false closing sections where codetta character proves to be the beginning of a new theme

# Context & Application
Codettas appear most regularly after subordinate themes, where they form the closing section. They are more extensive after subordinate themes than after main themes due to the greater energy built up in achieving the subordinate-theme cadential goal. The codetta character is important because it signals postcadential function; when this signal proves false, a false closing section results.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in C, Hob. XVI:35, i, mm. 63-67: codettas derived from main-theme basic idea -- two 2-measure codettas followed by fragmentary 1-measure codetta
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 31/3, ii, mm. 50-59: extended codetta passage emphasizing root-position tonic

# Common Errors
- Treating codettas as thematic material (they lack the formal functions of a theme)
- Confusing subdominant emphasis in codettas with a sign of modulation
- Failing to note the progressive shortening across successive codettas

# Common Confusions
- **Codetta vs. theme**: A codetta is a POSTCADENTIAL function, not a theme -- it lacks presentation, continuation, cadential functions
- **Codetta vs. false closing section**: Material with codetta character may actually begin a new subordinate theme; only subsequent events reveal the true function
- **Subdominant emphasis**: The subdominant in codettas is a characteristic tonic embellishment, not a modulatory sign
