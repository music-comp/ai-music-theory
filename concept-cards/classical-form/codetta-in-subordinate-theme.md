# Quick Definition
A short postcadential unit that prolongs the tonic following an authentic cadence, typically featuring a tonic pedal, prolongation of the tonic scale-degree in the soprano, and conventionalized melodic patterns.

# Formal Definition
A codetta is a postcadential unit that prolongs the tonic at the end of an authentic cadence, often within the context of a recessive dynamic that helps dissipate the energy built up in achieving the cadence. Multiple codettas form a closing section. The codetta's characteristics include a tonic pedal in the bass, prolongation of the tonic scale-degree (^1) in the soprano, and use of conventionalized melodic material such as scalar or arpeggiated patterns. Codettas can also establish motivic references, especially to the main theme. In closing sections, subsequent codettas are usually shorter than preceding ones, creating fragmentation. The codetta character may also appear at the beginning of a false closing section.

# Construction/Recognition
1. Follows an authentic cadence (usually PAC)
2. Tonic pedal in the bass
3. Tonic scale-degree prolonged in soprano
4. Conventionalized melodic patterns (scales, arpeggios) or motivic references
5. Often within a recessive dynamic (sometimes progressive for decisive close)
6. Typically short: 1-4 measures
7. Often repeated immediately, then followed by shorter codettas (fragmentation)
8. Emphasizes subdominant as a characteristic embellishment of the tonic pedal

# Musical Context
Codettas appear most regularly after subordinate themes, where they form the closing section. They also appear after main-theme expositions in small ternaries. In subordinate-theme contexts, the codetta character is particularly important because it signals postcadential function -- when this signal proves false (and the material develops into a new subordinate theme), a false closing section results. The codetta's recessive quality contrasts with the progressive dynamics typically associated with the preceding cadential function.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in C, Hob. XVI:35, i, mm. 63-67 (codettas derived from main-theme basic idea: two 2-measure codettas followed by fragmentary 1-measure codetta)
- Haydn, Symphony No. 100, iii, mm. 9-10 (closing section after exposition: 1-measure codetta repeated)
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 31/3, ii, mm. 50-59 (extended codetta passage emphasizing root-position tonic)

# Common Confusions
- A codetta is a POSTCADENTIAL function, not a theme -- it does not contain the formal functions (presentation, continuation, cadential) of a true theme
- Material with codetta character may actually begin a new subordinate theme (false closing section) -- only subsequent developments reveal the true function
- The subdominant emphasis in codettas is a characteristic embellishment, not a sign of modulation
- Codettas following subordinate themes are much more common and extensive than those following main themes
