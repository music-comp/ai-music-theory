# Quick Definition
A nonconventional main theme beginning with a long tonic pedal and heraldic, fanfare-style gestures that obscure clear basic idea and repetition structure, making classification as a standard theme type difficult -- especially common in symphonies and overtures.

# Core Definition
Some main themes begin with a long tonic pedal, over which may be found gestures that evoke a heraldic, fanfare style. Such powerful passages are ideal for projecting a strong opening, especially in the public genres of symphony or overture. They often obscure, however, a clear sense of basic idea and its repetition, or the juxtaposition of a basic idea with a contrasting idea. The lack of a conventional initiating function thus makes it difficult to classify such a theme as one of the standard types. The theme may have a general sentential quality (obvious cadential function, general acceleration of rhythmic activity) but the distinction between presentation and continuation functions is difficult to draw.

# Key Properties
1. Opens with a long tonic pedal
2. Features heraldic, fanfare-style gestures (often in unisons/octaves)
3. Obscures clear basic idea demarcation and repetition
4. Especially common in symphonies and overtures (public genres)
5. General sentential quality (acceleration, obvious cadential function)
6. Presentation and continuation functions are not clearly distinguishable
7. The powerful opening projects a strong formal beginning despite nonconventionality

# Construction / Recognition
- Look for extended tonic pedal at the opening
- Note unison/octave fanfare gestures
- Attempt to identify basic idea boundaries -- if obscured, the theme is likely nonconventional
- Recognize the general sentential quality without insisting on specific functional labels
- The cadential function at the theme's end is usually obvious

# Context & Application
Tonic-pedal fanfare themes are particularly associated with the public, ceremonial character of symphonic first movements. The powerful unison gestures project confidence and authority, making them ideal opening statements. The analytical difficulty they pose is not a compositional weakness but a reflection of their rhetorical purpose: they are designed to create impact, not to follow a textbook formal plan.

# Examples
- Haydn, Symphony No. 97 in C, i, mm. 14-25 (Ch. 10, Ex. 10.4): all orchestral forces in unisons and octaves; fanfare gestures over tonic pedal; cadential function obvious at m. 21; presentation and continuation indistinguishable

# Common Errors
- Forcing presentation/continuation labels onto a theme where these functions are indistinguishable
- Confusing the fanfare opening with an introduction (the theme has cadential substance)
- Treating the tonic pedal as harmonically uninteresting (it establishes a powerful sense of key)

# Common Confusions
- **Not an introduction**: Despite the powerful, rhetorical character, the theme has cadential substance and functions as a genuine main theme
- **Sentential quality without sentence form**: The acceleration and clear cadential ending create a sentential quality, but the theme is not a genuine sentence
- **Genre association**: Fanfare themes are most common in symphonies and overtures; they are less common in chamber and solo genres
