# Quick Definition
A transition that begins with codetta-like material following the main theme's PAC, initially suggesting a closing section but retrospectively understood as the presentation phase of the transition.

# Core Definition
A main theme ending with a PAC can be followed by codetta-like material. On occasion, such codettas merge directly into material more typical of a transition. In these cases, the codettas appear at first to have postcadential function but are then understood retrospectively to initiate (usually as a presentation) the transition proper. The grouping structure helps determine whether codetta material forms a genuine closing section or a false closing section beginning the transition. As a general rule, if codettas are followed by a LARGER unit, they form a genuine closing section independent of the transition. If followed by a SMALLER unit (fragmentation), they likely form a false closing section beginning the transition.

# Prerequisites
- **Transition**: The formal function being initiated
- **False closing section**: The concept of codetta material that proves to be something else
- **Codetta**: The unit type being reinterpreted

# Key Properties
1. Main theme ends with PAC
2. Codetta-like material follows: tonic pedal, tonic scale-degree prolongation, recessive character
3. Material merges into transition function instead of remaining postcadential
4. Retrospective reinterpretation: codettas function as presentation (basic idea + repetition) for the transition
5. Grouping-structure test: codettas followed by fragmentation (smaller units) = false closing section beginning transition
6. Codettas followed by larger unit = genuine closing section
7. The codetta character may include tonic pedal with subdominant emphasis

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify codetta-like material following the main theme's PAC
- Apply the grouping-structure test: are subsequent units smaller (fragmentation) or larger?
- If smaller: the codettas are a false closing section beginning the transition
- If larger: the codettas are a genuine closing section; transition begins with the larger unit
- Note the moment when codetta character gives way to transition character

# Context & Application
The false closing section at the beginning of a transition is a subtle formal ambiguity. The listener initially hears the codettas as winding down after the main theme, only to realize they have set up the transition. The technique creates a smooth connection between main theme and transition, avoiding an abrupt character change.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in C Minor, Op. 10/1, iii, mm. 8-18 (ex. 9.5): PAC at m. 8; codetta-like material mm. 8-11 with tonic pedal and subdominant emphasis; at m. 12, fragmentation and rhythmic acceleration express continuation, revealing mm. 8-11 as presentation
- Mozart, Piano Trio in D Minor, K. 442, iii, mm. 16-26 (ex. 9.4): PAC at m. 16; codetta-like idea repeated; at m. 24, V7/VI signals modulatory function; codettas reinterpreted as transition beginning

# Common Errors
- Recognizing the false closing section in real-time rather than retrospectively
- Ignoring the grouping-structure test
- Treating all codetta-like material after a PAC as a genuine closing section

# Common Confusions
- **Retrospective recognition**: The false closing section is only recognized in retrospect
- **Grouping-structure test**: Fragmentation following codettas signals continuation (transition); a larger unit signals a separate beginning
- **Same material, different function**: The codetta material itself may be identical in both interpretations; the difference lies in what follows
