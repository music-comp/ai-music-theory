# Quick Definition
A transition that begins with codetta-like material following the main theme's PAC, initially suggesting a closing section but retrospectively understood as the presentation phase of the transition.

# Formal Definition
A main theme ending with a PAC can be followed by a closing section consisting of codettas. On occasion, such codettas merge directly into material that is more typical of a transition. In these cases, the codettas appear at first to have a postcadential function in relation to the main theme, but they are then understood retrospectively to initiate (usually as a presentation) the transition proper. The grouping structure helps determine whether codetta material forms a genuine closing section or a false closing section beginning the transition. As a general rule, if codettas are followed by a LARGER unit, they belong to a separate closing section; if followed by a SMALLER unit (fragmentation), they likely begin the transition.

# Construction/Recognition
1. Main theme ends with PAC
2. Codetta-like material follows: tonic pedal, tonic scale-degree prolongation, recessive character
3. Material merges into transition function instead of remaining postcadential
4. Retrospective reinterpretation: codettas function as presentation (basic idea + repetition) for the transition
5. **Grouping-structure test**: If codettas are followed by fragmentation (smaller units), they begin the transition; if followed by a larger unit, they form a genuine closing section
6. The codetta character may include tonic pedal with subdominant emphasis (a typical codetta paradigm)

# Musical Context
The false closing section at the beginning of a transition is a subtle formal ambiguity. The listener initially hears the codettas as winding down after the main theme, only to realize that they have actually set up the transition. The technique creates a smooth, almost seamless connection between the main theme and the transition, avoiding an abrupt change of character. The retrospective reinterpretation adds a layer of formal sophistication to the exposition.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in C Minor, Op. 10/1, iii, mm. 8-18 (main theme ends with PAC at m. 8; codetta-like material in mm. 8-11 with tonic pedal and subdominant emphasis; at m. 12, fragmentation and rhythmic acceleration express continuation function, revealing mm. 8-11 as the presentation of the transition)
- Mozart, Piano Trio in D Minor, K. 442, iii, mm. 16-26 (PAC at m. 16; new codetta-like idea repeated; V^7/VI at m. 24 signals modulatory function; codettas reinterpreted as transition presentation)

# Common Confusions
- The false closing section is recognized only RETROSPECTIVELY -- at the moment of hearing, it genuinely sounds like a closing section
- The grouping-structure test is a useful heuristic: fragmentation following the codettas signals continuation (transition), while a larger unit signals a new formal beginning (separate from the closing section)
- A genuine closing section to the main theme is distinct from a false closing section -- the former truly concludes the main theme, while the latter begins the transition
- The codetta material itself may be musically identical in both interpretations; the difference lies in what follows
