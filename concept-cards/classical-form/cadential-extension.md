# Quick Definition
A loosening technique in which cadential function is extended because a promised perfect authentic cadence fails to materialize, motivating additional cadential units to achieve the requisite closure.

# Core Definition
Cadential extension occurs when the expected PAC of a subordinate theme is not realized, prompting one or more additional cadential attempts. The PAC can fail through five mechanisms: (1) an imperfect authentic cadence (IAC) appears instead, (2) a deceptive cadence (DC) replaces the expected tonic, (3) an evaded cadence thwarts the resolution, (4) an abandoned cadence disrupts the cadential progression itself, or (5) rarely, a genuine PAC appears but the phrase is then repeated to bring the same cadence again. Following the failed cadence, the subsequent music may restate prior continuation or cadential material, or introduce a new continuation, before attempting cadential closure again.

# Prerequisites
- **Cadential function**: The formal function being extended
- **Subordinate theme**: The primary context for cadential extension
- **Authentic cadence**: The expected goal that fails to materialize

# Key Properties
1. The implied PAC fails through one of five specific mechanisms
2. One or more additional cadential phrases follow the failure
3. Previous material (continuation or cadential) may be restated, sometimes with adjustments
4. New continuation material may appear before the next cadential attempt
5. Multiple failures can chain together, creating an extended cadential area
6. The eventual PAC is typically more emphatic than the initially expected one
7. The technique is particularly suited to subordinate themes, less common in main themes
8. Hierarchy of drama: evaded > deceptive > IAC > abandoned (in terms of immediacy)

# Construction / Recognition
- An implied PAC fails through IAC, DC, evaded cadence, abandoned cadence, or repeated PAC
- Following the failure, cadential material is restated or new continuation appears
- Multiple failures may chain together
- The evaded cadence is the most dramatic technique (no closure achieved)
- The deceptive cadence provides partial closure (substitute harmony groups backward)
- The IAC provides partial melodic closure (harmony resolves but melody lands on 3)
- The abandoned cadence disrupts the progression before resolution (least immediate drama)

# Context & Application
Cadential extension is one of the most important loosening techniques for subordinate themes because it directly dramatizes the process of confirming the subordinate key. The struggle to achieve cadential closure creates formal tension resolved only with the final PAC. Evaded cadences seldom appear in main themes; the techniques of partial closure (IAC, DC) are more typical of main-theme cadential extension.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Overture to Coriolanus, Op. 62, mm. 102-118: IAC at m. 110 extended by continuation repetition leading to PAC at m. 114
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in A-flat, Op. 26, iv, mm. 42-48: DC at mm. 44 and 46, then PAC at m. 48
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in C, Hob. XVI:35, i, mm. 58-62: evaded cadence at m. 60, "one more time" to PAC at m. 62
- Haydn, String Quartet in B Minor, Op. 64/2, i, mm. 26-34: abandoned cadence at m. 28, new cadential progression achieves PAC at m. 34

- Cadential expansion (enlarges the progression itself rather than delaying its resolution)

# Common Errors
- Confusing cadential extension (delayed PAC) with cadential expansion (enlarged progression)
- Treating an IAC followed by PAC as a period (lacking other periodic characteristics)
- Failing to distinguish evaded cadence from deceptive cadence

# Common Confusions
- **Extension vs. expansion**: Extension delays the PAC through failed cadences; expansion enlarges cadential function through expanded progressions. Both can operate simultaneously
- **Evaded vs. deceptive cadence**: In DC, a partial goal IS reached (substitute groups backward); in evaded cadence, NO goal is reached (event groups forward as new beginning)
- **"One more time" vs. repetition after DC**: Repeating material after an IAC or DC is NOT "one more time" technique -- those cadences achieve partial closure, making the aesthetic fundamentally different
