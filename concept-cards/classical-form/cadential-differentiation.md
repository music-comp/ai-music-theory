# Quick Definition
Cadential differentiation is the principle that the consequent phrase must close with a stronger cadence than the antecedent. This pairing of weak-then-strong cadences (e.g., HC followed by PAC) is the defining structural principle of the period form.

# Formal Definition
Essential to the concept of the period is the idea that a musical unit of partial cadential closure (antecedent) is repeated so as to produce a stronger cadential closure (consequent). Cadential differentiation operates on two levels: (1) the *type* of cadence — PAC is stronger than IAC, which is stronger than HC; and (2) the *temporal weight* of the cadential progression — the consequent's cadential progression typically begins earlier and spans more of the phrase than the antecedent's. The HC is the most common antecedent cadence because it maximizes the sense of cadential differentiation with the consequent's PAC.

# Construction/Recognition
1. **Cadential hierarchy** (weak to strong): HC < IAC < PAC
2. **Typical pairings**: HC (antecedent) + PAC (consequent) — most common; HC + IAC — rare; IAC + PAC — less common
3. The consequent's cadential progression usually begins earlier than the antecedent's
4. The consequent's pre-dominant and dominant harmonies are shifted backward to accommodate the penultimate dominant (authentic cadence) vs. the ultimate dominant (HC)
5. The contrasting idea of the consequent may be supported entirely by the cadential progression
6. Greater temporal weight in the consequent's cadential progression strengthens the sense of closure

# Musical Context
Cadential differentiation is what gives the period its characteristic sense of question-and-answer, incompleteness-and-completion. Without it, two successive phrases of equal closure would lack the motivating force that binds antecedent and consequent into a unified theme. The principle also applies to compound (sixteen-measure) periods and to the larger periodic relationship between exposition and recapitulation.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Concerto in F, K. 459, i, mm. 1-8: antecedent ends HC (m. 4), consequent's cadential progression (II6 at m. 7) begins earlier than antecedent's (VI as pre-dominant at m. 3), creating greater cadential weight
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in A, K. 331/300i, i: both antecedent and consequent display cadential differentiation through type and temporal weight

# Common Confusions
- Cadential differentiation involves both cadence *type* and cadential *weight* — a PAC following a HC is differentiated not only by type but also by the greater temporal span devoted to the cadential progression
- IAC + PAC is a valid but uncommon cadential differentiation — the IAC is not as "weak" as the HC, so the contrast is less pronounced
- HC + IAC is possible but rare — the IAC is only slightly stronger than the HC
