# Quick Definition
The principle that formal units can express varying degrees of cadential closure, enabling the period form's organization as a weak-cadence antecedent demanding completion by a stronger-cadence consequent.

# Core Definition
"Formal units can express varying degrees of cadential closure," which "allows for the possibility of creating thematic organization based largely on such cadential differentiation. If an initial unit ending with a weak cadence is repeated and brought to a fuller cadential close, then we can say, following traditional usage, that the first unit is an antecedent to the following consequent" (Ch. 1, p. 12). The cadential hierarchy runs from weakest to strongest: HC < IAC < PAC. The most common pairing is HC (antecedent) followed by PAC (consequent).

# Prerequisites
- **Authentic cadence** -- understanding PAC and IAC
- **Half cadence** -- understanding the weakest cadential closure

# Key Properties
1. Cadential hierarchy: HC < IAC < PAC
2. The organizing principle of the period form
3. Most common pairing: HC (antecedent) + PAC (consequent)
4. The weak cadence creates structural incompleteness demanding continuation
5. The stronger cadence provides the closure that completes the theme

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the cadence type closing each phrase
- Verify that the second phrase provides a stronger cadence than the first
- The difference in cadence strength is what binds antecedent and consequent into a unified theme

# Context & Application
Cadential differentiation gives the period its characteristic "question-and-answer" quality. Without it, two successive phrases of equal closure would lack the motivating force that binds them into a unified theme. The principle also operates at larger scales (exposition vs. recapitulation). Detailed treatment in Chapter 4.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 1, p. 12, ex. 1.3): Mozart, K. 525, ii: antecedent ends with HC (m. 4), consequent ends with PAC (m. 8).
**Example 2** (Ch. 1, pp. 12-13): The sentence's ability to close with either HC (weaker, as in Op. 2/1) or PAC (stronger, as in Op. 135) demonstrates the same principle of degrees of cadential closure.

# Common Errors
- Ignoring cadential strength differences between phrases

# Common Confusions
- Cadential differentiation involves both cadence TYPE and cadential WEIGHT -- a PAC following an HC is differentiated not only by type but also by the greater temporal span devoted to the cadential progression in the consequent (detailed in Ch. 4)
