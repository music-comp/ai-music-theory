# Quick Definition
Progressive distortions of the small ternary form that typically underlies an interior theme in the large ternary: the A' section may lack cadential closure, the A' section may be entirely eliminated (truncated small ternary), or both B and A' sections may be eliminated, leaving only a modulating A section followed by a retransition.

# Core Definition
The formal organization of an interior theme can usually be related to the small ternary (or small binary), specifically one whose A section modulates to a subordinate key of the minore. However, the small ternary at the basis of the interior theme is frequently left structurally incomplete, often in ways that significantly distort the form. Four progressively extreme distortions are identified: (1) the A' section is initially closed but then reopened (deceptive cadence in second ending); (2) the A' section lacks cadential closure, ending with a premature dominant arrival; (3) the entire A' section is eliminated, creating a truncated small ternary consisting of A and B sections only; (4) both B and A' sections are eliminated, and the modulating A section is followed directly by a retransition. The final harmony of the interior theme is usually the dominant of the home key, motivating the main theme's return.

# Prerequisites
- **Interior theme in large ternary**: The formal unit being distorted
- **Large ternary form**: The overall formal context
- **Contrasting middle**: The B section that may be eliminated

# Key Properties
1. Standard interior theme: complete small ternary ending with PAC in minore key
2. Distortion 1: A' initially closed, then reopened by deceptive cadence
3. Distortion 2: A' lacks cadential closure (premature dominant arrival)
4. Distortion 3: A' section eliminated entirely (truncated small ternary, A-B only)
5. Distortion 4: B and A' eliminated; modulating A followed directly by retransition
6. Each distortion results in the interior theme ending on dominant of the home key
7. The retransition (in Distortion 4) is distinguished from a B section by eliding with the end of the A section
8. A nonconventional organization may resemble a subordinate-theme complex or even a development section

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the A section of the interior theme's small ternary (modulates to subordinate key of the minore)
- Look for B section (contrasting middle) and A' section (return)
- Check for cadential closure: if reopened, Distortion 1; if lacking closure, Distortion 2
- If A' is absent and B leads directly to the main theme return, Distortion 3
- If B is also absent and only a retransition follows A, Distortion 4
- Distinguish retransition from B section: retransition elides with A's ending

# Context & Application
These distortions are common because the interior theme needs to end on the home-key dominant to prepare for the main theme's return. Achieving full cadential closure in the minore key and then transitioning back to the home key would be excessively lengthy for a slow movement. The distortions thus serve the practical goal of formal compression while maintaining the dramatic contrast of the minore.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Trio in A-flat, H. 14, ii, mm. 31-35 (ex. 17.4): Distortion 1 -- small binary; second ending brings deceptive cadence, reopening the form
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E-flat, H. 52, ii, mm. 28-33 (ex. 17.5): Distortion 2 -- A' lacks closure, premature dominant arrival
- Haydn, String Quartet in B-flat, Op. 64/3, ii, mm. 47-58 (ex. 17.6): Distortion 3 -- truncated small ternary (A-B only)
- Haydn, String Quartet in E, Op. 54/3, ii, mm. 25-40 (ex. 17.7): Distortion 4 -- A section followed by retransition, no B or A'
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, ii, mm. 23-52 (ex. 17.8): nonconventional -- interior theme organized like a development core

# Common Errors
- Expecting every interior theme to conform to a complete small ternary
- Confusing a retransition with a contrasting middle (the retransition elides with A's ending)
- Missing the truncated small ternary because the B section leads directly to the main theme return

# Common Confusions
- **B section vs. retransition**: A B section is independent; a retransition elides with the prior unit and is "attached" to it
- **Truncated small ternary**: A-B only, with no A' -- the B section ends on dominant, and the main theme returns
- **Development-like interior theme**: Occasionally the interior theme uses model-sequence technique resembling a core, but within the tonal framework of the minore
