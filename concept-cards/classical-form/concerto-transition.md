# Quick Definition
The transition in concerto form, which differs between the opening ritornello (nonmodulating, using new material) and the solo exposition (modulating, governed by Principles 1 and 2 for material selection).

# Core Definition
The transition in concerto form operates differently in the opening ritornello and the solo exposition. In the ritornello, the transition almost always begins with new material (unlike sonata expositions, where the transition may begin with main-theme material) and ends with a home-key HC, functioning as a nonmodulating transition since the ritornello remains in the home key. In the solo exposition, the transition modulates to the subordinate key. The choice of material for the solo transition is governed by two principles: (1) the solo should not reproduce the same main-theme-to-transition succession as the ritornello; and (2) an alternative main theme prompts the reappearance of the ritornello main theme. In the recapitulation, one transition or the other (or a new one) may be chosen.

# Construction / Recognition
- Ritornello transition:
  - Begins with new material (not main-theme material)
  - Ends with a home-key HC
  - Nonmodulating (ritornello stays in home key)
- Solo exposition transition:
  - Material governed by Principles 1 and 2
  - Modulates to the subordinate key
  - Four common scenarios for material selection (based on what the main theme uses)
- Recapitulation transition:
  - May use the ritornello transition, the solo transition, or a new transition
  - Occasionally both ritornello and solo transitions are used

# Context & Application
The different treatment of the transition in the ritornello versus the solo exposition reflects the distinct purposes of these sections. The ritornello transition builds toward the solo entrance while remaining in the home key; the solo transition accomplishes the crucial modulation that the ritornello withheld.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Concerto in E-flat, K. 482, i: Ritornello transition ends on home-key dominant; solo exposition transition uses different material
- Various Mozart concertos demonstrate the four scenarios described under Principles 1 and 2

# Common Confusions
- The ritornello transition is nonmodulating, unlike nearly all other transitions in sonata-based forms. It resembles a transition in character (loose organization, ending with HC) but not in tonal function.
- The ritornello transition almost always begins with new material, unlike sonata transitions, which frequently begin with main-theme material. This is a consistent difference between concerto and sonata practice.
