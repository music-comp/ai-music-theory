# Quick Definition
When a complete subordinate theme (ending with PAC) first appears as relatively tight-knit, the theme may begin to be repeated and then undergo significant expansion to render it considerably looser in formal expression, creating a dilemma for whether to count it as a new theme or a varied repeat.

# Core Definition
If a complete subordinate theme, one ending with a PAC, first appears as relatively tight-knit, the theme will sometimes begin to be repeated and then undergo significant expansion to render it considerably looser in formal expression. This expanded repetition is especially likely if the original theme itself omits an initiating function. But even a tight-knit theme beginning with a normal initiation may receive an expanded repetition. The expanded repetition loosens the form through techniques such as internal repetition of model and sequence units, evaded cadences, "one more time" technique, modal shift, and exploration of more remote tonal areas. The expansion often results in an extended expanded cadential progression closing the repeated theme.

# Prerequisites
- **Subordinate theme**: The formal unit being repeated
- **Loosening techniques**: The means by which the repetition is expanded
- **One-more-time technique**: Common technique used in expanded repetitions

# Key Properties
1. The original theme is relatively tight-knit, ending with PAC
2. The repetition begins with the same material but undergoes significant expansion
3. Expansion renders the repeated version considerably looser
4. Common loosening techniques: internal repetition, extended sequences, evaded cadences
5. Modal shift often accompanies the repeat (major to minor or vice versa)
6. Creates a dilemma for counting themes in the subordinate-theme group
7. Especially likely when the original theme omits an initiating function
8. The expanded repetition often features an enormous ECP closing it

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify a subordinate theme that closes with a PAC
- Note if the same material begins again immediately after the PAC
- Compare the second statement: is it significantly expanded?
- Track the loosening techniques employed in the expansion
- Determine whether to count the expanded repetition as a new theme or a varied repeat

# Context & Application
The expanded repetition creates a "counting the themes" dilemma. Do we call the expanded repetition "subordinate theme number 2" when it is obviously an expanded repeat of subordinate theme 1? In such cases, it seems more practical to show the motivic relationship by giving it the same "numerical label," even though the literal number of themes in the group may not be fully reflected by that label. The function of the expanded repetition is to provide the looseness that the original tight-knit statement lacked.

# Examples
- Haydn, String Quartet in B minor, Op. 64/2, i (Ch. 12, Ex. 12.7): second subordinate theme begins at m. 46 with continuation function and quickly closes with PAC at m. 50; expanded repetition at m. 51 extends model and sequence with internal repetitions; cadential idea evaded at m. 60 and repeated with "one more time" technique for PAC at m. 62
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in A, K. 331, first variation theme (Ch. 12, Ex. 12.11): tight-knit opening subordinate theme closes with PAC at m. 49; expanded repetition begins at upbeat to m. 50 with shift to minor mode; continuation extended to explore remote tonal areas; enormous expanded cadential progression closes the repeated theme

# Common Errors
- Treating the expanded repetition as an entirely new, unrelated theme
- Failing to recognize the motivic connection between the original and expanded statements
- Counting the expanded repetition separately when it clearly repeats earlier material

# Common Confusions
- **Counting themes**: It is more practical to label an expanded repetition with the same number as its original, acknowledging the motivic relationship
- **New theme vs. expanded repeat**: The key distinction is whether the material is recognizably derived from a prior theme
- **Loose vs. tight**: The expanded repetition is the LOOSE version of what was initially stated in TIGHT form
