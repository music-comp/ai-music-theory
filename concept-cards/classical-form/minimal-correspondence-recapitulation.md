# Quick Definition
A highly complex recapitulation whose structure corresponds only minimally to the exposition, with ideas shifted, reorganized, and new material introduced, yet retaining the overall cadential pattern.

# Formal Definition
In some highly complex cases, the recapitulation may be altered to the extent that its structure corresponds only minimally to the exposition. Ideas from the earlier section may be incorporated into the later one, but they are shifted about to produce quite a different formal plan. New material may appear that is not even derived from the exposition. Despite this, the overall shape of a standard recapitulation — including the cadential articulations (PAC for main theme, dominant arrival for transition, PAC for subordinate themes) — may be retained.

# Construction/Recognition
- Motivic material from the exposition is rearranged into a different formal plan
- New, non-derived material may appear
- The order of ideas differs significantly from the exposition
- Despite surface differences, the underlying cadential pattern may correspond to the standard plan
- Formal labels (main theme, transition, subordinate theme) become difficult to assign based on motivic content
- Cadential articulation provides the most reliable guide to formal function

# Musical Context
Minimal correspondence recapitulations are especially characteristic of Haydn, who delights in formal surprise. They call into question the very idea of adopting exposition labels for the recapitulation and challenge the analyst to look beyond motivic identity to cadential and functional criteria.

# Examples
- Haydn, String Quartet in B Minor, Op. 64/2, i (ex. 11.18): recapitulation redistributes exposition material entirely; the main theme is followed by transition material functioning as a subordinate theme; the original subordinate theme material serves transition function; new material appears; yet the cadential pattern is preserved (PAC at m. 77, dominant arrival at m. 80, PAC at m. 92, PAC at m. 102)

# Common Confusions
- Even the most radically altered recapitulation typically preserves the fundamental cadential pattern of the exposition
- From the point of view of motivic ordering, the recapitulation may seem unrelated to the exposition; from the point of view of cadential articulation, the connection is clearer
- This is a deviation from the norm, not a violation of form — the recapitulation function is still fulfilled
