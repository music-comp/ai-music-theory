# Quick Definition
The formal function that closes a theme through a conventionalized harmonic progression and melodic formula, typically of falling contour, confirming the tonality.

# Formal Definition
Cadential function contains a conventionalized harmonic progression (cadential progression) and a conventionalized melodic formula, usually of falling contour. The cadential idea stands opposed to the basic idea: its melody is conventional (non-thematic) and could close any number of themes. The purpose of motivic liquidation is to strip the basic idea of its characteristic features, leaving merely conventional material for the cadence. In the eight-measure sentence, cadential function typically fuses with continuation in the second four-measure phrase. In looser contexts, cadential function can occupy its own distinct phrase supported by an expanded cadential progression.

# Construction/Recognition
- Supported by a cadential progression (I-PD-V-I for PAC, ending on V for HC)
- Melodic formula typically descends, often stepwise
- Content is conventional (non-thematic), not characteristic
- Often the result of liquidation in the preceding continuation
- Can close with PAC (strongest), HC (weaker), IAC

# Musical Context
Cadential function appears at the end of themes and larger formal sections. Detailed treatment in Chapter 3.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 7-8: conventional cadential melody after liquidation, ending with HC
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 33-41: expanded cadential progression in the subordinate theme

# Common Confusions
- Cadential function and postcadential function are entirely different. Only material leading to the cadential arrival fulfills cadential function.
