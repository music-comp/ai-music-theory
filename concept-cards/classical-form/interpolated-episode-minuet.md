# Quick Definition
A passage of new material in a relatively remote tonal region, inserted into the contrasting middle or recapitulation of a minuet, providing additional contrast and formal complexity beyond what is typical in a small ternary.

# Core Definition
An interpolated episode in minuet form is a passage of entirely new material set in a relatively remote tonal region, appearing within the B section or the expanded A' section. This technique, which rarely finds a place in the more modest scope of the small ternary, adds formal complexity to the minuet. In the B section, the episode typically involves an abrupt change of dynamics, texture, and tonal region. In the A' section, the episode may be triggered by a deceptive cadence that moves to a remote harmony and may even confirm that remote region as a development key within the recapitulation (Caplin, pp. 643, 647-648).

# Prerequisites
- **Minuet contrasting middle** -- The formal section where episodes most typically appear
- **Minuet form** -- The larger context

# Key Properties
1. Introduces entirely new melodic-motivic material
2. Set in a relatively remote tonal region
3. May appear in the B section or the expanded A' section
4. Often marked by abrupt changes in dynamics and texture
5. A technique beyond the scope of the small ternary

# Construction / Recognition
1. Look for sudden changes in dynamics, texture, and tonal region within the B or A' section
2. New melodic-motivic material not derived from the A section
3. The episode typically disrupts the prevailing tonal trajectory
4. Following the episode, the music returns to the home-key dominant

# Context & Application
The interpolated episode is one of the techniques that distinguishes minuet form from the simpler small ternary. It provides the minuet with a degree of formal complexity approaching that of larger forms.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 644): Haydn, Symphony No. 98 in B-flat, iii, mm. 25-30 -- interpolated episode in B section marked by new melody, sudden shift from forte to piano, and move to A-flat major.
**Example 2** (p. 648): Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, iii, mm. 54-71 -- interpolated episode in the recapitulation, triggered by deceptive resolution to flat-VI, confirming that region as a development key within the A' section.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Expecting interpolated episodes in small ternaries. **Correction**: This technique is characteristic of minuet form's greater complexity, not the small ternary.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Whether the episode belongs to the B section or the A' section. **Clarification**: Check the position relative to the recapitulatory return of the opening idea; episodes can appear in either section.
