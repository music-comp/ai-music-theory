# Quick Definition
A ranked system of tonal levels in a movement -- home key, subordinate key, development keys, tonicized regions, and local tonicizations -- each distinguished by the degree of cadential confirmation it receives.

# Formal Definition
The structural hierarchy consists of: (1) home key at the top, confirmed by cadence early and at the end; (2) subordinate key, requiring PAC; (3) development keys, confirmed by HC, dominant arrival, or sometimes PAC; (4) tonicized regions, lacking cadential confirmation but potentially associated with initiating functions; (5) very local tonicizations at the bottom.

# Construction/Recognition
1. **Home key**: confirmed early and late; main theme and recapitulation
2. **Subordinate key**: requires PAC; subordinate theme
3. **Development keys**: HC, dominant arrival, or PAC; primary and secondary
4. **Tonicized regions**: no cadential confirmation; may have initiating-function association
5. **Local tonicizations**: brief chromatic inflections

# Musical Context
This hierarchy provides the framework for understanding tonal organization across the entire sonata movement.

# Examples
- In a major-mode sonata: I > V > vi > tonicized regions (IV, bVI) > local tonicizations

# Common Confusions
- The hierarchy is based on cadential confirmation, not duration spent in a key
- A region emphasized by duration but lacking cadential confirmation remains a tonicization
