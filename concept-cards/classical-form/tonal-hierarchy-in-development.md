# Quick Definition
A ranked system of tonal levels in a movement -- home key, subordinate key, development keys, tonicized regions, and local tonicizations -- each distinguished by the degree of cadential confirmation it receives.

# Core Definition
The structural hierarchy of tonal levels consists of: (1) the home key at the top, confirmed by cadence early in the exposition and again at the end of the recapitulation; (2) the subordinate key, requiring PAC for confirmation; (3) development keys, confirmed by HC, dominant arrival, or sometimes PAC; (4) tonicized regions, lacking cadential confirmation but potentially associated with initiating functions; and (5) very local tonicizations at the bottom (brief chromatic inflections). This hierarchy is based on cadential confirmation, not duration spent in a key. A region emphasized by duration but lacking cadential confirmation remains a tonicization, not a key.

# Prerequisites
- **Development section**: The formal context where the hierarchy is most apparent
- **Development key**: The mid-level rank in the hierarchy

# Key Properties
1. Home key: confirmed early and late; main theme and recapitulation
2. Subordinate key: requires PAC; subordinate theme
3. Development keys: HC, dominant arrival, or PAC; primary and secondary
4. Tonicized regions: no cadential confirmation; may have initiating-function association
5. Local tonicizations: brief chromatic inflections
6. Hierarchy based on cadential confirmation, NOT duration
7. Duration without confirmation = tonicization, not key

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the home key (confirmed by main theme's cadence and recapitulation)
- Identify the subordinate key (confirmed by subordinate theme's PAC)
- Identify development keys (confirmed by HC, dominant arrival, or PAC)
- Identify tonicized regions (emphasized but lacking cadential confirmation)
- Rank according to cadential strength

# Context & Application
This hierarchy provides the framework for understanding tonal organization across the entire sonata movement. The criterion of cadential confirmation (not duration or emphasis) determines the rank, creating a systematic way to analyze the development's tonal plan.

# Examples
- In a major-mode sonata: I (home key) > V (subordinate key) > vi (development key) > tonicized regions (IV, bVI) > local tonicizations

# Common Errors
- Ranking tonal levels by duration rather than cadential confirmation
- Treating prominent tonicized regions as development keys
- Assuming all emphasized harmonies constitute keys

# Common Confusions
- **Duration vs. confirmation**: The hierarchy is based on cadential confirmation, not duration spent in a key
- **Tonicized region vs. development key**: A region emphasized by duration but lacking cadential confirmation remains a tonicization
- **Criterion of rank**: Cadential strength (PAC > HC > dominant arrival > no confirmation) determines the level
