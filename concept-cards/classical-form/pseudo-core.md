# Quick Definition
A passage in the development that has the dramatic character, dynamics, and rhythmic activity of a core but lacks genuine model-sequence technique.

# Core Definition
A pseudo-core is a unit whose dynamics, rhythm, texture, and emotional character strongly resemble those of a core but whose material is not organized by model-sequence technique. It sometimes features prominent sequential organization of the harmonies, but they are not used to support the extensive model-sequence technique typical of a core. At other times, the pseudo-core appears to lack harmonic definition and takes on the improvisatory style of a "fantasia" or "toccata." In such cases, a prominent linear progression in the bass may help achieve coherence amid the seeming harmonic rambling. A pseudo-core is usually preceded by a unit functioning as a pre-core and generally closes with a half cadence (or dominant arrival) followed by a standing on the dominant.

# Prerequisites
- **Development section**: The formal context
- **Core technique**: The genuine organizational method the pseudo-core lacks

# Key Properties
1. Forte dynamic, thick textures, continuous rhythmic activity -- resembling a core
2. Absent: clearly defined model that is sequentially repeated at scale of 4+ measures
3. May feature sequential harmonies but WITHOUT model-sequence technique
4. May have fantasia-like or toccata-like improvisatory quality
5. Bass-line linear progression often provides structural coherence
6. Usually preceded by a pre-core
7. Typically closes with HC or dominant arrival followed by standing on the dominant
8. Haydn frequently employs pseudo-cores; less common in Mozart and Beethoven

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify core-like character (Sturm und Drang, forte, continuous activity)
- Check for model-sequence technique (4+ measure model sequentially repeated)
- If absent, the passage is a pseudo-core
- Look for bass-line linear progressions providing coherence
- Note whether harmonic progressions are sequential but without model-sequence structure
- Verify the concluding function (HC, dominant arrival, standing on dominant)

# Context & Application
Pseudo-cores appear in developments without a genuine core, particularly common in Haydn's works. The pseudo-core creates the emotional and dramatic impression of a core while organizing the material through alternative means. The distinction between core and pseudo-core is one of organizational technique, not dramatic effect.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in B-flat, K. 333, i, mm. 71-86 (ex. 10.16): shift to minor with continuous Alberti bass and restless melody of running sixteenth notes and syncopated quarter notes; character highly suggestive of a core but no model-sequence technique; bass oscillates chromatically between F and G; HC in G minor at m. 81

# Common Errors
- Treating a pseudo-core as a failed or deficient core (it is a legitimate alternative)
- Identifying sequential harmonies as model-sequence technique (they are not the same)
- Assuming all developments must have a core (pseudo-cores are a valid substitute)

# Common Confusions
- **Pseudo-core vs. core**: The distinction is organizational (model-sequence technique), not dramatic (both project Sturm und Drang)
- **Sequential harmonies vs. model-sequence technique**: The pseudo-core may feature sequential harmonies without the large-scale model repetition that defines a core
- **Legitimacy**: A pseudo-core is not a "failed core" -- it is a legitimate alternative organizational strategy
