# Quick Definition
A passage in the development that has the dramatic character, dynamics, and rhythmic activity of a core but lacks genuine model-sequence technique.

# Formal Definition
A pseudo-core is a unit whose dynamics, rhythm, texture, and emotional character strongly resemble those of a core but whose material is not organized by model-sequence technique. It sometimes features prominent sequential organization of the harmonies, but they are not used to support extensive model-sequence technique. At other times, the pseudo-core takes on the improvisatory style of a "fantasia" or "toccata." A prominent linear progression in the bass may help achieve coherence.

# Construction/Recognition
- Forte dynamic, thick textures, continuous rhythmic activity -- resembling a core
- Absent: clearly defined model that is sequentially repeated
- May feature sequential harmonies but without model-sequence technique
- May have a fantasia-like improvisatory quality
- A bass-line linear progression often provides structural coherence
- Usually preceded by a pre-core and closed by a half cadence with standing on the dominant

# Musical Context
Pseudo-cores appear in developments without a genuine core, particularly common in Haydn's works.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in B-flat, K. 333, i (ex. 10.16): shift to minor with continuous Alberti bass and restless melody; no model-sequence technique despite the core-like character; bass oscillates chromatically

# Common Confusions
- A pseudo-core is not a failed core -- it is a legitimate alternative organizational strategy
- The presence of sequential harmonies does not make a passage a true core
