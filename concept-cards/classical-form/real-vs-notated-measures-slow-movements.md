# Quick Definition
The distinction between notated bar lines and actual experienced metric units in slow movements, where each notated measure may be experienced as two real measures due to the slower tempo.

# Core Definition
In slow movements, the slower tempo causes individual beats within a measure to assume greater metric weight, so that a single notated measure may be experienced as two real measures. This affects phrase-structural analysis: what appears as an eight-measure theme in notation may function as a sixteen-measure theme in terms of real metric perception. The analyst must consider real measures when assessing formal functions and proportions.

- **Slow-movement sonata form**: The formal context where this issue arises

# Key Properties
1. Each notated measure may divide into two real (experienced) measures at slow tempos
2. Formal proportions should be assessed according to real measures
3. Common in Adagio, Largo, or Lento movements in 4/4, 3/4, and 6/8
4. A notated eight-bar sentence may function as a sixteen-bar sentence
5. Affects the perception of phrase structure and formal functions

# Construction / Recognition
- In very slow tempos, check whether each notated measure divides into two real measures
- Assess whether phrase lengths match standard models when counted in real measures
- Apply this conversion when notated measure counts seem anomalous

# Context & Application
This issue arises primarily in slow interior movements. Recognizing real versus notated measures resolves many apparent anomalies in phrase-structural analysis of slow movements.

# Examples
- Slow movements where a notated eight-bar sentence functions as a sixteen-bar sentence when beats are heard as measures
