# Quick Definition
The distinction between notated bar lines (notated measures) and actual experienced metric units (real measures) in slow movements, where each notated measure may be experienced as two real measures.

# Formal Definition
In slow movements, the slower tempo causes individual beats within a measure to assume greater metric weight, so a notated measure may be experienced as two real measures. This affects phrase-structural analysis: what appears as an eight-measure theme may function as a sixteen-measure theme. The analyst must consider real measures when assessing formal functions.

# Construction/Recognition
- In very slow tempos, each notated measure may divide into two real measures
- Common in 4/4, 3/4, and 6/8 at slow tempos
- Formal proportions should be assessed according to real measures

# Musical Context
This issue arises primarily in Adagio, Largo, or Lento movements.

# Examples
- Slow movements where a notated eight-bar sentence functions as a sixteen-bar sentence

# Common Confusions
- Students often count notated measures and arrive at phrase structures that do not match standard models. Recognizing real measures resolves these anomalies.
