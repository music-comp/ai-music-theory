# Quick Definition
The tendency for recapitulations to fill in or remove rhythmic breaks (rests, fermatas) that appeared in the exposition, fostering greater momentum following the emotionally charged development.

# Core Definition
The exposition of a movement often contains prominent interruptions in rhythmic activity -- rests and fermatas -- particularly in the main theme and at the end of the transition. The recapitulation often removes such discontinuities in order to foster greater rhythmic momentum. Following an emotionally charged development section, the flow of events may seem too inhibited if the music is continually checked in its progress by too many stops and starts.

# Prerequisites
- **Recapitulation**: The formal context

# Key Properties
1. Rests and fermatas from the exposition are filled in or eliminated
2. Most noticeable in the main theme and transition areas
3. Gaps in presentation phrases may be filled with imitative entries of the basic idea
4. Compression of two-part transitions also eliminates pauses between parts
5. Results in more continuous musical flow
6. Reflects the different expressive needs of the recapitulation vs. the exposition

# Construction / Recognition
- Compare rhythmic profiles of the exposition and recapitulation
- Look for rests or fermatas in the exposition that are absent in the recapitulation
- Note newly added imitative entries or melodic figures filling gaps
- Check whether compression of formal units also eliminates rhythmic breaks

# Context & Application
At the beginning of a movement, rhythmic breaks can create a sense of hesitancy appropriate to an "opening." After the development, continuous momentum is more appropriate to the recapitulation's function as resolution and return. This alteration is one of the additional features beyond the main categories of structural change.

# Examples
- Mozart, Clarinet Trio in E-flat, K. 498, i (ex. 11.16): rhythmic breaks in every even-numbered measure of the exposition's presentation (ex. 5.17, mm. 1-8) are filled in the recapitulation with newly added imitations of the initial two-measure basic idea; grouping structure somewhat extended
- Haydn, Piano Trio in E-flat, Hob. XV:30, i (ex. 11.7): compression of two-part transition into single part eliminates a pronounced pause in rhythmic momentum at the end of the first part

# Common Errors
- Treating filled-in gaps as mere ornamental additions rather than functionally motivated changes

# Common Confusions
- **Not simply adding notes**: Greater continuity reflects the changed expressive function of the recapitulation, not just decorative filling
- **Selective application**: Not all rhythmic breaks are eliminated; only prominent discontinuities that would impede momentum after the development
