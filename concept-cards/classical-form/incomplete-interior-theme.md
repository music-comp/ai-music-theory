# Quick Definition
An interior theme whose underlying small ternary (or binary) form is left structurally incomplete through the elimination, truncation, or distortion of its component sections.

# Formal Definition
Interior themes in large ternary form are frequently based on a small ternary (or small binary) that is left structurally incomplete. Several standard techniques occur: (1) the A' section is initially closed but then reopened on repeat (often via deceptive cadence); (2) the A' section lacks cadential closure (premature dominant arrival); (3) the A' section is eliminated entirely (truncated small ternary: A + B only); (4) both B and A' sections are eliminated, leaving only the modulating A section followed by a retransition. The final harmony is typically the dominant of the home key.

# Construction/Recognition
- The interior theme begins as expected (usually in minore)
- One of several deviations from complete small ternary form occurs
- The dominant of the home key at the end signals the main theme's return
- The degree of distortion varies on a continuum from slight to extreme
- Incomplete forms are more common than complete ones

# Musical Context
Incomplete interior themes are more common than complete ones in large ternary slow movements. The incompletion creates formal instability that heightens the need for the main theme's return. Similar incomplete interior themes appear in rondo forms.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Trio in A-flat, Hob. XV:14, ii: A' closed in first ending, reopened by DC in second ending
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Hob. XVI:52, ii: A' gets "stuck" on the dominant
- Haydn, String Quartet in B-flat, Op. 64/3, ii: Truncated small ternary (A + B only)

# Common Confusions
- Incomplete forms are standard practice, not anomalies. Students should not expect all interior themes to follow a complete small ternary plan.
