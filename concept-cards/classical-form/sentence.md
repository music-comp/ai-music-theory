# Quick Definition
An eight-measure theme built from a four-measure presentation phrase (basic idea + repetition) and a four-measure continuation phrase (fragmentation, acceleration, cadence), expressing the formal functions of presentation, continuation, and cadential.

# Core Definition
The sentence is an eight-measure theme built out of two four-measure phrases. The opening phrase contains **presentation function**: a two-measure basic idea is stated and immediately repeated, supported by tonic prolongation. The second phrase incorporates **continuation** and **cadential** functions. Continuation destabilizes the context through fragmentation, harmonic acceleration, increased surface rhythm, and sequential harmonies. The cadential function brings harmonic closure (HC or PAC). For simplicity, this second unit is termed a "continuation phrase," though it fuses continuation and cadential functions (abbreviated cont.=>cad.). The sentence's forward-striving character, created by the increased activity and compression of the continuation, is fundamentally different from the symmetrical repose of the period.

# Prerequisites
- **Basic idea**: the two-measure unit containing the theme's fundamental melodic-motivic material
- **Presentation function**: statement + repetition of basic idea over tonic prolongation
- **Continuation function**: destabilization via fragmentation, harmonic acceleration, sequential harmonies
- **Cadential function**: harmonic progression achieving closure (HC or PAC)

# Key Properties
- Normatively 8 real measures: presentation (4) + continuation (4)
- Presentation uses tonic prolongation; continuation destabilizes
- The continuation phrase fuses continuation and cadential functions (cont.=>cad.)
- Fragmentation is the most common continuation device
- The sentence has a "forward-striving character" (Ratz) unlike the period's symmetrical repose
- Real measures (R) = 4 x notated measures (N) is a useful heuristic for slow movements

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify a two-measure basic idea and its immediate repetition (pres.)
2. Confirm tonic prolongation supports the presentation
3. Look for fragmentation (unit reduction from 2 to 1 measures) in the continuation
4. Check for harmonic acceleration, surface-rhythmic increase, or sequential harmonies
5. Identify the closing cadence (HC or PAC)
6. The three types of repetition in the presentation: exact, statement-response (I->V or tonic->dominant version), or sequential
7. When all continuation characteristics are present, the identification is unambiguous; when few are present, the sense of continuation is weaker but may still suffice

# Context & Application
The sentence is one of the fundamental tight-knit theme-types of classical form. It commonly serves as a main theme in sonata movements but also appears in subordinate themes, interior themes, and as the basis for larger compound structures (compound sentence). The model is also applicable within looser formal regions where sentential characteristics appear without constituting a genuine sentence. The sentence can serve as the exposition (A section) of a small ternary, though the period is more common in that role.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in F Minor, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 1-8: "most archetypal" sentence; statement-response presentation (I then V), continuation with fragmentation into 1-measure units, HC at m. 8
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in C Minor, Op. 10/1, i, mm. 1-8: eight-measure sentence notated in four measures (R = 4N in fast tempo)
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C Minor, K. 457, i, mm. 1-8: sequential repetition in presentation, continuation with fragmentation and model-sequence technique
- Haydn, Symphony No. 104 in D, i, mm. 17-24: sentence with exact repetition and harmonic acceleration in continuation

# Common Errors
- Confusing any 8-measure theme with a sentence; the specific presentation->continuation structure is required
- Treating the four continuation characteristics as all required; any subset can express the function
- Failing to distinguish real from notated measures in slow or fast movements
- Analyzing a transitional passage as a sentence continuation when it is actually a new formal function

# Common Confusions
- **Sentence vs. period**: the sentence repeats the basic idea immediately (presentation), while the period follows it with a contrasting idea (antecedent). The sentence's second phrase brings new processes (fragmentation, acceleration); the period's second phrase repeats the first with stronger cadence.
- **Continuation phrase vs. continuation + cadential**: the single "continuation phrase" typically fuses both functions; labeling it cont.=>cad. is more precise but the simpler label is standard
- **Sentence-like vs. genuine sentence**: a four-measure antecedent with a sentential internal design (1+1 presentation-like basic idea + continuational contrasting idea) is sentential in character but is not a sentence proper
