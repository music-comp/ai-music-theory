# Quick Definition
A theoretical model viewing the opening ritornello and solo exposition as two presentations of an exposition (analogous to a sonata's repeated exposition), which is attractive because the recapitulation must synthesize both, but misleading because it implies the concerto is merely a variant of sonata form.

# Core Definition
Since both the opening ritornello and the solo exposition present an ordered succession (rotation) of expositional functions (main theme, transition, subordinate-theme group), some theorists speak of a "double exposition" at the beginning of concerto form, analogous to the sonata's repeated exposition. This idea is attractive especially when considering the solo recapitulation, where the composer must effectively recapitulate material from both the ritornello and the solo exposition in a single section. But the notion is misleading because it suggests that concerto form is a variant type of sonata form, rather than an independent form derived from Baroque antecedents that predate the classical sonata. For this reason, Caplin uses "ritornello" rather than "orchestral exposition" for the opening section (Caplin, p. 704).

# Key Properties
1. Both ritornello and solo exposition present rotations of expositional functions
2. The concept is attractive for understanding the recapitulation's task
3. The concept is misleading because it implies concerto = variant of sonata
4. Concerto form is an independent form with Baroque antecedents
5. "Ritornello" is preferred over "orchestral exposition" as terminology
6. The solo exposition rarely simply repeats the ritornello

# Construction / Recognition
1. Observe the parallel succession of themes in ritornello and solo exposition
2. Note significant differences in material, tonality, and elaboration between them
3. Analyze how the recapitulation synthesizes elements from both
4. Recognize that the parallels do not make the concerto a sonata variant

# Context & Application
The double-exposition concept is useful analytically when tracing how material from both sections reappears in the recapitulation. However, maintaining the distinction between ritornello and exposition preserves the concerto's historical independence.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating the opening ritornello as simply a first exposition. **Correction**: The ritornello differs fundamentally from a sonata exposition in tonality and phrase structure.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Whether concerto form is a type of sonata form. **Clarification**: It is an independent form derived from Baroque ritornello practice, not a sonata variant.
