# Quick Definition
A main theme that lacks a clear sense of functional initiation, seeming to start "in the middle" or "toward the end" with continuation or cadential functions rather than a conventional beginning.

# Formal Definition
Some main themes are nonconventional because they lack a clear sense of functional initiation. The theme seems to start "in the middle" (with continuation function) or "toward the end" (with cadential function). An initiating tonic harmony may be entirely absent, or the opening material may immediately express instability characteristic of a continuation. Despite this anomaly, the theme maintains enough tonal and phrase-structural substance to function as the exposition's true formal beginning.

# Construction/Recognition
- Opening measures project continuation or cadential function rather than presentation or basic idea
- Harmonic instability from the very beginning (no clear tonic prolongation at the start)
- May follow a slow introduction, with the instability of the introduction's end carrying into the main theme
- The theme may be preceded by a brief thematic introduction that provides the missing sense of initiation
- Despite the missing initiation, a logical succession of intrathematic functions is usually expressed

# Musical Context
Omitted initiating function creates a sense of the music being "already in progress," heightening the urgency and momentum of the opening. This technique is a marker of nonconventional theme construction.

# Examples
- Mozart, Symphony No. 38 ("Prague"), K. 504, i (ex. 13.2): after a one-measure introduction, the material immediately expresses continuation function through harmonic instability, fragmentation, and increased surface rhythm; the IAC at m. 43 is followed by fanfare codettas
- Haydn, Symphony No. 90 in C, i (ex. 13.5): the slow introduction ends on a pre-dominant; the main theme begins with continuation function, ending with PAC at m. 20; a false closing section precedes further continuation, leading to a second cadence

# Common Confusions
- Omitted initiation does not mean the theme lacks a beginning — it means the beginning does not express conventional initiating function
- The theme is still recognized as the main theme because of its placement, tonal context, and subsequent events
- A fanfare codetta may compensate for the missing initiating tonic (as in ex. 13.2)
