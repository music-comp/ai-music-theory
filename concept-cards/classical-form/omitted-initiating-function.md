# Quick Definition
A main theme that lacks a clear sense of functional initiation, seeming to start "in the middle" or "toward the end" with continuation or cadential functions rather than a conventional beginning.

# Core Definition
Some main themes are nonconventional because they lack a clear sense of functional initiation. The theme seems to start "in the middle" (with continuation function) or "toward the end" (with cadential function). An initiating tonic harmony may be entirely absent, or the opening material may immediately express the instability characteristic of a continuation. Despite this anomaly, the theme maintains enough tonal and phrase-structural substance to function as the exposition's true formal beginning. The theme may follow a slow introduction whose instability carries into the main theme. A brief thematic introduction may provide the missing sense of initiation. Despite the missing initiation, a logical succession of intrathematic functions is usually expressed.

# Prerequisites
- **Nonconventional main theme**: The broader category
- **Continuation function**: The function that replaces initiation

# Key Properties
1. Opening measures project continuation or cadential function, not presentation or basic idea
2. Harmonic instability from the very beginning (no clear tonic prolongation)
3. May follow a slow introduction whose instability carries into the theme
4. A brief thematic introduction may provide the missing sense of initiation
5. Despite the anomaly, a logical succession of intrathematic functions is expressed
6. The theme is recognized by its placement, tonal context, and subsequent events
7. A fanfare codetta may compensate for the missing initiating tonic

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify whether the opening measures express continuation (instability, fragmentation, acceleration) rather than initiation (tonic prolongation, basic idea)
- Note whether a slow introduction or thematic introduction precedes the theme
- Check whether a compensatory tonic emphasis appears later (e.g., fanfare codetta)
- Trace the logical succession of functions despite the missing initiation

# Context & Application
Omitted initiating function creates a sense of the music being "already in progress," heightening urgency and momentum. This technique also appears in subordinate themes (see omission-of-initiating-function) but is particularly striking in main themes because the main theme is expected to provide the movement's initiating function.

# Examples
- Mozart, Symphony No. 38 ("Prague"), K. 504, i (ex. 13.2): after a one-measure introduction, material immediately expresses continuation function through harmonic instability, fragmentation, and increased surface rhythm; IAC at m. 43 followed by fanfare codettas that compensate for minimal root-position tonic at the opening
- Haydn, Symphony No. 90 in C, i (ex. 13.5): slow introduction ends unusually on a pre-dominant (VII6/V); the main theme begins with continuation function, ending with PAC at m. 20; a false closing section at mm. 21-24 precedes further continuation leading to a second cadence (m. 32); the entire complex (mm. 17-32) is highly nonconventional but expresses a logical succession of functions

# Common Errors
- Confusing omitted initiation with the theme lacking a beginning (it has a beginning, but not conventional initiating function)
- Treating the theme as defective rather than nonconventional

# Common Confusions
- **Not missing a beginning**: The theme begins, but does not express conventional initiating function
- **Still the main theme**: Recognized by placement, tonal context, and subsequent events
- **Compensatory devices**: Fanfare codettas or later tonic emphasis may compensate for the missing initiation
- **Differs from subordinate-theme version**: In subordinate themes (Ch. 8), omitted initiation is a loosening device; in main themes, it creates a highly nonconventional design
