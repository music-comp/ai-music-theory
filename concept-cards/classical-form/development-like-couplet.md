# Quick Definition
A rondo couplet (typically couplet 2) organized along the lines of a development section, with model-sequence technique, tonal exploration, and motivic working-out.

# Core Definition
Couplet 2 of a rondo may be organized in a manner that cannot be easily assimilated to the category of interior theme, displaying instead a development-like quality. In sonata-rondo form, couplet 2 is frequently organized as a genuine development section, normally containing a transition-like pre-core followed by one or more cores (or core substitutes). The pre-core may be omitted, with the development beginning directly with a core. The development usually ends with a standing on the dominant of the home key to prepare for the recapitulation of the refrain. False recapitulations are more common in rondo developments than in sonata developments, since the rondo emphasizes the return of the refrain.

# Construction / Recognition
- Located in couplet 2 position (between refrains 2 and 3)
- Features model-sequence technique and tonal exploration
- In sonata-rondo: may contain a pre-core and one or more cores
- Pre-core often resembles a transition, based on prior ideas or new material
- Common strategies for beginning the development:
  - Refrain begins to repeat but shifts to minor, signaling departure from exposition
  - Original transition material returns but leads to a new tonal region
  - Incomplete refrain merges with transitional material
- May include false recapitulations (especially in Beethoven)
- Usually ends with a standing on the dominant

# Context & Application
The development-like couplet brings the rondo closest to sonata form. In sonata-rondos, a true development in couplet 2 makes the form considerably more sonata-like. The shift to minor at the beginning of this couplet is a standard signal that a development (rather than a repeated exposition) is under way.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Violin Sonata in D, Op. 12/1, iii, mm. 58-71: Refrain 2 begins to repeat at m. 60, but the immediate shift to minor reveals a second couplet rather than exposition repeat; music departs at m. 66 to modulate to III for a core
- Mozart, Piano Trio in B-flat, K. 502, iii, mm. 86-103: Antecedent of refrain closes at m. 87; consequent departs from antecedent's plan to develop the continuation motive sequentially, becoming a pre-core
- Beethoven's sonata-rondos frequently contain false recapitulations near the end of the development

# Common Errors
See Common Confusions.

# Common Confusions
- A development-like couplet in a five-part rondo is not the same as a full sonata development in a sonata-rondo. The five-part rondo's development-like couplet may have only superficial developmental qualities, while the sonata-rondo's development can be fully elaborated.
- The shift to minor at the beginning of couplet 2 is a signal for rondo form, not an indication of a minore interior theme. The minor mode here signals departure from the exposition, whereas in an interior theme it indicates the theme's tonal identity.
