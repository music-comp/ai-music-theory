# Quick Definition
The five regular functions that a coda performs to make up for events or procedures not fully treated in the main body of the movement.

# Core Definition
In addition to its primary function of expressing an after-the-end, most codas also treat compositional matters not directly implicated in the movement's more fundamental tonal and formal processes. The coda includes a variety of compensatory functions that allow the composer to make up for events or procedures not fully treated in the main body of the movement. Five such functions are regularly observed: (1) recollection of main-theme ideas, imparting a circular design to the overall form; (2) restoration of expositional material deleted from the recapitulation; (3) recapitulation of ideas from the development section; (4) shaping a concluding dynamic curve that differs from or surpasses the recapitulation's; and (5) realization of unrealized implications generated by various compositional processes left unrealized in earlier sections. The recapitulation is relatively constrained in addressing these matters because it must bring back exposition material in essentially the same order.

# Prerequisites
- **Coda**: The section serving these functions
- **Recapitulation**: The constrained section that cannot fully address these matters

# Key Properties
1. Five regular compensatory functions identified
2. The recapitulation's constraints motivate these functions
3. A single coda may fulfill multiple compensatory functions simultaneously
4. These are additional to the coda's primary after-the-end function
5. The five functions are regular but not exhaustive
6. Drawing on studies by Rosen ("unfinished business"), Kerman ("thematic completion"), and Morgan ("culmination")

# Construction / Recognition
1. **Recollection of main-theme ideas**: main-theme material returns, usually loosely organized; perhaps the most common function
2. **Restoration of deleted material**: passages deleted from the recapitulation reappear, often at original tonal level
3. **Development reference**: material from the development recalled, especially at the coda's start
4. **Dynamic curve**: progressive (building to climax) or recessive (fading) dynamic arc
5. **Realization of unrealized implications**: resolving compositional "problems" or anomalies from earlier sections

# Context & Application
These functions explain why the coda exists even though the fundamental tonal and formal processes are completed by the recapitulation. The coda addresses secondary processes and loose ends, providing genuine closure beyond the structural close of the recapitulation. The recapitulation must bring back material in the same order, limiting its ability to recall main-theme material late, reference the development, or shape a new dynamic curve.

# Examples
- Each function is detailed in its individual concept card
- A single coda may combine several: e.g., Beethoven, Overture to Coriolanus, Op. 62 (ex. 12.6) fulfills at least four functions (recollection, restoration, dynamic curve, realization)

# Common Errors
- Treating compensatory functions as the coda's primary function (that is after-the-end)
- Assuming every coda must fulfill all five functions

# Common Confusions
- **Primary vs. compensatory**: Compensatory functions are additional to, not replacements for, the coda's primary after-the-end function
- **Not exhaustive**: The five functions are regular but not exhaustive; other compositional goals may also be served
- **Multiple functions**: A single coda typically combines several compensatory functions

# Verification Notes
The five-function taxonomy is Caplin's own systematic categorization, building on work by Rosen, Kerman, and Morgan. The constraint on the recapitulation (material in the same order) is Caplin's explicit rationale.
