# Quick Definition
The five regular functions that a coda performs to make up for events or procedures not fully treated in the main body of the movement.

# Formal Definition
The coda includes a variety of compensatory functions that allow the composer to make up for events or procedures not fully treated in the main body of the movement. Five such functions are regularly observed: (1) recollection of main-theme ideas, imparting a circular design; (2) restoration of expositional material deleted from the recapitulation; (3) recapitulation of ideas from the development section; (4) shaping a concluding dynamic curve different from the recapitulation's; and (5) realization of unrealized implications generated earlier in the movement.

# Construction/Recognition
1. **Recollection of main-theme ideas**: circular design; most common compensatory function; material usually loosely organized
2. **Restoration of deleted material**: recapitulating passages omitted from the recapitulation
3. **Development reference**: recalling material from the development at the start of the coda
4. **Dynamic curve**: creating a progressive or recessive dynamic ending
5. **Realization of unrealized implications**: resolving compositional "problems" set up earlier

# Musical Context
These functions explain why the coda exists even though the fundamental tonal and formal processes are completed by the recapitulation. The coda addresses secondary processes and loose ends, providing genuine closure beyond the structural close of the recapitulation.

# Common Confusions
- Compensatory functions are not the coda's primary function (that is expressing "after-the-end"); they are additional roles the coda serves
- The five functions are regular but not exhaustive — other compositional goals may also be served
- A single coda may combine several compensatory functions
