# Quick Definition
The systematic elimination of characteristic motives from the basic idea during the continuation, leaving only conventional (non-thematic) material for the cadence.

# Core Definition
Liquidation is "the systematic elimination of characteristic motives" (Ch. 1, p. 11). Its purpose is "to strip the basic idea of its characteristic features, thus leaving the merely conventional ones for the cadence" (Ch. 1, p. 12). Not all themes feature liquidation; "when they do not, the composer achieves the same end by simply abandoning material from the basic idea and writing a cadential melody that is not directly derived from the earlier idea" (Ch. 1, p. 11).

# Prerequisites
- **Basic idea** -- whose characteristic motives are being eliminated
- **Continuation function** -- the context where liquidation occurs
- **Cadential function** -- which receives the resulting conventional material

# Key Properties
1. Concerns motivic content, not unit length (unlike fragmentation)
2. Gradually eliminates characteristic (thematic) motives
3. Leaves conventional (non-thematic) material for the cadence
4. Connects the basic idea's unique character to the cadential idea's conventional character
5. Not required of all themes -- abandoning basic-idea material entirely achieves the same end
6. Fragmentation and liquidation frequently work together but are independent processes

# Construction / Recognition
- Track which motives from the basic idea survive into the continuation
- Note the progressive elimination of characteristic features
- Identify where conventional cadential material replaces thematic material

# Context & Application
Liquidation bridges the basic idea's characteristic content and the cadential idea's conventional content. It explains the aesthetic logic of the sentence form: the unique is gradually transformed into the generic, preparing the conventional cadential close.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 1, pp. 10-11, ex. 1.1): Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 5-8: motive "a" is largely eliminated by m. 5 (only the leaping grace note and arpeggiated chord survive); the sixteenth-note triplet of motive "b" is abandoned in m. 7, replaced by eighth notes; the melodic descent via conventional material leads to the HC.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Basic idea (the source of characteristic motives being eliminated)
## Enables
- Cadential function (by preparing conventional material)
## Related
- Fragmentation (often co-occurs, but concerns length rather than content)
## Contrasts With
- Fragmentation: liquidation concerns CONTENT (motivic elimination); fragmentation concerns LENGTH (unit reduction)

# Common Errors
- Requiring liquidation in every sentence (some themes abandon basic-idea material outright)
- Conflating fragmentation with liquidation

# Common Confusions
- Liquidation and fragmentation are "different compositional processes: fragmentation concerns the lengths of units, and liquidation concerns the melodic-motivic content of units" (Ch. 1, p. 11)
- Not all continuations feature liquidation; the cadential idea can simply introduce entirely new conventional material
