# Quick Definition
A deviation in which fragmentation is deferred beyond its expected onset at the beginning of the continuation phrase, initially maintaining the unit size of the presentation before eventually reducing to shorter fragments.

# Core Definition
In the normative sentence, fragmentation begins immediately at the start of the continuation phrase (m. 5), reducing the two-measure basic idea to one-measure units. In delayed fragmentation, the continuation begins with two-measure units that maintain the unit size of the presentation before eventually reducing to one-measure fragments later in the phrase. Without fragmentation at the outset, continuation function must be expressed by other characteristics such as harmonic acceleration, increased surface rhythms, or sequential harmony. The technique is a hallmark of subordinate-theme construction (ch. 8), where withholding fragmentation extends the theme's middle portion and delays the cadential goal, but it also appears as a deviation in tight-knit themes (ch. 3).

# Prerequisites
- Fragmentation (the process being delayed)
- Continuation Function (the functional context)
- Sentence (the theme-type being altered)

# Key Properties
- Continuation begins with two-measure units (same size as presentation)
- Fragmentation to one-measure units occurs later than expected
- Other continuation characteristics (harmonic acceleration, sequential harmonies) may compensate
- Creates a more gradual acceleration toward the cadence
- In subordinate themes, the continuation function can extend across multiple phrases
- The final phrase combining fragmentation often serves as cont.=>cad.

# Construction / Recognition
1. At the start of the continuation phrase, check whether unit size immediately reduces
2. If two-measure units persist into the continuation, fragmentation is delayed
3. Locate the point where fragmentation finally occurs
4. Note whether other continuation characteristics compensate for the delayed fragmentation
5. In subordinate themes, fragmentation may not appear until a distinct second phrase of continuation

# Context & Application
Delayed fragmentation creates a continuation phrase with a more gradual sense of acceleration. It is relatively common in both tight-knit themes (ch. 3) and subordinate themes (ch. 8). In subordinate themes, it is one of the most effective loosening techniques: prolonging the listener's sense of being "in the middle" of the theme by withholding the strongest signal of continuation function.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in D, K. 576, i, mm. 34-41: continuation begins at m. 34 with 2-measure units (same as presentation); fragmentation delayed until m. 38, where 1-measure units signal cont.=>cad.

# Common Errors
- Interpreting the continuation's initial two-measure units as a further extension of the presentation rather than as delayed fragmentation within continuation function
- Assuming fragmentation must begin at the start of continuation; in subordinate themes, delay is normative, not exceptional

# Common Confusions
- **Delayed fragmentation vs. expanded presentation**: if the additional two-measure units at mm. 5-6 are still supported by tonic prolongation and repeat the basic idea, they may be part of an expanded presentation rather than a delayed-fragmentation continuation
- **Delayed fragmentation does not mean absent continuation function**: other characteristics (harmonic acceleration, surface rhythm) still signal continuation
