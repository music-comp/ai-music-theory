# Quick Definition
A core substitute in the development whose harmonic goal is an authentic cadence in a development key and which features loosening devices typical of a subordinate theme, such as extended continuations, expanded cadential progressions, and evaded or deceptive cadences.

# Core Definition
A core substitute can resemble a subordinate theme if its harmonic goal is an authentic cadence of a development key and if it features loosening devices such as an extended continuation, an expanded cadential progression, and evaded, deceptive, or abandoned cadences. Unlike a genuine subordinate theme, which must always close with a PAC, a subordinate-themelike unit can promise authentic cadential closure but never achieve a true cadential arrival. Frequently, the unit begins in one tonal region or key and modulates to another key, where it receives authentic cadential closure (or the promise of such closure). The first three parts of a development organized with this technique may resemble the exposition itself: pre-core like a main theme, second part like a transition, third part like a subordinate theme.

# Prerequisites
- **Development section**: The formal context
- **Core substitute**: The broader category to which the subordinate-themelike unit belongs
- **Subordinate theme**: The model it resembles in formal organization

# Key Properties
1. Harmonic goal is an authentic cadence in a development key
2. Features loosening devices: extended continuation, expanded cadential progression
3. May include evaded, deceptive, or abandoned cadences
4. Unlike a genuine subordinate theme, need not achieve a true PAC
5. May begin in one key and modulate to another
6. Obscuring of functional boundaries is common
7. Often appears in developments organized "rotationally" (Haydn especially)
8. The development may mirror the exposition's organization (main-theme-like, transition-like, ST-like)

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify a unit in the development whose harmonic goal is an authentic cadence in a development key
- Note loosening devices: expanded cadential progressions, deceptive cadences, one-more-time technique
- Observe that it lacks genuine model-sequence technique (distinguishing it from a core)
- Check whether the unit mirrors the subordinate theme's looseness of organization
- The unit may end with a PAC, but it may also end with an evaded or abandoned cadence

# Context & Application
Subordinate-themelike units are especially characteristic of Haydn, who constructs developments without a genuine core more frequently than Mozart or Beethoven. When the entire development mirrors the exposition's formal plan (pre-core like main theme, second part like transition, subordinate-themelike unit like subordinate theme), this exemplifies "rotational" form as described by Hepokoski and Darcy.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in B-flat, H. 41, i, mm. 77-85 (ex. 13.16): subordinate-themelike unit begins with basic idea in development key G minor, features expanded cadential progression, abandoned cadence, and eventual PAC; resembles loosely organized subordinate theme

# Common Errors
- Confusing a subordinate-themelike unit with a genuine core (it lacks model-sequence technique)
- Treating an authentic cadence in a development key as a structural close of the form
- Missing the rotational organization when the entire development mirrors the exposition

# Common Confusions
- **Subordinate-themelike unit vs. subordinate theme**: The key difference is formal placement (development key vs. subordinate key) and the possibility of not achieving genuine cadential closure
- **Rotational form**: When the development mirrors the exposition's ordering, this reflects Hepokoski and Darcy's concept of rotational form
- **Core vs. core substitute**: The absence of large-scale model-sequence technique is the defining difference
