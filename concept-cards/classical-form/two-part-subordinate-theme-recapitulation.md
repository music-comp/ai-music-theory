# Quick Definition
When a two-part subordinate theme from the exposition is recapitulated, one part may be deleted to avoid overemphasizing the home-key dominant, since the internal HC and the transition's ending would both articulate V in the home key.

# Formal Definition
The use of a two-part subordinate theme in the exposition can sometimes lead to major structural changes in the recapitulation. In the exposition, an internal half cadence and standing on the dominant emphasize dominant harmony of the subordinate key. If the two-part subordinate theme is left intact in the recapitulation, two cadential articulations of home-key dominant will result: the cadence of the transition and the internal half cadence of the two-part subordinate theme. To avoid overemphasizing the home-key dominant, the composer sometimes retains only one of these cadences, usually the internal half cadence. Therefore, either the first part of the subordinate theme is deleted, or portions of it are assimilated into the preceding transition.

# Construction/Recognition
- Exposition has a two-part subordinate theme with an internal HC and standing on the dominant
- In the recapitulation, the internal HC would now articulate V of the home key (same as the transition's ending)
- One solution: delete the first part of the subordinate theme
- Another solution: assimilate the first part into the transition
- The second part becomes the actual subordinate theme in the recapitulation
- The result is a single-part subordinate theme where the exposition had two parts

# Musical Context
This adjustment avoids the structural redundancy of two dominant emphases in close succession. It is one of the more significant structural changes that can affect the subordinate theme in the recapitulation.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C Minor, K. 457, i (ex. 11.6): transition concludes at m. 126 with material from the internal HC of the subordinate theme; first part deleted; second part functions as the recapitulation's subordinate theme

# Common Confusions
- The deletion is motivated by tonal adjustment needs, not simply a desire for brevity
- The internal HC of the two-part subordinate theme and the transition's ending serve different functions in the exposition but would be redundant in the recapitulation
- Not all two-part subordinate themes are altered; only those where the redundancy is problematic
