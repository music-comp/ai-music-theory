# Quick Definition
A texture featuring passage-work (arpeggiations, scale patterns) in a virtuosic idiom, often the first place in a movement where continuous rhythmic accompaniment is employed, characteristic of transitions and cadential climaxes.

# Core Definition
Transitions in the classical literature frequently feature melodic material characterized as "passage-work" -- arpeggiations and scale patterns projecting a "brilliant style." This contrasts with the more tuneful melodic material of main themes and subordinate themes. Transitions are often the first place in the movement where continuous rhythmic accompaniment (such as an Alberti bass) is employed. The brilliant style is also associated with concerto idioms, where virtuosic passage-work appears at climactic moments (especially the cadential trill). When concerto-like passage-work appears in chamber or sonata genres, it signals an adoption of the concerto style.

# Prerequisites
- **Transition**: The primary formal context for brilliant style
- **Subordinate theme**: Context for brilliant-style cadential climaxes

# Key Properties
1. Passage-work textures: arpeggiations, scale patterns, figuration
2. Less "tuneful" than main-theme or subordinate-theme melodies
3. Often the first use of continuous rhythmic accompaniment (e.g., Alberti bass) in the movement
4. Associated with increased dynamic intensity and forward drive
5. Prominent in transitions but also at cadential climaxes in subordinate themes
6. In concerto genres, features virtuosic solo writing with trill before cadential arrival
7. Transitions seldom feature material that would be characterized as "tuneful"

# Construction / Recognition
- Look for arpeggiations, scale patterns, and figuration replacing melodic ideas
- Note the onset of continuous rhythmic accompaniment
- Observe the association with forte dynamics and forward momentum
- At cadential climaxes, look for expanded dominant with virtuosic passage-work and trill
- In non-concerto genres, brilliant style signals concerto-style influence

# Context & Application
The brilliant style is a characteristic textural marker of transition function. Its appearance helps the listener identify the transition, even before the harmonic goal is reached. The contrast between the main theme's tuneful melodies and the transition's passage-work contributes to formal loosening. In subordinate themes, the brilliant style appears at the cadential climax, where it builds maximum intensity before cadential arrival. The concerto-style trill signals the approaching closure.

# Examples
- Haydn, Symphony No. 90, iv, mm. 16-28: brilliant passage-work prolonging tonic; three presentation phrases project lengthy prolongation
- Mozart, Clarinet Quintet in A, K. 581, i, mm. 61-64: expanded dominant with virtuosic passage-work and trill in the clarinet -- concerto style adopted for chamber genre
- Mozart, Violin Concerto in A, K. 219, i, mm. 108-112: cadential trill before PAC

- Energy gain (brilliant style contributes to increased intensity)

## Related
- Cadential trill (specific brilliant-style gesture marking cadential arrival)

- Sturm und Drang character (another transition style, but darker in affect)

## Contrasts With
- Tuneful main-theme melodies (brilliant style replaces melody with figuration)
- Lyrical subordinate-theme material (brilliant style appears at climax, not throughout)

# Common Errors
- Treating all virtuosic passages as brilliant style (it specifically refers to conventionalized passage-work)
- Confusing brilliant style in subordinate themes with transition function (in ST, it signals concerto influence)
- Assuming all transitions must feature brilliant style

# Common Confusions
- **Brilliant style vs. thematic material**: Passage-work is conventionalized figuration; thematic material has specific motivic identity
- **In transition vs. in subordinate theme**: In transitions, brilliant style is normative; in subordinate themes, it signals concerto-style influence at the cadential climax
- **Not all transitions are brilliant**: Some transitions, especially in slow movements, are more lyrical
