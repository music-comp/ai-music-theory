# Quick Definition
A type of repetition within a presentation phrase in which the basic idea is given first in a tonic version (statement) and then in a dominant version (response), the whole creating a tonic prolongational progression.

# Core Definition
In statement-response repetition, "the basic idea is given originally in a tonic version, also termed a statement, and the repetition in a dominant version or response" (Ch. 1, p. 10). When the V of the response resolves to I on the downbeat of the continuation phrase, "the resulting harmonic progression" is "tonic prolongational" (Ch. 1, p. 10). Statement-response is one of three types of presentation repetition, alongside exact repetition (no harmonic change) and sequential repetition (transposition to different scale-degrees).

# Prerequisites
- **Basic idea** -- the unit being repeated
- **Presentation function** -- the context in which the repetition occurs

# Key Properties
1. Statement: basic idea over tonic harmony (I)
2. Response: repetition over dominant harmony (V or V7)
3. The overall progression is tonic prolongational (I-V-I when V resolves at the start of continuation)
4. Creates harmonic motion within the presentation while maintaining tonic prolongation

# Construction / Recognition
- Basic idea in mm. 1-2 supported by I
- Repetition in mm. 3-4 supported by V (or V7)
- V resolves to I at the downbeat of m. 5 (start of continuation)

# Context & Application
Statement-response is one of the most common types of presentation repetition, creating a slight harmonic contrast that adds interest while maintaining the tonic prolongational framework essential to presentation function.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 1, p. 10, ex. 1.1): Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 1-4: statement on I (mm. 1-2), response on V7 (mm. 3-4), resolving to I at m. 5.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Basic idea and presentation function
## Enables
- Presentation function with harmonic variety
## Related
- Exact repetition and sequential repetition (the other two presentation repetition types)
## Contrasts With
- Exact repetition: no harmonic change between statement and repetition
- Sequential repetition: transposition to different scale-degrees

# Common Errors
- Failing to recognize that the V in a statement-response is prolongational, not cadential

# Common Confusions
- The V in a statement-response does not function as a cadential dominant; it is a neighboring chord within a tonic prolongation
