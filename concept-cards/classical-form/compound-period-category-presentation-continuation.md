# Quick Definition
In this compound period category, both the eight-measure antecedent and consequent units are built as sentences (presentation + continuation). The first sentence ends with a weak cadence, and the second sentence repeats the first but ends with a PAC.

# Core Definition
A compound period of this category is made up of two eight-measure sentences. The first sentence ends with a weak cadence and the second, a repetition of the first, ends with a PAC. This represents the full-fledged development of the simple period whose four-measure antecedent has a miniature sentential design. The restatement of the basic idea within the opening presentation is heard as a *repeat*, so it does not poorly anticipate the real *return* of the basic idea at the start of the compound consequent.

# Prerequisites
- **Compound period**: The overarching theme type
- **Sentence**: The internal structure of each eight-measure unit

# Key Properties
1. Both compound antecedent and consequent are built as eight-measure sentences
2. Opening presentation repeats the basic idea as statement-response -- this is a *repeat*, not the *return* that signals consequent
3. The consequent's cadential progression is typically more expanded than the antecedent's
4. The consequent's continuation may become continuation => cadential with an expanded cadential progression
5. Extension or expansion of the continuation in the consequent is common for cadential emphasis

# Construction / Recognition
1. **Large antecedent (mm. 1-8)**: presentation (mm. 1-4, statement-response repetition of b.i.) + continuation (mm. 5-8) ending with weak cadence (HC)
2. **Large consequent (mm. 9-16)**: restates presentation + alters continuation to end with PAC
3. Both units display sentential characteristics: fragmentation, harmonic acceleration, model-sequence technique
4. The consequent's continuation may become continuation => cadential with an ECP
5. The cadential progression of the consequent is typically more expanded than the antecedent's

# Context & Application
This is one of the three main categories of compound period. The sentential design of each eight-measure unit combines the forward-driving character of the sentence with the symmetrical closure principle of the period. The extension-expansion relationship can be complex: the continuation may be "extended" (adding extra material) in a way that results in an "expansion" of the overall consequent.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Concerto No. 2 in B-flat, Op. 19, iii, mm. 1-17: both antecedent and consequent as standard sentences; consequent expanded by one bar for cadential emphasis
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in C Minor, Op. 10/1, ii, mm. 1-16: antecedent with regular continuation; consequent alters continuation to continuation => cadential supported by ECP

# Common Errors
- Confusing two adjacent sentences for a compound period -- the cadential differentiation (weak then strong) is what binds them into a periodic structure
- Failing to notice the expansion of the consequent's cadential function relative to the antecedent's

# Common Confusions
- The consequent's cadential expansion relative to the antecedent is analogous to the expanded contrasting idea in a simple period's consequent
- The repeat of the basic idea in the presentation (mm. 3-4) is functionally different from its return at m. 9 (consequent signal)
