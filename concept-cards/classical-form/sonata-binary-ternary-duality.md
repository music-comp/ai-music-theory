# Quick Definition
Sonata form exhibits both ternary characteristics (three functional sections: exposition, development, recapitulation) and binary characteristics (two repeated halves), and the debate over which is primary is a false dichotomy.

# Formal Definition
Just as theorists and historians have debated whether small ternary is essentially two parts or three parts, so too have they disagreed over the fundamental partitioning of sonata form. The arguments on each side are largely the same as with the small ternary, and the dispute is likewise founded on a false dichotomy, since differing modes of organization generate both bipartite and tripartite characteristics in sonata form. Caplin's focus on formal functionality emphasizes the ternary aspects (three functions), but without devaluing the manifestly binary traits (exposition repeat; development-recapitulation repeat).

# Construction/Recognition
- **Ternary traits**: three distinct formal functions (exposition, development, recapitulation); the recapitulation brings back exposition material
- **Binary traits**: the exposition is repeated; development and recapitulation are sometimes repeated together; the form resembles the rounded binary version of small ternary
- Both perspectives are valid and complementary
- The form-functional approach privileges the ternary view because three distinct functions are at work

# Musical Context
This duality is not merely theoretical — it has practical implications for performance (observing repeats) and analysis (how we conceptualize the form's large-scale design). Earlier 18th-century practice, when the development/recapitulation was more commonly repeated, emphasized the binary nature more strongly.

# Examples
- All standard sonata-form movements exhibit both binary and ternary characteristics

# Common Confusions
- The debate is a "false dichotomy" — sonata form is neither purely binary nor purely ternary
- The binary traits (repeats) are more prominent in earlier classical works (pre-1780)
- Emphasizing ternary aspects does not mean the binary traits are unimportant
