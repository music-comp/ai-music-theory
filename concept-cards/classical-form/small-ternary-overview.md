# Quick Definition
A three-part theme type (A-B-A') consisting of an exposition (closed thematic unit), a contrasting middle (structurally open, emphasizing dominant), and a recapitulation (return ensuring complete closure in the home key).

# Core Definition
The small ternary embraces "two basic notions: (1) a relatively closed thematic unit is juxtaposed with a structurally open unit of contrasting content and formal organization, and (2) the original unit is brought back, but in a manner that ensures complete closure of the theme" (Ch. 1, p. 13). The three sections are designated exposition (A), contrasting middle (B), and recapitulation (A'). The A section is typically a conventional theme type (sentence or period) closing with PAC. The B section achieves contrast primarily through "harmonic means -- namely, by an emphasis on dominant" (Ch. 1, p. 14). The A' must "at the very least, begin with the basic idea of the A section and close with a perfect authentic cadence in the home key" (Ch. 1, p. 14).

# Prerequisites
- **Sentence and period** -- the theme types typically used for the A section
- **Formal function** -- understanding exposition, contrasting middle, and recapitulation functions

# Key Properties
1. A (exposition): conventional theme (sentence or period), closes with PAC; may modulate to subordinate key
2. B (contrasting middle): emphasizes dominant harmony, looser organization, often new motivic content
3. A' (recapitulation): returns basic idea from A, closes with PAC in home key
4. The B section's contrast is primarily harmonic (dominant emphasis), not merely melodic or textural
5. The simplest B section is a standing on the dominant (exclusive dominant prolongation)
6. The A' may be significantly reduced (e.g., a single consequent phrase)
7. If the A section modulated, the A' must adjust to remain in the home key

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify a closed thematic unit (A) with PAC
2. Look for a contrasting section (B) emphasizing dominant harmony
3. Verify the return (A') with the basic idea and PAC in the home key
4. The B section may have a nonconventional form (neither genuine sentence nor period)

# Context & Application
The small ternary is the third principal theme type alongside sentence and period. It is most commonly found as main themes in slow movements and minuets/trios. Its three-part design introduces the concept of formal return, which operates at larger scales in sonata form. Detailed treatment in Chapter 6.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 1, pp. 13-15, ex. 1.4): Beethoven, Op. 15, ii, mm. 1-18:
- A = period (mm. 1-8, PAC): basic idea + contrasting idea -> HC; consequent -> PAC
- B = contrasting middle (mm. 9-14): new idea beginning on dominant, exact repetition altered to HC at m. 12, then standing on the dominant (mm. 12-14) with fragmentation and V/V neighboring chords
- A' = single consequent phrase (mm. 15-18): basic idea returns, new contrasting idea, PAC in home key

# Common Errors
- Assuming the B section requires dramatic motivic contrast (harmonic contrast is primary)
- Expecting the A' to reproduce the entire A section

# Common Confusions
- The B section's contrast is primarily harmonic (dominant emphasis), not just motivic or textural. "New melodic material alone does not define the contrasting middle"
- The recapitulation need not restate the entire exposition; a single consequent phrase suffices
- The B section often has a nonconventional form; it should not be forced into sentence or period categories
- Detailed treatment occurs in Chapter 6
