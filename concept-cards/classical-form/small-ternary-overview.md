# Quick Definition
A three-part theme type (A-B-A') consisting of an exposition, a contrasting middle, and a recapitulation, based on the juxtaposition of a closed thematic unit with an open, contrasting section and a return that ensures complete closure.

# Formal Definition
The small ternary embraces two basic notions: (1) a relatively closed thematic unit (exposition) is juxtaposed with a structurally open unit of contrasting content and formal organization (contrasting middle), and (2) the original unit is brought back in a manner that ensures complete closure (recapitulation). The three sections are designated exposition (A), contrasting middle (B), and recapitulation (A'). The A section is typically a conventional theme type (sentence or period) that closes with a PAC. The B section achieves contrast primarily through harmonic means--emphasizing dominant harmony--and has a looser organization. The A' section returns the basic idea and closes with a PAC in the home key.

# Construction/Recognition
- A (exposition): conventional theme (sentence or period), closes with PAC; may modulate to subordinate key
- B (contrasting middle): emphasizes dominant harmony, often opens and closes with dominant, looser organization, new motivic content
- A' (recapitulation): returns basic idea from A, closes with PAC in home key; often reduced (may be a single consequent phrase)
- The B section's contrast is primarily harmonic (dominant emphasis), not merely melodic or textural

# Musical Context
The small ternary is the third principal theme type alongside sentence and period. It is most commonly found as main themes in slow movements and minuets/trios. Its three-part design introduces the concept of formal return, which operates at larger scales in sonata form.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 15, ii, mm. 1-18: A = period (mm. 1-8, PAC), B = contrasting middle emphasizing dominant (mm. 9-14, HC + standing on dominant), A' = single consequent phrase (mm. 15-18, PAC)

# Common Confusions
- The B section's contrast is primarily harmonic (dominant emphasis), not just motivic or textural. New melodic material alone does not define the contrasting middle.
- The recapitulation need not restate the entire exposition; a single consequent phrase suffices if it returns the basic idea and closes with PAC in the home key.
