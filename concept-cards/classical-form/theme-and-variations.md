# Quick Definition
A full-movement form consisting of a main theme (built as a small ternary or small binary) followed by an indefinite number of varied repetitions, sometimes concluding with a coda.

# Formal Definition
As a formal category, theme and variations consists of a main theme (small ternary or small binary) followed by varied repetitions. The variations adhere to the overall form and intrathematic functions of the theme. Three standard deviations occur: (1) a minore/maggiore variation altering the harmonic-tonal scheme; (2) extensions or interpolations before the final variation; (3) a closing section or coda after the final variation. Haydn employs an "alternating variations" (double variations) form using two alternating themes.

# Construction/Recognition
- Main theme in small ternary or small binary form
- Each variation preserves the overall form and intrathematic functions
- One variation typically in the opposite mode (minore/maggiore)
- Extensions or interpolations may precede the final variation
- The final variation may be followed by a coda
- The coda often brings back the original unvaried theme

# Musical Context
Theme and variations is the least complex full-movement form and is used in both slow movements and as independent compositions.

# Examples
- Haydn's alternating variations: two themes alternate and are varied on each reappearance
- Various theme-and-variations movements by Mozart and Beethoven

# Related Concepts

# Common Confusions
- Theme and variations as a formal category should be distinguished from variation technique as a compositional procedure.
