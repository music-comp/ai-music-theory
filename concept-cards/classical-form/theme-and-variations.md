# Quick Definition
A full-movement form consisting of a main theme (built as a small ternary or small binary) followed by an indefinite number of varied repetitions, with three standard modifications: minore variation, added extensions/interpolations, and a closing coda.

# Core Definition
As a formal category, theme and variations consists of a main theme constructed as either a small ternary or small binary (the former appearing somewhat more frequently), followed by an indefinite number of varied repetitions. The variations adhere not only to the overall form (ternary or binary) but also to the specific arrangement of intrathematic functions. Three standard deviations from the regular succession of equal-size variations are: (1) a minore/maggiore variation altering the harmonic-tonal scheme (sometimes so extensively that it resembles an entirely different theme); (2) extensions or interpolations before the final variation, breaking the regular succession; and (3) a closing section or full coda after the final variation, often bringing back the original unvaried theme and providing the structural expansions typical of movement endings.

# Prerequisites
- **Small ternary**: The primary formal model for the theme
- **Small binary**: The alternative formal model

# Key Properties
1. Main theme is invariably a small ternary or small binary
2. Each variation preserves the overall form and intrathematic functions
3. One variation typically in the opposite mode (minore/maggiore)
4. The minore variation may alter the formal structure (small ternary becoming small binary)
5. Extensions or interpolations may precede the final variation
6. A coda often follows, sometimes restoring the original unvaried theme
7. The small binary achieves prominence because variation-form restatements avoid the redundancy of recapitulation
8. Compared with other full-movement forms, it is the least complex

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the main theme's form (small ternary or small binary)
- Track which intrathematic functions are preserved in each variation
- Identify the minore/maggiore variation by its altered harmonic scheme
- Look for extensions or interpolations before the final variation
- Check for a coda that brings back the original theme or provides cadential expansion

# Context & Application
Theme and variations is well represented in Haydn, Mozart, and Beethoven as both independent compositions and individual movements. As a formal category, it is the least complex of the full-movement forms and presents the fewest analytical problems. The coda provides the only real opportunity for creating structural expansions (such as enormous cadential expansions) and achieves formal circularity by restoring the unvaried theme.

# Examples
- Haydn's alternating variations: two themes alternate and are varied on each reappearance
- Various theme-and-variations movements by Mozart and Beethoven featuring small ternary or small binary themes

# Common Errors
- Confusing variation technique (a compositional procedure) with theme and variations (a formal category)
- Expecting all variations to preserve the theme's formal structure exactly (the minore may alter it)
- Overlooking the coda's special role in providing structural expansion

# Common Confusions
- **Formal category vs. compositional procedure**: Theme and variations as a formal category is distinct from variation technique as a compositional procedure used throughout all classical forms
- **Small binary prominence**: The small binary achieves unusual prominence in variation movements because its lack of recapitulation avoids the redundancy that would come from multiple restatements of the opening idea
