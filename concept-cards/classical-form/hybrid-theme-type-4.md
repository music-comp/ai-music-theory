# Quick Definition
Hybrid type 4 consists of a compound basic idea (basic idea + contrasting idea without cadence) followed by a consequent phrase that brings back the basic idea and closes with a PAC. An "infrequently occurring type" that "strongly resembles a *period*" except that "the contrasting idea in mm. 3-4 of the theme does not bring a weak cadence" (Ch. 4, p. 103).

# Core Definition
"Following a compound basic idea, the use of a consequent phrase is motivated by the effective technique of bringing back the opening basic idea after an intervening contrasting idea, just as happens in the period form. But this hybrid differs from the period in one crucial respect: it lacks a weak cadence in m. 4 of the theme" (Ch. 4, p. 103). The consequent often displays increased harmonic activity compared to the c.b.i., "imparting a degree of continuational expression as well" (Ch. 4, p. 104). The tonic prolongation of the c.b.i. "may extend into the consequent phrase" so that "a single tonic prolongation underlies" much of the theme (Ch. 4, p. 104).

- **Compound basic idea** -- the hybrid phrase function without cadence

# Key Properties
1. First phrase: compound basic idea (b.i. + c.i., NO cadence)
2. Second phrase: consequent (basic idea returns + contrasting idea + PAC)
3. Differs from period ONLY in lacking the weak cadence at m. 4
4. The basic idea appears twice (once in c.b.i., once in consequent) -- like sentence and period
5. Infrequently occurring type
6. Consequent often has increased harmonic activity (some continuational quality)
7. Tonic prolongation may extend from c.b.i. into consequent
8. Return of basic idea in consequent often harmonized differently from its first appearance

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify mm. 1-2 as basic idea, mm. 3-4 as contrasting idea
2. Confirm NO cadence at end of m. 4
3. Confirm that mm. 5-6 bring a RETURN of the basic idea (possibly reharmonized)
4. Identify cadential closure (usually PAC) at end of theme
5. Note: the consequent may show harmonic acceleration relative to the c.b.i., but the return of the basic idea identifies it as consequent, not continuation

# Context & Application
Hybrid type 4 "closely resembles a *period*" on the spectrum of theme types (Ch. 4, p. 111). It is the only hybrid type in which the basic idea appears twice. "More often than with the period, the return of the basic idea in mm. 5-6 is supported by a different harmony" (Ch. 4, p. 104), sometimes delaying the completion of the opening tonic prolongation until the consequent is under way.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 4, p. 103, ex. 4.4): Beethoven, String Quartet in G, Op. 18/2, iv, mm. 21-28: compound basic idea (no harmonic motion into m. 24 prohibits any cadence); consequent brings back the basic idea (now with dominant harmony) and closes with emphatic PAC.
**Example 2** (Ch. 4, p. 104, ex. 4.12): Haydn, Symphony No. 87 in A, iii, mm. 1-8: II in mm. 3-5 creates a single tonic prolongation through m. 7; the cadential progression is compressed into the final measures.

## Contrasts With
- Period: the antecedent ends with a weak cadence; the c.b.i. does not
- Hybrid type 3: the continuation brings new material; the consequent returns the basic idea

# Common Errors
- Calling this a period when the first phrase lacks a cadence
- Ignoring the continuational qualities of the consequent phrase (harmonic acceleration relative to the c.b.i.)
- Missing the return of the basic idea and labeling the second phrase as continuation

# Common Confusions
- Hybrid type 4 vs. period: the only difference is the absence of a cadence at m. 4 -- if a cadence is present, it is a period; if not, it is hybrid type 4
- The consequent phrase of hybrid type 4 may have continuational qualities (harmonic acceleration relative to the c.b.i.) -- this does not change its consequent identity, which is established by the return of the basic idea
- This type is not used in compound (16-m.) periods because the basic idea would appear at both m. 5 and m. 9, spoiling the effect of the higher-level consequent
