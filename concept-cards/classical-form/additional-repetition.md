# Quick Definition
A loosening technique in which the basic idea or the entire presentation phrase is repeated more times than in a tight-knit context, creating asymmetrical grouping and functional redundancy.

# Formal Definition
Additional repetition takes two forms: (1) additional repetition of the basic idea, where the opening idea is stated three times instead of the normative two, creating an asymmetrical (2 x 3) grouping structure; and (2) repetition of the entire presentation phrase, where the four-measure presentation is restated before the continuation begins, creating functional redundancy. A repeated four-measure presentation gives rise to an eight-measure presentation, suggesting a sixteen-measure sentence, but the presentation of a true compound sentence consists of a repeated compound basic idea, not a repeated presentation. The distinction matters: a repeated presentation has four statements of the basic idea (looser), while a repeated compound basic idea has only two (tighter).

# Construction/Recognition
1. **Basic idea repetition**: Three statements of the basic idea instead of two; grouping (2 x 3) instead of (2 x 2)
2. **Presentation repetition**: Entire 4-measure presentation repeated before continuation; basic idea appears four times total
3. Both create functional redundancy -- a criterion for loose organization
4. Both create asymmetrical grouping structures
5. Presentation repetition often elides with the beginning of the continuation (enhancing rhythmic continuity)
6. Rarely found in main themes; typical of subordinate themes

# Musical Context
Additional repetition is one of the most straightforward loosening techniques. It extends the theme's beginning and delays the arrival of continuation and cadential functions. The technique is especially appropriate for subordinate themes because it creates the redundancy and asymmetry characteristic of loose organization. A single four-measure presentation eliding with continuation is rare in tight-knit themes but common in subordinate themes following a repeated presentation.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in D, K. 576, i, mm. 28-33 (first subordinate theme: basic idea stated three times due to harmonic predicament -- second statement sequenced to II requires third statement to return to I)
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in A, Op. 2/2, i, mm. 92-98 (second subordinate theme: four-measure presentation repeated; end of repeated presentation elides with continuation)

# Common Confusions
- A repeated presentation (4 basic idea statements) is NOT the same as a compound sentence's repeated compound basic idea (2 statements) -- the former is looser
- Additional repetition of the basic idea may be motivated by harmonic necessity (as in ex. 8.1, where a sequence to II requires a third statement to complete the tonic prolongation)
- A repeated presentation is seldom found with main themes; its appearance signals subordinate-theme loosening
- Elision between repeated presentation and continuation is a sign of looseness, not of tight-knit organization
