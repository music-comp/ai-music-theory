# Quick Definition
A normatively eight-measure theme type built from two four-measure phrases -- an antecedent ending with a weak cadence and a consequent ending with a stronger cadence -- based on the principle of cadential differentiation.

# Core Definition
"The period is normatively an eight-measure structure divided into two four-measure phrases" (Ch. 1, p. 12). The period is organized by cadential differentiation: "if an initial unit ending with a weak cadence is repeated and brought to a fuller cadential close, then we can say, following traditional usage, that the first unit is an antecedent to the following consequent" (Ch. 1, p. 12). The antecedent begins with a basic idea followed by a contrasting idea leading to a weak cadence (HC or IAC). The consequent returns the basic idea and closes with a stronger cadence (PAC).

# Prerequisites
- **Basic idea** -- opens both antecedent and consequent
- **Contrasting idea** -- follows the basic idea in each phrase
- **Antecedent** -- the first phrase with weak cadence
- **Consequent** -- the second phrase with stronger cadence
- **Cadential differentiation** -- the organizing principle

# Key Properties
1. Eight measures: 4 (antecedent) + 4 (consequent)
2. Antecedent: basic idea (2) + contrasting idea (2) ending with weak cadence (HC or IAC)
3. Consequent: basic idea returns (2) + contrasting idea (2) ending with stronger cadence (PAC)
4. Organized by cadential differentiation (weak then strong cadence)
5. The contrasting idea of the consequent may differ from that of the antecedent
6. Distinguished from the sentence by the absence of immediate repetition and by cadential differentiation

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify a two-measure basic idea followed by a contrasting idea (not a repetition)
2. Confirm weak cadence at the end of four measures (antecedent)
3. Verify the basic idea returns in the following phrase
4. Confirm stronger cadence closing the consequent

# Context & Application
One of the three principal theme types (with sentence and small ternary). Commonly used for main themes, slow movement themes, and minuet themes. Detailed treatment in Chapter 4.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 1, p. 12, ex. 1.3): Mozart, K. 525, ii, mm. 1-8: antecedent (basic idea mm. 1-2, contrasting idea mm. 3-4 with fragmentation and harmonic acceleration, HC) + consequent (basic idea returns mm. 5-6, distinctly different contrasting idea mm. 7-8, PAC).

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Basic idea, contrasting idea, antecedent, consequent, cadential differentiation
## Enables
- Analysis of main themes, compound periods, hybrid themes
- Understanding of A sections in small ternary forms
## Related
- Sentence and small ternary (the other two principal theme types)
## Contrasts With
- Sentence: the sentence is organized by presentation-continuation (repetition then destabilization), while the period is organized by cadential differentiation (weak then strong cadence)

# Common Errors
- Confusing a period with a sentence when the basic idea is followed by a contrasting idea (not a repetition)

# Common Confusions
- In a period, the basic idea is followed by a contrasting idea (not repeated), distinguishing it from a sentence's presentation phrase
- The contrasting idea in the consequent may be entirely different from the antecedent's contrasting idea
