# Quick Definition
An eight-measure theme type built from two four-measure phrases--an antecedent ending with a weak cadence and a consequent ending with a stronger cadence--based on the principle of cadential differentiation.

# Formal Definition
The period is normatively an eight-measure structure divided into two four-measure phrases. An initial unit (antecedent) ending with a weak cadence is repeated and brought to a fuller cadential close (consequent). The antecedent begins with a basic idea followed by a contrasting idea leading to HC or IAC. The consequent returns the basic idea and closes with PAC. Distinguished from the sentence by the absence of immediate repetition and by the principle of cadential differentiation.

# Construction/Recognition
- Eight measures: 4 (antecedent) + 4 (consequent)
- Antecedent: basic idea + contrasting idea -> weak cadence (HC or IAC)
- Consequent: basic idea returns + contrasting idea -> stronger cadence (PAC)

# Musical Context
One of the three principal theme types (with sentence and small ternary). Detailed treatment in Chapter 4.

# Examples
- Mozart, K. 525, ii, mm. 1-8: antecedent (HC) + consequent (PAC) with different contrasting ideas

# Common Confusions
- In a period, the basic idea is followed by a contrasting idea (not repeated), distinguishing it from a sentence's presentation phrase.
