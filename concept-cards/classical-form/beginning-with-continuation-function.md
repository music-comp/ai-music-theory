# Quick Definition
A subordinate theme may begin with continuation function -- most effectively expressed through sequential harmonic progressions and model-sequence technique -- bypassing a conventional initiating function and creating a loosened formal expression through formal dissonance.

# Core Definition
The most effective way of expressing continuation function at the start of a subordinate theme is by means of sequential harmonic progressions, usually in connection with model-sequence technique. Since the other continuational characteristics (fragmentation, harmonic acceleration, increased surface rhythm) create their most palpable effect in relation to an immediately preceding initiating function, they are infrequently used by themselves to begin a theme. They may, however, accompany the presence of sequential harmonies. A continuation function replaces a true structural beginning most often with a second or third subordinate theme of a group, where the start of the new theme can be understood to "continue" the subordinate group as a whole. Beginning the FIRST subordinate theme with continuation is an especially bold effect.

# Prerequisites
- **Continuation function**: The function being deployed at the theme's start
- **Subordinate theme**: The formal context
- **Model-sequence technique**: The primary compositional means
- **Intrinsic vs. contextual functionality**: The theoretical framework explaining the dissonance

# Key Properties
1. Sequential harmonic progressions are the most effective signal of continuation at a theme's start
2. Fragmentation, harmonic acceleration alone are insufficient signals without preceding material
3. Most common with second or third subordinate themes in a group
4. Beginning the FIRST subordinate theme with continuation is especially bold
5. Creates formal dissonance: intrinsically medial function in contextually initiating position
6. A tighter second theme may compensate with its own presentation function
7. Model-sequence technique is the primary compositional device

# Construction / Recognition
- Look for sequential harmonic progressions at the start of a subordinate theme
- Identify model-sequence technique (a model of 1-4 measures sequenced)
- Note the absence of a conventional presentation or compound basic idea
- Check whether fragmentation relative to preceding units reinforces the continuation effect
- Assess whether a later theme in the group compensates with clearer initiation

# Context & Application
Beginning with continuation function is a powerful loosening technique. When it opens the first subordinate theme of a group, the entire group lacks a sense of structural initiation -- a remarkably bold formal statement. In such cases, a more tightly knit second subordinate theme often emphasizes its own presentation function to compensate for the lack of formal initiation in the first theme.

# Examples
- Haydn, String Quartet in B minor, Op. 64/2, i (Ch. 12, Ex. 12.7): second subordinate theme begins at m. 46 with a 1-m. model sequenced by descending thirds; the 1-m. size creates fragmentation relative to the preceding 2-m. cadential idea
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in A, Op. 2/2, i (Ch. 12, Ex. 12.8): FIRST subordinate theme begins with minor-mode shift and a 3-m. model sequenced twice by ascending minor thirds; the entire theme group lacks structural initiation; the tighter second subordinate theme compensates with its own presentation function

# Common Errors
- Labeling sequential passages as "presentation" because they open the theme
- Failing to distinguish between genuine tonic prolongation (presentation) and sequential progression (continuation)
- Not recognizing the compensatory function of later themes in the group

# Common Confusions
- **Presentation vs. continuation at theme's start**: If the harmonic context is sequential (not tonic-prolongational), it is continuation regardless of its position
- **Fragmentation alone is insufficient**: Sequential harmonies are needed to signal continuation at a theme's start
- **Bold vs. common**: Beginning the FIRST subordinate theme with continuation is bolder than beginning the second or third
