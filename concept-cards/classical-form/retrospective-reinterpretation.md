# Quick Definition
Retrospective reinterpretation occurs when the listener's initial interpretation of a formal function is revised as the music moves forward. The double arrow symbol (=>) stands for "becomes" and represents this process -- e.g., "continuation => cadential" means what initially seems like continuation is retrospectively understood as cadential.

# Core Definition
"Because of how listeners perceive music, we sometimes find ourselves in situations where our initial interpretation of formal functionality needs to be revised as the music moves forward in time" (Ch. 2, p. 61). "In other words, as we first hear a passage, we may believe that it expresses such and such a function; but as the music continues, and we perceive more information, we may change our opinion and come to believe that a different formal function is more appropriately at hand" (Ch. 2, p. 61). "Following its introduction by theorist Janet Schmalfeldt, the double arrow (=>), which is read as 'becomes,' has been adopted by many scholars and is employed often in this text" (Ch. 2, p. 62). The concept "strongly relates to that of form-functional *fusion*, yet the two ideas are not identical. The former concerns a dynamic process of listening, whereby we change our interpretation of a functional situation in light of our hearing new musical material; the latter refers more generally to the presence of two formal functions occurring within the same grouping unit" (Ch. 2, p. 62).

# Prerequisites
- **Continuation function** -- the most common "initial" interpretation
- **Cadential function** -- the most common "revised" interpretation
- **Form-functional fusion** -- the related but distinct concept

# Key Properties
1. Concerns a dynamic process of listening, not a static labeling
2. The double arrow (=>) reads as "becomes"
3. Both initial and revised interpretations are valid for their temporal contexts
4. Restricted to cases of genuine reinterpretation, not all fusion situations
5. Introduced by theorist Janet Schmalfeldt
6. The "/" symbol is used for other types of fusion (e.g., main theme/transition fusion)
7. Most common application: continuation => cadential (when an ECP underlies the entire second phrase)

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify a passage where the formal function seems to change as it unfolds
- Ask: does the beginning suggest one function, but the whole passage turns out to express another?
- If continuation begins but an ECP underlies the entire phrase: continuation => cadential
- If an antecedent appears to close with a PAC in a new key but the consequent returns to the home key: PAC => HC (reinterpreted half cadence)
- Use => only for genuine retrospective reinterpretation, not for all cases of fusion

# Context & Application
The concept appears throughout Caplin's analysis. Its primary application is in sentences where the continuation phrase is supported by an expanded cadential progression (ECP), making cadential function prominent from the start. Another key application is the reinterpreted half cadence in periods. The concept acknowledges the temporal nature of musical perception -- formal analysis is not just about labeling static structures but about understanding how meaning unfolds in time.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 2, p. 62, ex. 2.24): Haydn, String Quartet in D minor, Op. 42, i, mm. 1-8: the continuation phrase brings fragmentation and harmonic acceleration but is supported entirely by an ECP. "The term *continuation => cadential* aptly describes the functional situation."
**Example 2** (Ch. 3, p. 91, ex. 3.19): Haydn, String Quartet in G, Op. 64/4, i, mm. 1-4: a PAC in the dominant key at the end of the antecedent "becomes" an HC when the consequent returns to the home key (PAC => HC).

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Form-functional fusion (the broader concept of two functions in one unit)
- Continuation and cadential functions (the most common pair for reinterpretation)
## Enables
- Nuanced formal analysis that respects the temporal nature of listening
- Accurate labeling of ECP-supported continuation phrases
- Understanding of the reinterpreted half cadence
## Related
- Reinterpreted half cadence (a specific application of =>)
- Hybrid type 2 (where context determines whether a phrase is "cadential" vs. "continuation => cadential")
## Contrasts With
- Simple fusion (represented by /), which does not involve changing one's interpretation over time
- Static formal labels that ignore the listener's temporal experience

# Common Errors
- Using => for all cases of functional fusion (reserve it for genuine reinterpretation)
- Ignoring the temporal dimension -- both the initial and revised interpretations are valid for their respective moments
- Confusing => with /: the slash is used for thematic fusion (e.g., main theme/transition)

# Common Confusions
- Retrospective reinterpretation and fusion are related but not identical -- reinterpretation is a dynamic listening process; fusion is the static presence of two functions in one unit
- The => symbol is restricted to cases where the listener genuinely revises an interpretation
- Both interpretations (before and after reinterpretation) are valid -- the revised one does not "cancel" the initial one
