# Quick Definition
The main theme of a rondo, always a tight-knit, conventional form (typically small ternary or period) that closes with a PAC in the home key, serving as the basis for all subsequent refrains.

# Core Definition
The first refrain functions as the main theme of the rondo. It is almost always a conventional, tight-knit theme closing in the home key with a PAC. In the majority of cases, it is built as a small ternary (or rounded binary) or, less often, a small binary. The refrain may also have a simpler form, such as a period or hybrid. A closing section is sometimes added after the cadence, and even a second main theme in the home key may appear. As a general tendency, main themes in rondo forms are more tightly knit than those in sonata form: a rondo theme always closes with a PAC (never a HC), and rondo refrains tend to be conventional and symmetrical, whereas the looser, nonconventional themes frequently found in sonata form rarely appear in a rondo. Sonata-rondos generally employ a wider variety of main-theme types, with the small ternary and the sixteen-measure period particularly favored.

# Construction / Recognition
- Always closes with a PAC in the home key
- Tight-knit, conventional, symmetrical form
- Most common types: small ternary (rounded binary), small binary, period, hybrid
- In sonata-rondos: wider variety of types; small ternary and sixteen-measure period favored
- May be followed by a closing section or additional main themes
- In sonata-rondos: may form part of a main-theme group with multiple PAC-ending themes
- Never ends with a HC (unlike some sonata main themes)

# Context & Application
The rondo main theme must be memorable and recognizable because it returns multiple times throughout the movement. Its tight-knit, conventional character ensures immediate recognition on each return. The theme's inherent stability contrasts with the variety and instability of the intervening couplets.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C, K. 545, iii: Simple period as rondo main theme
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Hob. XVI:49, iii: Small ternary as main theme
- Beethoven, Violin Sonata in D, Op. 12/1, iii: Main theme followed by main-theme group in sonata-rondo

# Common Confusions
- Rondo main themes are characteristically tighter knit than sonata main themes. A loose, nonconventional theme that works well in a sonata would be unusual in a rondo.
- The closing PAC is a strict requirement for rondo main themes. Students should not expect to find HC-ending rondo themes, unlike in sonata form where HC endings are common.
