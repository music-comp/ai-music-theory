# Quick Definition
The central contrasting section of a large ternary form, typically set in the minor mode (minore) of the home key, standing between two statements of the main theme and often based on a distorted or incomplete small ternary.

# Core Definition
An interior theme occupies the central position in a large ternary form, standing between statements of a main theme. It contrasts through changes in melodic-motivic material, texture, accompanimental figurations, and most strikingly through an immediate change in modality (usually to minore). The interior theme generally resides in the same tonality as the main theme but in the opposite mode, fundamentally distinguishing it from a subordinate theme (which resides in the standard subordinate key). Its formal organization is usually related to the small ternary or small binary, but is often altered, truncated, or left incomplete. The final harmony is usually the dominant of the home key, motivating the main theme's return. The prominence of minor modality can bring a Sturm und Drang affect, but the primary characteristics of harmony, tonality, and phrase structure make the interior theme an entirely different formal entity from a development core.

# Prerequisites
- **Large ternary form**: The formal context for the interior theme
- **Small ternary**: The form on which the interior theme is typically based
- **Minore/maggiore contrast**: The modal shift that defines the interior theme

# Key Properties
1. Located between two statements of the main theme
2. Usually shifts immediately to the minor mode (minore) of the home key
3. May reside in related regions: vi, bVI, or IV (but never the standard subordinate key)
4. Formal organization based on small ternary or binary, often distorted:
   - A' section initially closed, then reopened (deceptive cadence on repeat)
   - A' section lacks cadential closure (premature dominant arrival)
   - A' section eliminated (truncated small ternary: A + B only)
   - Both B and A' sections eliminated (only modulating A + retransition)
5. Final harmony is usually dominant of the home key
6. Often brings Sturm und Drang affect
7. Incomplete forms are more common than complete ones

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the immediate modal shift at the interior theme's beginning
- Determine the underlying small ternary model and assess what portions are complete
- Note whether the theme ends with home-key dominant or achieves cadential closure
- Distinguish between a contrasting middle and a retransition at the theme's end
- Check whether the tonal region is the home key's minore (most common) or another region

# Context & Application
Interior themes appear in the second part of large ternary slow movements and in similar positions in rondo forms. The distinction between a contrasting middle and a retransition at the end of an interior theme is subtle: a retransition typically elides with the end of the preceding section, while a contrasting middle forms an independent section. At times, the interior theme can give the impression of a subordinate-theme complex within the tonal framework of the minore (with transition, subordinate theme, and retransition functions identifiable), but this does not make it a genuine subordinate theme.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Trio in A-flat, Hob. XV:14, ii, mm. 17-34: Small binary; A' initially closed in first ending, reopened by deceptive cadence in second ending
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Hob. XVI:52, ii, mm. 28-33: A' gets "stuck" on the dominant (premature dominant arrival)
- Haydn, String Quartet in B-flat, Op. 64/3, ii, mm. 47-58: Truncated small ternary (A + B only); exposition repeated, then contrasting middle leads to main theme return

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Large ternary form (the formal context)
- Small ternary (the form on which it is modeled)
- Minore/maggiore contrast (the defining modal shift)

## Enables
- Understanding of interior themes in rondo forms (analogous position)
- Recognition of Sturm und Drang character in non-developmental contexts

## Related
- Interior theme (rondo) (analogous function in rondo forms)
- Incomplete interior theme (the more common variant)
- Retransition (follows or emerges from incomplete interior themes)

## Contrasts With
- Subordinate theme (which resides in the standard subordinate key, not the minore)
- Contrasting middle of small ternary (which is shorter, simpler, and ends on dominant)

# Common Errors
- Labeling the interior theme as a subordinate theme
- Expecting all interior themes to follow a complete small ternary plan
- Confusing a retransition at the end with a contrasting middle

# Common Confusions
- **Interior theme vs. subordinate theme**: An interior theme never resides in the standard subordinate key of the movement; this is the fundamental distinction
- **Contrasting middle vs. retransition**: A retransition typically elides with the preceding section and seems attached to it; a contrasting middle forms an independent section
- **Sturm und Drang affect vs. developmental core**: The interior theme may share the emotional intensity of a core but differs in harmony, tonality, and phrase structure

# Verification Notes
