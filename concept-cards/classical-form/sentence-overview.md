# Quick Definition
A normatively eight-measure theme type consisting of a four-measure presentation phrase (basic idea + repetition) and a four-measure continuation phrase (fragmentation + cadence), first identified as a distinct type by Schoenberg.

# Formal Definition
The sentence is normatively an eight-measure structure that begins with a two-measure basic idea, which is immediately repeated to form a four-measure presentation phrase. The presentation's tonic prolongation and ongoing quality generate demand for a four-measure continuation phrase, which features fragmentation (reduction in unit size), harmonic acceleration (increased rate of harmonic change), and liquidation (systematic elimination of characteristic motives), leading to a cadence. The three formal functions of the sentence--presentation, continuation, and cadential--typically see the latter two fuse into a single continuation phrase.

# Construction/Recognition
- 8 measures: presentation (4) + continuation (4)
- Presentation: basic idea (2) + repetition (2) over tonic prolongation
- Continuation: fragmented units (1 + 1 or smaller) + cadential close
- Types of repetition: statement-response (I -> V), exact, sequential
- Continuation features: fragmentation, harmonic acceleration, sequential progression, liquidation
- Closes with HC (weaker) or PAC (stronger)
- The continuation/cadential fusion is the norm; separate phrases are a loosening technique

# Musical Context
The sentence is one of the three principal theme types of classical music (with period and small ternary), identified by Schoenberg as a distinct form. It is commonly used for main themes in sonata forms and is the most archetypal tight-knit theme type. The sentence model also informs the construction of many looser formal regions.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 1-8: the "most archetypal manifestation" of the sentence; presentation with statement-response repetition (I-V), continuation with fragmentation and liquidation, HC at m. 8
- Beethoven, Op. 135, iii, mm. 3-10: presentation with exact repetition, continuation with sequential repetition (model-sequence, descending fifths), PAC at m. 10

# Common Confusions
- The sentence is not just any 8-measure theme; it must have the specific presentation -> continuation structure with its characteristic processes.
- The sentence was "virtually discovered" by Schoenberg, not by earlier theorists. It is distinct from the period, which is based on cadential differentiation rather than presentation-continuation.
- This chapter provides an overview; detailed treatment of the sentence occurs in Chapter 3.
