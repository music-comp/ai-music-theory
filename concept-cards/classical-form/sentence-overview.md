# Quick Definition
A normatively eight-measure theme type consisting of a four-measure presentation phrase (basic idea + repetition over tonic prolongation) and a four-measure continuation phrase (fragmentation, harmonic acceleration, and cadence), first identified as a distinct type by Schoenberg.

# Core Definition
"The sentence is normatively an eight-measure structure" (Ch. 1, p. 9). It begins with a two-measure basic idea, which is immediately repeated to form a four-measure presentation phrase. The presentation prolongs tonic harmony and creates an ongoing quality that "generates demand for a continuation phrase" (Ch. 1, p. 10). The continuation features fragmentation (reduction in unit size), harmonic acceleration (increased rate of harmonic change), and often liquidation (systematic elimination of characteristic motives), leading to a cadence. The three formal functions -- presentation, continuation, and cadential -- typically see the latter two fuse into a single continuation phrase.

# Prerequisites
- **Basic idea** -- the two-measure building block
- **Presentation function** -- the four-measure opening
- **Continuation function** -- the destabilizing second phrase
- **Cadential function** -- the closing progression

# Key Properties
1. Normatively 8 measures: presentation (4) + continuation (4)
2. Presentation: basic idea (2) + repetition (2) over tonic prolongation
3. Three types of repetition: statement-response (I then V), exact, sequential
4. Continuation features: fragmentation, harmonic acceleration, sequential progression, liquidation
5. Continuation and cadential functions typically fuse into a single phrase
6. Closes with HC (weaker) or PAC (stronger)
7. Identified by Schoenberg as a distinct theme type (the "virtual discoverer")

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the two-measure basic idea
2. Confirm repetition in mm. 3-4 (exact, statement-response, or sequential)
3. Verify tonic prolongation across mm. 1-4 (presentation)
4. Look for fragmentation to one-measure units starting in m. 5
5. Check for harmonic acceleration and/or sequential progression
6. Identify the cadential close (HC or PAC)

# Context & Application
The sentence is one of the three principal theme types of classical music (with period and small ternary). It is commonly used for main themes in sonata forms and is the most archetypal tight-knit theme type. The sentence model also informs the construction of many looser formal regions. Detailed treatment occurs in Chapter 3.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 1, p. 9, ex. 1.1): Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 1-8: "perhaps the most archetypal manifestation of the sentence form in the entire classical repertory." Presentation with statement-response repetition (I-V), continuation with fragmentation (2-measure to 1-measure units), liquidation (motive "a" eliminated), and HC at m. 8.
**Example 2** (Ch. 1, p. 11, ex. 1.2): Beethoven, Op. 135, iii, mm. 3-10: presentation with exact repetition (no harmonic change), continuation with sequential repetition (model-sequence, descending fifths: F-Bb-Eb-Ab-Db), fragmented units bring entirely different melodic material, PAC at m. 10.

# Common Errors
- Calling any 8-measure theme a sentence without verifying presentation + continuation structure
- Confusing motivic repetition with genuine presentation function (must have tonic prolongation)

# Common Confusions
- The sentence was "virtually discovered" by Schoenberg, not by earlier theorists; it is distinct from the period, which is based on cadential differentiation
- Fragmentation concerns unit length, not motivic content: fragments may or may not derive from the basic idea
- This chapter provides an overview; detailed treatment of the sentence occurs in Chapter 3
