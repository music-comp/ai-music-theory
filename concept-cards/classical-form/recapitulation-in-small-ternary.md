# Quick Definition
The A' section of a small ternary form, which brings back the fundamental melodic-motivic material of the exposition and reconfirms the home key with a PAC.

# Core Definition
The recapitulation must minimally satisfy two main requirements: (1) it begins by restating the initial basic idea from the A section, and (2) it closes with a perfect authentic cadence in the home key. If the A section has modulated, the A' section must be adjusted to remain in the home key. The recapitulation may be exact (structurally identical to A), compressed (shorter, typically reducing a period to a single consequent), or expanded (longer, developing motives in new continuations). Whether or not the recapitulation undergoes structural changes (in tonality, harmony, or phrase structure), it may experience ornamental changes (in melody, texture, dynamics, instrumentation). When compressed or expanded, the internal formal functions may change relative to the exposition.

# Prerequisites
- **Small ternary**: The overarching form
- **Exposition in small ternary**: The section being recapitulated

# Key Properties
1. Must restate the initial basic idea from the A section in the home key
2. Must close with a PAC in the home key
3. May be exact, compressed, or expanded relative to the exposition
4. When A modulates, A' must be tonally adjusted
5. Structural changes (harmony, grouping, function) vs. ornamental changes (melody, texture, dynamics)
6. Compressed recapitulation typically reduces 8-m. period to 4-m. consequent
7. Expanded recapitulation may develop motives in new continuation, add deceptive cadences, or include closing sections
8. Formal functions may change (e.g., material that was a consequent in A may become a continuation in A')

# Construction / Recognition
1. **Exact**: Restates A section material in same scheme (may differ in accompaniment, texture)
2. **Compressed**: Eliminates redundant material; often reduces 8-measure period to 4-measure consequent
3. **Expanded**: Develops ideas in new continuation, adds cadential expansion or closing section
4. **Tonal adjustment**: Required when A modulates -- typically turns toward the subdominant at the point where A originally modulated
5. **Change in function**: Material may shift function (e.g., consequent becoming continuation)
6. **Analytical procedure**: Compare A and A' bar by bar, note every change, classify as structural or ornamental, ask "why"

# Context & Application
The recapitulation fulfills the requirement of confirming the home key after the contrasting middle's harmonic instability. When compressed, it eliminates the internal HC (now redundant after B section's dominant emphasis) and the second basic-idea statement (already established in memory). When expanded, it demonstrates that motivic development is not confined to the B section. The tight-knit or loose character of A' depends on the specific modifications: a tonally adjusted A' staying in the home key may be more tightly knit than a modulating A.

# Examples
- Mozart, Eine kleine Nachtmusik, K. 525, ii, mm. 13-16: compressed to single consequent combining elements of both exposition phrases
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in D, Hob. XVI:37, iii, mm. 13-20: tonally adjusted with subdominant turn; closing section transposed to home key
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in D, Op. 28, ii, mm. 17-22: compressed with sequential extension of basic idea
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, ii, mm. 15-24: expanded with new continuation, deceptive cadence, fortissimo outburst, added closing section

# Common Errors
- Calling the dominant-to-tonic resolution between B and A' an authentic cadence -- it is the harmonic initiation of the recapitulation, not cadential closure
- Assuming the A' must exactly replicate the A section -- structural changes are common and analytically significant
- Failing to distinguish ornamental changes (texture, dynamics) from structural changes (harmony, grouping, function)

# Common Confusions
- The dominant-to-tonic resolution between B and A' is NOT a cadence
- A compressed recapitulation is not deficient -- it efficiently fulfills the section's formal requirements
- An altered recapitulation is usually looser than the exposition, but a tonally adjusted A' that stays in the home key may be tighter
