# Quick Definition
The A' section of a small ternary form, which brings back the fundamental melodic-motivic material of the exposition and reconfirms the home key with a PAC.

# Formal Definition
The recapitulation must begin with the basic idea from the A section in the home key and end with a PAC confirming that key. When the exposition modulates, the recapitulation must be tonally adjusted to remain entirely in the home key. The recapitulation may be exact, compressed, or expanded relative to the exposition, and may introduce different formal functions while retaining the original melodic-motivic material.

# Construction/Recognition
1. **Exact**: Restates A section material in same scheme (may differ in accompaniment, texture)
2. **Compressed**: Eliminates redundant material; often reduces 8-measure period to 4-measure consequent
3. **Expanded**: Develops ideas in new continuation, adds cadential expansion or closing section
4. **Tonal adjustment**: Required when A modulates
5. **Change in function**: Material may shift function (e.g., consequent becoming continuation)

# Musical Context
The recapitulation fulfills the requirement of confirming the home key after the contrasting middle's harmonic instability. When compressed, it eliminates the internal HC (now redundant after B section's dominant emphasis) and the second basic-idea statement (already established in memory).

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in G Minor, Op. 49/1, ii, m. 13 (compressed: consequent phrase transposed)
- Mozart, Eine kleine Nachtmusik, K. 525, ii, mm. 13-16 (compressed: single consequent combining elements)
- Haydn, Symphony No. 99, iv, mm. 13-20 (change of function: period becomes sentence)
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, ii, mm. 15-24 (expanded: new continuation, deceptive cadence, closing section)

# Common Confusions
- The dominant-to-tonic resolution between B and A' is NOT a cadence
- A compressed recapitulation is not a deficient one -- it efficiently fulfills the section's formal requirements
