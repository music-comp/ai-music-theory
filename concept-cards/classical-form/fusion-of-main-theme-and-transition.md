# Quick Definition
A formal compression in the recapitulation where the main theme and transition merge into a single thematic structure, typically by eliminating the end of one and the beginning of the other.

# Formal Definition
The main theme and transition usually remain discrete units in the recapitulation, but occasionally they fuse into a single thematic structure. This formal compression is usually brought about by eliminating the end of the main theme and the beginning of the transition and by attaching the close of the latter to what remains of the former. In some cases, the entire transition is eliminated, and the main theme, which closed with an authentic cadence in the exposition, ends instead with a half cadence.

# Construction/Recognition
- End of the main theme and beginning of the transition are eliminated
- Remaining material combined into a single thematic unit
- May result in the main theme ending with a half cadence instead of PAC
- Often accompanied by other alteration techniques: deleting repetitions, adding model-sequence technique, emphasizing flat regions
- The fused unit typically ends with a half cadence or dominant arrival to prepare the subordinate theme

# Musical Context
Fusion reflects the changed functions of both sections in the recapitulation: the main theme no longer needs to establish the home key fully, and the transition no longer needs to modulate. The eliminated material is often redundant in the recapitulation context.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Concerto No. 3 in C Minor, Op. 37, i (ex. 11.8): same presentation as exposition but entirely new continuation using model-sequence technique; ends with HC instead of PAC, followed by standing on the dominant
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in C, Hob. XVI:21, ii (ex. 11.9): main theme closes with deceptive cadence instead of PAC; subsequent cadential phrase leads to HC; entire transition from exposition deleted

# Common Confusions
- Fusion is not simply the deletion of the transition — it involves a genuine merging of formal functions
- The resulting unit is neither purely a main theme nor purely a transition but a hybrid serving aspects of both
- Fusion in the recapitulation does not imply fusion in the exposition; the sections remain discrete in the original statement
