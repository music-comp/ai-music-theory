# Quick Definition
The characteristic phrase-structural technique of the development section, involving a model that is sequentially repeated, followed by fragmentation leading to a half cadence or dominant arrival and a standing on the dominant.

# Formal Definition
The core of the development (Ratz's "Kern der Durchfuhrung") is the unit defined by the process of establishing a relatively large model (normally 4-8 measures), repeating it sequentially one or more times, fragmenting the material into progressively smaller units, and arriving at a half cadence or dominant arrival of either the home key or a development key, typically followed by a standing on the dominant. The core projects emotional instability, restlessness, and dramatic conflict, with forte dynamics and continuous rhythmic activity.

# Construction/Recognition
1. **Model**: A relatively long unit (4-8 measures), large enough to project a structural beginning and permit extensive fragmentation
2. **Sequence**: The model is sequenced one or more times; sequential patterns may change during the core
3. **Fragmentation**: Breaks down the grouping structure into progressively smaller units
4. **Concluding function**: Half cadence or dominant arrival (of home key or development key)
5. **Standing on the dominant**: Postcadential prolongation, often with anticipatory motives from the main theme

# Musical Context
Pre-core/core technique is pervasive in Mozart and Beethoven but appears much less often in Haydn. Lengthy developments may contain two cores: the first confirming a development key, the second leading to the home-key dominant.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in A Minor, K. 310, i (ex. 10.3): four-measure model in E minor, sequenced twice down a fifth, fragmented to one-measure and one-beat units, leading to HC
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Hob. XVI:49, i (ex. 10.4): four-measure model sequenced by descending thirds
- Mozart, String Quintet in E-flat, K. 614, i (ex. 10.6): ten-measure sentential model sequenced down a step

# Common Confusions
- Not all developments contain a core -- Haydn frequently omits it
- The core is not the entire development -- it is preceded by a pre-core and may be followed by a retransition
- Model-sequence technique in a core differs from smaller-scale sequences in that the model must be large enough (4+ measures)
