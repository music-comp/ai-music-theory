# Quick Definition
A short melodic-rhythmic figure (typically one or two measures) that supports the closing cadential progression in a continuation phrase, often using conventional, non-characteristic melodic material.

# Core Definition
The cadential idea is the melodic-motivic material that accompanies the cadential progression at the end of a continuation phrase. Unlike the basic idea, which introduces characteristic motives, the cadential idea typically employs conventional material--scales, arpeggios, stock figures--that facilitates closure rather than establishing thematic identity. The cadential idea often results from liquidation, the progressive elimination of characteristic motives in favor of conventional ones. In a period, the contrasting idea of the consequent may be supported entirely by a cadential progression and thus resemble a cadential idea.

# Prerequisites
- Cadential Function
- Continuation Function (the context in which cadential ideas typically appear)

# Key Properties
- Typically one or two measures at the end of the continuation phrase
- Supports the cadential progression (pre-dominant -> dominant -> tonic)
- Often uses conventional rather than characteristic melodic material
- Frequently results from liquidation of earlier motives
- In periods, the contrasting idea of the consequent may serve cadential-idea function

# Construction / Recognition
1. Locate the cadential progression at the end of the continuation phrase
2. Identify the melodic material accompanying this progression
3. Note whether it is conventional (scales, arpeggios, stock patterns) vs. characteristic
4. Compare with the basic idea's motives; cadential ideas often liquidate these motives
5. The cadential idea marks the final stage of the continuation phrase

# Context & Application
Cadential ideas appear at the end of continuation phrases in sentences, hybrid themes, compound themes, and any passage expressing cadential function. They also appear in consequent phrases of periods where the contrasting idea is supported by a cadential progression. In recapitulations, the cadential idea may be the material recapitulated when the rest of the exposition is omitted.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 7-8: conventional scale/arpeggio figure supporting the half-cadential progression
- Mozart, K. 421/417b, ii, m. 8: one-measure cadential idea confirming the subordinate key at the end of the exposition

# Common Errors
- Confusing cadential idea with contrasting idea; the cadential idea specifically supports cadential function, while the contrasting idea follows the basic idea in an antecedent
- Assuming all cadential ideas must be entirely conventional; some retain characteristic motives

# Common Confusions
- **Cadential idea vs. contrasting idea**: in a period, the contrasting idea of the consequent may function like a cadential idea when supported entirely by a cadential progression, but the terms describe different functional contexts
