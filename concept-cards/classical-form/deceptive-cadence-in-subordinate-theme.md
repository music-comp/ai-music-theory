# Quick Definition
A cadential progression in a subordinate theme where the final tonic is replaced by a substitute harmony (usually built over ^6 in the bass), providing partial closure and motivating further cadential attempts to achieve the required PAC.

# Formal Definition
An authentic cadential progression can be changed into a deceptive cadential progression if the final tonic is replaced by a related harmony, usually built over the sixth scale-degree in the bass. If the arrival of the substitute harmony truly groups with the preceding material and represents its melodic and harmonic goal, a deceptive cadence brings partial closure to the theme. The theme then continues (usually by repeating the previous idea or phrase) to reach full closure through a PAC. The sense of partial closure is projected largely through the soprano voice's motion from the cadential dominant to the substitute harmony. Depending on how the melody closes, the DC can substitute for a PAC (if melody ends on ^1) or, more rarely, an IAC (if melody ends on ^3).

# Construction/Recognition
1. Authentic cadential progression with final tonic replaced by a substitute (usually VI)
2. The substitute harmony GROUPS WITH the preceding material (unlike evaded cadence)
3. The substitute harmony represents a melodic and harmonic GOAL (partial closure)
4. The melody determines whether the DC substitutes for PAC (ending on ^1) or IAC (ending on ^3)
5. Following the DC, the previous idea or phrase is typically repeated
6. Subsequent attempts lead eventually to PAC

# Musical Context
The deceptive cadence provides greater motivation for a subsequent PAC than does the IAC, because the lack of harmonic closure is more palpable than the merely melodic incompleteness of the IAC. The DC is an intermediate cadential failure -- more dramatic than the IAC but less dramatic than the evaded cadence. It is particularly effective when used in series, with each deceptive resolution motivating another cadential attempt, building intensity toward the final PAC.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in A-flat, Op. 26, iv, mm. 42-48 (after standing on dominant, deceptive cadential progression concludes on VI at m. 44; melody ends on ^1 so DC substitutes for PAC; idea repeated, another DC at m. 46; third attempt achieves PAC at m. 48)

# Common Confusions
- The deceptive cadence is fundamentally different from the evaded cadence: in a DC, a GOAL is reached (the substitute harmony groups backward with the preceding material); in an evaded cadence, NO GOAL is reached (what follows groups forward as a new beginning)
- A DC provides PARTIAL closure; an evaded cadence provides NO closure at all
- The repetition of material after a DC is NOT the "one more time" technique -- "one more time" involves cadential evasion specifically, where the listener hears the approach but NOT the arrival
- Multiple DCs in succession create an effective buildup but are less common than chains of evaded cadences
