# Quick Definition
A cadential progression whose constituent harmonies are temporally stretched to support an entire phrase or large section, rather than occupying just one or two measures.

# Core Definition
An expanded cadential progression (E.C.P.) is a cadential progression in which the individual harmonies (initial tonic or pre-dominant, penultimate dominant, final tonic) are prolonged to span an entire phrase or significant portion thereof. When an E.C.P. supports the entire continuation phrase of a sentence, the phrase acquires continuation => cadential function. In more loosely organized contexts (such as subordinate themes), a phrase built on an E.C.P. usually has exclusive cadential function, following a separate continuation phrase. The majority of expanded cadential progressions contain all four harmonic functions (initial tonic, pre-dominant, dominant, and final tonic). Occasionally the preceding continuation concludes with tonic harmony and the cadential phrase begins with a pre-dominant.

# Prerequisites
- **Cadential function**: The formal function being expanded
- **Cadential progression**: The harmonic pattern (I-PreD-V-I) being stretched
- **Harmonic functions**: Initial tonic, pre-dominant, dominant, final tonic

# Key Properties
1. Individual harmonies of the cadential progression are prolonged to span entire phrases
2. Usually contains all four harmonic functions: initial tonic, pre-dominant, dominant, final tonic
3. I6 serves as a conventionalized "cue" for the onset of an expanded cadential progression
4. One harmony may receive special prominence through expansion
5. The E.C.P. is often not recognized until the root-position dominant arrives, triggering retrospective reinterpretation
6. In loose contexts, the ECP supports an exclusively cadential phrase (not fused with continuation)

# Construction / Recognition
1. The cadential progression (I or pre-dominant...V...I) is stretched across an entire phrase
2. Individual harmonies are prolonged through neighboring chords, passing tones, etc.
3. **Expanded initial tonic**: I6 prolonged by neighboring V4/3; may generate a presentation-like phrase
4. **Expanded pre-dominant**: variety of pre-dominant harmonies in single progression
5. **Expanded dominant**: cadential 6/4 greatly lengthened before resolving; virtuosic passage-work, trills (concerto style)
6. In tight-knit contexts (sentence): supports continuation => cadential phrase
7. In loose contexts: supports exclusively cadential phrase after a separate continuation

# Context & Application
The expanded cadential progression is one of the most important harmonic structures in classical form. In subordinate themes, it provides the harmonic basis for cadential expansion and often supports the theme's climax. The expanded dominant with virtuosic passage-work and trill (concerto style) signals the approaching cadential arrival. The conventionalized I6 allows listeners to anticipate the approaching cadential goal even in loosely organized contexts.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C, K. 545, i, mm. 22-26: simple ECP following continuation
- Mozart, Clarinet Quintet in A, K. 581, i, mm. 58-65: expanded dominant -- cadential 6/4 stretched over 3 measures with virtuosic passage-work and trill
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in C, Hob. XVI:35, i, mm. 36-45: expanded initial tonic -- I6 prolonged by V4/3 for 5 measures; entire theme supported by single ECP
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in F Minor, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 33-41: ECP with evaded cadences extending the cadential area

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Cadential progression (the harmonic pattern being expanded)
- Cadential function (the formal function being supported)

## Enables
- Cadential expansion (the loosening technique the ECP supports)
- Subordinate theme climax (the ECP often supports the dynamic high point)

## Related
- Continuation => cadential fusion (in tight-knit contexts)
- Cadential trill (marks expanded dominant in concerto style)
- Brilliant style (passage-work over expanded dominant)

## Contrasts With
- Cadential extension (extension delays PAC through failed cadences; expansion enlarges cadential function through broadened progression)
- Simple cadential progression (occupies one or two measures)

# Common Errors
- Confusing the dominant of an ECP (penultimate harmony) with a half cadence (ultimate harmony)
- Failing to recognize the ECP until the root-position dominant arrives
- Equating ECP with cadential extension (they are different techniques that may operate simultaneously)

# Common Confusions
- **ECP vs. cadential extension**: An ECP enlarges the cadential progression itself; cadential extension delays PAC through failed cadences. Both can operate simultaneously
- **ECP dominant vs. half cadence**: The dominant in an ECP is a penultimate harmony WITHIN the progression, not the goal harmony of a half cadence
- **I6 as cue**: An I6 at the beginning of a cadential phrase signals cadential function, not necessarily a tonic prolongation
