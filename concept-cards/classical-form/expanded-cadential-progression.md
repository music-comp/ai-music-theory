# Quick Definition
An expanded cadential progression (E.C.P.) is a cadential progression whose constituent harmonies are temporally stretched to support an entire phrase or large section, rather than occupying just one or two measures. It is the harmonic foundation of the "continuation => cadential" phrase type.

# Formal Definition
An expanded cadential progression (E.C.P.) is a cadential progression in which the individual harmonies (initial tonic or pre-dominant, penultimate dominant, final tonic) are prolonged to span an entire phrase or significant portion thereof. When an E.C.P. supports the entire continuation phrase of a sentence, the phrase acquires continuation => cadential function. In more loosely organized contexts (such as subordinate themes), a phrase built on an E.C.P. usually has exclusive cadential function, following a separate continuation phrase. In hybrid themes, an E.C.P.-based phrase following an antecedent may be labeled simply "cadential" (hybrid type 2) when continuational characteristics are absent.

# Construction/Recognition
1. The cadential progression (I or pre-dominant ... V ... I) is stretched across an entire phrase
2. Individual harmonies are prolonged through neighboring chords, passing tones, etc.
3. In tight-knit contexts (sentence): supports the continuation => cadential phrase
4. In loose contexts: supports an exclusively cadential phrase
5. After an antecedent (hybrid type 2): may support an exclusively cadential phrase
6. The E.C.P. is often not recognized until the root-position dominant arrives, triggering retrospective reinterpretation of earlier harmonies

# Musical Context
The expanded cadential progression is one of the most important harmonic structures in classical form. It provides the harmonic basis for cadential function at various formal levels — from four-measure phrases within sentences to multi-measure cadential areas in sonata-form expositions and recapitulations. The degree of expansion often increases in the consequent relative to the antecedent in periodic structures.

# Examples
- Haydn, String Quartet in D Minor, Op. 42, i, mm. 5-8: E.C.P. (I6-II6-V-I) supports the entire continuation => cadential phrase
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in C Minor, Op. 10/1, ii, mm. 13-16: E.C.P. spanning four measures in the consequent's continuation => cadential phrase
- Beethoven, Violin Sonata in D, Op. 12/1, iii, mm. 5-8: E.C.P. supporting exclusively cadential phrase after antecedent (hybrid type 2)

# Common Confusions
- An E.C.P. is not just any long cadential passage — it is specifically a single cadential progression whose harmonies are stretched, not multiple cadential progressions in succession
- The cadential function of an E.C.P. may not be recognized until the dominant arrives — before that point, the harmonies may appear to have different functions
- The same harmonic structure (E.C.P.) receives different labels depending on formal context: "continuation => cadential" after a presentation, "cadential" after an antecedent (when no continuation traits are present)
