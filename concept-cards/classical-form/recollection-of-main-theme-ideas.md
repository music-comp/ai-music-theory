# Quick Definition
The return of main-theme material in the coda, lending circularity to the overall form — perhaps the coda's most common compensatory function.

# Formal Definition
The notion that musical material from the beginning of a movement should return toward its end — lending a kind of circularity to the overall form — gained currency in the classical period. In sonata and minuet forms, the main theme returns only at the beginning of the recapitulation. If the composer wishes to bring back main-theme material late in the movement, it must normally be placed in the coda. Most codas refer at some point to main-theme ideas. Except in sonata-rondo form, main-theme material in a coda is not usually shaped into the same tight-knit form as in the exposition; rather, it tends to be structured more loosely.

# Construction/Recognition
- Main-theme material appears within a coda theme
- Usually structured more loosely than the original main theme
- Sometimes main-theme ideas form part of the closing section (codettas) rather than receiving cadential closure
- In sonata-rondo form, a tight-knit restatement is common
- May provide the tonic-key statement that was missing from a subdominant-beginning recapitulation

# Musical Context
This is perhaps the coda's principal compensatory function. It completes the "circular" design of the movement and provides a final reference to the work's most distinctive material.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Trio in C, Hob. XV:27, iii (ex. 12.8): main-theme material returns at m. 238 with rondo-like character; deceptive cadences loosen the structure
- Haydn, Symphony No. 97 in C, i (ex. 12.9): main-theme fanfare material returns at m. 279 with obsessive root-position tonic, functioning as closing section rather than receiving cadential closure
- Beethoven, Overture to Coriolanus, Op. 62 (ex. 12.6): main-theme material returns at m. 276, providing the first home-key restatement since the beginning

# Common Confusions
- Main-theme material in the coda is not a "second recapitulation" — it is loosely organized and serves a different function
- The material may be significantly transformed, not simply restated
- Sometimes main-theme ideas appear without cadential closure, functioning as part of the closing section rather than as a theme
