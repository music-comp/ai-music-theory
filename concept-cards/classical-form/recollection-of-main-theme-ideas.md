# Quick Definition
The return of main-theme material in the coda, lending circularity to the overall form -- perhaps the coda's most common compensatory function.

# Core Definition
The notion that musical material from the beginning of a movement should return toward its end -- lending circularity to the overall form -- gained currency in the classical period. Some full-movement forms (large ternary, five-part rondo, sonata-rondo) require a return of the main theme toward the close. Other forms (sonata, minuet) see the main theme return only at the beginning of the recapitulation. If composers wish to bring back main-theme material late in the movement, they normally place it in the coda. This is perhaps the coda's principal compensatory function, for most codas refer at some point to main-theme ideas. Except in sonata-rondo, main-theme material in the coda is not usually shaped into the same tight-knit form as in the exposition; it tends to be structured more loosely. At times, main-theme ideas do not receive cadential closure and function as part of the closing section.

# Prerequisites
- **Coda**: The section containing the recollection
- **Compensatory functions**: The broader category
- **Main theme**: The source of the recalled material

# Key Properties
1. Perhaps the most common compensatory function
2. Most codas refer to main-theme ideas at some point
3. Material usually structured more loosely than the original (except in sonata-rondo)
4. May function as a coda theme (with PAC) or as part of the closing section (without PAC)
5. Provides circularity to the overall form
6. Especially important when the main theme was deleted from or began in IV in the recapitulation
7. In sonata-rondo, a tight-knit restatement is common

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify main-theme material appearing within the coda
- Note whether it receives cadential closure (coda theme) or not (closing section)
- Compare the organization with the original main theme (usually looser)
- Check whether this is the first home-key statement since the recapitulation

# Context & Application
In sonata and minuet forms, the coda is the only opportunity to recall main-theme material late in the movement. When the recapitulation began in the subdominant or deleted the main theme, the coda's recollection provides the necessary tonic-key statement.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Trio in C, Hob. XV:27, iii (ex. 12.8): main-theme material returns at m. 238 with rondo-like character; deceptive cadences loosen the structure
- Haydn, Symphony No. 97 in C, i (ex. 12.9): main-theme fanfare returns at m. 279; root-position tonic retained obsessively, prohibiting cadential activity; material functions as closing section rather than theme
- Beethoven, Overture to Coriolanus, Op. 62 (ex. 12.6): main-theme material returns at m. 276, providing the first home-key restatement since the very beginning

# Common Errors
- Treating this as a "second recapitulation" rather than a compensatory function
- Assuming main-theme material in the coda must replicate its original form

# Common Confusions
- **Not a recapitulation**: Main-theme material in the coda serves a different function and is usually loosely organized
- **Theme vs. closing section**: Sometimes main-theme ideas appear without cadential closure, functioning as codettas rather than a theme
- **Sonata-rondo exception**: In sonata-rondo, tight-knit main-theme restatements in the coda are normative
