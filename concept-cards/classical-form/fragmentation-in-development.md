# Quick Definition
The process of progressively breaking down the model's grouping structure into smaller units within the development core, driving the music toward cadential closure.

# Formal Definition
Most cores produce an extensive process of fragmentation, which breaks down the grouping structure defined by the model (and its sequences) and eventually motivates a formal close to the core. The passage of fragmentation may continue to employ ideas from the model or may bring about a change in musical content. The fragmentation itself may establish a new model for sequential repetition at a smaller scale.

# Construction/Recognition
- Reduces the model's multi-measure grouping to progressively smaller segments (e.g., 4-bar to 2-bar to 1-bar to half-bar)
- Often establishes new, smaller models for sequential repetition
- May introduce new motivic content or continue with ideas from the model
- Typically accelerates harmonic rhythm
- Leads to the concluding function (HC, dominant arrival, or authentic cadence)

# Musical Context
Fragmentation is the third stage of the core technique (model -> sequence -> fragmentation) and creates the intensification driving toward the cadential goal.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in A Minor, K. 310, i (ex. 10.3): four-measure model fragments to one-measure ideas (m. 70), then to one-beat units (m. 73), leading to HC at m. 74
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Hob. XVI:49, i (ex. 10.4): four-measure model reduces to two-measure units (m. 97), then one-measure segments (m. 101)

# Common Confusions
- Fragmentation in the development core operates at a larger scale than the fragmentation in a sentence's continuation phrase
- The onset of fragmentation does not necessarily coincide with the end of sequential activity
