# Quick Definition
The B section of minuet form, bringing contrast through loose organization and tonal exploration, often more complex and longer than the contrasting middle of a small ternary.

# Formal Definition
The B section of the minuet shares characteristics of a contrasting middle in small ternary form but is usually more complex and longer. It may feature interpolated episodes of new material in remote tonal regions, false recapitulations, and model-sequence technique reminiscent of a developmental core. Its organization depends on whether the preceding A section modulated. Following a modulating A section, the B section may explore additional tonal regions or return to the home key. Following a nonmodulating A section, the B section may introduce transition and subordinate-theme functions.

# Construction/Recognition
- After modulating A: free to explore tonal regions; emphasis on continuational traits; may consist of standing on the dominant
- After nonmodulating A: may provide transition and subordinate-theme functions; may modulate to and confirm subordinate key
- May contain false recapitulations, interpolated episodes, model-sequence technique
- Usually ends with HC or dominant arrival in home key

# Musical Context
The contrasting middle is the section of greatest formal complexity in a minuet.

# Examples
- Haydn, Symphony No. 98 in B-flat, iii, mm. 21-41: Interpolated episode in remote region
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in A, K. 331/300i, ii, mm. 19-32: Model-sequence technique
- Haydn, String Quartet in G, Op. 54/1, iii: Transition and subordinate-theme functions following nonmodulating A

# Common Confusions
- The B section's function depends heavily on whether the A section modulated. Students should always consider the A section first.
