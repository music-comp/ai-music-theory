# Quick Definition
A loosening technique in which a subordinate theme begins with a medial function (continuation) or concluding function (cadential) instead of a standard initiating function, creating a sense of starting in medias res.

# Core Definition
A subordinate theme can acquire formal loosening by beginning with continuation or cadential function instead of a standard initiating function. This creates a kind of formal "dissonance" -- a middle or end appearing at a beginning. Classical instrumental music has the capacity for a passage to express the sense of beginning, middle, or end independent of its actual temporal location, because formal functions are so conventionalized and well-defined. Model-sequence technique alone can signal continuation function; a cadential progression can signal cadential function. Another possibility is replacing the initiating function with a standing on the dominant from the end of the transition.

# Prerequisites
- **Subordinate theme**: The formal context for this loosening
- **Continuation function**: Medial function that may open the theme
- **Cadential function**: Concluding function that may open the theme
- **Presentation function**: The standard initiating function being omitted

# Key Properties
1. Theme bypasses conventional "beginning" function (presentation, compound basic idea)
2. Three main variants: beginning with continuation, beginning with cadential, beginning with standing on dominant
3. Creates formal "dissonance" -- a middle or end appearing at a beginning
4. Model-sequence technique is the most effective signal of continuation function at the start
5. I6 onset and cadential progression signal cadential function
6. Standing on dominant blurs transition-theme boundary
7. Often found in second or later subordinate themes of a group
8. When the first ST begins with continuation, a later theme may compensate with emphasized presentation

# Construction / Recognition
- **Beginning with continuation**: Model-sequence technique replaces tonic prolongation; harmonic sequence instead of tonic pedal; no basic idea or presentation
- **Beginning with cadential**: Expanded cadential progression from the very start; I6 onset suggests cadential function
- **Beginning with standing on dominant**: Dominant from transition sustained into theme; new melodic material over dominant pedal; followed by continuation or cadential function
- In all cases, compare with the conventional initiating functions absent from the theme

# Context & Application
Beginning with a medial or concluding function is one of the boldest loosening techniques, appropriate to subordinate themes but rarely found in main themes. Beginning a MAIN theme with cadential function (as Beethoven does in Op. 31/3) is a radical procedure looking forward to the Romantic style. When the first subordinate theme begins with continuation, the group as a whole lacks initiation -- often compensated by a later theme emphasizing presentation.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in A, Op. 2/2, i, mm. 58-74: first ST begins with 3-measure model sequenced by ascending minor thirds -- pure continuation; no presentation
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in C, Hob. XVI:35, i, mm. 36-45: first ST supported by single ECP -- cadential function from the start
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 31/3, i, mm. 46-53: ST starts with I6 progressing to cadential function (same procedure used for main theme, mm. 1-8)
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in A-flat, Op. 26, iv, mm. 33-48: ST begins with 9-measure standing on dominant before cadential function

# Common Errors
- Treating the omission as a formal error rather than a controlled loosening device
- Failing to recognize model-sequence technique as a signal of continuation function
- Not noting the compensatory function of a later theme's presentation

# Common Confusions
- **Formal dissonance vs. formal error**: Beginning with continuation or cadential is a carefully controlled loosening device, not a mistake
- **Transition-theme boundary**: Beginning with standing on the dominant creates ambiguity about where the transition ends and the ST begins -- determined retrospectively
- **Main theme exception**: Beginning a MAIN theme with cadential function (Op. 31/3) is radical and exceptional
