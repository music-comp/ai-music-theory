# Quick Definition
A recapitulation in sonata-without-development form that brings back only the main theme, eliminating the transition, subordinate theme, and closing section.

# Formal Definition
In a number of works by Mozart and Haydn, the exposition of a sonata without development is followed only by the main theme, with the rest of the recapitulation eliminated. A coda may be added. This creates a form resembling a large ternary where the interior theme has been replaced by a transition and subordinate theme. Despite similarities, the forms are distinct based on the listener's experience.

# Construction/Recognition
- Only the main theme is restated after the exposition
- No transition, subordinate theme, or closing section follows
- A coda may be appended
- The overall structure resembles a large ternary form

# Musical Context
This deviation appears in works by both Mozart and Haydn in slow movements.

# Examples
- Haydn: String Quartet in G, Op. 33/5, ii; String Quartet in D, Op. 33/6, ii
- Mozart: Clarinet Quintet in A, K. 581, ii; String Quartet in D, K. 575, ii

# Related Concepts
- Sonata Without Development
- Large Ternary Form
- Recapitulation

# Common Confusions
- The distinction from large ternary lies in the listener's real-time experience: until the truncation becomes apparent, the movement is heard as a sonata without development.
