# Quick Definition
A deviation from standard practice where the recapitulation omits the opening material of the main theme (or the entire main theme), sometimes beginning directly with the subordinate-theme area.

# Core Definition
Some recapitulations delete the opening material of the main theme or even the entire theme. At times the transition may be eliminated as well, and the recapitulation begins directly with the subordinate-theme area. Though deviant in the high classical style, this procedure is normative in midcentury works and has its roots in baroque binary dance forms. It is especially associated with the sonata-rondo, since the theme has already had several earlier statements and will eventually be brought back in the coda. If the main-theme opening is deleted, these ideas usually return later in the movement -- incorporated into the subordinate-theme area or placed in the coda. The procedure is sometimes called a "reversed" recapitulation, but Caplin cautions that this term suggests the composer "simply shifted around the main and subordinate themes in an almost mechanical manner," when in fact the material is usually substantially recomposed.

# Key Properties
1. May omit just the opening of the main theme or the entire theme
2. The transition may also be eliminated
3. Recapitulation may begin directly with the subordinate-theme area in the home key
4. Deviant in high classical style but normative in midcentury works
5. Roots in baroque binary dance forms
6. Especially associated with sonata-rondo (theme has had multiple statements)
7. Main-theme ideas usually return later: in the coda or interpolated into the subordinate theme
8. The term "reversed recapitulation" is misleading
9. Raises the theoretical question of whether "recapitulation function" is truly present
10. The fundamental function of restoring subordinate-key material to the home key is still fulfilled

# Construction / Recognition
- Main theme's basic idea absent from the start of the recapitulation
- Check whether the recapitulation begins directly with subordinate-theme material
- Look for main-theme ideas returning later (in the coda, or interpolated into the subordinate theme)
- The large-scale form may appear more analogous to small binary than small ternary

# Context & Application
This deletion raises the question of whether a true "recapitulation function" is at hand, since the return of the basic idea normally distinguishes a recapitulation from the end of a small binary. However, the fundamental function of restoring subordinate-key material to the home key is still fulfilled, so the label is maintained by convention. Main-theme material appearing in the coda restores the circular design of the movement.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Overture to Coriolanus, Op. 62 (ex. 11.17): main theme returns at m. 152 in the subdominant during a broad model-sequence plan; listener initially uncertain whether this is the recapitulation; main-theme material in the home key eventually returns in the coda (ex. 12.6, m. 276)

# Common Errors
- Using the term "reversed recapitulation" without recognizing its misleading implications
- Assuming the main-theme material is simply rearranged mechanically

# Common Confusions
- **"Reversed recapitulation"**: Not a simple rearrangement; main-theme ideas may be incorporated into the subordinate-theme area or substantially recomposed, not simply moved to the end
- **Recapitulation function**: The deletion raises the question of whether true recapitulation function is present; the label is maintained by convention since the fundamental restorative function is still fulfilled
- **Distinct from subdominant beginning**: Deleting the main theme entirely differs from beginning the main theme in the subdominant (a separate deviation)
