# Quick Definition
A cadential progression in which the expected root-position final tonic is replaced by a related harmony (most commonly VI, with bass ascending stepwise from scale-degree 5 to scale-degree 6), thwarting authentic closure.

# Core Definition
"The deceptive cadential progression is created when the final tonic of the authentic cadential progression is replaced by a related harmony" (Ch. 2, p. 28). "The most common form of this progression sees the bass ascend stepwise from the fifth scale-degree to the sixth, which supports a submediant substituting for the implied final tonic" (Ch. 2, p. 28). Variants include: V resolving to I6 instead of root-position I, V resolving to V7/IV (making the tonic "too unstable to create cadential closure"), and various harmonies built over scale-degree 6 in the bass.

# Prerequisites
- **Cadential progression** -- the progression being subverted
- **Authentic cadence** -- the expected outcome

# Key Properties
1. Most common: V-VI (bass: scale-degree 5 ascending to scale-degree 6)
2. With passing chord: V-V7/VI-VI
3. To first-inversion tonic: V-I6 (often via passing V7)
4. To V7/IV: converts tonic into secondary dominant (dramatic deception)
5. Various harmonies can be built over scale-degree 6 in the bass
6. The cadential six-four often precedes the root-position dominant before the deception

# Construction / Recognition
- Cadential progression proceeds normally until the expected final I
- Final I is replaced by VI (most common), I6, V7/IV, or other harmony
- The substitute harmony groups BACKWARD as a goal (partial closure)

# Context & Application
Deceptive cadences are an important loosening technique. They deny expected cadential closure, requiring the cadential progression to be attempted again. In subordinate themes, deceptive cadences contribute to cadential extension.

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 2, ex. 2.9a): Basic: V-VI with stepwise ascending bass.
**Example 2** (Ch. 2, ex. 2.9b): With passing V7/VI: V-V7/VI-VI.
**Example 3** (Ch. 2, ex. 2.9d-e): To I6, often via passing V7 after six-four embellishment.
**Example 4** (Ch. 2, ex. 2.9f): V-V7/IV: tonic converted to secondary dominant.

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Cadential progression and authentic cadence
## Enables
- Cadential extension (motivating repetition of the cadential phrase)
- Loosening techniques in subordinate themes
## Related
- Substitute chord (VI shares two common tones with I)
## Contrasts With
- Authentic cadence: AC resolves to root-position I; DC substitutes
- Evaded cadence: in DC, substitute groups BACKWARD as goal (partial closure); in evaded cadence, event groups FORWARD as new beginning (NO closure)

# Common Errors
- Confusing deceptive cadence with evaded cadence

# Common Confusions
- A deceptive cadence achieves partial closure (the substitute groups backward); an evaded cadence achieves NO closure (the event groups forward as new beginning)
- The deceptive resolution to VI works because VI shares two common tones with I (substitute chord relationship)
