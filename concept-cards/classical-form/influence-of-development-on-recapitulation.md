# Quick Definition
The way the development section's content and organization influence changes in the recapitulation, through compensatory additions (secondary development for underused ideas) and motivated deletions (removing overused ideas).

# Formal Definition
The content and organization of the development section seem at times to influence changes made in the recapitulation. This is not a causal connection — what happens in the development does not necessarily determine the recapitulation. Rather, it is a question of the "appropriateness of occurrence." When an idea is highly exploited in the development, it is appropriate (though not required) that it be deleted in the recapitulation. When the development lacks sequential organization, it is appropriate that secondary development appears in the recapitulation.

# Construction/Recognition
- Ideas extensively used in the development may be deleted from the recapitulation
- Ideas underrepresented in the development may receive secondary development in the recapitulation
- A development without a core may motivate additional model-sequence technique in the recapitulation's main theme or transition
- Material from the development's pre-core that appeared in the subordinate theme of the exposition may justify deleting that material from the recapitulation
- The relationship is one of "appropriateness," not determination

# Musical Context
This principle helps explain many seemingly arbitrary changes in recapitulations. The movement as a whole functions as an organic structure where the treatment of material in one section affects the appropriate treatment in others.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C Minor, K. 457, i (ex. 11.6): first part of subordinate theme deleted because it appeared in the development's pre-core
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in G, K. 283, i (ex. 11.2): added sequence compensates for lack of core in the development
- Mozart, Violin Sonata in C, K. 403 (ex. 11.5): model-sequence technique added to transition because the development lacked sequential organization

# Common Confusions
- The development does not cause recapitulation changes — the relationship is one of appropriateness, not necessity
- "Counterexamples abound" — many movements do not follow this principle
- The influence works in both directions: additions (compensating for gaps) and deletions (avoiding redundancy)
