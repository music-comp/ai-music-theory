# Quick Definition
A full-movement form pairing two different minuets (the minuet proper and the trio), after which the first minuet is performed again (da capo), creating a tripartite structure.

# Formal Definition
Minuet/trio form is a tripartite full-movement form consisting of the minuet proper, the trio, and the da capo (restatement of the minuet proper). Each component minuet is typically constructed according to minuet form (resembling small ternary/rounded binary), with ~10% following small binary. The trio contrasts in melodic-motivic content, rhythmic configurations, texture, and sometimes mode or tonality. About half of trios remain in the same key; about one-quarter shift mode; one-quarter change key. The standard functional labels of exposition, contrasting middle, and recapitulation should not be applied.

# Construction/Recognition
- Three-part structure: Minuet, Trio, Da Capo
- Each component follows minuet form or binary minuet form
- The minuet proper begins and ends in the home key with a PAC
- The trio provides contrast
- The da capo restates the minuet (indicated by "Menuet da capo")
- A coda may follow the da capo

# Musical Context
The minuet is the premier dance type of the classical period. Minuet/trio form occupies an interior position in multi-movement works.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, iii
- Haydn, Symphony No. 98 in B-flat, iii
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in A, K. 331/300i, ii

# Common Confusions
- The term "minuet" is used in three ways: generically, specifically (first minuet), and for the whole movement.
- Minuet/trio form resembles large ternary form but should not be equated with it.
