# Quick Definition
A full-movement tripartite form pairing two different minuets (the minuet proper and the trio), after which the first minuet is performed again (da capo), creating a structure analogous to but distinct from both the large ternary and the sonata.

# Core Definition
Minuet/trio form is a tripartite full-movement form consisting of the minuet proper, the trio, and the da capo (restatement of the minuet proper). Each component minuet is typically constructed according to minuet form (resembling small ternary/rounded binary), with approximately 10% following small binary. The trio contrasts in melodic-motivic content, rhythmic configurations, texture, and sometimes mode or tonality. About half of trios remain in the same key; about one-quarter shift mode; one-quarter change key. The standard functional labels of exposition, contrasting middle, and recapitulation should not be applied to the full-movement form, since the trio bears no relation to a B section or development, and the da capo neither tonally adjusts nor structurally alters the original minuet. The form has a stronger relationship to the large ternary than to the sonata, but the two should not be equated.

# Prerequisites
- **Small ternary**: The form on which most component minuets are built
- **Small binary**: The alternative form for component minuets
- **Minuet form**: The internal structure of each component

# Key Properties
1. Three-part structure: Minuet proper, Trio, Da Capo
2. Each component follows minuet form or binary minuet form
3. The minuet proper begins and ends in the home key with a PAC
4. The trio provides contrast in material, texture, and sometimes mode/key
5. The da capo restates the minuet (indicated by "Menuet da capo")
6. A coda may follow the da capo
7. No consistent functional relations justify applying sonata-form labels
8. Relationship to large ternary exists but should not be equated

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify two distinct minuets followed by a restatement of the first
- Determine the key/mode relationship between minuet and trio
- Assess whether the trio is independent or dependent on the minuet
- Check for a coda following the da capo

# Context & Application
The minuet is the premier dance type of the classical period, the only baroque dance to survive into classical instrumental cycles. The scherzo is a variant style with faster tempo. The tripartite scheme is highly suggestive of an overall ternary structure, but the spectrum of trio-minuet relationships (from total subordination to complete independence) prevents a single functional interpretation.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, iii: Modulating exposition with fused transition/subordinate-theme functions
- Haydn, Symphony No. 98 in B-flat, iii: Multiple subordinate themes in the A section
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in A, K. 331/300i, ii: Subordinate theme bypassing transition function

# Relationships

## Related
- Large ternary form (analogous tripartite structure)
- Da capo (the restatement mechanism)
- Trio (the contrasting component)

## Contrasts With
- Large ternary form (despite similarities, the forms are distinct)
- Sonata form (functional labels do not apply)

# Common Errors
- Applying sonata-form labels (exposition, development, recapitulation) to the full-movement form
- Equating minuet/trio form with large ternary in all cases

# Common Confusions
- **Terminological ambiguity**: "Minuet" can refer to (1) either component generically, (2) the first minuet specifically, or (3) the whole movement
- **Relationship to large ternary**: The resemblance is strongest when the trio changes mode/key and remains incomplete; weakest when the trio is in the same key and fully closed
