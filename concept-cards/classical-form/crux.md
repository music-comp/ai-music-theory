# Quick Definition
The specific point in the recapitulation where the tonal path diverges from the exposition, marking where the music is "adjusted" to remain in the home key.

# Formal Definition
The tonal adjustment required to keep the subordinate theme in the home key must occur at some specific point in the recapitulation. This moment — where the recapitulation's harmonic path begins to differ from the exposition's — can be called the crux or point of recapitulatory adjustment. The adjustment can occur at any place in the transition (early, middle, or late) and can be accomplished by various harmonic and phrase-structural means. Frequently, the subdominant region is tonicized at this point, promoting a logical succession to the dominant.

# Construction/Recognition
- Compare the recapitulation with the exposition measure by measure
- The crux is the first moment where the harmonic content diverges
- May occur at the beginning of the transition (early crux: much of the transition is recomposed)
- May occur at the end of the transition (late crux: only the final modulation is adjusted)
- May occur within the subordinate theme itself (if the transition is nonmodulating)
- Often involves a shift toward the flat side (IV, bVI) before returning to the home key

# Musical Context
The location of the crux determines how much of the transition is recomposed. An early crux gives the composer more freedom to create new material (secondary development, flat-region emphasis); a late crux preserves more of the exposition's structure.

# Examples
- The crux is discussed implicitly throughout Chapter 11 in connection with each transition example
- Beethoven, Symphony No. 7, i (ex. 11.4): early crux — the transition begins with material from the middle of the exposition's transition, deleting the opening tonic-pedal passage
- Haydn, Piano Trio in E-flat, Hob. XV:30, i (ex. 11.7): the mode change at m. 158 marks the crux

# Common Confusions
- The crux is not always a dramatic moment — it may be a subtle harmonic change
- The crux need not occur at the same formal position in every movement; it depends on the exposition's tonal structure
- In movements with nonmodulating transitions, the crux occurs within the subordinate theme or even at its transposition into the home key
