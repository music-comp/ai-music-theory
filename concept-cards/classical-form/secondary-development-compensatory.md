# Quick Definition
A developmental passage within the recapitulation of a sonata without development, compensating for the absence of a genuine development section by providing harmonic-tonal contrast and motivic manipulation.

# Core Definition
In sonata-without-development form, a secondary development frequently appears within the recapitulation to provide harmonic-tonal contrast and motivic manipulation that would otherwise be supplied by a genuine development section. This compensatory function gives it heightened importance compared to secondary developments in regular sonata recapitulations. The secondary development typically occurs between the main theme and subordinate theme in the recapitulation.

# Prerequisites
- **Sonata without development**: The formal context requiring compensation
- **Secondary development**: The general concept being applied
- **Recapitulation**: The section containing the compensatory passage

# Key Properties
1. Located within the recapitulation, typically between main theme and subordinate theme
2. Features modulatory activity, model-sequence technique, and motivic development
3. Provides tonal contrast absent from the movement due to the missing development
4. May be more extensive than secondary developments in regular sonata recapitulations
5. A standard feature of sonata-without-development form

# Construction / Recognition
- Locate the recapitulation and look for developmental passages between its component themes
- Note modulatory activity and motivic development
- Assess whether the passage compensates for the absent development

# Context & Application
The secondary development is a standard feature of recapitulations in both regular sonata form and sonata without development. In the latter, it takes on heightened importance as the only source of developmental activity in the movement.

# Common Errors
- Treating the secondary development as a substitute for a full development section (it is a localized passage within the recapitulation)

# Common Confusions
- **Compensatory vs. ordinary**: In sonata without development, the secondary development has heightened importance; in regular sonata form, it is less essential since a full development has already occurred
