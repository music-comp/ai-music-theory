# Quick Definition
A developmental passage within the recapitulation of a sonata without development, compensating for the absence of a genuine development section.

# Formal Definition
In sonata-without-development form, a secondary development frequently appears within the recapitulation to provide harmonic-tonal contrast and motivic manipulation that would otherwise be supplied by a genuine development section. This compensatory function makes it especially important in this form.

# Construction/Recognition
- Located within the recapitulation, typically between the main theme and subordinate theme
- Features modulatory activity, model-sequence technique, and motivic development
- Provides tonal contrast absent from the movement due to the missing development section
- May be more extensive than secondary developments in regular sonata recapitulations

# Musical Context
The secondary development is a standard feature of recapitulations in both regular sonata form and sonata without development. In the latter, it takes on heightened importance.

# Common Confusions
