# Quick Definition
The central harmonic function of a key, represented primarily by the major or minor triad on the first scale-degree (^1), to which all other harmonies ultimately relate and from which they derive meaning.

# Formal Definition
Tonic function is the most important of the three fundamental harmonic functions. It is the central harmony of a key, the one to which all others ultimately relate and derive their meaning. Tonic function is usually represented by the major or minor triad built on the first scale-degree (I). In certain contexts, the triad built on the submediant (VI) degree also has a tonic function and can frequently substitute for an expected I chord, especially when following a root-position V (the "deceptive resolution" of the dominant).

# Construction/Recognition
- Primary chord: I (major or minor triad on ^1)
- Substitute: VI (submediant triad, especially after V in deceptive resolutions)
- Root-position I is the most stable form
- First-inversion I (I6) is less stable and sometimes used as an "initial tonic" in cadential progressions
- Tonic function appears in prolongational progressions (as the prolonged harmony), at the beginning and end of cadential progressions, and as the goal of authentic cadences

# Musical Context
Tonic function is essential to virtually every formal function in classical music. Presentation function requires tonic prolongation. Cadential progressions begin with an initial tonic and end with a final tonic. The confirmation of a tonic through cadential closure is what defines the main theme's formal function (home-key tonic) and the subordinate theme's function (subordinate-key tonic).

# Examples
- In any cadential progression: the initial I (or I6) and the final I are both tonic function
- VI substituting for I: the deceptive cadential progression, where V resolves to VI instead of the expected I

# Common Confusions
- VI can have tonic function (as a substitute), not just subdominant or pre-dominant function. This is specifically in the context of deceptive resolutions.
- The initial tonic and final tonic in a cadential progression serve different structural roles: the initial tonic begins the progression, the final tonic confirms the tonality.
