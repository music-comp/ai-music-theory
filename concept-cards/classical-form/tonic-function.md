# Quick Definition
The central harmonic function of a key, represented primarily by I (the triad on the first scale-degree), to which all other harmonies relate; VI can substitute for I in specific contexts.

# Core Definition
"Tonic function is the most important of the three fundamental harmonic functions. It is the central harmony of a key, the one to which all others ultimately relate and derive their meaning" (Ch. 2, p. 23). Tonic function is "usually represented by the major or minor triad built on the first scale-degree" (I). "In certain contexts, the triad built on the submediant (VI) degree of the scale has a tonic function and can frequently 'substitute' for an expected I chord, especially when following a root-position V (the 'deceptive resolution' of the dominant)" (Ch. 2, p. 23).

- **Harmonic functions overview** -- the three-function framework

# Key Properties
1. Primary chord: I (major or minor triad on scale-degree 1)
2. Substitute: VI (especially after V in deceptive resolutions)
3. Root-position I is the most stable form
4. First-inversion I (I6) is less stable; often used as "initial tonic" in cadential progressions
5. Appears at both beginning and end of cadential progressions (initial and final tonic)
6. Prolonged in presentation function (tonic prolongation)

# Construction / Recognition
- I in root position = strongest tonic function
- I6 = weaker, often initial tonic in cadential progressions
- VI after V = tonic substitute (deceptive resolution)

# Context & Application
Tonic function is essential to virtually every formal function. Presentation function requires tonic prolongation. Cadential progressions begin with an initial tonic and end with a final tonic. The confirmation of tonic through cadential closure defines main-theme function (home-key tonic) and subordinate-theme function (subordinate-key tonic).

# Examples
**Example 1** (Ch. 2): In any cadential progression, the initial I (or I6) and the final I are both tonic function.
**Example 2** (Ch. 2): VI substituting for I in the deceptive cadential progression (V -> VI).

# Common Errors
- Ignoring VI as a possible tonic substitute

# Common Confusions
- VI can have tonic function (as substitute), not just pre-dominant function, specifically in deceptive resolutions
- The initial tonic and final tonic in a cadential progression serve different structural roles
