# Quick Definition
The sentence and period are the two fundamental eight-measure theme types. Both begin with a basic idea and end with a cadence, but the sentence repeats the basic idea (presentation + continuation) while the period contrasts it (antecedent + consequent). The sentence projects mobility and forward drive; the period projects balance and repose.

# Core Definition
Caplin explicitly compares the two fundamental theme types (Ch. 3, pp. 92-93):

**Similarities:**
- Both themes are normatively eight measures in length, divided into two 4-m. phrases
- Both themes end with a cadence
- Both themes begin with a 2-m. basic idea, which is restated at some point in the theme

**Differences:**
- The sentence has one cadence; the period has two cadences
- The sentence can end with any cadence type (PAC, IAC, or HC); the period cannot end with an HC
- In the sentence, the basic idea is *repeated* within the presentation phrase; in the period, the basic idea *returns* at the start of the consequent phrase

"The sentence projects the character of mobility and forward drive because of the open-ended nature of the presentation and the destabilizations offered by the continuation. The period, on the contrary, projects the character of balance and repose owing to the similar construction of the antecedent and consequent, both of which bring a degree of cadential closure" (Ch. 3, p. 93).

# Prerequisites
- **Sentence** -- presentation + continuation (one of the two fundamental types)
- **Period** -- antecedent + consequent (the other fundamental type)

# Key Properties
1. Both are normatively 8 measures, divided into two 4-m. phrases
2. Sentence: one cadence; period: two cadences (weak then strong)
3. Sentence: basic idea repeated (mm. 1-4); period: basic idea returns (mm. 5-6)
4. Sentence can end with HC; period cannot end with HC (PAC or IAC only for consequent)
5. Sentence: mobility and forward drive (open-ended presentation, destabilizing continuation)
6. Period: balance and repose (similar phrase construction, cadential closure in both phrases)
7. The basic idea alone cannot predict which type will follow -- what follows determines the type
8. Repetition (immediate restatement) vs. return (restatement after intervening material)

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the basic idea in mm. 1-2
2. Check mm. 3-4: is the basic idea repeated (sentence) or is there a contrasting idea with cadence (period)?
3. If repeated: presentation -> expect continuation in mm. 5-8
4. If contrasting idea with cadence: antecedent -> expect consequent in mm. 5-8
5. Confirm: does the basic idea return at m. 5 (period) or does new material appear (sentence)?

# Context & Application
Understanding the sentence-period distinction is foundational for analyzing all classical themes, since hybrid themes are defined as intermediaries between these two poles. The aesthetic qualities of each type influence their deployment in larger formal contexts: sentences are often used for main themes (where forward drive is appropriate), while periods appear frequently in subordinate themes and slow movements (where balance is valued).

# Examples
**Example 1**: Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 1-8 (sentence): basic idea repeated (statement-response), continuation with fragmentation, HC.
**Example 2**: Mozart, K. 525, ii, mm. 1-8 (period): basic idea followed by contrasting idea + HC (antecedent), basic idea returns + contrasting idea + PAC (consequent).

# Common Errors
- Assuming all 8-measure themes must be either sentence or period (many are hybrids)
- Confusing "repetition" (immediate, in presentation) with "return" (after contrast, in consequent)
- Treating the basic idea alone as sufficient to identify the type

# Common Confusions
- The basic idea does not determine the theme type -- what follows it does: repetition leads to sentence, contrasting idea with cadence leads to period
- "Repetition" and "return" are technically distinct: repetition is immediate; return follows intervening material
- A sentence can end with HC but a period cannot -- the period requires a PAC or IAC to end
