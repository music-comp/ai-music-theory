# Quick Definition
The comparative aesthetic effects of different cadential extension techniques, ranging from the least effective (repeated PAC) to the most dramatic (evaded cadence), each serving the classical goal of dramatizing the subordinate key's confirmation.

# Formal Definition
Caplin establishes a hierarchy of aesthetic effects among the cadential extension techniques. A repeated PAC is the least effective and hence least used means, as it creates cadential redundancy and poses the question of which cadence truly effects closure. An IAC followed by PAC is somewhat redundant but effectively motivates the latter through melodic incompleteness. A deceptive cadence provides even greater motivation because harmonic closure is more palpable than melodic incompleteness alone. The evaded cadence is unquestionably the most dramatic: imminent closure is thwarted at the last second, arousing powerful expectation. The abandoned cadence is less dramatic but promotes significant loosening as the music "wanders off" before returning to cadential activity. Evaded cadences suit subordinate themes' dramatic needs; deceptive and imperfect authentic cadences are more typical of main themes' more moderate cadential extension.

# Construction/Recognition
**Hierarchy from least to most dramatic**:
1. **Repeated PAC**: Cadential redundancy; least effective; question of which cadence is "real"
2. **IAC then PAC**: Somewhat redundant; open-ended quality motivates repetition
3. **DC then PAC**: Greater motivation; lack of harmonic closure more palpable than melodic incompleteness
4. **Evaded cadence**: Most dramatic; imminent closure thwarted; powerful expectation aroused; particularly suited to subordinate themes
5. **Abandoned cadence**: Less dramatic but significant formal loosening; music loses cadential direction

# Musical Context
The aesthetic hierarchy directly relates to the different formal needs of main themes versus subordinate themes. Main themes, which do not require dramatic cadential articulation, typically use the more moderate techniques (IAC, DC). Subordinate themes, which must dramatically confirm the subordinate key, favor the evaded cadence for its unparalleled dramatic effect. The choice of cadential extension technique is thus not arbitrary but reflects the formal function of the passage in which it appears.

# Common Confusions
- The hierarchy is about AESTHETIC EFFECT, not structural importance -- all techniques serve valid formal purposes
- Evaded cadences are dramatic because NO goal is achieved, unlike DCs (partial harmonic closure) or IACs (partial melodic closure)
- The abandoned cadence's lower drama compared to the evaded cadence does not make it less useful -- its loosening quality serves different formal purposes
- Main themes' preference for moderate techniques (IAC, DC) and subordinate themes' preference for dramatic techniques (evaded) reflects their different formal roles, not different compositional skill
