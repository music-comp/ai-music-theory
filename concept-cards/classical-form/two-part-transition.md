# Quick Definition
A transition in which tonal destabilization occurs in two distinct stages: a first part leading to a half cadence or dominant arrival in the home key, followed by a second part that modulates to the subordinate key.

# Core Definition
The process of tonal destabilization sometimes takes place in two distinct stages, yielding a two-part transition. Following a main theme ending with a PAC, the first part leads to a HC (or dominant arrival) in the home key, like a nonmodulating transition. The second part then modulates to the subordinate key, like a modulating transition. Frequently, the second part begins with the basic idea of the main theme supported by the home-key tonic, creating a structure that resembles a small-ternary recapitulation retrospectively understood as the transition's beginning. The fundamental difference depends on whether the passage leading to the home-key dominant is a contrasting middle or the first part of a transition. The principle prohibiting elision between A and B sections helps resolve this ambiguity.

# Prerequisites
- **Transition**: The broader formal function
- **Modulating transition**: The model for the second part
- **Nonmodulating transition**: The model for the first part

# Key Properties
1. Two distinct stages of tonal destabilization
2. First part: leads to HC or dominant arrival in home key (nonmodulating)
3. Second part: modulates to subordinate key (modulating)
4. First part often begins with false closing section to main theme
5. Second part frequently opens with main theme's basic idea over home-key tonic
6. Standing on the dominant may follow the HC of the first part
7. A second standing on the dominant may follow the HC ending the second part
8. Both parts are components of a SINGLE formal function (transition)

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify two stages of tonal destabilization
- Check that the first part ends on home-key dominant
- Verify that the second part modulates to the subordinate key
- Note whether the second part begins with main-theme material
- Apply the elision test: if the passage preceding the home-key dominant elides with the main-theme ending, it cannot be a contrasting middle (ruling out small ternary interpretation)

# Context & Application
The two-part transition is the most expansive form of transitional process. It combines both types of destabilization: first weakening the home key, then fully abandoning it. Particularly useful in large-scale movements where the distance between main and subordinate themes requires gradual transition. The return of the basic idea at the second part's start creates apparent recapitulation that is then redirected.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Trio in D Minor, K. 442, iii, mm. 16-48: first part begins with false closing section, leads to HC in home key at m. 26; second part begins with basic idea at m. 32, modulates, HC in subordinate key at m. 42 followed by standing on dominant
- Mozart, Piano Trio in D Minor, K. 442, iii (ex. 9.15): PAC at m. 16; false closing section leads to HC at m. 24 and standing on dominant; m. 32 brings basic idea return; modulation at m. 37; HC in subordinate key at m. 42

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Nonmodulating transition (model for first part)

- Gradual tonal destabilization (two-stage process)

## Related
- False closing section (often begins the first part)
- Transition with main-theme material (often begins the second part)
- Standing on the dominant (may follow either part's HC)

## Contrasts With
- No direct contrast; the two-part transition combines the two single-type transitions

# Common Errors
- Interpreting the two parts as two separate transitions
- Confusing the return of the basic idea with a small-ternary recapitulation
- Mixing up the home-key HC (first part) with the subordinate-key HC (second part)

# Common Confusions
- **Two-part transition vs. incomplete small ternary**: If the passage before the home-key dominant elides with the main theme's ending, it cannot be a contrasting middle (no elision between A and B), ruling out small ternary
- **Single function**: Both parts belong to a SINGLE formal function (transition), not two separate transitions
- **HC keys**: First part's HC is in HOME key; second part's HC is in SUBORDINATE key
