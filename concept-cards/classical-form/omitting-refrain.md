# Quick Definition
A standard deviation in sonata-rondo form where one of the four refrains is eliminated to avoid redundancy: either refrain 3 at the beginning of the recapitulation (frequent in Mozart) or the basic structure of refrain 4 in the coda.

# Core Definition
Since the sonata-rondo normally includes four appearances of the refrain, a composer may feel that this many statements is unnecessarily redundant. One standard deviation, adopted frequently by Mozart, eliminates refrain 3 at the beginning of the recapitulation. In such cases, the end of couplet 2 (development or interior theme) brings material from the transition of couplet 1 (subordinate-theme complex) so that couplet 3 (recapitulation) can begin directly with the subordinate-theme group. Another option is to eliminate the basic structure of the refrain within the coda, employing instead only the initial motives of the refrain. Where Mozart retains refrain 3, he usually eliminates refrain 4, although its opening motives may pervade the coda texture (Caplin, p. 683).

# Prerequisites
- **Sonata-rondo** -- The form where this deviation occurs

# Key Properties
1. One of four refrains eliminated to reduce redundancy
2. Option 1 (Mozart's preference): eliminate refrain 3 at start of recapitulation
3. When refrain 3 is omitted, transition material leads directly to recapitulated subordinate theme
4. Option 2: eliminate refrain 4's basic structure in the coda, retaining only motives
5. Mozart typically eliminates one or the other, not both
6. Omitting one refrain does not significantly impair the rondo effect

# Construction / Recognition
1. Count the refrain returns in the sonata-rondo
2. If only three refrains are present, determine which was eliminated
3. If refrain 3 is missing, look for transition material bridging couplet 2 to couplet 3
4. If refrain 4 is missing, check the coda for motivic references to the refrain

# Context & Application
This is a common deviation that shows the flexibility of the sonata-rondo scheme. The recapitulatory sense of "beginning again" is often more effectively conveyed by subordinate-theme materials than by the original transition, which is why omitting refrain 3 and proceeding directly to the recapitulated subordinate theme is viable.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming all sonata-rondos must have exactly four refrains. **Correction**: One refrain may be eliminated without impairing the rondo effect.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Whether omitting a refrain makes the movement a sonata rather than a rondo. **Clarification**: The movement remains a sonata-rondo; the omission is a standard deviation within the form.
