# Quick Definition
An A section in minuet form that modulates to a subordinate key, expressing main-theme, transition, and subordinate-theme functions within a compact structure.

# Formal Definition
When the A section modulates, its interthematic functionality becomes complex. Main-theme function arises from initial tonic-stabilizing progressions. Transition function emerges through harmonic pivots. Subordinate-theme function is expressed by cadential confirmation of the subordinate key. These functions may be fused or given distinct groups. Because the modulating A section may represent the principal tonal conflict of the movement, the subordinate key's presence is of particular importance.

# Construction/Recognition
- Begins in the home key, ends with a cadence in the subordinate key
- Main-theme expression ranges from minimal (no home-key cadence) to strong (early PAC)
- Transition and subordinate-theme functions may be fused or separate
- Multiple subordinate themes possible with additional PACs in subordinate key
- A closing section may follow

# Musical Context
The modulating A section creates the principal tonal conflict within the minuet exposition.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, iii, mm. 1-24
- Haydn, Symphony No. 98 in B-flat, iii, mm. 1-20
- Mozart, String Quartet in A, K. 464, ii, mm. 9-28

# Common Confusions
- Unlike the small ternary, modulation in a minuet's A section may represent the principal tonal conflict of the entire movement.
