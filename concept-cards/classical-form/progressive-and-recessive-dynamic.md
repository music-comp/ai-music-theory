# Quick Definition
Progressive dynamic refers to a systematic buildup of tension and energy (associated with introductions), while recessive dynamic refers to the dissipation of energy (associated with postcadential areas).

# Formal Definition
Dynamic activity involves the systematic growing or diminishing of tension and excitement created by various musical means, including changes of intensity. A progressive dynamic features an increasing buildup of energy and anticipation, functioning as an anacrusis whose thesis is the structural beginning. A recessive dynamic dissipates energy accumulated in the cadential drive. These profiles are associated with framing functions: progressive with introductions, recessive with postcadential areas.

# Construction/Recognition
Progressive: crescendo, accumulating texture, rising register, anacrustic quality
Recessive: diminuendo, thinning texture, stable register, centered on tonic or final cadential harmony

# Musical Context
These dynamic profiles articulate theme boundaries by marking what comes "before the beginning" and "after the end."

# Examples
- Progressive: Beethoven, Op. 135, iii, mm. 1-2 (staggered instrumental entrances with crescendo)
- Recessive: closing sections and standings on the dominant after cadences

# Common Confusions
- "Dynamic" here means more than loud/soft. Caplin uses the term (following Wallace Berry) for systematic tension/release patterns across multiple musical parameters.
