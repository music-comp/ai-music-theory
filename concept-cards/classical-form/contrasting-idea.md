# Quick Definition
A two-measure idea that follows the basic idea in an antecedent or consequent phrase, providing melodic contrast and leading to a cadence.

# Formal Definition
The contrasting idea is understood as "not-a-repetition" of the basic idea. It follows the basic idea in antecedent and consequent phrases and leads to a cadence. The contrast may range from striking to minimal, but the idea must be sufficiently distinct that the listener does not perceive it as a repetition (which would suggest a presentation). The contrasting idea often features continuation-like characteristics: fragmentation, increased harmonic rhythm, and a conventionalized melodic formula for the cadence. The contrasting idea in the consequent may or may not match the antecedent's.

# Construction/Recognition
- Typically two measures, immediately following the basic idea
- Must be perceptibly different from the basic idea
- Often features fragmentation, harmonic acceleration
- Contains a cadential formula leading to HC, IAC, or PAC

# Musical Context
Contrasting ideas appear in antecedent and consequent phrases of period forms. They transition from the stable basic idea toward cadential closure. Detailed treatment in Chapter 4.

# Examples
- Mozart, K. 525, ii, mm. 3-4: contrasting idea in the antecedent with fragmentation and harmonic acceleration, leading to HC
- Mozart, K. 525, ii, mm. 7-8: distinctly different contrasting idea in the consequent, leading to PAC

# Common Confusions
- "Contrasting" does not mean dramatically different; it means "not a repetition."
