# Quick Definition
A subordinate theme divided into two distinct parts: the first part ends with an internal half cadence (possibly followed by standing on the dominant), and the second part begins with a new basic idea in an initiating function and eventually achieves the PAC that closes the entire theme.

# Core Definition
In a two-part subordinate theme, the first part ends with a HC, which may be followed by a standing on the dominant. The second part begins with a new basic idea, usually incorporated into a presentation or compound basic idea, and eventually leads to PAC for the entire theme. The cadential endings of the two parts create a certain "antecedent-consequent" expression (HC in part 1, PAC in part 2), but the overall structure should not be considered a period because too many other characteristics of periodic organization are lacking -- especially the notion that the consequent "repeats" the antecedent by beginning with the same basic idea.

# Prerequisites
- **Internal half cadence**: The cadence dividing the two parts
- **Subordinate theme**: The formal unit being divided
- **Standing on the dominant**: Often follows the internal HC

# Key Properties
1. First part ends with HC (possibly followed by standing on the dominant)
2. Second part begins with a NEW basic idea (different from first part)
3. New basic idea appears in an initiating function (presentation, compound basic idea)
4. Second part achieves PAC to close the entire theme
5. HC-PAC cadential pattern creates antecedent-consequent expression
6. But NOT a true period: second part does not "repeat" first part's material
7. Maintains the principle that subordinate themes must end with PAC

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify an internal HC dividing the subordinate theme
- Verify that a new (different) basic idea begins the second part
- Confirm that the new material appears in an initiating function
- Check that PAC eventually closes the second part and the theme as a whole
- Compare with alternative interpretation of two separate subordinate themes

# Context & Application
The two-part subordinate theme represents Caplin's solution to situations where a subordinate theme leads to an HC followed by a new basic idea. Rather than interpreting this as two separate subordinate themes (the first ending with HC, violating the PAC requirement), Caplin proposes a single two-part theme. This preserves the principle that subordinate themes end with PAC while acknowledging the internal structural complexity.

# Examples
- Mozart, Violin Concerto in A, K. 219, i, mm. 74-98: first part has presentation + compressed continuation ending HC at m. 80; second part begins with new basic idea at m. 81, organized as 16-measure sentence with compressed continuation and PAC at mm. 94/98

# Relationships
## Builds Upon
- Internal half cadence (divides the two parts)
- Subordinate theme organization (the formal context)

## Enables
- PAC requirement satisfaction (the two-part reading preserves the requirement)
- Compensatory dominant emphasis (first part provides dominant articulation)

## Related
- Nonmodulating transition (often motivates the internal HC that creates the two-part structure)
- Period (similar HC-PAC pattern but fundamentally different construction)

## Contrasts With
- Multiple subordinate themes (each ends with its own PAC; two-part is a single theme)
- Period (requires the consequent to repeat the antecedent's basic idea)

# Common Errors
- Treating the two-part structure as a period despite different basic ideas in each part
- Interpreting the structure as two separate themes where the first ends with HC
- Confusing a two-part ST with a theme followed by continuation after an internal HC

# Common Confusions
- **Two-part ST vs. period**: Despite the HC-PAC cadential pattern, the second part begins with a DIFFERENT basic idea, violating the periodic requirement of repetition
- **Two-part vs. two separate themes**: Two separate themes each end with PAC; the two-part interpretation maintains the PAC requirement for subordinate themes
- **Two-part vs. single theme with internal HC**: When continuation (not a new basic idea) follows the HC, the theme is a single unit with an internal HC, not a two-part theme
