# Quick Definition
In this compound period category, both the eight-measure antecedent and consequent units are built as hybrid type 1 (antecedent + continuation). The lower-level antecedent closes with a weak cadence at m. 4, followed by a continuation closing with a second weak cadence at m. 8.

# Core Definition
This category features hybrid type 1 as the basis of both compound antecedent and consequent. The initial four-measure antecedent closes with a weak cadence, and the continuation closes with a second weak cadence at m. 8. The return of the basic idea is saved for mm. 9-10 to signal the large-scale consequent. The cadential situation is complex: the compound antecedent usually ends with some kind of half cadence (regular or reinterpreted), not an IAC. The cadential distribution often produces unusual patterns such as HC-HC-HC-PAC, which seem to violate normal syntax but are typical of this category.

# Prerequisites
- **Compound period**: The overarching theme type
- **Hybrid theme type 1**: The internal structure (antecedent + continuation) of each unit

# Key Properties
1. Each eight-measure unit structured as hybrid type 1 (antecedent + continuation)
2. The basic idea appears at m. 1 and not again until m. 9
3. Lower-level continuation at mm. 5-8 prevents a lower-level consequent (which would prematurely return the basic idea)
4. The compound antecedent ends with an HC (regular or reinterpreted), not an IAC
5. Cadential distribution may produce unusual patterns (e.g., HC-HC-HC-PAC)
6. The reinterpreted HC is particularly useful when the compound antecedent modulates
7. Material at m. 5 may resemble the basic idea but with different harmonization, confirming continuation function

# Construction / Recognition
1. **Large antecedent (mm. 1-8)**: four-measure antecedent (b.i. + c.i. + weak cadence) + four-measure continuation (fragmentation, acceleration, second weak cadence)
2. **Large consequent (mm. 9-16)**: basic idea returns at m. 9; structure repeats but closes with PAC
3. The basic idea appears at m. 1 and not again until m. 9
4. Lower-level continuation at mm. 5-8 -- NOT a lower-level consequent
5. The compound consequent's final phrase may be supported by an ECP (hybrid antecedent + cadential)

# Context & Application
The avoidance of a genuine "double period" is a deliberate compositional strategy. If the basic idea returned at m. 5 (lower-level consequent), its reappearance at m. 9 (higher-level consequent) would be preempted and its dramatic effect diminished. The reinterpreted HC is especially useful here: the compound antecedent can modulate, achieving a PAC in the new key that is retrospectively understood as an HC when the basic idea returns in the home key at m. 9.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in A-flat, Op. 26, i, mm. 1-16: model form; four-measure antecedent followed by continuation with fragmentation into two-beat units, harmonic acceleration, and second HC; basic idea returns at m. 9
- Mozart, String Quartet in C ("Dissonance"), K. 465, iv, mm. 1-16: two HCs in a row (mm. 4 and 8); consequent's final phrase supported by ECP
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in C Minor, Op. 10/1, ii, mm. 1-16: compound antecedent modulates; reinterpreted HC at m. 8

# Common Errors
- Labeling this a "double period" -- the lower level uses hybrid structure (antecedent + continuation), not periodic structure (antecedent + consequent)
- Hearing all HCs as equivalent -- the two HCs serve different hierarchical functions
- Mistaking material at m. 5 for a genuine return of the basic idea when the harmonization differs

# Common Confusions
- This is NOT a "double period" -- the lower level uses hybrid structure, not periodic structure
- When material at m. 5 resembles the basic idea, check the harmonization -- different harmonies confirm continuation function; the true consequent begins at m. 9
- The two weak cadences (at mm. 4 and 8) serve different hierarchical functions: the first closes the lower-level antecedent; the second closes the large-scale antecedent unit
