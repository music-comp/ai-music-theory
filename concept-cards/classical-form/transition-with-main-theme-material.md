# Quick Definition
A transition that opens with a restatement of the main theme's basic idea, initially suggesting a repetition of the main theme but retrospectively understood as the transition's beginning when the music modulates.

# Core Definition
This opening is typically employed when the main theme ends with a HC. The return of the opening basic idea initially creates the impression that the main theme is not yet over and that a consequent is beginning. When the music no longer corresponds to the antecedent and instead modulates, the listener understands retrospectively that the return of the basic idea marked the beginning of the transition and that the HC truly closed the main theme. The strategy can also be used when the main theme ends with PAC, frequently with structural elision. In such cases, the combined structure may give the impression of a "reversed period" (consequent followed by antecedent).

# Prerequisites
- **Transition**: The formal function being initiated
- **Main theme**: The source of the restated material
- **Half cadence**: The typical main-theme ending motivating this beginning type

# Key Properties
1. Opens with the main theme's basic idea or close variant
2. Most common after a main theme ending with HC
3. Initially suggests the beginning of a consequent phrase
4. Modulation or divergence reveals transition function retrospectively
5. The FORMAL beginning is at the return of the basic idea, NOT at the modulation
6. After PAC-ending main themes, structural elision is frequent
7. May create "reversed period" impression (PAC-ending main theme + HC-ending transition)
8. The chromaticism introduced in the transition's version often contributes to tonal destabilization

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify the return of the main theme's basic idea
- Note the main theme's cadence type (HC most common for this beginning)
- The formal beginning is at the return of the basic idea
- The point of modulation/divergence is in the MIDDLE of the transition, not its beginning
- Check for structural elision with the main theme's ending
- Note any "reversed period" impression

# Context & Application
Beginning with main-theme material creates formal continuity while generating ambiguity. The retrospective reinterpretation exemplifies a key principle: the beginning of a formal unit may not be immediately recognizable as such; its function becomes clear only in retrospect. The formal beginning of the transition is at the return of the basic idea, not at the point of modulation.

# Examples
- Haydn, String Quartet in B-flat, Op. 50/1, iv, mm. 9-15 (ex. 9.3): main theme (sentence) ends HC at m. 8; sentence repeated from m. 9; modulation at m. 12 and HC at m. 15 confirm transition; formal beginning is at m. 9, not m. 12
- Mozart, Violin Sonata in C, K. 403/385c, i, mm. 5-8 (ex. 9.1): sentence repeated but ends HC instead of PAC; "reversed period" impression

# Common Errors
- Locating the transition's start at the modulation point rather than the basic-idea return
- Treating the "reversed period" impression as an actual period
- Failing to note that the formal beginning is retrospectively determined

# Common Confusions
- **Formal beginning**: The transition begins at the return of the basic idea, NOT at the point of modulation -- the modulation is in the MIDDLE of the transition
- **"Reversed period"**: The impression is best explained by thematic functions (main theme + transition), not reversed phrase functions
- **Retrospective determination**: The transition's start is often recognized only after modulation begins
