# Quick Definition
In sonata without development form, a retransition connects the exposition's closing section to the recapitulation, replacing the development; it may include model-sequence technique and chromatic harmonies but must not project the sense of a true development.

# Core Definition
In a sonata without development, the exposition is not repeated (to avoid a redundant third run-through and to prevent arousing expectations for a development). A retransition regularly appears to lead the music, without a break, into the recapitulation. This retransition can sometimes be relatively long and may feature model-sequence technique and chromatic harmonies; however, the sense of a true development must not be sufficiently projected (otherwise, we would speak of a regular sonata form). The recapitulation is constructed in essentially the same manner as that of a regular sonata, with the main theme and transition often altered. A secondary development frequently appears in either the main theme or the transition to provide harmonic-tonal contrast and motivic manipulation in the absence of a genuine development section.

# Prerequisites
- **Sonata without development**: The formal context
- **Retransition**: The passage connecting exposition to recapitulation

# Key Properties
1. The exposition is NOT repeated (two reasons given)
2. A retransition connects exposition to recapitulation
3. The retransition may be relatively long
4. Model-sequence technique and chromatic harmonies may appear
5. The retransition must NOT project a genuine development sense
6. The recapitulation follows the same principles as in regular sonata
7. A secondary development often appears in the recapitulation's main theme or transition
8. The secondary development compensates for the absence of a genuine development

# Construction / Recognition
- Identify a sonata form where no development section appears
- Note the retransition between closing section and recapitulation
- Assess whether the retransition projects development function (if so, reconsider as regular sonata)
- Check for secondary development in the recapitulation

# Context & Application
The sonata without development is used frequently in slow movements and sometimes in fast overtures. The retransition's scope determines the boundary between "sonata without development" and "regular sonata with a very short development."

# Common Errors
- Expecting an exposition repeat (it is deliberately omitted)
- Treating the retransition as a development section (if it projects development, reconsider the form)
- Missing the secondary development in the recapitulation

# Common Confusions
- **Retransition vs. development**: The retransition may use developmental techniques but must not project a genuine development
- **No exposition repeat**: Two reasons -- (1) would arouse expectations for a development; (2) recapitulation would be a redundant third hearing
- **Secondary development**: Compensates for the missing development by providing motivic work in the recapitulation
