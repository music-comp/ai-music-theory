# Quick Definition
The hierarchical arrangement of discrete, perceptually significant time spans in a musical work, identified by measure length and content relationships.

# Formal Definition
Grouping structure describes the form of a musical work as a hierarchical arrangement of discrete, perceptually significant time spans. Each group is a self-contained "chunk" of music at one level of the hierarchy, identified most neutrally by its measure length. Groups combine hierarchically: a two-measure idea groups with another to form a four-measure phrase, which groups with another phrase to form an eight-measure theme, and so on.

# Construction/Recognition
- Identify groups by their measure lengths and boundaries
- Recognize hierarchical nesting: ideas within phrases, phrases within themes, themes within sections
- Note symmetrical groupings (4 + 4, 8 + 8) versus asymmetrical ones
- Use letter labels (A, B, A') to indicate content similarity/difference between groups

# Musical Context
Grouping structure provides the temporal skeleton of a work. In classical music, symmetrical groupings (especially 4 + 4 at the phrase level) are normative for tight-knit themes, while asymmetrical groupings characterize looser formal regions like transitions and subordinate themes.

# Examples
- A typical sentence: 2 + 2 + 4 grouping (basic idea + repetition + continuation)
- A typical period: 4 + 4 grouping (antecedent + consequent)
- Beethoven's subordinate theme in Op. 2/1, i: asymmetrical 4 + 8 + 4 + 5 grouping

# Common Confusions
- Grouping structure and formal function are not the same thing. Traditional theories often assign a single functional label to each group, but Caplin shows that function and group can be incongruent: a single group may express multiple functions, or multiple groups may express a single function.
