# Quick Definition
The expression of main-theme function within a minuet's A section, created by initial tonic-stabilizing progressions and ranging from minimal to strong depending on whether the home key is cadentially confirmed.

# Formal Definition
Main-theme function in a minuet's A section always arises by virtue of initial tonic-stabilizing progressions of the home key. The strength of this expression depends on the extent of home-key activity before the modulation: (1) if the music modulates before any home-key cadence, main-theme expression is minimal and the form is likely sentential; (2) if the opening leads to a home-key HC, main-theme expression is stronger and the form is likely periodic; (3) if a PAC in the home key closes the music before the end of the section (an early authentic cadence), main-theme expression is strongest. In a nonmodulating A section, interthematic expression is exclusively main theme.

# Construction/Recognition
- Minimal expression: tonic prolongation only, no cadence in home key, sentential form
- Moderate expression: home-key HC achieved, periodic form
- Strong expression: home-key PAC (early authentic cadence) before modulation
- Strongest expression: sufficiently complex passage constituting a complete "main theme" of several phrases, the last closing with an early PAC
- In nonmodulating A sections, the entire section expresses main-theme function

# Musical Context
Main-theme function in minuet form operates at the interthematic level, unlike in the small ternary where the corresponding function is intrathematic. The strength of main-theme expression affects the perception of subsequent transition and subordinate-theme functions within the same A section.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, iii: Presentation phrase expresses main-theme function through tonic prolongation alone (minimal)
- Beethoven, Violin Sonata in F, Op. 24, iii: Four-measure phrase with early PAC (strong)
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in A, K. 331/300i, ii: Expanded hybrid main theme with early PAC at m. 10 (strongest)

# Common Confusions
- Main-theme function in a minuet is an interthematic function, not an intrathematic one. It refers to how the passage functions within the movement as a whole, not within a single theme.
- The degree of main-theme expression exists on a continuum; it is not an all-or-nothing classification.
