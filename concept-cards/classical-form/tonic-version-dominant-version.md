# Quick Definition
A type of repetition within the presentation phrase where the basic idea is first stated on tonic harmony (statement/tonic version) and then repeated on dominant harmony (response/dominant version), creating a stable I-V prolongation.

# Core Definition
Statement-response repetition is the most common type of repetition in the presentation phrase. The basic idea is first stated with tonic harmonic support (the "tonic version" or "statement") and then repeated with dominant harmonic support (the "dominant version" or "response"). The resulting I-V motion remains within the framework of tonic prolongation. This pairing can be reversed (V-I, as "response-statement"), which sometimes occurs at the start of the second part of a small binary. A dominant version does not constitute a modulation to the dominant key.

# Key Properties
- Statement = tonic version (I); Response = dominant version (V)
- Together they create tonic prolongation (I-V or I-V-I)
- Most common repetition type in presentation phrases
- The dominant version does not modulate
- Can also appear in the consequent of a period (basic idea returns with dominant support)
- Reversed arrangement (response-statement, V-I) is associated with looser structure

# Construction / Recognition
1. Identify the basic idea and its repetition in the presentation
2. Check if the first statement is on tonic and the repetition on dominant (or vice versa)
3. Confirm that the overall progression remains within tonic prolongation
4. Distinguished from exact repetition (same harmonies) and sequential repetition (transposition to other degrees)

# Context & Application
Statement-response repetition appears in the presentation phrases of sentences and in compound sentences. It also appears in the consequent of a period when the basic idea returns with dominant harmonic support. In the second part of a small binary, a reversed response-statement pattern (V-I) may occur, suggesting a looser arrangement.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 1-4: statement on I (mm. 1-2), response on V7 (mm. 3-4)--the archetypal statement-response
- Beethoven, Serenade, Op. 8, iv, mm. 23-26: basic idea returns in consequent with dominant version
- Beethoven, Op. 131, iv, mm. 17-20: reversed response-statement (V-I) at start of binary's second part

# Common Errors
- Interpreting the dominant version as a modulation; it remains within tonic prolongation
- Failing to recognize a dominant version when the melody is varied along with the harmony

# Common Confusions
- **Dominant version vs. modulation**: the dominant version is a local harmonic change within tonic prolongation, not a modulation to the dominant key
- **Statement-response vs. antecedent-consequent**: statement-response is a sub-phrasal repetition within a presentation; antecedent-consequent is a phrase-level relationship with cadential differentiation
