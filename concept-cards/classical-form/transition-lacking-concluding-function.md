# Quick Definition
A transition that does not close with a clear HC or dominant arrival, making the boundary between transition and subordinate theme difficult to locate — requiring retrospective harmonic analysis.

# Formal Definition
The location of the boundary between the transition and the subordinate theme can be confusing if the former is not closed by means of a half cadence or a dominant arrival. Readily identifying the beginning of the subordinate theme therefore depends on a decisive melodic change in the context of a strong initiation. If these conditions are not met, then perceiving a clear boundary is considerably more difficult. When harmonic and melodic/textural criteria conflict, preference should normally be given to the powerful, form-defining role of harmony.

# Construction/Recognition
- No clear HC or dominant arrival at the end of the transition
- The transition flows without cadential interruption into the subordinate theme's tonal area
- Root-position tonic prolongation in the subordinate key provides the best harmonic marker for the subordinate theme's beginning
- Melodic change may come later than the harmonic shift, creating a temporal gap between the two markers
- The boundary emerges only in retrospect

# Musical Context
This situation challenges the analyst to look beyond surface cues (melody, rhythm, texture) and attend to harmonic structure. It is most common in through-composed textures where the composer avoids clear sectional boundaries.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in F Minor ("Appassionata"), Op. 57, iii (ex. 13.7): transition begins at m. 64 with main-theme material; repeats in dominant of subordinate key (mm. 68-71); root-position tonic at m. 72 is the most likely subordinate-theme beginning (harmonically), though the first real melodic change occurs at m. 76

# Common Confusions
- The absence of a cadential conclusion does not mean the transition is "incomplete" — it simply makes the boundary less clear
- Harmonic criteria (root-position tonic in the new key) take priority over melodic/textural criteria for locating the boundary
- This situation differs from Tr/ST fusion, where no subordinate-theme initiation can be found even in retrospect
