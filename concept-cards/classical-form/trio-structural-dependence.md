# Quick Definition
Techniques by which a composer makes the trio structurally dependent on the surrounding minuets, including changing mode or tonality, adding a retransition, and leaving the trio formally incomplete or truncated.

# Core Definition
A composer may forge a stronger sense of overall minuet/trio form by making the trio dependent on the minuet proper through three principal methods. First, changing the trio's mode or tonality motivates a restoration of the original key in the da capo. Second, a retransition linking the trio's end to the da capo aids integration, especially when the tonal region is remote. Third, the trio may remain structurally incomplete, analogous to what typically happens at the end of an interior theme in large ternary form. Techniques for inhibiting closure include: the A' section initially cadences but remains open on dominant harmony when repeated; the recapitulation gets stuck on a dominant; or the trio is truncated (consisting of A and B sections only), with the B section's dominant leading directly to the da capo (Caplin, pp. 651-652).

# Prerequisites
- **Minuet/trio form** -- The full-movement context
- **Trio** -- The contrasting section being made dependent

# Key Properties
1. Mode or tonality change motivates restoration of the original key
2. Retransition may link trio to da capo, anticipating minuet motives
3. Trio may remain structurally incomplete (no cadential closure)
4. A' section may cadence initially but remain open on dominant when repeated
5. Trio may be truncated (A and B sections only, no A')
6. Incomplete trio's final harmony is usually dominant of the minuet's home key

# Construction / Recognition
1. Check whether the trio changes mode or tonality from the minuet proper
2. Look for a retransition passage between trio and da capo
3. Determine whether the trio achieves cadential closure
4. If no closure: identify whether A' is left open or the trio is truncated
5. Check whether the final harmony functions as dominant of the minuet's home key

# Context & Application
Between the extremes of total subordination and complete independence lies a spectrum of possibilities. When the trio is in the same key and fully closed, the movement seems like a stringing together of parts. When the trio is dependent, the overall form achieves greater integration. This parallels the relationship between interior themes and main themes in large ternary form.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 652): Haydn, Symphony No. 98 in B-flat, iii -- trio in C minor is truncated (A and B sections only), with standing on the dominant leading directly to "Menuetto d.c." The truncated trio remains structurally subordinate to its surrounding minuets.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming all trios must achieve cadential closure. **Correction**: Many trios are left structurally incomplete to create dependence on the da capo.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Whether a truncated trio is an error or intentional. **Clarification**: Truncation is a deliberate compositional technique for integrating the trio into the larger form.
