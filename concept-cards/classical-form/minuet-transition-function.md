# Quick Definition
The expression of transition function within a minuet, accomplished through the modulation from the home key to the subordinate key, which may occur within the A section or within the B section.

# Core Definition

# Prerequisites
See front matter.

# Key Properties
See Core Definition.
Transition function in a minuet emerges when the modulation to the subordinate key occurs by means of a harmonic pivot within a phrase. If a new phrase begins immediately in the subordinate key (a direct modulation), no sense of transition results. The expression of transition is more palpable when the change of key is accompanied by loosening devices typical of this function. Transition may be fused with subordinate-theme function (especially when both occur in the same phrase), or it may occupy its own distinct group. In nonmodulating A sections, transition function may appear in the B section instead, when that section modulates to and confirms a subordinate key.

# Construction / Recognition
- A modulating passage that departs from the home key
- More palpable when accompanied by loosening devices
- May be a distinct group or fused with subordinate-theme function
- Direct modulations (new phrase starting in new key) do not express transition function
- In modulating A sections: transition function appears within the A section
- In nonmodulating A sections: transition function may appear at the start of the B section
- Transition function in the B section often begins with a shift to vi (submediant harmony)

# Context & Application
Transition function in a minuet can appear in either the A section or the B section, depending on whether the exposition modulates. Its expression ranges from a brief harmonic pivot (minimal) to a distinct transitional group (strong). In the compact structure of a minuet, transition function is often fused with subordinate-theme function.

# Examples
- Mozart, String Quartet in A, K. 464, ii, mm. 9-12: A four-measure unit functioning exclusively as transition, accomplishing the modulation and ending with dominant harmony of the subordinate key
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in E-flat, Op. 7, iii: Transition and subordinate-theme functions fused within the continuation of a hybrid form
- Haydn, String Quartet in G, Op. 54/1, iii: Transition function in the B section following a nonmodulating A section, beginning with a shift to vi

# Common Confusions
- In a minuet, transition function may be expressed within the A section or the B section, depending on where modulation occurs. This flexibility differs from sonata form, where transition is always a distinct section between main and subordinate themes.
- A direct modulation (beginning a new phrase in the new key) does not express transition function; the sense of transition requires a within-phrase harmonic process.
