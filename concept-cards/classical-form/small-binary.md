# Quick Definition
A theme-type consisting of two parts, each normatively eight measures long and usually repeated, distinguished from the small ternary primarily by its lack of genuine recapitulation -- the second part does not bring back the opening basic idea in the home-key tonic.

# Core Definition
The small binary consists of two parts whose functional characteristics differ from the small ternary. Each part is normatively eight measures long and is usually repeated. The essential difference from the small ternary is that the small binary does not recapitulate its opening materials: the initial basic idea, harmonized by tonic of the home key, does not return later in the theme. Unlike the small ternary's three well-defined sections, the two parts of the small binary are less distinct in overall functional meaning, identified by the neutral terms "part 1" and "part 2." Letter labels (A-B or A-A') are inappropriate because they carry functional meanings that do not apply. The second part usually begins with ideas directly related to the opening, which dampens expectations for a subsequent recapitulation. More than any other theme type, the small binary emphasizes structural symmetry (8 + 8).

# Prerequisites
- **Sentence**: One of the possible structures for each part
- **Period**: One of the possible structures for part 1
- **Small ternary**: The form from which the small binary is distinguished

# Key Properties
1. Two-part structure: ||: Part 1 :||: Part 2 :||
2. Each part normatively 8 measures (total 16 real measures)
3. No genuine recapitulation of the basic idea in the home-key tonic
4. Second part usually begins with material related to the first part's opening (motivic connection)
5. First part may end with HC (unlike small ternary, whose A section ends with PAC)
6. Second part always concludes with PAC in the home key
7. Emphasizes structural symmetry; standard phrase deviations are infrequent
8. Letter labels (A-B, A-A') inappropriate -- they carry functional meanings that do not apply
9. Neutral terms "part 1" and "part 2" used instead
10. Tends to appear in movements with multiple theme recurrences (rondos, variations)

# Construction / Recognition
1. Two-part structure with each part repeated
2. No genuine recapitulation of the basic idea in the home-key tonic
3. Part 2 may or may not contain a contrasting middle
4. Part 2 always ends with PAC in the home key
5. Part 2 opening usually connects motivically to part 1's opening
6. Check Figure 8.1 criteria: rounded binary always has recapitulation; small binary never does; rounded binary always has contrasting middle; small binary sometimes lacks one; A section ends PAC; part 1 may end HC

# Context & Application
The small binary tends to appear in movements featuring multiple recurrences of a theme (rondos, theme-and-variations). By using this form, the composer minimizes overexposure of the basic idea, since it will not be recapitulated within the theme itself. The return of the basic idea can be reserved for the beginning of each new variation. The small binary is especially appropriate for variation forms. Erwin Ratz, building on Schoenberg's theories, provided the foundational description of this form, though he incorrectly identified the absence of a contrasting middle (rather than absence of recapitulation) as the key distinction from the small ternary.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Trio in A-flat, Hob. XV:14, ii, mm. 1-16: sentence as part 1; contrasting middle + continuation in part 2
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in E, Hob. XVI:31, iii, mm. 1-16: antecedent + failed consequent as part 1; model-sequence continuation in part 2
- Beethoven, Violin Sonata in A, Op. 30/1, iii, mm. 1-32: c.b.i. opening; nonconventional periodic qualities
- Beethoven, String Quartet in C-sharp Minor, Op. 131, iv, mm. 1-32: sentential part 1 and part 2
- Beethoven, Fifteen Variations, Op. 35, mm. 66-81: contrasting middle + cadential in part 2
- Mozart, String Quartet in D Minor, K. 421, iv, mm. 1-24: elaborate B section modulating to III; followed by cadential phrase with Neapolitan emphasis

- Theme-and-variations forms (basic idea reserved for variation beginnings)

## Related
- Bipartite structure (the organizing principle)
- Contrasting middle (may or may not appear in part 2)
- Failed consequent (a characteristic part 1 construction)

## Contrasts With
- Small ternary: small ternary HAS recapitulation; small binary does NOT
- Rounded binary: rounded binary HAS recapitulation; small binary does NOT
- Compound period: both are 16 measures, but compound period has antecedent-consequent structure

# Common Errors
- Confusing with rounded binary (small ternary) -- the key distinction is the ABSENCE of recapitulation
- Using letter labels (A-B or A-A') for the parts -- these carry functional meanings inappropriate for the small binary
- Identifying the absence of contrasting middle as the key distinction from small ternary (Ratz's error) -- many small binaries DO have contrasting middles

# Common Confusions
- When the first part ends with a PAC, the small binary cannot be distinguished from a small ternary until the second part fails to recapitulate the basic idea
- When the first part ends with an HC, a compound period interpretation may be implied but fails when the repeat also ends with HC
- The motivic connection between part openings is a tendency, not a requirement
- Material resembling the basic idea may appear in the second part, but if it serves continuation or cadential function (not recapitulatory), the form remains binary
