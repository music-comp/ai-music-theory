# Quick Definition
The opening formal function of a sentence, in which a two-measure basic idea is repeated to form a four-measure phrase that establishes the theme's melodic material over tonic harmony.

# Formal Definition
Presentation function is defined by two criteria: (1) melodic-motivic: a basic idea is stated and then immediately repeated, and (2) harmonic: the phrase is grounded in tonic prolongation. The repetition helps the listener learn the melodic-motivic material, confirms the boundaries of the basic idea, and creates a strongly ongoing quality that generates demand for a continuation phrase. Presentation, in principle, does not end with a cadence.

# Construction/Recognition
- Four measures: two-measure basic idea + two-measure repetition
- Supported by tonic prolongation
- Types of repetition: statement-response (I then V), exact, sequential
- Does not end with a cadence
- Creates an ongoing, open quality demanding continuation

# Musical Context
Presentation function occurs at the beginning of sentence themes and is characteristic of main themes in sonata forms. Its stable character makes it ideal for establishing the home key and introducing primary melodic material. Detailed treatment in Chapter 3.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Op. 2/1, i, mm. 1-4: statement (I) and response (V7)
- Beethoven, Op. 135, iii, mm. 3-6: exact repetition with no harmonic change

# Common Confusions
- A presentation must have tonic prolongation, not just a repeated idea. Without tonic harmony, the phrase is not a genuine presentation.
- Presentation does not end with a cadence. The presence of a cadence rules out a genuine presentation.
