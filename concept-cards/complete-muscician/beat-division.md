# Quick Definition
Beat division is the equal splitting of a beat into smaller note values -- into twos in simple meter, into threes in compound meter.

# Formal Definition
Beat division refers to the primary level of subdivision of the beat. In simple meters, undotted beat values divide naturally into two equal parts (e.g., a quarter note divides into two eighth notes). In compound meters, dotted beat values divide naturally into three equal parts (e.g., a dotted quarter note divides into three eighth notes). Further division of each subdivision is called subdivision of the beat, which always divides into twos at the subdivision level.

# Musical Context
Beat division determines the fundamental rhythmic character of a piece. Music with duple division (simple meter) has a different feel from music with triple division (compound meter). Beat division is the primary criterion for distinguishing simple from compound meters.

# Examples
- Simple meter: a quarter-note beat divides into two eighth notes (Example 2.18A).
- Compound meter: a dotted-quarter beat divides into three eighth notes (Example 2.18B).
- Subdivision: at the subdivision level, both simple and compound beats divide into twos (Example 2.18C).

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes confuse division with subdivision. Division is the first level of splitting the beat; subdivision is the further splitting of each division. In 4/4, the quarter note divides into two eighths (division), and each eighth subdivides into two sixteenths (subdivision).
