# Quick Definition
Chorale style uses two staves (short score) or four staves (open score) to notate four voices. In short score, soprano and tenor stems point up, alto and bass stems point down. Keyboard style uses three upper voices in the treble and bass alone in the bass clef.

# Formal Definition
Three notational formats for four-voice writing: (1) Open score (full score) -- each voice on its own staff with voice-specific clefs. (2) Short score -- two staves (treble for SA, bass for TB), with soprano and tenor stems pointing up, alto and bass stems pointing down. Open score and short score are collectively called chorale style. (3) Keyboard style -- soprano, alto, and tenor in the treble staff (within an octave for comfortable right-hand playing), bass alone in the bass staff. In keyboard style, soprano stems point up, alto and tenor stems point down; bass follows general stem rules.

# Musical Context
Short score is the standard format for voice-leading exercises and chorale harmonization throughout the text. Keyboard style is used for keyboard realization of figured basses and for practical playing. Bach chorales are the quintessential examples of chorale-style writing.

# Examples
Bach's "Nun ruhen alle Walder" is notated in short score. Beethoven's Symphony no. 9 slow movement shows open-score orchestral writing reduced to short score format for analysis.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes mix up stem directions in short score. The rule is simple: soprano and tenor always have upward stems, alto and bass always have downward stems. In keyboard style, the three upper voices should fit within an octave for comfortable right-hand playing.
