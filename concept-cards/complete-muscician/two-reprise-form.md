# Quick Definition
Another name for binary form, derived from the fact that repeat signs almost always mark both sections of the form.

# Formal Definition
Two-reprise form is a synonym for binary form, emphasizing the presence of repeat signs that demarcate the two sections. Each section (or "reprise") is typically repeated, creating an AABB performance structure. The term highlights the performative aspect of binary form -- that listeners hear each section twice -- which affects how modulations and tonal areas are perceived. For example, a modulation to V at the first double bar may sound like a mere tonicization on first hearing (since the music immediately returns to the home key at the repeat) but is confirmed as a modulation when the music continues in the new key after the second hearing.

# Musical Context
The term two-reprise form is used particularly in discussions of Baroque dance movements and Classical minuets/scherzos, where repeat signs are standard features.

# Examples
- All binary form types (simple, rounded, balanced; sectional, continuous) are also two-reprise forms

# Common Confusions
- The repeats affect how listeners perceive modulations and formal structure
- Not all pieces with two sections have repeat signs, but two-reprise form specifically implies their presence
