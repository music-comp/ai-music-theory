# Quick Definition
Dance-derived movements cast in binary form: the minuet originated in the Baroque suite and was maintained in Classical compositions; the scherzo, a more energetic relative, became prominent from Beethoven onward.

# Formal Definition
The minuet is a dance movement in triple meter that was a standard component of the Baroque suite and was retained in Classical instrumental works (sonatas, string quartets, symphonies). It is typically cast in rounded binary form. The scherzo, meaning "joke," is a more energetic and often faster movement that increasingly replaced the minuet in the late Classical and Romantic periods, particularly in Beethoven's works and those of later nineteenth-century composers. Both are cast in binary form and follow the same structural principles of two-reprise form.

# Musical Context
Minuets and scherzos are among the most common movements in instrumental music from the Baroque through the Romantic periods. They serve as interior movements in multi-movement works.

# Examples
- Haydn, Sonatina no. 4 in F major, Scherzo: rounded sectional binary form
- Beethoven symphonies: scherzos replacing traditional minuets

# Common Confusions
- Both minuets and scherzos are cast in binary form, despite being dance movements with characteristic rhythmic profiles
- The scherzo is not simply a "fast minuet"; it has its own character and often features more dramatic contrasts
