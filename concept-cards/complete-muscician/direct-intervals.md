# Quick Definition
Direct intervals (also called hidden or similar intervals) occur when two voices approach a perfect consonance by similar motion, creating the impression of parallel fifths or octaves.

# Formal Definition
Direct intervals arise when two voices move in similar motion (same direction, different intervals) to arrive at a perfect consonance (fifth or octave). Such motion draws unwanted attention to the perfect interval's hollow sound and can give the impression of hidden parallel fifths or octaves, because the ear tends to fill in the intervening intervals. To avoid direct intervals, approach perfect consonances by contrary motion. The one exception: direct motion to an octave or fifth is permissible when the upper voice moves by step to the perfect interval.

# Musical Context
Avoiding direct intervals is important for maintaining voice independence and preventing the perception of parallel perfect intervals. Horn fifths are a special, accepted form of direct fifths where the upper voice moves by step and the lower voice leaps, creating a rustic, open sound associated with horn calls.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes confuse direct intervals with parallel intervals. Parallel intervals involve the same generic interval size in succession (e.g., 5 to 5); direct intervals involve similar motion arriving at a perfect interval from a different interval. Direct intervals are also called "hidden" because the ear fills in the gap, creating the illusion of parallels.
