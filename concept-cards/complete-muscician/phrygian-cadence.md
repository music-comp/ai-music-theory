# Quick Definition
A half cadence in minor where iv6 moves to V, with the bass descending by half step from ^6 to ^5, creating a characteristically intense sound.

# Formal Definition
The phrygian cadence (abbreviated PHRY) occurs when iv6 moves to V in minor, with the bass falling from ^6 to ^5 by half step. The soprano usually moves from ^4 to ^5 against the descending bass. The other voices double ^1 to avoid voice-leading errors. The powerful falling half step in the bass (the lowered ^6 to ^5) intensifies the tonal motion to dominant, giving this cadence its distinctive expressive character.

# Musical Context
Baroque composers frequently closed slow inner movements of multimovement works with a phrygian cadence, leaving the listener with a strong expectation of tonic in the opening of the next movement. The cadence is named for its resemblance to the half-step descent characteristic of the Phrygian mode.

# Examples
- Bach, "Wo soll ich fliehen hin," Cantata no. 5, BWV 5: phrygian cadence closing a slow movement
- Corelli, Violin Sonata no. 11 in D minor, op. 1: phrygian cadence intensified by double anticipation

# Common Confusions
- The phrygian cadence is a type of half cadence, not an authentic cadence -- it ends on V
- The bass must descend (^6 to ^5), not ascend -- this distinguishes it from the standard iv-V motion
- Only occurs in minor mode, where the half step between ^6 and ^5 is naturally present
