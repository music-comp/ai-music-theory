# Quick Definition
A prepared chromatic tonicization is a tonicization of a mixture chord (such as bVI or bIII) in which a pivot chord smoothly connects the original key to the new chromatic key area.

# Formal Definition
A prepared chromatic tonicization uses a pivot chord that functions diatonically or chromatically in both the original key and the target chromatic key. The transition is smooth because the pivot chord provides a logical harmonic connection. This contrasts with unprepared chromatic tonicization, where the new key arrives abruptly without a mediating pivot. The most common targets for prepared chromatic tonicization are bVI and bIII, which are the most commonly tonicized chromatic harmonies in art music.

# Musical Context
Prepared chromatic tonicizations are the norm in Classical and early Romantic music, where smooth modulations are prized. The technique becomes particularly important as composers extend mixture beyond single chords to establish full key areas. Both diatonic and chromatic pivot chords may be used, depending on the relationship between the two keys.

# Examples
- The parallel minor mode (e.g., C minor within C major) commonly serves as a pivot connecting the tonic to bVI, since it shares harmonies with both key areas.
- Schubert frequently prepares chromatic tonicizations through modal shifts that establish a common harmonic ground between the original and target keys.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes label any modulation involving a chromatic chord as "unprepared." Look carefully for pivot chords: if a single chord can be analyzed with dual function in both keys, the tonicization is prepared, even if the key relationship is remote.
