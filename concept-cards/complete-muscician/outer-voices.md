# Quick Definition
The outer voices are the highest (soprano) and lowest (bass) sounding parts in a musical texture, which are the most aurally prominent and provide the structural skeleton of tonal music.

# Formal Definition
In any multi-voice texture, the outer voices -- soprano (highest) and bass (lowest) -- are the most perceptually salient. The bass is harmonically the most important voice, determining chord inversions and driving the harmonic progression. The soprano carries the primary melody and draws the listener's attention through its registral prominence. Harmony can be understood as the filling in of musical space between these two structural voices with inner voices (alto and tenor).

# Musical Context
Outer-voice counterpoint is the primary concern in voice leading. Parallel perfect intervals between outer voices are particularly audible and must be avoided. The relationship between soprano and bass determines the quality of the harmonic progression, regardless of what occurs in the inner voices.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes give equal attention to all four voices. While all voices must follow voice-leading rules, the outer voices demand the most care. Errors in outer-voice counterpoint are more audible than errors in inner voices. Always check outer-voice relationships first.
