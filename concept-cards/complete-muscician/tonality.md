# Quick Definition
Tonality is the system of organizing pitches around a central pitch (the tonic), creating a hierarchy of stability and instability that drives musical motion.

# Formal Definition
Tonality refers to the hierarchical organization of pitches around a single pitch class called the tonic. In tonal music, all other pitches derive their meaning and function from their relationship to the tonic. This system, which governed Western art music from approximately 1650 to 1900 (the common-practice period), creates expectations of tension and resolution that give music its sense of directed motion.

# Musical Context
Tonality is the foundational organizing principle of Western music from the Baroque through the Romantic era and continues to underpin most popular music today. It governs not only individual melodies and harmonies but also large-scale formal structures, as composers move away from and return to the tonic to create musical narratives.

# Examples
- Schubert's "Gute Nacht" (Example 1.10) demonstrates tonality by establishing D minor as the tonic, with all pitches gravitating toward D as a point of stability.
- Bach's Violin Partita no. 3, Prelude (Example 1.22) shows how E functions as the tonic through metric accent, frequency of appearance, and its role as a point of arrival and stability.

# Common Confusions
Students often confuse tonality (the system of pitch organization) with key (the specific tonic and mode of a piece). Tonality is the broader system; key is a specific instance within that system. A piece "in D minor" has a key of D minor, but it operates within the tonal system.
