# Quick Definition
The viio7 chord (fully diminished seventh, built on ^7 in minor) is a highly unstable sonority containing two tritones, used primarily to expand tonic. It shares three pitches with V7 and is interchangeable with V7 inversions.

# Formal Definition
The viio7 (fully diminished seventh chord) is built on ^7 and contains ^7-^2-^4-^6 (in minor). It is highly dissonant, containing the interval of the diminished seventh and two tritones (^7-^4 and ^2-^6). Unlike V7, which shares one common tone with tonic (^5), viio7 shares no common tones with tonic. viio7 and its inversions function identically to V7 and its inversions because they share three pitches (^7, ^2, ^4): viio7 behaves like V65 (neighbor to I); viio65 behaves like V43 (passing between I and I6); viio43 behaves like V42 (passing between I and I6, or neighbor to I6); viio42 is rare and usually neighbors V.

# Musical Context
viio7 appears more often in minor-mode compositions because the chord occurs naturally within the minor scale. It is more dissonant than V7, making it a more intense embellishing chord. Composers sometimes substitute viio7 for V7 inversions when ^6 is needed in the soprano (since no V7 inversion contains ^6). viio7 provides particularly expressive support of ^6 in the soprano.

# Examples
In Gluck's Orfeo (Example 11.8), viio7 and its inversions create neighboring and passing expansions of tonic, similar to V7 inversions. In Mozart's Symphony K. 132 (Example 11.13), all four positions of viio7 occur in a dramatic passage where tonic plays a subordinate passing role, extending viio7 through an outer-voice contrary-motion diminished seventh with voice exchange.

# Common Confusions
Students may confuse viio7 (fully diminished, in minor) with viio7 (half-diminished, in major). The fully diminished seventh has two tritones and is much more dissonant than the half-diminished seventh. Also, viio7 expands tonic (contrapuntal function), not dominant. Since all four members are tendency tones, voice leading must be very careful.
