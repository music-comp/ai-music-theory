# Quick Definition
Embellishment is the process of adding tones of figuration and textural variety to a basic harmonic framework. Reduction is the reverse: stripping away surface detail to reveal the underlying two-voice contrapuntal structure.

# Formal Definition
Embellishment takes a skeletal two-voice counterpoint or four-voice homophonic texture and adds tones of figuration (passing tones, neighbors, chordal leaps), rhythmic variety, and textural elaboration to create a richly ornamented surface. Reduction reverses the process, removing surface embellishments to reveal the underlying two-voice contrapuntal framework of a passage. Both processes are essential analytical and compositional techniques. Through repeated embellishment of a basic model, an actual piece of music can be generated. Through reduction, the structural foundations of complex passages are revealed.

# Musical Context
These complementary processes are fundamental to understanding and creating tonal music. Reduction shows that superficially different passages may share the same underlying structure. Embellishment shows how composers create variety and interest from simple foundations.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes think embellishment means adding random ornaments. In fact, embellishment follows strict rules: tones of figuration must be consonant with the underlying harmony or resolve properly if dissonant. Similarly, reduction is not just deleting notes -- it requires understanding which pitches are structural and which are embellishing.
