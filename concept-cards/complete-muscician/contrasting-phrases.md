# Quick Definition
Two phrases in a period that have different melodic material, labeled a and b in formal diagrams.

# Formal Definition
When the two phrases of a period are melodically dissimilar, they are called contrasting phrases. The second phrase (b) presents melodic material that is distinctly different from the first phrase (a). Contrasting melodic structure appears in period labels such as CIP (contrasting interrupted period) and CCP (contrasting continuous period). Contrasting phrases often arise when the harmonic structure is continuous, because the second phrase begins on a non-tonic harmony, which naturally encourages different melodic content.

# Musical Context
Contrasting phrases provide variety within the period structure. They are somewhat less common than parallel phrases in interrupted periods but appear frequently in continuous and progressive periods.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata no. 11 in B-flat major, op. 22, Menuetto: second phrase begins with new melodic material (b) contrasting with the first phrase (a)

# Common Confusions
- Contrasting does not mean unrelated; the two phrases still form a coherent period through their harmonic relationship
- Some phrase pairs fall on a spectrum between parallel and contrasting
