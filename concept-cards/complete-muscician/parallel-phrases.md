# Quick Definition
Two phrases in a period that share similar melodic material, labeled a and a' in formal diagrams.

# Formal Definition
When the two phrases of a period are melodically similar to each other, they are called parallel phrases. The second phrase (a') typically begins with the same or very similar melodic material as the first phrase (a) but diverges near the cadence to achieve a different (usually stronger) harmonic goal. Parallel melodic structure is the first component of period labels such as PIP (parallel interrupted period) and PSP (parallel sectional period).

# Musical Context
Parallel phrases provide a sense of recognition and unity within periods. The listener's familiarity with the melodic material from the first phrase creates expectations that the second phrase can either fulfill or subtly modify.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in B-flat major, K. 333: second phrase begins with nearly identical melodic material, diverging only at the cadence

# Common Confusions
- Parallel phrases need not be identical; a' may include ornamental changes, especially near the cadence
- "Parallel" refers to melodic similarity, not to parallel voice leading or parallel keys
