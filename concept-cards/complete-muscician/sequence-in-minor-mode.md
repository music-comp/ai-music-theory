# Quick Definition
Sequences in minor keys require special attention to the placement of the bass tritone, the treatment of the leading tone, and the avoidance of augmented seconds.

# Formal Definition
When sequences occur in minor keys, several adjustments are necessary. In the D2 (-5/+4) sequence, the melodic tritone in the bass occurs near the end of the sequence (whereas in major it occurs near the beginning). The tritone leap is acceptable and necessary to keep pitches within the key. In D3 (-4/+2) sequences in minor, the minor v chord (without raised leading tone) may be necessary to avoid the augmented second between ^6 and ^7. Root-position diminished triads (ii in minor) appear within sequences as members of the larger pattern, even though they are normally prohibited outside sequential contexts.

# Musical Context
Minor-mode sequences require the same types of adjustments that minor-mode harmony generally requires (leading tone treatment, augmented second avoidance), applied within the sequential framework.

# Examples
- D2 (-5/+4) in G minor: the bass tritone E-flat to A appears near the end, and ii (A diminished) appears in root position
- D3 (-4/+2) in minor: minor v chord used to avoid augmented second

# Common Confusions
- The bass tritone in minor-mode D2 sequences falls in a different location than in major
- Minor v (without leading tone) is acceptable within sequences to avoid augmented seconds
- Root-position diminished triads within sequences are acceptable as part of the sequential pattern
