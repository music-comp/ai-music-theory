# Quick Definition
Elaboration transforms a skeletal harmonic structure into a performed piece through arpeggiation, added figuration, and textural variety. Reduction reverses this, simplifying a complex score to reveal its essential voice leading and harmony.

# Formal Definition
Elaboration and reduction are complementary analytical and compositional processes. Elaboration takes a skeletal two-voice counterpoint, four-voice homophonic texture, or figured bass and expands it through arpeggiation, changes in contour, added tones of figuration, and varied textures. Reduction takes a florid example from the literature and simplifies it to reveal only the essential outer-voice counterpoint and homophonic inner voices. The reduction process involves: (1) determining the key, (2) identifying harmonic function and rhythm by focusing on accented bass notes, (3) finding the soprano and establishing outer-voice counterpoint, (4) adding inner voices. The elaboration process involves: (1) determining chords from figures, (2) adding a soprano, (3) adding inner voices, (4) horizontalizing through arpeggiation, (5) adding figuration.

# Musical Context
These processes bridge the gap between analytical understanding and practical musicianship. Reduction allows performers to see through complex scores to their structural foundations. Elaboration allows instrumentalists to improvise creative realizations of harmonic frameworks, connecting theoretical knowledge to performance practice, particularly for continuo playing and improvisation.

# Common Confusions
Students may think reduction means losing musical content. In fact, reduction reveals the essential structure that the embellishments elaborate. Similarly, elaboration is not random decoration but follows strict voice-leading and harmonic protocols. Both processes require understanding the underlying four-voice framework.
