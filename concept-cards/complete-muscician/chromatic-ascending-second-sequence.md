# Quick Definition
The chromatic A2 (-M3/+P4) sequence ascends by half step with all structural chords as major triads, each transformed into an applied 6/5 chord through a sustained common-tone bass.

# Formal Definition
The chromatic ascending second sequence A2 (-M3/+P4) is the symmetrical counterpart of the diatonic A2 (-3/+4) sequence. Like its diatonic parent, it derives from the contrapuntal 5-6 motion. In the fully chromatic form, the structural first chord of every repetition is a major triad, and the sequence ascends by half step. The bass is sustained through the two-chord repetition, with every second chord transformed into an applied 6/5 chord (reflected in enharmonic respelling of the bass to show its leading-tone function). Two chromatic variants exist: one with diatonic roots but chromatically altered structural chords, and one that is fully chromatic. Applied chords may appear in root position, a technique often used in popular music. A second sustained common tone can produce an augmented triad on the second chord.

# Musical Context
The chromatic A2 sequence is a versatile tool appearing in both art music and popular music. Its half-step ascent creates a sense of rising intensity and harmonic richness. The technique of sustaining the bass while transforming the harmony above it is particularly effective for building dramatic tension.

# Common Confusions
The diatonic A2 (-3/+4) sequence with applied chords contains accidentals but is still considered diatonic because its main structural chords fall on diatonic scale degrees. The truly chromatic A2 (-M3/+P4) is distinguished by having chromatically altered main chords that ascend by consistent half steps.
