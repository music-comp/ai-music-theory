# Quick Definition
Guidelines for composing smooth modulations: balance key durations, choose PD pivot chords, avoid crashing into cadences, and use stepwise soprano lines.

# Formal Definition
Four guidelines for writing modulations: (1) Balance the duration of the two tonal areas; place the pivot chord about halfway through the phrase. (2) Line up diatonic chords for each key to identify potential pivots; the best pivots act as PD in the new key; avoid using the dominant of the new key as pivot. (3) Do not crash immediately into a PAC after the pivot; insert a contrapuntal cadence or EPM first, then close with a structural cadence. (4) Use a stepwise soprano line to move to the strongest possible cadence in the new key (PAC using cadential 6/4). All modulations must be tested on the piano, as what looks successful on paper may not sound convincing.

# Musical Context
These guidelines ensure that modulations sound smooth and convincing rather than abrupt. They apply to all pivot-chord modulations in common-practice style.

# Examples
- Modulating from F major to D minor: identify all possible pivot chords by comparing diatonic triads in both keys, then select the most effective one (typically one functioning as PD in D minor)

# Common Confusions
- A modulation that looks correct on paper may sound harsh if the pivot chord does not convincingly function in both keys
- The new key must be established through at least a few chords before the cadence
- Avoid using V of the new key as the pivot chord
