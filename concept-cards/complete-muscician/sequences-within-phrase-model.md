# Quick Definition
Techniques for fitting sequences into phrases by considering harmonic rhythm, balance, proportion, and the relationship between the sequence and surrounding harmonic functions.

# Formal Definition
When composing sequences within phrases, three solutions address the challenge of fitting sequences into limited space: (1) use faster rhythmic values for sequence chords (least effective, can sound forced); (2) use only part of a sequence, starting or ending mid-pattern (e.g., starting a D2 on iii rather than I); (3) increase the phrase length to accommodate the sequence. Common strategies include overlapping the end of a tonic expansion with the beginning of the sequence, and overlapping the end of the sequence with the pre-dominant. The three basic components of phrases containing sequences are: tonic expansion, sequence, and cadence.

# Musical Context
These compositional techniques allow sequences to fit naturally into phrases of varying lengths. A four-measure phrase can contain a complete sequence if the meter accommodates enough harmonic changes and overlapping is employed.

# Examples
- Four-measure phrase: tonic expansion (m. 1), D2 (-5/+4) sequence overlapping with tonic and PD (m. 2), PD-D-T cadence (mm. 3-4)
- Six-measure phrase: extended interior from inserted sequence
- Complete D2 (-5/+4) + 6/3 sequence can fit in a four-measure phrase with first-inversion chords and overlapping

- Sequences should fit logically within the musical fabric, not sound wedged in
- The beginning and ending of a sequence can overlap with surrounding functional harmonies
