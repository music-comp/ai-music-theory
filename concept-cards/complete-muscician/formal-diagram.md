# Quick Definition
A visual representation of phrase structure showing melodic relationships, cadences, and harmonic content of periods and larger forms.

# Formal Definition
A formal diagram uses arcs to represent phrases, with an overarching curve indicating that phrases combine into a period. Letter names represent melodic relationships: "a" for the first phrase's melody, "a'" for similar melodic material in the second phrase (parallel), and "b" for contrasting material. The diagram also indicates the initial harmony and cadence type (HC, IAC, PAC) for each phrase. A double slash (//) after HC indicates a harmonic interruption. The three-word period label (e.g., "parallel interrupted period") accompanies the diagram.

# Musical Context
Formal diagrams are essential analytical tools for representing and comparing phrase structures across pieces. They allow quick identification of period types and facilitate discussion of formal relationships.

# Examples
- PIP diagram: arc labeled "a" with "I...HC //" followed by arc labeled "a'" with "I...PAC"
- CCP diagram: arc labeled "a" with "I...HC" followed by arc labeled "b" with phrase 2 beginning on non-tonic harmony, ending PAC

# Common Confusions
- The formal diagram represents both melodic and harmonic dimensions; students sometimes label only one
- The interruption symbol (//) applies only when the consequent phrase restarts on tonic after a HC
