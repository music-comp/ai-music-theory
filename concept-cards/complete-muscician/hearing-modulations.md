# Quick Definition
Aural strategies for detecting and identifying modulations, including listening for persistent new chromatic pitches, comparing opening and closing modes, and singing the tonic pitches.

# Formal Definition
Three strategies for hearing modulations: (1) Listen for new chromatic pitches that persist through the end of the phrase, signaling a modulation. (2) Compare the mode at the beginning and end: major-to-minor likely modulates to iii or vi; minor-to-major likely modulates to III or VI. (3) For modulations staying in the same mode: physically sing the opening tonic and compare it to the closing tonic; if they differ, a modulation has occurred. For major-to-major modulations, the target is most likely V; for minor-to-minor, most likely v.

# Musical Context
Aural identification of modulations is a fundamental ear-training skill. It requires listening to complete harmonic spans rather than focusing on individual pitches.

# Examples
- Music begins in a major key and ends in a minor key: modulation is most likely to iii or vi
- Music begins in a minor key and ends in a major key: modulation is most likely to III (very common) or VI
- Music begins and ends in major: modulation is most likely to V

# Common Confusions
- A single hearing may not distinguish between tonicization and modulation; repeat signs can change the perception
- Always listen to the complete harmonic progression before determining the modulation target
- Process of elimination is essential: the possibilities are limited to closely related keys
