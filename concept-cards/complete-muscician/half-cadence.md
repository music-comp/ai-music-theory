# Quick Definition
A half cadence (HC) is a phrase ending on a root-position dominant chord, creating an open, unresolved effect like a musical question.

# Formal Definition
A half cadence (HC) closes on a root-position dominant chord. No formal terms distinguish different types of half cadence, although the root-position dominant usually supports ^2 in the soprano. The HC creates an effect of incompleteness -- a musical question that demands a continuation or answer. At a half cadence, the dominant triad (not V7) should be used, as the seventh would add too much instability.

# Musical Context
Half cadences typically end the first phrase of a two-phrase pair, creating an antecedent-consequent relationship. The opening of Beethoven's Sixth Symphony demonstrates: a four-measure phrase leads from tonic to dominant, creating the effect of a musical question that needs an answer.

# Examples
In Mozart's Rondo in C major K. 144, the first phrase (m. 4) ends with a half cadence on V, with the soprano arriving on ^2. The melody arpeggiates the dominant (D5-B4-G4) to confirm the arrival. Beethoven's "Pastorale" Symphony opens with a four-measure phrase ending on V, creating a question that is answered by the subsequent phrase.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes use V7 at a half cadence. Since the HC is a resting point, the dominant triad (without the destabilizing seventh) is preferred. Also, a HC is defined solely by ending on root-position V -- what precedes V is not prescribed.
