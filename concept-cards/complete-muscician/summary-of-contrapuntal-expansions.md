# Quick Definition
A comprehensive overview of all the chords and techniques used to expand tonic and dominant through contrapuntal means: chordal skips, passing motion, neighboring motion, and their combinations.

# Formal Definition
The complete inventory of contrapuntal expansion techniques using the chords introduced in Chapters 8-11: I and I6 (chordal leaps extending tonic); V and V6 (chordal leaps extending dominant; V6 as neighbor to I); V7, V65, V43, V42 (inversions of V7 extending tonic through passing and neighboring); viio6 (passing between I and I6); viio7 and inversions (parallel to V7 inversions in minor); IV and IV6 (arpeggiating bass for tonic expansion; passing for dominant expansion). All embellishing chords arise from three melodic motions in the bass: chordal leaps, passing tones, and neighbor tones. Structural harmonies are root-position I and V at cadences; everything else is prolongational.

# Musical Context
This summary represents the complete toolkit for Part 2 of the text. With only tonic and dominant functions (plus their contrapuntal extensions), a remarkable amount of musical content can be generated. Part 3 will introduce the third harmonic function (pre-dominant) and accented dissonance to expand the vocabulary further.

# Common Confusions
Students may feel overwhelmed by the number of possible progressions. The key organizing principle is that all contrapuntal expansions derive from just three melodic motions (chordal leaps, passing, neighboring) applied to just two harmonic functions (tonic and dominant). The chord choices follow from harmonizing those melodic motions.
