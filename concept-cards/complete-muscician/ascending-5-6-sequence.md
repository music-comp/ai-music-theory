# Quick Definition
An alternate name for the A2 (-3/+4) + 6/3 sequence, the oldest of all sequences, in which 5-6 motion occurs in the outer voices as the bass sustains a common tone.

# Formal Definition
The ascending 5-6 sequence is the first-inversion variant of the A2 (-3/+4) sequence. In this pattern, the bass is sustained as a common tone throughout the model and each copy, while other voices create alternating fifths and sixths above it. The 5-6 contrapuntal motion (usually in the outer voices) averts the parallels that would occur in a simple stepwise ascending progression. This is perhaps the oldest of all sequences, found in music by Josquin Desprez (d. 1521) and remaining common through the present day. It forms the basis for the applied-chord A2 (-3/+4) sequence.

# Musical Context
The ascending 5-6 sequence is foundational to the development of sequential writing in Western music. Its common-tone bass and smooth voice leading make it one of the most elegant and widely used sequential patterns.

# Examples
- Found in early sixteenth-century music by Josquin Desprez
- Forms the diatonic basis for the chromatic ascending bass line created by the applied-chord A2 (-3/+4) sequence

# Common Confusions
- "Ascending" 5-6 is distinct from "descending" 5-6 (D3 variant); they move in opposite directions
- The ascending 5-6 forms the basis for the applied-chord variant; raising the bass of the 6/3 chord creates the applied dominant
