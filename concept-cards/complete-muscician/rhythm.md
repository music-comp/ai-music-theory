# Quick Definition
Rhythm is the ever-changing combination of longer and shorter durations and silences that populate the musical surface, creating patterns that divide, align with, or extend over beats.

# Formal Definition
Rhythm refers to the temporal patterning of musical events on the surface of a piece. Unlike meter, which provides a regular underlying framework, rhythm is the specific sequence of durations and rests that gives music its particular character. Rhythmic groupings may occur at various levels relative to the beat: they may subdivide the beat, coincide with it, or span multiple beats. Rhythmic notation uses a system of note values (whole, half, quarter, eighth, sixteenth notes) and corresponding rests, each related by a 2:1 ratio. Dots extend a note's duration by half its value; ties connect two notes of the same pitch into a single sustained duration.

# Musical Context
Rhythm, along with pitch, is one of the two primary dimensions of music. It exists on the "surface" of music, animated by the underlying metric grid. Rhythmic patterns contribute significantly to musical style, mood, and character.

# Examples
- Durational symbols: whole note, half note, quarter note, eighth note, sixteenth note, thirty-second note, with corresponding rests.
- A dotted quarter note equals a quarter note plus an eighth note (1.5 beats in simple meter).
- Ties allow durations to extend across bar lines and beat boundaries.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes confuse rhythm with meter. Meter is the recurring pattern of strong and weak beats; rhythm is the specific pattern of durations that unfolds against the metric framework. Rhythm is variable and changing; meter is regular and constant (unless deliberately disrupted).
