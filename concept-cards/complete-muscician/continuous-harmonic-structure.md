# Quick Definition
A harmonic structure in binary form where the first section closes away from the tonic (typically on V or III), and the second section continues away from tonic before returning.

# Formal Definition
When the first section of a binary form closes with a cadence in a key other than the tonic, the harmonic structure is called continuous. The second section typically begins in or near the non-tonic key before working back to the home key. This creates a large-scale harmonic motion away from and back to the tonic. Common modulation targets for the first section: V in major keys, III or v in minor keys. The continuous harmonic structure is the most common type in binary form and parallels the continuous period.

# Musical Context
Most binary forms are continuous, particularly in Baroque dance movements and Classical minuets/scherzos. The rounded continuous binary form is the single most common type of two-reprise form.

# Examples
- Weber, Six Ecossaise, no. 1: simple continuous binary (first section closes on V, which continues after the double bar before returning to tonic)
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in D major, K. 284, Theme: rounded continuous binary (first section modulates to V)

# Common Confusions
- The dominant at the end of the first section in continuous binary is weakened by the addition of the seventh, forcing it back toward the tonic
- Listening hint: if you hear a chord change from the cadence to the repeat of the opening, the form is continuous; if not, it is sectional
