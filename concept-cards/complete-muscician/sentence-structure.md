# Quick Definition
A rhetorical formal device in which a short musical idea is stated, restated, and then elaborated in a longer continuation, following the proportional model 1:1:2.

# Formal Definition
A sentence structure unfolds as a short-short-long pattern, typically in the proportion 1:1:2. The first segment (A) presents a basic idea; the second segment (A') restates it with slight variation; and the third segment (B) is exactly twice as long and develops the idea, culminating in a cadence. In an eight-measure sentence, this yields a 2+2+4 structure. The harmonic rhythm often accelerates in the B section, creating a paradox where the longer unit is internally fragmented.

# Musical Context
Sentence structure is found across many genres and periods, from folk tunes and blues to art music from the seventeenth century to the present. It provides a natural rhetorical shape analogous to spoken communication patterns (statement, restatement, elaboration/conclusion).

# Examples
- Beethoven, "Lustig-Traurig" in C major, WoO 54: mm. 1-2 (A, I to V), mm. 3-4 (A', V56 to I), mm. 5-8 (B, continuation to cadence)
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata no. 1 in F minor, op. 2: eight-measure sentence with acceleration of harmonic rhythm in B section; nested sentence within B (1+1+2)
- Haydn, Piano Sonata no. 38, Adagio: four-measure sentence
- Mozart, Violin Sonata in C major, K. 14: eight-measure sentence

# Common Confusions
- A sentence is not a period; it is a single phrase or can span a period, but its defining feature is the 1:1:2 proportional structure
- The B section of a sentence is not simply "new material" but a development and continuation of A/A'
- Sentences can be nested: the B portion may itself contain a miniature 1:1:2 structure
