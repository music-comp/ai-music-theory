# Quick Definition
A progression whose roots arpeggiate a triad by descending thirds (e.g., I-vi-IV, with roots ^1-^6-^4), distinct from a melodic bass arpeggiation where the bass notes themselves trace the arpeggiation.

# Formal Definition
A harmonic arpeggiation occurs when the roots of a chord progression trace the notes of a triad, typically through descending thirds (e.g., ^1-^6-^4 in I-vi-IV). This is distinct from a melodic bass arpeggiation, where the actual bass notes (which may differ from roots due to inversions) trace the arpeggiation pattern. The descending harmonic arpeggiation I-vi-IV is one of the most characteristic progressions in tonal music, with the submediant acting as a bridge between tonic and pre-dominant.

# Musical Context
Harmonic arpeggiations can be ascending (^1-^3-^5, as in I-iii-V) or descending (^1-^6-^4, as in I-vi-IV). They provide a smooth harmonic trajectory through the phrase, connecting functional areas through third-related root motions.

# Examples
- I-vi-IV (descending): roots trace ^1-^6-^4
- I-iii-V (ascending): roots trace ^1-^3-^5
- Schubert, "Fruhlingstraum": vi-ii6 creates both harmonic arpeggiation (roots ^6-^2) and melodic bass arpeggiation (bass ^6-^4)

# Common Confusions
- Harmonic arpeggiation refers to root motion, not necessarily bass motion; the bass may be inverted
- Melodic bass arpeggiation refers to the actual bass notes tracing an arpeggiation pattern
- The two types may coincide (as in root-position progressions) or differ (when inversions are used)
