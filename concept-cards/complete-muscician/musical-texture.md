# Quick Definition
Musical texture refers to the density and arrangement of voices in music, classified into three basic types: monophonic (single melody), polyphonic (multiple independent melodies), and homophonic (melody with accompaniment).

# Formal Definition
Texture describes music's density, including the number of voices, their spacing, and how they relate to one another. Three basic texture types exist: (1) Monophonic texture: a single melodic line with no accompaniment (even if played in octaves by multiple instruments). (2) Polyphonic (contrapuntal) texture: two or more independent melodies combined with no clear distinction between melody and accompaniment. (3) Homophonic texture: a melody accompanied by other voices, ranging from simple block chords (chorale texture) to complex broken-chord patterns. Homophonic texture is the most widespread in common-practice music due to the richness of possible accompanimental patterns.

# Musical Context
Understanding texture is essential for analysis because the same harmonies can be presented in radically different textures. Identifying the texture type helps determine the analytical approach: polyphonic textures require attention to individual voice leading; homophonic textures require grouping broken-chord patterns into underlying harmonies.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes think a texture with a clearly prominent melody is always homophonic. However, if the "accompaniment" is equally melodic and independent, the texture is polyphonic. The distinction lies in whether there is a clear hierarchy between melody and accompaniment (homophonic) or equal melodic independence (polyphonic).
