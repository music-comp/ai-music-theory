# Quick Definition
Melodic writing guidelines are principles for creating well-crafted tonal melodies, emphasizing stepwise motion, balanced contour, a single climax, and proper treatment of leaps and tendency tones.

# Formal Definition
Good tonal melodies follow these principles: (1) Begin on a member of the tonic triad (^1, ^3, or ^5) and end with ^2-^1 or ^7-^1 (a melodic cadence). (2) Maintain a range of about a tenth with a tessitura (comfortable span) of about a sixth. (3) Move primarily by step (conjunct motion), with occasional skips (thirds) and leaps (fourths or larger). (4) Leaps should be consonant intervals no larger than a m6, should lead to change of direction, and should ideally be prepared by a change of direction (the law of recovery). (5) Two consecutive skips of a third are permitted if followed by a change of direction. (6) Avoid repeated notes, sequences, and the dissonant augmented second in harmonic minor. (7) Build toward a single melodic climax, usually near the midpoint. (8) Resolve tendency tones, especially ^7 to ^1.

# Musical Context
These guidelines reflect common-practice melodic conventions observable across centuries of tonal music. They produce singable, memorable, and well-balanced melodies that create and dissipate tension effectively.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes think these guidelines are rigid rules. They are principles observed in good tonal melodies, not absolute laws. However, early melody-writing exercises should follow them closely. The guidelines also do not address rhythm and motive, which further shape melodic character.
