# Quick Definition
A repeating bass line pattern that forms the foundation for a set of continuous variations.

# Formal Definition
A ground bass is a specific type of ostinato consisting of a repeating bass line pattern, typically one to eight measures long, over which the upper voices create freely varying material. The ground bass provides harmonic and structural stability while surface elements change. Composers often harmonize the ground bass with a consistent harmonic pattern, blurring the distinction between ground bass and chaconne. Ground basses tend toward V at their end, allowing cadential overlap between variations. The descending tetrachord (e.g., ^1-^7-^6-^5) is one of the most common ground bass patterns.

# Musical Context
Ground bass compositions flourished in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, particularly in English music (Purcell) and Italian vocal music (Monteverdi). The form remains important in later music and in popular music.

# Examples
- Purcell, Ground in G: eight-measure ground bass with circled bass notes and harmonization
- Monteverdi, "Lamento della ninfa": descending tetrachord ground bass

# Common Confusions
- Ground bass and chaconne are often used interchangeably; pure examples of one without the other are rare
- The ground bass may be slightly varied in later repetitions without losing its identity
