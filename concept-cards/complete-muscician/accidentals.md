# Quick Definition
Accidentals are symbols placed before notes to raise or lower their pitch: sharp (#) raises by a half step, flat (b) lowers by a half step, natural cancels a previous accidental, and double sharp/double flat raise/lower by a whole step.

# Formal Definition
Five accidental symbols exist: the sharp (#) raises a pitch by one half step; the flat (b) lowers it by one half step; the natural sign cancels any previous accidental or key signature alteration; the double sharp (x) raises a pitch by two half steps (one whole step); the double flat (bb) lowers it by two half steps. Accidentals placed in a key signature apply throughout the piece; accidentals placed before individual notes apply only within that measure (and carry through the measure for that specific pitch and octave).

# Musical Context
Accidentals are essential for precise pitch specification beyond what key signatures provide. They create chromaticism, indicate leading tones in minor keys, and specify non-diatonic pitches. The correct use of accidentals (vs. enharmonic equivalents) communicates harmonic function and context.

# Examples
- Sharp: F becomes F#
- Flat: B becomes Bb
- Natural: cancels key-signature Bb to produce B natural
- Double sharp: F becomes Fx (sounds as G)
- Double flat: B becomes Bbb (sounds as A)

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes forget that accidentals carry through the measure. If an F# appears on beat 1, all subsequent F's in that measure and octave are also sharp unless a natural sign cancels it. Accidentals also do not carry across bar lines (unlike key signature accidentals, which persist until changed).
