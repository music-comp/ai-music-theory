# Quick Definition
A binary form in which all or part of the opening material returns in the tonic key after a digression in the second section, creating a cyclical three-part thematic design within a two-part tonal structure.

# Formal Definition
In rounded binary form, the second section begins with a digression (unstable, often fragmented material) that leads to a structural half cadence (the interruption, marked //). After the interruption, the opening material returns in the tonic key (the recapitulation). This creates a three-part thematic design (A - digression - A') within a two-part harmonic structure. The rounded binary structure resembles a parallel interrupted period at a larger scale. The rounded continuous binary form (with first section closing away from tonic) is the most common type of two-reprise form.

# Musical Context
Rounded binary form is the most common binary form type and the basis for the Classical minuet and scherzo. It is also the structural foundation from which sonata form evolved.

# Examples
- Haydn, Sonatina no. 4 in F major, Scherzo: rounded sectional binary (first section closes on tonic, opening material returns at m. 17 after HC at m. 16)
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in D major, K. 284, Theme: rounded continuous binary (first section closes away from tonic, digression in mm. 9-12, recapitulation at m. 14)

# Common Confusions
- Rounded binary has a three-part thematic design but a two-part tonal structure; this apparent conflict is a defining feature
- The interruption in rounded binary occurs at the structural HC within the second section, not at the double bar
- The recapitulation may restate only part of the opening material
