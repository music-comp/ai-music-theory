# Quick Definition
The Hollywood cadence is a chromatically embellished plagal cadence in which a contrapuntal 5-6 motion and modal mixture transform IV into ii-half-dim-6/5 before resolving to I.

# Formal Definition
The Hollywood cadence is a specific type of plagal motion in which the subdominant (IV) is chromatically altered through a 5-6 contrapuntal motion combined with modal mixture to produce ii-half-dim-6/5, which then resolves directly to the tonic without passing through the dominant. This cadence substitutes for the traditional authentic cadence by using pre-dominant mixture harmony in place of the dominant function. The term reflects its frequent use in popular music of the 1920s-1950s and in film scores.

# Musical Context
This cadence became a staple of Tin Pan Alley songwriting, jazz standards, and Hollywood film scoring. It provides a warm, satisfying close that avoids the directness of V-I while still creating a clear sense of arrival. Its roots lie in nineteenth-century Romantic harmony and the growing acceptance of plagal motions as cadential substitutes.

# Common Confusions
The Hollywood cadence is not simply IV-I (a standard plagal cadence). The distinctive element is the chromatic alteration: the 5-6 motion transforms IV into a half-diminished seventh chord containing b^6, which then resolves to I. The chromaticism gives this cadence its characteristic warm, bittersweet quality.
