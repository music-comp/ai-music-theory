# Quick Definition
A famous melody and harmonic pattern used as the basis for variation sets by numerous composers from the mid-seventeenth to mid-twentieth centuries.

# Formal Definition
The folia (originally meaning "crazy" or "empty-headed," referring to fast dance tempos) is a specific melody with a fixed harmonic setting that has served as a ground for variations by many composers. It consists of a theme with a characteristic harmonic and phrase structure in the minor mode. Famous composers who wrote folia variations include Lully, Handel, C.P.E. Bach, Carl Nielsen (Maskarade), and Rachmaninoff (Variations on a Theme by Corelli). Corelli's Folia from his op. 5 Violin Sonatas is among the most famous settings.

# Musical Context
The folia represents one of the most enduring compositional traditions in Western music, spanning over three centuries. It demonstrates how a single theme can inspire radically different treatments while maintaining its identity.

# Examples
- Corelli, Folia from op. 5 Violin Sonatas: theme with variations including study of harmonic setting, form, and phrase structure

# Common Confusions
- The folia is not a generic form but a specific theme and harmonic pattern
- "Variations on a Theme by Corelli" (Rachmaninoff) actually uses the folia, which predates Corelli
