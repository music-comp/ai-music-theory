# Quick Definition
Harmonic paradigms are short, recurring chord models (typically three to four chords) that form the building blocks of tonal music across all common-practice periods and into popular music today.

# Formal Definition
Harmonic paradigms are short chord progressions that serve as models for the unfolding of tonal music. They include patterns like I-V65-I (neighboring), I-viio6-I6 (passing), I-V43-I6 (passing), I-V42-I6 (passing with voice exchange), and their various combinations. These paradigms are crucial because they recur across the Baroque, Classical, Romantic, and late-nineteenth-century periods, as well as in commercial and popular music today. Performers and analysts must be intimately acquainted with each paradigm to recognize them immediately regardless of key, mode, meter, instrumentation, or texture.

# Musical Context
Harmonic paradigms are the skeletal frameworks that composers "flesh out" with their own distinctive figurations. Chopin's most complex textures, for example, still rely on these paradigms at surface and deeper levels. Recognizing paradigms allows rapid comprehension of harmonic structure in any style.

# Examples
Chopin's Etude op. 10/8 consists entirely of I-V65-I (neighboring paradigm) extended over eight measures with elaborate figuration. Chopin's Impromptu op. 29 uses I-V43-I (neighboring) and I-V43-I6 (passing) paradigms in mm. 1-4. These simple paradigms underlie the complex Romantic surface.

# Common Confusions
Students may assume that elaborate music requires elaborate harmonic structures. In fact, the most complex surfaces often rest on the simplest paradigms. The ability to recognize familiar paradigms beneath unfamiliar textures is a critical analytical skill.
