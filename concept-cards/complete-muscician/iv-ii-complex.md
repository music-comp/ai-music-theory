# Quick Definition
A voice-leading technique where IV and ii6 work together seamlessly to extend the pre-dominant function, using a 5-6 contrapuntal motion above the bass to avoid parallel fifths.

# Formal Definition
The IV-ii complex is a voice-leading procedure that extends the pre-dominant function. When a composer wishes to move the bass and soprano in the same direction from IV to V, parallel fifths are likely. To avoid this, the soprano creates a contrapuntal 5-6 motion above the bass, anticipating the dominant pitch and producing an apparent ii6 chord. This motion gives the impression that IV and ii6 are working together; however, at the second level, both chords are grouped under a single PD heading. The goal-oriented motion IV-ii(6) is common, but the weaker reverse motion ii-IV is not.

# Musical Context
The IV-ii complex appears throughout common-practice music whenever composers need smooth voice leading between pre-dominant and dominant while maintaining parallel outer-voice motion. It is a fundamental voice-leading solution found in works from the Baroque through the Romantic period.

# Examples
- Mozart, Symphony no. 35 in D major ("Haffner"), K. 385, Menuetto: Mozart shifts the upper note of the fifth by step to create ii6, avoiding parallel fifths from IV to V

# Common Confusions
- The IV-ii complex is not a chord change in the usual sense; it is a voice-leading shift within a single pre-dominant function
- The motion is goal-oriented: IV to ii(6) works, but ii to IV does not
- At the second level of analysis, both IV and ii6 appear under a single PD label
