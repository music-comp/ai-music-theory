# Quick Definition
The medial caesura is the pause or break that occurs between the end of the transition and the beginning of the second tonal area, marking the approximate midpoint of the exposition.

# Formal Definition
The medial caesura (MC) is a structural articulation point that divides the exposition into its two main parts: the FTA with its transition and the STA with its closing section. It typically occurs as a brief silence, rhythmic break, or harmonic pause at the end of the transition, usually on the dominant of the new key (a "V: HC MC") or on the new tonic itself. The medial caesura signals to the listener that new thematic material in the contrasting key is about to begin. It serves as a formal landmark that helps define the boundary between the transition and STA.

# Musical Context
The medial caesura is an important structural marker in sonata-form analysis. Its location helps identify where the transition ends and the STA begins, which can be especially important in works where thematic boundaries are unclear (as in monothematic sonata forms). The strength and clarity of the medial caesura varies across composers and periods.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata no. 19, op. 49, no. 1 (Example 33.2): The transition ends with a half cadence in Bb major (III), creating a clear medial caesura before the STA entrance.
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in C major, Hob. XVI.35 (Example 33.9): V/V at m. 35 marks the end of the transition and the medial caesura before the STA.

# Common Confusions
The medial caesura is not always a literal silence. It can be a rhythmic break, a harmonic arrival, or simply a clear articulation point. Also, not every sonata form has a clearly articulated medial caesura; some transitions merge seamlessly into the STA.
