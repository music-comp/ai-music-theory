# Quick Definition
A period in which the two phrases have different melodic material (contrasting) and the harmonic motion is interrupted at the half cadence, restarting on tonic in the consequent.

# Formal Definition
Abbreviated CIP. The antecedent phrase ends with a HC and the consequent phrase restarts on tonic with new melodic material (labeled a and b), pushing through to PAC. This is the rarest of the eight period types, because the combination of harmonic interruption (which implies restart) with contrasting melodic material creates a somewhat contradictory effect: the listener expects thematic continuity from the restart but hears new material instead.

# Musical Context
The CIP is the least common period type. It appears occasionally in Classical-era music but is less natural than the PIP (where the restart invites melodic repetition) or the CCP (where contrasting melody fits the continuous harmonic motion).

# Common Confusions
- The rarity of the CIP makes it important to distinguish from the common CCP; the key difference is whether the consequent restarts on tonic (interrupted) or continues from a non-tonic harmony (continuous)
