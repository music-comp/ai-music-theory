# Quick Definition
A melodic interval occurs when two pitches sound in succession; a harmonic interval occurs when two pitches sound simultaneously.

# Formal Definition
When one pitch follows another in time, the resulting interval is a melodic (or linear) interval, and we can specify its direction as ascending or descending. When two pitches sound simultaneously, the interval is a harmonic interval, and contour cannot be specified since the pitches occur together. Both melodic and harmonic intervals are classified using the same system of generic size and specific quality.

# Musical Context
Melodic intervals are the building blocks of melody and voice leading. Harmonic intervals are the building blocks of chords and vertical sonorities. In counterpoint, both types interact: each voice creates melodic intervals through its horizontal motion, while the simultaneous combination of voices creates harmonic intervals vertically.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes forget that contour (ascending/descending) applies only to melodic intervals. Harmonic intervals have no contour because the pitches sound simultaneously. When labeling harmonic intervals, simply give the generic size and quality.
