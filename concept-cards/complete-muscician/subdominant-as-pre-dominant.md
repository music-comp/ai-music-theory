# Quick Definition
The subdominant (IV in major, iv in minor) serves as a pre-dominant chord, moving to the dominant with smooth stepwise bass motion from ^4 to ^5.

# Formal Definition
The subdominant functions as pre-dominant because the I-IV motion proceeds by descending fifth (the most convincing root motion in tonal music) and the bass ascent from ^4 to ^5 creates smooth voice leading to the dominant. When the bass ascends by step, upper voices usually descend in contrary motion to avoid voice-leading problems. IV can also appear in first inversion (IV6), with the bass falling from ^6 to ^5, which is especially effective in minor where the half step intensifies motion to V.

# Musical Context
IV is frequently chosen as pre-dominant in both major and minor modes. The soprano motion ^1-^7-^1 associated with IV-V-I is less dynamic than the ^2-^7-^1 double-neighbor motion of ii-V-I. In minor, iv6 moving to V creates the phrygian cadence.

# Examples
- When IV moves to V7, the soprano's ^4 prepares the seventh of the upcoming dominant as part of an IAC

# Common Confusions
- IV as pre-dominant (functional, moving to V) vs. IV as embellishing chord (prolonging tonic through neighbor motion)
- In minor, use contrary motion when moving from iv to V to avoid voice-leading errors
- ^6 must descend to ^5 in minor; ^7 must be approached from above
