# Quick Definition
Dictation is the process of listening to music and notating it from memory, developing the crucial skill of translating aural perception into written form.

# Formal Definition
Dictation involves memorizing and notating musical passages without the aid of a score. It develops musical memory, aural skills, and understanding of musical structure. The recommended approach is to avoid note-by-note transcription; instead, first internalize the musical context (key, mode, phrase structure), develop the ability to sing the passage from memory, and only then notate. Long-range memory and contextual understanding yield better results than attempting to write each note as heard.

# Musical Context
Dictation skills inform sight-reading, memorization, intonation, ensemble playing, score learning, and understanding of musical style. The process begins by establishing the key (singing ^1 and ^1-^3-^5), listening for large-scale features (beginnings, endings, phrase divisions), and gradually refining the details.

# Examples
- Begin by singing ^1, then ^1-^3-^5 to situate yourself in the key.
- Listen for beginnings and endings first; consider whether the example divides into smaller units.
- Only notate a melody once you can sing it from memory.

# Common Confusions
The most common mistake is trying to write each note the instant you hear it. This note-by-note approach loses musical context, hinders the development of musical memory, and compounds errors (if one note is wrong, all subsequent notes may be wrong). Always listen first, memorize, then write.
