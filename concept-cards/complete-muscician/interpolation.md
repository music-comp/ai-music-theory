# Quick Definition
A developmental technique involving the substantial addition of new material within a motivic context, creating the effect of a parenthetical insertion.

# Formal Definition
Interpolation involves the substantial addition of pitches or material to a motive, creating the effect of an insertion rather than a mere embellishment. The effect is equivalent to parentheses or an independent clause in language. Musical interpolations can appear at various levels from the phrase level to large structures such as the cadenza in a concerto. Composers often clarify the insertional nature of interpolated material by changing dynamic level, texture, or other musical parameters.

# Musical Context
Interpolation is a developmental motivic technique that disrupts the expected continuation of a motive with new or parenthetical material before the motive resumes. It provides contrast and variety within a motivically unified passage.

# Examples
- Haydn, String Quartet in Bb major, op. 33, no. 2, Scherzo: interpolation in mm. 5-6 prolongs the dominant in echo fashion, clarified by a shift from forte to piano and back to forte; only the neighboring portion of the motive is stated during the interpolation

# Common Confusions
- Interpolation adds substantial new material; embellishment adds small ornamental pitches to the existing motive
- Interpolations often feature changed dynamics or texture to signal their parenthetical nature
- The interpolated material may itself be derived from the motive (e.g., fragmented portions)
