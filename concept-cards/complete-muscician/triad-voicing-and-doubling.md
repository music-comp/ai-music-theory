# Quick Definition
Voicing refers to the distribution of chord tones across registers (close or open position); doubling adds a fourth voice by duplicating one chord member, most commonly the root.

# Formal Definition
Close position arranges the three chord members directly above one another in the tightest possible spacing. Open position distributes chord members across wider intervals, typically with wider spacing below (to preserve clarity in the bass register). Doubling increases the number of sounded notes from three to four by duplicating one chord member at the unison or octave. The root is most commonly doubled because it is the most stable member, though thirds and fifths may also be doubled. In four-voice writing, doubling is necessary since triads have only three distinct pitches.

# Musical Context
Open spacing is generally preferred in practice because tightly spaced triads in the bass register are hard to hear clearly. The choice of which member to double affects the sonority's stability and voice-leading options. Doubling the root reinforces the chord's foundation; doubling the third gives greater emphasis to the chord's quality.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes confuse voicing (the distribution of notes in register) with inversion (which chord member is in the bass). A chord can be in root position in either close or open voicing. Also, doubling does not change the chord's identity or inversion; it simply adds another instance of an existing member.
