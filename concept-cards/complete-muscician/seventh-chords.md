# Quick Definition
Seventh chords are four-note chords built by stacking three thirds above a root, spanning the dissonant interval of a seventh, which creates an inherent instability requiring resolution.

# Formal Definition
A seventh chord contains four distinct pitches stacked in thirds: root, third, fifth, and seventh. Because the chord spans a seventh (a dissonant interval), all seventh chords are dissonant to varying degrees. Five principal types exist, named by the quality of their triad and seventh: (1) major-major (MM7, major seventh chord), (2) major-minor (Mm7, major-minor or dominant seventh chord), (3) minor-minor (mm7, minor seventh chord), (4) diminished-minor (dm7, half-diminished seventh chord, notated with slashed degree sign), (5) diminished-diminished (dd7, fully diminished seventh chord, notated with degree sign). Root-position seventh chords may omit the fifth and double the root.

# Musical Context
Seventh chords add harmonic richness and dissonance to tonal music. They are common from the seventeenth century onward and are fundamental to jazz and popular music. The dissonant seventh creates a drive toward resolution, making seventh chords powerful agents of harmonic motion.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes confuse the names of seventh chord types. "Major seventh" (MM7) refers to the quality of the seventh interval (major), not just the triad. A "dominant seventh" (Mm7) has a major triad but a minor seventh. The naming convention is: [triad quality]-[seventh quality]. Also, the diminished seventh chord contains all minor thirds and diminished fifths, making it symmetrical.
