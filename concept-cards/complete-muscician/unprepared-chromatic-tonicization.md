# Quick Definition
An unprepared chromatic tonicization arrives at a new chromatic key abruptly, without a mediating pivot chord, creating a startling tonal shift.

# Formal Definition
An unprepared chromatic tonicization (or unprepared chromatic modulation) occurs when a new, chromatically related key area arrives without any mediating pivot chord to smooth the transition. Unlike prepared chromatic tonicizations, where a chord functions in both keys, unprepared tonicizations feature an abrupt juxtaposition of two distantly related harmonies. The dramatic effect of the sudden tonal shift is often the compositional goal, as the surprise serves expressive or dramatic purposes.

# Musical Context
Unprepared chromatic tonicizations are more characteristic of Romantic composers who valued dramatic surprise over smooth modulation. They frequently appear at points of poetic or dramatic significance in vocal music, where a sudden tonal shift mirrors a sudden change in the text's meaning or emotion.

# Examples
- Schumann, "Waldesgesprach" (Example 28.14): The abrupt shift from E major to C major (bVI) at the Lorelei's entrance is unprepared, with no pivot chord mediating between the two keys. The sudden juxtaposition enhances the mysterious character of the supernatural figure.

# Common Confusions
An unprepared tonicization is not the same as a modulation without any connection between the keys. Even in unprepared tonicizations, there may be common tones or voice-leading connections between the two keys; what is missing is a chord that functions simultaneously in both keys as a formal pivot.
