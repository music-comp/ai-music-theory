# Quick Definition
Diatonic refers to pitches, intervals, or chords that belong to the prevailing major or minor scale, as opposed to chromatic elements that fall outside it.

# Formal Definition
The term diatonic describes musical elements drawn exclusively from a given major or minor key's pitch collection. A diatonic melody uses only the seven pitch classes of the key; diatonic intervals are those formed between members of the same key; diatonic chords are built entirely from scale degrees of the key. Diatonic is contrasted with chromatic, which refers to pitches outside the prevailing scale.

# Musical Context
Most tonal music is predominantly diatonic, with chromatic pitches used for emphasis, embellishment, or modulation. Understanding which elements are diatonic versus chromatic is essential for harmonic analysis and for understanding a pitch's function within a key.

# Examples
- In C major, the pitches C, D, E, F, G, A, B are diatonic; F#, Bb, etc. are chromatic.
- A diatonic interval in G major: B up to E (a perfect fourth) -- both pitches are in the G major scale.
- All seven triads built on the scale degrees of a major key are diatonic triads.

# Common Confusions
In minor keys, both the raised and lowered forms of ^6 and ^7 are considered diatonic, since all forms of the minor scale are part of the same minor collection. A raised ^7 (leading tone) in minor is diatonic, not chromatic.
