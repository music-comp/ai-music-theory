# Quick Definition
An unaccented passing tone is a dissonant note on a weak beat that fills the melodic space of a third between two consonant notes by stepwise motion in a single direction.

# Formal Definition
The unaccented passing tone (PT) is the only type of dissonance permitted in second-species counterpoint. It occurs on a weak beat, filling the interval of a third between two strong-beat consonances. The motion into and out of the PT must be stepwise and in a single direction (either ascending or descending). Passing tones can also be consonant, as in the 5-6 technique where step motion between two consonances creates a consonant passing tone.

# Musical Context
The passing tone is one of the most fundamental dissonance types in tonal music. It provides smooth melodic connection between consonances and adds forward momentum. In later chapters, passing tones appear in accented positions and in chromatic forms, but the unaccented diatonic passing tone introduced in second-species counterpoint is the prototype for all passing dissonances.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes confuse passing tones with neighbor tones. A passing tone moves through by step in one direction; a neighbor tone moves by step away from and back to the same pitch. In second-species counterpoint, only passing tones are permitted as dissonances; neighbor tones must be consonant.
