# Quick Definition
A part exchange is a multiple-pitch swapping between voices, extending the concept of voice exchange from single pitches to pairs or groups of pitches.

# Formal Definition
A part exchange occurs when two or more pitches are swapped between voices, as opposed to a voice exchange where only a single pitch is traded. Part exchanges create contrapuntal connections between subphrases and extend harmonic prolongations. They are particularly common in passages using multiple inversions of V7 to prolong tonic.

# Musical Context
Part exchanges reveal how composers create coherence across larger spans through systematic pitch relationships between voices. They often appear in conjunction with combined inversions of V7, linking subphrases and maintaining voice-leading connections.

# Examples
In Mozart's Presto K. 280 (Example 11.6B), V43 and V42 expand tonic through part exchanges: the two-note pairs Bb-A and G-F are melodically swapped between the upper voice and bass. These part exchanges are members of a longer stepwise falling fifth from C down to F in the upper voice.

# Common Confusions
Students may confuse part exchanges with simple voice crossings. Part exchanges are deliberate compositional techniques that maintain voice-leading connections while extending prolongations. They follow the logic of the underlying harmonic and contrapuntal structure.
