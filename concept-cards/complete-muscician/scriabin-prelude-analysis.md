# Quick Definition
Scriabin's Prelude, op. 39, no. 2 demonstrates the convergence of symmetry, ambiguity, double tonality, and intervallic cells at the boundary between tonal and atonal music.

# Formal Definition
This 28-measure prelude (1903) represents a transitional work between Scriabin's Chopin-influenced early style and his later atonal works. The opening sonority (F#-A#-C-E) is an altered D-major tonic that functions as V6/5 of IV. This chord is symmetrical: all its intervals belong to a single whole-tone track, and its pitch content returns at the tritone transposition. Through enharmonic reinterpretation, the sonority can function as an altered V7 (in B or F), a French augmented sixth (in Bb or E), or a FrV4/3 (in F or B), giving access to six potential key areas. The form consists of two halves (mm. 1-12 and 13-24) with the second half transposing the first down a major second, followed by a cadential area (mm. 25-28). The deep-level harmonic plan is traditional: I-(IV)-bVII-bII-V-I in D major.

# Musical Context
This prelude lies at the exact boundary between tonal and atonal music, summarizing the trends of Chapters 34-37: tonal ambiguity, asymmetrical vs. symmetrical structures, double tonality, and cells. It demonstrates how traditional tonal structures can underlie a surface of extreme chromaticism. Scriabin's career trajectory from this piece toward atonality exemplifies the broader historical path of Western harmony.

# Examples
- The opening chord (F#-A#-C-E) projects its pitches across the structural bass notes of mm. 1-13 (F#-A#-C-E), creating a motivic parallelism where a dissonant surface sonority controls deep-level harmonic events.
- The whole-tone properties of the opening chord connect to the French augmented sixth and altered dominant structures explored throughout Chapters 35-37.

# Common Confusions
Despite its extreme chromaticism, this piece is tonal. The tonic D major is confirmed in the final two measures. The analysis demonstrates that traditional tonal principles (prolongation, voice leading, functional harmony) still operate beneath the chromatic surface, even when those principles are pushed to their limit.
