# Quick Definition
A pause or break in the musical flow that may (but does not always) signal the boundary between subphrases within a phrase.

# Formal Definition
A caesura is a pause in the melodic or rhythmic flow that can delineate subphrases within a larger phrase. However, the presence of a caesura does not guarantee the presence of subphrases: if the harmony reveals a single continuous motion across the caesura, the passage may be a single undivided phrase. Conversely, subphrases may exist without caesuras when the motivic structure clearly delineates internal divisions.

# Musical Context
Caesuras help listeners perceive internal structure within phrases. They are created through rests, long notes, changes in register, or changes in texture. Performance interpretation should consider whether a caesura represents a genuine structural boundary or merely a surface articulation over continuous harmonic motion.

# Examples
- Haydn, Symphony no. 100 ("Military"): caesuras mark the boundaries of three subphrases within an eight-measure phrase
- Schubert, "Des Mullers Blumen": vocal line divided into two gestures by a caesura, but harmony reveals a single continuous phrase without subphrases

# Common Confusions
- A caesura does not automatically create a subphrase; harmonic continuity may override the surface pause
- Subphrases can exist without caesuras when motivic repetition defines the divisions
- Do not confuse caesuras with cadences; caesuras are internal pauses, cadences are structural arrivals
