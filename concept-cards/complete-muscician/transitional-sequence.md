# Quick Definition
A sequence that moves from one harmonic function to another, such as from tonic to pre-dominant (T-PD).

# Formal Definition
A transitional sequence connects two different harmonic functions within the phrase model. For example, a sequence beginning on I (tonic function) and ending on ii or IV (pre-dominant function) transitions from T to PD. Transitional sequences are one of the two primary functions of sequences within phrases (the other being prolongational). They frequently overlap with the surrounding harmonic functions: the beginning of the sequence may coincide with the end of a tonic expansion, and the end of the sequence may coincide with the pre-dominant.

# Musical Context
Transitional sequences provide elegant, goal-directed means for moving from one harmonic area to another within the phrase model. They are especially common in longer phrases where composers need to fill the space between tonic expansion and cadential progression.

# Examples
- A D2 (-5/+4) sequence starting on I and ending on ii, transitioning from tonic function to pre-dominant function

# Common Confusions
- Composers often blur the distinction between prolongational and transitional sequences by overlapping the end of the sequence with the next harmonic function
- Simply changing the rhythmic placement of chords can transform a transitional sequence into a prolongational one
