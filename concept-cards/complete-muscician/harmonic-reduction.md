# Quick Definition
A harmonic reduction simplifies a complex musical texture by notating only the underlying chords in close position, stripping away rhythmic and textural elaboration to reveal the harmonic skeleton.

# Formal Definition
A harmonic reduction extracts the essential harmonic content from a passage by notating the bass note and the chord members in close position, omitting rhythmic patterns, repetitions, and embellishments. Reductions capture harmonic motions and repeating patterns, making the harmonic structure visible at a glance. The soprano (highest note in the original texture) is preserved as the top voice of the reduction. This technique reveals the rate of harmonic change and the underlying chord progression.

# Musical Context
Harmonic reductions are invaluable for analysis, sight-reading, memorization, and score learning. They allow musicians to see the harmonic logic beneath complex textures, whether broken-chord patterns, contrapuntal lines, or elaborate figuration. With practice, musicians can perform such reductions mentally without writing them out.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes include too much detail in reductions, defeating the purpose. A reduction should show only the essential harmonic content: bass note, chord identity, and soprano pitch. Rhythmic patterns, doublings, and embellishments are stripped away. The goal is clarity, not completeness.
