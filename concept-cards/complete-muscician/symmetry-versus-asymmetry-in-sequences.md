# Quick Definition
Diatonic sequences are inherently asymmetrical (varying chord qualities and interval sizes) and goal-directed, while chromatic sequences are symmetrical (consistent chord quality and intervals) and tonally ambiguous.

# Formal Definition
The fundamental distinction between diatonic and chromatic sequences lies in their relationship to the diatonic scale's asymmetry. Diatonic sequences conform to the scale, resulting in varying chord qualities (major, minor, diminished) and varying interval sizes between repetitions. This asymmetry creates goal-directed motion, as the tritone within the scale breaks the pattern and pulls toward resolution. Chromatic sequences maintain consistent chord quality and exact intervallic distances, dividing the octave into equal parts. This symmetry eliminates the tritone-based pull toward a tonal goal, creating ambiguity. To restore tonal focus, chromatic sequences must break off (usually at the pre-dominant) and rejoin functional harmonic syntax.

# Musical Context
Understanding this distinction is essential for analyzing nineteenth-century music, where chromatic sequences increasingly replaced or extended diatonic ones. The transition from asymmetrical to symmetrical sequential thinking mirrors the broader historical trajectory from tonal clarity to tonal ambiguity.

# Examples
- Example 36.1A vs. 36.1B: The same D3 (-4/+2) model produces a goal-directed diatonic sequence (varying chord qualities, reaching ii) in A, but an ambiguous chromatic D2 (-P4/+m3) sequence (all major triads, landing on bIII) in B.
- The diatonic D2 (-5/+4) contains a tritone that creates directionality; the chromatic D2 (-P5/+P4) replaces it with all perfect fifths, requiring 12 steps to complete.

# Common Confusions
The mere presence of accidentals does not make a sequence chromatic. Applied-chord sequences contain accidentals but are diatonic because their main structural chords are diatonic. True chromatic sequences have chromatically altered main chords that maintain consistent quality and interval across all repetitions.
