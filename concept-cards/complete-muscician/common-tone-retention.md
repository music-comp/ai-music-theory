# Quick Definition
When two chords share a pitch (common tone), that pitch should be retained in the same voice to ensure smooth voice leading and minimal motion between chords.

# Formal Definition
Common tone retention is the practice of keeping a pitch shared between two successive chords in the same voice, either by tying or repeating it. This is a fundamental voice-leading guideline that promotes smooth chord connections. In the I-V-I progression, ^5 is a common tone (the fifth of I and the root of V) and should be maintained in the same voice. Common tone retention reduces the total motion needed between chords and helps avoid voice-leading errors such as parallel fifths and octaves.

# Musical Context
Common tone retention is one of the most basic and important voice-leading techniques. It ensures that voices move the minimum necessary distance when changing chords, producing smooth, singable lines. When combined with stepwise motion in the remaining voices, it creates the ideal voice-leading texture.

# Common Confusions
Common tone retention is a guideline, not an absolute rule. Voice exchanges specifically require abandoning common tones for the sake of the exchange. Similarly, when all voices must resolve tendency tones, common tone retention may be impossible. The principle is to maintain common tones when possible, not at the expense of other voice-leading requirements.
