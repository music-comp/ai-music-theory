# Quick Definition
Figured bass is a shorthand notation system that indicates the intervals above bass notes, revealing chord construction, inversion, and melodic motion between chords.

# Formal Definition
Figured bass (or thoroughbass) describes intervals above the bass using numbers listed from largest to smallest. Standard figures: root position = 5/3 (usually omitted); first inversion = 6/3 (abbreviated to 6); second inversion = 6/4 (always written). Conventions: compound intervals are reduced to simple forms; doublings need not be indicated; a dash indicates melodic motion of a single voice (e.g., 5-6 shows a voice moving from a fifth to a sixth above the bass); accidentals attached to numbers indicate chromatic alterations of pitches above the bass; an accidental alone refers to the third above the bass; accidentals in the bass voice are not shown in figures.

# Musical Context
Figured bass originated as a performance practice (c. 1600-1800) where keyboard players realized (filled out) harmonies from a bass line with figures. Today it serves both as an analytical tool and as a component of roman numeral analysis. Combined with roman numerals, figured bass provides a complete description of chordal function (roman numeral = root and quality; figures = inversion and voice leading).

# Common Confusions
Students often think figured bass numbers indicate specific chord voicings. They do not; they show generic intervals above the bass and can be realized in any register. Also, chromatic alterations in figured bass apply only to the immediate pitch above, not through the measure (unlike accidentals in the score). The figures describe intervals, not scale degrees.
