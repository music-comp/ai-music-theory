# Quick Definition
A ritornello is an instrumental interlude that punctuates the formal sections of a da capo aria or other vocal composition, often presenting thematic material that relates to the vocal melody.

# Formal Definition
In Baroque da capo arias, one or more instrumental interludes called ritornellos usually punctuate the formal parts and sections of text. The opening ritornello typically establishes the key and presents melodic material that the voice will later take up or develop. Subsequent ritornellos may separate formal sections, provide transitions between key areas, or mark the return to the tonic. The ritornello principle, in which a recurring instrumental passage alternates with vocal or solo sections, is a foundational organizing principle of Baroque concertos and arias.

# Musical Context
The ritornello is central to Baroque vocal and instrumental music. In arias, it frames the singer's material and provides structural punctuation. In concertos, the ritornello principle governs the alternation of orchestral tutti and solo passages. The concept influenced later formal types, including rondo form.

# Examples
- Bach, "Ich will Dir mein Herze schenken," St. Matthew Passion, BWV 244 (Example 31.7): Instrumental ritornellos punctuate the A and B sections. The opening ritornello establishes G major and presents a sequential progression whose tune relates to the soprano's opening melody.

# Common Confusions
The ritornello is not the same as a refrain. A refrain returns with the same text and music in a vocal context; a ritornello is an instrumental passage that frames or punctuates vocal sections. However, the ritornello principle of alternating recurring and contrasting material is conceptually related to the refrain-episode alternation of rondo form.
