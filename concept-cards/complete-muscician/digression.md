# Quick Definition
The unstable, often fragmented passage at the beginning of the second section of a rounded binary form that leads away from the opening material before the recapitulation.

# Formal Definition
In rounded binary form, the digression is the portion of the second section that precedes the return of opening material. It begins after the double bar and typically features fragmented, unstable material that wanders from the tonic before leading to a structural half cadence (the interruption). The digression may develop motives from the first section, introduce sequences, tonicize non-tonic keys, or present new thematic ideas. It creates the formal tension that makes the recapitulation feel satisfying and necessary.

# Musical Context
The digression is a defining feature of rounded binary form and an ancestor of the development section in sonata form. Its length relative to the first section can create formal balance or deliberate asymmetry.

# Examples
- Haydn, Sonatina no. 4 in F major, Scherzo: the second section's first 8 measures constitute the digression, leading to HC at m. 16 before the recapitulation
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in D major, K. 284, Theme: sequential figuration in mm. 9-12 constitutes the digression

# Common Confusions
- The digression is not random wandering; it is goal-directed, typically leading to a structural HC
- In simple binary form, the second section may not have a digression in the formal sense since there is no thematic return
