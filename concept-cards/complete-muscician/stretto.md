# Quick Definition
A type of overlapping imitation where a new voice enters with the motive before the previous voice has completed its own statement.

# Formal Definition
Stretto is a process in which imitative statements of a motive overlap so that another voice enters with its statement before the previous voice has completed its own. This overlapping creates intensified texture and momentum. Stretto is commonly combined with other techniques such as transposition and sequence to create passages of high contrapuntal density.

# Musical Context
Stretto is an important device in fugal writing, typically reserved for climactic moments or the closing sections of a fugue where maximum contrapuntal density is desired. It also appears in non-fugal contexts throughout the common-practice period.

# Examples
- Bach, G minor Fugue, WTC Book 1: near the end, the subject is stated three times in three voices and three octaves, each entry occurring before the previous is completed

# Common Confusions
- Stretto is a specific type of imitation (overlapping), not a general synonym for imitation
- Not all fugues use stretto; it is an optional intensification technique
- Stretto entries can occur at various intervals and with various transformations
