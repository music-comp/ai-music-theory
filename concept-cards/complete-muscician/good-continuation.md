# Quick Definition
Good continuation is a Gestalt principle stating that listeners expect established musical patterns -- whether of texture, mood, harmonic rhythm, or melodic direction -- to continue.

# Formal Definition
Good continuation is a Gestalt psychological principle applied to music, in which listeners expect an established pattern to persist. This includes patterns in harmonic rhythm, texture, mood, melodic direction, and rate of harmonic change. Composers generally maintain established patterns, and knowledge of this tendency provides a powerful analytical tool. Changes in harmony usually coincide with metric accents, and the established rate of harmonic change guides expectations about when new harmonies will appear.

# Musical Context
Good continuation helps analysts determine harmonic rhythm and predict chord changes. When a pattern of harmonic change is established (e.g., one chord per beat), listeners expect it to continue. Composers exploit this by either maintaining patterns (fulfilling expectations) or breaking them (creating surprise and accent).

# Examples
In Bach's Solo Violin Sonata no. 3 (C major, Allemande), the opening establishes a two-beat harmonic rhythm. This pattern allows analysts to predict chord changes in subsequent measures. A steadily rising chromatic line (C-C#-D-D#-E) is expected to continue to F, and the listener maintains that expectation even across 25 intervening pitches.

# Common Confusions
Good continuation does not mean patterns are never broken -- composers deliberately break patterns for dramatic effect. The principle describes listener expectation, not an ironclad compositional rule.
