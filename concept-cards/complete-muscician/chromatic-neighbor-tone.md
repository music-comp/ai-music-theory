# Quick Definition
A neighbor tone that uses a chromatic (nondiatonic) pitch, creating a half-step motion that is highly dissonant yet expressively beautiful.

# Formal Definition
The chromatic neighbor (CN) includes one or more nondiatonic pitches in the neighbor-tone figure. It can be either accented or unaccented. Chromatic neighbors increase the intensity of the neighbor-tone figure by narrowing the interval to a half step, producing heightened dissonance and expressive color. They appear in both upper and lower neighbor configurations.

# Musical Context
Chromatic neighbors feature prominently in variation sets of the late Classical and Romantic periods, where they provide contrast and drama against the more diatonic original theme. They also appear as double-neighbor figures in accompanimental patterns.

# Examples
- Schubert, Impromptu no. 3 in Bb, D. 935, Variation 2: opens with complete chromatic neighbor D-C#-D, and contains accented incomplete chromatic neighbors
- Schubert, Impromptu no. 3, Variation 3: left hand contains chromatic unaccented neighbors forming a double-neighbor figure

# Common Confusions
- Chromatic neighbors are distinct from diatonic neighbors only in their use of nondiatonic pitches
- They can be unaccented or accented; accented chromatic neighbors are the most dissonant type of neighbor figure
- Do not confuse chromatic neighbors with chromatic passing tones: neighbors return to the original pitch
