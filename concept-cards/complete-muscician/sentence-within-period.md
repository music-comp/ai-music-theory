# Quick Definition
A period whose phrases are cast in sentence structure, either by repeating the sentence in each phrase or by spanning a single sentence across the entire period.

# Formal Definition
There are two common techniques for casting a period in sentence structure. In the first, each phrase of the period is itself a sentence: the antecedent phrase has its own 1:1:2 structure ending with a HC, and the consequent phrase repeats the sentence structure ending with a PAC (a repeated sentence period). In the second technique, the A and A' of the sentence form the antecedent phrase, and the B forms the consequent phrase, so a single sentence structure unfolds over the entire period.

# Musical Context
Combining sentence and period structure allows composers to create formally rich passages that exploit both the rhetorical shape of the sentence and the antecedent-consequent relationship of the period.

# Examples
- Repeated sentence period: each four-measure phrase contains its own 1+1+2 sentence, first ending HC, then PAC
- Single sentence period: mm. 1-4 contain A and A' (antecedent), mm. 5-8 contain B (consequent)

# Common Confusions
- The two techniques produce very different effects: repeated sentence periods sound more symmetrical, while single-sentence periods have a more dramatic arc
- Not all periods with sentence-like proportions are true sentence structures; the 1:1:2 ratio and developmental B section must be present
