# Quick Definition
A consonant skip is a motion by a third in the contrapuntal voice that lands on a pitch consonant with both the preceding melody note and the cantus firmus.

# Formal Definition
In second-species counterpoint, a consonant skip occurs when the contrapuntal voice moves by a third (a skip) to a weak-beat pitch that is consonant with the CF. Consonant leaps (intervals larger than a third) are also possible but must satisfy the same requirement: the destination pitch must be consonant with both the preceding melody note and the CF. Consonant skips and leaps provide melodic variety and balance the stepwise motion of passing tones. They are more effective within measures than between measures, since leaps on accented beats draw disproportionate attention.

# Musical Context
Consonant skips are used to balance the stepwise motion of passing tones, adding melodic interest and allowing the contrapuntal voice to change register. They outline the underlying chord through arpeggiation, foreshadowing the concept of chordal skips in later harmonic contexts.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes forget that consonant skips must be consonant with both the CF and the preceding melody note. A pitch that is consonant with the CF but dissonant with the preceding contrapuntal voice note would create an unacceptable melodic interval.
