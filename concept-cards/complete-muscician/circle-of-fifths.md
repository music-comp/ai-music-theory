# Quick Definition
The circle of fifths is a diagram that arranges all twelve major and minor keys by ascending fifths (adding sharps) or descending fifths (adding flats), showing the systematic relationship between keys and their signatures.

# Formal Definition
The circle of fifths organizes keys so that each successive key clockwise is a perfect fifth higher (adding one sharp), and each successive key counterclockwise is a perfect fifth lower (adding one flat). Starting from C (no accidentals), moving clockwise yields G (1 sharp), D (2 sharps), A (3 sharps), and so on. Moving counterclockwise yields F (1 flat), Bb (2 flats), Eb (3 flats), etc. Enharmonic keys meet at the bottom of the circle (e.g., F#/Gb, B/Cb, C#/Db).

# Musical Context
The circle of fifths is essential for understanding key relationships, transposition, and modulation. Keys adjacent on the circle are closely related (sharing most pitch content) and are common modulation destinations. The circle also governs the order in which sharps and flats are added to key signatures.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes forget that the circle includes both major and minor keys. Each position on the circle represents a major key and its relative minor sharing the same key signature. The circle also shows why certain keys are "close" (adjacent, sharing most pitches) and others are "distant" (far apart, sharing few pitches).
