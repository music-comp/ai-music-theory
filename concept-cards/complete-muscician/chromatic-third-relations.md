# Quick Definition
Chromatic third relations are key relationships in which the tonics lie a major or minor third apart and involve at least one chromatic alteration from the diatonic norm, such as I to bIII, I to bVI, I to III, or I to VI.

# Formal Definition
Chromatic third relations arise when modal mixture expands the available third-related keys beyond the diatonic iii and vi. Through mixture, a major tonic gains access to lowered chromatic thirds (bIII, bVI) in addition to diatonic thirds (iii, vi) and raised chromatic thirds (III, VI). These tonicized keys function identically to their diatonic counterparts: upper thirds lead to ^5 through arpeggiation, and lower thirds lead to ^4 through arpeggiation. The resulting chromatic tonicizations became one of the hallmarks of nineteenth-century music.

# Musical Context
Chromatic third relations became prominent from the late eighteenth century onward. They provide a richer palette of key relationships than the traditional fifth-related modulations of the Classical period. Schubert, in particular, exploited these relations extensively, often moving to bVI or bIII within the first few phrases of a piece. These relations later became foundational to the symmetrical harmonic structures explored in Chapters 35-37.

# Examples
- Schubert, "Der Flug der Zeit" (Example 28.10): A major modulates to C major (bIII) through a modal shift to the parallel minor, demonstrating a typical chromatic third relation.
- Schubert, Sonata in Bb major, D. 960 (Example 28.8): The opening of the sonata famously moves from Bb major to Gb major (bVI), a chromatic third relation that defines the work's tonal character.

# Common Confusions
Not all third-related keys are chromatic. The diatonic mediant (iii) and submediant (vi) are diatonic third relations. Chromatic third relations specifically involve altered roots: bIII (lowered mediant), bVI (lowered submediant), III (raised mediant in minor context used in major), and VI (raised submediant). The type of third (major vs. minor) and the quality of the chord (major vs. minor) together determine the specific relationship.
