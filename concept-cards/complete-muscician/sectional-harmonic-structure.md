# Quick Definition
A harmonic structure in binary form where the first section ends on the tonic, making both sections tonally closed.

# Formal Definition
When the cadence at the end of the first section of a binary form closes on the tonic (PAC or IAC), the harmonic structure is called sectional. Both sections are tonally self-contained. This parallels the terminology used for periods: just as a sectional period has both phrases closing on tonic, a sectional binary form has both sections closing on tonic. Sectional binary forms are less common than continuous forms.

# Musical Context
Sectional binary form appears in shorter binary pieces and some variation themes where tonal closure at the end of the first section is desired.

# Examples
- Hummel, Bagatelle in C major: simple sectional binary
- Haydn, Sonatina no. 4 in F major, Scherzo: rounded sectional binary
- Beethoven, variation theme "Nel cor piu non mi sento": sectional binary (first period closes on tonic)

# Common Confusions
- Sectional does not mean the sections are independent; the second section still provides continuation and development
- The terminology parallels period analysis: sectional = both parts close on tonic
