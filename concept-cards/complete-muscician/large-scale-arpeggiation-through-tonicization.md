# Quick Definition
A technique where successive tonicizations of diatonic harmonies outline the tonic triad at a deeper structural level.

# Formal Definition
When a composer tonicizes scale degrees that collectively spell out the tonic triad (e.g., ^1, ^3, ^5 in minor: i, III, v), the result is a large-scale arpeggiation of the tonic chord through tonicization. Each tonicized harmony occupies a distinct section or phrase, and the succession of temporary tonics creates a deeper-level melodic and harmonic pattern that reinforces the home key even as it departs from it. This technique demonstrates how tonicizations participate in the underlying diatonic structure.

# Musical Context
Large-scale arpeggiations through tonicization are a sophisticated structural technique found in Romantic-era Lieder and instrumental works. They create unity at a deep level while providing surface harmonic variety.

# Examples
- Robert Schumann, "Sangers Trost," op. 127: brief tonicizations of G minor (i), B-flat major (III), and D minor (v) create a large-scale arpeggiation of the G-minor tonic triad

# Common Confusions
- The tonicized harmonies must collectively outline the tonic triad for this to be a true large-scale arpeggiation
- This is an analytical observation about deep structure, not a compositional rule
