# Quick Definition
In species counterpoint, the cadence is the closing gesture where ^2 and ^7 in the penultimate measure converge by contrary stepwise motion to an octave or unison on ^1 in the final measure.

# Formal Definition
The cadence in species counterpoint requires: (1) The final measure must contain ^1 in both voices, forming an octave or unison. (2) The penultimate measure must contain ^2 in one voice and ^7 in the other, moving by step in contrary motion to ^1. (3) In minor, ^7 must be raised to create a leading tone in the penultimate measure; if ^6 precedes ^7, it should also be raised to avoid the augmented second. (4) Use only lowered ^6 and ^7 within the body of the exercise in minor.

# Musical Context
The contrapuntal cadence establishes the prototype for all tonal cadences. The convergence of ^7 (ascending to ^1) and ^2 (descending to ^1) by contrary motion creates the strongest sense of closure. This cadential pattern underlies the authentic cadence (V-I) in harmonic contexts.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes forget to raise ^7 (and ^6 if it precedes ^7) at the cadence in minor. Also, they may attempt to approach the final ^1 by leap rather than by step. The cadence must always involve stepwise motion in contrary motion to the final octave or unison.
