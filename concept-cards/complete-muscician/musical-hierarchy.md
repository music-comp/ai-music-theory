# Quick Definition
Musical hierarchy is the organizing principle in tonal music where some pitches, beats, and harmonies are more structurally important than others, creating layers of significance from foreground detail to background structure.

# Formal Definition
Hierarchy in tonal music means that musical events exist at different levels of structural importance. The tonic pitch is the most important in the pitch hierarchy, supported by the dominant (^5) and other satellite pitches. Less important pitches function as connective tissue between structural pitches. This hierarchy extends to rhythm and meter (strong vs. weak beats) and to harmony (structural chords vs. passing harmonies). Hierarchy is a defining feature of tonal music.

# Musical Context
Hierarchy allows listeners to distinguish between foreground events (surface detail, embellishments) and deeper structural events (cadential arrivals, key areas). Understanding hierarchy is essential for analysis, performance, and composition, as it reveals the difference between what is structurally essential and what is decorative.

# Examples
- In Bach's Violin Partita no. 3, Prelude (Examples 1.22-1.23), E is the most structurally important pitch, with G# and B as secondary important pitches. All remaining pitches function as connective material within melodic gestures.
- Example 1.23A shows how accented pitches on each beat create a slower-moving structural layer beneath the rapid surface.
- Example 1.23B reveals two types of melodic activity: sweeping gestures and stationary gestures, each operating at different hierarchical levels.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes treat all pitches as equally important. Developing the ability to hear and identify structural hierarchy -- distinguishing embellishing pitches from structural arrivals -- is one of the most important skills in music theory. Not every pitch or chord carries the same weight.
