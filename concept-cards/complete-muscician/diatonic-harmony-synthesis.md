# Quick Definition
The complete system of all seven diatonic harmonies (I through vii-diminished) organized by their functional roles within the phrase model, with tonic, dominant, and pre-dominant as structural pillars and other chords in supporting roles.

# Formal Definition
With the addition of the mediant (iii/III) and submediant (vi/VI), all seven diatonic harmonies are available for phrase construction. Despite using chords built on all seven scale degrees, hierarchy remains central: tonic, dominant, and pre-dominant are the most structural chords, while the remaining harmonies play supporting, ornamental roles. This hierarchy is represented through two-level analysis, where first-level roman numerals describe all sonorities and second-level functional labels interpret their roles. The three root motions (descending fifth, ascending second, descending third) govern how diatonic harmonies connect.

# Musical Context
Part 4 of The Complete Musician completes the diatonic harmonic vocabulary. From this point forward, all compositional and analytical work draws on the full spectrum of diatonic chords, organized by the phrase model (T-PD-D-T) and interpreted through contextual, two-level analysis.

# Common Confusions
- Having all seven diatonic chords available does not mean they are all equally important; the three functions (T, PD, D) still govern structure
- The mediant and submediant enrich the harmonic palette but remain subordinate to the tonal pillars
- Contextual analysis is essential: the same chord can have different functions depending on its position and role in the phrase
