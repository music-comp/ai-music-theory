# Quick Definition
Polyphonic (contrapuntal) texture combines two or more independent melodies simultaneously, with no clear distinction between melody and accompaniment.

# Formal Definition
Polyphonic texture, also called contrapuntal texture, involves the combination of two or more melodic lines that maintain their independence while sounding together. Each voice has its own melodic profile, rhythm, and contour. There is no hierarchy between a "main" melody and "supporting" accompaniment; all voices are of relatively equal melodic importance. Polyphonic texture includes everything from simple two-voice counterpoint to complex fugal writing.

# Musical Context
Polyphonic texture is central to the Baroque era, particularly in the works of Bach (fugues, inventions, canons). However, contrapuntal textures appear throughout all periods of tonal music. Even predominantly homophonic textures often contain polyphonic elements in specific passages.

# Examples
- Bach, Fugue in G minor, Well-Tempered Clavier, Book 1 (Example 6.7): three independent voices enter successively, each maintaining melodic independence.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes confuse polyphonic texture with any texture that has multiple voices. The key criterion is independence: in polyphonic texture, each voice has its own melodic identity. In homophonic texture, supporting voices are subordinate to the melody. A Bach chorale, despite having four voices, is primarily homophonic because the three lower voices support the soprano melody.
