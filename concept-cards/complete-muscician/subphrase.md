# Quick Definition
A relatively independent part of a phrase marked by a pause (caesura) and/or by the repetition and variation of short melodic gestures.

# Formal Definition
A subphrase is a relatively independent part of a complete phrase that is marked by a caesura (pause) and/or by the repetition and variation of short melodic gestures. Subphrases do not have their own cadences; only the complete phrase has a cadence. The presence of a caesura does not guarantee subphrases if the harmony reveals a single continuous motion. Conversely, subphrases may exist without caesuras if the motivic structure clearly delineates them.

# Musical Context
Subphrases help divide longer phrases into perceivable segments. Identification and interpretation of subphrases is often subjective and debatable. Phrases containing three or more subphrases are called composite phrases.

# Examples
- Haydn, Symphony no. 100 in G major, "Military": eight-measure phrase divides into three subphrases (2+2+4 measures), each ending with a caesura
- Schubert, Symphony no. 4, "Tragic": eight-measure phrase contains three subphrases (2+2+4) delineated by motivic structure despite nearly continuous movement

# Common Confusions
- A caesura does not automatically create a subphrase; the harmony must also show some degree of independence
- Subphrases are parts of phrases, not independent phrases; they do not have their own cadences
- Identification of subphrases is often interpretive and subjective
