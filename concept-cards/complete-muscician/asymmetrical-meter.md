# Quick Definition
Asymmetrical meters have beat groupings that are not evenly divisible by 2 or 3, creating measures with beats of unequal duration, such as 5/8 (2+3 or 3+2) or 7/4 (2+2+3).

# Formal Definition
Asymmetrical meters are defined either as meters whose number of beats is not divisible by 2 or 3 (e.g., 5/8, 7/4) or as meters that combine groupings of both 2's and 3's (e.g., 10/4 as 2+3+3+2). In moderate-to-fast tempi, these meters are felt as larger beats of unequal duration. Composers may indicate the desired grouping in the meter signature itself (e.g., 3+2+2 for 7/8).

# Musical Context
Asymmetrical meters are common in the folk music of Eastern Europe and the Balkans and appear increasingly in Western art music from the late nineteenth century onward. They create a distinctive rhythmic character due to their uneven beat groupings.

# Examples
- Greek folk song in 7/8 (Example 2.27): felt as a combination of 3/8 + 4/8 (or 3+2+2).
- 5/8 can be conducted in 2, with beats of either 2+3 or 3+2.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes try to force asymmetrical meters into regular simple or compound patterns. The key is to identify the internal grouping of 2's and 3's. A 7/8 measure might be 2+2+3, 2+3+2, or 3+2+2, and each creates a different rhythmic feel.
