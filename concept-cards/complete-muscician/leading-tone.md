# Quick Definition
The leading tone is ^7 when it lies a half step below ^1, creating a strong pull toward the tonic through its inherent melodic tension.

# Formal Definition
The leading tone is the seventh scale degree when it lies a chromatic half step below the tonic. In major keys, ^7 is naturally a leading tone. In minor keys, ^7 must be raised to create a leading tone (as in harmonic and melodic minor). The leading tone is the most important tendency tone in tonal music: it has a strong melodic pull upward to ^1 and should resolve there by step, except when part of the descending line ^1-^7-^6-^5. When ^7 lies a whole step below ^1 (as in natural minor), it is called the subtonic rather than the leading tone.

# Musical Context
The leading tone is essential for creating authentic cadences and for establishing a sense of tonal closure. It appears in the dominant triad (V) and the dominant seventh chord (V7), where its resolution to ^1 is a fundamental voice-leading obligation. In minor keys, the leading tone is created by raising ^7, which is why V in minor requires a chromatic alteration.

# Examples
- In C major, B is the leading tone, lying a half step below C.
- In D minor, C# is the leading tone (raised from the natural C).
- Schubert adds the leading tone C# in the piano accompaniment of "Gute Nacht" (Example 1.10, m. 10) to create closure.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes forget that in minor, the leading tone must be raised with an accidental since key signatures reflect natural minor. The subtonic (lowered ^7, a whole step below ^1) does not function as a leading tone and does not resolve upward to ^1; it typically descends as part of a line moving toward ^5.
