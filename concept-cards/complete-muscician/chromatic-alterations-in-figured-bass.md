# Quick Definition
Chromatic alterations in figured bass are accidentals attached to figured bass numbers to indicate that a pitch above the bass should be sharped, flatted, or naturalized, overriding the key signature for that specific pitch.

# Formal Definition
When a pitch above the bass requires an accidental not present in the key signature, the accidental is attached to the corresponding interval number in the figured bass. If the altered interval is the third above the bass, the number 3 is omitted and only the accidental is written. A sharp (#) raises the pitch; a flat (b) lowers it; a natural sign cancels a previous accidental. The symbols + and / (slash through a number) also indicate raising a pitch by a half step. Chromatic alterations in figured bass apply only to the immediately following pitch; they do not carry through the measure like accidentals in the score. Accidentals in the bass voice itself cannot be shown in figured bass.

# Musical Context
Chromatic alterations are essential for indicating raised leading tones in minor keys, applied (secondary) dominants, and other chromatic harmonies. They represent one of the most practical aspects of figured bass notation, allowing precise specification of non-diatonic pitches.

# Common Confusions
Students often forget that an accidental appearing alone in figured bass (without a number) refers to the third above the bass. Also, unlike accidentals in the score, chromatic alterations in figured bass do not carry through the measure -- each new figure requires its own accidental indication.
