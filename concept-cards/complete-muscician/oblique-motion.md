# Quick Definition
Oblique motion occurs when one voice moves to a new pitch while the other voice sustains its current pitch, creating a combination of melodic activity and stasis.

# Formal Definition
Oblique motion is the fourth type of contrapuntal motion (along with contrary, similar, and parallel). It arises when one voice moves while the other remains stationary. Oblique motion first appears in second-species counterpoint, where the contrapuntal voice moves to a new pitch on the weak beat while the cantus firmus sustains. Oblique motion provides a distinct type of voice independence because the moving voice creates new intervals against the held note.

# Musical Context
Oblique motion is essential in textures where voices move at different rates, which is the norm in most music beyond first-species counterpoint. It is particularly important in pedal point textures and in passages where a sustained bass note supports changing harmonies above.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes overlook oblique motion when analyzing contrapuntal textures, focusing only on contrary, similar, and parallel. Oblique motion is particularly common and should be identified whenever one voice sustains while another moves.
