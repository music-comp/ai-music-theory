# Quick Definition
A cantus firmus (CF) is a pre-existing, fixed melody in even note values that serves as the structural foundation against which a counterpoint voice is composed.

# Formal Definition
The cantus firmus (plural: cantus firmi, meaning "fixed song") is a given melody, typically in whole notes, that functions as the structural pillar in species counterpoint exercises. The newly composed voice written against the CF is called the contrapuntal voice (or counterpoint). The CF can appear in either the upper or lower voice, and the contrapuntal voice is written above or below it. Johann Fux's Gradus ad Parnassum established the use of cantus firmi as the pedagogical basis for teaching counterpoint through a series of five species of increasing complexity.

# Musical Context
The cantus firmus tradition predates Fux, with roots in medieval plainchant. In species counterpoint, the CF provides a controlled environment for learning voice-leading principles. Each species adds complexity to the contrapuntal voice while the CF remains constant, isolating specific aspects of melodic and harmonic interaction.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes think the CF is less important than the counterpoint. In fact, the CF is the structural foundation. The contrapuntal voice must work with the CF, not against it. Both voices must be good melodies individually and produce acceptable intervals when combined.
