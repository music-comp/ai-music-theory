# Quick Definition
An ascending sequence with root motion by ascending fifths and descending fourths, creating a large-scale stepwise ascent, typically leading to the pre-dominant.

# Formal Definition
The A2 (+5/-4) sequence is less common than the D2 because it is less goal-directed; the ascending fifth from V to ii, for example, creates a weak retrogression. However, it is occasionally used because: (1) the overall ascent provides some sense of drive, and (2) the second chord of each pair prevents potential parallels. This sequence typically occurs in root position only and leads to a pre-dominant on ^4 or to ^6 as part of a Phrygian cadence. The soprano usually has a "sawtooth" motion moving in contrary motion with the bass.

# Musical Context
The A2 (+5/-4) is the least common of the four basic diatonic sequence types. Its ascending character makes it useful for building tension, but its retrogressive root motions limit its frequency in common-practice music.

# Examples
- Typically leads to PD on ^4 or to ^6 for a Phrygian cadence

# Common Confusions
- The ascending fifth motion creates retrogressions (e.g., V-ii) which are normally weak progressions; within the sequence, this is acceptable
- Distinguished from A2 (-3/+4) by its larger leaps in the bass
