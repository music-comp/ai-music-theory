# Quick Definition
Borrowed divisions occur when the beat division from one meter type is imported into the other: triplets bring compound division into simple meter; duplets bring simple division into compound meter.

# Formal Definition
Borrowed divisions import the characteristic division of one meter type into the other. Triplets are three-note divisions borrowed from compound meter into simple meter, dividing a simple-meter beat into three equal parts instead of the natural two. Duplets are two-note divisions borrowed from simple meter into compound meter, dividing a compound-meter beat into two equal parts instead of the natural three. Less common borrowed divisions include quintuplets (5), septuplets (7), and other irregular divisions. Borrowed divisions are indicated with a bracket and the appropriate number.

# Musical Context
Borrowed divisions add rhythmic flexibility and expressiveness. They are common throughout tonal music and are especially prominent in nineteenth-century piano music. Two against three (duplets against triplets or vice versa) is a particularly common texture in Romantic piano music.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes notate triplets without the bracket and number, or confuse triplets with dotted rhythms. A triplet divides the beat into three exactly equal parts; a dotted rhythm (e.g., dotted eighth plus sixteenth) divides the beat into unequal parts (3:1 ratio). Also, borrowed divisions are not the same as asymmetrical meters.
