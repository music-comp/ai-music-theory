# Quick Definition
A technique for smoothing the connection between contrasting sections of a piece by retaining and transforming a motivic element across the boundary.

# Formal Definition
Linkage is a compositional technique in which a motivic element is retained across contrasting formal sections but is necessarily transformed to fit into the new musical environment. This creates a smooth transition between sections that might otherwise feel disjunct. Linkage operates at the motivic level, bridging changes in texture, dynamics, and harmonic language.

# Musical Context
Linkage is particularly important at major formal boundaries such as the transition from a development section to a recapitulation, or between contrasting themes. It provides continuity while allowing for the dramatic contrast that formal structure demands.

# Examples
- Beethoven: development section's broken-chord figure is converted to a scalar motive (G-F#-E-D#) that is progressively augmented (sixteenth notes to eighths to quarters to half notes), with imitation at the octave in stretto and falling register, before rushing into the recapitulation
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in F major, K. 332: rising broken-chord figure beginning the development is subtly prepared in the preceding cadential section

# Common Confusions
- Linkage is not a formal section itself; it is a motivic process that spans formal boundaries
- The transformation must be significant enough to fit the new context while retaining enough identity to connect the sections
- Linkage can be subtle and easily overlooked without careful analysis
