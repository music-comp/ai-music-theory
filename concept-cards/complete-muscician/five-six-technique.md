# Quick Definition
The 5-6 technique is a stepwise motion between a fifth and a sixth (or sixth to fifth) above the bass, creating smooth consonant voice leading in counterpoint.

# Formal Definition
The 5-6 technique refers to the stepwise motion from a consonant fifth to a consonant sixth (or vice versa) between two voices. It is the only way to create a consonant step motion in two-voice counterpoint, since only this intervallic combination contains two adjacent consonances. The step motion creates a consonant passing tone that connects the fifth and sixth. The 5-6 technique is fundamental to contrapuntal expansion and becomes increasingly important in harmonic contexts, where it allows smooth voice leading while avoiding parallel fifths.

# Musical Context
The 5-6 technique is one of the most important voice-leading devices in tonal music. It appears in species counterpoint as a method for creating smooth melodic motion using only consonances. In later harmonic contexts, it becomes a primary tool for expanding tonic and dominant harmonies through contrapuntal motion.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes confuse 5-6 motion with a dissonant passing tone. In the 5-6 technique, both intervals (5 and 6) are consonant. A dissonant passing tone, by contrast, creates a dissonance on the weak beat. The 5-6 technique produces consonant passing tones.
