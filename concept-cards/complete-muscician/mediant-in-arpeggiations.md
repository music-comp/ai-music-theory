# Quick Definition
The mediant (iii/III) can substitute for I6 in ascending bass arpeggiations (^1-^3-^5), dividing the fifth between tonic and dominant and extending tonic through a root-position chord on ^3.

# Formal Definition
In ascending-bass arpeggiations, ^3 is most often harmonized by I6, but it can also be harmonized by a root-position mediant chord (iii or III). In this way, iii substitutes for I6 to create a tonic extension, just as vi can substitute for tonic in other contexts. The mediant divides the fifth from ^1 to ^5 in the bass, and its bridging function from T to PD is shown with an arrow in analysis. In minor, iii can combine with vi in a descending arpeggiation (I-vi-iii or I-iii-vi) to create extended harmonic arpeggiations.

# Musical Context
The mediant in arpeggiations provides a root-position alternative to I6, creating a stronger harmonic presence on ^3. The soprano commonly descends stepwise (^1-^7-^6) when iii supports a passing ^7. In minor, III is often preceded by V/III (an applied dominant).

# Examples
- The ascending bass arpeggiation ^1-^3-^5 harmonized as I-iii-V (instead of I-I6-V)
- A combined descending arpeggiation: I-vi-iii-IV connecting tonic to pre-dominant through two descending-third motions

# Common Confusions
- iii and I6 share the same bass note (^3) but are different chords with different root and quality
- In minor, III is often preceded by V/III; this applied chord is not a modulation
- iii is less common than I6 in this position; use it when a root-position sound on ^3 is desired
