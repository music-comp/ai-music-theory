# Quick Definition
A period in which the two phrases have different melodic material (contrasting) and share a single, sweeping harmonic motion across both phrases (continuous).

# Formal Definition
Abbreviated CCP. The antecedent phrase ends with a HC or an IAC but the consequent phrase does not restart on tonic. Instead, it begins on a non-tonic harmony (often a V7 chord extending the dominant, or a pre-dominant), creating a single continuous harmonic progression T-PD-D-T spanning the entire period. The melodic material of the two phrases is dissimilar (labeled a and b).

# Musical Context
The CCP is somewhat common in tonal music. The continuous harmonic sweep across the phrase boundary creates a sense of forward momentum, while the contrasting melodic material provides variety. Composers often use this when the consequent phrase begins with a pre-dominant harmony after a HC that is heard as a back-relating dominant.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata no. 11 in B-flat major, op. 22, Menuetto: first phrase ends HC, second phrase begins on V7, creating single harmonic motion

# Common Confusions
- A continuous period does not restart on tonic at the beginning of the consequent phrase, unlike an interrupted period
- The single harmonic sweep means the second-level analysis shows one overarching T-PD-D-T progression
