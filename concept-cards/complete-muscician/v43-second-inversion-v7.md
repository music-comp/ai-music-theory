# Quick Definition
V43 places ^2 in the bass and functions like viio6 -- primarily as a passing chord between I and I6, or as a neighbor to I. The soprano often moves in parallel tenths with the bass.

# Formal Definition
V43 (second inversion of V7) places the chordal fifth (^2) in the bass. Since ^2 also appears in the bass of viio6, V43 functions in much the same way: as a passing chord between I and I6 (or I6 and I) or as a neighboring chord to I. The soprano often moves in parallel tenths with the bass. A notable exception to normal voice leading: when V43 connects I and I6 with the bass ascending ^1-^2-^3, the chordal seventh (^4, typically F) may ascend to ^5 (G) instead of resolving downward. This exception is permitted because the strong parallel-tenth motion between bass and soprano overrides the normal resolution of the seventh.

# Musical Context
V43 is a versatile contrapuntal chord that adds seventh-chord color to the common viio6 passing function. Its bass note ^2 creates smooth stepwise motion between ^1 and ^3. Schubert's "Des Mullers Blumen" opens with I-V43-I6, the outer voices tracing parallel tenths.

# Common Confusions
The exception allowing the seventh to ascend (when the bass rises ^1-^2-^3 in parallel tenths with the soprano) is unique to V43. Students must not generalize this exception to other V7 inversions. The strong parallel-tenth motion specifically justifies this departure from normal seventh resolution.
