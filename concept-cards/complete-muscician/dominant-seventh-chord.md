# Quick Definition
The dominant seventh chord (V7) is a major-minor seventh chord (Mm7) built on ^5, combining a major triad with a minor seventh to create four tendency tones that powerfully drive toward tonic resolution.

# Formal Definition
The dominant seventh chord (V7) adds the minor seventh above the root of the dominant triad. This creates the most goal-directed harmony in tonal music, containing four tendency tones: ^5 (root, resolving to ^1 in the bass), ^7 (leading tone, ascending to ^1), ^2 (resolving to ^1 or ^3), and ^4 (seventh, descending to ^3). The chordal third (^7) and seventh (^4) form a tritone that resolves by contrary motion: the diminished fifth contracts to a third, and the augmented fourth expands to a sixth. The Mm7 is called the "dominant seventh" because of its ubiquitous association with the dominant function. In C major, V7 = G-B-D-F.

# Musical Context
The Mm7 has been used regularly since the early seventeenth century and remains commonplace in popular music, particularly the blues. V7 participates in authentic cadences but should not be used at half cadences, where the stability of the dominant triad is needed. Its dissonant seventh intensifies the drive to tonic beyond what the dominant triad alone provides.

# Examples
- In C major, V7 = G-B-D-F (root-third-fifth-seventh).
- Example 6.2: the Mm7 built on C showing major triad (C-E-G) and minor seventh (Bb).
- In Beethoven's Fourth Symphony, repeated V7 chords linking the introduction to the Allegro vivace provide a powerful springboard to tonic.
- A complete V7 resolves to an incomplete I (missing the fifth); an incomplete V7 (missing the fifth) resolves to a complete I.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes confuse "dominant seventh" (Mm7, on ^5) with "major seventh" (MM7, which has a major seventh interval). The first word describes the triad quality, the second the seventh quality. Also, students may try to use V7 at a half cadence; use V (triad) at half cadences because V7 is too unstable for a resting point. The leading tone may descend to ^5 in an inner voice to create a complete tonic chord.
