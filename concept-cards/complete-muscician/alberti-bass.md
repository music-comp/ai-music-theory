# Quick Definition
The Alberti bass is a broken-chord accompaniment pattern common in Classical keyboard music, where chord tones are arpeggiated in a low-high-middle-high pattern.

# Formal Definition
The Alberti bass is a specific type of broken-chord accompaniment figure in which the chord members are played in a repeated pattern: lowest note, highest note, middle note, highest note. Named after the Italian composer Domenico Alberti (c. 1710-1740), this pattern distributes a single harmony over time, creating rhythmic interest while the harmony remains static. The listener collects the individual pitches into a single chord, even though they are not sounded simultaneously.

# Musical Context
The Alberti bass is one of the most characteristic accompaniment patterns of the Classical style, appearing throughout the keyboard works of Haydn, Mozart, and early Beethoven. It is an effective way to sustain a harmony with rhythmic vitality while keeping the texture light and allowing the melody to be clearly heard above.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in C major, K. 545, Allegro (Example 6.8C): the left hand plays an Alberti bass pattern while the right hand carries the melody, with only seven chords across four measures.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes try to analyze each note of an Alberti bass as a separate harmonic event. The entire pattern represents a single chord; only when the pattern changes does the harmony change. This is why the harmonic rhythm of a passage with Alberti bass can be much slower than the rhythmic surface suggests.
