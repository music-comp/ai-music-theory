# Quick Definition
The interconnection between large-scale and small-scale motives, where a surface-level motive is contained within or generates a longer structural version of itself.

# Formal Definition
Motivic nesting occurs when motives at different structural levels are interconnected, with the small-scale motive contained within or generating the large-scale version. This creates an organic relationship between the musical surface and its underlying structure. Nesting can involve simultaneous presentation of a motive at different speeds or structural levels, revealing the motive as a fundamental organizing force at multiple hierarchical levels.

# Musical Context
Motivic nesting is a sign of sophisticated compositional craft, particularly in the works of Beethoven, Brahms, and other composers who valued organic unity. Recognition of nesting enriches both analytical understanding and performance interpretation.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Bagatelle, op. 126: right-hand eighth-note figure outlines a double neighbor (E-F-D-E), and the triplet-sixteenth figure is its inversion and transposition (G-F#-A-G); both figures share the same motivic identity despite contrasting surface presentations

# Common Confusions
- Nesting is not the same as simple repetition at different octaves; it involves the motive operating at different structural levels
- Recognition of nesting often requires stripping away surface embellishments to see the underlying contrapuntal framework
- Nested motives may appear in different voices and at different speeds simultaneously
