# Quick Definition
A bass line that ascends stepwise from ^1 to ^5, connecting tonic through pre-dominant to dominant, found in thousands of phrases from the Baroque to popular music.

# Formal Definition
With the addition of pre-dominants, a stepwise bass line can ascend from ^1 to ^5, typically harmonized as I-vii6(or V4/3)-I6-IV(or ii6)-V. This ascending bass creates an especially powerful effect in major-mode pieces, given that the bass of I6 (^3) lies only a half step from the pre-dominant on ^4. This bass pattern appears in countless phrases across style periods.

# Musical Context
The ascending bass from ^1 to ^5 is one of the most common and powerful bass progressions in tonal music. It connects tonic expansion through pre-dominant to dominant in a smooth, goal-directed motion. Previously, stepwise motions between ^1 and ^5 were harmonized in the soprano; pre-dominants now enable this motion in the bass.

# Examples
- Chopin, Nocturne in F# minor, op. 48, no. 2: ascending bass line from ^1 to ^5 through tonic expansion and pre-dominant

# Common Confusions
- The ascending bass is not merely a scale passage; each note is harmonized with a specific functional chord
- The half step between ^3 (I6) and ^4 (PD) in major creates a particularly strong pull toward the pre-dominant
- This bass pattern supports the phrase model: tonic occupies ^1-^3, PD on ^4, D on ^5
