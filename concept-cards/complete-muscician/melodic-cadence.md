# Quick Definition
A melodic cadence is the stepwise approach to the final pitch of a melody, typically ^2-^1 or ^7-^1, creating a sense of melodic closure.

# Formal Definition
The melodic cadence is the concluding gesture of a melody, defined by the stepwise arrival on the final pitch. The two standard melodic cadences approach ^1 from above (^2-^1, a descending step) or from below (^7-^1, an ascending half step via the leading tone). This stepwise approach to closure is a fundamental principle of tonal melody and is reflected at all structural levels.

# Musical Context
Melodic cadences are essential for creating a sense of arrival and completion. They interact with harmonic cadences to create the sense of phrase endings. The requirement for stepwise approach to the final note ensures a smooth, convincing close.

# Examples
- Mozart, Sonata in A minor, K. 310, Presto (Example 3.21): the melody ends with B (^2) resolving to A (^1) in the repetition at m. 16.
- The initial statement of the theme ends on ^2, leaving the listener "hanging" and expecting resolution to ^1.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes confuse melodic cadence with harmonic cadence. A melodic cadence is specifically the stepwise arrival of a melodic line on its final pitch. A harmonic cadence involves the progression of chords (e.g., V-I). In practice, both often occur together, but they are distinct concepts.
