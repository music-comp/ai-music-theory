# Quick Definition
A series of phrases punctuated by weak cadences where none is strong enough to close the passage, distinguishing it from a true period.

# Formal Definition
A phrase group (also called a phrase chain) consists of multiple phrases connected by weak cadences. Unlike a period, a phrase group lacks the antecedent-consequent relationship that requires a final strong cadence to close what was left open. The phrases may share melodic material or present contrasting ideas, but the absence of a conclusive closing cadence means they do not constitute a period structure.

# Musical Context
Phrase groups appear in transitional passages, developmental sections, and passages where composers deliberately avoid formal closure. They create a sense of forward momentum through continuous tonal instability.

# Examples
- Distinguished from asymmetrical periods by the absence of a strong closing cadence

# Common Confusions
- A phrase group is not a period; the distinction depends on whether the final cadence is strong enough to provide closure to the preceding phrases
- Multiphrase passages with new material in each phrase may sound like phrase groups even if they technically qualify as asymmetrical periods
