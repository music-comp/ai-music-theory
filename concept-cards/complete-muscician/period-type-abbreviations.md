# Quick Definition
Standard three-letter abbreviations for the eight period types, combining melodic (P/C), harmonic (I/C/S/P), and form (P) labels.

# Formal Definition
Eight period types are classified by combining melodic and harmonic labels:
- PIP: Parallel Interrupted Period (common)
- CIP: Contrasting Interrupted Period (rare)
- PSP: Parallel Sectional Period (somewhat common)
- CSP: Contrasting Sectional Period (somewhat common)
- PCP: Parallel Continuous Period (rare)
- CCP: Contrasting Continuous Period (somewhat common)
- PPP: Parallel Progressive Period (common)
- CPP: Contrasting Progressive Period (common)

The first letter indicates melodic relationship (P=parallel, C=contrasting). The second letter indicates harmonic type (I=interrupted, C=continuous, S=sectional, P=progressive). The third letter is always P for period.

# Musical Context
These abbreviations provide efficient shorthand for period analysis and are used throughout subsequent chapters when discussing formal structures.

# Examples
- PIP is the most common period type overall
- PPP and CPP are common in pieces that modulate within the period

# Common Confusions
- The second "P" in PPP and CPP stands for "progressive," not "period" (which is the third letter)
- PCP and CIP are the rarest types; their scarcity should raise analytical suspicion if identified
