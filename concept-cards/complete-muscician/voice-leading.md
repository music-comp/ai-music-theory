# Quick Definition
Voice leading refers to the way individual voices move from one chord to the next, governed by principles of smooth motion, voice independence, and proper counterpoint.

# Formal Definition
Voice leading describes how individual voices connect the members of one chord to those of the following chord. The primary principle is to move voices the shortest possible distance when changing chords. Voice-leading rules derive from two-voice counterpoint principles and include: resolving tendency tones by step, retaining common tones, moving upper voices mostly by seconds and thirds, avoiding parallel perfect intervals, maintaining voice independence through proper register and spacing, and following leaps larger than a fourth with a step in the opposite direction.

# Musical Context
Voice leading is the practical discipline of connecting chords in a four-voice texture. Good voice leading produces smooth, singable lines for each voice while maintaining harmonic correctness. Poor voice leading results in awkward leaps, parallel fifths and octaves, voice crossings, and muddled textures. Writing the outer voices first and then filling in alto and tenor is a recommended procedure.

# Common Confusions
Voice leading is not just about avoiding errors -- it is fundamentally about creating smooth, singable lines. A useful diagnostic: if two voices simultaneously leap by more than a third, something may be wrong. Try switching the pitches between those two voices.
