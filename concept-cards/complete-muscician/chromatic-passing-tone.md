# Quick Definition
A passing tone that uses a chromatic pitch to fill the space between two diatonic pitches a major second apart, creating a series of half-step motions.

# Formal Definition
Chromatic passing tones (CPTs) fill the smaller intervallic space between diatonic pitches, most often a major second, creating half-step motions. Less commonly, CPTs fill the space between diatonic pitches a third apart. CPTs occur in both unaccented and accented contexts. Ascending CPTs are more common than descending ones because they create miniature leading tones to the pitch they precede, thereby intensifying melodic direction. CPTs increase in aural prominence as they move along a continuum from unaccented and diatonic to accented and chromatic.

# Musical Context
CPTs add expressive intensity to melodic lines. They are especially prominent in variation sets of the late Classical and Romantic periods, where rhythmic and diatonic regularity is varied for contrast and drama. Composers often indicate their expressive importance through articulation markings such as two-note slurs.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in Bb, K. 570: both unaccented CPTs (Bn4 and C#5 filling Bb4 to D5) and accented CPT (En5 filling Eb to F on strong beat 2)

# Common Confusions
- CPTs fill the space between diatonic pitches, not between chord tones specifically
- Ascending CPTs are more common because they act as miniature leading tones
- An accented CPT is more aurally prominent than an unaccented one, even though both are chromatic
