# Quick Definition
Each scale degree supports a specific type of seventh chord determined by the key, with particular seventh chord types associated with particular scale degrees in both major and minor.

# Formal Definition
In major keys, the most common seventh chords are: I7 (MM7), ii7 (mm7), iii7 (mm7), IV7 (MM7), V7 (Mm7), vi7 (mm7), and vii-half-diminished-7 (dm7). In minor keys (with raised ^7): i7 (mm7), ii-half-diminished-7 (dm7), III7 (MM7), iv7 (mm7), V7 (Mm7), VI7 (MM7), and vii-diminished-7 (dd7). Roman numeral conventions: Mm and MM seventh chords use uppercase; mm, dm, and dd use lowercase. The dd7 adds a degree sign; the dm7 adds a slashed degree sign.

# Musical Context
Knowing which seventh chord type occurs on each scale degree is essential for harmonic analysis. The V7 (Mm7) is by far the most common and important seventh chord in both major and minor keys. The vii7 (half-diminished in major, fully diminished in minor) is the next most frequently encountered.

# Examples
- Example 6.5: the most common seventh chords on each scale degree in major and minor keys.
- In C major: I7 = C-E-G-B (MM7); V7 = G-B-D-F (Mm7); vii-half-dim-7 = B-D-F-A (dm7).
- In C minor: V7 = G-B-D-F (Mm7, requiring raised ^7); vii-dim-7 = B-D-F-Ab (dd7).

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes forget that V7 in minor requires a chromatic alteration (raised ^7) to create the leading tone. Without the leading tone, the chord on ^5 would be a mm7, not a Mm7. Also, the distinction between half-diminished (dm7, with slashed degree sign) and fully diminished (dd7, with degree sign) is important and often confused.
