# Quick Definition
Supermetrical figurations are embellishing figures (passing tones, neighbors) whose durations extend beyond a single beat, spanning multiple beats or measures, always made consonant by supporting harmonies.

# Formal Definition
Supermetrical figurations are tones of figuration whose duration extends beyond the value of the beat, operating at a level above the surface meter. Because they span multiple beats or even measures, they are always made consonant by supporting bass notes and harmonies. These deep-level melodic motions connect structurally important pitches across large spans and reveal the underlying voice-leading structure of a passage.

# Musical Context
Supermetrical figurations reveal the deeper structure of tonal music. They show how a single passing motion or neighbor figure can control an entire phrase or passage. Recognizing them requires locating the entrance and resolution of key pitches (such as the seventh of V7) across chord changes.

# Examples
In Beethoven's "Tempest" Sonata (Example 9.5), the extended upper-voice passing motion D-E-F (^1-^2-^3) spans eight measures. The passing E5 occupies three measures of dominant harmony before resolving to F5 over tonic. The bass D-A-D supports this supermetrical passing motion with the progression i-V-i. In Mozart's Eine Kleine Nachtmusik Trio, similar large-scale passing figures span multiple measures.

# Common Confusions
Supermetrical figurations can be difficult to perceive because they unfold slowly across many surface events. The key to finding them is locating the entrance and resolution of structurally important dissonances (especially the seventh of V7) and tracing the deeper stepwise motion they participate in.
