# Quick Definition
The outer-voice framework is the contrapuntal relationship between the bass and soprano, which controls the harmonic and melodic flow of a passage.

# Formal Definition
The outer-voice framework is the combination of bass and soprano voices that forms the essential contrapuntal skeleton of a musical passage. For every change of harmony (bass note), there is a single primary soprano note. This two-voice framework, placed in a rhythmical-metrical context, is "fleshed out" with complete chords and textural embellishments. The outer voices control the flow of events, and inner voices fill in the harmonic texture. Analysis is conceived as a "triangle" of melody, harmony, and counterpoint between bass and soprano, each dependent on the other two.

# Musical Context
The outer-voice framework is the most important structural relationship in tonal music. Writing the outer voices first is the recommended procedure for part-writing, as their counterpoint controls everything. The bass defines harmony while the soprano carries the melody; together they establish the contrapuntal intervals (thirds, sixths, tenths, fifths, octaves) that govern the passage.

# Examples
In Example 10.3C (Brahms), the outer-voice intervals on downbeats are all active (sixths and tenths), creating forward momentum. In Beethoven's "Tempest" Sonata, the outer-voice counterpoint reveals the structural harmonic progression i-V-i supporting a large-scale melodic ascent ^1-^2-^3.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes focus too much on inner voices or harmonic labels while neglecting the outer-voice framework. The bass-soprano counterpoint is the primary structural relationship. Inner voices support and fill out the texture but do not control the harmonic direction.
