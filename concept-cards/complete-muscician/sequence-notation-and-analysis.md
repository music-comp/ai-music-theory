# Quick Definition
When analyzing sequences, provide only the roman numeral for the first and last chords, plus a sequence label indicating overall motion, specific root motions, and any inversions.

# Formal Definition
Given the contrapuntal derivation of sequences, individual chords within a sequence are not analyzed with separate roman numerals. Instead, the analysis consists of three components: (1) the roman numeral for the first chord in the sequence, (2) the roman numeral for the final chord, and (3) a sequence label that includes the overall motion direction and interval (e.g., D2), the specific root motions between chords (e.g., -5/+4), and any inversions used (e.g., + 6/3s). For sequences with seventh chords, indicate whether the sevenths are alternating or interlocking. Bracket the model and copies in the score and circle the outer-voice pitches.

# Musical Context
This abbreviated analytical method reflects the theoretical understanding that sequences are contrapuntal patterns rather than independent harmonic progressions. It simplifies analysis while capturing the essential structural information.

# Examples
- D2 (-5/+4): overall descending by second, root motions of descending fifth and ascending fourth
- D3 (-4/+2) + 6/3: overall descending by third with first-inversion variants
- A2 (-3/+4) + 6/3: ascending 5-6 sequence

# Common Confusions
- Do not analyze every chord in a sequence with its own roman numeral; only the first and last receive numerals
- Sequential progressions (which lack true melodic replication) do receive full roman numeral analysis
- Bracket the model and copies separately in the score for clarity
