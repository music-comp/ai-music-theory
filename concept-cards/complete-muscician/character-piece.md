# Quick Definition
A character piece is a short, expressive work, often for solo piano, that emerged in the nineteenth century as a vehicle for Romantic expression, frequently cast in ternary form.

# Formal Definition
The character piece is a genre of short, expressive composition, typically for solo piano, that flourished in the Romantic era. Important types include Beethoven's bagatelles, Schubert's impromptus and Moments musicaux, Chopin's ballades, nocturnes, polonaises, and mazurkas, and Schumann's titled cycles (Papillons, Carnival). Character pieces are often in ternary form and frequently employ motivic transformation to link independent sections. Composers such as Liszt, Brahms, Scriabin, and Grieg continued the tradition throughout the nineteenth century.

# Musical Context
Character pieces reflect the intense Romanticism of the nineteenth century, with titles that suggest literary or emotional programs. They replaced earlier forms like the da capo aria and provided a framework for exploring chromatic harmony, motivic development, and tonal ambiguity within compact structures.

# Examples
- Grieg, "Hjemve" ("Homesickness"), Lyric Pieces, op. 57, no. 6 (Example 31.2): A character piece in ternary form contrasting brooding outer sections with a folklike central section.
- Chopin, Mazurka in A minor, op. 17, no. 4 (Example 31.13): A ternary form with nested aba design in the A section, using motivic transformation to connect sections.

# Common Confusions
Not all short piano pieces are character pieces. The term specifically refers to the Romantic tradition of expressive, often titled works that convey a particular mood or character. Baroque dance movements and Classical sonata movements, while short, belong to different formal traditions.
