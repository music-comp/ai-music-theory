# Quick Definition
Range is the total span of pitches a voice can produce (roughly a twelfth), tessitura is the comfortable middle register used in four-voice writing (about a sixth or seventh), and ambitus is the specific range of a particular melody.

# Formal Definition
Range covers the total span of pitches a voice can sing, roughly a twelfth. Tessitura is the "optimal range" -- a more comfortable subset of the range, better suited for four-voice writing, spanning about a sixth or seventh for upper voices. Ambitus refers to the specific range of a given melody, as opposed to the full range of the instrument or voice performing it. The bass register is typically one octave below the alto; the tenor is one octave below the soprano. The bass has a larger effective range than upper voices because of its dual function as melodic line and harmonic foundation.

# Musical Context
Writing for voices or instruments in their comfortable tessitura ensures clarity and blend in four-voice textures. Soprano lines typically have fewer and smaller leaps; tenor and alto lines have even fewer skips; the bass line leaps more freely to fulfill its harmonic role.

# Examples
In four-voice chorale writing, the upper voices should be kept in their midrange. The three upper voices should fit within a single octave for close position. The bass is allowed greater freedom of motion and range due to its harmonic responsibilities.

# Common Confusions
Range and tessitura are not the same. Range is the maximum span; tessitura is the comfortable subset. Writing at the extremes of a voice's range should be saved for solo passages, not four-voice harmonic textures. Ambitus is a property of the melody, not the voice itself.
