# Quick Definition
Tempo is the speed of the beat -- how fast or slow the pulses occur in a piece of music.

# Formal Definition
Tempo refers to the rate at which beats occur in time. It is typically indicated by Italian terms (Allegro, Andante, Adagio, etc.) at the beginning of a piece or section, and may be specified precisely by a metronome marking (e.g., quarter note = 120 BPM). Tempo affects the perceived character of a piece: fast tempi create energy and excitement; slow tempi create gravity and contemplation.

# Musical Context
Tempo interacts with meter and rhythm to determine the overall temporal character of music. The same meter signature can produce very different effects at different tempi. Tempo also affects the perception of beat level: in very fast triple meter, listeners may hear the measure as the beat rather than individual quarter notes.

# Examples
- Common tempo markings range from very slow (Grave, Largo) through moderate (Andante, Moderato) to very fast (Vivace, Presto).
- Beethoven was among the first composers to use metronome markings for precise tempo indications.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes confuse tempo with meter. Tempo is the speed of the beat; meter is the grouping of beats into patterns of strong and weak. A piece in 3/4 can be played at any tempo. Also, tempo markings are approximate and subject to interpretation, while metronome markings are precise.
