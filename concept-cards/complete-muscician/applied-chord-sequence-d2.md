# Quick Definition
A D2 (-5/+4) sequence in which applied dominant chords substitute for diatonic voice-leading chords, intensifying the sequential motion with chromaticism.

# Formal Definition
In the applied-chord version of the D2 (-5/+4) sequence, the diatonic chords that serve as voice-leading chords are replaced by applied dominants (or applied dominant sevenths) of the following chord. This creates a chromatic sequence where each pair consists of an applied dominant resolving to a diatonic triad. Seventh chords may be added in alternating or interlocking patterns. Parallel tritones commonly appear in the upper voices. The sequence follows the same overall D2 root motion but with chromatic intensification.

# Musical Context
Applied-chord D2 sequences are extremely common in Baroque, Classical, and Romantic music. They significantly intensify sequential motion through the addition of leading-tone chromaticism.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata in C# minor, op. 27, no. 1 ("Moonlight"), Trio: D2 (-5/+4) with applied chords, featuring parallel tritones in upper voices
- D2 (-5/+4) with alternating applied seventh chords
- D2 (-5/+4) with interlocking applied seventh chords

# Common Confusions
- The root of each chord must remain diatonic to the key, even though the chords themselves are chromatically altered
- When using interlocking sevenths in applied-chord D2 sequences, every other chord will be incomplete (same as diatonic interlocking sevenths)
