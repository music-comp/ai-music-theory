# Quick Definition
A dominant chord that prolongs the preceding tonic without resolving forward to a following tonic, shown only in second-level analysis as part of the tonic function.

# Formal Definition
A back-relating dominant (BRD) is a V chord that prolongs a previously sounding tonic without resolving to a following tonic. There is no functional connection between the BRD and the chord that follows it (typically a pre-dominant). The BRD is shown only in second-level analysis: first level shows I-V-ii, while second level shows T (with BRD marked) followed by PD. The half cadence is viewed as a common and important type of back-relating dominant, since the music following a half cadence is a restart, not a continuation.

# Musical Context
BRDs resolve an analytical dilemma: when V appears to move to ii (a retrogression from D to PD), the BRD interpretation shows that V actually looks backward to the preceding tonic rather than forward to the following pre-dominant. This interpretation better reflects how we perceive the music -- the ii chord sounds like a fresh pre-dominant, not a continuation of dominant function.

# Examples
- Bach, Prelude in Eb Major, WTC Book 2, BWV 876: V in m. 2 prolongs the preceding tonic; the overall progression is I-ii7-V7-I, with each harmony supporting the soprano descent Bb4-Ab4-G4

# Common Confusions
- A BRD is not a structural dominant; it does not progress forward to tonic or participate in a cadence
- V moving to ii is not a retrogression when V is understood as a BRD; the V is connected backward to T, not forward to PD
- BRDs can only be shown in second-level analysis, where functional groupings are revealed
- The half cadence is essentially a back-relating dominant, since the music afterward restarts rather than continuing
