# Quick Definition
A complete musical work parsed into two sections, typically marked by double bars and repeat signs, also known as two-reprise form.

# Formal Definition
Binary form describes a work in two sections, each usually repeated (hence "two-reprise form"). It is labeled with two components: (1) the thematic design (simple, rounded, or balanced) describing how melodic material is distributed, and (2) the harmonic structure (sectional or continuous) describing whether the first section closes on tonic or away from it. The feature common to all binary forms is the presence of two sections marked by double bars. Binary form can be traced to before 1700 and is the basis for Baroque suite movements, Classical minuets and scherzos, and variation themes.

# Musical Context
Binary form is one of the most fundamental formal structures in Western music. Baroque dances (sarabandes, gigues, minuets), Classical minuets and scherzos, and variation themes are typically cast in binary form.

# Examples
- Hummel, Bagatelle in C major, op. 107: simple sectional binary form
- Weber, Six Ecossaise, no. 1, J. 29: simple continuous binary form
- Haydn, Sonatina no. 4 in F major, Scherzo: rounded sectional binary form
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in D major, K. 284, Theme: rounded continuous binary form

# Common Confusions
- Binary form has a two-part tonal structure but may have a three-part thematic design (in rounded binary)
- The relationship between harmonic structure and thematic design can create formal conflicts that enrich analysis
- Binary form terminology (sectional, continuous) parallels period terminology
