# Quick Definition
Specific voice-leading guidelines govern the writing of mixture chords, including rules about doubling, stepwise resolution, avoiding cross relations, and maintaining chromatic consistency through the dominant.

# Formal Definition
Three principal guidelines govern voice leading for mixture harmonies: (1) Avoid doubling a chromatically altered tone unless it is the root of the chord (as in bVI). (2) Since b^6 functions as a neighbor tone (^5-b^6-^5) or descending passing tone (^6-b^6-^5), prepare and resolve it by step; keeping the chromatic line b^6-^5 in a single voice helps avoid outer-voice cross relations. (3) Once modal mixture is introduced, continue its use until reaching the dominant function, because b^3 and especially b^6 possess such powerful drives to ^2 and ^5 respectively that any intrusion of their diatonic forms would create jarring cross relations and ruin the drive to the dominant.

# Musical Context
These voice-leading rules apply whenever mixture chords appear in tonal contexts. They are extensions of the rules for writing applied chords and reflect the broader principle that chromatic tones create strong linear tendencies that must be respected in voice leading.

# Common Confusions
Students often make the mistake of reverting to the diatonic form of ^6 or ^3 after introducing mixture, creating unintended cross relations. The rule is simple: once you start borrowing from the parallel minor, maintain the borrowed pitches through to the dominant arrival.
