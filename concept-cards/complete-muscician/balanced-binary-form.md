# Quick Definition
A binary form in which the closing material of the first section is restated (transposed to the tonic) as the closing material of the second section.

# Formal Definition
In a balanced binary form, the term "balanced" refers primarily to the harmonic underpinning rather than the repeated melody. Because most binary forms have a first section closing in a non-tonic key, the restatement of closing material in the second section naturally occurs in the tonic to provide convincing tonal closure. Balanced binary can be either simple or rounded. In simple balanced binary, there is no return of the opening material, but the closing material matches. An extensive digression may precede the balanced closing, creating an imbalance in section lengths that the balanced ending helps resolve.

# Musical Context
Balanced binary form provides melodic and harmonic closure by restating the ending of the first section in the home key. It is found in Baroque keyboard and chamber works.

# Examples
- Handel, Allemande: the first section closes in V (D major) at m. 8; after a 13-measure digression with sequences, the closing material from the first section returns in the tonic, creating a balanced continuous binary form

# Common Confusions
- "Balanced" refers to the correspondence of closing material between sections, not to the proportional length of the sections
- A form can be both rounded and balanced if both the opening and closing material return
- The transposition of closing material to tonic is the defining feature
