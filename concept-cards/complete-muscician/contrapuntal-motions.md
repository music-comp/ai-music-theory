# Quick Definition
Contrapuntal motions describe how two voices move relative to each other: contrary (opposite directions), similar (same direction, different intervals), parallel (same direction, same generic interval), and oblique (one voice moves while the other stays).

# Formal Definition
Four types of contrapuntal motion exist: (1) Contrary motion: voices move in opposite directions, creating maximum independence. (2) Similar motion: voices move in the same direction but by different intervals. (3) Parallel motion: voices move in the same direction maintaining the same generic interval size (e.g., parallel thirds or sixths). (4) Oblique motion: one voice moves while the other sustains a pitch (arises in second species and beyond). Contrary motion is preferred for voice independence. Parallel motion is restricted to imperfect consonances (thirds and sixths, maximum three consecutive) to avoid monotony and maintain independence.

# Musical Context
The mixture of contrapuntal motions creates musical variety and balance. Contrary motion is used most frequently because it maximizes voice independence. Parallel motion in thirds and sixths is beautiful but reduces independence. Similar and oblique motion provide additional variety.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes confuse similar and parallel motion. Parallel motion requires the same generic interval to be maintained (e.g., 3-3-3 or 6-6-6); similar motion means same direction but with changing interval sizes. Also, parallel motion with different specific qualities (M3 followed by m3) is still parallel, since only the generic size matters.
