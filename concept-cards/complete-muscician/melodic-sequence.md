# Quick Definition
A repeating melodic pattern that is transposed to successive pitch levels, which may or may not be accompanied by a harmonic sequence.

# Formal Definition
A melodic sequence is the transposition of a melodic pattern to successive pitch levels. While melodic sequences often coincide with harmonic sequences (where both the melody and harmony follow a sequential pattern), a melodic sequence can exist independently of harmonic sequence (as in a sequential progression, where the root motions are sequential but the melodic figuration changes). Melodic sequences are identified by the consistent replication of a melodic figure at different pitch levels.

# Musical Context
Melodic sequences are ubiquitous in tonal music, appearing in instrumental figurations, vocal melodies, and accompaniment patterns. They provide motivic unity and directional motion.

# Examples
- Any figuration within a harmonic sequence that is exactly replicated in each copy constitutes a melodic sequence
- Sequential passages in solo instrumental music (violin, flute) often feature prominent melodic sequences

# Common Confusions
- A melodic sequence without harmonic sequence is a sequential progression, not a true harmonic sequence
- Melodic sequences are a surface phenomenon; harmonic sequences operate at a deeper structural level
