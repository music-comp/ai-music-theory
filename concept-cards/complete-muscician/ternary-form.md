# Quick Definition
Ternary form is a three-part composite form (ABA or ABA') with a three-part tonal structure (original key, contrasting key, original key), in which each section is self-contained with strong melodic contrast and tonal closure.

# Formal Definition
Ternary form features a three-part melodic design (ABA or ABA') and a three-part tonal structure. Unlike rounded binary form, which shares the three-part melodic design but has only a two-part tonal structure, ternary form sections are tonally independent. Three subtypes exist: (1) Full sectional ternary (A||B||A||): both A and B close in their respective tonics. (2) Sectional ternary: either A or B (but not both) closes away from its tonic. (3) Continuous ternary (ABA): both A and B close away from their respective tonics. Ternary and rondo forms are composite forms, constructed of multiple self-contained sections.

# Musical Context
Ternary form appears across many genres and style periods: da capo arias (Baroque), minuet-trio movements (Classical), and character pieces (Romantic). The contrasting B section typically features a different key (major mode: IV, vi, iv, bVI, i; minor mode: III, iv, VI, I), and the return of A provides structural balance.

# Examples
- Grieg, "Hjemve," Lyric Pieces, op. 57, no. 6 (Example 31.2): A full sectional ternary form with a brooding A section in minor contrasted by a folklike B section in the parallel major.
- Brahms, Romanze in F, op. 118, no. 5 (Example 31.3): A sectional ternary form with the B section in the remote key of D major (VI).

# Common Confusions
The main difficulty is distinguishing continuous ternary from rounded binary. Consider how independent the B section is from A: if there is little thematic or motivic connection and a clear change of key, ternary is indicated. If the B section is developmental and motivically connected to A, rounded binary is more appropriate.
