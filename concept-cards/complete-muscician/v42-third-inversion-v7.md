# Quick Definition
V42 places the chordal seventh (^4) in the bass, creating the least stable V7 inversion. It typically passes between I and I6 (often including a voice exchange) or neighbors I6 from above.

# Formal Definition
V42 (third inversion of V7) places the chordal seventh (^4) in the bass. Given the prominent placement of the dissonant seventh, V42 is the least stable of the inverted dominants. It participates in tonic expansions from I to I6, often including a voice exchange. Composers frequently soften V42 by preparing the seventh as a passing tone in the bass (^5-^4-^3, passing from I through V42 to I6). V42 can also function as an upper neighbor to I6, creating a fleeting, less stable prolongation. Placing the passing seventh in the bass at a cadence destabilizes the dominant, transforming it into part of a tonic prolongation rather than a structural cadence, creating an evaded cadence.

# Musical Context
V42 is the most dissonant V7 inversion due to the bass placement of the seventh. Its instability makes it ideal for creating motion within tonic prolongations. When V42 appears where a structural V might be expected (at a cadence), it "evades" the cadence by folding the dominant back into the tonic.

# Common Confusions
Students may treat V42 as a structural dominant. Its bass placement of the dissonant seventh makes it inherently unstable and contrapuntal, not progressional. When V42 appears at a potential cadence point, it evades the cadence rather than completing it. The seventh in the bass must resolve down to ^3, leading to I6.
