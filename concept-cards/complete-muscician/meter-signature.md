# Quick Definition
The meter signature (time signature) is the pair of numbers at the beginning of a piece that indicates the number of beats per measure and the note value assigned to the beat.

# Formal Definition
In simple meters, the top number indicates beats per measure (2, 3, or 4) and the bottom number indicates the beat unit (2 = half note, 4 = quarter note, 8 = eighth note). In compound meters, the top number shows total beat divisions per measure (6, 9, or 12); dividing by 3 gives the number of beats. The bottom number shows the division note value; the beat unit is the dotted version of the next-larger note value. Special symbols include C (common time = 4/4) and the cut-C (cut time/alla breve = 2/2).

# Musical Context
The meter signature appears once at the beginning of a piece, after the key signature. It remains in effect until changed. Performers must interpret the meter signature to understand the beat grouping and division pattern, which in turn affects phrasing, articulation, and emphasis.

# Examples
- 3/4: three quarter-note beats per measure (simple triple)
- 6/8: two dotted-quarter-note beats per measure, each divided into three eighth notes (compound duple)
- 9/8: three dotted-quarter-note beats per measure (compound triple)
- 12/8: four dotted-quarter-note beats per measure (compound quadruple)

# Common Confusions
In compound meters, the numbers do not directly show beats and beat values. A 6/8 signature does not mean "six eighth-note beats" but rather "two dotted-quarter beats, each divided into three eighths." The top number must be divided by 3 to find the actual number of beats.
