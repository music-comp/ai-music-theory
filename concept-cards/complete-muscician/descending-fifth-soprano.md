# Quick Definition
The descending fifth soprano line (^5-^4-^3-^2-^1) is a fundamental melodic pattern harmonized by I-V7-I, made possible by V7's ability to support ^4 in the soprano.

# Formal Definition
With the addition of V7 to the harmonic vocabulary, ^4 can be harmonized in the soprano as the chordal seventh. This enables the complete descending fifth soprano melody from ^5 to ^1: ^5 (harmonized by I), ^4 (harmonized by V7), ^3 (harmonized by I), ^2 (harmonized by V), ^1 (harmonized by I). Since ^3 down to ^1 was already possible, the new element is the descent from ^5 through ^4 to ^3, with ^4 functioning as a passing tone within the V7 chord. The passing motion ^5-^4-^3 in the soprano is often imitated in other voices.

# Musical Context
The descending fifth from ^5 to ^1 is one of the most fundamental melodic paradigms in tonal music. It appears as the basic structure of many phrase endings and complete phrases. Schenker identified it as one of the three fundamental melodic structures (Urlinien) of tonal music.

# Common Confusions
Students may not recognize that ^4 in the soprano is the seventh of V7, not an independent melodic event. It functions as a passing tone between ^5 and ^3, and it must resolve down by step. The descending fifth can unfold at various speeds -- from a few beats to an entire movement.
