# Quick Definition
A Picardy third is a major tonic chord used to close a piece in a minor key, achieved by raising the third of the final tonic triad.

# Formal Definition
The Picardy third is an idiom in which the tonic harmony closing a minor-key composition contains a raised third, transforming it into a major triad. Late-Renaissance and Baroque composers viewed it as a particularly satisfying form of closure. It is one of two common chromatic alterations in minor (the other being the raised ^7 to create a leading tone in the dominant).

# Musical Context
The Picardy third appears primarily at final cadences in minor-key works from the Renaissance through the Baroque period. It was considered more consonant and restful than ending on a minor triad, reflecting the period's aesthetic preference for major-triad finality. The practice declined in the Classical period but occasionally appears in later music.

# Examples
In a piece in C minor, the final chord would be C-E-G (C major) instead of C-Eb-G (C minor). The raised third (E natural instead of Eb) creates the Picardy third effect.

# Common Confusions
The Picardy third applies specifically to the final tonic chord of a minor-key piece (or section), not to any major chord that appears in a minor key. It is a cadential phenomenon, not a general modal mixture technique.
