# Quick Definition
An incomplete neighbor tone that occurs on a metrically stressed beat, leaping from one chord tone and resolving by step to a different chord tone, behaving essentially like an appoggiatura.

# Formal Definition
An accented incomplete neighbor (AIN) enters by leap from a chord tone and resolves by step (up or down) to a different chord tone, occurring on a metrically stressed beat. Unlike a complete neighbor, which returns to its starting pitch, the incomplete neighbor resolves to a different pitch. The accented incomplete neighbor is functionally equivalent to an appoggiatura -- both enter by leap, are accented, and resolve by step. The terms are often used interchangeably.

# Musical Context
Accented incomplete neighbors add expressive intensity through their combination of leap, dissonance, and metrical accent. They are common in variation sets where increasing embellishment provides contrast with a simpler theme.

# Examples
- Schubert, Impromptu no. 3 in Bb, D. 935, Variation 2: G as incomplete upper neighbor leaps down a third from Bb and falls by step to F; A leaps from F and resolves in neighbor fashion to Bb

# Common Confusions
- Accented incomplete neighbors and appoggiaturas are essentially the same figure; the terminology varies by context
- Unlike complete neighbors, incomplete neighbors do not return to their starting pitch
- The leap distinguishes the AIN from an accented passing tone, which moves stepwise throughout
