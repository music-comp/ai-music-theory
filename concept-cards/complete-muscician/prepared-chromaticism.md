# Quick Definition
The practice of preceding a chromatic tone with its diatonic version in the same voice, softening the aural effect of the chromaticism.

# Formal Definition
Prepared chromaticism occurs when a chromatic pitch is preceded by its unaltered diatonic form in the same voice, creating a smooth chromatic line (e.g., G-G#-A). This technique is always preferred in part writing because it minimizes the harshness of chromatic alteration. When preparation in the same voice is impossible (due to voice-leading constraints), the result is a cross relation. Prepared chromaticism is particularly important when writing applied chords, where the temporary leading tone ideally follows its diatonic predecessor in the same voice.

# Musical Context
Prepared chromaticism is a fundamental principle of good chromatic voice leading throughout the common-practice period. It ensures that chromatic tones sound like natural inflections of diatonic pitches rather than jarring intrusions.

# Examples
- Soprano line G-G#-A: the chromatic passing tone G# is prepared by the diatonic G in the same voice
- When writing V7/vi in C major, ideally the voice containing G-natural will move to G-sharp

# Common Confusions
- Preparation must occur in the same voice to avoid a cross relation
- Not always possible to prepare chromaticism; in such cases, minimize the effect by placing the cross relation between less prominent voices
