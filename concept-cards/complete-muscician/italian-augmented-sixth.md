# Quick Definition
The Italian augmented sixth chord (It6) is the simplest augmented sixth chord, containing only three distinct pitch classes: b^6 in the bass, #^4 in an upper voice, and ^1 doubled.

# Formal Definition
The Italian augmented sixth chord (It6) contains b^6, ^1 (doubled), and #^4. It is the most basic form of the augmented sixth chord, with only three distinct pitch classes. Because of the doubled ^1, always double the third above the bass. The It6 resolves directly to V (unlike the Ger6/5, which typically moves first to a cadential 6/4). It sounds like an incomplete V7 chord (without the fifth), which is the basis for its enharmonic equivalence with dominant seventh chords used in modulations.

# Musical Context
The Italian sixth is the least commonly used of the three augmented sixth varieties but provides the cleanest resolution directly to V. Its sparse, three-note sonority (with one doubled pitch) gives it a distinctive open quality compared to the fuller German and French sixths.

# Common Confusions
The It6 sounds like an incomplete dominant seventh chord. This aural similarity is the basis for enharmonic reinterpretation but should not lead to confusing function: the It6 is a pre-dominant moving to V, while a V7 is a dominant moving to I. Context (resolution and voice leading) determines which interpretation applies.
