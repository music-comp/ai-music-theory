# Quick Definition
The return of opening material in the tonic key after the digression in a rounded binary form, completing the cyclical thematic design.

# Formal Definition
In rounded binary form, the recapitulation is the restatement of all or part of the opening material (A) from the first section, now presented in the tonic key, after the digression has led to a structural half cadence (interruption). The recapitulation provides both melodic return (satisfying thematic expectations) and harmonic closure (completing the tonal journey back to tonic). It may restate the opening material exactly, with slight modifications, or in an abbreviated form. The recapitulation creates the "rounding" that gives rounded binary its name.

# Musical Context
The recapitulation is the defining feature that distinguishes rounded binary from simple binary form. It is also the conceptual ancestor of the recapitulation in sonata form.

# Examples
- Haydn, Sonatina no. 4 in F major, Scherzo: literal restatement of opening material at m. 17 after digression and interruption at m. 16
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in D major, K. 284, Theme: partial restatement of opening theme beginning at m. 14

# Common Confusions
- The recapitulation may not restate the entire first section; sometimes only part of the opening material returns
- The recapitulation must be in the tonic key; a return of melodic material in a non-tonic key does not constitute a true recapitulation
