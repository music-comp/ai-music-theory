# Quick Definition
The pre-dominant is the third harmonic function in tonal music, occurring between tonic and dominant to intensify harmonic drive toward the cadence.

# Formal Definition
Pre-dominant (PD) chords are independent sonorities that provide connective tissue between the tonal pillars of tonic and dominant. Unlike contrapuntal chords that merely embellish, pre-dominants are functional harmonies that usually precede the dominant. The most important pre-dominant chords are IV (subdominant) and ii (supertonic). At the first analytical level, use appropriate roman numerals and figured bass; at the second level, use the symbol PD.

# Musical Context
Pre-dominants appear in virtually every tonal phrase that follows the phrase model (T-PD-D-T). They create an intensified harmonic drive that propels the music from the relative stability of tonic toward the tension of dominant. Pre-dominants are found consistently from the Baroque era through late Romantic music and into popular music.

# Examples
- Meyerbeer, "Se non ti moro allato," Sei canzonette italiane, no. 5: IV functioning as pre-dominant
- Foster, "Beautiful Dreamer": ii as pre-dominant
- Verdi, Overture to La forza del destino: pre-dominant usage in orchestral context

# Common Confusions
- Pre-dominants are not contrapuntal embellishing chords; they are independent functional harmonies
- The T-PD-D-T order is not reversible: PD must follow T and precede D
- Pre-dominants should not be confused with passing chords that happen to use ii or IV
