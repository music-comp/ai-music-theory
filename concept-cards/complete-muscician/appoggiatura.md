# Quick Definition
An accented, unprepared dissonance that enters by leap and resolves by step, usually in the direction opposite its leap to balance the melodic contour.

# Formal Definition
The appoggiatura (APP) is an important and striking type of figuration. Unlike passing tones and complete neighbor tones (which are flanked by chord tones), appoggiaturas enter by leap and are both dissonant and accented. They resolve by step to a chord tone, usually in the direction opposite their leap. The appoggiatura behaves like an accented incomplete neighbor. The most common intervallic structures are 4-3 and 9-8 against the bass.

# Musical Context
Appoggiaturas are among the most emotive elements in tonal music. They create intense expressive moments because of the combined effect of leap, dissonance, and metrical accent. Notable exceptions to the "resolve in opposite direction" rule exist, such as the "Maria" motive from Bernstein's West Side Story.

# Examples
- Bernstein, West Side Story, "Maria": the appoggiatura resolves in the same direction as its leap, an important exception to the general rule

# Common Confusions
- Appoggiaturas enter by leap; suspensions enter by common tone (tie or sustained pitch)
- Appoggiaturas are unprepared; suspensions require preparation on a weak beat
- Accented incomplete neighbors and appoggiaturas are essentially the same figure and may be referred to interchangeably
- Do not confuse with the Baroque-era ornamental "grace note" appoggiatura
