# Quick Definition
An agogic accent occurs when a pitch is emphasized by having a longer duration than surrounding notes, drawing the listener's attention through its sustained presence.

# Formal Definition
An agogic accent is a durational accent created when a note lasts longer than surrounding notes, causing it to stand out perceptually. Agogic accents are particularly powerful when they align with metric accents (occurring on strong beats), but they can also create tension when they conflict with the meter. When accented duration and metric placement align, the listener perceives a particularly strong emphasis that helps group musical events into intelligible units.

# Musical Context
Agogic accents are a primary tool for creating emphasis in melody and for establishing phrase structure. They frequently mark important structural pitches and cadential arrivals. In Bach's music, agogic accents often coincide with harmonically significant events.

# Examples
- In Bach's Violin Partita no. 3, Prelude (Example 1.22), pitches that occupy more time than surrounding sixteenth notes create agogic accents that help identify the tonic E as the most important pitch.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes confuse agogic accents with dynamic accents. Agogic accents come from duration (length), not loudness. A note can be soft yet still accented if it is longer than its neighbors.
