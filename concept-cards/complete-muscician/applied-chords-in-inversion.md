# Quick Definition
Applied dominant chords placed in first, second, or third inversion to create smoother bass lines and prolong diatonic harmonies, just as inversions of V prolong I.

# Formal Definition
Inversions of applied chords serve the same functions as inversions of V7 in relation to I. Any inversion of V7/X can expand harmony X in the same way that V6, V6/5, V4/3, and V4/2 expand the tonic. Root-position applied chords create angular bass lines; inversions smooth these out. No matter how extensively a non-tonic harmony is expanded by applied chords in inversion, that harmony still functions within the phrase model. Applied chords in inversion frequently serve as neighboring or passing chords within the expanded harmony.

# Musical Context
Inversions of applied chords appear frequently in common-practice music to create chromatic passing tones and neighbor tones in the bass. They allow extended tonicizations of diatonic harmonies without disrupting the overall contrapuntal flow.

# Examples
- V6/5/ii as a passing chord between ii and ii6
- V4/3/iv creating a chromatic passing bass between iv and iv6
- vii-7/ii and its inversions expanding the supertonic over multiple beats

# Common Confusions
- The inversion labels for applied chords follow the same figured-bass conventions as regular seventh chords
- Even extensive expansion of a non-tonic harmony through applied inversions does not change that harmony's function in the phrase model
