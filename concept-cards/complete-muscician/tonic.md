# Quick Definition
The tonic is the central, most stable pitch in a key, serving as the point of rest and the gravitational center toward which all other pitches are drawn.

# Formal Definition
The tonic (^1) is the first scale degree and the primary tonal pillar in tonal music. It is the pitch around which the entire tonal hierarchy is organized. The tonic is determined by its prominence through factors including frequency of occurrence, metric placement, duration, and its function as a point of arrival and stability. In any key, the tonic gives its name to the key (e.g., D is the tonic of both D major and D minor).

# Musical Context
The tonic typically opens and closes musical phrases, sections, and entire compositions. It serves as the "home base" from which musical journeys depart and to which they return. The relationship between the tonic and other scale degrees creates the tension-resolution dynamic that drives tonal music.

# Examples
- In Bach's Violin Partita no. 3, Prelude (Example 1.22), E is the tonic, established through metric prominence, frequent occurrence after bar lines, and its role as a focal point of several types of musical accent.
- The tonic pitch E in the Bach example is supported by satellites G# and B, which form the tonic triad.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes confuse the tonic pitch with the tonic triad. The tonic pitch is ^1 alone; the tonic triad is the chord built on ^1 (I or i). Both function as points of stability, but they operate at different levels of musical structure.
