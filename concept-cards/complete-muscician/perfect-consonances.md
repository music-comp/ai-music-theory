# Quick Definition
Perfect consonances (P1, P5, P8) are the most stable intervals, characterized by a pure, open sound that conveys a sense of rest and finality.

# Formal Definition
Perfect consonances include the perfect unison (P1), perfect fifth (P5), and perfect octave (P8). They are the most stable class of intervals and provide the structural framework at beginnings and endings of phrases in counterpoint. In species counterpoint, perfect consonances are used sparingly within the exercise (to avoid monotony) but are required at the beginning (P1, P5, or P8) and end (P8 or P1). Perfect consonances must be approached by contrary motion to avoid parallel and direct intervals. Their hollow, open sound makes them ideal for structural points but less suitable for creating forward momentum.

# Musical Context
Perfect consonances form the pillars of tonal structure. The octave defines the boundaries of the scale. The fifth is the most important interval after the octave, defining the relationship between tonic and dominant. Perfect consonances are the foundation of just intonation and were the first intervals recognized as consonant in Western music history.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes include the perfect fourth (P4) among the perfect consonances. While the P4 is "perfect" in name and in interval quality, it is treated as a dissonance in two-voice counterpoint and when formed with the bass. It belongs with the dissonant intervals for practical purposes in counterpoint.
