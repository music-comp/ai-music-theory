# Quick Definition
A repeated musical idea that forms the foundation for continuous variations, typically appearing in the lower voices.

# Formal Definition
An ostinato is a persistently repeated musical pattern that provides the backbone for a set of continuous variations. Ostinati generally appear in the lower voices and take three forms: (1) ground bass -- a repeating bass line pattern; (2) chaconne -- a repeating harmonic pattern; (3) passacaglia -- both a repeating bass line and harmonic pattern. In practice, these three terms are often used interchangeably because composers typically pair repeating harmonies with repeating bass lines. Over the ostinato, changes in register, texture, and motivic design create the variations.

# Musical Context
Ostinato-based compositions span the entire common-practice period and beyond, from Renaissance grounds to Baroque chaconnes to twentieth-century works. The ostinato provides stability while surface activity creates variety.

# Examples
- Monteverdi, "Lamento della ninfa": descending tetrachord ground bass (a common ostinato pattern)
- Purcell, Ground in G: eight-measure ground bass with consistent harmonic pattern
- Bach, Chaconne: repeating harmonic pattern beginning in mid-measure for continuity

# Common Confusions
- Ground bass, chaconne, and passacaglia are often used interchangeably; the distinction between repeating bass and repeating harmony is frequently artificial
- The ostinato may be obscured or varied in later variations while maintaining its structural function
