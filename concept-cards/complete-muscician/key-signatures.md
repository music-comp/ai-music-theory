# Quick Definition
A key signature is the set of sharps or flats placed at the beginning of a staff that indicates which pitches are consistently altered throughout a piece, thereby establishing the key.

# Formal Definition
Key signatures represent the accidentals required to produce a major or natural minor scale from any tonic. Sharps are added in the order F-C-G-D-A-E-B (ascending fifths), and flats in the reverse order B-E-A-D-G-C-F (descending fifths). Each key signature corresponds to one major key and its relative minor. Keys range from zero accidentals (C major / A minor) to seven sharps or flats.

# Musical Context
Key signatures appear once at the beginning of a piece and apply throughout unless changed. They do not indicate whether the music is in the major or relative minor key; that determination requires examining the music itself, particularly the presence of raised ^7 (leading tone) in minor. Minor keys use their natural minor key signature, with raised ^6 and ^7 appearing as accidentals.

# Common Confusions
A key signature alone does not determine whether a piece is in major or minor mode. Two sharps could indicate D major or B minor. Look for raised ^7 as an accidental (A# in B minor) to identify the minor mode. Also, not all accidentals in a piece come from the key signature; chromatic alterations appear as individual accidentals.
