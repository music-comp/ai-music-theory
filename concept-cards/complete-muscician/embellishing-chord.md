# Quick Definition
A root-position IV chord that prolongs the tonic through neighbor motion rather than functioning as a pre-dominant, distinct from both the pedal six-four and the pre-dominant IV.

# Formal Definition
An embellishing chord (EC) occurs when IV appears in root position within a tonic prolongation, with its bass leap rendering the upper voices (which would otherwise be neighbor notes in a pedal six-four) temporarily consonant. The chord is a stable root-position triad on IV, yet tonic still controls the passage. The subdominant can function as either a pre-dominant (moving to V) or an embellishing chord (prolonging I); context determines which interpretation is correct.

# Musical Context
Embellishing IV chords appear when the bass moves to the subdominant scale degree but the overall harmonic context remains within a tonic prolongation. Multiple statements of IV may appear in a single passage, some functioning as pre-dominants and others as embellishing chords.

# Examples
- Mozart, Symphony no. 39 in Eb major, K. 543, Menuetto: IV appears as embellishing chord in m. 2 (prolonging I through neighbor motion) and as pre-dominant in mm. 3-4 (leading to V)

# Common Confusions
- Not every IV is a pre-dominant; context determines whether IV leads to V (pre-dominant) or returns to I (embellishing)
- The embellishing IV is similar to a pedal six-four but uses a root-position triad instead of a second-inversion chord
- At the second level, the embellishing IV falls under "T" (tonic), not "PD"
