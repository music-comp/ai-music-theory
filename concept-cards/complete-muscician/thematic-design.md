# Quick Definition
The melodic structure of a binary form, classified as simple (no thematic return), rounded (opening material returns after digression), or balanced (closing material returns transposed to tonic).

# Formal Definition
Thematic design describes how melodic material is distributed across the two sections of a binary form. Three types: (1) Simple: no thematic material from the first section recurs in the second section. (2) Rounded: all or part of the opening material returns in the tonic key after a digression in the second section, creating a three-part melodic design (A-digression-A') within the two-part harmonic structure. (3) Balanced: the closing material of the first section is restated (transposed to tonic) at the end of the second section. A form can be both rounded and balanced.

# Musical Context
Identifying the thematic design is the first step in classifying a binary form. Combined with the harmonic structure (sectional or continuous), it produces the complete binary form label (e.g., "rounded continuous binary form").

# Examples
- Simple: no melodic material returns in the second section
- Rounded: opening theme A returns as A' after digression
- Balanced: closing material from first section appears in tonic at the end

# Common Confusions
- "Rounded" refers to the cyclical effect of the return, not to the shape of the music
- A form can be both rounded and balanced simultaneously
- Simple binary may still develop motives from the first section without constituting a "return"
