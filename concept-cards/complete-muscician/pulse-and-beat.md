# Quick Definition
Pulses are undifferentiated, equally spaced clicks or taps; beats are pulses within a musical context where some feel stronger than others.

# Formal Definition
A pulse is a regular, recurring temporal event without differentiation in quality, loudness, or length. When pulses occur within a musical context that creates patterns of strong and weak emphasis, they become beats. The tempo is the speed of the beat. Beats are the fundamental unit of musical time, serving as the reference point for rhythm and meter.

# Musical Context
Beats are what listeners tap their feet to. The beat level is the primary temporal reference against which rhythmic patterns are measured. Faster note values divide the beat, while slower values group beats together. The beat is the middle level of a three-tiered temporal hierarchy: subdivision (below), beat (middle), and meter (above).

# Examples
- When you nod your head steadily to music, each nod corresponds to a beat.
- Durational symbols representing common beat values: whole note, half note, quarter note, eighth note.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes confuse the beat with the smallest note value they hear. The beat is the primary pulse level, not necessarily the fastest. In 4/4, the quarter note is typically the beat even though eighth and sixteenth notes may be present. The beat is what you naturally tap along with.
