# Quick Definition
The first tonal area (FTA) presents material in the tonic key, and the second tonal area (STA) presents material in a contrasting key (usually V in major, III in minor); both appear in the exposition and recapitulation.

# Formal Definition
The exposition and recapitulation of a sonata form are each divided into two harmonic sections. The first tonal area (FTA) presents material in the tonic key; in the recapitulation, it returns in the tonic. The second tonal area (STA) presents material originally in the contrasting key (V in major, III in minor) in the exposition; in the recapitulation, it returns in the tonic, fulfilling the sonata principle. Themes are labeled by tonal area and order: 1a, 1b, 1c for FTA themes; 2a, 2b for STA themes. The FTA depends on rounded-binary characteristics (returning after a digression), while the STA depends on balanced-binary characteristics (material at the end of the first section returns at the end of the piece in the tonic).

# Musical Context
The FTA and STA are defined by harmonic function, not by theme. In monothematic sonata forms (common in Haydn), the same theme appears in both tonal areas. The labeling system (1a, 2a, etc.) avoids confusion between "first theme" and "second theme" terminology, which incorrectly implies that themes define these areas.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata no. 19 in G minor, op. 49, no. 1 (Example 33.2): FTA in G minor (mm. 1-8), STA in Bb major (III) beginning at m. 16.
- Haydn, Piano Sonata no. 48 in C major, Hob. XVI.35 (Example 33.9): FTA in C major (mm. 1-19), STA in G major (V) at m. 36.

# Common Confusions
The FTA and STA are not defined by theme but by key. A common error is labeling any new theme as the beginning of the STA; the STA begins when the new key is established, which may or may not coincide with new thematic material. In monothematic sonata forms, the same theme serves both areas.
