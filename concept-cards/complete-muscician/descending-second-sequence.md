# Quick Definition
A sequence in which each copy of the model descends by step, with root motion alternating between descending fifths and ascending fourths (or vice versa).

# Formal Definition
The D2 (-5/+4) sequence, also called the descending-fifth sequence, follows the circle of fifths: I-IV-vii-iii-vi-ii-V-I. The root motion from chord to chord alternates between descending fifth (-5) and ascending fourth (+4), creating a deeper-level descent by second. Range limitations prevent using only descending fifths, so ascending fourths compensate. The major-mode D2 contains a melodic tritone in the bass near the beginning; in minor, the tritone occurs near the end. The first-inversion variant, D2 (-5/+4) + 6/3, creates a smoother, more melodic bass with outer-voice counterpoint consisting exclusively of imperfect consonances (10-6).

# Musical Context
The D2 (-5/+4) is the most common of all sequence types. It can prolong tonic function or transition from tonic to pre-dominant. A complete diatonic cycle traverses all seven diatonic chords before returning to tonic.

# Examples
- Complete diatonic circle of fifths in C major: C-F-B-E-A-D-G-C (I-IV-vii-iii-vi-ii-V-I)
- Bach, Chaconne from Partita no. 2 in D minor: D2 (-5/+4) sequence maintaining pattern through entire circle of fifths

# Common Confusions
- The bass tritone leap within the sequence is acceptable and necessary to keep pitches within the key
- Root-position diminished triads (vii in major, ii in minor) are permitted within this sequence
- When analyzing, provide only the first and last roman numerals plus the sequence label
