# Quick Definition
A chromatic step-descent bass is a fully chromaticized descending bass line from ^1 to ^5, made possible by importing b^6 and b^7 from the parallel minor into the major mode.

# Formal Definition
In minor keys, the two diatonic forms of ^6 and ^7 already allow a completely chromatic descending bass (^1-^7-b^7-^6-b^6-^5). Through modal mixture, this powerful progression can be imported into major-mode pieces. Common settings include: (1) a direct descent to V, and (2) an indirect descent to PD on ^4, followed by the dominant. The descent often includes passing chords such as minor v6 leading to the pre-dominant, which may be expanded through a chromatic voice exchange.

# Musical Context
Chromatic step-descent basses are a hallmark of expressive writing from the Baroque through the Romantic era. Geminiani demonstrated the technique in minor (Example 27.10A), and the importation of this progression into major keys through mixture greatly expanded its expressive range.

- Example 27.10C: An indirect descent to PD on ^4, with a passing minor v6 leading to the pre-dominant, expanded through chromatic voice exchange.

# Common Confusions
The chromatic step-descent bass is distinct from a simple chromatic bass line. It is a specific harmonic technique that connects tonic to dominant through a fully chromaticized descending path, with each chromatic pitch harmonized as part of a functional chord or passing sonority.
