# Quick Definition
The process by which chromatic pitches replace diatonic pitches, creating applied dominant chords and other chromatic harmonies that intensify motion toward diatonic goals.

# Formal Definition
Chromatic alteration occurs when a diatonic pitch is replaced by its chromatic counterpart in order to create a leading-tone relationship to a target chord. A chromatically raised note typically functions as the third of an applied dominant chord (temporary leading tone), while a chromatically lowered note typically functions as the seventh of an applied dominant chord. Chromatic alteration is the mechanism by which diatonic triads are transformed into applied dominants that point toward specific diatonic targets.

# Musical Context
Chromatic alteration is the foundation of applied-chord chromaticism. It extends the principle of the raised ^7 in minor (which gives V its dominant function) to other scale degrees, allowing any major or minor diatonic triad to be preceded by its own dominant.

# Examples
- In C major, altering the diatonic D-minor chord (ii) by raising F to F-sharp transforms it into a D-major chord that functions as V/V
- A chromatically raised note signals its function as a temporary leading tone; a chromatically lowered note signals the seventh of an applied dominant

# Common Confusions
- Chromatic alteration creates tendency tones that must resolve: raised notes resolve up, lowered notes resolve down
- Not all chromaticism involves chromatic alteration; chromatic passing tones may be unharmonized
