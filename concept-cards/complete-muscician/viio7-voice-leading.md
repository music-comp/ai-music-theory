# Quick Definition
All four members of viio7 are tendency tones requiring careful resolution: ^7 rises to ^1, ^6 falls to ^5, ^4 falls to ^3, and ^2 usually rises to ^3 (creating a doubled third in tonic).

# Formal Definition
Since all notes in viio7 are tendency tones, resolution follows strict guidelines: ^6 (the chordal seventh) must be prepared by step or common tone and resolved down to ^5. ^4 moves down to ^3. ^2 usually moves up to ^3 (resulting in the common doubled third in the tonic chord), or it can move down to ^1. ^7 must move up to ^1. The diminished seventh (or augmented second) between ^7 and ^6 resolves by contrary motion to ^1 and ^5. Tritones resolve normally: diminished fifths contract, augmented fourths expand. Resolution to tonic chords with doubled thirds is common and acceptable.

# Musical Context
The strict resolution requirements of viio7 reflect its extreme instability. Since the chord shares no common tones with tonic, every voice must move when resolving. The approach to ^6 (the unique member distinguishing viio7 from V7) requires preparation -- typically by step from ^5 -- which is why viio7 functions best as an embellishing chord where the voice leading can be carefully controlled.

# Common Confusions
The doubled third in the tonic resolution is normal and expected for viio7 -- students should not try to avoid it. Also, ^6 must be both prepared (approached by step from ^5 or common tone) and resolved (down to ^5). Students who substitute viio7 for V7 must remember to replace ^5 with ^6 and follow the specific voice-leading protocols for that substitution.
