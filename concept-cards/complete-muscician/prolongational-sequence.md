# Quick Definition
A sequence that expands a single harmonic function, such as tonic prolongation (T-T), without changing the underlying functional area.

# Formal Definition
A prolongational sequence expands one harmonic function within the phrase model. For example, a D2 (-5/+4) sequence beginning on I and returning to I prolongs the tonic function. The sequence serves to embellish and extend the duration of the function without transitioning to a new one. Prolongational sequences typically occur after a tonic expansion and serve to delay the arrival of the pre-dominant and dominant functions.

# Musical Context
Prolongational sequences are common in phrases where composers wish to extend the tonic area before moving to the cadential progression. They create a sense of harmonic motion within stasis.

# Examples
- A D2 (-5/+4) + 6/3 sequence that begins on I and returns to I, with the entire sequence prolonging tonic function before PD-D-T cadential motion

# Common Confusions
- The distinction between prolongational and transitional sequences can be blurred; changing the rhythm of a transitional sequence can make it sound prolongational
- A sequence can prolong tonic even though it passes through many different chords
