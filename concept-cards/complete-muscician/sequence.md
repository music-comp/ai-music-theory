# Quick Definition
A sequence is the immediate restatement of a musical idea on different scale degrees, transposed up or down by a consistent interval.

# Formal Definition
A sequence is the immediate restatement of a musical idea on different scale degrees. This transposition preserves the intervallic and rhythmic pattern of the original idea while shifting it to a new pitch level. Sequences are a fundamental compositional technique for extending and developing melodic material.

# Musical Context
Sequences appear throughout common-practice music as a means of generating forward motion and developing musical ideas. They can operate at the surface level (melodic sequences) or at deeper structural levels (harmonic sequences). They are one of the primary means of prolongation and continuation in tonal music.

# Examples
In "God Save the King," mm. 9-10 imitate mm. 7-8 by repeating the material down the interval of a second. The passing tone Bb connects C5 to A4 in m. 8, and this pattern is restated a step lower in mm. 9-10.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes confuse sequence (transposition of a pattern to a new pitch level) with simple repetition (restatement at the same pitch level). A sequence specifically involves a change of pitch level while preserving the intervallic pattern.
