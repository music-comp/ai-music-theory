# Quick Definition
Keyboard style places three upper voices (soprano, alto, tenor) in the right hand within one octave and the bass in the left hand; chorale style places soprano and alto in the treble clef and tenor and bass in the bass clef.

# Formal Definition
Keyboard style is a four-voice texture where the right hand plays three voices (soprano, alto, tenor) within the span of one octave, while the left hand plays the bass alone. This hand position emphasizes the outer voices and is the most common texture used in keyboard harmony exercises. Chorale style distributes the four voices across two staves: soprano and alto share the treble clef (stems up and down respectively), while tenor and bass share the bass clef (stems up and down respectively). Both styles represent the same four-voice harmonic content in different notational formats.

# Musical Context
Keyboard style is practical for playing harmonic progressions at the keyboard. Chorale style, modeled on Bach's harmonizations of Lutheran chorales, is the standard notation for four-part writing exercises. Both styles are used throughout music theory studies for learning voice leading, harmonic progression, and chord connection.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes confuse style (the notational format) with voicing (the distribution of chord tones). A chord can have the same voicing in either style; the difference is only in how it is notated on the page. Also, in chorale style, stem direction indicates which voice a note belongs to (up = soprano/tenor; down = alto/bass).
