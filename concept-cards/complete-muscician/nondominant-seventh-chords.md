# Quick Definition
Seventh chords that do not have dominant (major-minor) or leading-tone (diminished) quality, requiring preparation and resolution of their seventh.

# Formal Definition
Nondominant seventh chords are a broad category of seventh chords that lack the dominant (major-minor seventh) or leading-tone (diminished-diminished seventh) chord quality. They include ii7, IV7, vi7, and iii7 in major, and their minor-mode equivalents. Their part-writing rules require: (1) the seventh must be prepared by common tone (preferred) or by step from above; (2) the seventh must resolve downward by step; (3) inverted forms should be complete; root-position forms can omit the fifth and double the root to avoid parallels.

# Musical Context
Nondominant seventh chords are widely used throughout the common-practice period. They provide harmonic color and forward motion. The most commonly encountered is ii6/5, which combines the pre-dominant function of ii with the added tension of a seventh.

# Examples
- Chopin, Nocturne in B major, op. 62, no. 1: ii7 chord creates tension that makes the following V7 sound like a resolution

# Common Confusions
- Nondominant sevenths require preparation; dominant sevenths do not necessarily require preparation in all contexts
- A suspended pitch over a pre-dominant can look like a different nondominant seventh chord; always check context
- ii6/5 is by far the most common nondominant seventh chord type
