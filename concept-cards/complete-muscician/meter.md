# Quick Definition
Meter is the grouping of beats into recurring patterns of strong and weak emphasis, organized into measures by bar lines.

# Formal Definition
Meter is the regular, recurring pattern of accented (strong) and unaccented (weak) beats. Three basic metrical patterns exist: duple (strong-weak), triple (strong-weak-weak), and quadruple (strong-weak-semistrong-weak). Each pattern defines a measure, which is bounded by bar lines. The meter signature (time signature) appears at the beginning of a piece and specifies the number of beats per measure and the note value assigned to the beat.

# Musical Context
Meter provides the temporal framework against which rhythm unfolds. It creates expectations of regular accent patterns that composers can either fulfill or subvert (through syncopation and hemiola). Meter is perceived rather than explicitly sounded; it is the mental grid listeners construct to organize temporal events.

# Examples
- Duple meter: 2/4, 2/2 (strong-weak)
- Triple meter: 3/4, 3/8 (strong-weak-weak)
- Quadruple meter: 4/4, 4/2 (strong-weak-semistrong-weak)
- Common time (C) = 4/4; cut time (alla breve) = 2/2

# Common Confusions
Students often confuse meter with rhythm. Meter is the underlying framework of regular accent patterns; rhythm is the specific durational pattern on the surface. A piece in 3/4 meter can have vastly different rhythmic surfaces. Also, the beat that carries the metric accent need not be literally louder; the accent is implied by the metric position.
