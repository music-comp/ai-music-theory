# Quick Definition
Techniques for prolonging the pre-dominant function through passing chords, voice exchanges, the IV-ii complex, and restatement of tonic material up a step.

# Formal Definition
The pre-dominant function can be expanded using the same principles applied to tonic and dominant expansions. Four main techniques exist: (1) A passing chord between ii and ii6 (I6 serves as passing chord, with voice exchange). (2) A passing chord between IV and IV6 (i6/4 or I passes between them). (3) Motion from IV6 (or IV6/5) to ii6/5 through a passing 6/4 chord and voice exchange -- the IV-ii complex with seventh chords. (4) Restatement of tonic material up a step, expanding the supertonic by briefly invoking its own leading tone. Note: a weak I6 can expand ii, but ii(6) cannot expand tonic.

# Musical Context
Pre-dominant expansion creates richer and more extended phrases, providing more time and harmonic interest between the tonic area and the cadential dominant. It is especially effective when combined with pre-dominant seventh chords.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Concerto no. 4 in G major, op. 58: pre-dominant expanded by voice exchange (ii6 to ii or vice versa)
- Bach, "Gerne will ich mich bequemen," St. Matthew Passion: expanded iv using a passing i6/4 chord with voice exchange
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in D major, K. 576: tonic material restated up a step to expand ii, with chromatic D#5 as temporary leading tone

# Common Confusions
- I6 can expand ii (subordinate to PD), but ii cannot expand I (subordinate to T) -- the relationship is one-directional
- Passing chords within a PD expansion are subordinate to the pre-dominant function, not independent harmonies
- Restatement up a step may introduce chromaticism (temporary leading tone) but does not constitute a modulation
