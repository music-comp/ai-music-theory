# Quick Definition
Monophonic texture consists of a single melodic line with no accompaniment, even when performed by multiple instruments in unison or octaves.

# Formal Definition
Monophonic texture is defined as a single-line melody without any harmonic accompaniment. A texture remains monophonic even when the melody is doubled at the unison or octave by additional voices or instruments, because no independent melodic or harmonic material is added. Monophonic texture is the simplest of the three basic texture types.

# Musical Context
Monophonic texture is relatively rare as a sustained texture in common-practice music but appears at key structural moments, such as the opening of a piece or at transitions. A cantus firmus and a tune you whistle are both examples of monophonic texture.

# Examples
- Schubert, Symphony no. 9 in C major (Example 6.6): the opening horn melody is monophonic, even though it may be played by multiple horns in octaves.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes think a melody played in octaves by two instruments is not monophonic. It is, because octave doubling does not add independent melodic or harmonic content. The key criterion is whether there is only one melodic line, regardless of how many instruments perform it.
