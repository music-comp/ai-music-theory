# Quick Definition
Preparation is how a dissonance is approached (preferably by step or common tone); resolution is how it is left (the seventh of V7 must always descend by step).

# Formal Definition
Preparation refers to the manner in which a chordal dissonance is approached: by step (preferred), by common tone, or by leap (uncommon, to be avoided for V7). Resolution refers to the manner in which the dissonance is left: the chordal seventh of V7 must always resolve by descending step, regardless of which voice it appears in. Preparation by step is the preferred method -- the chordal seventh enters as a passing tone from above (8-7) or as a neighbor. The resolution rule is absolute: the seventh always descends.

# Musical Context
The discipline of proper preparation and resolution of chordal dissonances is central to common-practice voice leading. These protocols ensure that dissonances are introduced smoothly and resolved satisfyingly, maintaining the tension-release dynamic fundamental to tonal music.

# Common Confusions
Students may forget that while preparation can vary, resolution of the seventh is invariable: it must descend by step. One exception exists: in the progression I-V43-I6, the seventh (^4) may ascend to ^5 when the soprano moves in parallel tenths with the bass, overriding the normal resolution.
