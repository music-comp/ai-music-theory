# Quick Definition
Motives that operate within a musical texture at various structural levels, often concealed by surface figuration, yet determining the harmonic and melodic trajectory of the music.

# Formal Definition
Hidden motivic repetitions are developmental motives that exist at various levels of musical structure, camouflaged by surface embellishment. Like chameleons, they are modified to fit within their musical environment. These hidden repetitions are particularly important because they can determine not only the motivic content but also the harmonic trajectory of the music. A surface motive may be expanded structurally so that its neighbor-note or passing-tone figure controls the underlying counterpoint and harmony over a span of measures. Motives often gain structural importance as a piece unfolds.

# Musical Context
Hidden motivic repetitions reward close analytical investigation and inform performance interpretation. Recognition of how a small surface gesture grows to control deeper structural levels reveals the organic unity of a composition.

# Examples
- Haydn, Divertimento, "St. Anthony Chorale": the upper-neighbor figure D-Eb-D appears as a one-beat surface motive, then expands structurally in the next measure where Eb occupies an entire beat and is harmonically supported by IV

# Common Confusions
- Hidden repetitions may not be audible without analytical investigation; they operate at structural rather than surface levels
- A motive that appears insignificant at the surface may prove to be the most important structural element
- Hidden repetitions demonstrate that harmonic choices are often motivated by motivic considerations
