# Quick Definition
An ascending sequence with root motion by descending thirds and ascending fourths, whose first-inversion variant is the ascending 5-6 sequence.

# Formal Definition
The A2 (-3/+4) sequence features root motion that falls a third and springs up a fourth to begin the next copy. The propulsion comes from the ascending fourth motion. The first-inversion variant, A2 (-3/+4) + 6/3, also known as the ascending 5-6 sequence, is one of the oldest sequences in tonal music (found in early sixteenth-century music). In this variant, the bass sustains as a common tone throughout the model while one voice creates 5-6 motion above it. The ascending 5-6 sequence averts parallels through the intervening 6/3 chord. This sequence also leads to the pre-dominant function.

# Musical Context
The ascending 5-6 sequence is one of the oldest and most important sequences, dating back to Josquin Desprez (d. 1521). It appears frequently in Renaissance, Baroque, and Classical music and forms the basis for the applied-chord variant that produces chromatic ascending bass lines.

# Examples
- The ascending 5-6 pattern creates outer-voice counterpoint of alternating fifths and sixths
- Commonly leads to PD function

# Common Confusions
- The 6/3 variant (ascending 5-6) is much more common than the root-position form
- The soprano note typically repeats as it starts each new copy, creating a distinctive sound
- Distinguished from A2 (+5/-4) by the smaller leaps and the characteristic common-tone bass in the 6/3 variant
