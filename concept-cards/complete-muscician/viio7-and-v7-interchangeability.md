# Quick Definition
viio7 and V7 are interchangeable because they share three pitches (^7, ^2, ^4). Each inversion of viio7 functions identically to the corresponding V7 inversion, with ^6 substituting for ^5.

# Formal Definition
viio7 and V7 share three common pitches (^7, ^2, ^4) and differ only in one: viio7 contains ^6 while V7 contains ^5. This semitone difference (^5 vs. ^6) makes them functionally interchangeable. The correspondence is: viio7 = V65 (neighbor to I); viio65 = V43 (passing between I and I6); viio43 = V42 (passing/neighbor to I6); viio42 is rare (neighbor to V). Composers choose between them based on whether ^5 or ^6 is needed in the voice leading, particularly in the soprano. viio7 is preferred when ^6 appears in the soprano (since no V7 inversion contains ^6).

# Musical Context
This interchangeability is a powerful compositional resource. viio7 provides a more dissonant alternative to V7 inversions (two tritones vs. one), offering greater intensity. Composers often combine V7 and viio7 inversions within a single prolongation. viio7 appears more frequently in minor because it occurs naturally in the minor scale.

# Common Confusions
Students may not realize the systematic correspondence between viio7 and V7 inversions. The best approach to writing viio7 is to think of the corresponding V7 inversion first, then substitute ^6 for ^5. Since all viio7 members are tendency tones (unlike V7, which includes stable ^5), voice leading requires extra care.
