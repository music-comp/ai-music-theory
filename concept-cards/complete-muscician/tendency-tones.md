# Quick Definition
Tendency tones are scale degrees with a strong melodic pull toward another pitch, usually a half step away, creating tension that demands resolution.

# Formal Definition
Tendency tones are pitches whose position within the scale creates a strong directional pull, typically toward a pitch lying a half step away. The most important tendency tone is the leading tone (^7), which has a powerful upward pull to ^1. Other tendency tones include ^4, which tends to resolve down to ^3, and raised ^6 in minor, which tends to resolve up to ^7. In the V7 chord, all four members (^5, ^7, ^2, ^4) function as tendency tones. Tendency tones should have their tendency resolved in good melodic writing, except in specific contexts (e.g., ^7 descending as part of ^1-^7-^6-^5). Tendency tones should not be doubled in voice leading because correctly resolving both would create parallel octaves.

# Musical Context
Tendency tones create the sense of directed motion that characterizes tonal melody and harmony. Their resolution is a fundamental aspect of voice leading and counterpoint. Unresolved tendency tones create tension; resolved tendency tones provide satisfaction and closure. The leading tone (^7) must ascend to ^1 when in the soprano or bass but may descend to ^5 in an inner voice.

# Examples
- The leading tone (^7) resolves upward by step to ^1, except when part of the descending line ^1-^7-^6-^5.
- In Mozart's Sonata in A minor, K. 310 (Example 3.21), G# (^7) resolves to A (^1) at the cadence.
- The augmented second between ^6 and ^7 in harmonic minor should be avoided in melody.
- In V7 in C major (G-B-D-F), B (^7) rises to C, F (^4) falls to E, D (^2) moves to C or E.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes assume tendency tones must always resolve immediately. While resolution is expected, the timing and manner can vary. The leading tone (^7) must ascend to ^1 when in the soprano or bass, but may fall to ^5 in an inner voice to create a complete tonic triad. Also, ^2 is unique because it can resolve in either direction (to ^1 or ^3).
