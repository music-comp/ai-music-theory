# Quick Definition
A technique for intensifying a cadence by repeating elements of it -- including the pre-dominant and the dissonant part of the cadential six-four -- before finally stating the authentic cadence.

# Formal Definition
Cadential extension occurs when a composer intensifies an expected cadence by evading it (typically through V4/2-I6) and then repeating the pre-dominant function and/or the cadential six-four chord, often with greater duration or emphasis, before the authentic cadence finally arrives. This creates a harmonic crescendo that heightens the listener's desire for closure and makes the eventual cadence more powerful by contrast.

# Musical Context
Cadential extensions are common at important structural cadences where maximum dramatic effect is desired. They can involve multiple evasions, with each successive attempt at cadential closure extending and intensifying the pre-cadential material.

# Examples
- Mozart, "Hm! Hm! Hm!" from The Magic Flute, Act I: three attempts at cadential closure; the first two are evaded through V4/2, with the second cad 6/4 lasting almost two measures (vs. two beats for the first); only the third attempt resolves to V7-I

# Common Confusions
- Cadential extension is different from elision: extension repeats and intensifies cadential material, while elision hides phrase boundaries
- The evasion itself is not the extension; the extension is the additional material that results from the evasion
- Each successive attempt typically increases in duration or intensity
