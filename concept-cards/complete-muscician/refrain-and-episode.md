# Quick Definition
In rondo form, the refrain (A) is the recurring section that always appears in the home key, while episodes (B, C) are contrasting sections in different keys that alternate with the refrain.

# Formal Definition
The refrain is the opening and recurring thematic section of a rondo form, always presented in the tonic key. Refrains are labeled A1, A2, A3, etc. for each appearance. Episodes are contrasting sections labeled B, C, etc., typically in different keys (five-part: B often in V or III, C often in i, iv, or vi; seven-part: B in V/i/IV, C in various keys). Episodes provide tonal and thematic variety. After A1 and B1, composers often omit repeats or write out ornamented versions, as seen when A2 occupies twice as many measures as A1 due to written-out ornamentation.

# Musical Context
The alternation of refrains and episodes embodies the fundamental musical principle of repetition and contrast. Refrains provide stability and recognition; episodes provide novelty and tonal adventure. The balance between these elements is what makes rondo form so effective as a closing movement.

# Examples
- Clementi, Piano Sonatina (Example 32.2): Three refrains (A1, A2, A3) in the tonic alternate with two episodes (B in V, C in i).
- Haydn, String Quartet in D major, op. 33, no. 6 (Example 32.4): A2 is a written-out, ornamented version of A1, occupying exactly twice as many measures.

# Common Confusions
Not every return of familiar material constitutes a refrain. A true refrain returns the original theme in the original key. Material that restates themes in a different key (as in a development section) is not a refrain. Also, double bars demarcating sections do not always appear; changes in key, motive, or texture must be used to identify section boundaries.
