# Quick Definition
The German diminished third chord (Ger7) is an inversion of the German augmented sixth chord with #^4 in the bass and b^6 in an upper voice, producing a contracting diminished third that resolves inward to ^5.

# Formal Definition
The German diminished third chord (Ger7) occurs when the pitches of a Ger6/5 are reordered so that #^4 appears in the bass and b^6 in an upper voice. The figured bass is "7," hence the label Ger7. The individual voices behave identically to those in any augmented sixth chord: b^6 descends to ^5 and #^4 ascends to ^5. However, because the pitches are switched from the traditional arrangement, the voices contract on resolution instead of expanding. The Ger7 is enharmonically equivalent to V4/2, which enables it to serve as a pivot chord for modulation.

# Musical Context
The Ger7 typically appears within pre-dominant expansions, often connected to a regular Ger6/5 through a voice exchange. It can also elaborate the dominant as a chromatic neighbor chord. Its enharmonic equivalence with V4/2 makes it useful for modulations, particularly to bII.

# Examples
- Example 30.12: A Ger6/5 on beat 1 and a reordered Ger7 on beat 3 create a voice exchange within a PD expansion.
- Tchaikovsky, Sleeping Beauty, scene 1 (Example 30.14): The Ger7 appears ff in m. 72 within an extended PD involving IV, ii6, and Ger7. It returns in m. 76 as a neighbor to V.

# Common Confusions
The Ger7 looks and sounds like a V4/2 chord, but its function is pre-dominant, not dominant. The resolution distinguishes the two: Ger7 contracts inward to V (b^6 descends, #^4 ascends), while V4/2 resolves to I6 with the bass ascending by step.
