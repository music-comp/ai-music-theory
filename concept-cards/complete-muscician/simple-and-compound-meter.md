# Quick Definition
In simple meter, beats divide naturally into two equal parts; in compound meter, beats divide into three equal parts.

# Formal Definition
Simple meters use undotted note values as the beat unit, which divide naturally into twos. Compound meters use dotted note values as the beat unit, which divide naturally into threes. Combined with the three accent patterns (duple, triple, quadruple), this yields six basic meter types: simple duple (e.g., 2/4), simple triple (e.g., 3/4), simple quadruple (e.g., 4/4), compound duple (e.g., 6/8), compound triple (e.g., 9/8), and compound quadruple (e.g., 12/8). Beat division refers to the first level of division; subdivision is the further division of each division.

# Musical Context
The distinction between simple and compound meter is fundamental to rhythmic character. Simple meters create a more straightforward, march-like feel, while compound meters produce a lilting, rocking quality. Meter signatures with upper numbers of 2, 3, or 4 indicate simple meter; upper numbers of 6, 9, or 12 indicate compound meter.

# Common Confusions
The most common confusion is between 3/4 (simple triple) and 6/8 (compound duple). Both have six eighth notes per measure, but 3/4 has three beats divided into twos, while 6/8 has two beats divided into threes. The grouping of eighth notes (beaming) clarifies: 3/4 beams in groups of 2; 6/8 beams in groups of 3.
