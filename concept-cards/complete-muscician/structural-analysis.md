# Quick Definition
Structural analysis reveals the deeper melodic and harmonic framework of a piece by identifying which pitches are structurally important and which are surface embellishments.

# Formal Definition
Structural analysis goes beyond labeling surface events to reveal the underlying pitch structure of a composition. It uses a specialized notation: the most important notes receive stems and beams, less important notes appear as noteheads only, slurs connect subordinate pitches to structural ones, and ties show restatements of important pitches. By "pruning away" embellishing pitches (reduction), the analyst discovers structural stepwise lines and the nonadjacent pitches that form the skeleton of the piece. This process reveals that works with very different surfaces may share similar underlying structures.

# Musical Context
Structural analysis is the central analytical methodology of the text, derived from Schenkerian principles. It reveals how surface events relate to deeper structural levels and how composers elaborate simple frameworks into complex musical surfaces. It informs performance decisions by showing which events are structurally significant.

# Examples
Structural analysis of "Clementine" reveals that the first phrase arpeggiates F4-A4-C5 (^1-^3-^5) on accented downbeats, while the second phrase descends stepwise from Bb4 to F4. Structural analysis of "God Save the King" reveals the same underlying trajectory despite a very different surface.

# Common Confusions
Students may confuse structural analysis with simple Roman numeral labeling or surface-level chord identification. Structural analysis requires interpretive decisions about which events are more important than others, making it a creative analytical endeavor, not a mechanical one.
