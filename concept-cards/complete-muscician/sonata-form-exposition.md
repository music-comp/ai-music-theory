# Quick Definition
The exposition is the first major section of a sonata form, presenting thematic material in the tonic (FTA) and a contrasting key (STA), connected by a transition and concluded by a closing section.

# Formal Definition
The exposition presents all primary thematic material and establishes the tonal polarity between the tonic and contrasting key. Its components are: FTA (first tonal area, in tonic), transition (Tr, modulating to the STA), STA (second tonal area, in the contrasting key), and closing section (Cl, reinforcing the new key). An optional codetta may follow. Transitions are either dependent (DTr, beginning with FTA material) or independent (ITr, using new material). Both types modulate to the STA and end on the new tonic or dominant. The medial caesura is the pause between the end of the transition and the beginning of the STA. The exposition is typically repeated (marked by repeat signs).

# Musical Context
The exposition establishes the fundamental tonal conflict of the sonata: tonic versus contrasting key. This conflict drives the rest of the form. The closing section, which follows the STA's conclusive cadence, often occupies more measures than the STA itself and may contain new thematic material.

# Examples
- Haydn, Piano Sonata in C major, Hob. XVI.35 (Example 33.9): FTA (mm. 1-19), ITr (mm. 20-35), STA (mm. 36-45), Cl (mm. 46-62), Codetta (mm. 62-67).
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in Bb major, K. 333 (Example 33.14): FTA (mm. 1-10), DTr (mm. 11-22), STA (mm. 23-38), Cl in two subsections (mm. 38-58), Codetta (mm. 59-63).

# Common Confusions
Students often underestimate the closing section, which can be longer than the STA and may contain significant thematic material. Also, not every transition modulates to the dominant; in minor-mode sonatas, the transition leads to III. The presence of accidentals often marks the beginning of the transition in major-mode sonatas.
