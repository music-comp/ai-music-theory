# Quick Definition
A direct step-descent bass (^1-^7-^6-^5) in the minor mode, named for its traditional association with melancholy or sorrowful texts and affects.

# Formal Definition
The lament bass is the minor-mode version of the direct step-descent bass, descending ^1-^7-^6-^5. The expressive half step from ^6 to ^5 and the overall descending motion in the melodic minor scale give it its characteristic sorrowful quality. When repeated throughout a piece as an ostinato, it forms a ground bass or chaconne. Lament basses may be diatonic or may include chromatic passing tones that intensify the descent.

# Musical Context
Lament basses have been associated with sorrow and mourning since the early Baroque, appearing in opera, sacred music, and instrumental works. The pattern persists into modern popular music, showing its enduring expressive power.

# Examples
- Purcell, "When I Am Laid in Earth" (Dido's Lament): chromatic lament bass as ground bass
- Bach, "Crucifixus," Mass in B minor: chromatic lament bass
- "Hit the Road, Jack": popular song built on lament bass formula
- "Erie Canal": folk song using lament bass

# Common Confusions
- Not all step-descent basses are lament basses; the term specifically applies to the minor-mode version
- Chromatic lament basses include passing tones between the diatonic steps, intensifying the descent
- The lament bass is an expressive convention, not just a technical pattern; its association with grief is historically deep
