# Quick Definition
Six common variations on the basic suspension pattern: embellished, change of bass, restruck, double/triple, retardation, and suspension chain.

# Formal Definition
Six modifications of the basic suspension pattern are commonly encountered: (1) Embellished suspensions use submetrical figurations to ornament the dissonant note. (2) Suspensions with change of bass involve a bass leap to another consonance at the point of resolution, often resulting in a harmonic change. (3) Restruck suspensions omit the tie, allowing the suspended note to be re-articulated at the point of dissonance. (4) Double and triple suspensions involve two or three simultaneous dissonances, each resolving correctly. (5) Retardations are upward-resolving suspensions, relatively rare; the 7-8 type is the only common one. (6) Suspension chains interlock multiple suspensions in a patterned series.

# Musical Context
These modifications allow composers to vary the expressive effect of suspensions while maintaining their fundamental preparation-suspension-resolution structure. Embellished and restruck suspensions are especially common in Baroque and Classical music.

# Examples
- Various modifications illustrated through elaboration of basic suspension progressions in four-voice textures

# Common Confusions
- A restruck suspension still functions as a suspension even without a tie -- the preparation is implied
- Retardations resolve upward, which is the opposite of regular suspensions; they are much rarer
- In suspensions with change of bass, include the horizontal dash in figured bass notation to show the resolution
