# Quick Definition
An anacrusis (pickup or upbeat) is one or more notes that occur before the first strong beat of a phrase, leading into the downbeat.

# Formal Definition
An anacrusis consists of one or more notes preceding the first metrically accented beat (downbeat) of a phrase or piece. When a piece begins with an anacrusis, the first measure is incomplete (a partial measure). The duration of the anacrusis and the final measure typically combine to equal one complete measure. The anacrusis creates forward momentum toward the downbeat.

# Musical Context
Anacruses are extremely common in tonal music, particularly in vocal music and dance forms. They give melodies a sense of lift and forward motion into the first downbeat. Many well-known melodies begin with an anacrusis.

# Examples
- "Amazing Grace" begins with a single quarter-note anacrusis before the downbeat.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes forget that an anacrusis creates a partial first measure and that the last measure should be shortened accordingly so that the total duration adds up to a full measure. Also, not every piece that begins with a rest on beat 1 has an anacrusis -- the anacrusis is specifically a melodic pickup.
