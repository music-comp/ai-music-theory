# Quick Definition
Voice-leading chords inserted between two harmonies whose roots move by step, used to avoid parallel perfect intervals.

# Formal Definition
Helping chords (also called voice-leading chords) are inserted between chords whose roots move by step (e.g., IV-V) in order to avoid parallel perfect intervals, especially when the outer voices move in the same direction. A common helping chord emerges from a 5-6 contrapuntal motion above the bass. The resulting chord is not a functional chord change but a subtle voice-leading shift. This procedure is the basis of the IV-ii complex.

# Musical Context
Helping chords appear whenever composers wish to write bass-soprano gestures that move in the same direction between step-related chords. They are fundamental to smooth part writing in four-voice textures throughout the common-practice period.

# Examples
- The 5-6 motion above a bass note creates a first-inversion chord that breaks up potential parallel fifths between IV and V

# Common Confusions
- Helping chords are not independent harmonies; they serve a voice-leading function
- They should not receive independent functional labels at the second level of analysis
- The term "voice-leading chord" is used interchangeably with "helping chord"
