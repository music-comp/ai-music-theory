# Quick Definition
Harmonic analysis combines roman numerals and figured bass to identify each chord's root, quality, and inversion within a key, revealing the harmonic progression of a passage.

# Formal Definition
A complete harmonic analysis involves: (1) Determine the key from the key signature and musical context (look for leading tones in minor). (2) Write the key name followed by a colon (uppercase for major, lowercase for minor). (3) Identify triad roots and assign appropriate roman numerals. (4) Add figured bass for inverted chords (6 for first inversion, 6/4 for second inversion). (5) Do not repeat the roman numeral for immediately repeated chords; draw a horizontal line instead. Roman numerals show hierarchical relationships (how each chord relates to the tonic); figured bass reveals contextual voice-leading connections between chords.

# Musical Context
Harmonic analysis is the primary tool for understanding how chords function within a key. It reveals the underlying harmonic logic beneath the musical surface and enables comparison of harmonic procedures across different keys, composers, and style periods.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes struggle to determine whether a passage is in the major key or its relative minor. Look for raised ^7 as an accidental -- this strongly suggests the minor key. Also, some key signatures do not agree with the key of the music (e.g., a piece in a closely related key), requiring careful examination of the musical content.
