# Quick Definition
Conjunct motion is stepwise melodic movement (seconds); disjunct motion involves skips (thirds) and leaps (fourths or larger).

# Formal Definition
Conjunct motion refers to melodic movement by step (major or minor seconds), creating smooth, connected lines. Disjunct motion refers to melodic movement by intervals larger than a second: skips (thirds) create moderate disjunct motion, while leaps (fourths or larger) create more dramatic disjunct motion. Good tonal melodies are predominantly conjunct, using disjunct motion sparingly for variety and interest.

# Musical Context
The balance of conjunct and disjunct motion is a fundamental aspect of melodic construction. Stepwise motion provides coherence and singability; leaps create energy and dramatic interest. The law of recovery states that leaps should be followed (and ideally preceded) by a change of direction and stepwise motion, "filling in" the musical space created by the leap.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes use "leap" to describe any interval larger than a second. Technically, a skip is specifically a third, while a leap is a fourth or larger. Both are forms of disjunct motion, but the distinction matters in counterpoint, where skips and leaps are treated differently.
