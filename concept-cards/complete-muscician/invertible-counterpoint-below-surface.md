# Quick Definition
The use of invertible counterpoint as a structural agent beneath a homophonic or embellished surface, where the bass-soprano relationship governs the harmonic progression even when not immediately obvious.

# Formal Definition
Invertible counterpoint often operates below the music's surface, disguised by full homophonic textures and various embellishing processes. Through contrapuntal reduction, the underlying soprano-bass relationship can be revealed as an inversion of previous material. In such cases, identifying roman numerals alone captures symptoms rather than causes; the melodic relationship between soprano and bass is the true governing force. Understanding this deeper contrapuntal layer reveals how passages are motivated by voice-leading relationships rather than chord-by-chord harmonic thinking.

# Musical Context
Recognizing invertible counterpoint below the surface reveals important structural relationships that inform performance interpretation. It connects seemingly different passages through shared contrapuntal frameworks and shows how harmonic progressions arise from melodic-contrapuntal thinking.

# Examples
- Schubert, Piano Sonata: tonic expanded through V4/3, V6/5, and V4/2, where the structural counterpoint reveals a large neighbor figure (^1-^7-^1) over passing motion (^1-^2-^3) that is then inverted in the following measures

# Common Confusions
- Surface-level roman numeral analysis may miss the deeper invertible counterpoint that generates the passage
- The invertible relationship may not be literal; it can be decorated and varied while maintaining its essential contrapuntal framework
- Invertible counterpoint below the surface is an analytical interpretation, not always an intentional compositional device
