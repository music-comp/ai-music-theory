# Quick Definition
Roman numeral analysis identifies chords by the scale degree of their root and their quality, using uppercase for major (I, IV, V) and lowercase for minor (ii, iii, vi) and diminished (vii) triads.

# Formal Definition
Roman numerals represent triads by indicating: (1) the scale degree on which the chord is built (shown by the numeral's position: I = ^1, ii = ^2, etc.), and (2) the chord's quality (uppercase = major triad; lowercase = minor triad; lowercase with degree sign = diminished triad, e.g., vii). Combined with figured bass, roman numerals provide a complete harmonic analysis: the numeral shows root and quality; the figures show inversion and voice leading. The key is written before the analysis (uppercase for major, lowercase for minor, followed by a colon).

# Musical Context
Roman numeral analysis is the standard method for harmonic analysis in tonal music theory. It reveals harmonic function by showing each chord's relationship to the tonic, making it possible to compare harmonic progressions across different keys. While roman numerals show hierarchical relationships, figured bass reveals how intervals connect chords contrapuntally.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes forget that roman numerals are relative to the key, not absolute. A IV chord in C major is F major; a IV chord in G major is C major. The same triad can have different roman numerals in different keys. Also, the case (upper vs. lower) indicates quality, not importance.
