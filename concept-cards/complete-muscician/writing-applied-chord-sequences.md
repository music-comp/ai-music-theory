# Quick Definition
Guidelines for composing applied-chord sequences: construct a careful two-chord model, maintain voice leading in copies, keep diatonic roots, and use complete seventh chords in inversion.

# Formal Definition
Four rules govern writing applied-chord sequences: (1) Construct the two-chord model carefully, ensuring correct voice leading between the two chords and into the first chord of the copy. (2) Copies must follow the voice leading of the model exactly. (3) The root of each chord must be diatonic to the key. (4) Write complete seventh chords, especially in inversion; the only exception is D2 (-5/+4) sequences with interlocking sevenths, where every other chord is incomplete.

# Musical Context
These guidelines apply to all applied-chord sequence types (D2, D3, A2) and ensure that chromatic sequences maintain proper voice leading while preserving the underlying diatonic framework.

# Examples
- D2 (-5/+4) applied-chord sequence: model consists of applied V7 resolving to diatonic triad; copies replicate this pattern at successive pitch levels
- D3 (+3/-5) with 4/3 inversions: each pair consists of applied 4/3 chord resolving to root-position triad

# Common Confusions
- The roots remain diatonic even though the chords are chromatically altered
- Complete seventh chords are required in inversion (unlike root-position interlocking sevenths where omission is necessary)
