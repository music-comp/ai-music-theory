# Quick Definition
Close position packs the three upper voices within an octave; open position spreads them across more than an octave. Proper spacing keeps adjacent upper voices within an octave of each other.

# Formal Definition
In close position, the three upper voices (soprano, alto, tenor) are packed within a single octave with no additional chord tones possible between any pair. In open position, the total spacing of upper voices is an octave or more. Only the upper three voices determine spacing -- the bass is excluded because it must leap more freely to define harmony. Adjacent upper voices (soprano-alto and alto-tenor) must be kept within an octave of each other, though more than an octave between tenor and bass is acceptable. Beginning in close position is recommended; moving to open position for contrast is fine, but returning to close position helps avoid problematic parallels.

# Musical Context
Proper spacing creates a balanced, well-blended chord sound. Placing voices too close together in low registers creates a muddled sound. Close position helps avoid parallel fifths because potential P5s become P4s. The three upper voices should generally be kept in a comfortable middle-to-high register.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes apply spacing rules to the bass voice. Only adjacent upper voices (S-A and A-T) must be within an octave. The bass can be more than an octave below the tenor. Also, close and open position refer only to the upper three voices, not the entire chord.
