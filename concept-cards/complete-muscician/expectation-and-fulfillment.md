# Quick Definition
Expectation and fulfillment is a Gestalt principle describing how composers create tension by delaying or thwarting expected musical events, making the eventual arrival more dramatic.

# Formal Definition
Expectation/fulfillment is a Gestalt psychological process central to the unfolding of musical works. Composers establish patterns and trajectories that create listener expectations, then momentarily change direction or postpone expected arrivals. The longer the postponement, the more dramatic the effect when the expected event finally occurs. This process involves the buildup toward a climax and the subsequent release, the thwarting of expectations, surprise, and ambiguity.

# Musical Context
This principle operates at every level of tonal music, from the momentary delay of a melodic arrival to the large-scale postponement of a tonic return. It is the basis for much musical drama, including deceptive cadences, extended dominant preparations, and delayed resolutions.

# Examples
In Corelli's Sarabande in D minor, a clear stepwise fifth descent from A to D is postponed by the upper-neighbor figure A-B-A after the initial descending arpeggiation. Four of the five pitches of the descent accelerate into mm. 3-4, creating a satisfying fulfillment. In Bach's Solo Violin Sonata no. 3, a rising chromatic line pauses on E for one and a half measures before finally arriving on F over D-minor harmony.

# Common Confusions
Expectation/fulfillment is not just about delaying resolutions; it also includes the initial establishment of the pattern that creates the expectation. Without a clearly established pattern, there is no expectation to fulfill or thwart.
