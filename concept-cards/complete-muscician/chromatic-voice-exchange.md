# Quick Definition
A chromatic voice exchange is a voice exchange in which the swapped pitches involve chromatic alteration, typically occurring between a diatonic chord and a mixture or augmented sixth chord.

# Formal Definition
A chromatic voice exchange occurs when two voices swap pitches, but one or both of those pitches are chromatically altered. For example, in a IV6 chord, the bass and an upper voice might exchange pitches, with the resulting chord being ii-half-dim-6/5 through mixture (e.g., A and F in IV6 swap to become F and Ab in ii-half-dim-6/5). Unlike diatonic voice exchanges, chromatic voice exchanges necessarily involve cross relations, but intervening passing chords diminish any harshness.

# Musical Context
Chromatic voice exchanges appear in two primary contexts: (1) within pre-dominant expansions involving modal mixture (Chapter 27), where IV6 exchanges with ii°6/5 or similar mixture chords; and (2) within augmented sixth chord progressions (Chapter 30), where iv and an augmented sixth chord exchange pitches. They are a powerful contrapuntal device for extending pre-dominant function chromatically.

# Examples
- Example 27.10C: A passing minor v6 leads to the pre-dominant, which is expanded through a chromatic voice exchange where A/F in the IV6 chord become F/Ab in the ii°6/5 chord.
- C.P.E. Bach, Flute Sonata no. 6 (Example 30.11B): Both a diatonic voice exchange and a chromatic voice exchange occur in succession.

# Common Confusions
Students may confuse chromatic voice exchanges with ordinary cross relations. The key distinction is that in a chromatic voice exchange, two specific voices systematically swap their pitches with chromatic alteration; it is a controlled contrapuntal technique, not a haphazard juxtaposition of accidentals.
