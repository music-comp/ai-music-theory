# Quick Definition
The figured bass notation 8-7 indicates that the octave above the bass descends by step to the seventh, typically showing the voice leading when the chordal seventh of V7 enters as a passing tone.

# Formal Definition
The figured bass symbol 8-7 represents a specific voice-leading pattern above a bass note: the pitch initially forming an octave with the bass descends by step to form a seventh. The dash connecting the two numbers indicates that this voice leading takes place in a single voice (any upper voice, or the vocal part in a song). This notation captures the passing-tone derivation of the chordal seventh: what begins as a consonant octave becomes a dissonant seventh through stepwise descent. This is the most common and preferred way to introduce the seventh of V7.

# Musical Context
The 8-7 figure is the standard way to prepare the dominant seventh as a passing tone in figured bass realization. It appears throughout Baroque, Classical, and Romantic music wherever the dominant seventh is introduced smoothly from above. Understanding this figure connects the melodic (passing tone) and harmonic (chordal seventh) aspects of the dissonance.

# Examples
In Schubert's "Wasserfluth," C#5 (octave above bass C#) descends to B4 (the seventh), notated as 8-7 in figured bass. In Example 9.8B, the soprano G4 (octave above bass G) passes through F4 (seventh) to E4, labeled 8-7 in the figured bass.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes read 8-7 as two separate chords. The dash between the numbers indicates motion within a single chord (the dominant), not a chord change. The figure describes voice leading above a sustained bass note, showing how the passing seventh enters.
