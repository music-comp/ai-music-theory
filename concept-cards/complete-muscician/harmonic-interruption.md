# Quick Definition
The point in a period where a half cadence halts the harmonic progression, and the music restarts on tonic in the consequent phrase.

# Formal Definition
An interruption occurs when the antecedent phrase of a period ends with a half cadence and the consequent phrase restarts the harmonic motion from tonic. The harmonic progression is "interrupted" at the dominant and must begin again to reach its eventual tonic goal. Marked with a double slash (//) in formal diagrams. The dominant at the interruption is a back-relating dominant that extends the initial tonic rather than a structural dominant resolving forward. In larger forms such as rounded binary, an interruption marks the structural HC at the end of the digression before the recapitulation.

# Musical Context
Interruption is the fundamental harmonic principle behind the most common period type (PIP) and also underlies the structure of rounded binary form. It creates large-scale expectation for tonal completion.

# Examples
- In a PIP: I...HC // I...PAC — the double slash represents the interruption
- In rounded binary form: the structural HC before the return of opening material

# Common Confusions
- The HC at the interruption does not resolve forward to tonic; it is a back-relating dominant
- Interruption is a structural concept at the level of form, not just a surface-level pause
