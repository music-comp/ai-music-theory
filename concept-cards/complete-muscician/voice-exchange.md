# Quick Definition
A voice exchange occurs when two voices swap pitches, with one voice taking a pitch that the other had in the previous chord, typically creating an X-shaped pattern between voices.

# Formal Definition
A voice exchange is a specific type of revoicing in which two (or even all three) upper voices swap notes when a harmony repeats or when moving between root position and first inversion of the same chord. For example, the soprano and tenor might exchange the notes C and E, marked by diagonal lines forming an X. Voice exchanges often involve movement in all voices and create a sense of harmonic extension without actual harmonic change. Multiple-pitch swappings are called part exchanges.

# Musical Context
Voice exchanges are a primary means of extending a single harmony over time. They are especially common in tonic expansions (I to I6), where the bass moves from ^1 to ^3 while the soprano moves from ^3 to ^1 (or vice versa). Voice exchanges create contrapuntal interest while prolonging a single harmonic function.

# Examples
In Example 8.15B, the soprano and tenor swap C and E when moving from open to close position on the same harmony. In Brahms's "Vom verwundeten Knaben" (Example 10.3D), voice exchanges appear in mm. 1 and 3, with pitches swapped between bass and soprano as the harmony moves between root position and first inversion. In the Beethoven Fifth Symphony example, part exchanges (multiple-pitch swaps) extend the tonic.

# Common Confusions
A voice exchange is not the same as voice crossing. In a voice exchange, the pitches are swapped between voices deliberately to extend a harmony; both voices end up in their proper registers. Also, when a voice exchange occurs, all voices may need to move, sometimes requiring leaps that would otherwise be avoided.
