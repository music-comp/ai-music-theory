# Quick Definition
Gap-fill is a melodic principle (a Gestalt law) in which a melodic leap opens a "gap" that is subsequently filled in by stepwise motion in the opposite direction.

# Formal Definition
Gap-fill is a Gestalt principle predicated on the listener's expectation that a melodic leap will eventually be filled in by stepwise motion in the opposite direction. The leap creates a musical "gap" or space, and the subsequent stepwise motion provides balance and satisfies the listener's expectations. This procedure, first encountered in counterpoint studies, is a crucial component of successful composition.

# Musical Context
Gap-fill appears constantly in tonal melody. It creates melodic balance by ensuring that large intervallic leaps are counteracted by smooth stepwise motion. Composers use it to generate both tension (the leap) and resolution (the filling-in).

# Examples
In Donizetti's Lucrezia Borgia, a chordal leap of a fourth (B to E) in m. 1 is immediately filled in as the fourth retraces itself by stepwise motion. Several of Chopin's Nocturnes (op. 15/1 in F major, op. 55/1 in F minor, op. posth. 72/1 in E minor, op. 15/3 in G minor) each begin with a leap that opens a musical space immediately filled in by stepwise contrary motion.

# Common Confusions
Gap-fill does not require immediate filling -- the stepwise motion can unfold gradually. Also, gap-fill applies specifically to the principle of contrary-direction stepwise motion following a leap, not just any change of direction.
