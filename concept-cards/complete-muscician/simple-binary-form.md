# Quick Definition
A binary form in which no thematic material from the first section recurs in the second section.

# Formal Definition
When the two sections of a binary form share no melodic material -- that is, the second section does not restate any themes from the first section -- the thematic design is called simple. Simple binary form can be either sectional (first section closes on tonic) or continuous (first section closes away from tonic). In simple binary, the digression in the second section may begin with motives from the first section but does not present a literal return of the opening material.

# Musical Context
Simple binary form is common in Baroque dance movements and short character pieces. It represents the most straightforward type of binary form, without the complication of thematic return.

# Examples
- Hummel, Bagatelle in C major, op. 107: simple sectional binary (no thematic return, first section closes on tonic)
- Weber, Six Ecossaise, no. 1, J. 29: simple continuous binary (no thematic return, first section closes on V)
- Handel, Concerto Grosso in F major, op. 3, no. 4, Allegro: simple continuous binary

# Common Confusions
- "Simple" refers only to the thematic design (no return of opening material), not to the complexity of the music
- The second section may still develop or fragment motives from the first section; "simple" means no literal restatement of the opening theme
