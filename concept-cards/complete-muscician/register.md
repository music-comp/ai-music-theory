# Quick Definition
Register refers to the relative highness or lowness of a pitch or group of pitches, determining their octave placement and their perceptual brightness or depth.

# Formal Definition
Register designates the octave location of a pitch, indicated by a superscript number following the letter name (e.g., C4 for middle C). The standard octave designation system assigns numbers from the lowest audible pitches upward: C1 is the lowest C on the piano, C4 is middle C, and C8 is the highest C. Register is distinct from pitch class: all C's share a pitch class but occupy different registers. Higher registers produce brighter, more penetrating sounds; lower registers produce darker, fuller sounds.

# Musical Context
Register affects the perception of emphasis, weight, and character in music. Registral accents occur when pitches in extreme high or low registers draw attention. In counterpoint and harmony, wider spacing between bass and upper voices is preferred for clarity, while tightly spaced chords in the low register sound muddy.

# Examples
- Middle C is C4; the C an octave higher is C5.
- Registral accents: low bass notes in Chopin's waltzes create accents on beat 1 (Example 2.12).
- Open spacing in triads places wider intervals in the bass register for clarity (Example 5.3).

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes use "range" and "register" interchangeably. Range is the span from the lowest to highest pitch in a melody or instrument; register refers to the specific octave placement of pitches. A melody can have a narrow range but occupy a high register.
