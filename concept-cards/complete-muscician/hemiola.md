# Quick Definition
A hemiola is a metrical disturbance in which a duple accent pattern is temporarily imposed on a triple meter, or vice versa, creating the effect of a competing meter.

# Formal Definition
Hemiola occurs most commonly when a duple grouping is superimposed on a triple meter. In 3/4, for example, accents placed on every other beat rather than every third beat create two groups of three quarter notes that sound like three groups of two, effectively replacing two measures of 3/4 with one measure of 3/2. Hemiola can be created by literal accent markings, by durational accents, by harmonic accents, or by other nontemporal accentual techniques.

# Musical Context
Hemiola is particularly common in Baroque music, where it frequently signals the approach to a cadence. The sudden shift from triple to duple grouping creates a striking effect that marks structural boundaries. It also appears regularly in Romantic music and in folk music traditions.

# Examples
- Example 2.38A: articulative accents on every other quarter note in 3/4 create duple grouping.
- Example 2.38B: durational accents (half notes in 3/4) create duple grouping.
- Mozart, Eine kleine Nachtmusik, Menuetto (Example 2.39): mm. 6-8 shift from the established triple grouping (1 2 3 | 1 2 3) to a duple feel (1 2 1 | 2 1 2 | 1) approaching the cadence.

# Common Confusions
Students sometimes label any rhythmic irregularity as hemiola. True hemiola specifically involves the ratio of 3:2 -- regrouping triple patterns into duple or vice versa. A simple accent on a weak beat is syncopation, not hemiola. Hemiola must create the perception of an alternative meter.
