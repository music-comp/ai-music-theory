# Quick Definition
A type of continuous variation built on both a repeating bass line and a repeating harmonic pattern.

# Formal Definition
A passacaglia combines the features of ground bass and chaconne: it employs both a repeating bass line pattern and a repeating harmonic progression as the foundation for variations. In practice, the term is often used interchangeably with ground bass and chaconne, since most ostinato-based variations combine repeating bass lines with repeating harmonies. The passacaglia is typically in triple meter and in a minor key, though these are conventions rather than strict requirements.

# Musical Context
Passacaglias are associated with Baroque organ and orchestral music. Notable examples span from the seventeenth century through the twentieth century.

# Examples
- The Corelli Folia: a famous ground bass/harmonic pattern used by numerous composers including Lully, Handel, C.P.E. Bach, Nielsen, and Rachmaninoff

# Common Confusions
- The distinction between passacaglia, chaconne, and ground bass is often academic; composers rarely maintain strict boundaries
- All three terms describe continuous variation forms built on repeating patterns
