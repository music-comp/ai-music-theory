# Quick Definition
Inversions of V7 follow the same basic voice-leading rules as root-position V7: ^7 rises to ^1 (in outer voices), ^4 falls to ^3 (with one exception), and all voices move by step or common tone.

# Formal Definition
Voice-leading rules for inversions of V7: (1) Move all voices by step or common tone when leading to and from inversions. (2) Inversions of V7 should appear as complete chords (no omitted members). (3) They tend to fall on metrically unaccented beats, connecting more stable tonic triads on strong beats. (4) Resolve tendency tones: ^7 (leading tone) must ascend in outer voices but may fall to ^5 in inner voices; ^4 (seventh) must descend to ^3, except in I-V43-I6 where parallel-tenth motion permits the seventh to ascend.

# Musical Context
These voice-leading principles ensure smooth connections between inversions of V7 and the tonic chords they prolong. Maintaining complete chords (unlike root-position V7, where the fifth may be omitted) is important because the inversions derive their identity from having all four members present.

# Examples
In all three inversions, the chordal third (^7) rises and the chordal seventh (^4) resolves by step down. V65 resolves to I (bass ^7 to ^1). V43 resolves to I or I6 (bass ^2 to ^1 or ^3). V42 resolves to I6 (bass ^4 to ^3). The special exception in V43: when bass ascends ^1-^2-^3 with soprano in parallel tenths, the seventh may ascend.

# Common Confusions
The main exception students struggle with is the ascending seventh in V43 when parallel tenths control the outer voices. This is the only context where the seventh may ascend. In all other inversions and voicings, ^4 must descend to ^3. Also, unlike root-position V7, inverted V7 chords should be complete (no omitted fifth).
