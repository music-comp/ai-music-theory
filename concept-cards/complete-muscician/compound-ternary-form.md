# Quick Definition
A compound ternary form is a ternary structure in which the larger A, B, and/or A' sections themselves divide into smaller forms, typically rounded binary or simple binary.

# Formal Definition
Compound ternary forms contain nested smaller forms within their larger sections. The larger ABA structure is preserved, but each section (A, B, A') may internally be organized as a rounded binary, simple binary, or other small form. This creates multiple levels of formal structure. The minuet-trio form is a prime example: the minuet (A) and trio (B) are each typically in rounded binary form, and the da capo return of the minuet creates the outer ternary structure.

# Musical Context
Compound ternary forms are extremely common in Classical and Romantic music. They appear in minuet-trio movements of symphonies, string quartets, and sonatas; in da capo arias; and in character pieces. The nesting of smaller forms within the larger ternary creates a satisfying hierarchy of repetition and contrast at multiple structural levels.

# Examples
- Grieg, "Hjemve" (Example 31.2): Both the A and A' sections are rounded binary forms nested within the larger full sectional ternary.
- Haydn, Divertimento in G major, Hob. XVI.11 (Example 31.5): Both the A and B sections contain rounded binary forms, creating a compound ternary.

# Common Confusions
Do not confuse compound ternary form (a ternary with nested smaller forms) with compound meter or compound intervals. Also, the term "compound" here is different from its use in "compound rondo" (Chapter 32), though the principle of nesting is the same.
