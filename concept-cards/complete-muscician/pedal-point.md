# Quick Definition
A sustained pitch (usually tonic or dominant in the bass) that remains stationary while multiple harmonic changes occur above it, grounding the music on a single static harmony.

# Formal Definition
A pedal (PED) or pedal point is a sustained pitch or harmony that sits motionless during multiple harmonic changes. Derived from the organ's ability to sustain a pitch indefinitely, the pedal tone usually occurs in the bass and almost always is a tonic or dominant scale degree. The pedal slows harmonic motion and firmly grounds the music. Harmonic successions unfolding above a pedal are subordinate to the sustained pitch and are shown as such in two-level analysis.

# Musical Context
Pedals frequently occur in cadential situations, where harmonic successions unfold over a single held note. They are common at the ends of fugues and other contrapuntal works, and at climactic or transitional moments in sonata forms. Both tonic and dominant pedals serve important structural functions.

# Examples
- Bach, Fugue in C minor, The Well-Tempered Clavier, Book 1, BWV 847: pedal point at the cadential close with harmonic motion unfolding above the sustained bass

# Common Confusions
- Harmonies sounding above a pedal are subordinate to it; they do not represent structural chord changes
- Pedals can be on tonic or dominant but rarely on other scale degrees
- The pedal six-four chord is a specific type of embellishment that arises from neighbor motion above a sustained bass, and should not be confused with the general concept of a pedal point
