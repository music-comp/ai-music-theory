# Quick Definition
A compositional technique where a miniature sentence structure (1:1:2) appears within the B portion (or A portion) of a larger sentence.

# Formal Definition
When the B portion of a sentence itself subdivides into a 1+1+2 pattern, the result is a nested sentence. In notation, uppercase letters (A, A', B) designate the larger sentence and lowercase letters (a, a', b) designate the nested structure within A. If both A and B portions contain nested structures, the nested structures in B are labeled with new letters (c, c', d) to distinguish them from those in A.

# Musical Context
Nested sentences create additional layers of formal hierarchy within a single phrase. They are characteristic of sophisticated compositional technique, appearing frequently in Beethoven's works.

# Examples
- Beethoven, Piano Sonata no. 1 in F minor, op. 2: the B section (mm. 5-8) contains a nested sentence where mm. 5-6 (a-a') repeat a step higher and mm. 7-8 (b) provide the climactic gesture

# Common Confusions
- Use uppercase letters for the larger sentence and lowercase for the nested one
- When both A and B contain nested structures, use different letter sets (a/a'/b for A's nesting, c/c'/d for B's nesting) to avoid confusion
