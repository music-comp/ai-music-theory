# Quick Definition
The most common type of two-reprise form, combining a first section that modulates away from tonic with a second section that digresses, reaches a structural HC, and returns the opening material in the tonic.

# Formal Definition
Rounded continuous binary form has: (1) a first section (A) that modulates to a closely related key (V in major, III or v in minor); (2) a second section beginning with a digression that develops material and leads to a structural HC (interruption); (3) a recapitulation (A') that restates the opening material in the tonic key. This creates a three-part thematic design (A-digression-A') within a two-part harmonic structure. In major keys, the typical tonal plan is I-V || digression-HC || I (recapitulation). In minor keys: i-III (or v) || digression-HC || i.

# Musical Context
This is the most prevalent binary form type and the structural model from which sonata form evolved. It is the standard form for Classical minuets, scherzos, and variation themes.

# Examples
- Mozart, Piano Sonata in D major, K. 284, Theme: A section modulates to V (mm. 1-8), digression with sequences (mm. 9-12), interruption, recapitulation of theme in tonic (mm. 14-17)

# Common Confusions
- The three-part thematic design (A-digression-A') does not make this ternary form; the two-part harmonic structure (modulation then return) is the defining binary feature
- The interruption occurs within the second section, not at the double bar
