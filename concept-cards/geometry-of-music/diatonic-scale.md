# Quick Definition
The most nearly even seven-note scale in twelve-tone equal temperament, constructible both as a stack of seven perfect fifths and as a two-octave stack of three major and four minor thirds. It lies at the center of the seven-note scale lattice.

# Core Definition
The diatonic scale is the most even seven-note chord in twelve-tone chromatic space, with steps alternating between one and two semitones (pattern: 2-2-1-2-2-2-1). It can be constructed as a stack of seven perfect fifths (with one "near fifth" — the tritone) or as a two-octave stack of thirds (three major + four minor, in the particular arrangement M-m-M-m-M-m-M). As the most even seven-note scale, it lies at the center of the scale lattice (Figure 3.11.8), connected to all other seven-note scales by efficient voice leading. The diatonic circle of fifths — a chain of diatonic scales linked by single-semitone voice leadings — zigzags through the center of seven-dimensional chord space.

# Prerequisites

# Key Properties
1. Steps of 1 and 2 semitones; thirds of 3 and 4 semitones
2. Inversionally symmetrical
3. Constructible from both fifths and thirds
4. Most even seven-note scale in 12-TET
5. Lies at the center of the scale lattice
6. Adjacent diatonic scales on the circle of fifths differ by one semitone

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Stack seven perfect fifths: F-C-G-D-A-E-B
2. Fold into a single octave: C-D-E-F-G-A-B
3. Alternatively, stack thirds in a two-octave span: C-E-G-B-D-F-A

# Context & Application
The diatonic scale is the primary scale of Western music from the medieval period through the common practice era and remains central in popular music. From Tymoczko's geometric perspective, its significance stems from its mathematical properties (maximal evenness, saturation with fifths and thirds, position at the center of scale space) rather than from historical convention alone. It anchors the scale lattice, with other important scales radiating outward from it.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking the diatonic scale is "natural" while other scales are "artificial"
  **Correction**: All eight important scales arise from the same mathematical principles; the diatonic is distinguished by maximal evenness, not by naturalness

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Is "diatonic" the same as "major"?
  **Clarification**: "Diatonic" refers to the collection of seven notes; "major" refers to a specific mode (rotation) with a particular tonic. The diatonic collection includes all seven traditional modes.
