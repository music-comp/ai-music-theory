# Quick Definition
Voice independence is the perceptual quality of polyphonic voices being heard as distinct melodic lines rather than fusing into a single composite sound, maintained through contrary motion, avoidance of parallel perfect intervals, and varied intervallic patterns.

# Core Definition
Voice independence refers to the listener's ability to perceive multiple simultaneous melodic lines as distinct entities. It is a fundamental goal of Western counterpoint from the Renaissance onward. The primary threats to voice independence are: parallel motion (especially at perfect intervals), which causes voices to "fuse" perceptually; close spacing in the same register; and identical rhythmic patterns. The prohibition on parallel fifths and octaves is traditionally explained as preserving voice independence, since these intervals promote auditory fusion. Tymoczko adds that diatonic scale structure plays a role: parallel diatonic fifths produce uniform chromatic intervals (all 7 semitones), creating maximal parallelism, while parallel thirds produce varied intervals (3 and 4 semitones), maintaining some distinctness. Contrary motion, varied rhythmic profiles, and registral separation all enhance voice independence.

# Key Properties
1. Perception of distinct melodic lines rather than composite sound
2. Threatened by parallel motion at perfect intervals
3. Enhanced by contrary motion, varied rhythms, registral separation
4. The diatonic scale's uneven interval distribution helps: parallel thirds vary in size, supporting independence
5. Connected to auditory streaming research (Huron 2001)
6. Not purely acoustic — scale structure contributes

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Favor contrary motion between voices
2. Avoid parallel perfect fifths and octaves
3. Use varied rhythmic patterns across voices
4. Maintain registral separation
5. Limit duration of parallel motion even at imperfect intervals
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Can each voice be followed as a distinct melody?
2. Check for extended passages of parallel motion
3. Listen for whether voices fuse into a single composite line

# Context & Application
Voice independence is the fundamental rationale for many contrapuntal rules. It explains not only the prohibition on parallel fifths/octaves but also the preference for contrary motion, the avoidance of voice crossings in some styles, and the practice of giving voices distinct rhythmic profiles. The concept connects traditional counterpoint pedagogy with modern research on auditory streaming. In twentieth-century music, the relaxation of parallel-fifth prohibitions (in jazz, rock, and some art music) reflects a shift in priorities — harmonic consistency may be valued over voice independence.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 228): Parallel diatonic fifths — voices move in lockstep through 7-semitone intervals, maximizing fusion. Parallel diatonic thirds alternate 3 and 4 semitones, maintaining some independence.

**Example 2** (p. 228-229): In Messiaen's mode 3, four-step intervals alternate 6 and 7 semitones, producing roughly similar independence to three-step intervals (always 4 semitones), despite the former containing perfect fifths.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating voice independence as purely an acoustic phenomenon
  **Correction**: Scale structure, rhythmic differentiation, and registral placement all contribute alongside acoustic factors

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking any parallel motion destroys independence
  **Clarification**: Parallel motion at imperfect intervals (thirds, sixths) is a standard technique; only parallel perfect intervals are prohibited
