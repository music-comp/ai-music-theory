# Quick Definition
The five components of tonality are not independent; they interact and constrain each other in musically significant ways, particularly the tension between harmonic consistency and conjunct melodic motion.

# Core Definition
Tymoczko argues that the five features of tonality are deeply interrelated. The most important interaction is between conjunct melodic motion (horizontal coherence) and harmonic consistency (vertical coherence): these two goals are in fundamental tension, since moving voices by small distances between structurally similar chords is not always possible. Only certain kinds of chords — those that are nearly symmetrical under transposition, inversion, or permutation — allow both constraints to be satisfied simultaneously. This leads to the remarkable result that the most acoustically consonant chords (which divide the octave nearly evenly) are precisely the ones that permit efficient voice leading between transpositions.

# Key Properties
1. Harmonic consistency and conjunct melodic motion are in tension
2. Only nearly symmetrical chords can satisfy both constraints simultaneously
3. Acoustic consonance implies near evenness, which implies the ability for efficient voice leading
4. The same chords are optimal for both consonance and contrapuntal flexibility
5. Limited macroharmony and centricity interact with the other three features

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Recognize that combining harmonic consistency with stepwise voice leading requires special chords
2. Identify which chord types are nearly symmetrical
3. Understand that these happen to be the acoustically consonant chords
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Check whether a passage achieves both harmonic consistency and efficient voice leading
2. If so, the chords being used are likely nearly symmetrical (and probably consonant)

# Context & Application
This interdependence is one of the book's central insights. It explains why Western tonal music uses the particular chords it does: major/minor triads, dominant sevenths, etc. are not arbitrary choices but are mathematically constrained by the desire to combine harmonic consistency with smooth voice leading. The fact that these same chords also happen to be acoustically consonant is a "remarkable accident" that made Western counterpoint possible.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 82): "Suppose God asked you, at the dawn of time, to choose the chords that humanity would use in its music." Whether you optimized for efficient voice leading with harmonic consistency or for acoustic consonance, "God would hand you the very same chords."

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking the five features are completely independent
  **Correction**: They are deeply constrained by each other, especially the harmonic-melodic tension

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking efficient voice leading is always possible between any similar chords
  **Clarification**: Only nearly symmetrical chords permit efficient voice leading between their transpositions/inversions
