# Quick Definition
A seven-note nearly even scale (step pattern: 2-2-2-1-2-1-2) that approximates the first seven pitch classes of the harmonic series. Also known as the melodic minor ascending or Lydian dominant scale. It is the second-most even seven-note scale and mediates between diatonic and whole-tone/octatonic collections.

# Core Definition
The acoustic scale (labeled relative to C as C-D-E-F#-G-A-Bb) is a nearly even seven-note scale with steps of one and two semitones. It is so called because it approximately matches the first seven distinct pitch classes of the harmonic series. It can also be described as the mixolydian mode with raised fourth degree, or the lydian mode with lowered seventh, or the ascending form of the melodic minor scale (starting from a different degree). Constructible as a two-octave stack of three major and four minor thirds (in a specific arrangement different from the diatonic), it is the second-most even seven-note scale. On the scale lattice, acoustic scales are generated by rearranging adjacent voice leadings on the diatonic circle of fifths. Every acoustic scale is one semitone away from two diatonic scales and from an octatonic and whole-tone scale.

# Key Properties
1. Step pattern: 2-2-2-1-2-1-2 (inversionally symmetrical)
2. Steps of 1 and 2 semitones; thirds of 3 and 4 semitones
3. Second-most even seven-note scale
4. One semitone away from diatonic scales on the scale lattice
5. Mediates between diatonic and whole-tone/octatonic collections
6. Shares 5 of 6 notes with a whole-tone scale; 6 of 8 with an octatonic scale; 6 of 7 with diatonic scales

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Take a major scale and raise the fourth degree and lower the seventh: C-D-E-F#-G-A-Bb
2. Or: take the melodic minor ascending and relabel starting from the fourth degree
3. Or: arrange 3M+4m thirds in the two-octave stack specific to this scale

# Context & Application
The acoustic scale plays a pivotal role in twentieth-century tonal music. Debussy frequently uses it as a transition between diatonic and whole-tone collections (Section 4.7). In jazz, it is the basis of the "lydian dominant" chord sound. Its position on the scale lattice — between diatonic and the transpositionally symmetrical scales — makes it a natural bridge between tonal and post-tonal harmonic worlds.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking the acoustic scale is just a mode of the melodic minor
  **Correction**: While it contains the same pitch classes as the ascending melodic minor (starting from a different degree), Tymoczko treats it as an independent scale type defined by its internal intervallic structure

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Why "acoustic"?
  **Clarification**: The first 13 harmonics of C produce approximately C-C-G-C-E-G-Bb-C-D-E-F#-G-A; the acoustic scale is the best equal-tempered approximation to these seven pitch classes.
