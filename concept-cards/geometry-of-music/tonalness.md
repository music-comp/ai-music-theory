# Quick Definition
Tonalness, orderedness, and pleasantness are three perceptual dimensions that psychologists have identified as components of the experience of consonance and dissonance.

# Core Definition
Research in music perception has identified multiple perceptual dimensions underlying the experience of consonance. Tonalness refers to the degree to which a sound is heard as having a clear pitch; orderedness refers to the sense of regularity and predictability; pleasantness refers to the hedonic quality of the sound. These dimensions are partially but not completely correlated: a sound can be tonal (clear pitch) without being particularly pleasant, for example. Tymoczko draws on this perceptual research to ground his concept of acoustic consonance in empirical psychology rather than purely in acoustics or music theory.

# Key Properties
1. Multiple perceptual dimensions underlie the consonance/dissonance experience

3. They provide an empirical, perception-based approach to consonance
4. Western listeners tend to agree broadly on these dimensions

## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Tonalness: Does the sound have a clear, identifiable pitch?
2. Orderedness: Does the sound seem regular and structured?
3. Pleasantness: Does the sound produce a positive hedonic response?

# Context & Application
These perceptual dimensions provide empirical support for Tymoczko's claims about acoustic consonance as a component of tonality. By grounding consonance in perception research, Tymoczko avoids purely theoretical or culturally relativist accounts of consonance and connects his theory to the broader scientific study of auditory perception.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 25-29): Tymoczko cites psychological research showing that listeners across different backgrounds agree on the relative consonance of intervals, with octaves and fifths perceived as more consonant than seconds and tritones.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating these three dimensions as identical or perfectly correlated
  **Correction**: They are partially independent perceptual dimensions

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Equating "tonalness" (a perceptual dimension) with "tonality" (the musical system)
  **Clarification**: Tonalness is a property of individual sounds; tonality is a property of musical passages involving all five components
