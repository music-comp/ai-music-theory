# Quick Definition
Conjunct melodic motion is the tendency for melodies to move by small intervals (steps) rather than large ones (leaps), forming smooth, connected lines.

# Core Definition
One of the five basic components of tonality identified by Tymoczko. Conjunct melodic motion means that melodies tend to move by short distances, with each note close to its predecessor in pitch space. This property is observed across virtually all musical cultures and styles, and is fundamental to the perception of coherent melodic lines. Tymoczko describes it as the horizontal or contrapuntal dimension of musical coherence.

# Key Properties
1. Melodies tend to move by short distances in pitch space
2. This property is cross-cultural and nearly universal in human music
3. Small melodic intervals create a sense of connectedness between successive notes
4. Conjunct motion is the melodic counterpart to harmonic consistency

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Move each successive note by a small interval (typically a semitone or whole tone)
2. Minimize the distance between successive pitches
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Check whether successive notes in a melody are close in pitch space
2. Predominantly stepwise motion indicates conjunct melodic motion

# Context & Application
Conjunct melodic motion is one of the basic constraints that shapes tonal music. It operates as a horizontal (melodic) principle that interacts with vertical (harmonic) principles. In counterpoint, the requirement for conjunct motion in individual voices constrains the voice leadings available between chords. This principle applies across the "extended common practice" spanning medieval through contemporary Western music, as well as much non-Western music.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming conjunct motion means only diatonic steps
  **Correction**: Conjunct motion refers to small distances in pitch space generally, including chromatic semitones

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Conflating conjunct melodic motion with voice leading
  **Clarification**: Conjunct melodic motion describes single melodic lines; voice leading describes how multiple voices move simultaneously between chords
