# Quick Definition
Geometric representations (two- and three-dimensional lattices) that map the voice-leading relationships among scales, used to visualize and analyze how twentieth-century composers navigate between scalar collections.

# Core Definition
Scale lattices represent scales as points and single-semitone voice leadings as edges, creating a geometric map of scalar relationships. In twentieth-century analysis, these lattices reveal that composers systematically explore compact regions of scale space, moving between nearby collections rather than jumping randomly. The lattices show that diatonic, acoustic, harmonic, whole-tone, and octatonic scales form an interconnected network. Composers like Debussy, Janacek, Shostakovich, and Reich trace characteristic paths through this space, and the lattice clarifies at a glance relationships that would be tedious to describe verbally.

# Prerequisites
- Familiarity with the basic scale lattice from Chapter 4
- Understanding of single-semitone scale voice leading

# Key Properties
1. Scales connected by single-semitone voice leading are adjacent on the lattice
2. Modulatory journeys trace paths through the lattice
3. Tonic and secondary scalar regions appear as compact clusters
4. The lattice extends to include nondiatonic scales (acoustic, harmonic, whole-tone-plus-one)
5. Flatward motion on the lattice corresponds to lowering scale degrees
6. Different composers explore characteristic regions and paths

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Place familiar scales as nodes in a graph
2. Connect scales differing by exactly one semitone
3. Arrange so that adjacent scales are visually close
4. The zigzag of diatonic fifths forms the central axis
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Extract all scales used in a passage or piece
2. Plot them on the lattice
3. Look for compact regions, characteristic paths, and systematic exploration

# Context & Application
Scale lattices are the primary analytical tool for scale-first composition. They reveal commonalities across diverse styles: Debussy, Janacek, Shostakovich, and Reich all trace similar kinds of paths, suggesting a shared twentieth-century common practice.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating the lattice as prescribing how composers should navigate
  **Correction**: The lattice describes possibilities; composers navigate freely, though they tend toward compact regions

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing the scale lattice with chord space
  **Clarification**: Scale lattices operate in seven-dimensional chord space (for seven-note scales), distinct from the three- or four-dimensional spaces for triads and seventh chords
