# Quick Definition
Chopin's F minor Mazurka Op. 68 No. 4 embodies an "open form" — a set of partially determining rules where dominant seventh chords descend semitonally through staggered voice-leading, with the order of individual voice descents free but the constraint that the root always descends last.

# Core Definition
The F minor Mazurka moves through a series of dominant seventh chords, each transformed into the next by a staggered sequence of single-semitone descents. The "directions for improvisation": (1) Begin with a dominant seventh chord; (2) Successively lower the third, fifth, and seventh by semitone, in any order, eventually producing a diminished seventh; (3) Lower the note that was the root, producing a new dominant seventh a semitone below; (4) Repeat. What is fixed is the descending chromatic sequence and the constraint that bass descends last; what is free is the order of the other three voices' descents. This "bonus rule" allows multiple notes to descend simultaneously. The resulting harmonies — dominant sevenths, diminished sevenths, minor sevenths, half-diminished sevenths, and French sixths — are all tertian, because the root-last constraint prevents non-tertian sonorities.

# Key Properties
1. Bass (root) always descends last — this ensures tertian harmonies throughout
2. The order of other voices' descents is free (combinatorial possibilities)
3. Produces only dominant 7ths, diminished 7ths, minor 7ths, half-diminished 7ths, French 6ths
4. Each "cycle" moves from dominant 7th through diminished 7th to next dominant 7th
5. The second phrase repeats the sequence with different embellishments — true "open form"
6. The geometric representation is a cube (one of the tesseract's constituent cubes)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Start with any dominant seventh chord
2. Lower third, fifth, or seventh by semitone (choose freely)
3. Lower the remaining two in any order
4. Lower the root to reach the next dominant seventh (a semitone below)
5. Repeat; optionally skip intermediate chords by lowering multiple notes simultaneously

## To Identify/Recognize:
1. A sequence of seventh chords descending overall by semitone
2. Individual voices descending at different rates
3. All resulting chords are tertian (stacks of thirds)
4. The pattern is repeated but varies in detail

# Context & Application
This analysis challenges standard formal categories: the music is neither an exact sequence (repeated verbatim) nor free composition. It resembles the "open-ended musical game" more associated with twentieth-century music, yet predates it by over a century. Chopin was likely an improvising composer, and these rules may capture something of how he actually thought at the piano. The open form also connects the Mazurka to the E minor Prelude, which uses virtually the same voice-leading procedures but with different cycle connections.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Analyzing the Mazurka as a conventional sequence
  **Correction**: It is an open form — the underlying pattern is fixed but the surface detail varies freely

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the constraint is arbitrary
  **Clarification**: The root-last constraint has a precise musical function — it ensures all intermediate harmonies are tertian
