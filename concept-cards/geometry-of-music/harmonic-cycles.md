# Quick Definition
A harmonic cycle is a sequence of rightward motions from the tonic along the chain of descending thirds, followed by a leftward return to I — the basic unit of harmonic motion in functional tonality.

# Core Definition
In Tymoczko's thirds-based model, a harmonic cycle begins at the tonic (I), moves rightward along the chain I-vi-IV-ii-vii°-V by any number of steps, and then returns leftward to I via one of the permitted arrows (typically V->I, vii°->I, or IV->I). Classical pieces consist largely of concatenated harmonic cycles. The model predicts that I-V-I is the most fundamental cycle, with I-ii-V-I, I-IV-V-I, I-IV-I, and I-vii°-I being the next most common. Statistical analysis of Bach chorales and Mozart piano sonatas confirms these predictions.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of the thirds-based harmonic grammar and its rightward/leftward motion rules

# Key Properties
1. I-V-I is overwhelmingly the most common cycle (1026 instances in Mozart sonatas)
2. All common cycles conform to the thirds-based model
3. Cycles are concatenated "like beads on a string" in traditional harmonic analysis
4. The pluralist Schenkerian view adds a recursive nesting interpretation on top of this concatenated view
5. Minor-mode cycles follow essentially the same pattern as major-mode cycles

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Begin on I
2. Move rightward by any number of steps: vi, IV, ii, vii°, V
3. Return to I via a permitted leftward arrow
4. Optionally insert applied dominants or cadential I-six-four before V

## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Identify the tonic chord at the beginning and end
2. Verify that intermediate chords move generally rightward on the chain
3. Confirm that the return to I uses a standard dominant or subdominant resolution

# Context & Application
Harmonic cycles are the building blocks of tonal form. Traditional harmonic analysis consists primarily of identifying and labeling these cycles. While Schenkerian analysis views the same passages through a recursive lens (nesting cycles within cycles), Tymoczko's model treats cycles as a first-order description of tonal syntax that is both accurate and pedagogically efficient.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Expecting every passage to form a complete cycle
  **Correction**: Sequences, chromatic passages, and special idioms may not conform to the cycle model

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking harmonic cycles and Schenkerian levels are competing descriptions
  **Clarification**: For pluralist Schenkerians, they are complementary — cycles describe harmonic grammar, Schenkerian analysis adds contrapuntal depth
