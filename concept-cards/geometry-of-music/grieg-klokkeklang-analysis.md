# Quick Definition
Grieg's "Klokkeklang" (Bell Ringing) Op. 54 No. 6 demonstrates the subset technique with three fixed notes (D, A, E) and four mobile notes creating five different scalar collections, from diatonic to harmonic minor.

# Core Definition
The piece uses open fifths and a C-G/F-C drone to create an austere soundworld evoking distant bells. Three notes (D, A, E) remain fixed throughout while four others (Bb/B, F/F#, C/C#, G/G#) appear in either inflected or uninflected forms. The play of inflections suggests five scalar collections: C diatonic, D diatonic, A diatonic, F diatonic, and D melodic minor, all contained within two adjacent cubes of the scale lattice. The piece progresses from a neutral "white-note" state to a climactic section presenting all four inflections within eleven measures, like participants in a pistol duel walking away from each other.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of the subset technique

# Key Properties
1. Three fixed pitches: D, A, E (stacked fifths)
2. Four mobile pitches: Bb/B, F/F#, C/C#, G/G#
3. Five resultant scales contained in two adjacent lattice cubes
4. Progressive separation of the two diatonic regions
5. Algorithmic echo/repetition structure evoking change-ringing

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Identify the fixed pitches in the ostinato (D-A-E)
2. Track which form of each mobile pitch appears in each passage
3. Determine the resulting scale for each section

# Context & Application
This 1891 piece is one of the earliest clear examples of the subset technique, predating the twentieth-century composers who use it more systematically. It connects to Stravinsky's "Petit airs" and Reich's City Life through shared compositional concerns.
