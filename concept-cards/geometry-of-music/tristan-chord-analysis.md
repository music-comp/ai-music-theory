# Quick Definition
Wagner's Tristan prelude systematically explores all eight most efficient voice leadings from half-diminished seventh to dominant seventh chords, using voice crossings to accommodate the ascending-third leitmotif while the underlying "background" voice leadings are maximally efficient.

# Core Definition
The eight most efficient voice leadings from Fø7 to the twelve dominant sevenths connect to the eight nearest dominant sevenths in four-note chord space. They pair up: two map root to root, two map root to third, two map root to fifth, two map root to seventh. In each pair, one involves predominantly descending motion, the other ascending. The prelude's opening measures present a systematic demonstration of these possibilities: mm. 1-3 (Fø7->E7, root to root), mm. 6-7 (A#ø7->G7, root to root transposed by minor third), mm. 10-11 (Dø7->B7, root to third). Wagner's voice leadings have crossings (the ascending-third Sehnsucht motif), but removing them reveals maximally efficient background voice leadings. Wagner uses all eight shortest pathways throughout the opera, employs tritone substitution between half-diminished chords, and reproduces Chopin-like quasi-sequences in the climax.

# Key Properties
1. Eight most efficient voice leadings from any half-diminished seventh to dominant sevenths
2. They connect to the eight nearest dominants in four-note space
3. Grouped in pairs by which note maps to root, third, fifth, or seventh
4. Wagner uses ALL eight types throughout the opera
5. Voice crossings are surface decorations of efficient background voice leadings
6. The prelude uses AAB form ("bar form") at multiple structural levels
7. French sixth replaces half-diminished at mm. 36ff (minimal perturbation)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Start with Fø7 = {F, Ab, Cb, Eb}
2. For each of the 12 dominant sevenths, find the most efficient crossing-free voice leading
3. Seven require 2 semitones total, one requires 4, four require 6
4. Focus on the 8 requiring 2 or 4 semitones
5. Classify by which note in the half-diminished maps to which note in the dominant

## To Identify/Recognize:
1. A half-diminished seventh resolving to a dominant seventh
2. Remove voice crossings to reveal the background voice leading
3. Classify the resolution by root-to-X mapping
4. Check if the dominant seventh is among the 8 nearest in four-note space

# Context & Application
Tymoczko's analysis demonstrates that the fundamental logic of Tristan is contrapuntal — the geometry of four-note chord space determines which resolutions Wagner uses. The competition between conventional (iiø7-V7) and Tristan-chord resolutions at the climax (mm. 81-83) symbolizes the opera's thematic conflict between social propriety and passion. The analysis connects Wagner to Chopin through shared tesseract geometry and to jazz through tritone substitution of half-diminished chords.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Analyzing Tristan solely in terms of tonal function
  **Correction**: The fundamental logic is contrapuntal; functional labels apply to some but not all of the chord's resolutions

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the voice crossings invalidate a voice-leading analysis
  **Clarification**: The crossings are surface embellishments of efficient background voice leadings; Wagner's harmonic choices are guided by the background structure
