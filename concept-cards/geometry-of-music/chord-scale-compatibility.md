# Quick Definition
The principle that chords can be understood as subsets of familiar five-to-eight-note scales, with the chord and scale being "compatible" when one is contained within the other.

# Core Definition
Chord-scale compatibility is the foundational principle underlying three major twentieth-century compositional techniques (chord-first, scale-first, and subset). Jazz theorists articulate this as the idea that every chord implies (or is compatible with) one or more scales. This creates a two-tiered harmonic structure where either chords or scales can take the lead in determining musical progression. Scales are understood not merely as byproducts of chordal accidentals but as recognizable objects with their own syntactical role. This "musical twofoldness" distinguishes twentieth-century tonal practice from earlier approaches.

# Prerequisites
- Basic understanding of scales and modes
- Familiarity with chord construction (triads, seventh chords, extended chords)

# Key Properties
1. Creates a two-tiered structure: chords as subsets of scales
2. Either element (chord or scale) can take the compositional lead
3. Central to jazz pedagogy and twentieth-century tonal composition
4. Scales must be "virtuous" (possessing desirable scalar qualities) rather than merely "parasitic" (determined only by chordal accidentals)
5. Synthesizes functional harmony with scalar thinking

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Identify the chord tones present at a given moment
2. Determine which familiar scales contain all those chord tones
3. Select the most appropriate scale based on context, voice leading, or desired color
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Look for passages where melodic content extends beyond chord tones into a consistent scalar collection
2. Check whether the non-chord tones belong to a recognizable scale containing the chord

# Context & Application
Chord-scale compatibility is the theoretical foundation of jazz improvisation pedagogy and appears in virtually every jazz textbook. It also describes practices found in impressionism (Debussy, Ravel), modernism (Stravinsky, Bartok), postminimalism (Reich, Adams), and rock music. The principle represents a twentieth-century "common practice" that crosses stylistic boundaries.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating scales as mere byproducts of chordal accidentals
  **Correction**: Scales should be "virtuous" -- possessing independent scalar qualities like even octave division and consonant interval content

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking chord-scale compatibility is uniquely a jazz concept
  **Clarification**: While codified by jazz theorists, the principle operates in impressionism, minimalism, rock, and other twentieth-century styles
