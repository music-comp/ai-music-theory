# Quick Definition
Dominant voicings that combine the essential notes (root, third, seventh) in the bass with a triad foreign to the underlying key in the upper voices, creating polychordal effects that can be categorized by tritone substitution.

# Core Definition
Upper structure voicings place the root, third, and seventh of a dominant chord in the lower register and a foreign triad in the upper register. Voicings containing F# (creating a chromatic cluster with F-G) or C (prematurely resolving the leading tone) are eliminated. The remaining options can be organized by upper-voice tritone substitution, which provides a convenient way to remember and categorize them. Soloists create polychordal effects by emphasizing upper-structure triads (e.g., prominent E major or Eb major arpeggios over G7). Although these could be described as altered dominant chords, they evoke a sensation of polytonality.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of tritone substitution and playing outside

# Key Properties
1. Combine root, 3rd, 7th in bass with foreign triad in upper voices
2. Exclude voicings with F# (chromatic cluster) or C (premature resolution) over G7
3. Organized by upper-voice tritone substitution
4. Create polytonal effects within functional harmony
5. Soloists use upper-structure arpeggios to "play outside"

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Place root, 3rd, 7th of dominant in lower register
2. Add a major or minor triad in the upper register
3. Eliminate triads containing the avoid note or creating chromatic clusters
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Listen for a clear triadic sound that doesn't match the underlying harmony
2. Check whether the bass contains root, 3rd, 7th of a dominant

# Context & Application
Upper structure voicings are standard in modern jazz piano and are taught as a systematic approach to dominant chord coloring.
