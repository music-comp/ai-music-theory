# Quick Definition
Harmonic consistency is the tendency for the chords in a passage of music to be audibly similar to one another, typically belonging to the same type (e.g., all major triads, all dominant sevenths).

# Core Definition
The third of Tymoczko's five basic components of tonality. Harmonic consistency means that harmonies used in a piece or passage tend to be structurally related — transpositionally related (same chord type), inversionally related (same set class), or at least approximately so. This creates a sense of vertical coherence across a musical passage. Tymoczko treats it as the vertical or harmonic dimension of musical coherence, complementing the horizontal dimension of conjunct melodic motion.

# Key Properties
1. Chords in a passage tend to belong to the same type or related types
2. Creates a sense of harmonic unity across a passage
3. Can be understood in terms of transposition, inversion, or near-equivalence
4. Exists in tension with conjunct melodic motion — both cannot always be maximally satisfied simultaneously
5. Different styles enforce harmonic consistency to different degrees

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Choose a chord type (e.g., major triad, dominant seventh)
2. Use transpositions (and possibly inversions) of that chord throughout the passage
3. Allow approximate relationships when exact transposition is not possible
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Check whether successive chords in a passage belong to the same chord type
2. Look for transpositional or inversional relationships between chords
3. Assess whether the chords share similar interval content

# Context & Application
Harmonic consistency is a fundamental organizing principle in Western music. In common-practice tonality, passages typically use major and minor triads (related by inversion, hence same set class). In jazz, dominant seventh chords may predominate. In some twentieth-century music, a single set class may govern harmonic choices. The tension between harmonic consistency and conjunct melodic motion is a central concern of Chapter 2, where Tymoczko shows that only nearly symmetrical chords can satisfy both constraints simultaneously.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming harmonic consistency requires identical chord types
  **Correction**: Approximate relationships (near-transposition, near-inversion) count; the concept exists on a continuum

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking harmonic consistency means chords must share common tones
  **Clarification**: Harmonic consistency is about structural similarity (same type), not about shared pitch content
