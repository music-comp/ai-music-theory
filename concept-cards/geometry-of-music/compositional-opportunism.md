# Quick Definition
Compositional opportunism is Tymoczko's explanatory principle that composers, like mountaineers ascending a cliff, naturally take advantage of whatever structural possibilities present themselves — the five tonal components offer "handholds and footholds" that guide musical development along paths of least resistance without determining it.

# Core Definition
Tymoczko uses the analogy of a mountaineer ascending a cliff to explain how music history follows structural constraints without being determined by them. The five components of tonality impose nontrivial constraints on composers' choices, "boxing them in on some sides while allowing them to move freely in other directions." Just as the structure of a rock face naturally suggests certain routes, offering handholds and footholds that guide a sensible climber, the constraints of tonal music suggest certain developments: triads emerge as natural solutions for three-voice consonant counterpoint; fourth progressions prove most flexible for harmonizing stepwise melodies; chromatic voice leading naturally produces major-third and minor-third systems; nondiatonic scales solve the chord-scale problem inherent in chromaticism. Tymoczko explicitly rejects determinism: alternate histories are possible (Western music might never have prohibited parallel fifths), but the actual history often follows the "path of least resistance."

# Key Properties
1. Composers exploit structural possibilities, like mountaineers using handholds
2. The five tonal components create constraints that suggest (but don't determine) certain developments
3. Alternate histories are possible but actual history often follows paths of least resistance
4. Explains recurring patterns: triads, fourth progressions, chromatic systems, scalar solutions
5. Not a deterministic or Hegelian view of history
6. Applies at both historical (Western music development) and individual (a composer exploring chromatic space) levels

# Context & Application
The principle operates at two levels. Historically, it explains major developments: Renaissance triads as a solution to three-voice consonance, functional harmony as a systematization of fourth progressions, scalar music as a solution to the chord-scale problem. Individually, it explains why chromatic composers like Chopin and Schubert consistently produce major-third-related triads and minor-third-related seventh chords — the geometry of chord space ensures that intuitive exploration follows these paths. The principle frames music history neither as inevitable progress nor as arbitrary cultural convention, but as rational exploitation of mathematical structure.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 229-230): The mountaineer analogy — structure of the rock face suggests certain routes without determining them; similarly, the five tonal components suggest certain compositional developments.

**Example 2** (p. 238): Chromatic composers at the piano "simply sat...and exploited the most efficient voice leadings that came to hand" — the geometry of chord space ensures this produces the major- and minor-third systems.

**Example 3** (p. 220-221): Triads as "unbidden byproduct" of preferring three-pitch-class consonances — a clear case of structural constraints guiding development.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Interpreting compositional opportunism as historical determinism
  **Correction**: Tymoczko explicitly allows for alternate histories and contingency; the principle explains tendencies, not inevitabilities

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the principle implies composers are conscious of the structural constraints
  **Clarification**: Composers may exploit structural possibilities intuitively; the principle describes outcomes, not conscious decision-making
