# Quick Definition
Wagner's Tristan voice leadings are not themselves maximally efficient (they include crossings), but removing the crossings reveals an efficient "background" — the ascending-third Sehnsucht motif decorates what would otherwise be a stationary voice, creating crossing where two voices take the long way to their destinations.

# Core Definition
The melody in Tristan climbs yearningly from G# to B while the tenor swoops from B down to G# — if each voice remained stationary, we would have a two-semitone voice leading between Fø7 and E7, nicely modeled by the hypercube. Wagner deliberately rejects this efficient option, preferring the crossing that yields the ascending-third leitmotif. The hypothesis is that Wagner's harmonic choices are influenced by voice-leading relationships not directly manifested by the surface: he consistently avoids the four most distant dominant sevenths (requiring 6-semitone voice leadings) in favor of those closer in four-note space. The crossing always involves three- or four-semitone motion in two separate voices. Removing crossings throughout the opera consistently reveals efficient background voice leadings.

# Key Properties
1. Surface voice leadings have crossings; background voice leadings are efficient
2. The ascending-third motif is always one of the crossing voices
3. Crossings involve 3-4 semitone motion in two voices simultaneously
4. Wagner avoids dominant sevenths requiring 6-semitone total voice leading
5. The distinction between surface and background is an old analytical observation (Mitchell 1973, Gauldin 1997)
6. Later in the opera, the resolutions often appear WITHOUT crossings

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Start with an efficient voice leading from half-diminished to dominant seventh
2. Choose two voices and redirect them: one ascends by minor third (the motif), the other descends by the same amount
3. The net voice-leading distance increases, but the melodic motif is achieved

## To Identify/Recognize:
1. A half-diminished to dominant progression where voices cross
2. Remove the crossing by having each voice take the shorter path
3. The result should be a 2-semitone total voice leading

# Context & Application
This analysis provides a way to reconcile Wagner's evident interest in voice-leading efficiency with the surface non-efficiency of his actual voice leadings. It suggests that the creative process involved choosing efficient background voice leadings and then decorating them with the characteristic ascending-third motif.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Concluding that voice-leading analysis is irrelevant because the surface is not efficient
  **Correction**: The surface non-efficiency is a motivated decoration of an efficient background

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking voice crossings always indicate non-functional voice leading
  **Clarification**: In Tristan, crossings are a specific compositional technique that decorates but does not eliminate the underlying contrapuntal logic
