# Quick Definition
Rather than a binary tonal/atonal distinction, tonality exists on a multi-dimensional spectrum defined by the degree to which each of the five components is present.

# Core Definition
Tymoczko rejects the binary classification of music as either "tonal" or "atonal" (or "post-tonal"). Instead, he proposes that tonality is a matter of degree along multiple dimensions corresponding to the five components. A piece can exhibit strong conjunct melodic motion and harmonic consistency while lacking centricity; another might have centricity and limited macroharmony without acoustic consonance. This multi-dimensional view explains how medieval music, common-practice tonality, impressionism, jazz, and much twentieth-century music can all be "tonal" in different ways and to different degrees.

# Key Properties
1. Tonality is not binary but exists on a continuum
2. The continuum is multi-dimensional (one dimension per component)
3. Different styles occupy different regions of this space
4. Even "atonal" music may exhibit some tonal features
5. Enables meaningful comparison of vastly different musical repertoires

# Context & Application
This framework dissolves several long-standing debates in music theory. It explains why "post-tonal" is an unsatisfying label for music ranging from Arvo Pärt to Varèse, since these composers inhabit very different regions of the tonal spectrum. It also provides a basis for Tymoczko's concept of the "extended common practice" — the broad tradition of music sharing significant tonal features across centuries and styles.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating the spectrum as a single linear scale from "less tonal" to "more tonal"
  **Correction**: The spectrum is multi-dimensional; two pieces can be equally "tonal" in overall degree but in very different ways

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking that more components present = "better" music
  **Clarification**: The spectrum is descriptive, not evaluative; different positions yield different but equally valid musical possibilities
