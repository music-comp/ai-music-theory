# Quick Definition
A chain of nearly even chords (near interval cycles) linked by single-step voice leading, generalizing the familiar circle of diatonic fifths. It lies as close as possible to the center of chord space and forms the core of Type 2 voice-leading lattices.

# Core Definition
When the number of notes in a chord and the number of notes in the containing scale are relatively prime (coprime), the most nearly even chords form "near interval cycles" — circular sequences of notes where all but one interval is the same size. These can be linked by a chain of single-step voice leadings that shifts the position of the odd interval, producing a "generalized circle of fifths." The familiar circle of diatonic fifths is one instance: diatonic scales are near interval cycles (all fifths except one tritone), linked by single-semitone voice leading (F -> F#, C -> C#, etc.). Similar circles exist for diatonic fifths within seven-note space, diatonic triads, and any other near interval cycle. The generalized circle of fifths lies as close as possible to the center of chord space and forms the central zigzag of the Type 2 voice-leading lattice.

# Prerequisites
- Voice-leading lattices (Type 2)
- Nearly even chords as near interval cycles

# Key Properties
1. Links near interval cycles by single-step voice leading
2. Lies as close as possible to the center of chord space
3. Forms a zigzag pattern (2D or 3D depending on the lattice)
4. The rest of the lattice is generated by rearranging voice leadings on this central chain
5. Each step shifts the position of the "odd" interval in the near interval cycle

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Identify the near interval cycle (most even chords of the given size in the given scale)
2. Find the single-step voice leading that shifts the odd interval to the next position
3. Chain these voice leadings together until the cycle completes

# Context & Application
The generalized circle of fifths reveals that the familiar diatonic circle of fifths is not an isolated phenomenon but an instance of a universal pattern. Every combination of chord size and scale size (when coprime) produces an analogous structure. This concept is particularly important for the scale lattice used in Chapters 4, 6, and 9.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking the circle of fifths is unique to diatonic scales
  **Correction**: Every coprime pair of chord-size and scale-size produces an analogous circle

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Is this the same as the circle of fifths for keys?
  **Clarification**: When applied to seven-note scales in 12-note space, it is exactly the familiar circle of fifths for key signatures. But the concept generalizes far beyond this single instance.
