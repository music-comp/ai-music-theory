# Quick Definition
Acoustic consonance is the perceptual quality of certain combinations of pitches sounding stable, restful, and pleasing, as opposed to dissonant combinations that sound harsh or unstable.

# Core Definition
The second of Tymoczko's five basic components of tonality. Acoustic consonance arises when the partials (overtones) of two or more pitches are aligned or sufficiently far apart to avoid interference. Following Helmholtz, dissonance occurs when partials are close but not perfectly aligned, creating a sense of "roughness." Consonant intervals have fundamental frequencies related by simple whole-number ratios: 2:1 (octave), 3:2 (perfect fifth), 4:3 (perfect fourth), 5:4 (major third), 6:5 (minor third). Larger chords are consonant when they contain a preponderance of consonant intervals, which requires their notes to be relatively evenly distributed in pitch-class space.

# Key Properties
1. Based on the alignment of overtone partials between pitches
2. Consonant intervals have frequencies in simple whole-number ratios
3. Western listeners broadly agree on which chords sound consonant vs. dissonant
4. Highly consonant chords divide the octave nearly evenly
5. Consonance is somewhat dependent on timbre (overtone content of the sound)

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Combine pitches whose fundamental frequencies are related by simple ratios
2. Ensure the resulting chord divides the pitch-class circle relatively evenly
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Listen for stability, restfulness, and lack of "roughness"
2. Check whether the intervals between notes correspond to simple frequency ratios

# Context & Application
Acoustic consonance serves as a foundation for harmonic practice across Western music. Consonant sonorities typically function as points of rest or arrival, while dissonant sonorities create tension and instability. The remarkable connection between consonance and near evenness means that the chords chosen for their pleasant sound are also the very chords that permit efficient voice leading between transpositions — a coincidence that made Western counterpoint possible.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating consonance as purely subjective or culturally determined
  **Correction**: While perception varies, acoustic consonance has a physical basis in overtone alignment that appears to be cross-cultural

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Equating acoustic consonance with musical stability
  **Clarification**: While correlated, they are distinct; acoustically consonant chords can function as unstable in certain harmonic contexts
