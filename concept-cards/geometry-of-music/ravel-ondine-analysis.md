# Quick Definition
The opening of Ravel's "Ondine" from Gaspard de la nuit exemplifies scale-first composition, progressing through a series of single-semitone voice leadings between closely related scales.

# Core Definition
The opening begins with a shimmering wash in the C# mode of B acoustic (C#-D#-E#-F#-G#-A-B, or "C# mixolydian b6"). A series of single-semitone changes creates C# harmonic major, C# diatonic, and G# acoustic. The passage has a distinctively scalar logic, as if Ravel deliberately moved between closely related scales by changing one or two notes at a time. When plotted on the scale lattice, these collections systematically explore a small region of scale space.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of scale-first composition
- Familiarity with single-semitone scale voice leading

# Key Properties
1. Begins with B acoustic / C# mixolydian b6
2. Single-semitone changes produce successive scales
3. Many collections are incomplete (typical for extended passages)
4. Scales explore a compact region on the scale lattice
5. The logic is distinctively scalar rather than chordal

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Extract the pitch collections measure by measure
2. Identify the closest familiar scale for each collection
3. Note that successive scales differ by single-semitone changes
4. Plot on the scale lattice to confirm compact exploration of scale space

# Context & Application
This passage is presented as the paradigmatic example of scale-first composition, contrasting with Scriabin's chord-first approach. It demonstrates Debussy and Ravel's "distinctively scalar logic" that Tymoczko considers central to impressionism.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Trying to explain the passage in purely chordal terms
  **Correction**: The scalar logic is primary; chords arise from the scales, not vice versa

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming all Ravel is scale-first
  **Clarification**: Ravel uses both scale-first and chord-first techniques in different contexts
