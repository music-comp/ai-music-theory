# Quick Definition
Harmonic minor and harmonic major scales extend the four central jazz scales (diatonic, acoustic, whole-tone, octatonic) for specific contexts: harmonic minor for minor-key dominant chords, harmonic major over major-seventh tonic chords, providing the hexatonic and harmonic collections needed for the rare chord types not covered by the four main scales.

# Core Definition
While the four central scales (diatonic, acoustic, whole-tone, octatonic) suffice for most jazz contexts, harmonic minor and major fill specific gaps. Harmonic minor is used for minor-key dominant chords (as in classical practice), while harmonic major can be played over a major-seventh tonic chord. The acoustic scale also appears over ii-half-diminished-7 in "locrian #2" mode. Together with pentatonic and hexatonic scales, these provide the seven scale types needed to contain every nonchromatic chord (Chapter 4). Only four nonchromatic set classes of five or fewer notes are not contained in the four central scales, making the harmonic collections rarely needed in practice.

# Key Properties
1. Harmonic minor: used over minor-key V7 chords
2. Harmonic major: used over Imaj7 chords
3. Acoustic in locrian #2 mode: used over ii-half-diminished-7
4. These supplement the four central scales for rare chord types
5. Pentatonic scales add "fourthy" flavor to any chord function

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Listen for the characteristic augmented second in harmonic scales
2. Harmonic minor over dominant: raises 7th in minor context
3. Harmonic major over tonic: lowers 6th in major context

# Context & Application
These supplementary scales complete the jazz chord-scale system, ensuring coverage for virtually all chord types encountered in standard jazz repertoire.
