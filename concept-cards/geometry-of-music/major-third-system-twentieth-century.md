# Quick Definition
The juxtaposition of major-third-related triads, inherited from Schubert but transformed in the twentieth century by replacing major triads with minor triads to create a darker, more ominous quality, appearing across Russian modernism, minimalism, and film music.

# Core Definition
The major-third system (juxtaposing triads related by major third) appears in twentieth-century styles as diverse as Russian modernism, American minimalism, and film music. The progressions are distinctive in replacing Schubert's major triads with minor triads that produce a more ominous and brooding sound, often highlighted by driving or asymmetrical rhythms. A genealogy traces the system from Schubert's Vienna to contemporary America via Russian modernist composers like Prokofiev and Shostakovich, acquiring a darker quality in the process. The voice leadings are mostly parallel but articulate semitonal connections in pitch space.

# Key Properties
1. Triads related by major third (four semitones apart)
2. Twentieth-century versions favor minor triads over major
3. Darker, more ominous quality than Schubert's uses
4. Often accompanied by driving rhythms
5. Genealogy: Schubert -> Prokofiev/Shostakovich -> Glass/Reich/film music
6. Voice leadings articulate semitonal connections despite parallel surfaces

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Look for triads whose roots are four semitones apart
2. Note whether minor triads predominate (twentieth-century style)
3. Check for driving rhythms and darker affect

# Context & Application
This system connects nineteenth-century Romantic harmony to contemporary film and television music, demonstrating a continuous tradition of harmonic practice.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 337-338): Nyman's "The Mood That Passes Through You" -- Bb minor, D major, E major triads.

**Example 2** (p. 338-339): Prokofiev's Overture on Hebrew Themes -- Ab minor, E major, C minor over pulsating eighths.

**Example 3** (p. 339-340): Shostakovich's G minor Prelude, Glass' Einstein on the Beach, Battlestar Galactica opening credits.
