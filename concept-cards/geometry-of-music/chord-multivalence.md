# Quick Definition
Chord multivalence is the property of a chromatic chord (such as the Tristan chord or a dominant seventh) having multiple efficient resolutions to different consonant chords — exploited by Romantic composers to propel harmonic motion while maintaining unity through recurring pivot sonorities.

# Core Definition
A chord is "multivalent" when it can resolve efficiently in multiple different ways to different consonant chords. The Tristan chord (half-diminished seventh) can resolve to eight different dominant seventh chords via maximally efficient voice leading; a dominant seventh can function as a German augmented sixth in another key; a diminished seventh can resolve to four different keys. Romantic composers exploit this multivalence structurally: Brahms' Op. 76 No. 4 returns repeatedly to the Tristan chord and Eb minor triad, resolving them differently each time. The multivalence propels harmonic motion (each resolution leads to a different tonal region) while endowing the music with harmonic unity (the same chord keeps returning). At the climax of the Tristan prelude (mm. 81-83), two competing resolutions of the Tristan chord are presented explicitly — a dramatic statement of the chord's multivalence.

# Key Properties
1. A single chord can resolve efficiently to multiple different consonant chords
2. This creates a "choose your own adventure" quality in harmonic progression
3. Recurring use of a multivalent pivot chord creates both motion and unity
4. The number of efficient resolutions depends on the chord's position in voice-leading space
5. Composers can make the competition between resolutions dramatically significant

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Choose a dissonant chord (dominant seventh, half-diminished, etc.)
2. Catalog its efficient resolutions using the voice-leading framework
3. Use different resolutions at different points in a piece
4. Return to the same chord to create unity

## To Identify/Recognize:
1. A recurring dissonant chord that resolves differently each time
2. The different resolutions lead to different tonal regions
3. The chord serves as a structural pivot point

# Context & Application
Multivalence is the mechanism by which chromatic harmony achieves both variety and coherence without relying on traditional tonal function. It is the key compositional principle shared by Brahms, Wagner, and Schoenberg in their chromatic works.
