# Quick Definition
The technique of mapping music from one scale type to another while preserving the scalar step pattern, used in twentieth-century music to create thematic connections across different scalar contexts.

# Core Definition
Interscalar transposition maps the notes of one scale onto the notes of another in a way that preserves the ordering of scale degrees. In Debussy's "Fetes," the relevant interscalar transposition is labeled under each voice leading between half-diminished and dominant seventh chords, showing how the root of one chord maps to the third, fifth, seventh, or root of the other. In Janacek's "On an Overgrown Path," phrase 8 uses interscalar transposition to shift phrase 7 into Eb harmonic major, creating a harmonic recapitulation. The technique exploits all possible ways of mapping one chord type onto another.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of interscalar interval matrices (Appendix D)
- Familiarity with scalar transposition

# Key Properties
1. Preserves scalar step pattern while changing the scale
2. Systematically exploits all possible mappings between chord types
3. Used in both chordal (Debussy) and scalar (Janacek) contexts
4. Labels describe which degree maps to which (root to third, etc.)
5. Connected to the geometry of chord space

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Identify the two scale or chord types being connected
2. Choose which degree of the first maps to which degree of the second
3. Apply the mapping to all voices simultaneously
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Look for voice leadings that systematically connect different chord or scale types
2. Check whether the voice leading preserves a consistent mapping pattern

# Context & Application
Interscalar transposition provides a systematic way to understand how twentieth-century composers connect different chord and scale types, extending the analytical vocabulary beyond simple chromatic and diatonic transposition.

# Examples
**Example 1** (p. 335, Fig. 9.2.3): Debussy's "Fetes" -- the music exploits all different types of crossing-free voice leadings between half-diminished and dominant seventh chords.

**Example 2** (p. 344-346): Janacek uses interscalar transposition to shift a phrase from one scale type to another, creating a harmonic recapitulation.
