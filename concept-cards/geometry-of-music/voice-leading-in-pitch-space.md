# Quick Definition
A voice leading in pitch space is a mapping from one collection of pitches to another, specifying exactly how each voice moves from its note in the first chord to its note in the second.

# Core Definition
A voice leading in pitch space represents a mapping from one ordered collection of pitches to another, showing how individual musical voices move between chords. Formally, it is an equivalence class of pairs of basic musical objects generated by uniform applications of the permutation symmetry (uniform P). The notation uses a single arrow: (G2, G3, B3, D4, F4) -> (C3, G3, C4, C4, E4) indicates that the voice sounding G2 moves to C3, the voice sounding G3 stays on G3, and so on. Since uniform permutation is applied, the overall labeling of voices does not matter — what matters is the mapping between specific pitches.

# Key Properties
1. Specifies how each voice moves from one pitch to another
2. The "atomic constituents of musical scores" — basic building blocks of polyphony
3. Uniform P means the overall voice labeling is irrelevant
4. Geometrically: a collection of paths in linear pitch space
5. Specific pitches and registers are preserved (unlike pitch-class voice leadings)
6. Uses single arrow notation: ->

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Assign each voice a starting pitch and an ending pitch
2. The mapping from starting pitches to ending pitches defines the voice leading
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Check whether specific voice-to-voice mappings are indicated
2. If the passage specifies which note moves to which, it is a voice leading

# Context & Application
Voice leadings in pitch space are the most concrete representation of how music moves between chords. They correspond directly to what we see in a musical score — specific notes in specific registers moving to other specific notes. Every instance of polyphonic music can be analyzed as a sequence of pitch-space voice leadings.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking voice leading requires all voices to move
  **Correction**: Some voices can be held (move by zero semitones), which is still a voice leading

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Confusing voice leading with chord progression
  **Clarification**: A voice leading specifies which note moves to which; a chord progression specifies only what chords occur
