# Quick Definition
The eight important scales are connected by networks of efficient (single-semitone or "split/merge") voice leadings. These relationships are organized in a pyramid, with transpositionally symmetrical scales at the top, the four non-diatonic seven-note scales in the middle, and the diatonic at the bottom.

# Core Definition
Section 4.6 maps the voice-leading relationships among the eight scales. The four most even seven-note scales (diatonic, acoustic, harmonic minor, harmonic major) are connected by single-semitone voice leadings on the scale lattice (Figure 4.6.1). A second "circle of fifths" links the non-diatonic scales: A harmonic major -> A harmonic minor -> D acoustic -> E harmonic major -> ... These relationships extend to the transpositionally symmetrical scales through "split" and "merge" operations: whole-tone transforms to acoustic by splitting a note into two chromatic neighbors; octatonic transforms to acoustic by merging a major second; hexatonic transforms to harmonic major/minor by splitting a semitone. The scale pyramid (Figure 4.6.3) organizes these relationships hierarchically: transpositionally symmetrical scales at top, harmonic and acoustic scales in the middle (mediating role), and diatonic at the bottom.

# Key Properties
1. Diatonic circle of fifths: single-semitone connections among diatonic scales
2. Non-diatonic circle: harmonic major -> harmonic minor -> acoustic -> ... (3-step cycle)
3. Split: one note becomes two chromatic neighbors (increases cardinality)
4. Merge: two adjacent notes collapse into one (decreases cardinality)
5. Scale pyramid: symmetric -> non-diatonic seven-note -> diatonic
6. Every acoustic scale shares 6/7 notes with a diatonic, 6/8 with an octatonic, 5/6 with a whole-tone

# Context & Application
These voice-leading relationships are the primary tool for analyzing modulation and scalar change in Chapters 6 and 9. Debussy, Prokofiev, Stravinsky, and Shostakovich all navigate this network, using non-diatonic scales to mediate between diatonic and transpositionally symmetrical collections.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming all scale changes involve single-semitone voice leading
  **Correction**: Some involve split/merge operations or multi-semitone changes

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Is the scale pyramid a hierarchy of importance?
  **Clarification**: No — it organizes scales by evenness and symmetry, not by musical importance or frequency of use
