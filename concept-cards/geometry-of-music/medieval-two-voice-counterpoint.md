# Quick Definition
The earliest Western polyphonic style, exemplified by the eleventh-century treatise Ad Organum Faciendum, features two voices moving stepwise through consonant intervals within a diatonic macroharmony — already exhibiting several of the five components of tonality.

# Core Definition
Tymoczko analyzes the Allelujia Justus et Palma from the eleventh-century treatise Ad Organum Faciendum as the first style in his five-style survey. Despite its simplicity, this music already exemplifies multiple tonal features: (1) conjunct melodic motion with contrary motion preferred; (2) acoustic consonance — steps, sevenths, and tritones are forbidden; thirds/sixths are imperfect consonances for mid-phrase use; unisons, fourths, fifths are perfect consonances for any position; (3) no harmonic consistency in the modern sense — intervals categorized only by consonance; (4) diatonic macroharmony with 7.5 notes (Bb/B-natural as a flexible "mobile" degree); (5) weak centricity with cadences articulated by convergence on unisons or octaves. The Mobius strip provides a "game board" for this style: perfect consonances account for ~90% of intervals, with stepwise or near-stepwise voice leadings ~70% of the time.

# Key Properties
1. Two-voice note-against-note counterpoint
2. Perfect consonances (unisons, fourths, fifths, octaves) predominate (~90%)
3. Imperfect consonances (thirds, sixths) used only mid-phrase
4. No parallel fifths or octaves prohibition yet
5. "Mobile" seventh degree: Bb and B-natural as alternative forms that do not intermix
6. Cadences by contrary stepwise convergence onto unisons/octaves
7. The Mobius strip accurately models the compositional "game board"

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Write two voices using primarily stepwise motion
2. Favor contrary motion; parallel motion is permitted including parallel fifths
3. Use perfect consonances (unisons, 4ths, 5ths, 8ves) as primary vertical intervals
4. Use imperfect consonances (3rds, 6ths) only in mid-phrase positions
5. Cadence by converging stepwise onto a unison or octave
6. Stay within the diatonic collection with flexible Bb/B-natural
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Two voices moving primarily by step
2. Predominance of perfect consonances
3. Convergent cadential motion onto unisons/octaves
4. Diatonic pitch content

# Context & Application
This style demonstrates that the basic two-dimensional coherence of Western music — combining recognizable melodies with consonant harmonies — was present from the very beginning of the polyphonic tradition. Early treatises helped composers satisfy both constraints by listing progressions exhaustively. The Mobius strip provides a far more efficient representation: the graph of perfect consonances linked by stepwise voice leadings encapsulates the essential compositional knowledge. However, the placid character of this music depends not just on consonant intervals but on macroharmonic context — "octatonic organum" using the same consonant intervals sounds like a "black mass" rather than medieval piety.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming medieval music is too simple to be analytically interesting
  **Correction**: The Mobius strip reveals hidden repetitions and structural patterns not visible in notation, such as the echoed wedge shapes in the Allelujia's seventh phrase

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking that consonant intervals alone explain the music's placid character
  **Clarification**: The diatonic macroharmony is equally important; the same intervals within an octatonic collection sound completely different
