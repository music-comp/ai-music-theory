# Quick Definition
Tonal practice combines two components identified by Fétis: "harmonic tonality" (tonic-to-dominant motion along the circle of thirds, where tonal gravity obscures the thirds preference) and "sequential tonality" (sequences where tonal gravity dissipates and the preference for descending-thirds motion becomes visible).

# Core Definition
In harmonic tonality, music moves from tonic to dominant along the circle of descending thirds, forming strong progressions. However, the gravitational attraction of tonic, subdominant, and dominant is strong enough to obscure the underlying thirds structure — progressions like I-V-I and I-IV-I move symmetrically along the circle and betray no preference for strong progressions. In sequential tonality, tonic and dominant lose their attractive power as a single pattern is whirled through diatonic space. Here the preference for strong progressions shines forth more clearly. The circle of thirds plays a distinctive role in each: harmonic tonality uses it to organize tonic-to-dominant motion, while sequential tonality emphasizes short steps along it.

# Key Properties
1. Harmonic tonality: tonal gravity present, thirds preference obscured
2. Sequential tonality: tonal gravity absent, thirds preference visible
3. Both organized by the descending circle of thirds, but differently
4. Harmonic tonality uses the circle for long-range tonic-dominant motion
5. Sequential tonality uses short steps along the circle

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Harmonic tonality: chord progressions oriented toward tonic and dominant, forming cycles
2. Sequential tonality: patterns repeated at different pitch levels, losing tonal center
3. The shift between the two modes often occurs within a single piece

# Context & Application
The distinction clarifies why sequences and nonsequential harmony appear to operate differently while sharing the same underlying structure. It also helps explain why tonal analysis sometimes struggles with sequential passages — they operate under different rules than harmonic cycles.
