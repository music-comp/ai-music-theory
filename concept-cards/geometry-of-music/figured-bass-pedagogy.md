# Quick Definition
Traditional figured-bass pedagogy, from Heinichen through C. P. E. Bach, embeds two simple physical keyboard gestures that together produce all strongly crossing-free four-voice triadic voice leadings — a practical solution to a complex mathematical problem achieved centuries before formal geometric understanding.

# Core Definition
The 3+1 and nonfactorizable schemas correspond to simple physical gestures teachable to young keyboard players. In the 3+1 schema, the right hand moves between complete triads while the bass moves independently. In the nonfactorizable schema, the right hand switches from a complete triad to an incomplete chord (an octave enclosing another note, or an interval with one doubling) while the bass completes the second triad. Heinichen's 1728 treatise always recommends a complete triad in the right hand (the 3+1 schema), while C. P. E. Bach's Essay (1753/1762) discusses the wider range including nonfactorizable alternatives. Together, these two gestures generate all strongly crossing-free four-voice triadic voice leadings. This is a remarkable example of the practical pedagogical tradition solving a complex mathematical problem more than three centuries ago.

# Key Properties
1. Two gestures generate ALL strongly crossing-free four-voice triadic voice leadings
2. 3+1: right hand moves between complete triads, bass independent
3. Nonfactorizable: right hand moves from complete triad to incomplete chord, bass completes
4. Simple enough for improvisational use, yet powerful enough for extraordinary contrapuntal variety
5. Documented in treatises from Heinichen (1728) to C. P. E. Bach (1753/1762) to modern university teaching

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. For 3+1: play complete triads in the right hand, move bass independently
2. For nonfactorizable: play a complete triad, then move right hand to an incomplete chord (octave + note or interval + doubling), bass fills in the missing note

## To Identify/Recognize:
1. In keyboard texture, check if right hand moves between complete triads (3+1)
2. Or if right hand moves from complete triad to incomplete sonority (nonfactorizable)

# Context & Application
The convergence of practical pedagogy and geometric theory is one of the most beautiful results in the book. Working musicians arrived at optimal voice-leading solutions through centuries of trial and error, without the mathematical framework to explain why their solutions were comprehensive. The two-gesture system provides a complete inventory of efficient voice leadings suitable for real-time performance.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Assuming figured-bass pedagogy is merely a historical curiosity
  **Correction**: The two-gesture system remains optimal and is still taught in universities today

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking not every right-hand move from complete to incomplete triad is nonfactorizable
  **Clarification**: Some such moves are factorizable; the specific nonfactorizable pattern requires the split-merge structure
