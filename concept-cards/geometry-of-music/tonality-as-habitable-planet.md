# Quick Definition
Tymoczko's metaphor that tonality is not one among an infinity of equally viable musical systems but is closer to being "the only habitable planet that we have discovered so far," because the constraints of the five components (consonance, voice leading, harmonic consistency, centricity, macroharmony) are so tight that only a narrow range of musical possibilities satisfies them all.

# Core Definition
The geometric spaces of Chapter 3 reveal that combining harmonic consistency and conjunct melodic motion requires exploiting unusual features of the geometry: either the global "twist" (traditional tonality) or the mirror boundaries (micropolyphony). Scales must contain many consonances while dividing the octave nearly evenly. These constraints are so severe that familiar chords and scales are "overdetermined and multiply optimized," pointing toward the same collections from many independent directions. Tonality is therefore not arbitrary convention but a deeply constrained solution to the problem of combining the five musical properties simultaneously. "Just as we have an interest in conserving the ecology of this, our only habitable planet, so too might we have an interest in protecting the sophisticated tonal languages that manage to survive in today's unfriendly economic climate."

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of the five components of tonality
- Familiarity with near-evenness and chord space geometry

# Key Properties
1. The five components are extremely difficult to satisfy simultaneously
2. Familiar chords and scales are overdetermined by multiple criteria
3. Only a narrow range of musical possibilities satisfies all constraints
4. Tonality is not one among many equally viable options
5. The geometric spaces show why specific chords and scales recur
6. An argument for the protection of tonal musical languages

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Observe how multiple independent criteria converge on the same musical objects
2. Note the difficulty of finding alternatives that satisfy all five components
3. Recognize the "habitable zone" as the region where all five properties overlap

# Context & Application
This metaphor is the conclusion's most memorable image and encapsulates the book's central theoretical claim: the mathematical structure of pitch constrains tonality far more than most musicians realize.

- **Error**: Interpreting the metaphor as a claim that atonality is impossible
  **Correction**: Atonality is possible but represents a different (and more difficult) set of constraints; tonality is uniquely well-suited to the five properties
