# Quick Definition
Jazz represents the realization of Schoenberg's and Piston's prophecy that early twentieth-century developments would coalesce into a coherent musical language -- synthesizing chromatic voice leading, quartal harmony, polychords, impressionist scales, and polytonality into a codified common practice, albeit achieved by improvising musicians rather than the classical avant-garde.

# Core Definition
A simple jazz ii-V-I is an accretion of techniques formed over ages: at its core, a ii-V-I schema from Monteverdi's time; in the upper voices, efficient chromatic voice leading between third-related triads reminiscent of Schubert, Brahms, and Wagner; on top, scales characteristic of early twentieth-century modernism (acoustic over V, lydian over I). Jazz musicians acted as "custodians of a tradition of advanced tonal thinking," bridging Debussy/Ravel and late twentieth-century composers like Reich and Adams. The synthesis was achieved not by the notated avant-garde but by "improvising musicians, many poor and African American, who carved an alternative musical tradition out of the materials afforded by popular culture."

# Key Properties
1. Jazz synthesizes five centuries of tonal technique in a single idiom
2. A ii-V-I contains Baroque voice leading, Romantic chromaticism, and modernist scales
3. Jazz was the vehicle for Schoenberg/Piston's predicted modernist synthesis
4. Improvising musicians, not the classical avant-garde, achieved the synthesis
5. Jazz bridges impressionism and postminimalism historically
6. Jazz has been underrepresented in academic music theory despite its importance
7. The synthesis is a hybrid that incorporates modernist devices within functional tonality

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Identify the historical layers in a jazz performance: functional harmony, chromatic voice leading, scalar coloring, polytonal effects
2. Note how each technique was originally associated with a different historical period
3. Recognize the synthesis as uniquely achieved in jazz practice

# Context & Application
This section makes the book's strongest argument for jazz's centrality to music history. Jazz is "not merely some marginal offshoot of classical music" but is "more continuous with early twentieth-century tonality than many forms of more avant-garde music." The claim that tritone substitution has only 15 JSTOR hits versus 2688 for serialism illustrates the academic neglect of these important concepts.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating jazz as separate from the history of Western tonal music
  **Correction**: Jazz is central to that history, bridging impressionism and postminimalism

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking the "modernist synthesis" is atonal
  **Clarification**: The synthesis is fundamentally tonal, incorporating modernist devices within functional harmony
