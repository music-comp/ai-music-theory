# Quick Definition
The rare interposition of a mediant (iii) triad between V and I, completing the full circle of diatonic thirds, where the engine of falling-thirds harmony temporarily overcomes the V-I dominant-tonic paradigm.

# Core Definition
The iii chord is omitted from the standard thirds-based grammar because it is rare in nonsequential harmony. However, tonal composers sometimes utilize the full circle of diatonic thirds by interposing iii between V and I. In these passages, the descending-thirds engine temporarily overcomes the V-I paradigm, blurring the expected dominant resolution. The iii chord is far more common in sequential contexts (where its inclusion completes the diatonic circle) than in nonsequential harmony. The mediant's rarity in functional harmony but regularity in sequences is one of the distinctive features of the thirds-based model.

# Prerequisites
- The thirds-based grammar and the role of descending thirds

# Key Properties
1. iii is omitted from the standard harmonic grammar because it is rare
2. It appears most often in sequential contexts
3. When interposed between V and I, it completes the full diatonic thirds circle
4. The insertion temporarily weakens the dominant-tonic resolution
5. In sequences, iii participates in regular root motion patterns just like other diatonic triads

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. In a V-I progression, insert iii between them: V-iii-I
2. This completes the thirds chain: ...vii°-V-iii-I
3. All motions are by descending third

## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Look for iii chord appearing between V and I
2. More commonly, find iii in sequential passages (especially descending-thirds sequences)

# Context & Application
Mediant insertion illustrates the tension between the falling-thirds engine and the dominant-tonic paradigm. When the engine prevails, we get the rare V-iii-I progression. When the paradigm prevails (the normal case), iii is skipped and V resolves directly to I.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Including iii as a standard functional harmony chord
  **Correction**: It is explicitly excluded from the standard grammar because of its rarity

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Conflating the mediant triad (iii) with the submediant (vi)
  **Clarification**: vi is fully integrated into the harmonic grammar; iii is rare and peripheral
