# Quick Definition
An eight-note scale built by combining two diminished seventh chords a perfect fifth apart, with alternating steps of one and two semitones. It is nearly even, transpositionally symmetrical (only three distinct forms exist), and rich in both minor thirds and perfect fifths.

# Core Definition
The octatonic scale is constructed by combining two diminished seventh chords (minor-third cycles) separated by a perfect fifth. Its step pattern alternates between one and two semitones (1-2-1-2-1-2-1-2 or 2-1-2-1-2-1-2-1), making it considerably more even than the hexatonic scale. It has four-fold transpositional symmetry, meaning only three distinct octatonic collections exist in twelve-tone equal temperament. It is maximally saturated with minor thirds while also containing many perfect fifths. Because its steps are only one and two semitones, it supports both stepwise melodies and tertian harmonies, making it one of the most versatile non-diatonic scales.

# Prerequisites
- Stacks of thirds construction (minor third cycles)

# Key Properties
1. 8 notes with alternating steps of 1 and 2 semitones
2. Transpositionally symmetrical: only 3 distinct forms
3. Saturated with minor thirds and perfect fifths
4. Supports stepwise melodies and tertian harmony
5. Can transform into an acoustic scale by "merging" a major second
6. Each octatonic contains four acoustic and four harmonic (major or minor) scales within one semitone

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Take a diminished seventh chord: e.g., C-Eb-Gb-A
2. Add a second diminished seventh a perfect fifth above (or below): D-F-Ab-B
3. Combine: C-D-Eb-F-Gb-Ab-A-B

# Context & Application
The octatonic scale is central to the music of Stravinsky, Bartok, Messiaen, and many other twentieth-century composers. In Tymoczko's framework, it sits at the top of the scale pyramid (Figure 4.6.3), connected by efficient voice leading to the acoustic, harmonic major, and harmonic minor scales. In jazz, it is the "diminished scale" used over dominant chords.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking there are many different octatonic scales
  **Correction**: Due to four-fold symmetry, there are only 3 distinct octatonic collections

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Is the octatonic the same as the "diminished scale"?
  **Clarification**: Yes — jazz musicians call it the diminished scale (or half-whole/whole-half depending on the starting step)
