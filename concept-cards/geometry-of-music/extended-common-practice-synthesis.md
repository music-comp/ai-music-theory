# Quick Definition
Tymoczko's historical narrative tracing a genuine common practice from impressionism through jazz to contemporary postminimalism, fusing nineteenth-century voice leading with twentieth-century scalar thinking, and arguing that the history of twentieth-century tonality runs straight through jazz.

# Core Definition
The book's historical narrative traces a systematic progression: increasing interest in three- and four-voice composition led to triadic harmony, which paved the way for classical functional conventions. Standardization of major and minor modes led to chromatic transposition, which underwrote expanded twentieth-century modal vocabulary. Nineteenth-century chromatic voice leading created more chromatic macroharmonies, splitting composers between atonalists (following chromaticism to its conclusion) and scalar composers (ameliorating chromaticism with the three scalar techniques of Chapter 9). The "most novel feature" of this narrative concerns the transition from nineteenth to twentieth centuries: amid the fog of recent history, it is "just possible to discern the outlines of a genuine common practice, stretching from impressionism through jazz to contemporary postminimalism," fusing voice leading with scalar thinking, flexible centricity with macroharmonic awareness.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of the twentieth-century scalar common practice
- Understanding of jazz as modernist synthesis

# Key Properties
1. Traces systematic progression from medieval music to the present
2. Identifies genuine twentieth-century common practice crossing style boundaries
3. Claims jazz is central, not peripheral, to the history of twentieth-century tonality
4. The common practice fuses voice leading with scalar thinking
5. Flexible centricity combined with macroharmonic awareness
6. The narrative challenges atonality-centered histories
7. Five components of tonality (Chapter 1) are "fairly basic" and "not likely to be supplanted soon"

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Look for the shared techniques across impressionism, jazz, and postminimalism
2. Identify the five components of tonality in any tonal style
3. Note the historical continuities across apparent stylistic boundaries

# Context & Application
This synthesis represents the book's ultimate argument: tonality has continued to develop throughout the twentieth century, and the most interesting developments occurred at the intersection of notated and non-notated traditions.
