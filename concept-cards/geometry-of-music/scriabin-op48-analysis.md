# Quick Definition
Scriabin's Prelude Op. 48 No. 2 (1905) is a paradigmatic example of chord-first composition, where standard nineteenth-century semitonal voice leading in the upper voices gives rise to a series of diatonic scales.

# Core Definition
In this prelude, three upper voices contain a standard nineteenth-century progression connecting D minor, Bb minor, and an incomplete C major seventh (C-E-B) through semitonal voice leading. Scriabin departs from post-Wagnerian practice by assigning a different scale to each chord: F diatonic, Db diatonic, and G diatonic. The chords are clearly leading the scales: one can easily imagine composing the efficient chordal voice leadings first, but it is much more difficult to imagine starting with those three diatonic scales and composing the chords only later.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of chord-first composition
- Familiarity with semitonal voice leading between chords

# Key Properties
1. Upper voices use standard nineteenth-century semitonal voice leading
2. Each chord is accompanied by a different diatonic scale
3. Scales would seem arbitrary without the chordal logic
4. Composed in 1905 -- a particularly clear early example
5. Demonstrates the transition from Wagnerian chromaticism to scalar awareness

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Extract the voice leading in the upper three voices
2. Note the standard chromatic connections between D minor, Bb minor, C major seventh
3. Observe that each chord occupies a different diatonic collection
4. Confirm that the chordal logic is primary, scalar logic secondary

# Context & Application
This prelude represents an early and particularly clear example of chord-first composition. It shows how the technique emerged naturally from nineteenth-century chromatic voice-leading practice, with the innovation being the assignment of full diatonic scales to each chord rather than treating everything chromatically.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating the piece as simply a chromatic exercise in the Wagnerian manner
  **Correction**: The scalar dimension is a genuine innovation beyond Wagner

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming the three diatonic scales have an independent scalar relationship
  **Clarification**: F, Db, and G diatonic are not closely related on the scale lattice; their connection is through chords, not scalar voice leading
