# Quick Definition
Stravinsky uses the subset technique in both concrete form ("Petit airs" -- fixed pitches G-D-A-E-B with mobile F/F# and C/C#, the same as Grieg's "Klokkeklang") and abstract form ("Dance of the Adolescents" -- fixed intervallic pattern 2-2-1-2 embedded in different modal contexts).

# Core Definition
In "Petit airs" from Histoire du soldat, Stravinsky divides pitches into fixed notes (G-D-A-E-B) and mobile notes (F/F#, C/C#), producing four scales on a square face of the scale lattice, including an explicit G acoustic statement. Stravinsky juxtaposes mobile notes more freely than Grieg, often assigning different accidentals to different instruments, creating pervasive polytonality. In "Dance of the Adolescents," the technique becomes abstract: not specific pitches but an interval pattern (major pentachord 2-2-1-2) is held fixed, appearing in three modal contexts (aeolian, dorian, locrian #2). Stravinsky exhaustively exploits all ways of embedding his folk-like theme in familiar modal contexts.

# Key Properties
1. "Petit airs": same fixed/mobile pitches as Grieg (G-D-A-E-B)
2. Four familiar scales on a square face of the scale lattice
3. More pervasive polytonality than Grieg (different accidentals in different instruments)
4. "Dance of the Adolescents": abstract fixed interval pattern (2-2-1-2)
5. Three modal contexts: aeolian, dorian, locrian #2
6. Exhaustive exploitation of embedding possibilities
7. Historical connections: Grieg -> Ravel -> Stravinsky -> jazz -> Reich

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Identify the fixed element (pitches or interval pattern)
2. Determine which mobile notes/scale degrees change
3. List all resulting scales and check for systematic exploration

# Context & Application
Stravinsky's use demonstrates both the concrete and abstract forms of the subset technique. The resemblances to Grieg and the Beatles are "symptomatic of shared musical concerns crossing stylistic boundaries."
