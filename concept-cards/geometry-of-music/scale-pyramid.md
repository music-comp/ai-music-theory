# Quick Definition
An inverted pyramid organizing the eight scales: transpositionally symmetrical scales (whole-tone, hexatonic, octatonic) at the top, non-diatonic seven-note scales (acoustic, harmonic minor, harmonic major) in the middle, and the diatonic scale at the bottom. Scales are connected by efficient voice leadings.

# Core Definition
Figure 4.6.3 presents the scale pyramid (inverted), organizing the eight important scales by their degree of transpositional symmetry and their voice-leading relationships. The three transpositionally symmetrical scales are at the top, with the most symmetry and fewest distinct transpositions. The four non-diatonic seven-note scales (acoustic, harmonic minor, harmonic major) occupy the middle tier, each connected by efficient voice leadings to both the diatonic scale (bottom) and one or more transpositionally symmetrical scales (top). The diatonic, with no transpositional symmetry, is at the bottom. The non-diatonic seven-note scales thus serve as mediators — bridges between the familiar diatonic world and the more exotic symmetric collections. Numbers on the connecting lines indicate how many scales of each type can be reached by the specified voice-leading operation.

# Prerequisites
- The eight important scales and their properties
- Voice leading between scales (splits, merges, semitone shifts)

# Key Properties
1. Three levels: symmetric (top), non-diatonic seven-note (middle), diatonic (bottom)
2. Middle scales mediate between diatonic and symmetric
3. Each middle scale shares most notes with nearby diatonic and symmetric collections
4. Connections labeled with voice-leading counts
5. The acoustic scale connects to whole-tone (by merge) and octatonic (by split)
6. The harmonic scales connect to hexatonic and octatonic (by split/merge)

# Context & Application
The pyramid provides a birds-eye view of the scale ecosystem. Twentieth-century composers like Debussy navigated this pyramid, using acoustic and harmonic scales as stepping stones between diatonic and symmetric collections. The pyramid also provides an organizing framework for the analyses in Chapters 6 and 9.
