# Quick Definition
Voice leading provides a more fundamental explanation of nineteenth-century chromaticism than alternative harmonic theories (equal division, chromatic mediants, hexatonic scales, common-tone retention), because it subsumes all of them and additionally explains the asymmetry between triadic and seventh-chord root motion.

# Core Definition
Tymoczko argues that voice leading should be prioritized as the explanation for chromatic practice over competing harmonic theories from Proctor (equal divisions of the octave), Kopp (chromatic mediant relations), Cohn/Lerdahl (hexatonic scales), and Clark (common-tone retention). Three arguments: (1) Voice leading provides a single explanation for an enormous variety of chromatic techniques — augmented sixths, Tristan resolutions, Chopin's hypercubic procedures, etc. — where no harmonic theory covers all of them. (2) Voice leading explains the asymmetry between triadic major-third and seventh-chord minor-third root motions, which harmonic theories do not. (3) Voice leading can potentially subsume the harmonic explanations: a concern with voice-leading efficiency naturally gives rise to mediant relations (geometry shows triads are close to major-third transpositions), equal divisions (repeated major-third motion divides the octave evenly), and hexatonic scales (cubes in three-note space contain hexatonic scale chords). Voice leading is not the last word in chromatic analysis, but it is a very reasonable starting point.

# Key Properties
1. Voice leading explains more chromatic phenomena than any single harmonic theory
2. It explains the triad/seventh-chord root-motion asymmetry
3. It subsumes mediant relations, equal divisions, and hexatonic scales as byproducts
4. Composers did also think harmonically — voice leading is primary but not exclusive
5. The priority of voice leading over harmony is specific to the chromatic second practice

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify:
1. When analyzing chromatic music, start with voice-leading analysis
2. Identify the most efficient paths between the chords involved
3. Check whether harmonic labels (mediant, hexatonic, equal division) arise naturally from the voice-leading relationships
4. If so, voice leading provides the more parsimonious explanation

# Context & Application
This methodological argument shapes how one approaches all chromatic analysis from Schubert onward. It does not deny that composers had harmonic intentions, but argues that voice-leading geometry provides the most comprehensive and explanatory framework. The argument has implications for understanding the continuity between Romantic chromaticism and twentieth-century practices.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking Tymoczko denies any role for harmonic thinking
  **Correction**: He explicitly acknowledges that composers thought about harmony too; the claim is about explanatory priority, not exclusivity

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Thinking "voice leading explains chromaticism" means every chromatic passage is voice-led
  **Clarification**: Some passages may have primarily harmonic motivations; voice leading is the best starting point, not the only consideration
