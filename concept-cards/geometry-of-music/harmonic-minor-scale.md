# Quick Definition
A seven-note scale with one three-semitone step (the augmented second between the sixth and seventh degrees), constructed as a two-octave stack of three major and four minor thirds. It is not inversionally symmetrical; its inversion produces the harmonic major scale.

# Core Definition
The harmonic minor scale (e.g., C-D-Eb-F-G-Ab-B) is one of the four seven-note scales constructible as a two-octave stack of three major and four minor thirds. It contains one three-semitone step (Ab to B, the "augmented second"), placing it in Group 2 of Figure 4.4.5 — scales with at least one large step. Despite this, its "thirds" (two-step intervals) are still three or four semitones, supporting tertian harmony. It is the only commonly used seven-note scale that is not inversionally symmetrical; its inversion is the harmonic major. On the scale lattice, it is connected to both diatonic and octatonic/hexatonic scales by efficient voice leading.

# Prerequisites
- Stacks of thirds construction

# Key Properties
1. Steps: 2-1-2-2-1-3-1 (contains one 3-semitone step)
2. Not inversionally symmetrical (unlike diatonic and acoustic)
3. Its inversion is the harmonic major scale
4. Thirds are 3 or 4 semitones: supports tertian harmony
5. One semitone from both diatonic and octatonic collections
6. Connected to hexatonic by a "split" operation

# Context & Application
The harmonic minor is ubiquitous in classical music and plays an important mediating role in Tymoczko's framework, sitting between the diatonic and transpositionally symmetrical scales on the scale pyramid. Together with the harmonic major, it forms a pair of inversionally related scales investigated by nineteenth-century theorists like Weitzmann and Rimsky-Korsakov.

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Is the harmonic minor the same as the natural minor with raised seventh?
  **Clarification**: Yes, but Tymoczko emphasizes its construction from thirds stacks and its position on the scale lattice rather than its derivation from the natural minor.
