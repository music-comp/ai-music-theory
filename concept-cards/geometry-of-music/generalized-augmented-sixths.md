# Quick Definition
Generalized augmented sixth chords are seventh chords resolving to triads where two converging voices are separated by ten semitones (augmented sixth) and converge semitonally to an octave — a subset of the twelve basic schemas for efficient four-to-three-note voice leading.

# Core Definition
To find efficient voice leadings from a seventh chord to a triad, the crossing-avoidance principle tells us that two adjacent notes in the seventh chord must converge on a single note in the triad. This yields twelve abstract schemas (four pairs of converging notes times three possible targets). Augmented sixth resolutions are the special subset where the converging notes are separated by two semitones (enharmonically an augmented sixth = ten semitones) and converge on the note between them, permitting all four voices to move semitonally. These need not have dominant function: while some contain a leading tone and sound "dominant-like," others do not. The familiar Italian, French, and German sixths are merely a noteworthy species within the genus of efficient seventh-chord-to-triad voice leadings.

# Prerequisites

# Key Properties
1. Twelve basic schemas for crossing-free voice leading from seventh chord to triad
2. Four pairs of converging notes (root+third, third+fifth, fifth+seventh, seventh+root)
3. Three possible target notes (root, third, fifth of the triad)
4. Augmented sixth = special case where converging pair is 10 semitones apart
5. Voice leadings in the same schema square are individually T-related
6. Not all generalized augmented sixths have dominant function

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Choose a seventh chord
2. Select two adjacent notes to converge onto one note of the target triad
3. The remaining two notes connect independently to the other two triad notes
4. For augmented sixth quality: ensure the converging pair is 10 semitones apart

## To Identify/Recognize:
1. A seventh chord resolving to a triad by efficient voice leading
2. Two voices converging (often semitonally) to an octave
3. An augmented sixth interval resolving outward to an octave
4. Classify by which notes converge and which triad note is the target

# Context & Application
This framework provides precise vocabulary for comparing chromatic progressions across composers and centuries. Schubert's "Am Meer" and Schoenberg's "Erwartung" Op. 2 No. 1 use the same schema (r,t,f,s)->(f,r,t,f) despite being separated by decades. Wagner's Tristan uses (r,t,f,s)->(r,t,f,r). Debussy's Pelléas reimagines the augmented sixth between different chord members. Brahms' Second Piano Concerto finale uses a non-augmented-sixth schema where converging voices are only 9 semitones apart.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Limiting augmented sixth chords to the Italian, French, and German types
  **Correction**: These are three instances of a much larger family of efficient voice leadings

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming generalized augmented sixths must have predominant or dominant function
  **Clarification**: The framework is contrapuntal, not functional — some instances have dominant function, others do not
- **Confusion**: Thinking the twelve schemas all produce different-sounding progressions
  **Clarification**: Voice leadings within the same schema square are individually T-related and sound very similar
