# Quick Definition
Tymoczko identifies eight scales as particularly important: diatonic, acoustic (melodic minor ascending), whole-tone, octatonic, hexatonic, harmonic minor, harmonic major, and pentatonic. They are all nearly even, maximally saturated with consonances, and constructible from stacks of fifths or thirds.

# Core Definition
These eight scales emerge from systematic construction principles — stacking perfect fifths (producing pentatonic, diatonic, chromatic) and combining cycles of major and minor thirds (producing hexatonic, octatonic, whole-tone, and the four seven-note scales). They share the property of near-evenness and can be classified by step sizes. The first group (diatonic, acoustic, whole-tone, octatonic) has steps of at most two semitones and thirds of three or four semitones, permitting traditional stepwise melodies and tertian harmonies. The second group (harmonic minor, harmonic major, hexatonic, pentatonic) has at least one three-semitone step, producing more exotic melodies but still supporting tertian harmony (except pentatonic, whose "thirds" sound like fourths). Together, these eight scales contain every chord that does not include a chromatic cluster like {C, C#, D}, making them comprehensive reservoirs for tonal composition.

# Key Properties
1. Group 1 (steps <= 2 semitones): diatonic, acoustic, whole-tone, octatonic
2. Group 2 (at least one 3-semitone step): harmonic minor, harmonic major, hexatonic, pentatonic
3. All are nearly even and maximally saturated with consonant intervals
4. Together they contain every cluster-free chord
5. The four seven-note scales (diatonic, acoustic, harmonic minor, harmonic major) are all stacks of thirds
6. In 12-TET, these scales have additional useful property: they contain no chromatic clusters

# Construction / Recognition
## Construction Methods:
1. **Stacks of fifths**: pentatonic (5 fifths), diatonic (7 fifths), chromatic (12 fifths)
2. **Cycles of thirds combined**: hexatonic (2 augmented triads), octatonic (2 diminished sevenths), whole-tone (2 augmented triads a whole step apart)
3. **Two-octave stacks of thirds**: diatonic, acoustic, harmonic minor, harmonic major (4 arrangements of 3 major + 4 minor thirds)

# Context & Application
These eight scales form the vocabulary of the "extended common practice" described in the book's second half. Medieval through classical composers primarily used the diatonic and (later) harmonic minor; Romantic composers added the harmonic major and acoustic; twentieth-century composers embraced all eight. The identification of these specific eight scales, and the demonstration that they arise from multiple independent construction principles, is one of Tymoczko's key contributions.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking these are the *only* possible scales
  **Correction**: These are the most important for the Western extended common practice; other scales exist and are sometimes used

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Why include the pentatonic when it is a subset of the diatonic?
  **Clarification**: The pentatonic has its own independent significance as a nearly even five-note scale, particularly in contexts where its "thirds" function as fourths
