# Quick Definition
The Beatles' "Help!" uses the subset technique with the lead vocal confined to five fixed pitches (A-B-C#-D-E) contextualized within A major and A mixolydian, with timbre reinforcing the fixed/mobile distinction (lead vocal = fixed notes, guitar/backup vocals = mobile G/G#).

# Core Definition
Lennon's lead vocal confines itself to the first five pitches of A major (A-B-C#-D-E), with F# appearing only in the introduction. These become fixed notes contextualized within two different scales: A major and A mixolydian (the only two diatonic collections containing those fixed pitches). The mobile pair G/G# appears in guitar and backup vocals, reinforcing the distinction by timbre. The mixolydian cadence D->C# is harmonized not with V7-I but with a "striking modal progression in pure major triads, D-G-A" -- a technique characteristic of the Beatles and rock more generally, whereby common melodic schemas are modally reharmonized.

# Key Properties
1. Five fixed pitches in lead vocal: A-B-C#-D-E
2. Two possible diatonic contexts: A major and A mixolydian
3. Timbre distinguishes fixed (vocals) from mobile (guitar) notes
4. Modal reharmonization of traditional melodic schemas
5. Opening b-G-E7 introduces seventh chord when root motion changes from major third to minor third

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Note the limited pitch range of the melody
2. Identify which scales can contain those fixed pitches
3. Observe the timbral separation between fixed and mobile notes

# Context & Application
The resemblance to Stravinsky's "Dance of the Adolescents" is "relatively clear": both use deliberately simple melodic material contextualized within sophisticated scalar environments, both extend fixed material with either G or G#, and both use Debussian parallel triads.
