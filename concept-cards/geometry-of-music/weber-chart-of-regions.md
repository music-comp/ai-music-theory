# Quick Definition
The Weber chart of the regions (actually originating with F. G. Vial) arranges keys on a torus where SW/NE diagonals link fifth-related keys and SE/NW diagonals alternate parallel and relative relationships — the standard model of key distance, but one that fails to explain the major/minor modulatory asymmetry.

# Core Definition
In its equal-tempered form, the Vial/Weber chart arranges keys so that motion along one diagonal links modally matched fifth-related keys (C major to G major) and motion along the other diagonal changes mode, alternating between parallel (C major to C minor) and relative (C major to A minor) relationships. The chart suggests that fifth-related major keys are particularly close, but does not explain why: it could be acoustic affinity of tonic notes, or it could be voice-leading proximity of scales. The model predicts symmetry between major and minor modulatory behavior, but actual data shows marked asymmetry: minor keys strongly favor the relative major (III) and subtonic major (VII), unlike the Weber prediction. Tymoczko's scalar model achieves correlations of .91-.96 with actual modulation data, compared to .77-.84 for Weber.

# Prerequisites
- Basic understanding of key relationships and modulation

# Key Properties
1. Torus topology (top glues to bottom, left to right)
2. Fifth relations on one diagonal, parallel/relative on the other
3. Predicts symmetry between major and minor modulatory behavior
4. Fails to explain why minor keys favor relative major over dominant
5. Does not predict the close relation between a major key and its supertonic minor
6. Has been used and adapted by Krumhansl and Lerdahl

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Arrange major keys along fifth-related diagonals
2. Place minor keys on the alternate diagonal via parallel and relative relationships
3. The resulting grid wraps around as a torus

## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Any model of key distance based primarily on fifth-relationships and parallel/relative
2. Predicts similar modulatory patterns for major and minor keys

# Context & Application
The Weber chart has been the default model of key distance for over 200 years. Tymoczko uses it as a foil for his scalar model, demonstrating that voice-leading between scales provides a better explanation of actual modulatory practice.
