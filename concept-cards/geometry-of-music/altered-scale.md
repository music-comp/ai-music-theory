# Quick Definition
A mode of the acoustic scale where whole-tone and octatonic tritone fragments switch positions compared to the lydian dominant: the lower tritone is octatonic (G-Ab-Bb-B-C#) and the upper is whole-tone (C#-D#-F-G), containing b9, #9, #11/b5, and #5/b13.

# Core Definition
The altered scale arises when the pianist plays a tritone substitution while the bass player does not: the pianist plays Db lydian dominant, but listeners hear G as the root due to the bass, producing a G "altered scale." This scale reverses the tritone-fragment structure of lydian dominant: the lower tritone G-Ab-Bb-B is octatonic, the upper tritone C#-D#-F-G is whole-tone. Jazz musicians call it "altered" because it contains many nondiatonic alterations: b9, #9, #11/b5, and #5/b13. Its clearest classical precedent occurs in minor-mode passages where lowered and raised forms of the leading tone collide.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of lydian dominant and tritone substitution

# Key Properties
1. Seventh mode of the acoustic scale (or fourth mode with switched fragments)
2. Lower tritone: octatonic; upper tritone: whole-tone
3. Contains b9, #9, #11, and b13 over the dominant root
4. Generated by playing Db lydian dominant over a G bass
5. Contains the same notes as Db acoustic / Ab melodic minor ascending
6. Maximum "alteration" while remaining within the four central scales

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Take a melodic minor ascending scale and start on its seventh degree
2. Or: take lydian dominant and transpose the upper voices by tritone while keeping the bass
3. Result: 1-b2-#2-3-#4-#5-b7
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Listen for a very chromatic dominant sound with multiple alterations
2. Check for the characteristic reversed fragment structure
3. Often appears in highly chromatic jazz passages

# Context & Application
The altered scale is one of the four central scales of jazz and is played by countless improvisers. Upper-voice tritone substitution exchanges lydian dominant and altered modes, making them two sides of the same harmonic coin.
