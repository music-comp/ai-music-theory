# Quick Definition
Independent application of tritone substitution to either the bass or the upper voices (but not both), producing reinterpretation of chord function: bass substitution keeps the same upper voices over a new root, while upper-voice substitution transposes the voicing by tritone over the same bass, exchanging lydian dominant and altered scales.

# Core Definition
In bass-only tritone substitution, the same upper voices are reinterpreted as belonging to a root a tritone away (e.g., the 7th, 9th, 3rd, 13th of G7 become the 3rd, b13th, 7th, #9 of Db7 altered). Both voicings belong to the same G acoustic collection. In upper-voice substitution, the bass stays fixed while the upper voices transpose by tritone, changing the scale more dramatically: the G diatonic voicing becomes Db acoustic/G altered. This means whole-tone and octatonic voicings are unchanged by upper-voice substitution (they are tritone-symmetrical), while lydian dominant and altered exchange places. A soloist can therefore always play chromatically descending melodic sequences over descending-fifth bass lines by applying upper-voice substitution to every other chord.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of tritone substitution, lydian dominant, and altered scales

# Key Properties
1. Bass substitution: upper voices stay, bass moves by tritone; same acoustic collection
2. Upper-voice substitution: bass stays, upper voices move by tritone; changes scale
3. Whole-tone and octatonic voicings are invariant under upper-voice substitution
4. Lydian dominant and altered exchange under upper-voice substitution
5. Diatonic voicings can clash under independent substitution
6. Descending-semitone voicings over descending-fifths bass = standard jazz idiom

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. For bass substitution: keep the pianist's voicing, transpose only the bass by tritone
2. For upper-voice substitution: keep the bass, transpose only the upper voices by tritone
3. For descending sequences: apply upper-voice substitution to every other chord
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Bass motion by half step (rather than fifth) to the tonic suggests bass substitution
2. Chromatically descending upper voices over descending-fifth bass suggest upper-voice substitution on alternating chords

# Context & Application
These ideas are anticipated in Debussy and Ravel. Debussy's Prelude to "The Afternoon of a Faun" uses acoustic scales over V7. Ravel's "Ondine" applies bass substitution to dominant chords. Debussy's "La danse de Puck" alternates altered and diatonic voicings. Debussy's Six epigraphes antiques uses descending-semitone voicings over descending-fifths exactly as in jazz practice.
