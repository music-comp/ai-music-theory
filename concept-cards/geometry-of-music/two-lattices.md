# Quick Definition
The circle of thirds (voice leading among triads) and the circle of fifths (voice leading among scales) are structurally analogous, arising from the same mathematical principle applied at different levels — giving functional harmony a remarkable fractal-like self-similarity.

# Core Definition
Both the triad and the major scale divide the octave nearly (but not precisely) evenly: the triad trisects the diatonic octave into 2, 2, and 3 scale steps, while the major scale divides four chromatic octaves into six perfect fifths and one "near fifth." Both circles arise from shifting the position of the unusual interval, linking transpositionally related collections by single-step voice leading. When these circles are "scrambled" (reordering adjacent voice leadings), they produce three-dimensional cubic lattices: the chord lattice contains triads, fourth chords, and incomplete seventh chords; the scale lattice contains diatonic, acoustic, and other collections. The chord lattice is essentially the same geometry as the scale lattice, giving functional harmony a self-similarity evocative of fractals — the same contrapuntal relationships appear whether we zoom out to key relations or zoom in to chord-to-chord connections.

# Prerequisites
- The circle of thirds as a voice-leading structure for chords
- The circle of fifths as a voice-leading structure for scales

# Key Properties
1. Both circles arise from "near interval cycles" — collections that almost evenly divide the octave
2. Both can be "scrambled" to produce three-dimensional cubic lattices
3. The chord lattice parallels the scale lattice in its mathematical structure
4. Fourth chords play the role of acoustic scales (generated by reversing adjacent voice leadings)
5. Suspensions on the chord lattice parallel modal shifts on the scale lattice

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Start with the circle of thirds (triads linked by single diatonic step)
2. Scramble adjacent voice leadings to produce a 2D lattice of squares (triads + fourth chords)
3. Continue scrambling to produce the full 3D cubic lattice
4. Compare with the analogous procedure applied to the circle of fifths (scales)

## To Identify/Recognize:
1. A progression from C to G by way of suspension {C, D, G} on the chord lattice parallels the shift from D major to C major by way of D melodic minor on the scale lattice
2. Self-similar patterns across chord and key levels indicate the two-lattice structure

# Context & Application
The structural analogy between chord-level and key-level voice leading provides an alternative to Schenkerian claims about hierarchical self-similarity. Where Schenker requires controversial recursive reduction, Tymoczko achieves a similar result through transparent, mathematically precise geometry. The lattice also provides a "game board" for understanding suspension patterns and their resolution to triads.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking the two lattices are identical
  **Correction**: They are structurally analogous (same mathematical form) but operate at different levels — chords vs scales

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Conflating this with Schenkerian hierarchical self-similarity
  **Clarification**: Tymoczko's self-similarity is between chord-level and key-level geometry; Schenker's is between foreground and background levels through recursive reduction
