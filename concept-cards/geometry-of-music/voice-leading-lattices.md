# Quick Definition
Low-dimensional graphs (typically 1-3 dimensions) that represent single-step voice-leading connections among nearly even chords, providing practical tools for navigating the musically important regions of high-dimensional chord spaces.

# Core Definition
Voice-leading lattices are discrete graphs whose vertices represent nearly even chords and whose edges represent single-step voice leadings. There are two fundamental types. Type 1 arises when the chord size exactly divides the scale size (e.g., 2-, 3-, 4-, or 6-note chords in 12-note space): the lattice consists of cubes linked by shared vertices, where the shared vertices are perfectly even chords. Type 2 arises when the chord size and scale size are relatively prime (e.g., 5- or 7-note chords in 12-note space, or any chord in 7-note diatonic space): the lattice consists of cubes sharing common faces, with a "generalized circle of fifths" zigzagging through the center. Both types can be constructed systematically by scrambling the order of voice leadings on the central zigzag. These lattices faithfully represent voice-leading distances despite having far fewer dimensions than the chord spaces from which they are extracted.

# Prerequisites
- Nearly even chords and their central position in chord space

# Key Properties
1. Two types: cubes sharing vertices (Type 1) and cubes sharing faces (Type 2)
2. Type 1: shared vertices are perfectly even chords (augmented triads, diminished sevenths)
3. Type 2: central zigzag is a generalized circle of fifths
4. Edges represent single-step voice leadings
5. Lattice dimension depends on how many chord types are included, not the chord's size
6. Lattices faithfully represent voice-leading distances from the full high-dimensional space
7. Constructed by scrambling the order of voice leadings on the central chain

# Construction / Recognition
## Type 1 (chord size divides scale size):
1. Start with the perfectly even chords at the center
2. These are shared vertices between adjacent cubes
3. Edges show ways of sequentially raising/lowering notes by one step
## Type 2 (chord size coprime to scale size):
1. Find the generalized circle of fifths (near interval cycles linked by single-step voice leading)
2. This forms a central zigzag
3. Rearrange adjacent voice leadings to complete squares (2D), cubes (3D), etc.

# Context & Application
Voice-leading lattices are the principal analytical tools for the second half of the book. They compress the essential information from high-dimensional chord spaces into visualizable, practical graphs. The three-note chromatic lattice (cubes of major/minor triads sharing augmented triad vertices) is used extensively for chromatic triadic analysis. The seven-note scale lattice is used for analyzing modulation as voice leading. These lattices appear throughout Chapters 6-10.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking any discrete graph faithfully represents voice-leading distances
  **Correction**: Only the lattices described here are "faithful" — the familiar Tonnetz, for example, does not accurately model voice-leading distances

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Why can the circle of fifths (1D) represent seven-note diatonic voice leading (7D)?
  **Clarification**: It works because "for most practical purposes we can make do" with this compression — six of the seven dimensions are geometrically redundant for nearly even diatonic scales. This is a remarkable mathematical fact, not an obvious one.
