# Quick Definition
Efficient voice leading is voice leading in which all voices move by short distances, the polyphonic manifestation of the general preference for conjunct melodic motion.

# Core Definition
Efficient voice leading describes voice leadings where individual voices move by small intervals, ideally a semitone or two. It is the primary goal of contrapuntal writing: composers seek to connect chords using the smoothest possible voice motion. Tymoczko shows that efficient voice leading between harmonically consistent chords (i.e., between transpositions or inversions of the same chord) is possible only when the chords are nearly symmetrical — near a chord that is invariant under some transposition, inversion, or permutation. This constraint explains why Western tonal music uses the particular chords it does: major triads, dominant sevenths, etc., are all nearly symmetrical.

# Key Properties
1. All voices move by short distances (typically 0-2 semitones)
2. Central goal of contrapuntal writing
3. Possible between transpositions/inversions only when chords are nearly symmetrical
4. The more evenly a chord divides the octave, the smaller the voice leadings to its transpositions
5. Acoustic consonance implies near evenness, which implies efficient voice leading
6. Synonym for "voice leadings in which all voices move by short distances"

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Choose two chords to connect
2. Find the voice mapping that minimizes total voice motion
3. If the minimum is small, an efficient voice leading exists
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. All voices move by 0-2 semitones
2. No large leaps in any voice

# Context & Application
Efficient voice leading is a basic norm across the extended common practice. From Bach chorales to jazz voicings to Wagnerian chromaticism, composers seek smooth voice connections between chords. The theoretical question of which chord pairs admit efficient voice leading is answered by the near-symmetry theory of Section 2.9. The practical consequence is that nearly even chords (triads, seventh chords, scales) are privileged for polyphonic writing.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking efficient voice leading means all voices must move
  **Correction**: Holding a voice on the same note (common tone) is maximally efficient for that voice

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Equating efficient voice leading with "good" voice leading
  **Clarification**: Efficient voice leading is one desirable property; other factors (avoiding parallels, maintaining independence, etc.) also matter
