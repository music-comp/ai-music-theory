# Quick Definition
Renaissance music (exemplified by Josquin) represents the emergence of triadic harmony — the strong preference for three-note consonances without doublings — as an "unbidden byproduct" of favoring richer sonorities in three-voice polyphony.

# Core Definition
Tymoczko analyzes Josquin's "Tu pauperum refugium" as exemplifying Renaissance practice: 85% of four-voice sonorities are triadic. The triadic quality traces to combinatorial facts about the diatonic scale: when writing three-voice counterpoint using only perfect and imperfect consonances, there are exactly two possibilities — doubled consonances (with repeated notes) and triads (with three distinct pitch classes). Triads are sonically richer, and considered as unordered pitch-class sets, all diatonic triads are related by transposition. Thus a weak preference for three-distinct-pitch-class consonances automatically generates a much stronger harmonic consistency. Historical data shows the proportion of complete triads rising from ~46% of three-voice chords in Dufay to ~72% in Lassus, with four-voice textures increasingly standardized because a fourth voice provides the compositional freedom to maintain complete triads consistently.

# Key Properties
1. 85% of four-voice sonorities in Josquin are triadic
2. Triads arise as the only three-note consonances without doubled notes
3. All diatonic triads are related by scalar transposition — harmonic consistency emerges automatically
4. Parallel fifths/octaves now prohibited; fourths over bass treated as dissonances
5. Four-voice textures standardize because they allow consistent triadic writing with melodic freedom
6. Proportion of complete triads increases from Dufay (~46% of 3-voice) to Lassus (~72%)
7. Diminished triads tolerated in first inversion, suggesting triadic identity trumps dissonant content

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Write four voices with the upper three in close position
2. Favor complete triads over doubled intervals
3. Avoid parallel fifths and octaves
4. Treat fourths over the bass as dissonances resolving to thirds
5. Use conjunct melodic motion, preferring contrary motion
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Predominance of complete triads
2. Four-voice textures with identifiable bass and three upper voices
3. Absence of parallel perfect intervals
4. Cadential convergence anticipating V-I

# Context & Application
The rise of triadic harmony is one of the most consequential developments in Western music. Tymoczko argues it was not a deliberate invention but an emergent property of basic musical preferences — richer three-note consonances over doubled intervals. Once triadic harmony was established, it provided the foundation for the 3+1 voice-leading schema, the circle of thirds, fourth progressions, and eventually functional harmony. The remarkable continuity between Renaissance triadic writing and common-practice harmony (and even contemporary rock guitar) supports the extended common practice thesis.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating triadic harmony as a deliberate theoretical invention
  **Correction**: Tymoczko argues it emerged as the "unbidden byproduct" of preferring three-pitch-class consonances over doubled intervals — a preference based on sonic richness

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Assuming Renaissance harmony and functional harmony are the same
  **Clarification**: Renaissance triads progress freely without functional constraints; classical music adds rules about which chords may follow which
