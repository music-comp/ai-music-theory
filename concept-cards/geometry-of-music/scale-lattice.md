# Quick Definition
A three-dimensional lattice (Type 2 voice-leading lattice) that represents single-semitone voice-leading connections among the four most even seven-note scales: diatonic, acoustic, harmonic minor, and harmonic major. The diatonic circle of fifths zigzags through its center.

# Core Definition
The scale lattice is the specific instance of the Type 2 voice-leading lattice for seven-note scales in twelve-tone chromatic space. Constructed by scrambling the order of three consecutive voice leadings on the diatonic circle of fifths, it is a three-dimensional graph of stacked cubes sharing common faces (Figure 3.11.9). The diatonic circle of fifths zigzags three-dimensionally through the center (alternately up, right, and into the paper). The second-most even seven-note scale (the acoustic) is generated by rearranging pairs of adjacent voice leadings, adding a second dimension. The third-most even scales (harmonic minor and harmonic major) complete the three-dimensional cubes. Each cube contains the six reorderings of three adjacent single-semitone changes. The lattice faithfully represents voice-leading distances despite compressing seven-dimensional chord space into three dimensions.

# Key Properties
1. Three-dimensional: cubes sharing common faces
2. Center: diatonic circle of fifths (zigzagging in 3D)
3. Second layer: acoustic scales
4. Third layer: harmonic minor and harmonic major scales
5. Edges = single-semitone voice leadings between scales
6. Faithfully represents voice-leading distances
7. Used throughout Chapters 6 and 9 for modulation analysis

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Start with the diatonic circle of fifths as a 3D zigzag (up, right, in)
2. Scramble the order of three consecutive voice leadings to generate cubes
3. Example: from C diatonic, applying F->F#, C->C#, G->G# in all 6 orders produces one cube

# Context & Application
The scale lattice is one of the book's "principal analytical tools" (stated in the chapter's opening). It is used to track modulations in Mozart, Prokofiev, Debussy, Shostakovich, and many others. Its power lies in reducing the impossibly complex seven-dimensional scale space to a manageable three-dimensional graph that accurately captures the essential voice-leading relationships.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking the scale lattice is exhaustive
  **Correction**: It includes only the four most even seven-note scales; adding a fourth dimension would include less even scales

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: How can a 3D graph faithfully represent 7D relationships?
  **Clarification**: Because the nearly even seven-note scales cluster in a low-dimensional subregion of 7D chord space; their voice-leading relationships are essentially three-dimensional
