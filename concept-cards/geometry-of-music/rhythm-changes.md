# Quick Definition
The 32-bar AABA chord progression derived from George Gershwin's "I Got Rhythm," serving alongside the blues as one of the two most common harmonic frameworks in jazz, with many ostensibly distinct tunes sharing this progression.

# Core Definition
Rhythm changes is the chord progression from Gershwin's "I Got Rhythm," used as the harmonic framework for numerous jazz compositions. Tunes like "Oleo," "Anthropology," and "Rhythm-a-ning" all use this progression while having different melodies and titles. The form is 32 bars in AABA structure, with the A sections based on I-vi-ii-V-type patterns and the B section featuring a cycle of dominant seventh chords descending by fifths. Together with the blues, rhythm changes account for "a substantial fraction of the jazz repertoire." The B section often features the descending-semitone-over-descending-fifths technique discussed in Section 10.5.

# Key Properties
1. 32-bar AABA form from Gershwin's "I Got Rhythm"
2. One of two foundational jazz forms (with blues)
3. Many distinct tunes share this progression
4. A sections: I-vi-ii-V patterns
5. B section: descending-fifth dominant cycle
6. Repetition across the repertoire aids listener recognition

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. 32-bar AABA form
2. A sections with I-vi-ii-V-type progressions in Bb major
3. B section with dominant sevenths descending by fifths (D7-G7-C7-F7)

# Context & Application
Rhythm changes is the "contrafact" framework par excellence in jazz. Bill Evans' "Oleo" analysis (Section 10.7) demonstrates the full range of jazz techniques applied to this form.
