# Quick Definition
Janacek's "On an Overgrown Path" Series II, No. 1 (1908) exemplifies scale-first composition using two networks of scales connected by single-semitone displacements, generating exotic collections like the Gypsy scale through alteration of harmonic major.

# Core Definition
The piece uses a different scale for each phrase, beginning with Eb harmonic major and moving through Eb harmonic minor, Ab Gypsy, Eb major, and other collections. Two scale networks emerge: a tonic hub-and-spoke network centered on Eb harmonic major, and a nontonic "railroad car" network containing Cs dorian, Cs natural minor, B harmonic minor, and B Gypsy. The scales are more exotic than Debussy's because they begin with harmonic major rather than diatonic, and semitonal alteration generates colorful Gypsy and Gypsy major scales. Phrase 8 uses interscalar transposition to shift phrase 7 into Eb harmonic major, creating a harmonic (but not thematic) recapitulation.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of scale-first composition
- Familiarity with harmonic major, Gypsy, and other exotic scales

# Key Properties
1. Each phrase uses a different scale
2. Tonic network: hub-and-spoke design around Eb harmonic major
3. Nontonic network: railroad-car design (Cs dorian to B Gypsy)
4. Exotic scales arise from semitonal alteration of harmonic major
5. Uses interscalar transposition for recapitulation
6. The two networks are closer together than in Debussy

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Identify the scale for each phrase
2. Arrange scales into networks connected by single-semitone voice leading
3. Note the hub-and-spoke vs. railroad-car topologies

# Context & Application
This piece demonstrates that scale-first composition is not exclusively French/impressionist but appears with a distinctive Eastern European flavor. The analysis shows how the starting collection determines the character of derived scales.
