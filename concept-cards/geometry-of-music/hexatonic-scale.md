# Quick Definition
A six-note scale built by combining two augmented triads a perfect fifth apart, with alternating steps of one and three semitones. It is transpositionally symmetrical (four distinct forms) and rich in major thirds and perfect fifths.

# Core Definition
The hexatonic scale combines two augmented triads (major-third cycles) separated by a perfect fifth. Its step pattern alternates between one and three semitones (1-3-1-3-1-3), making it notably less even than the octatonic. It has three-fold transpositional symmetry, producing four distinct hexatonic collections in twelve-tone equal temperament. It is maximally saturated with major thirds while also containing perfect fifths. Because one step is three semitones, it can produce more exotic melodic intervals than the Group 1 scales, but its "thirds" (stacks of two steps) are still three or four semitones, supporting tertian harmonies.

# Prerequisites
- Stacks of thirds (major-third cycles)

# Key Properties
1. 6 notes with alternating steps of 1 and 3 semitones
2. Three-fold transpositional symmetry: 4 distinct forms
3. Saturated with major thirds and perfect fifths
4. Can transform into harmonic major or minor by "splitting" a semitone into a three-note span
5. Close cousin of the octatonic — both combine cycles at a fifth

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct:
1. Take an augmented triad: C-E-G#
2. Add another a semitone above (or a fifth away): C#-F-A (= Db-F-A)
3. Combine: C-Db-E-F-G#-A

# Context & Application
The hexatonic scale is central to nineteenth- and twentieth-century chromatic harmony. In Tymoczko's framework (and Richard Cohn's "Audacious Euphony"), it is the collection containing the major and minor triads on adjacent cubes of the three-note chord lattice. The hexatonic system organizes triadic voice leading among the major and minor triads that share two augmented triads.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking the hexatonic has evenly spaced steps
  **Correction**: Steps alternate 1-3, which is notably uneven; this places it in Group 2 of Figure 4.4.5

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: How is the hexatonic related to the "hexatonic system" of neo-Riemannian theory?
  **Clarification**: They are the same thing — the hexatonic collection contains the triads that participate in the hexatonic LP cycles described by Cohn and others
