# Quick Definition
Debussy's "Des pas sur la neige" exemplifies scale-first composition with two scalar regions: a D-centered tonic region of five closely related scales and a contrasting Ab-centered region, connected by the whole-tone and acoustic scales as intermediaries.

# Core Definition
The prelude explores five closely related scales in a tonic region (C diatonic, F diatonic, A harmonic minor, D harmonic minor, Bb acoustic), all containing the ostinato D-E-F and all nearby on the scale lattice. A contrasting region contains Ab mixolydian, Ab dorian, and C whole tone. The transition to the secondary region occurs through semitonal shifting (C7 alternating with C#7); the return uses the C whole-tone scale as a bridge, with Bb acoustic serving as intermediary between whole-tone and diatonic. This piece demonstrates how Debussy creates a distinctively scalar sense of tonal structure using "tonic" and "contrasting" scalar regions analogous to tonal key areas.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of scale-first composition
- Familiarity with the acoustic scale as intermediary

# Key Properties
1. Two distinct scalar regions (tonic and contrasting)
2. Tonic region: five scales containing ostinato D-E-F
3. Contrasting region: Ab-centered with tritone-distant tonic
4. Whole-tone scale connects both regions
5. Acoustic scale bridges whole-tone and diatonic
6. A substantial number of Debussy pieces use similar networks

# Construction / Recognition
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Identify all scales in the piece
2. Group them into regions of closely related collections
3. Note the transitional mechanisms between regions
4. Map onto the scale lattice to visualize the relationships

# Context & Application
This analysis is presented as a model for understanding Debussy's scalar practice more generally. Similar networks appear in "Le vent dans la plaine," "Les collines d'Anacapri," "La fille aux cheveux de lin," L'isle joyeuse, and many other works.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Analyzing only in terms of traditional key relationships
  **Correction**: The scalar relationships extend beyond diatonic modulation to include acoustic, harmonic, and whole-tone collections

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Whether this is scale-first or subset technique
  **Clarification**: Both labels apply; the D-E-F ostinato suggests subset technique, but successive scales are also linked by small voice leadings, suggesting scale-first. Tymoczko notes this overlap.
