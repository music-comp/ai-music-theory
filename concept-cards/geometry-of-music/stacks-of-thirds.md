# Quick Definition
Scales can be built by combining cycles of major thirds (augmented triads) or minor thirds (diminished seventh chords) to create hexatonic, octatonic, and whole-tone scales, or by arranging two-octave stacks of major and minor thirds to create the four seven-note scales (diatonic, acoustic, harmonic minor, harmonic major).

# Core Definition
Three major thirds almost close an octave (falling short by about 0.41 semitones), and four minor thirds almost close an octave (overshooting by about 0.62 semitones). Individual third-cycles are too small to serve as scales, but combining them creates larger collections: the hexatonic scale combines two augmented triads a fifth apart; the octatonic combines two diminished sevenths a fifth apart; the whole-tone combines two augmented triads a second apart. Additionally, two-octave stacks of three major thirds and four minor thirds can be arranged in four ways, producing the diatonic, acoustic, harmonic minor, and harmonic major scales. These stacks are the source of the four most important seven-note scales.

# Prerequisites
- Goldilocks Principle

# Key Properties
1. Major-third cycle: C-E-G#-(C) ≈ 3 nearly equal parts of the octave
2. Minor-third cycle: A-C-Eb-Gb-(A) ≈ 4 nearly equal parts
3. Hexatonic = 2 augmented triads at a fifth; octatonic = 2 diminished sevenths at a fifth
4. Whole-tone = 2 augmented triads at a second (perfectly even)
5. Four seven-note scales from 3M+4m thirds in different arrangements: diatonic (MMMMMM pattern), acoustic, harmonic minor, harmonic major

# Construction / Recognition
## Single-Octave (Small Scales):
1. Combine two augmented triads at different intervals -> hexatonic or whole-tone
2. Combine two diminished sevenths at a fifth -> octatonic
## Two-Octave Stack (Seven-Note Scales):
1. Arrange 3 major thirds and 4 minor thirds in a two-octave stack
2. Four distinct arrangements produce the four seven-note scales (Figure 4.4.4)

# Context & Application
The stacks-of-thirds construction reveals the deep structural relationships among the eight scales. It explains why the hexatonic, octatonic, and the four seven-note scales are all saturated with consonant thirds. The construction also underlies the voice-leading relationships explored in Section 4.6, where these scales are shown to be connected by efficient voice leading.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking there are more than four seven-note arrangements
  **Correction**: In principle there is a fifth possibility, but it duplicates a note and is not used in practice (footnote 15)

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Are these the only ways to construct the eight scales?
  **Clarification**: No — the diatonic also arises from stacking fifths. The multiple construction routes are part of what makes these scales "overdetermined."
