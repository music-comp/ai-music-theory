# Quick Definition
The fourth mode of the acoustic scale, combining a whole-tone lower tritone (G-A-B-C#) with an octatonic upper tritone (C#-D-E-F-G), functioning as the default scale for dominant seventh chords when the avoid note (tonic) is raised to #4.

# Core Definition
The lydian dominant scale (jazz name for the acoustic scale in dominant contexts) arises when the avoid note over V7 is raised by semitone: the tonic C becomes C# (the #4 or #11), producing G-A-B-C#-D-E-F. This scale combines a whole-tone lower fragment with an octatonic upper fragment, each spanning a tritone. It contains the essential notes of V7 (root, 3rd, 7th) plus extensions (9th, #11th, 13th) with no avoid notes. The jazz term "lydian dominant" reflects a widespread tendency to conflate scale and mode. The scale was a favorite of Debussy, who frequently accompanied dominant seventh chords with a sharp eleventh.

# Key Properties
1. Fourth mode of the acoustic scale
2. Lower tritone: whole-tone (G-A-B-C#)
3. Upper tritone: octatonic (C#-D-E-F-G)
4. Contains #4/#11 (the raised avoid note)
5. No avoid notes -- all tones are usable
6. The default "altered" dominant scale in jazz
7. Debussy's favorite dominant coloring

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Take a mixolydian scale and raise the fourth degree by semitone
2. Or: take a major scale and lower the seventh while raising the fourth
3. Result: 1-2-3-#4-5-6-b7
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Listen for the characteristic #4 over a dominant seventh chord
2. Check for the whole-tone lower fragment and octatonic upper fragment

# Context & Application
The lydian dominant is the most common non-diatonic dominant scale in jazz. When a bass player makes a tritone substitution independently of the pianist, the pianist's lydian dominant becomes the altered scale over the substituted root.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Treating "lydian dominant" and "acoustic" as different scales
  **Correction**: They are the same scale; "lydian dominant" is the jazz name for one mode of the acoustic scale
