# Quick Definition
Chord voicings built from stacks of perfect fourths rather than thirds, creating an "open" modern sound associated with McCoy Tyner and the post-1950s jazz tradition, which also make the logic of tritone substitution more transparent.

# Core Definition
Postwar jazz musicians reconceived traditional chords as quartal rather than tertian: the dominant and tonic A voicings can be rearranged as stacks of diatonic fourths. Each quartal voicing can be extended upward by one or two fourths before encountering an avoid note. These voicings create a distinctively modern feel associated with Stravinsky, Bartok, and Hindemith, whose quartal harmonies jazz musicians deliberately borrowed. Three-note quartal voicings are now a standard jazz piano technique. Crucially, quartal voicings make the logic of tritone substitution transparent: when a chord is arranged in fourths, it is clear that tritone substitution preserves its quartal organization.

# Prerequisites
- Understanding of left-hand voicings (A and B forms)

# Key Properties
1. Built from stacks of perfect fourths (occasionally with one major third)
2. Create an "open," modern sound
3. Each can be extended before reaching an avoid note
4. Make tritone substitution logic transparent
5. Associated with McCoy Tyner, Bill Evans, and post-1950s jazz
6. Guitar tuning is inherently quartal (strings tuned in fourths)
7. Borrowed from Stravinsky, Bartok, Hindemith into jazz

# Construction / Recognition
## To Construct/Create:
1. Take the A voicing and rearrange as a stack of fourths
2. Extend upward by adding fourths until reaching an avoid note
3. For three-note voicings, select three consecutive notes from a stack of fourths
## To Identify/Recognize:
1. Listen for open, "modern" sound without clear tertian identity
2. Check whether notes can be arranged as consecutive fourths
3. Often associated with modal or static harmonic contexts

# Context & Application
Quartal harmony is now "so central to jazz as to be virtually identified with the style." The popularity of 1960s jazz means composers who build quartal harmonies are more likely to be heard as referencing jazz than Stravinsky or Hindemith.

# Common Errors
- **Error**: Thinking quartal voicings are unrelated to traditional harmony
  **Correction**: They are reconfigurations of the same diatonic material, not a new harmonic system

# Common Confusions
- **Confusion**: Quartal voicings vs. quartal harmony as a harmonic system
  **Clarification**: Jazz quartal voicings operate within functional harmony; they are not a separate harmonic system like pure quartal harmony in some twentieth-century concert music
