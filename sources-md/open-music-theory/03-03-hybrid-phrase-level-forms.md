# Hybrid Phrase-Level Forms

Key Takeaways

  * A [pb_glossary id="2108"]hybrid form[/pb_glossary] combines the beginning of one archetype with the ending from another. 
    * Possible beginnings are [pb_glossary id="1853"]antecedents[/pb_glossary], [pb_glossary id="1831"]presentations[/pb_glossary], or [pb_glossary id="2273"]compound basic ideas[/pb_glossary].
    * Possible endings are [pb_glossary id="1855"]consequents[/pb_glossary], [pb_glossary id="1832"]continuations[/pb_glossary], or [pb_glossary id="2284"]cadential endings[/pb_glossary].
  * Although any beginning could be combined with any ending, some pairings are more common than others (see Example 7).

# What's a hybrid form?

In [The Phrase, Archetypes and Unique Forms](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/phrase-archetypes-unique-forms/), we looked at two phrase-level formal [pb_glossary id="2107"]archetypes[/pb_glossary]: the [pb_glossary id="1826"]sentence[/pb_glossary] and the [pb_glossary id="1852"]period[/pb_glossary]. We found that these forms divide into two parts. A sentence divides into two [pb_glossary id="1209"]subphrases[/pb_glossary]: a [pb_glossary id="1831"]presentation[/pb_glossary] and a [pb_glossary id="1832"]continuation.[/pb_glossary] A period divides into two [pb_glossary id="1220"]phrases[/pb_glossary]: an [pb_glossary id="1853"]antecedent[/pb_glossary] and a [pb_glossary id="1855"]consequent[/pb_glossary]. [caption id="attachment_2109" align="alignright" width="150"][![](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/app/uploads/sites/12/2019/07/Example_001_AntCont-e1623950650588-150x150.png)](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/app/uploads/sites/12/2019/07/Example_001_AntCont-e1623950650588.png) Example 1. An antecedent + continuation in Beethoven, Piano Sonata Op. 13, II, mm. 1–8 ([0:00–0:32](https://open.spotify.com/track/2zSiV8Ze0yyrFtbVzX2Ytg?si=-Zj8tWQQSYSAf1mSPCv-xw)).[/caption] A [pb_glossary id="2108"]hybrid form[/pb_glossary] occurs when the beginning of one archetype is paired with the ending from another archetype. In Example 1, an antecedent is followed by a continuation. 

  * How do we know the first part is an antecedent rather than a presentation? 
    * It ends with a weaker cadence, whereas presentations don't end with cadences.
    * It has a [pb_glossary id="1827"]basic idea[/pb_glossary] followed by a [pb_glossary id="1854"]contrasting idea[/pb_glossary], whereas presentations have a basic idea followed by a repetition of the basic idea.
  * How do we know the second part is a continuation rather than a consequent? 
    * Rather than beginning as consequents do with a basic idea, it begins with [pb_glossary id="1844"]fragmentation[/pb_glossary], which is characteristic of continuations.
    * It begins with a feeling of instability created by the dominant that continues after the half cadence, rather than the stability that's typical of a consequent's beginning.

So far, we know two beginnings (presentation and antecedent) and two endings (continuation and consequent), but there's actually one more possible beginning ([pb_glossary id="2273"]compound basic idea[/pb_glossary]) and one more possible ending ([pb_glossary id="2284"]cadential[/pb_glossary]). Below, we'll outline these new beginnings and endings and provide ways to help distinguish between them. 

# Beginnings

## New: The Compound Basic Idea (c.b.i.)

[caption id="attachment_2276" align="alignright" width="150"][![ A compound basic idea in Haydn’s Piano Sonata in C, Hob. XVI:35, I, mm. 1–8. ](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/app/uploads/sites/12/2019/07/Example_002a_compound_basic_idea-e1623951301511-150x150.png)](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/app/uploads/sites/12/2019/07/Example_002a_compound_basic_idea-e1623951301511.png) Example 2. A compound basic idea in Haydn's Piano Sonata in C, Hob. XVI:35, I, mm. 1–8 ([0:00–0:05](https://open.spotify.com/track/2MX7ZGLIM4lTBP8rIEbnXU?si=00V9I8XNTGC-7urHU2ovLQ)).[/caption] A [pb_glossary id="2273"]compound basic idea (c.b.i.)[/pb_glossary] is an antecedent without a cadence, as seen in Example 2.[footnote]What is "compound" about the compound basic idea? It often functions as the basic idea of a large sentence, one that is sixteen measures in length, where the presentation is eight measures long and comprises two c.b.i. units. It's "compound" in the sense that these large basic ideas are themselves composed of two units: a b.i. and a c.i.[/footnote] 

  * How is Example 2 like an antecedent? 
    * It contains a basic idea followed by a contrasting idea.
  * How is Example 2 different from an antecedent? 
    * It doesn't end with a cadence. Notice how the bass sits on G across mm. 3–4: although the melody comes to a point of rest, the lack of harmonic motion in the bass evades the [pb_glossary id="2811"]half cadence[/pb_glossary] (HC) that might have appeared there.

# Endings

## New: Cadential (cad.)

[caption id="attachment_2281" align="alignright" width="150"][![A cadential ending in Haydn, String Quartet Op. 64, No. 4, II, mm. 1–8. ](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/app/uploads/sites/12/2019/07/Example_004a_cadential-e1623951336715-150x150.png)](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/app/uploads/sites/12/2019/07/Example_004a_cadential-e1623951336715.png) Example 4. A cadential ending in Haydn, String Quartet Op. 64, No. 4, II, mm. 1–8 ([0:05–0:10](https://open.spotify.com/track/2Dyh8LMXHEv4Hxchepyubj?si=1IMP3wmEQ_ut75jSngeJsA)).[/caption] A [pb_glossary id="2284"]cadential (cad.)[/pb_glossary] ending harmonizes a particular bass pattern (demonstrated in Example 4).[footnote]What is the difference between a cadential _idea_ and cadential _ending_? The cadential idea comes at the end of a continuation, and essentially, the cadential ending is like the cadential idea being expanded to four measures and replacing the continuation. That means that "cadential" can occur at two formal levels: the idea level and the subphrase level.[/footnote] The [pb_glossary id="2279"]core bass pattern[/pb_glossary] is _mi–fa–sol–do_[latex](\hat3-\hat4-\hat5-\hat1)[/latex].[footnote]This can also occur in minor, of course, as _me–fa–sol–do_ [latex](\downarrow\hat3-\hat4-\hat5-\hat1)[/latex].[/footnote] The common harmonization of each of these notes is: 

  * _mi_[latex](\hat3)[/latex]: I6
  * _fa_[latex](\hat4)[/latex]: ii6 or IV
  * _sol_[latex](\hat5)[/latex]: V(7) (often elaborated with cadential [latex]^6_4[/latex])
  * _do_[latex](\hat1)[/latex]: I

This core _mi–fa–sol–do_ __[latex](\hat3-\hat4-\hat5-\hat1)[/latex] bass line may be embellished. A common embellishment is to [pb_glossary id="2121"]tonicize[/pb_glossary] the dominant by adding _fi_ before _sol_([latex](\uparrow\hat4-\hat5)[/latex], making the bass line _mi–fa–fi–sol–do_ __[latex](\hat3-\hat4-\uparrow\hat4-\hat5-\hat1)[/latex]. The clearest cadential endings are four measures long with one bass note per measure.
