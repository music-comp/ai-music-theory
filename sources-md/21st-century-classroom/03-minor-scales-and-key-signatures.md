## <span id="page-22-1"></span>3.1 Minor Scales

There are three minor scales: the **natural minor** scale, the **harmonic minor** scale, and the **melodic minor** scale. Play or sing through each one and notice the differences.

![](./images/_page_22_Figure_4.jpeg)

The melodic minor scale has an ascending version, shown above, and a descending version that is the same as the natural minor scale.

![](./images/_page_22_Figure_6.jpeg)

So far, we have looked at minor scales in relation to the major scale. However,

<span id="page-23-1"></span>we will typically encounter minor scales in music using minor key signatures.

## <span id="page-23-0"></span>3.2 Minor Key Signatures

Minor key signatures agree with the notes of the natural minor scale. Since the C natural minor scale had  $E^{\flat}$ ,  $A^{\flat}$ , and  $B^{\flat}$ , the key signature of C minor has three flats, written in the order of flats— $B^{\flat}$ ,  $E^{\flat}$ ,  $A^{\flat}$ .

Natural Minor scale written with major key signature

![](./images/_page_23_Figure_6.jpeg)

Natural Minor scale written with minor key signature

![](./images/_page_23_Figure_8.jpeg)

Therefore, a minor key signature will have three lowered notes—the 3rd, 6th, and 7th—in relation to the corresponding major key signature. We use the term **parallel minor** when referring to a minor scale that *has the same 1st scale degree* (in this case C) as the major. We say, "The parallel minor of E major is E minor," and "The parallel major of F minor is F major." One method of figuring out a minor key signature is to add three flats to the parallel major key signature. This is the same as subtracting three sharps.

![](./images/_page_23_Figure_10.jpeg)

Note on uppercase versus lowercase: When writing below the five-line staff to designate keys, we will use the shorthand of upper case for major (C) and lowercase for minor (c). When writing prose, we will use uppercase: C major and C minor.

We use the term **relative minor** when referring to a minor key that *has* the same key signature as a major key. For example, the relative minor of  $E^{\flat}$  major is C minor because both have three flats in the key signature. Conversely, one could say the relative major of C minor is  $E^{\flat}$  major. The relative major is three half steps above the relative minor.
